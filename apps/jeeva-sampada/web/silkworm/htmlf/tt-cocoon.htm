Cocoon Production

 

          Production of tasar silk at the beginning of 1st Five Year Plan (1951-56) was 141 MT. It rose to 465 MT during VII plan period (1985-90). In the year 1986-87 maximum production of 548 MT tasar raw silk was registered. Thereafter, drastic decline was recorded (235 MT during VIII plan).  The present production of tasar silk is 249 MT (IX plan period 1997-2002). Economics of cocoon production

 

Tasar silk industry has proved to be an excellent example for alleviating unemployment and rural poverty for tribals in India. It is a labour intensive, export oriented cottage industry providing employment with minimum investment. An investment of about Rs.200/- towards the cost of seed and miscellaneous expenses can bring a return of about Rs.1,750/- (Table 4) within a period of 35-50 days up to cocoon formation when the existing forest plants are used whereas by utilizing economic plantation and employing family labour, the rearers’ family can earn Rs. 12,425  to Rs. 14,950 with improved rearing technology (Table-5).
