Identified future needs

 

In  the present era of WTO,  the time is ripe now when  India should  start producing large scale bivoltine silk in the areas suitable for bivoltine silkworm rearing.  The production which is now restricted only to less than 1000 M.T. has to be increased significantly to reach a production level of about 5000 MT.  Since quantum jump in the bivoltine production will require stupendous effort and creation of new facilities, India should look into the improvement of production of cross breed cocoons and its quality.  This could be done if the local races are replaced by some productive multivoltines which when crossed with the better combining bivoltines will be able to produce 2-A grade silk.  Another issue which remains un-tapped is the potentiality of mulberry.  Mulberry leaf production which is very low now, has to be increased significantly by utilizing the tropical climatic condition (longer day light and more sunshine). Under a crash programme, the old mulberry varieties needs to be replaced by the potential ones with new package of practices of cultivation comprising of organic farming, water harvesting and integrated pest  management.

 

          With regard to silk productivity improvement, more emphasis is necessary on the following aspects during the development of silkworm breeds / hybrids:

 

Ø      Development of silkworm breeds/hybrids resistant to pathogens like BmNPV, BmDNV, BmIFV etc.

Ø      Development of silkworm breeds/ hybrids to suit varied agro-climatic zones of the country, especially for sub-optimal conditions.

Ø      Development of more sex-limited silkworm breeds for easy grainage operation and egg recovery.

Ø      Enrichment of gene bank with silkworm stocks having specific characters like shorter larval duration, thin filament, thick filament, longer filament, high neatness, low boil off loss, disease resistance, thermo-tolerance, etc.

Ø      Application of other breeding techniques like androgenesis, parthenogenesis etc., to develop outstanding genotypes with less phenotypic variation, more hybrid vigour and combining ability.

Ø      Isolation and cloning of genes from other organisms by recombinant DNA technology for key characters in silkworm viz., disease resistance, thermo-tolerance, high productivity, growth rate etc.

Ø      Innovation of rearing techniques and equipments for reduction in cost of rearing and maximizing cocoon yield.

Ø      Development of integrated methods involving eco-friendly approaches for control of diseases and pests of silkworm.
