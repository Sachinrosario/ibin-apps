Threats

 

Tasar culture faces threats of its own due to the wild nature and outdoor rearing on the forest trees. The annual tasar silk production depends upon the biotic (food, pest/parasites/predators and silkworm diseases) and abiotic (temperature, humidity and rainfall) factors.

 

I.  BIOTIC

1.Blepharipa zebina (Diptera: Tachiniidae)

 

 

 

Adult flies greyish in colour, looks bigger than housefly (12-14 mm), lay eggs (250-300) on tasar silkworm.

Flies lay eggs glued to the body of host generally near the head end. Eggs hatch after three days of ovipostion. The young maggot bores through skin into the body leaving black scar mark and consumes internal organs. Maggots are pale yellow and body is cylindrical in form and 16 mm in length and come out from cocoon by making hole in the posterior end.

2. Xanthopimpla pedator (Hymenoptera:
Ichneumonidae)

Adult fly bright yellow in colour with number of black bands, black spot on each sternum dorso-laterally, Long needle like ovipositor (1 cm long) with two identical styletes in female of the fly.

Female pierces its ovipositor inside the body of the larva and lays egg. Normally single egg is laid, but instances of laying more than one egg are not unknown. The young one after hatching consumes the tissues and pupates inside. The adult bores & cuts the anterior end of the cocoon and comes out.

3.Canthecona furcellata (Hemiptera: Pentatomidae)

Adult with brownish triangular body (15 mm) & a long sting on the mouth.

Both young and adult of the sting bug suck the blood of silkworm leading to death.

4. Sycanus collaris (Hemiptera: Reduviidae)

Adult black coloured bug of approximately 2.5 cm long. Head long and conical, mouth parts modified into a long proboscis lies in a cross striated groove between front coxae while in rest.

Both young and adult suck the haemolymph of the larva by inserting proboscis into the body of the host.

5.Vespa orientalis (Hymenoptera: Vespidae)

Abdomen yellow and dark brown bands with well developed poisonous stings and clubbed antenna.

First and second stage worm of tasar silkworm is predated by wasp.

 

c) Silkworm diseases: Tasar silkworm is susceptible to various diseases that pose great threat to the industry. It has been observed that during rearing period 30-35% crop is lost due to silkworm diseases. There are four types of diseases causing considerable loss to the industry. II.  ABIOTIC

 

a) Abiotic conditions indirectly influence the tasar silk industry. The existing causes of diseases depends upon certain predisposing factors. Important factors which contribute towards the quick multiplication of the micro organisms are temperature and humidity. It has been observed that high temperature (above 35°C) associated with high humidity (above 65%) i.e. muggy and sultry weather creates a congenial pathological state for provoking virosis in A. myitta while the induction of bacteriosis is accelerated with the decrease in the two factors.

 

b) During summer preservation of seed cocoons grainage temperature should not be above 35°C and humidity range should be 40-60 per cent. In some of the tasar growing areas, temperature shoots up to 48°C and humidity dips below 40 per cent. So, long term preservation of seed cocoons during pupal diapause, loss of live stock to the tune of 30-50% in the form of pupal mortality and stray emergence is also a great threat to the tasar industry. Abiotic factors are equally responsible to a greater extent.
