The silkworm cultivation began 5000 years ago in China. Sericulture spread to Korea and later to Japan and southern Asia. During the eleventh century European traders carried several eggs and seeds of the mulberry tree and began rearing silkworms in Europe. Today, in Europe. the silkworm moth lives in captivity, and have been domesticated, that they can no longer survive independently in nature. Wild populations are presumably extinct in many parts of the world; however, old relatives still exists in Asia. India Is known for a rich fauna of sericigenous insects and it is the only country in the world producing all five types of natural silk viz., Mulberry, Oak, Tasar, Muga and Eri. These silkworm bioresources are wild, distributed throughout the country and have bearing not only on the economy of the country but also have a socio-traditional value. Therefore, there is an urgent need to create an exhaustive, dynamic, easy accessible database for evaluating, indexing and utilization of these bioresource and to develop suitable strategy for in-situ widely ex-situ conservation and sustainable utilization of this bioresource.

 

         Building a comprehensive database on sericigenous material, which includes all levels of species information (including phylogeny) will be of tremendous value in annual to all biologists and in particular to silkworm breeders. Our intent is to enable anyone to reach just what he or she needs from the vast amount of well-documented raw data. Traditional methods of preservation have ensured the longevity of research materials, but at the cost of reduced access. The Advances in the Information Technology, development of on-line databases, development of new tools in phylogenetic analysis and renewed interest and support of biological systematics in the form of digital technology has revolutionised the traditional concepts of preservation, distribution, access and meeting the strategic goals. Besides, digitization makes the information as well as resource sharing more feasible, flexible and enhanced capabilities for analysis and manipulation.

 

          The Department of Biotechnology, Ministry of Science & Technology, Government of India has identified several areas with a main goal of collection and compilation of the existing knowledge and to make the digitized Inventory of the information. The Department has identified the Central Sericultural Research and Training Institute, Mysore as the nodal agency for the compilation of the digitized Inventory of silkworm bioresource. I am happy to be associated with this Institute and congratulate the commendable work done in this direction by the Director, Dr. S.B. Dandin and his fellow scientists in bringing out this volume on the Digitized Inventory on all five forms of natural silk producing bioresource of the country.
