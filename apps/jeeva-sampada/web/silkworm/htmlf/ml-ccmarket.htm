Cocoon marketing system

 

            The marketing of mulberry silk cocoons is officially  regulated in all the major silk producing states except West Bengal. Market regulation was introduced during late 1950s to curb the exploitation by the middle men. Generally, cocoons are sold  and reeled within a week of their harvest.  It was made obligatory for all cocoon transactions to take place at the notified markets where only licensed rearers and reelers can conduct their business. The sale of cocoons at these markets is carried out by open auction to the highest bidder.

 

 

          In Andhra Pradesh, a rearer can obtain permit to sell cocoons in a larger market in Karnataka. However, in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, all  transactions are supposed to be done within the state.  Market fee of 1% is collected from the rearer and the reeler on each transaction. Weighing and payment is effected by market officials in a systematic way to avoid delay in transactions and money is paid to the cocoon growers immediately after the transaction.

 

          In India, there is no scientific grading system in the cocoon markets. Grading, inspection and fixation of cocoon price are based on the proportion of good, double, pierced, stained and pressed cocoons. The major markets in Karnataka are Ramanagaram, Kollegal, Kolar, Kanakpura, Siddlaghatta, T. Narasipur and Vijayapura, in Tamil Nadu Dharmapuri, Hosur, Vaniyambadi and Coimbatore and in Andhra Pradesh Hindupur, Madanapalli and Dharmavaram. In Jammu and Kashmir, sericulture has been practiced under state monopoly and only state was allowed to market cocoons and yarns. Recently, silk marketing and reeling has been de-monopolized. Cocoon markets and silk exchanges are being established and support prices are increased substantially.
