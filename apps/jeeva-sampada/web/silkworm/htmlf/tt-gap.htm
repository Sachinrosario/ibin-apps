Gap Areas Identified

 

Initially, the major thrust in tasar culture was given to develop technologies for nursery technique, plant propagation, spacing, trial for leaf yield, development of rearing technique, quality seed production, grainage management and crop improvement through conventional breeding approach. In the process, it has been experienced that a big gap exists between the potential yield and the realized yield, mainly due to the wild nature of insect, lack of information on nutritional requirement of silkworm, uncertainty of crop due to environmental rigors, attack of parasites and predators, high incidence of mortality due to diseases, inadequate knowledge of diapause physiology non-synchronization of moth emergence, lack of proper identification of genetic marker with phenotypic expression etc. Important gap areas are detailed below.

 

Insect nutrition: Quality of leaf with respect to total moisture and primary metabolites like nitrogen, proteins, minerals, fibre and carbohydrate play a significant role in promoting healthy development of silkworm larvae and in-turn on qualitative and quantitative silk production. Further, food plant selection is solely determined by secondary metabolites such as oils, glycosides, alkaloids etc. Therefore, the whole gamut of nutritional aspect of the silkworm has to be studied considering the nutritional requirement of the worm vis-à-vis the nutrients available to them through food plant.  Observations made on enhancement of tropical tasar silk production through the manipulation of insect herbivore interaction in A. mylitta with respect to feeding behaviour suggests that β-sitosterol acts as an attractant and phagostimulant, ellagic acid serves as deterrent for A. mylitta. Standardized dose of β-sitosterol at 4.0 µg/g body weight significantly enhances the larval body, silk gland and gonad weight in both the sexes of tasar silkworm.  The expression of feeding behaviour is under physio-genetic control. The physiological aspects need to be investigated more thoroughly are the feeding rhythm and endocrine regulation of the fecundity behaviour. On the other hand, genetic aspects viz. intra- and inter-population levels and mode of inheritance of such traits will also enable in understanding the mechanism of host race formation and speciation.

 

Keeping in view the insect herbivore interaction pertaining to silkworm and their food plants numerous fields of investigations are open leading to biotechnologal applications in tasar culture. Some of them are:

 

1)              While some work has been done on various types of chemical stimuli in Bombyx mori this field is still barren in case of tasar silkworm with respect to feeding behaviour which may be attended in future for understanding nutritional as well as behavioural requirements.

2)              Host species population may exhibit polymorphyism with respect to anti nutritional factors/deterrents. Hence, it can be used as selectable marker for selection of genotypes devoid of phenolic feeding deterrents.

3)              Well defined feeding attractants, incitants and phagostimulants of tasar silkworm may open avenues for their domestication in future by spraying / mixing them in artificial diets.

4)              Whether feeding stimulants/incitants do also influence fecundity of the silkworm in positive direction by acting as precursors of fecundity enhancing substances (FES) or just contribute towards palatability of the silkworms to their feed vis-à-vis silk conversion ratio should be investigated in detail in tasar.

5)              Precursors of FES, if any may be isolated from food plant varieties of silkworm and such varieties may be multiplied for their future plantation.

6)              Isolation and characterization of such specific phytochemicals associated with fertility and fecundity in grainage of silkworm may facilitate increase in oviposition rates and fecundity in grainage thereby influencing silk productivity in positive manner.

7)              β-carotenoids, precursor of vitamin A act as photoreceptor pigment and have been reported to play a significant role in the incidence of diapause in conjunction with photoperiodism, temperature and humidity. Effect of carotenoids in induction/termination of diapause is required to be explored in wake of controversy as to whether it is a physiological or a genetical phenomenon.

8)              β-sitosterol – a phytosteroid is metabolized by the insects into cholesterol which in turn is converted into ecdysteroids or moulting hormone of insects. The study on variation pattern in β sitosterol among gene pools may lead to selection of host species genotype with high β sitosterol content which in turn may lead to shortening of the silkworm life cycles.

 

Pests, parasites and predators:  Uzi fly, Blepharipa zebina and Ichneumon fly, Xanthopimpla pedator are the two important parasites of tasar silkworm which cause severe damage to the crops. Sometimes crop loss was experienced to the tune of 70-75% during commercial crop. Likewise, stink bug, Canthecona furcellata; preying mantis, Hierodula bipapilla and Reduvid bug, Sycanus collaris are the major predators of silkworm, A. mylitta causing considerable crop loss. It is not possible to control these parasites and predators since the tasar silkworm rearing is outdoor.  For the control of uzi fly, in Bombyx mori, eight hyper parasites were identified and cultures have also been established. Observations reveal that release of Nesolynx thymus could bring down the uzi infestation considerably.  Studies have been initiated to find out suitable biocontrol agent for the control of uzi fly Blepharipa zebina in tasar silkworm. Laboratory cultures of five parasitoids namely Nesolynx thymus, Spalangia endius, Brachymeria lasus, Detrastichus  howardi and Exoristobia philippinesis are maintained on uzi fly and screening on natural parasitization is under progress. Studies to find out biocontrol agents for control of other parasites and predators of tasar silkworm are required to be taken up.

 

Silkworm diseases

 

Tasar silkworm is susceptible to various diseases that pose a big threat to the industry. It is established that in conventional rearing about 60 -65% crop loss is due to diseases. There are four types of diseases which cause considerable loss to the industry. Protozoan pathogen Nosema spp. is the causal organism of pebrine. Virosis, bacteriosis and mycosis are other diseases which are caused by virus, bacteria and fungi resulting in crop losses. This is largely due to the non availability of specific detection method in the early stages of the disease at field level. For proper and precise identification of diseases, diagnostic kit has to be developed.  Experimentation was carried out for control of tasar silkworm diseases and application of phylloplane bacteria showed a significant reduction in mortality due to bacteriosis and virosis.  It has been observed that,  continuous rearing in a plot leads to heavy mortality which results in unstable production. Therefore, an evaluation is necessary on the cause of the disease and methods for ensuring stable production, such as the potentiality of transmission of viruses by insects inhabiting the farm and a method to inactivate the pathogen in soil and rearing trees. Viruses remaining in the soil and on tree trunks also contribute for secondary infection in the field. Therefore, vectors of viruses in rearing needs to be identified and work on inactivation of viruses accumulated in the soil are the urgent needs of the hour.

 

Domestication of tasar silkworm:  Information is available for formulating the artificial diets for B. mori, A. yamamai and A. mylitta. Formulation of artificial diet for domesticate various precious stocks of A. mylitta needs to be worked out.  Studies on nutritional parameters for higher survival, higher fecundity and higher silk yield which would ultimately form the basis for the evolution of suitable plant varieties through conventional plant breeding or through the aids of biotechnology are needed.

 

Physiology of diapause:  A. mylitta produces 2 to 3 crops in a year (July-August, Sep.-Oct. and Nov.-Jan) and it undergoes pupal diapause for a period of 5 to 7 months in trivoltine and bivoltine, respectively. A major portion of seed cocoons is lost due to erratic moth emergence during diapause besides pupal mortality. The reasons may be high fluctuation in temperature and relative humidity in the environment.  Some studies have already been made in A. mylitta on bio-molecular status of different tissues during diapause including termination of diapause by some phyto-hormones. But the threshold point of induction, maintenance and termination of diapause in the line of biochemical and physiological changes during post embryonic and pupal period are still not understood.

 

Non-synchronisation of emergence and low multiplication rate of genetic material:  Variation in maturity is a great challenge to tasar silk industry. Like other lepidopteran  insects, the males mature early and females mature relatively late, causing non-synchronization of male and female emergence, prolonged grainage activities and low dfl to dfl ratio. Because of such differences in maturity only 70-75% of the total moths emerged can be used to produce dfls. Therefore work is to be initiated to synchronise the male and female emergence and to minimize the emergence period, maximise the dfls productors.  Experiments are required to be taken up to get maximum egg laying and maximum hatching in 24 hours making rearing and grainage activities less problematic and uniform.

 

Establishment of cell lines:  Studies on silkworm cell lines did not receive much interest and attention in India. There is no dispute regarding the commendable role it can play in various basic studies like cyto-pathology, physiology etc. along with different aspects of gene regulation specially the synthesis of stress proteins and immuno-genetics. Therefore cell lines of A. mylitta along with other Antheraea species is required to be established.

 

Molecular approach for silkworm breeding:  Macromolecular contents viz. protein, lipid, carbohydrate, acetylcholine etc. were studied in the haemolymph of diapausing pupae of economically important ecoraces. Based on analysis of variance, the ecoraces were categorised into different groups, taking into account the critical difference. However, the different groups were not identical for all parameters. All the studies were indicative of the fact that Raily and Daba are least different from each other. In the biochemical constituents the trivoltine cocoons like Sarihan, Bhandara and Andhra local seem to be different from Daba.

 

Molecular characterization of Daba and Andhra local showed that there are certain bands which are common in both the ecoraces which is indicative of the fact that there is possible genetic proximity between the two ecoraces and may help in chalking out a common breeding programme.  Extensive work needs to be taken up to test the DNA fingerprinting in the inbred and out-bred varieties to standardize the number of bands and band specificity. Further, four ecoraces of tasar silkworms were utilized for molecular characterization. Genomic DNA of the samples was isolated and quantification was carried out. Some of 10 bp oligonucleotides, and some ISSR primers were used for DNA analysis. The different ecoraces of tasar silkworm were similar but clear demarcation among band patterns also existed. Application of biotechnological tools in crop improvement programme of silkworm, A. mylitta is required to be taken up for evolving novel genotypes. The approach of the strategy should be based on linkage map to localize the Quantitative Trait Loci (QTLs) and other related genes with marker. Markers can be identified by using advanced technology RFLP, RAPD, PCR technology. This may help in breeding through gene transfer technology for race improvement to enhance quality tasar production.
