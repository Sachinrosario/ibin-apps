VALUE OF SNAKE

  

Snakes have plenty of impact on the life and economy of man.

These are much feared, ignored but extremely interesting creatures, which have colonized almost all regions of this earth and are adapted to the most varied and diverse ecological conditions.

Snakes as Food

          Snakes serve as an important protein-rich food item  for many tribal in India as they relish the flesh of many large species of snakes like pythons, rat snakes and other colubrids. Many species of rattle snakes and marine snakes are regarded as a delicacy in many  foreign  countries. In China, Thailand and Vietnam  the white meet of phython and rat snakes is consumed by many people as a regular diet and it is said to be tasty like the flesh of a chicken.

 

nake Skin Trade

          There is a great demand of snake skins in many foreign countries for making articles like hand bags, shoes, belts and other such articles. Snake skin is also said to be used in the construction of musical instruments like heads of drums. As a result of snake skin trade, large number of snakes are killed in India and as a result in many areas the entire population of many larger species has been wiped out.

 

Snake as Strong Predators on Pests of Agriculture

          It is evident that the control of insect and rodent pests is an important agriculture input and the role of snakes in this respect is significant.

The studies of Minton (1966), Sharma and Vazirani (1977), Sharma (1982) and Whitakar and Dattatri (1986), revealed that at least eleven species of snakes are predators on serious food pests (the most important ones are certain large snake species like Rajat bansi, Sphalerosophis diadema; Cobra, Naja naja; Krait, Bungarus caeruleus and Saw scaled viper, Echis carinatus).

 

Many species of snakes namely Typhlops braminus, Eryx johni, Eryx conicus, Argyrogena ventromaculatus, Argyrogena fasciolatus, Sphalerosophis atriceps, Sphalerosophis diadema, Sphalerosophis arenarius, Ptyas mucosus, Boiga trigonata and Echis carinatus exhibit strong predation on the numerous insect and rodent pests of agriculture in India and thus serve as a friend of the farmer.Rodents constitute the major component of the diet of pit vipers like Trimersurus monticola of North-East India (Mathew, 1990).

Thee Worm Snake, Ramphotyphlops braminus feed on termites, small caterpillars, crickets and other small insects which damage the agriculture crops.

 

Food Web

Because of their carnivorous food habit, one species or the other occupies specific & live niche in ecosystem and live at the top of the food pyramid. In Rajasthan, Thar desert and Aravalli foot hills, a commonly available species, saw-scaled viper Echis carinatus tops the food web.

 

Snakes in Medicine

          In homeopathy and allopathic systems the snake venom is considered as an important tool in curing many diseases like Cancer,  Tuberculosis, Leprosy, Lumbago, Leucoderma,  Syphilis, Elops, Asthama, Arthritis, Neurotoxic Lumbago and Neuralgia. In the United States of America, snake oil is still used as a remedy for numerous aches and muscular pains. In Japan dried snake powder is used in curing Tuberculosis and Arthritis. In India for sore eyes the snake skin is rubbed on the eyes for treatment.
