Snake bite : symptoms, first aid, treatment and prevention

Symptoms

1. Weakness

2. Drowsiness

3. Paralysis of the throat

4. Haemorrhages and lack of Blood Coagulation (Fibrinogen of blood dissolve and coagulation is prevented).

5.  Symptoms appear in less than one hour and progress rapidly so that Weakness, respiratory distress and cardiac arrest may follow.

Symptoms of Cobra, Krait and King Cobra bite

1. Neurotoxic signs combined with local swelling indicate Krait venom.

2.  Local lesion is formed in all the cases.

3.  In 46% of CObra bite cases­—Tissue necrosis develops.

Symptoms of Russells's Viper and Pit-viper

1.  Blood comes out with urine. (Haemorrhagic signs indicated).

2. Coagulation of blood stops.

3.  Strong swelling develops.

4.  Local haemorrhages develop and red laisons and spots appear.

6.  Blisters develop in very large number on the effected portion.

Treatment of Snake bite : First aid should be given after seeing that

1.  What snake has bitten Poisonous or non-poisonous.

2.   Identification of bitten snake will help in the proper treatment by judging the symptoms produced.

First Aid

1.  Wipe the Venom laying on the skin around the pric.

2.  Site of bite should be washed by cold water.

3.  Wound should be sucked with the help of suction pump or cup.

4.   Incision should be given at the place of bite to remove the blood mixed with venom and suction should be applied.

5.  To localise the effect of the venom a Tourniquet (Bandage) should be used tightly. After every tenth minute this tourniquet should be loosen or released for
1 to 1½ minutes.

6.  Patient should take complete bed rest, no exercise or any kind of physical strain.

7.  Bitten part or limb should be immobilized by splinters.

8.   Treatment for shock or respiratory paralysis should be given as soon as possible.

9.   Application of KmNO­4 (Pot. Permegnate) is not suitable as such not suggested at all.

10.   Medical aid (Hospitalisation) should be given as early as possible.

Specific Treatment–Hospitalisation

1.  Skin test should be done before Antivenin administration.

       —A subcutaneous injection of 0.2 ml of antivenom should be given. If the diameter of the wheel is more than 1 cm. Methyl pridnisolone (100 mg) should be given intravenously with Antivenom for 3 times (after the interval of one hour).

2.  After above treatment­—Polyvalent Anti Snake Venom (Antivenin) should be given in the following way : Inject at least 50 ml concentrated serum initially intravenously. This may be repeated if required (depending on the condition of the patient).

3.  Give hot coffee to the patient after administration of Antivenin.

4.  Give an injection of 1 ml of 1 : 1000 solution of Adrenaline.

5.  If tissue decaying (Necrosis) has occurred a skin graft should be applied which helps in shortening the duration of hospitalisation 88% Cobra bite patient with tissue narcosis require skin graft.

6.   Appropriate antibiotic should be given to prevent septic action of the wounds.

7.  The antivenom serum should be stored at 2-4°C in dark place and should not be exposed to light.

8.  Only the specific antivenom serum should be given when symptoms of systematic poisoning are clinically evident.

9.  A routine practice to inject an antivenin in all cases of suspected snake bites may prove dangerous.

10. In most severe poisoning cases

      (i)         Electrocardiogram should be taken.

     (ii)         Blood platelet count should be done.

    (iii)         Kidney function test should be performed.

(iv)                         Complete blood count should be done.

11. In Viper Venom poisoning, Urine Examination should be done to note the presence or RBC.

12. In all cases of snake bites, Pulse rate, B.P., Respiration should be checked and anti-shock therapy should be applied.

13. At the very first sign of respiratory distress-oxygen should be given and artificial respiration should be restored to.

Prevention

1.   Wearing proper shoes, Hat, Cap or Turban.

2.  By covering feet and calves.

3.  Snake control is effected by destroying rodents (Snake prey) and by introducing—Peacocks, Owls, Hawks, Kites, Falcons and Mongooses, etc. which are strong predators on snakes.

4.   Chemical control; By spraying Carbolic Acid which is a most effective Snake Repellent.

5.  Onions also repel snakes.

6.   Nicotinic acid is also an effective repellent for snakes.

(v)             7.          By sprinkling tobacco powder around homes, mudhutments and tents and area used for sleeping during night on sandy areas or sand dunes.
