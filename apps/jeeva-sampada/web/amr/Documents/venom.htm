SNAKE VENOM

 

Snake Venom

    Snake venom is a mixture of many complex proteins (Toxic Proteins). The main component of snake venom can be defined briefly as follows :

    1. Proteolytic Enzymes : Contain Endopeptids like Trypsin­—causes Necrosis—Cobra, Krait, Vipers.

    2. Phosphadases—Causes Haemolysis and mostly effect Heart and circulation—Cobra, Krait and Vipers.

    3. Neurotoxins—Effect on nervous system causes Paralysis—Cobra, Krait.

    4. Cardiotoxins—Effect the Heart—King Cobra.

    5. Hemolysins & Haemorrigins—Cause bleeding in organs—Kraits.

6.       Crotamines & neurocytolysins—Effect Nervous system—Vipers.

 

Quantity of Venom Produced by a Snake

    A snake can bite and continue to secrete the Venom a number of times in succession. Snakes at Haffkins Institute, Bombay are milked after an interval of 1 month and following is the average yield :

        Cobra     —    0.6 gm dry Venom

        Krait        —    0.022 gm dry Venom

        Russell's Viper    —    0.15 gm dry Venom

        Echis       —    0.0046 gm dry Venom

 

Cobra Venom

    Faint transparent yellow—Becomes turbid in sunlight.

 

Russell's Viper Venom

    White or yellowish, snake Venom is acidic to litmus paper. On drying it settles down to fine needle-like crystals which dissolves in water readily like salt or sugar but rather more quickly. It is proved fatal only if enters in blood or mucous membrane. It can be taken orally provided there is no cut or wound from mouth to the anus in alimentary canal.

 

Average maximum lethal dose of Venom needed to kill a Healthy Man

        Cobra     —    12 mg.

        Krait        —        6 mg.

        Russell's viper    —    15 mg.

        Echis       —        8 mg.
