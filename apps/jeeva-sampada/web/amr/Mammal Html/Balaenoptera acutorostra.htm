HABIT & HABITAT

The Minke Whale prefers to live in deep waters, migrates for more than 900 km distance towards equator in winter season. Feeds on palagic crustaceans, small fish etc. Lives singly or in pairs, but congregates in several hundreds within areas with abundant food. The Killer Whale is its predator.

Habitat Structure: Deep water oceanic form. Habitat structure appears to be stable.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Minke Whale is comparatively smaller in size. The mysticet Whale possesses horny plates on upper jaw useful for filter mechanism. The plate replaces the teeth in the jaws in Whale. Baleens are flat sheaths of keratinous nature projecting down from the upper jaw to act as filters. Head narrow and 'V' shaped possessing a ridge on it. Dorsal fin relatively longer and placed at posterior one third of the back. Pectoral filppers oblong in shape, broader at the base with the white band on it. Tail fluke horizontally flattened, notched in middle and black in colour. Baleen plate size 20 X 12 cm with 230 -360 plates on each side. The Minke Whale is black dorsally while white ventrally in colour. Baleen plates are yellowish white anteriorly, while brownish black posterior. There are about 50 - 70 ventral grooves terminating behind the end of flippers. Females slightly larger in size than males.  

Measurements: (In Meter): Total Length: Male: 6.7 - 9.8; Female 7.3 - 10.7. 

Weight: (In Kg): Whales of 6 m length weigh about 2.0 to 2.7 metric ton.

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: Three subspecies namely davidsoni, acutorostrata and bonarensis are known to occur in Indian waters. A dwarf form, thalmacha, has been reported from Sri Lanka

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Records from carcasses found along the Indian shores (Lal Mohan, 1999).Sighting records of only four specimens of Minke whales from Indian waters during the period between 1800 to 2000 have been reported by Kumaran (2000) and Kumaran (2002).
