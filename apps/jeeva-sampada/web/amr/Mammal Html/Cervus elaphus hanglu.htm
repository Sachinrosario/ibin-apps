HABIT & HABITAT

Kshamir stag prefers to live in dense riverine forests in Dachigam National Park. However, it also undertakes seasonal altitudianl migration. It lives in broad leaved forest between 1450-2480 m altitude in winter, while in coniferous forest to subalpine grass land between 3000-4000 m altitude in summer. It lives in small parties of 2-18 individuals. During winter it may gather in lager herds. The stag shed alters in around March and are replaced by fully grown new ones by September. The rut begins by mid-September and lasts till October end. Single young one is born. The stag lives with harem. Stag is very shy and takes to quick flight at the slightest alarm. After shedding their antlers in March- April, the stags go uphill and congregate near snowline. Kashmir stags can undertake migration up to 15 Km. in the valley. The main preadtors sharing the biotope of hangul are Black Bear, Brown Bear, Snow Leopard an Leopard. 

Habitat Structure: Dense forest, Riverine Habitat areas of Dachigam National Park between 1450-4000 m altitude. Lives in broad leaved forest in winter, while in coniferous forest to subalpine grassland in summer. Habitat structure has decreased >20% in past.

Distribution:

In past Kashmir Stag was distributed in Jammu & Kashmir and to some extent in North Chamba district in Himachal Pradesh. At present it is now restricted to northern and eastern valleys in Kashmir. The distributional range now has been confined to Dactigam National Park near Srinagar and possibly to some adjoining valleys.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

: Kashmir stag is the only surviving but critically endangered subspecies of the Red Deer species(Cervus elaphus). It is a lrage deer with a pair of magnificently spreading antlers. It is related to European Red Deer and Wapti. It is smaller than Sambar (Cervus unicolor). Each anter has generaly 5 or 6 tines (Branches), sometimes even more (Total 10 to 16 points or tines) with brow tines curved upwards. The second tine exceeds the first tine considerably in length. The general colour of the Kashmir Stag is light to dark brown in winter which fades to white on lips, chin and underparts in summer. The white rump patch or "Caudal DIsc" which extends for a very short distance over tail. The white rum patch is divided by a broad median stripe extending down to the base of the tail or even to its extremity. The tail is short. Fawns are spotted.

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: Out of 23 global subspecies of Red Deer only one Cervus elaphus hanglu is found in India.The Indian subspecies is light in colour and possesses simple antlers. The subsepcies at present is endemic to India.

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Dhanze (1997), Inayatullah (1981, as cited in Anonymous, 1994) and Kurt (1978, as cited in Sharma, 1994) reported sightings of hangul in Dachigam National Park. 

Taxon related studies recommended: In-situ Survey, Monitoring, Life history studies, Limiting Factor studies. 

Remarks: The subspecies at present is endemic to India.
