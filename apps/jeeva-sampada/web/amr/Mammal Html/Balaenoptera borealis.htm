HABIT & HABITAT

        

Marine form. Sie whale is basically pelagic form living in small groups of 2-5 or even more individuals. Larger groups are seen in feeding zones. They feed on surface swimming planktons, krils, copepods, amphiphods etc. 

Habitat Structure: Ocean, prefers to live in deep water but also found along cost (Agrawal and Alfred, 1999; Lal Mohan, 1999). Habitat Structure: Stable.

Distribution:

World wide. Found in temperate as well as in sub-tropical oceanic waters. They undertake annual migration from lower altitudes (wintering zone) to higher altitude zone (feeding zone). They are found primarily south of latitude 30' in winter and 40' in summer and autumn. Indian ocean and Arabian sea form part of migratory pathway for the species which comes to tropical waters for calving. Not very common in Indian waters (CAMP reports, 1998)

Population: 

Global Population: Not Known; No. of mature individuals in all the populations: greater than 2500.

Captive Population: Sexual maturity is attained at 5-6 years of age (Agrawal and Alfred, 1999) 

Life span:  (Wild): 60 years; (Captivity): Sie whales can not be kept in captivity due to its large size. 

Population Trend: Declining. Decline @ of more than 20% in last 60 years (Lal Mohan, 1999)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A medium sized whale, smaller in size than Finner and Blue whales. Head 'V'shaped with only one prominant ridge on it from the blow hole. Stout throat groves extend between pectoral flippers and naval. 32-60 grooves in number terminating well before naval. Dorsal fin conspicious, errect and 25-60 cm long, strongly falcated and situated in the postrior one third of the back. Dorsal fin black in colour, pectoral flipper oblong with tips pointed, unusually short (1/11th of total body length) and totally black in colour with no white spots. Tail fluke horizontal and flat. Baleen plates 73 X 40 cm in size. 300-410 plates on each sides, 60 bristles per sq. cm on baleen plate. Body dorsally grey while ventrally light grey or white. Females slightly larger than males of same age group.

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: No subspecies

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Most of the standings reported along the Indian coast reported as B. borealis may be that of B. edeni (Lal Mohan, 1999). However, there are sighting records from Tuticorin, Mandapam and Naduvattaum along the East cost of India (Agrawal and Alfred, 1999). Sighting records of twelve specimens of Sie whales from Indian waters during the period between 1800 to 2000 have been reported by Sathashivam (2000) and Kumaran (2002). 
Type of Sighting: ; Field Notes:  

Taxon related studies recommended: Monitoring 

Remarks: International Whaling Commission (IWC) has progressively shut down Sie whaling operations in North Pacific and Southern Hemisphere during 1970's except in Iceland where hundreds of Sie Whales are caught every year.
