HABIT & HABITAT

Sperm Whale may be found solitary or in groups. Breeding: group size 20-40, individual isolated groups may go up to 150. However, bachelor group size may contain 6-50 individuals also. Sperm Whales are migratory in habit, males may be sighted near poles. They can dive as deep as 2800 meters and be under water for more than an hour. Further they can remain on surface and blow nearly 50 times for an hour before next deep dive. They feed mainly on squids and mesopelagic species.

Habitat Structure: Deep water oceanic forms. Usually not found in areas with less than 180 m depth. No change in the habitat structure.

Distribution:

World wide. Sperm Whale females and young males are confined between 45'N and 45'S latitudes, while mature males travel up to Arctic and Antarctic oceans. Reported from east and west coasts of India and also from Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Sperm Whale is a large sized toothed whale with enormous truncated head. It can be easily identified by its large sized head which is almost 1/3 rd of its body size. The head accommodates a large organ filled with spermaceti. Lower jaw narrow and under slung studded with 18 - 27 large thick and conical teeth on each side. Upper jaw toothless. All seven cervical vertebrae are fused. The single blow hole is placed on the left of the midline near tip of the snout. The hump of the Sperm Whale is distinct, rounded and placed 2/3 rd of back from tip of the snout. This is followed by a series of humps up to the tail. Dorsal fin low, small, pointed and ridge like. Pectoral flippers broad and paddle like. Tail fluke broad and notched in the middle. Body colour deep bluish gray above with white marbling around lips. Under surface lighter. Males are nearly 1/2 times larger than females in size.

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: None

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Sighting records reported from East and West coast and Andaman and Nicobar islands in Indian waters by Agrawal and Alfred (1999). Sighting records of twenty five specimens of Sperm Whales from Indian waters during the period between 1800 to 2000 have been reported in details by Kumaran (2000) and Kumaran (2002).  

Taxon related studies recommended: Survey and Monitoring 

Remarks: Since 1979 International Whaling Commission has totally banned palagic catches of sperm whales.
