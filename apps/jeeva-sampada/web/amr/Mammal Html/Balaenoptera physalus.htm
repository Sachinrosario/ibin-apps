HABIT & HABITAT

Fin whale is a oceanic and pelagic inhabitants. It is also involved in seasonal long distance migrations. Lives in a group of 3-10 or even 20 individuals. Many form larger schools in feeding ground. Feed mainly on Krills, squids and copepods. It's is main predator is Killer Whale. In highly contaminated Mediterranean sea, the heavy metal ions get accumulated in Fin whale which becomes weak and die due to collisions with boats and ships. 

Habitat Structure: Marine forms living in open seas. In Mediterranean sea the Habitat structure is changing due to contamination of heavy metal ions.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: 

Fin whale is a second large sized whale species which is smaller than Blue whale only. Fin whale possesses 'V' shaped head with prominent central ridge over it. There are 56-100 ventral furrows extending up to naval. Back is ridged from dorsal fin to the tail fluke. Dorsal fin distinct but small (about 60 cm) and sharply angled, placed at posterior 1/3 rd of the back. Pectoral filppers falcate. Tail fluke horizontally flattened. Baleen 42 X 24 cm in size. 230-370 plates on each side with 15-35 bristles per sq. cms. Plates are stripped with alternate yellow and blue bands. Baleen fringes coarse, stiff and thick. Body colour black with no or gray mottling dorsally while white ventrally. Dorsal fin, flippers and fluke black in colour. Flippers and fluke white below. Colour of head asymmetrical. Lower lip and palate white in colour.  

Measurements: (In meters): Total Length Male:17.7-22, Female: 18.3-24. 

Weight: (In Tons): 30-80.

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: Some workers recognise two subspecies: B. physalus physalus, (Northen Hemisphere) and B. physalus inoyi, (Gambell) (Southern Hemisphere).

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Sightings reported off Mumbai and Kolkata coasts by de Silva (1987) (as reported in Agrawal and Alfred, 1999). Sighting records of twelve specimens of Fin whale from Indian waters during the period between 1800 to 2000 have been reported in details by Sathashivam (2000) and Kumaran (2002).  

Taxon related studies recommended: Survey and Monitoring 

Remarks: Declaration of Indian Ocean Sanctuary by International Whaling Commission (IWC, 1979) has extended full protection to all whales in the region
