HABIT & HABITAT

The Tibetan Gazelle inhabits the Tibetan plateau at altitudes from 3950-5500 m in small groups varying from 2-12 individuals. The gazelle is an antelope of high-altitude temperature biotopes. It prefers grassy valleys and plateau. Unlike other gazelles, it is a very shy animal and can be startled by any noise. It feeds in areas of scanty vegetation. The gazelle breeds in December and gives birth to one or two young ones in May. 

Habitat Structure: grassy valleys and plateau at higer elevation. The Habitat structure is deacreasing with general decline.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tibeaten Gazelle is slightly smaller than Tibetan Antelope. The beautiful gazelle is gery in colour. The summer coat is pale in colour with slaty grey hairs. The winter coat is denser with pale or sandy fawn hairs. There is a distinct white patch on rump which extends all around the base of the tail forming a sort of caudal disc. The tail is short with blackish tip. The underparts are white but do not contrast sharply with the back. Horns, in bucks, rise vertically, curve sharply backwards after a short distance. The horn may possess 25 - 30 rings. Females without horns. 

Measurements: (In cm): Haed and Body Length: 100-110; Hegiht at shoulders: 60; Length of horns: 30-39.4.
