HABIT & HABITAT

: Blue whale prefers to live in cold water and open sea. It is involved in seasonal inter continental migration. Lives singally or in pairs, seldom seen in large schools. Feeds mainly on euphausids (Krills), amphipods, copepods and cephalopods. 

Habitat Structure: Marine forms living in open seas. Cold and open waters, Oceans. (Agrawal and Alfred, 1999; Lal Mohan, 1999). Habitat Structure: Stable

Distribution:

From polar to tropical waters. Living in polar waters during summer, migrating towards equator in winter. One population has spread and separated from the one in Southern hemisphere and lives in North Indian ocean waters at all the times of the year. This population has been named as Pygmy Blue Whales. They have been reported from Mangalore, Cochin, Tuticorin, Calicut, Surat, Okha and number of other places along the Indian cost. Records mostly from carcasses washed towards shore.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Blue whales are largest living mammals reaching up to total body length of 34 metres weighing maximum up to 160 tons. Head 'V'shaped from above with a single ridge on it. 60 to 100 parallel plates present on the throat and chest extending up to naval. Dorsal fin small (15-45 cm in height), falcate and low placed in the last quarter of the back.Pectoral flippers small and tapering about 1/7 th - 1/8 th body length. Blackish above, baleen and its fringes all black in colour. 260 -400 baleen plates on each side. The plates 100 X 55 cm in size with up to 30 bristles per sq. cm. Blow hole situated on top of the posterior end of the head.Tail fluke dorsoventrally flat and notched in middle. In general tail portion is narrow. The Blue whales are light blue grey or greyish mottled with white colour. Underparts lighter. Females are slightly larger than the males.

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: Out of three recognised subspecies Balanoptera musculus brevicauda, Ichihara (Pygmy Blue Whale) is known to occur in Indian waters.

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Sighting records of caracasses reported at number of places mostly along West coast of India (Lal Mohan, 1999). Sighting records of about fifty specimens of Blue whales from Indian waters during the period between 1800 to 2000 have been reported in details by Sathashivam (2000) and Kumaran (2002).  

Taxon related studies recommended: Survey and Monitoring 

Remarks:  Declaration of Indian Ocean Sanctuary by International Whaling Commission (IWC, 1979) has extended full protection to all whale species in this region.
