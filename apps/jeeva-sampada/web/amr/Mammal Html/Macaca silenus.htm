HABIT & HABITAT

The Lion-tailed macaque is gregarious and is a native of dense evergreen or semi evergreen rain forests of high trees in secluded areas. Its occurrence in tea and cardamom plantations interspersed with thick forests has also been reported. It lives in group of 4-30 individuals. The group prefers tall trees of 20m or more in height. It feeds on fruits, seeds, buds, leaves, flowers, insects etc. Though more arboreal in habit, its food plant species are said to belong to climber and undergrowth categories. Diurnal in behavior.

                       Habitat Structure: Decreasing.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 A medium sized macaque distinguished from all other macaque species by presence of great mane of long dark-gray on brownish gray hairs growing from the temples and checks, by its glossy black coat and also by its tuft of hairs at the tail tip like lion's tail. Hence the name lion tailed macaque. The tuft of hairs is more pronounced in males. The tail length is one-half to two third of the head and body length. The face is bare and black.

Measurements: (In mm) Head and Body Length: Male:508-610, Female: 415-481 Tail Length: Male 254-386, Female: 254-318.

Weight: 6-7 Kg

Miscellaneous Information   

Intraspecific variations: No subspecies. However, Wynaad Plateau population and the one living on high wavy mountains are reported to be larger than the Anaimalai Hill populations (Anonymous, 1994).

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Sighting records reported by Ajith Kumar (2001) and many others. Status surveys were conducted by Kerala Forest Research Institute also during 1993-2001(CAMP Report, 2003). 

Taxon related studies recommended: Survey, Monitoring, Habitat management
