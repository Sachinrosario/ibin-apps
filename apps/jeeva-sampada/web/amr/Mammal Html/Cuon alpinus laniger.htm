HABIT & HABITAT

 The most distinctive differences between the habits of Ladakh Dhole and those of the other two subspecies are: 1) Ladakh Dholes live at very high altitudes in almost cold desert region of Ladakh. 2) They are diurnal in habit and live in packs of 2-30 individuals per pack in open country. The other habits such as living in packs with a leader in dens or caves, hunting skills and undertaking long distance migration are same as those for other two subspecies . They attack and kill in team work animals such as wild sheep and antelope in Ladakah and Markhor, musk deer and goral in Kashmir. Little or no information is availbe about breeding habits of Ladakh Dhole. Ladakh Dholes are also prone to fluctuations in populations due to fatal diseases.

Habitat Structure: Barren cold desert region at higher altitudes of Ladakh

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

The Ladakh Dhole shows all the physical characteristics of Indian Wild Dog or Dhole (Cuon alpinus dukhunensis) described earlier. The only difference between the two subspecies lies in possession of thicker and paler red coloured pelage than the peninsular Dhole and the Himalayan Dhole. The Measurment and weight ranges mentioned for Cuon alipinus dukhunensis are same for all the three subspecies. Sighting of Ladakh Dhole is very rare. In size Ladakh Dhole is midway between wolf and Jackal.

 Measurements: Same as those of Indian Wild Dog or Dhole.

Weight: Same as those of Indian Wild Dog or Dhole.

Miscellaneous Information   

Intraspecific variations: The Ladakh population has been recognised as a separate Cuon subspecies viz: Cuon alpinus laniger, Pallas

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct/Indirect; Field Notes: Johnsingh (1985,1986) and Sharma (1994) reported occurrence of Ladakh Dhole in Kashmir and Tibet region. 

Taxon related studies recommended: In-situ Survey, Monitoring, Taxonomic studies, Life history studies, Limiting factor research, Habitat management, Population status studies etc.

Remarks:  The higher category of IUCN has been recommended for the subspecies reported from Jammu and Kashmir (Ladakh.)
