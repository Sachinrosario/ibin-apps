Other Biological Details

 

 

 

HABIT & HABITAT

                       Phayre's Leaf Monkey is seen in dense Sal-forests. It's preferred habitat is bamboo and banana plantations along hill sides in the vicinity of streams. It is predominantly arboreal and folivorous. It's food consists mainly of leaves, followed by flowers and fruits and bamboo shoots. It is very shy in nature. When alarmed the whole group rushes to the upper canopy in the forest leaping from tree to tree. The group size consists of about 30 individuals.

Habitat Structure: Sal forest, Mixed moist deciduous forests. Middle to upper story dweller. Rarely seen at the canopy.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 The Phayre's Leaf Monkey is a dark ashy brown langur. The head some times has a crest but not a cap like capped langur. There is a distinct white patch around the eyes and lips, otherwise the face is black in colour. The shoulders and upper arms have silvery luster. Ventrum white in colour. Females possess pale patch on the thighs. Young ones are straw coloured.

Measurements: (In mm): Head and Body Length: Male: 420-600, Female: 442-570; Tail Length: Male: 648-858, Female: 720-795

Miscellaneous Information   

Intraspecific variations: Brandon - Jones et. al.(2002) has considered Phayre's laef Monkey as a subspecies of Trachepithecus obsurus in his unpublished working document for CAMP workshop organised by Zoooutreach organisation, Coimbatore, in March 2002. However, Trachepithecus phayrei (Blyth) has been retained here following Corbet and Hill (1997), Wilson and Reeder (1993) and ENVIS Report of wildlife institute of India on Non-Human primates of India (2001).

Project Specific Records:  Record not available.; 

Museum Records:  Locality: Record not available.;
