HABIT & HABITAT

                   The Himalyan Dholes shows similarities in habit, ecology and behaviour with those of the Peninsular population of Indian wild DOg or Dhole. The Red Dog prefers to restrict itself to forest which may be dry or moist deciduous forest or even alpine type. Sudden fluctuations in the populations due to fatal diseases have been reported in this species also. No detailed information is available on its breeding biology. Dholes in Himalaya are known to feed on markhor, musk deer, goral, chital, samber, swamp deer , pig, gaur , buffalo etc.  

Habitat Structure: Dry and Moist deciduous forests

Distribution:         

Himalyan Red dog is known to occur in the entire Himalayan range from Kumaon (Uttaranchal Pradesh) to Sikkim, West Bengal and Assam and to North Indian states within Indian Limits.

No.of Locations: Many; Type: Fragmented

Extent of Occurrence         greater than 20000 sq.kms.

         
Area of occurrence:           greater than 2001 sq.kms            

Altitude:                            May go up to 2440 m or even more.

Population:                      

Global Population: Regional Population About 1000. Mature individuals between 500-600 (CAMP Report,1998); No. of mature individuals in all the populations: less than 2500

Captive Population: None in Indian Zoos

Population Trend: Declining

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 The Himalyan Red Dog shows all the cahracteristics of Indian Wild Dog or Dhole ( Cuon alpinus dukhunensis) described eralier. The only difference between the three subspecies lies in possession of thick and darker coloured (Deep red colour) pelage than the peninsular and Ladakh population. However, pelage of Ladakh population is comparatively more thicker than that of Himalyan Red Dog. The measurements and weight ranges mentioned for Cuon alpinus dukhuneusis are same for all the three subspecies. Sighting of Himalyan Red Dog is infrequent although it still has an excellent and wide-spread wildlife habitat with wider range of distribution.

Measurements: Same as that of Cuon alpinus dukhunensis.

Weight: Same as that of Cuon alpinus dukhunensis

Miscellaneous Information   

Intraspecific variations: The Himalyan Wild dog population has been recognised as a separate subspecies viz. Cuon alpinus primaevus (Hodgson)

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect; Field Notes: Sighting records reported by Johnsingh (1985, 1986), Agrawal et al. (1992) Sharma (1994) and Shreshtha (1997) . 

Taxon related studies recommended: In-situ survey, monitoring, Taxonomic studies, Life history studies, Limiting Factor research, Habitat management, Population status studies, Breeding Biology and Ecology of Himalayan Populations.

Remarks: Sudden fluctuations in the wild dog populations in India due to diseases have been reported for all the subspecies. Scientific reasons for the same may be studied in details.
