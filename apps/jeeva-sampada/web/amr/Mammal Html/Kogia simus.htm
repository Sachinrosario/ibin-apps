HABIT & HABITAT

Though inhabitants of deep oceans, Dwarf Sperm Whales are more fond of inshore waters along the edge of continental shelf. Juveniles and sub adults feed closer to the inshore inside the continental shelf, though still foraging on the sea bed. It is also a specialist bottom feeder, thriving on squids and crustaceans. It is capable of deep dives and of remaining for considerable periods under water. Group size does not exceed ten individuals. The group size may consists of females, calves, immature and adults. The calf remains with its mother for about one year. 

Habitat Structure:  Dwarf Sperm Whale is an inhabitant of deep oceanic waters. No change in the Habitat structure.

Distribution:

Worldwide, found in temperate, subtropical and tropical waters. It is more inshore along the edge of the continental shelf than Pygmy Whale. In Indian waters it has been reported from Thiruananthapuram (Trivandrum) and Vishakhapatnam coasts.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Dwarf Sperm Whale is similar in mophology to Pygmy Sperm Whale in having a large bulbous and blunt forehead, underslung and narrow lower jaw with 8-13 pairs of simple small teeth and possession of false gills. However, it differes in having smaller size and in possession of tall dorsal fin (more than 1/20th of total body length) which is placed in the anterior half of the back. Pectoral flippers obtusely falcate. Tail fluke horizontally flattened and deeply notched in the middle. Blowhole placed at the anterior end of the forehead. Dwarf Sperm Whale is dark steel grey in colour above, while pale grey or whitish below. There is a prominant gill shaped white marked on both the sides of the head. Males are usually larger than females.  

Measurements: (In meters): Total Length: 2.12 to 2.7
