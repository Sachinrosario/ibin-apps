HABIT & HABITAT

        Terrestrial, entirely arboreal and diurnal forest dwelling langur. Inhabits dense and secluded tropical and secondary forests including bamboo forest intersected by streams.   

Habitat Structure: Dense and Secluded tropical and secondary forests intersected by streams.

CITES APPENDIX: I; Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act (1972) (As amended up to 2002): Schedule I; Part I; Indian Red Data Book (IUCN, 1994): Vulnerable; IUCN (1998) (Proposed): Lower Risk-Near Threatened (National) Data Deficient (Global); IUCN (2002) (Proposed): CAMP Workshop Report (2003) has assigned different conservation status for the four subspecies of Trachypithecus pileatus. However, Endangered (National) status has been taken into consideration, here, for the entire species based on all their assessments.;     

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 Langur with robust built and with tail length longer than head and body length. Harsh hair directed backwards from the forehead to form a cap. Cheek hairs long and cover ears partly. Dorsal colouration dusky gray to ashy gray. However, hands, feet, mid dorsal region and occasionally crown dark brown or black. Venfrum, cheeks and sides of neck pale fulvous to golden brown. Face black, while tail dark brown with distal half blackish in colour.

Measurements: (In mm): Head and Body Length: Male: 533-710, Female: 490-610, Tail Length: Male: 859-1040, Female: 750-955.

Weight: (In Kg): Male: 11.5-14, Female: 9.5-11.3

Miscellaneous Information  

Intraspecific variations: Four subspecies are recognized (Pocock, 1939): i) T.p. brahma (Lakhimpur, Assam); ii) T. p. durga (Below 600m, Northeast India), iii) T.p. pileatus (Above 600m, Northeast India) and iv) T. p. tenebricus (Kamrup Dist, Assam)

Museum Records:  Locality: Record not available.;   

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct; Field Notes: Direct sighing records reported by number of authorities at different places and times in North-East India (CAMP Report, 2003).   

Taxon related studies recommended: In-Situ Survey, Monitoring and Habitat Management
