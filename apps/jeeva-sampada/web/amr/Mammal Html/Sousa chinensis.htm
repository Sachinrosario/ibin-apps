HABIT & HABITAT     

The Humpback Dolphins, though marine forms, they are also known to occur in inshore and coastal waters of India. It has been reported from number of places off Indian coast and also from backwaters near Cochin. They are generally found concentrated near the mouths of the rivers. They feed mainly on pelagic and benthic fishes. They move in shoals near shore or inshore waters. They may live in group size of 3-30 (Agrawal and Alfred, 1999.) 

Habitat Structure: Coastal, estuaries and confluence of rivers with the seas. >20% Habitat structure appears to have decreased in last 20 years.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: 

The most distinctive character of Humpback Dolphin is a distinct long and low hump on middle of the back. Dorsal fin triangular and is on a raised platform. It is falcate. Beak slender and sharply defined from the forehead. Flippers broad near the base and taper distally. Caudal fluke with a distinct notch. Some forms may lack the hump but posses larger dorsal fin. Body colour variable in the shades between gray to steel gray to ultimately white. Ventral side creamy white with or with out blue elongated spots. Tip of beak, base of dorsal fin and throat possess white patches. Teeth well developed in both the jaws with 68-76 teeth. 

Measurements: (In m): Total Length: Male: 3.25; Female : 2.70; New born: 1.0. 

Weight: (In Kg): 1.0 m long Dolphin weighed 37 Kg, while 2-3 m long weighed 139 Kg.

Miscellaneous Information 

Intraspecific variations: Nothing definite can be said due to lack of sufficient material. However, Leatherwood and Reeves (1983) have considered two separate groups: 1) Dark coloured with a hump on back. Distribution : West of Indonesia. and 2) White coloured lacking any hump on the back. Distribution : Chinese waters.

Sighting Records:  Type of Sighting: Direct and Indirect ; Field Notes: Sighting have been recorded during recent field studies carried out from 1985-1997 by Lal Mohan at Calicut. Recorded in a tributary of Ganges 250kms from sea (Bay of Bengal) by Ghosh and Choudhary, 1986.Sighting records of 221 Humpbacked Dolphins from Indian waters during the period between 1800 to 2000 have also been reported by Kumaran (2000) and Kumaran (2002). 
Type of Sighting: ; Field Notes:  

Taxon related studies recommended: In-situ Survey, Monitoring, Limiting factor research, Habitat management 

Remarks: Considering the nature of threat and IUCN category ( Endangered), it is recommended that Sousa chinensis (Osbeck) be listed in schedule I, Part I of Indian wildlife ( Protection ) Act, 1972.
