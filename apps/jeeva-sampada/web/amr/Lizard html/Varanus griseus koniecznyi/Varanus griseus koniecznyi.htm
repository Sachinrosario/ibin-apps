Diagnostic Features

_____________________________________________________________

Size:

Head: The snout is depressed at the end. The nostrils are oblique slits, lying much nearer to the eyes than the end of the muzzle.

Body: The scales on the crown are usually larger than the nuchal scales. The dorsal scales are obtusely keeled, and the lateral caudal scales are indistinctly keeled.

Limbs: The digits are moderately elongated.

Tail: The tail is rounded and whip-like.

Other Biological Details _____________________________________________________________

Distribution: It occurs in the drier parts of Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Punjab in India. Extralimitally, it extends to Pakistan, Afghanistan, Iran and north Africa

Habitat:

This monitor lives in burrows in undulating sandy grounds, with sparse vegetation. It can attain great speed in its movement, if required. During quick movements, however, the body remains raised above the ground. Its food mainly consists of locusts, grasshoppers and crickets; it also takes small vertebrates. The number of eggs in a clutch varies from 15 to 20, and they are kept buried in the sand till hatching.
