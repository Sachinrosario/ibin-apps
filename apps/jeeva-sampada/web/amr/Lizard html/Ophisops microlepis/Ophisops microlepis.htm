Taxonomic Details

_____________________________________________________________

Class: Reptilia

Order: Sauria

Family:Lacertidae

Genus: Ophisops

Species: microlepis

 

Synonyms: 1870.. Ophiops (Gymnops) microlepis Blanford, J. Asiat. Soc. Beng. 39 : p. 351, pl. 15, figs. 1-5 (type loc. Korba, Bilaspur, Madhya Pradesh); 1872.. Gymnops microlepis, Stoliczka, J. Asiat. Soc. Beng. 41 : p. 90.; 1935.. Ophisops microlepis, Smith, Fauna Brit. Ind. 2 : p. 380.

_____________________________________________________________

Diagnostic Features

_____________________________________________________________

Size: Standard length 65 mm.; tail length 145 mm.

Head: Snout elongated and pointed; upper head shields smooth; canthus rostralis sharp; nostril is between two large nasals (upper and lower); frontonasal is single; supralabials are four, the second and third are large and separated from the supraciliaries by a row of small granular scales; the first supraocular is subtriangular, larger than the fourth, which is small and transversely elongate; interparietal long and narrow, narrower than the occipital; fifth labial is subocular; temporal scales are keeled, the upper two, which border the parietal, being smooth and much larger than the others; tympanic shield is large, smooth; no proper collar, but its position is indicated by a series of enlarged plates;

Body: dorsal scales rhomboidal, almost equal, except the outermost rows, in oblique longitudinal series converging towards the vertebral line, these are much smaller than the caudal scales; ventral plates in 6 longitudinal and 24-27 transverse series; 56-66 scales round the middle of the body; a large preanal plate is generally present; digits with very sharply keeled scales beneath,

Limbs: the hind-limbs reaches to the ear or a little beyond; 12-16 femoral pores on each side;

Tail: tail is cylindrical, long and tapering to a point.

Other Biological Details _____________________________________________________________

Distribution: India : Bihar, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan

Habitat:

These diurnal, most agile lacertids, share the same ecological environment with Acanthodactylus cantoris in Gujarat coastal areas. They make thin burrows on the soft soil under the dense bushes of Acacia juliflora and Cactus sp. The lizards are  having an acute sense of homing behaviour and always choose to live in the burrows made by themselves. At Porbandar in Gujarat their population was observed to be the maximum and they live with a perfect harmony in the association of tiny member of the family Ophisops jerdoni, in the crevices on the railway tracks and other such places.
