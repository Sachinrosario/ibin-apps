HABIT & HABITAT

                       Rocky, grass arid areas with plenty of other vegetation. The Star tortoise is  quite active during morning or evening hours of the day and feeds on grass, flowers, vegetables, fruits and all other available vegetable material.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: 

Size :               Small tortoise in which the length of the largest female reaches up to 25 cm.  Males never exceed more than 16 cm in length.

 Body :             Head is of moderate size, its anterior portion is bulged and some what convex.  The upper jaw is slightly tricuspid.  The skin at top of head is divided into small irregular shields. 

 Limbs :            Forelimbs are with five claws, outer side of the arm of forelimb and heel are provided with large imbricate, sharply pointed, bony scales,   a patch of such comparatively more larger scales are present on the back of the thigh. 

 Tail :               Tail is moderately long and terminating  into a spur like scute.

 Carapace :  Elongated, highly domed, vertebral and costal shields form well marked conical humps in the adults, the apex of each hump correspond with the areola, lateral aspects of carapace are almost vertical, anterior and posterior margins reverted, posterior one is strongly serrated.  Nuchal shield absent.  Vertebral shields five in number, costal four in number, marginals are 22 excluding the supracaudal shields.    Plastron large, truncate, notched anteriorly, deeply notched posteriorly.   

Colouration :  Shell is black above, each vertebral and costal shield is with yellow areols from which radiate as many as eight streaks, ultimately producing a radiated pattern which is continuous over the marginals to the plastron. Head and limbs are yellow, more or less spotted with dark brown or black.  Juveniles are almost entirely yellow or orange with black marks along the sutures.

Breeding system:

.Nesting :         Breeding  period throughout the rainy season from June to October.  A single clutch of 3 – 6 hardshelled eggs are laid.  The eggs are laid  at least three times in a year and deposited in a self dug nest pit of  15 cm deep and 10 cm diameter
