DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Size :               Moderately large,  exhibits sexual dimorphism in size, females attains maximum of 39 cm than males (<20 cm ). 

 Body :             Head moderately long, snout pointed, slightly upturned, shorter than the orbit and projecting  much beyond the lower  jaw. Skin of the posterior portion of the head is divided into shields.  Upper jaw is feebly bicuspid in the adult individuals.  Limbs are with fully and broad webbed digits and have narrow, transeversly enlarged scales. 

 Limbs :            Forelimbs are provided  with five claws. 

 Tail :               Tail remain short throughout life and exhibits no difference between juveniles and adults. 

 Carapace :      Depressed, unicarinate, the keel is interrupted and remains most prominent upon the second and third vertebral shields, where it terminates as a knob like structure in juveniles, while it disappears in the adult. Posterior margin of the carapace is crenulated in the young ones.  Plastron is long and narrow, considerably narrower than the opening of the shell, angulate laterally in the young, truncate anteriorly, openly emarginated or notched posteriorly.

 Colouration :  General dorsal colouration is  olivaceous or brownish. Ventrum yellowish,  Neck light brown with seven  reddish longitudinal  stripes.  Lateral aspects of head bluish, throat with a pair of oblong red or yellow markings. Top of the head in male has a brilliant red colour during breeding season. 

 

Breeding system:

Nesting :         Eggs are laid on the sandy banks of Ganga during March  month.

Miscellaneous Information  Threat 

The population is towards decline in recent years on account of large scale exploitation for food on account of habitat destruction and changes in the ecology caused by  various dams and reservoirs.   Nesting areas on the banks of the River Ganga and other rivers are destroyed by the removal of sand and earth for commercial purposes.  Loss of macrophytic vegetation along the banks of rivers has caused scarcity of food for turtles and gradually cause decline in the number due to starvation
