Other Biological Details  Threat : Ruthless exploitation for protein rich flesh and eggs.  Human predation and urbanization are the main cause of worry

HABIT & HABITAT

                     Slow, docile and considerably large tortoise prefer to live in hilly forested areas where the water facility is abundant.  Herbivorous, lives on vegetable matter, occasionally feeds on invertebrates and small amphibians.

Conservation status:                         Extremely rare and threatened .  Listed in Appendix – II of  CITES

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 Size :               Large terrestrial species. Largest of Asiatic Tortoises, reaching  slightly over 47 cm in length and weighs 31 kg. or more.  Length of the shell 470, bredth 340, and depth 195 mm. 

 Body :             The head is comparatively massive, upper jaw is feebly hooked. Top of the head is covered by large symmetrical shields like frontal and two pairs of shields anterior to it signify the most constant character. 

 Limbs :            Fore limbs with five claws, their front is having pointed, bony, imbricate scales, forming four or five longitudinal series.  Hind limbs are four clawed, with large, pointed scales on the sole and heel, adults have a greately enlarged  conical scute on the back of the thigh and this is why it is known as six footed tortoise. 

 Tail :               Tail is moderate and terminates in a grooved spur like tubercle.

 Carapace :   Convex or dome shaped, depressed on the vertebral region in juveniles but slightly elevated in adults, anterior and posterior margins are flared and serrated, nuchal shields are small to moderate. Vertebrals five, costals four and marginals 25. Plastron large, sometimes larger than the carapace. 

 Colour :              Shell dark brown or blackish, Head and limbs blackish

 

Breeding system:

.Nesting :         Not much is known on the breeding behaviour. Lays 5 – 8 eggs in a single clutch.
