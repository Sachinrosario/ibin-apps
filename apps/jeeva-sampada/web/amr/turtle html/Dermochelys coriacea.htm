Other Biological Details

Economic importance : Flesh of this is not suitable for human consumption, however, its eggs are considered delicacy.  The oil extracted is used for caulking boats in South American Islands.  Skin also have good value.

HABIT & HABITAT

:   A pelagic species depending on land for nesting.  A powerful swimmer with a strong instinct for migration and prefer to stay in open seas. Longitudinal ridges of the carapace is stream lined, boat shaped provides extraordinary capacity to swim and migrate with great speed.  Most secretive turtle rarely seen except during nesting.   Major food consists of  jelly fishes ( Stomolophus meleagris, Catostylus mosaicus, Cyanea capillata, Rhizostoma melagris etc., ) ,  tunicates.  Speciality of this species is that it solely depends on jelly fishes.

Distribution:

Circumglobal species, nesting takes place on the beaches of Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans.   Feeding in the warm tropical water and therefore, migrates considerable.    In India nesting is reported from Kerala, Goa, several parts of east coast, Andaman and Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands.  Usual nesting site is detected in Great Nicobar and Little Andamans.

 

Extent of Occurrence

Circumglobal species, nesting takes place on the beaches of Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans. 

Area of occurrence:  In India nesting is reported from Kerala, Goa, several parts of east coast, Andaman and Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands.  Usual nesting site is detected in Great Nicobar and Little Andamans.

Altitude:  Does not arise

Population   Circum global and hence global number not available

Conservation status:

Major threat for the eggs are the predators such as crabs pigs, monitor lizards, small mammals.  Sharks devour adults.   Habitat destruction is the major threat for egg laying.  Plastic wastes along the shallow areas of the sea has become a great threat, as these turtles confuse for jelly fishes and swallow them, clogging oesophagus and intestine.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

  Size     :              Largest and heaviest of all living turtle and tortoises of the world.  Body size varies between 150 – 220 cm, average weight is 400 kg and maximum recorded is 725 kg.

 Body   :           Body is triangular, resembling that of a musical instrument ‘lute’ as such its name is Luth comes after lute only.   Youngones have scaled and their complete body  and limbs are covered small irregular polygonal shields which are largest up on carapace and plastron.   Scales are replaced by smooth skin in fully grown adults. The flesh and skin of the leatherback are extremely oily and the species is devoid of all cornified epidermal structures.  The jaws are devoid of  flattened horny plates.  The lower jaw bears a median hook and the upper jaw bears a pair of strong cusps flanked by three notches.  Head is large, neck is massive.

Limbs :            Limbs are paddle shaped and clawless, front flippers are triangular, hind flippers are broad and connected to tail fold by a thick skin. 

Tail :               Tail short.

 Carapace :      In adults, carapace is smooth skinned and rubber like and is made up of seven strong nodular longitudinal ridges which provide considerable rigidity. No sharp angle between carapace and plastron.  In young 7 dorsal and 5 ventral keels are available, the 5 ventrals obliterate in  he adult, as such the ventral plastral surface becomes absolutely smooth. The 7 dorsal keel is replaced by 7 strong ridges in the adult.

Colouration  :  Carapace is slaty black with white spots, ventral colouration is variable  and  generally a mixture of pink, white and black.  Mature females, the crown of the head has a  pink spot. Tail is black.

Breeding system:

Nesting takes place during night between March to July in North Atlantic and between October to February in the countries of the East of  Pacific.   Deposits eggs in shallow nest pits of 60 – 100 cm deep.   An adult female nests as nine times in a season at intervals of 10 days. Remigration takes place at an interval of  2 – 3 years.  Clutch size- 80 – 85 eggs. Incubation period 56 – 70 days. Hatching rate is 63 – 76 percent. Optimal incubation temperature for this species is around 29 degree Celsius. Sex determination is male biased in cool temperatures and vice versa. Hatchlings are tiny ranges from 5 – 6 cm and 40 – 50 grams.
