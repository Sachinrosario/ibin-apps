Other Biological Details      Threat : Possibly vulnerable on account of habitat destruction and large scale exploitation of eggs and adult for food

HABIT & HABITAT

                       Prefer to live in ponds and occasionally bask on stones and logs projecting form the water.  They are equally comfortable on land also and sometimes are found far away from water.  Herbivorous.

Back to Top

 

Distribution:         

:    Circumglobal species, nesting takes place on the beaches of Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans.   Feeding in the warm tropical water and therefore, migrates considerable.    In India nesting is reported from Kerala, Goa, several parts of east coast, Andaman and Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands.  Usual nesting site is detected in Great Nicobar and Little Andamans.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

Size     :           Largest and heaviest of all living turtle and tortoises of the world.  Body size varies between 150 – 220 cm, average weight is 400 kg and maximum recorded is 725 kg.

Body   :               Body is triangular, resembling that of a musical instrument ‘lute’ as such its name is Luth comes after lute only.   Youngones have scaled and their complete body  and limbs are covered small irregular polygonal shields which are largest up on carapace and plastron.   Scales are replaced by smooth skin in fully grown adults. The flesh and skin of the leatherback are extremely oily and the species is devoid of all cornified epidermal structures.  The jaws are devoid of  flattened horny plates.  The lower jaw bears a median hook and the upper jaw bears a pair of strong cusps flanked by three notches.  Head is large, neck is massive.

Limbs :            Limbs are paddle shaped and clawless, front flippers are triangular, hind flippers are broad and connected to tail fold by a thick skin. 

Tail :               Tail short.

Carapace :      In adults, carapace is smooth skinned and rubber like and is made up of seven strong nodular longitudinal ridges which provide considerable rigidity. No sharp angle between carapace and plastron.  In young 7 dorsal and 5 ventral keels are available, the 5 ventrals obliterate in  he adult, as such the ventral plastral surface becomes absolutely smooth. The 7 dorsal keels is replaced by 7 strong ridges in the adult.

Colouration:  Carapace is slate black with white spots, ventral colouration is variable and generally a mixture of pink, white and black.  Mature females, the crown of the head has a pink spot. Tail is black.

Breeding system:

Nesting:          Nesting takes place during night between March to July in North Atlantic and between October to February in the countries of the East of Pacific.   Deposits eggs in shallow nest pits of 60 – 100 cm deep.   An adult female nests as nine times in a season at intervals of 10 days. Remigration takes place at an interval of  2 – 3 years.  Clutch size- 80 – 85 eggs. Incubation period 56 – 70 days. Hatching rate is 63 – 76 percent.
