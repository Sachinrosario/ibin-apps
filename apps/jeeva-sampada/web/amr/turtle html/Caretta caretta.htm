Taxonomic Details

Scientific Name :           Caretta caretta (Linnaeus, 1758) 


Family :                                Chelonidae Gray 1825

Classification:                Order : Testudinidae

                            Suborder : Cryptodira

                            Family : Chelonidae

                            Genus : Caretta Rafinsque 1814


Synonyms:


Common Names:                       Loggerhead Sea Turtle

Other Biological Details      Threat : Loss of nesting habitat due to urbanisation of coastal areas, ,electrical lights attract them towards interior road and thus cause deaths due to traffic accidents as well as descication.  Ocassionally caught in the trawlers net

HABIT & HABITAT

                     Adult carnivorous, feeding on benthic invertebrates such as molluscs, crustaceans, sponges, crabs, fish, sea urchin and star fishes.

Back to Top

 

Distribution:         Cosmopolitan species. Temperate and tropical oceans of the world.  Nesting in sandy beaches.

                                            Nesting throughout Indian coasts. In Indian Ocean nesting sites are recorded from Mozambique, Tongaland, Coast of  South Pakistan, 

 

Extent of Occurrence              Cosmopolitan species.    

Area of occurrence:                     Nesting throughout Indian coasts.  

Altitude:                                           Does not arise as it is marine and pelagic 

Population:                                     

Conservation status:               Extremeley vulnerable for hunting pressure, hunted for eggs and meat.  Included in Appendix-I of CITES    

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 Size :               Large Sea turtle, reaches 30cm  in length. Largest record of 2.7 m., weighing 385 Kg. IUCN Red Data Book - 114 Cm, weighing 91 - 152 kg. 

Body :              Pronounced beak and large brown eyes, head covered with symmetrical shields, two pairs of prefrontals, dorsal keel present in young ones which becomes inconspecious in adults

Colouration :  Brown or reddish brown, carapace brownish red with large spots, eyes brown, flippers grey brown.

Carapace :   serrated Posteriorly, Costal scutes - 5, Neural or Central scutes - 5, Marginal Shields -27

Limbs/Flippers :  Two claws in each flipper

Tail : Short

 

Breeding system:

.Nesting :         Nesting usually during night May - January, Clutch of 60 - 200 eggs laid in 12 - 56 cm deep sand sites.  Incubation 55 - 60 days. Female lays 4-5 times in an year. Maturity 10 - 15 years.  Males never leave water, while females visit nesting sites for egg laying.
