Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Fully aquatic, inhabits freshwater bodies with full of macrophytic vegetation.  Less active and poor swimmer. Feeds mainly on vegetable matter (Herbivorous ).

Back to Top

 

Distribution:

Ganga, Brahmaputra and Sind Rivers of India. Also found in Bangladesh and Pakistan

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Size :              Small, females reach not more than 23 cm, males are still smaller

 Body :             Head is moderately small, pointed, shorter than the orbit, skin of the posterior part of the head is divided into large shields. Skull with a bony temporal arch.  Upper jaw is not notched.  

 Limbs :            Limbs are fully and broadly  webbed digits and have transversely enlarged scales. 

 Tail :               Tails remain short throughout its life and exhibits no difference between juvenile and adult. 

 Carapace :         Elevated and round in the adult individuals.  Vertebral keel is prominent, abruptly transforms into a strong spinous process at the posterior margin of the third shield.  Posterior margin of the carapace is not much serrated.  Plastron is as long as carapace, angulated laterally, truncate anteriorly; hind plastral love is smaller than the shell opening, notched posteriorly, its length is slightly less or almost equal to the width of the plastron bridge.

 Colouration :  Adults exhibit  dark olive brown colouration and spots become inconspecious.  The rim of the shell remain pinkish yellow throughout the life.  Ventrum is reddish, yellowish or reddish yellow with large dark brown or black spots.  Head is blackish, temporal region orange or yellow, neck is blackish with thin yellow longitudinal lines.  Limbs dark olive with yellow spots, back of thighs with dark transverse streaks. 

 

 

Breeding system:

Nesting :         Not much is known about its breeding habits. Eggs laid are round or oval in  shape and in the same clutch.

Miscellaneous Information      Threat

Nesting sites are excavated for sand mining. Removal aquatic vegetation along the river beds caused extensive damage to its foraging sites. considerably reduced in number in recent years, owing to mass exploitation for food and habitat destruction.  Exploited for food.  Consruction of Hydro-electric projects along the river course is responsible for the ecological imbalance in their feeding and breeding area.
