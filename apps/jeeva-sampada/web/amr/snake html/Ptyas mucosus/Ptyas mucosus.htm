Classification:               Genus : Ptyas Fitzinger 1843


Synonyms:                           


Common Names:               Dhaman, Indian Rat snake

Other Biological Details  

HABIT & HABITAT                       This is most active, diurnal snake, is available in varied habitats like plains, agricultural fields, grasslands, trees and hollows in them under dead wooden logs, under stones, crevices in rocks, old inhabited houses. 

Food :  Its food comprises small mammals, birds, reptiles, eggs of the birds, and amphibians. 

Back to Top

 

Distribution:      Whole of India and Andaman Islands.

 

  Altitude:  
                          

Conservation status:       Fairly common but is subject to the commercial exploitation on account of its large scale killing for skin.

 

              

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

Length : Body is slender, cylindrical, tapering with a long head which is quite distinct from the neck; total body length reaches up to 2800 mm. in males and 2300 mm. in females; 

Body : Snout obtusely pointed.    Hemipenis extends to the 10th, 11th or 12th caudal plate, not forked, distal one-third is flounced, the fold at the tip are much finer than which are available downwards, this part is followed by the area where the flounces are with much thicken walls and joined together in part to form calyces; this thicker walled area is followed abruptly by a spine-bearing zone, the spines are thick and fleshy and terminate in to a spicule; the hemipenal spines are 11 or 12 in a lateral series; at the base of the hemipenis there are two very large spines; the distal one half or one-third of the organ is incompletely divided into two parts by the invaginations of the external wall of the organ (the intimate connection between both the parts is effected by a connective tissue) are firmly united with the sulcus.

Scales :   Body scales in 10 or 20 or 21 (at neck): 16 or 17 (at middle of body): 14 or 15 at tail just anterior to vent; all scales with a pair of apical pits; scales on the anterior portion of body are smooth; posterodorsal scales of the body are feebly keeled; ventral shields are 190-213; subcaudal shields 100-146; anal shields are two;

Colouration : Dorsal colouration is olive-green, brown, yellowish, grey, dull tan, dark olive brown, many posterior scales are with black tips and edges, other anterior scales are with yellow or light tan edges; labials, and in many individual other lateral head shields are markedly edged with black; top of the head is devoid of the spots; ventrum is creemish or turbid white, free edges of the ventrals and subcaudals are blackish. In juveniles these markings are more conspicuous. 

Breeding Biology : The breeding, season starts in April which extends up to September.
