Taxonomic Details

Genus . Pelamis Daudin 1803

The monotypic genus includes strongly pelagic species distributed widely in Indian and Pacific oceans. In these snakes maxillary bone never extend anteriorly beyond the palatine bone; poison fangs are followed after an interval of 7-11 teeth; head shields are entire; nostrils are in a superior position; nasal shields are in contact with one another; body is short, very stout, covered with squarish or hexagonal, juxtaposed scales, 49-67  round the thickest portion of the body; ventral shields are very small, divided by a median longitudinal furrow, in many individuals ventrals can not be distinguished from the adjoining body scales.

Other Biological Details  

HABIT & HABITAT                   Pelagic, swift swimmers; most docile and viviparous.   

Distribution:      Complete coastal line of India.

  Altitude:  
                          

Conservation status:       Common throughout the range

 

              

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

Length : The length of this sea snake never exceeds 800 mm. in males and 970 mm. in females.

Body : The body is much compressed, the maximum diameter posteriorly is generally more than two times that of the neck; head is narrow and snout is elongated.  Hemipenis is forked near the tip, it is having spines every where except near the base of the penis, where there are longitudinal folds.

Scales :   49-67 scale-rows on the thickest portion of the body, the scales are hexagonal, or quadrangular in shape, the lowermost rows with two or three small tubercles, which are strongest in grown up males; ventral shields are from 264-406, generally divided by a median longitudinal furrow or broken up in to minute scales, which can not be distinguished from the other body scales; preanal shields are moderately enlarged;

Colouration :   Dorsal colouration is extremely variable, this may be black or lemon yellow, with black dorsal stripe; ventrum, top and side of the head are of the same colouration; chin is pigmented with dark; tail is with black and white bars or vermiculation.

Breeding Biology : viviparous.
