Other Biological Details  

HABIT & HABITAT         This snake is docile, gentle, strongly borrowing, sluggish and nocturnal in habits and prefers to live in flat arid areas with loose clay soil with sparse grass; it is not common in extreme sandy areas, and is rare or absent in rocky and damp places. 

Food :  Its food comprises small mammals, birds and insects and the prey is killed by constriction.              

Back to Top

 

Distribution:        Gujarat : Junagarh, Bhavnagar, Kachchha, Dangs, Vadodara, Panchmahal and Gandhinagar districts; Recorded from Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh in India.

 

  Altitude:  
                          

Conservation status:       Common snake throughout its range

 

              

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Length : The body is robust, cylindrical which tapers a little just anterior to vent, total length not exceeding 1080 mm in females and 980 mm in males; 

Head :  Head not distinct from the neck, snout broad, wedge-shaped. Hemipenis is almost as available in conicus, but the bifurcation of the sulcus is far then back, and the calyces is more distinct.

Tail :   Tail ending into a large blunt shield; 

Eyes :    Eyes are distinct. 

Scales :   Dorsal scales are smooth or feebly keeled on the anterior but are heavily keeled in the posterior region; scale rows at the middle of body are 51-67; ventral scales, In males 193-212, in females 198-215; anal shield is small and undivided, boarded by a small plate on either side; sub caudals in males 28-40, in females 29-34; 

Colouration : Dorsal colouration is sandy, grayish, yellowish or bronzy, the scales edged with dark brown in many individuals; many snakes are almost uniformly  coffee coloured to dark brown with slight pale mottling on the flanks and with indistinct and light coloured tail rings; many snakes are with uniform or with more or less distinctly visible darker transverse bands, which are more distinct on the tail region; ventrum is whitish, spotted with dark brown or almost entirely brown. 

Breeding Biology :    The breeding season starts in June and extends up to September, it is a viviparous and 5 to 14 young ones are produced.
