MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Carapace width, 20-40 mm in early juvenile and 41-80 mm in advanced juvenile stages. The former group develops with all adult characters and have pelagic life, while the later group possesses specially well developed chelate legs and lead benthic life.In adult body devisible into cephalothorax and abdomen. Cephalothorax much broader than long and composed of five pars of head segments and eight pairs of thoracic segments. Both the cephalothorax and abdomen completely covered by a hard chitinous partly calacified carapace. Anterior three pairs of thoracic appendages modifed into maxillipeds but retained with original biramous characters, and posterior five pairs enlarged and formed legs. Legs deviod of exopodites.First pair of legs large, sout and strong, and bear two powerful chelae for grasping food and servigng as offence and defence organs. Outer margin of carpus of chelipeds bears two sharp spines. Second to fourth pairs of legs normal in size and end in simple claws used for walking, while the fifth pair paddle-like and adapted for swimming. Head bears one pair each of antennule and antenna, and stalk-eye placed within the sockets of the carapace. Third maxillipeds broad, flat and valve-like. The abdomen, in male, narrow and possesses only two pairs of reduced pleopods which modified in the form of copulatory organs. In female, the abdomen broad and bears four pairs of relatively larger pleopods for attachment of eggs. Uropods and tail absent. Dorsal aspects of both cephalothorax and abdomen appear a complete coverage with the carapace. Nine sharp acuminate teeth of equal size present at either side of the antero-lateral margin of the carapace. Carapace light to dark green in colour.
