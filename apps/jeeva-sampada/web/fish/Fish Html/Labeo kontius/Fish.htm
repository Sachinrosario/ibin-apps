MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate, its dorsal profile more convex than ventral, with a characteristic distinct hump at base of dorsal fin but the profile before eyes rather concave. Head fairly small; snout very conspicuous and thick, overhangs mouth, with a short fleshy lateral prolongation, studded with pores. Eyes moderate, not visible from underside of head, the diameter 4.7 to 5 .

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:L. kontius is heterosexual and the sexual dimorphism is identifiable only during maturing and breeding season when their secondary sexual characters are developed. In male, the pectoral fins are relatively longer reaching 9th lateral line scale when extended backward; whereas it reaches to 7th or 8th scale in female. The roughness on the pectoral fins appears in male when mature. The pectoral fins in female are smooth in touch. The distended abdomen and swollen extrovert pinkish vent are other characters from ripe female, while the oozing milt on slight pressure on the abdomen and slit-like introvert vent are distinguishing feature of the mature male.

 

Breeding season:. Like other Indian major carps L. kontius breeds once in a year in the nature during rainy months inloticenvironment. They do not breed in pond. The spawning period is June-August (Alikunhi and Rao, 195Ib;Alikunhi, 1957) 9r June-September(Hora and Pillay, 1962). The monsoon flood and the associated factors are mainly responsible for natural spawning. In the Cauvery river, the breeding was recorded all the hours of the day and night in water with pH ranging from 6.6 to 7.1 and temperature from 22.3 to 24.2°C (Alikunhi and Rao, 1951 b).

BIOLOGY:L. kontius is a medium sized carp and is said to be a major fish of South India. The name 'Pig-mouth carp' for kontius perhaps originates from the Tamil words 'Pannivayi Kendai' which has meaning of pig-mouth, and it speaks at the upper reaches of the Bhabani river. Being very active and hardy they are found to be jumping up in the air very often in their attempt to migrate the cascade-like steps and barriers in the rivers and move upstreams. In natural habitat they can withstand wide ranges in the conditions of existance. While citing an example, Alikunhi and Rao (1951b) stated that fingerlings and adults of the pigmouth carp abound in the black, malodorous water surcharged with sulphurated hydrogen issuing forth from the slucies of the Mettur Dam and also from the tail race of the Mettur Power House during period of summer stagnation in the reservoir, are found to actively feeding on algal growth and diatoms in the area and none faced the ~orse for the presence of the poisonous gas, But fingerlings can not tolerate confinement and thus fetched mortality during conditioning and transport.

Economic Importance

Uses: This carp is indigenous to the Cauvery river system and yields a valuable fishery ranging from 5% to 50% of the catches in the various fishing centres along the stretches of the river.Known to be a hardy fish, able to withstand polluted waters.

Miscellaneous Information

 In the Tangore area this species forms an important item of the tank fisheries, in most cases the fry and fingerlings getting naturally stocked during rainy months when the tanks get connected with the irrigation canals leading from the river. Breeding takes place in June-July. It attains a length of 23-31 cm in the first year and a weight of about 3 kg; maximum length attained is 61 cm. This carp is cultivated in ponds in South India but does not breed in them.
