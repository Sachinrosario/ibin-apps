HABIT & HABITAT: Warm  Water


Distribution :Cauvery, Krishna, Godavari river system in S.India and freshwaters of Orissa, M.P., U.P., Bihar, W.B. & Assam.

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate, its dorsal profile more convex than the ventral. Snout slightly projecting beyond mouth, often studded with pores. Eyes large, not visible from underside of head, the diameter 4 to 4.3 times in head. Mouth inferior; lips thin, lower lip slightly fringed and folded back and joined to isthmus by a narrow bridge; a small tubercle above mandibular .

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:L. bata is heterosexual. Like major carps, the male and the female sex can be differentiated by the aid of the following morphological characters: (i) The pectoral fin when extended backwards and towards the dorsal side of the body reaches the 8th lateral line scale in male and the 7th scale in female (Chaudhuri, 1959); (ii) The rough dorsal surface of the pectoral fin and the oozing milt by slight pressure on the abdomen in mature male during the breeding season are the secondary sexual key characters for easy segregation from the female having smooth and slippery pectoral fins and soft bulging abdomen with fleshy pinkish extrovert vent.

 

Breeding season: L. bata spawns once in a year in the nature. Prolific breeding is seen in the rivers, reservoirs, 'bundhs' and 'nallahs' in lacustrine conditions. Spawning season of bata is of a short duration and is over during the. early part of the monsoon season. Spawning coincides with the rains break during May in the north-eastern region of India and in Bangladesh, and during July-August in Punjab and in the northern India where monsoon starts late. Spawning normally occurs during the early hours of the morning, in shallow pockets adjacent to rivers and reservoirs, which when get innundated with the monsoon flood the breeders perform the breeding migration for spawning. After spawning the swell-up fertilised eggs gradually settle down on the bottom. Eggs are hatched out within 14-24 hours depending upon the temperature of the water. The eggs and spawn are drifted down to the main body of the water with the receed of the water level, and the seed is collected from the river by spawn collection net.

Breeding techinique: Bata do not breed in small confined waters, natural breeding may occur in larger tanks under the influence of heavy run-off fresh water in rush during spawning season. However, breeding can be taken in confinement also by hypophysis injection following the same technique as practised in the case of major carps. The first success of induced breeding of bata by pituitary hormone treatment was made at Killa Fish Farm in 1957 (Alikunhi, 1957) by two doses to female and one dose to males. The doses of pituitary gland are relatively smaller than those given in rohu and mrigal. Bata could be bred nicely in the dry-bundh under the influence of PG injection in premonsoon period by creating an artificial flow of water for the first time, and the same breeders could be used again for the second time within the same spawning season during monsoon (July-August) when pregnated again.

 

Fishing methods:Gillnet, driftnet, purse or clapnet, dragnet, fixed net, castnet, scoop or dip or handnet, fish traps, hooks and lines, and fish spear are normally employed for fishing at different depths of various types of water sheets.



BIOLOGY:Bata is a freshwater medium sized Indian minor carp, normally attains a length of 20-25 cm in pond, and 40-60 cm in large tanks and reservoirs. Compared to major carps L. bata is a slow-growing fish. It has herbivorous feeding habit and high fecund. It has nice domestic nature. Fry and fingerlings readily accept and palate powdered rice bran, oil cakes, etc. as artificial food. It is not so hardy in nature as the major carps are. It dies earlier than the major carps with smaller ecological changes and pesticide action. It can not tolerate the salinity and stand the long distance transportation even under oxygen packing for a longer period. It is highly suceptible to many protozoan infection and other diseases.

Continuous embryonic fin-fold extends anteriorly to the same level on the dorsal and ventral aspects of the body. Larva, about 18 hours after hatching (Fig. 12 b). Chromatophores not yet appeared. Yolk-sac fairly conspicuous and tapers posteriorly. Eyes transparent with a ventro-median dark spot. Pectoral fin-buds flat, flap-like and functional. Snout blunt, prominent. Buccal invagination distinct. Lower jaw not fully differentiated. 2 concretions in each auditory vesicle. 24 preanal and 10 postanal myotomes. Hatchlings inactive, occassionally swim up and immediately drop down to settle. Larva, about 42 hours after hatching (Fig. 12c). Total length, 5.400-5.955 mm; post anal length, 1.710-1.860 mm; maximum height of body, 0.855-0.990 rom; height of body at anal level, 0.630-0.720 mm; and diameter of eye, 0.330 x 0.315 mm and 0.345 x 0.330 mm (Alikunhi, 1956). Chromatophores appeared in a number on the dorsum of the head and on the dorsal aspect of the air-bladder, and one in each myotome forming a faint line along the lateral line, and several along the ventral aspect of the body behind the level of the vent. Mouth better differentiated and shifted, being almost terminal in position. Alimentary canal straight, tube-like and functional. Yolk almost completely absorbed, except for a small anterior mass. Eyes fully pigmented, dark. Air-bladder distinct, oblong and dorsal to foregut. Rudimentary gill filaments visible beneath the operculum. Tip of notochord straight. Postlarva, about 66 hours after hatching. More chromatophores appeared on the body. Yolk completely absorbed. Anterior portion of dorsal embryonic fin-fold broadened indicating the position of dorsal fin. Tip of notochord upturned little. Caudal fin not yet differentiated, rudiments of 3-4 rays just visible. Air-bladder becomes larger. Postlarva, about 4-day-old (Fig. 12d). Alimentary canal coiled anteriorly. Dorsal fin rudiment visible but without fin-rays. Caudal fin still rounded with 15-16 rudimentary rays. Tip of notochord distinctly upturned. Anal fin rudiment appearing. Postlarva, about 5-day-old (Fig. 12e). Dorsal, anal and caudal fins fully differentiated with 2/10 and 2/5 rays respectively in the former two. Ventral fairly well differentiated in larger specimens.

Miscellaneous Information

 This minor carp is cultivated along with the Indian major carps in India.It does not breed normally in ponds. Medium-sized carps attain a maximum length of 61 cm. It breed in rivers during monsoon months. Induced breeding successful.
