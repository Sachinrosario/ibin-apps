MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate and cylindrical, its dorsal profile more convex than ventral profile. Snout overhanging mouth, with a distinct lateral lobe on each side, studded with pores. Eyes large, not visible from underside of head, the diameter 3.5 to 4.5 times in head. Mouth rather small; lips fimbriated and continuous.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY: L. angra has sexual dimorphism, and male and female are identifiable only during maturing and spawning seasons when their secondary sexual characters are developed. The mature males can be selected by the help of oozing milt (by slight pressure on the abdomen), roughness of the pectoral fins, sandy texture on the dorsum, and the introvert slit-like vent; and the females are segregated by soft buldging abdomen, extrovert swollen genital apperture and smooth pectoral fin.

 

Breeding season: L. angra, an annual breeder, breeds during monsoon months (June-August in India) in rivers, canals, and in the large sheet of waters with some lacustrine influence. They do not breed in stagnant ponds, although attain full maturity in the lentic conditions. The riverine floods and the associated factors are responsible for natural breeding. 4.3 Age and Growth L. angra is a slow growing fish attaining about 160 mm in the first year. Qureshi and Qureshi (1983) has, however, recorded the maximum growth as 150 mm.
