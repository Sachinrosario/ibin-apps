HABIT & HABITAT:  Warm  Water


Distribution :Freshwaters of E.Punjab & Haryana, Gangetic estuaries, Bihar, W.B.,Dargeeling and Indo-Gangetic Basin.

Back to Top

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES:Body elongate and compressed, its depth 4 to 6 times in standard length. Mouth wide and terminal, obliquely directed upwards. Barbels two pairs; maxillary pair minute, lie in grooves and are liable to be overlooked; the mandibular barbels vestigial and embedded in the skin. Dorsal fin placed considerably (but not wholly) in advance of pelvic fins.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:Silond is bisexual. Males and females are identifiable morphologically when secondary sexual characters are appeared during breeding season. The mature males ooze milt by a slight pressure on the abdomen near the slit-like vent and females show bulging abdomen with wide genital opening.

 

Breeding season:S. silondia is a seasonal breeder spawning during the rainy season and probably in the freshwater zone of the river. For breeding purpose, the silond ascends the river when spades after the rains and becomes stranded in large pools. In the river Ganga it breeds in the upper freshwater stretches during the south-west monsoon months (June-August) and their fingerlings of the size of 75-200 mm are commonly available at Nawabgunge (about 35 km above Calcutta) in the HoOghly estuary in a fairly good number during October-December when they descend (Hora, 1938).

Fishing methods:Gillnet gives better fishing, although shore seines, encircling nets, etc. are also effectively used. Rod and lines with spoon or fish-bait frequently and successfully used in the stronger streams and clear deep waters. It is also caught on a mahseer fly.

 



BIOLOGY:The word 'Silond' has been derived from the Sanskrit names of the fish 'Silendhra' or 'Silindha' (Hora, 1938). The so-called shark of the Ganges silond is considered as good for eating and fetched certainly better priced. The gigantic size (1.5-2.0 m) of the fish with powerfully build body, speedy movement and voracious carnivorous feeding habits perhaps make them to call as 'freshwater shark'. They have developed large canniform teeth in both the jaws for highly predaceous habits. Silond has a large hag-like stomach, about twice as long as broad (c28 cm x 14 cm in a 125 cm fish) and slightly convoluted alimentary canal. The fish length-gut length ratio (RLG) of a 9.3 cm fingerling is 0.86 and that of a 125 cm adult is 1.46 (Hora, 1938). Silond possesses a small and greatly reduced air-bladder with two oval chambers connecting through a narrow canal, and its both the sides are osseus in young specimens (Day, 1889). The urophysial gland of the caudal neurosecretory system is simple having a single fold of the central canal. It has a few outgrowths, eventually to their origin from ependymal wall of the central canal (Srivastava and Srivastava, 1979). Silond is hardy in nature and can tolerate a high range of temperature (7-400C) and moderate range of salinity (up to 14 ppt). Silond is recorded as game fish for giving real fights and providing strong sports to anglers. Silond is susceptible to helminth and trematod infections. Prosorhynchoids magnum, a helminth parasite, is commonly encountered in the intestine. Srivastava (1981) estimated this infection in 43.89% of specimens examined and observed seasonal fluctuation in the diseased population of silond of the Ganga and Jamuna. The frequent occurrence of the monogenetic and digenetic trematod parasites in the digestive tract and on the gills have also been reported.

Economic Importance

Uses: It is very common in the Gangetic estuaries and a very common foodfish of West Bengal where its flesh is prized. This species commonly attains a size of 100 cm in length, but occasionally to twice that size. It causes considerable damage to fisheries being a voracious feeder, but it, however, provides considerable sport to anglers and is regarded a game-fish .

Miscellaneous Information

This catfish is primarily a fluvatile fish, but flourishes in tanks and large reservoirs as well. This is a gregarious catfish moving in shoals and breeding during the monsoon months. During the monsoons the adults ascend from the estuaries into large rivers for breeding but after the water level recedes, this fish gets stranded in small pools.
