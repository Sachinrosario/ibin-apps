REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:L.boggut is a heterosexual fish with well marked sexual dimorphism in the snout characters of the mature male and female (Misra, 1950), as shown in Fig.13 and in the descriptions given below: Characters - Female Interorbital groove on the Transverse, very deep and more pronounced Transverse and very shallow. head. between the nostrils. Protruberences near nostril. Two, very small, one on each side of nostril. Absent. Tubercles on the snout. Well developed cornified epidermal tubercles. Poorly developed tubercles, represent"pearl organs" on the dorso-lateral surface of sented by small open pores. the snout. The other secondary sexual characters as roughness on the pectoral fins and oozing of milt in male; and soft bulging abdomen with extrovert pink colour vent in female are also observed during breeding season. The morphometric and meristic characters between the two sex are not much dependable for distinguishing male and female.

 

Breeding season: L boggut is an annual breeder with a short breeding season during monsoon. Breeding performs fully in a single burst. The spawning season in different localities has been referred as May-June in the flooded river of Mahanadi in Orissa (Job et al., 1955), July-October during flood season in Madras (Madras Fish. Dept. Report 1954-55), and June-July in flooded drainage and irrigation canals of Panna of Madhya Pradesh (Selvaraj et al., 1971, 1972). The breeding requirements of bog gut are similar to those of Indian minor, medium and major carps. The flood and the associated factors are responsible for natural breeding. The spawning grounds are normally small and are seen in the shallower inundations of the monsoon flooded rivers and connecting channels. Spawning normally effected in the early and late morning hours.

BIOLOGY:L. boggut is a slow growing herbivorous bottom-dwelling freshwater cyprinid with high fecund. The ripe ova of L. bog gut are smaller than those of catla, rohu, mrigal, grass carp and silver carp but are slightly larger than those of L. bata, L. boga, C. rebaand L. goniu.;. The fertilised and fully swollen eggs of L. boggut are, however, within the size range of the major and medium sized carps and larger than those of C. reba (Chaudhuri,1960). The hatchlings and the postlarvae are slightly larger than those of C. reba and P. sarana (Selvaraj et al., 1972).
