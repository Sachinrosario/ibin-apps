Distribution : Throughout India, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal..

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate and fairly rounded in cross-section. Eyes moderate, its diameter 6 to 7 times in head length. Mouth large; lower jaw with 4 to 7 canines behind a single row of villiform teeth which deepens to six rows at symphysis; villiform teeth on vomer and palatines. Pectoral fin about 2 times in head length. Caudal fin rounded. Scales on summit of head large, rosette of head-scales lies between orbits and hence frontal head-scales forms the central plate of rosette; 9 scale-rows between preopercular angle and posterior border of orbit; predorsal scales 18 to 20; scales 50 to 57 in lateral series.

 

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:. C. striatus is heterosexual. Female is larger than male in size. Sexual dimorphism is conveniently distinguishable by the following secondary sexual characters developed during breeding season: Characters Make Female Abdomen Not bulging Slightly bulging Vent Pale and slit-like Round and reddish Anal papilla-like structure Prominent, with pointed tip Broad, slightly reddish, and tip blunt with a reddish dot. A case of synchronous hermaphroditism has been reported in shol, in which the ovarian and testicular portions were in the same stage of maturity (Swarup and Srivastava, 1976).

 

Breeding season:. C. striatus breeds througout the year, but intensive spawning occurs during rainy months. Alikunhi (1953, 1957) and Rahimullah (1946) have also reported that shol breeds throughout the year, with peak in the months immediately preceding and during monsoon. In north India including the Punjab,the striped murrel has a single spawning season between April and August (Khan, 1924; Bhattacharya, 1946; Mookerjee et al., 1948b). According to Qasim and Qayyum (1961) the striped murrel breeds in the river of Uttar Pradesh during June-October, with a peak in July- August. In southern and peninsular India, the double peak spawning seasons are seen in the common murrel coinciding limited breeding in other months also. In Tamil Nadu, the breeding season falls on January-February and June-July (Arumugam 1966). Jones(1946) reported intensive spawning of shol during May-June in southern Kerala. In south Canara (Kamataka) shol breeds twice a year, i.e. in June and December (Thomas, 1870). In Malnad (Karnataka) region, the breeding takes place throughout the year excepting in the colder months (November-January), but the peak spawning season appears during June-July with the peak rainfall of the south-east monsoon (Parameswaran, 1975; Parameswaran and Murugesan, I 976a).

BIOLOGY:The popular standard name for C. striatus is the common murrel. This perhaps derives for its common stay in all types of freshwater and availability in the market. Shol is popular also for its quality flesh, flavour, and rich nutritive and medicinal values, high content of protein and low quantity of fat, and rich iron content and calorific values. C. striatus is a hardy fish and can endure extreme change of temperature. It can tolerate a fair range of salinity; and withstand very low oxygen, high carbon dioxide, BOD and obnoxious gases like methene, hydrogen sulphide, etc. on account of its possession of accessory respiratory organ and modified blood physiology. Murugesan et al. (1979) reported that 49-97mm long fingerlings withstand salinity upto 5 ppton direct transfer without mortality and upto 12 ppt if the salinity increase is made in stages. Murugesan and Parameswaran (1977) pointed out the lethel levels of 00(0.56, 0.44, 0.40 ppm), CO2 (146.4, 180.6,290.4 ppm), amonical nitrogen (18.4,32.0,52.6 ppm) and the duration to withstand starvation (57,86, 98 hrs) for the fry of5.9, 9.0 and 16.3 mm size, respectively. Young fish can withstand the minimum temperature of 11°C (Bhattacharya, 1946). The striped murrel has nest building habit and deep parental care. Both the parents guard their eggs and larvae till the fry attain 57 mm size, when the parental care cease and the fry disperse (Willey, 1908, 1909, 1910; Raj, 1916; Alikunhi, 1953; Jones, 1946; Bhattacharya, 1948; Mookerjee et al., 1948b; Parameswaran and Kamal, 1988). Arumugam (1966) described the peculiar mud stiring and hibernation habit of shol. Singh and Srivastava (1979) give the structural plan of the kidney and the head kidney of C. striatus and other fishes to signify the phylogenetic importance, with the remark that the typical shape of the kidney of shol indicates its belonging in the more advanced group of Pisces and having more active movement. The structure of the swim-bladder of shol is similar to that of the giant murrel, but it is smaller in size (2.0 in the total length of the fish). Swarup and Srivastava (1975) reported the occurrence of collagenous capsules (2-40 nos. in each ground of50-320 1.1 size) during the spawning phase. These nodules originate as capsules, but with the increased fibrosis of the capsular wall the lumen gets completely obliterated or reduced to insignificance. Srivastava (1979) described two types of atresia in the immature oocytes and stated that the poor nourishment of the oocytes may be the possible cause of the atresia. The first type of atresia appeared in the early stages of the oocytes is initiated by vacuolisation of the ooplasm, simultaneous loss of nucleus and entire cytoplasm; and the second type seen in the yolk- nucleus stage of the oocytes begins with the distortion of the shape of the oocytes, followed by disintegration of cytoplasm, leaving behind finally an empty space within the follicle.

The rim gradually encroaches upon the yolk-mass. Yolk-invasion advances within 30 minutes. 5-6" Yolk-plug stage. Yolk invasion completed. Embryonic ridge destined to form the embryo marked on the dorsal surface of the gastrula. Blastopore marks the posterior end of the future embryo. c 8" Formation of embryo. c 10-11 " Blastopore reduced to form the anus. Tail region of the embryo pushed beyond the blastopore. Soon after this stage, appeared a rudiment of brain at anterior region and behind that the future spinal cord. c 12.. " Embryonic fin-fold visible. 13-17 " Heart rudiment appears. Distinct spinal chord and brain rudiment seen thereafter. Cardiac pulsation starts soon after. Eye rudiments and statocysts faintly marked within a short time. Tail region further lengthened becoming free from the yolk mass. c 18 " Eye ball rudiments as two distinctly raised sacs appeared; and statocysts, the two transparent bodies, glitter behind it. Myotomes getting prominent and increase in number on age. 19-20 " Head, eye, statocysts and tail region further developed. Brain rapidly differentiated. Pectoral fin rudiments appeared behind statocysts. Tail further elongates, becomes largely free from the yolk mass. At this stage, a peculiar tremor starts from the middle of the trunk. 21-22 " Head region gradually assumes piscine condition. Pigment deposition on the anterior end of the embryo begins. Pectoral fin bud rudiment conspicuous. Tremors in the trunk region become stronger. c 24 " Embryo hatched out by bursting of the egg shell. The tail comes out of the shell first and then the entire body. Embryo lies horizontally at the bottom. Economic Importance

Uses: A popular and highly priced fish especially when freshly caught, in West Bengal. This species is most widely distributed and economically the most important member of the genus.The flesh is firm, white, practically boneless, and of a most agreeable flavour. It is cultivated in India and Pakistan; tolerates slightly brackish water.

Miscellaneous Information

This species is fairly common in the plains of India and is the mainstay of the tank fishery in Tamil Nadu. The fish takes bait readily. Breeds almost throughout the year; the female matures at a size of about 30 cm when two years old, and lays from a few hundred to over a thousand amber- coloured eggs. It attains a length of 60 to 75 cm; common size 30 to 40 .
