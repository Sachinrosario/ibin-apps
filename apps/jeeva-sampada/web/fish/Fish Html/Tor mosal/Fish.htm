HABIT & HABITAT: Cold Water


Distribution :Hill streams and rivers of Himalayas, fairly common in Ramganga, Uttar Pradesh.

Back to Top

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES:Body fairly elongate, its depth 3.3 to 4 times in standard length and almost equal to head length. Eyes small, not visible from underside ofhead. Mouth moderate; lips fleshy and continuous at corners of mouth. Barbels two pairs; maxillary barbels equal to eye-diameter, ostral pair shorter. Gillrakers 11 to 16 on lower arm offirst arch. Scales large; lateral line with 23 to 26 scales; lateral transverse scale-ows 4/2½. Snout and cheeks devoid of tubercles.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:. Tor mosal is heterosexual. Like other mahseer, the male and female mosal is differentiated by the secondary sexual characters developed during breeding season. The shape and the size of pectoral fin, the structure of vent and the oozing milt or the roe can be read as similar to Tor tor to distinguish the sex. The body colour of T mosal  has not taken as a firm character for segregation of male from female.

 

Breeding season: Mahanadi mosal breeds after south-west monsoon season, during winter months between October and January with the peak period in the later half of November (David, 1953). An established breeding ground of T. mosal  mahanadicus could be located close to the Huma  sacnctuary where large numbers of fry are available during November-December (Job et al., 1955). Flood waters are not found suitable for breeding since the fish leaves summer habitats during the flood season. A lowering of water temperature might have a great influence upon breeding and the range of water temperature (72-83°F) of the river during winter appears to be congenial for natural spawning of this species. The breeding season of Tor mosal in Burma is prolonged, commencing from September-October to March- April with two peaks, one at September-October and the other in March.

Fishing methods:,  It is generally caught by angling. It accepts life and artificial baits, fly, spoon, etc. well like other mahseer.

 



BIOLOGY:T mosal  is a large scaled Barbel. It is a hardy and sporting fish. It is more graceful than the other mahseer. It has very powerful mouth; the power of compression excercised by the lips and jaws is so great that small fish etc. are instantly killed when preyed upon them. Like other mahseer, T mosal has also 10 teeth on each pharyngeal bone, arranged in three rowsof5.3.2 respectively. The teeth are hooked and pointed up to 3.15 kg weight of the fish; but from 4.0 kg onwards they show signs of wear and are invariably flattened and smooth on the tops. As the fish grows older this wear and flattening become more and more marked, getting suitable for crustation of hard objects, like crabs, molluscs, etc. (Hora, 1940 b).

Stages of development: The embroynic and the larval deyelopment of Tor mosalare lacking. David (1953) has, however, described a few stages of the larval forms of Mahanadi mosal, collected from the river. Post larva, 10.4 mm stage (Fig. 25 a). Body slender, gently tapering. Tubercles on the snout in 2-3 rows. Yolk-sac disappeared. Intestine tube-like, straight. Larvae actively feeding upon plankton. Dorsal fin with 1 unbranched and 9 branched rays; anal fin with 6; caudal, which is slightly forked, with 21 rays; pectoral without rays; and pelvic fins in the form of buds. Chromatophores on the dorsal aspects of the head behind the eyes continuous along the dorsal profile up to tail in a row. Another single row of pigments along the ventral profile from the tail to the pelvic. A dark spot on the caudal present. Post larva, 15.0 mm stage (Fig. 25 b). Body lengthened behind dorsal fin. Embryonic fin-fold disappeared. Tubercles on the snout much more prominent. First looping of the intestine visible. Dorsal with 3 branched and 9 unbranched rays, the third unbranched ray being thicker than the rest, which forms by ossification from 13.8-14.0 mm onwards. Pectorals with 10-' 12 rays; 7-8 rays in pelvics. Caudal deeply forked. Chromatophores increased in number on the dorsum and on the dorsal half of the body. Caudal spot becomes prominent. Fry, 23.0 mm stage (Fig. 25c). Body stout, depth behind the head proportionately greater. Tubercles on the snout absent. Lower lip continuous with a small lobe projecting backwardly. Fins with complete rays appeared. Fry, 35.0 mm stage (Fig. 25d). Body very much like that of an adult. Lobe of the lower lip much more prominent. Barbels 2 pairs, 2 maxillary and 2 rostral, both well developed. Scales appeared on the entire body. Lateral line visible.
