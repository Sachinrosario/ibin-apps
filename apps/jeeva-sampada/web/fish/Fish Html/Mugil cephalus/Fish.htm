MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES:Body robust; head broad and much flattened dorsally, its length 27 to 29% of standard length. Lips thin, lower lip with a high symphysial knob; teeth labial, one to 6 rows on upper lip, one to 4 rows on lower lip, outer rows unicuspid, inner rows usually bicuspid; hind tip of maxilla reaching to vertical from anterior border of eye. Preorbital slender.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:. M cephalus is homosexual. Male and female sexes are separate and can be distinguished only when the secondary sexual characters are developed during the breeding season. The differentiating characters for male and female are same as those of L. tade(ReferSection4.2.1 of the Chapter L. lade). In immature specimens, no extemally observable characters are known to distinguish males from females.

 

Breeding season:The natural spawning of M cephalus occurs in the offshore region of the sea. Its spawning season in different countries/localities considerably varies. The spawning season extends over the late summer, autumn and early winter months, earlier in higher latitudes but later nearer the tropics (Table 40.5). Nash and Shehadesh (1980) reviewed the breeding of cephal us mullet.

Breeding techinique: Artificial maturation and spawning of M cephalus by treatment of exogenous hormones have although become successful but commercial propagation has yet to be developed. The artificial spawning was first achieved in Italy in 1930 by stripping of oozing adults (Varghese, 1975), but significant breakthrough was acquired in Taiwan in 1964 when pituitary gland in combination of Synahorin (@ 2-3 glands+ 20 RU of Synahorin in 2 intramuscular injections at 12-16 hours intervals), but hatchlings did survive maximum upto 4 days. Rearing of hatchlings became unsuccessful till 1969- 70 when Taiwanese Biologists could succeed in raising 431 fingerlings of 190 mm/63g in June, 1970. Artificial breeding and rearing of fry of M cephalus have been intensified following various techniques during 1970s in Taiwan. Various ovulating agents were used: such as (i) homoplastic PG+Synahorin (Tang, 1964; Liao et al., 1971,1972), (ii) salmon pituitary extract+Synahorin (Shehadeh and Ellis, 1970), (iii) partially purified salmon gonadotropin (Shehadeh, 1973), (iv) extract of common carp pituitary gland (Yashov, 1969), and (v) homoplastic PG (Chaudhuri et al., 1977). Chaudhuri et al. (1977) is in opinion that no significant advantage is there in the use ofSynahorin. According to Kuo and Nash (1975) the salmon pituitary gonadotropin (SG-G 100) appears to be the best ovulating agent for reliable result of spawning out of follicle stimulating hormone (FSH), human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG), luteinized hormone (NIH-LH-S 11), pregnant mere's serum gonadotropin (PMSG), gonadal steroids (Estrone, Estradiol, Progesterone, Dehydroepiandrosterone-DHA, Pregnenatone and Testosterone) and SG-G 100. Liao (1977) stated that mullet pituitary homogenate with Synahorin (mammalian gonadotropin) are the most effective ovulating agent. Chondar (Ms) is in view that carp PG homogenate+RHLH-A are most promising ones. The effective dose of homoplastic PG is 8-16mgikg offish or 2-8 glands/fish depending upon the state of gonadal maturation of the breeders and the environments (mainly salinity and temperature). Knock-out dose has been followed in Taiwan, which gave successful ovulation within 48 hours (Ling, 1970). In India, generally two injections at an interval of 6-8 hours have been advocated following stripping in another 4-12 hours (Chaudhuri et al., 1977), though record is there for successful ovulation within 10-20 hours of the first injection. Yashouv (1969) administered three injections at an interval of7'hours to get spawning 17 hours after the third injection. At present two methods are mainly followed for artificial spawning of cephalus and other mullets: (1) stripped spawning method and (2) spontaneous spawning method. The stripped spawning method is of dry stripping and wet stripping. There is no difference in fertilisation rate of ova between the 'dry method' and 'wet method'. But in the former method fertilisation can be done any time within one hour the ova are stripped in a dry basin, while it has to be done within 5 minutes when the ova are stripped into water. The stripped males generally die. In spontaneous spawning, the same males can be used repeatedly, but the main demerits of this method are either the milt tends to become viscous and non-dispersing or the gonads regress. However, maturation in males can be induced throughout the year in a very wide range of salinities (freshwater to normal sea water) under different photoperiods (ranging from 8 to 18 h of light) by feeding/injecting/ implanting 17(1; methyl testosterone. With the treatment of 17-MT @ 12.5 mg/kg body weight daily, males are matured within three weeks (Weber and Lee, 1985; Lee and Weber, 1986), when the maturity is assessed during weeks 3, 6,8,10 and 12 after implantation. This method has now become a routine practice in mullet spawning at the Oceanic Institute in Hawii. Hormone therapy in females for advancement of vitellogenesis has so far not become successful. But constant controlled temperature at 21 DC and photoperiod of6L/18D gave best results in bringing the oocytes in refractory stage out of season (Kuo  and Nash, 1975).

BIOLOGY: There are several articles on the anatomical and the biological peculiarities of grey mullets. Gunther (1861,1880) gives a short account of the viscera of mullets describing the pharyngeal filtering apparatus, gizzard-like stomach, highly convoluted intestine as the stricking features of the alimentary tract. Goodrich (1909) described the long internal papillae in the oesophagus, Jacot (1920) reported the development of the convolutions of the intestine, and Rabimulla (1945, 1947- 48) stated about the number and arrangement of pyloric caeca. The habit of shedding gill raker process in mullets including cephalus has been pointed by Luther (1964) with his opinion that this function of the digestive system may be due to excessive feeding on its favourite item of food or due to clogging of the branchial apparatus by the suspended particulate matter present in the environment. Ishidal (1935) stated that the alimentary tract did not have either gastric gland or proteolytic enzymes of the system thus being adapted for herbivorous feeding. Jacot (1920) studied on the adipose eyelids and scales, and Pillay (1951) described the structure and development of the scales of five species of mullets indicating the scale characters as an useful key for identification. From taxonomical point of view the figures and descriptions of the skull (Gregory, 1933; Berg, 1940) and otoliths (Chaine and Duvergier, 1928) of M cephalus are very important. The partial success in the cryogenic preservation of grey mullet sperms (Hwang et al. , 1972; Chao et al. , 1973,1974) ensures a step ahead in the formation of mullet-sperm bank in near future for selective breeding and artificial propagation of mullets: M cephalus, a euryhaline fish in true sense, can tolerate an extreme fluctuation of salinity from seawater to freshwater; and is also fairly resistant to changing temperature. Thus, this species is found highly suitable for culture in both brackishwater and freshwater environments of temperate and tropical regions, where their population acclimatised well in natural process of physiological adaptation of the body. However, a negligible to minimum casuality is seen in captivity during acclimatisation of fry (2.5-3.5cm) from saline to freshwater or long distance transportation in containers.

Stages of development:Several authors (Sanzo, 1936; Liao, 1975; Chaudhuri et al., 1977) described the life-history of M. cephalus. The differences recorded by them in the characteristic features of the developing embryo might be due to environmental variations and or variations in the biological characters of individual parents. The descriptions given by them on the embryonic development of the cephalus grey mullet are briefed here. Miscellaneous Information

This is one of the common species of mullets in the Indian region (rare in Bengal waters) and is capable of surviving in ponds with a high salinity. In the Chilka lake (Orissa) this is the most abundant mullet with an annual catch ranging between 270 and 355 tonnes; fishing season is throughout the year.
