MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate, its dorsal profile more convex than the ventral. Head rather small, its length 4.5 times in standard length. Snout very prominent, overhanging mouth, without any lateral lobe but with a distInct groove across it and generally covered with pores. Eyes fairly small, not visible from underside of head, the diameter 4.5 to 5 times in head length.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY: L. dero is heterosexual. Like other carps the secondary sexual characters of kursa bata are developed and become prominent during pre-spawning and spawning season, when males are recognised by the roughness on the pectoral fin surface and with the oozing milt on slight pressure on the abdomen, and the females by the distended and bulging abdomen with extrovert, swollen, pale pinkish genital organ. The pectoral fins offemales are smooth in touch and slightly smaller than in males in most of the specimens examined (Chondar, unpubl.). The slightly marked transverse groove on the snout becomes comparatively much deeper in mature male than female, and as a result, the snout in male appears to be very greatly enlarged and fleshy. The rudimentary 'pearl organs' on the dorso-lateral surface of the snout in male become more prominent, while in female the tubercles represent as small open pores. In mature male. a few anterior rays of the dorsal fin become greatly elongated, reaching to the tip of the posterior- most rays when adpressed against the posterior rays. In some cases the anterior rays may even extend the tip of the posterior rays by about] 0 cm. In female, the anterior rays do not extend even on maturity (Hora, ] 936a).

 

Breeding season:. L. dero breeds in July-August in the principal streams of Himachal Pradesh and Kashmir (India) and in Nepal. when the rivers spade. In Mahanadi the breeding period is during June-July, coinciding the south-west monsoon. The breeders perform the spawning migration on the appearance of riverine envioronment. Bhatnagar (] 964) describes that the Bhakra reservoir's dero performs spawning migration from their resident to the connecting streams when get flooded with commencement of rains in the area. Among some of the breeding grounds located are in the neighbourhood of Kursa village at a place where Lunkhar Khadjoins the Bhakra reservoir with water level when raised about 466 msl; in the Hirakund stretch of the Mahanadi river (Job et el., ] 953) and in the Alaknanda river (Jhingran and Sehgal, ] 978 b). L. dero has a short seasonal breeding habit. Each individual breeds only once during early part of the monsoon. Breeding takes place in cloudy weather at noon in the turbid water (turbidity 50 mm) at 27°C water temperature on the sandy bottom of the breeding ground in a shallow depth of water (0.7-].2 m) at Bhakra, and it may continue for 5-6 hours. The spent fishes after breeding migrate to the deeper waters of the reservoir (Bhatnagar, ]964). The physico-chemical characteristics of the breeding ground recorded to be as: water depth 46-60 mm; gravel and sandy nature of substratum; diamater of bottom stones 5-8 cm; water transparency 70-80 mm; water temperature 23.5-25.0oC; PH of water 8-8.]; dissolved oxygen] ].6-]2.] ppm; and total alkalinity 92-]]0 ppm. BIOLOGY:. Labeo dero is of the Bangana kind (Hamilton, 1822). It is a minor carp and forms a commercially important food fish in the upland waters. The narrow gill-opening and the reduced gills suit their dwelling in the oxygen-rich torrential streams and rivers. The mouth bounded by thick lips and lined internally by a cartilaginous base is highly suitable for their feeding habit of attached herbs from the hard substratum. Both the jaws are edentulous, and the vomarine and palatine teeth are absent. Two median palatal cushioned-pads, one anterior and the othersterior in position, are present on the roof of the buccopharyngeal cavity. The anterior pad is small (say, 6 mm x 3 mm in a 180 mm long specimen- Das and Nath, 1965) and elliptical with 10-13 fleshy comb-like protuberences and 2 small fleshy backwardly directed 'tentacles' on its each side. The posterior pad is large (say 13 mm x 14 mm) and somewhat hexagonal with numerous closely-set papilla-like outgrowths, which are developed from the mucous membrane lining the buccopharyngeal cavity internally. The gill rakers are also fine, almost hair-like and closely set-up, but do not meet those of the adjacent visceral arches unless pharynx is contracted. By nature the khursa bata is more hardy performing swift locomotary function in the torrential current, but can not stand even little salinity for exclusively freshwater physiological constituents of the body. Kursa bata has both external and internal parasitic infections, the quantum of infections is generally not much. Chauhan and Malhotra (1981) estimated the correlation between Parasitism Index (PI) and Gastro-Parasitic Index (GP1) in the fish-cestode relationship of L. dero to be poor

Miscellaneous Information

 This minor carp of northern India, is characterised by the presence of a groove along the snout, the snout being often covered by pores; the anterior dorsal convexity changes gradually to a concave profile, giving it an elegant slender appearance.
