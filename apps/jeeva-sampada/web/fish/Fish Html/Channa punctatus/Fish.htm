MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate and fairly rounded in cross-section. Eyes moderate, its diameter 7 to 8.5 times in head length. Mouth large; lowe~ jaw with 3 to 6 canines behind a single row of villi form teeth which deepens to 5 or 6 rows at symphysis; villiform teeth on vomer and palatines. Pectoral fins extend to anal fin; pelvic fin about 75% of pectoral fin length. Caudal fin rounded. Scales on summit of head large, rosette of head-scales lies between orbit and hence the frontal head- scale with cephalic sensory-pit in centre forms central plate of rosette; 5 scale- rows between preopercular angle and posterior border of orbit; predorsal scales 12; scales 37 to 40 in lateral series.

 

 

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:Anatomically both the male and female sex ofC. punctatus can be identified even injuvenile (70 mm) stage. In males, testes do not extend behind the vent, whrereas, ovaries in females extend behind the vent (Dutta and Reddy, 1977). The sexual dimorphism is identifiable externally during the breeding season only when the secondary sexual characters developed. However, the numerous minute black pin-head dots on the dark vertical bands in males once developed persist throughout the year. In female, these dots mayor may not be present, but dark diffiIsed blotches once appeared on the vertical bands persist throught the year (Parameswaran, 1975). According to Banerjee(1974a) these black pin-head dots are developed on the ventral region of the body of males as against the blotches in the corresponding parts of the females. Dehadrai et at. (1973) reported some colour difference between the sex, but Dutta and Reddy (1977) do not consider it to be a reliable secondary sexual character in large number (2400) of data examined from Gunther. The non- bulging abdomen, pale coloured oblong vent with brown to dark periphery, and a pinkish dot often seen at its centre are prominent secondary sexual characters of the male, while females possess conspicuously bulging abdomen and slightly protruding reddish colour round vent (Parameswaran, 1975). A case of hermaphroditism has been reported (Duttaand Reddy. 1977) for the first time in lata. Externally the gonad looked like a testes which did not extend beyond the vent, but microscopical examination revealed both testicular and ovarian tissue with spermatocytes and ova (including some larger ova of 0.023-0.23 mm dia. with deposition of yolk started).

 

Breeding season:. C. punctatus is a prolific breeder. It breeds in any types of freshwaters, viz. lakes, reservoirs, rivers, tanks, ponds, ditches, jheels, beels, baors, swamps, paddy fields. It may breed once or twice in a year. Its breeding season is not confined to a definite period but varies from locality to locality. In Madras, it breeds twice, once in January- February and next in July-August (Raj 1961; Arumugam, 1966), while Chacko (1956) mentioned about its breeding season in Madras in June-November, and Jones (1946) recorded in August-September in Tamil Nadu. In Andhra Pradesh, it breeds throughout the year (Hosaini and Rahimulla, 1946). In Malnad region of Kama taka it breeds from early May to late August, with the peak in June-July coinciding the peak rainfall (Parameswaran, 1975; Parameswaran and Murugesan, 1976a). In Madhya Pradesh of central India, its breeding season extends from May to end of September (Swarup, 1954). Based on the development of gonads and seasonal changes in the ovaries, Belsare (1962, 1966) pointed out the breeding season of C. punctatus in M.P. during June-September. Its breeding season in Punjab is from mid-April to July end (Khan, 1924). Qasim and Qayyum (1961, 1963) and Qayyum and Qasim (1964) identified two distinct groups of ova in the mature ovary by ova diameter frequency polygon method and suggested that this species may possibly breed more than once during the same spawning season extending from June to September, with the peak in July-August in Uttar Pradesh. In Bengal, it breeds during June-August. In Sri Lanka, its spawning season remains for a short period from April to May (Willey, 1908). However, the great variations in the breeding period of the spotted murrel in different parts of India may be attributed to difference in climatic conditions.

BIOLOGY:Compared to the giant and stripped murrels the spotted murrel is more delicate and susceptible to infection and death due to environmental change. It even dies due to osmotic stress when caused by the increase in salinity above 6%0 (Mansuri etal., 1979). Chanchalet al. (I 977) work out the decrease of oxygen consumption rate to a tone ofI0.3%, 15.3% and41. 7% with the increase of water salinity to 10%0, 20%0 and 30%0 respectively and comment that due to dehydration of blood through osmotic transference from the blood vessel to the exterior a metabolic stress is caused leading to the death of the fish. The accessory respiratory feculty ofC. punctatus is poorly developed, and thus it dies relatively earlier than C. marulius and C. striatus. The fry and fingerlings of the common pond murrel can not withstand handling, unlike those of carps. It is a slow growing air-breathing fish and does not normally attain beyond 30 cm in size. For of its small size it is being easily preyed upon by bigger murrels and other piscivorous fishes. As a result, the natural population of C. punctatus in bigger water sheets is dwindling rapidly. The large mouth; conical teeth on the lower jaw, vomers and palate; blunt teeth-like gill-rakers (7 nos); short alimentary canal (RLG 0.80) with short, muscular oesophagus, sac-like muscular stomach externally non-differentiated into cardiac and pyloric limbs, short narrowly-coiled muscular intestine demarcating deodenum externally by a constriction, and a pair of short pyloric caeca are in the plan of a carnivore feeding habit in C. punctatus. But for low carnivorous tendency and piscivorous habits the spotted murrel can be tolerable in carp culture ponds. Cannibalism is not also so pronounced in it. Some are in opinion that it is predominantly an insectivorous and not destructive to other fish in large measures. Mookerjee et al.(1946d) expressed the utility of this species in pond fishery and stated that the increased proportion of copepods mainly cyclops in the gut of fry is the definite proof for its utilisation as a biological controller of guinea worm. Like other four common Indian murrels C. punctatus has a ill-developed swim-bladder. The length of the swim- bladder is 2.5 in the total fish length. The posterior chamber is 5.0 in the length of the anterior chamber of the bladder. The accessory respiratory organ is a simple pouch-like structure with a bony lamellar labyrinth covered by a thin vascularised musculature. It is relatively less developed in spotted murrel than in C. marulius and C. striatus.

Miscellaneous Information

This species attains a length of 31 cm; prefers stagnant waters, in muddy streams. Prolific breeder and rapid development; breeds in ponds almost throughout the year by building circular nets between marginal weeds; peak breeding before and during monsoon months; matures in first year. The spotted snakehead is common throughout the plains of India .
