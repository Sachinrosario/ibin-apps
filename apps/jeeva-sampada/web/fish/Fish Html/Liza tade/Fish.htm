HABIT & HABITAT: Warm Brakish Marine Water


Distribution :Bay of Bengal , Westwards to Bombay , Hoogly-Matla estuary.

 

Back to Top

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES:Body slender and elongate. Head wide, much depressed and pointed, its length 19 to 25% of standard length. Adipose tissue covers most of iris. Teeth labial; 5 to 9 rows of small teeth on upper lip, one row of sparse ciliiform teeth on lower lip. Preorbital wide, filling space between lip and eye, anteriorly notched. First dorsal fin inserted nearer to snout-tip than to caudal fin base (but midway in young ones); second dorsal fin origin on vertical through posterior half of anal fin base. Pectoral fin length 75 to 79% of head length, inserted slightly below horizonalline through centre of pupil; pelvic fin origin nearer to anal fin origin than to tip of snout. Caudal fin forked. Pectoral axillary scale rudimentary or absent; second dorsal and anal fins densely scaled; scales in lateral series 30 to 35.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:. Male and female sex are separate and become distinguishable only when their secondary sexual character developed. The abdominal bulginess in the females is getting prominent even from the later part of the second stage (maturing) of the ovary and distended fully in the fourth stage of the hard roes. The genital apperture of male is slit-like and colourless, while that of the female is extrovert and red in colour. On slight pressure on the abdomen near the vent males ooze milt and females lay a few ova in fully mature ones.

 

Breeding season:L. tade is a seasonal spawner and breeds in the sea. Seaward spawning migration of the mature fishes of both the sex occurs from the estuary during spawning season. It has an extended spawning period and it breeds more than once in the season (Pillay, 1954a; Pillay, 1979).

Fishing methods:Mullets including L. tade are caught by different types of gillnets and drag and cast nets with pockets from  estuaries. Many inshore fishing devices are used to catch the mullets from the coastal waters. The main gears employ in the Hooghly and the Mahanadi estuaries are 'patajal' (set barriers), 'been jal' (bag nets) and 'ber  jal' (shore seines).

 



BIOLOGY:L. tade is a most desirable brackish  water fish for high consumer's demand. Its flesh and hard roes in fresh or salted and sun  dried conditions are considered to be a good food of delicacy. Canned mullet has taste like European salmon (Sarojini, 1951). Smaller ones form a popular live bait in long line fishing (Devanesan and Chidambaram, 1948). From culture point of view L. tade is taken as one of the most desirable species of the grey mullets for its hardy nature, high percentage of survivality, high tolerance to salinity fluctuations (0-38 ppt) and temperature (7 -40°C), herbivorous feeding habits with low in food chain, compatibility with carps in the same ecosystem and very rapid growth in freshwater environments. L. tade is well known for its leaping movement behaviour, when scared, and they impart more rigidly in leaping by di9tending their pelvic fin apparatus (Smith, 1935). The morphohistological features of the digestive tract of L.tade with slightly inferior V-shaped suctorial mouth, so-called well-marked pharyngeal apparatus, exceedingly thick muscular gizzard-like pyloric stomach, five long semi-  pyloric caeca, and a long gastro-intestinal canal having specially organised mucosa are exclusively suited for illiophagous feeding habits of the fish from the benthic zone. The setiform teeth of the two pharyngeal cushions of the pharyngeal cavity form only a straining apparatus. The tuft-like growths of long pointed bristles of the gill rakers of the second, third and fourth gills interlocked with the similar structures of the adjacent gill-arch provide a very efficient mechanism of filtration of food components from mud (Pillay, 1953). L. fade is relatively less prone to parasitic attacks. Mortality due to parasitism has not been reported even from farms. Leeches probably infest the mullets of the enclosed fisheries (Pillay, 1954a). Ghosh (1978) reported the Larnea infection in L. tade. A Protozoan infection, the Zoothamnium horai, being attach to scale is recorded by Khajuria and Pillay (1950). The Neoechinorhynchus sp., a Nematode, has been observed from the intestine of L. fade (Pillay, 1954a).

Stages of development: The larval characters of L. tade described here are based on some stray information available for different stages of the fish. Larva, about 4 mm stage. Planktonic, transparent, elongate body with narrow embryonic fm-fold, a moderately large oval-shaped yolk-sac without any posterior elongation, and a medially placed shallow depression for the site of the future anal opening. Post larva, 6.5 mm stage. First dorsal fin indicated as a narrow strip of the embryonic fm-fold, second dorsal well differentiated. Anal  fin also  differentiated.  Ventral fin rudimentary. Fan-like pectoral at mid-girth of the body well developed. Yolk-sac absorbed. Barbels absent. Anal opening near about middle of the body. Eyes pigmented. Post larva, 8.6 mm stage (Fig. 39 a). Head length 4.5 and body height 6.0 in total length, and eyes 2.9 in head length. Eyes pigmented. the lateral line, dorsal profile from second dorsal to caudal base and from mid-anal to caudal base along the ventral line. 24 (11 + 13) vertebrae countable. All fins well marked out. First dorsal fin with 3 rudimentary spines and second dorsal having 9 un articulated rays. Caudal fin with 15 well-developed rays has narrow small strips of embryonic fin-fold at its base. Anal fin with 2 spines and 8 rays, originating a little advance to second dorsal continues with a narrow strip of pre- anal fin-fold. Pelvic fins rudimentary, rays yet to be formed. Pectorals fan-like, well developed, rays indicated at dorsal aspect. Digestive tract fairly developed with an anal opening at the mid-body (Pillay, 1954a). Post larva, 9.0 mm stage. No remarkable change in the characters seen when compared to previous stage. Post larva, 12.0 mm stage. All the fins fully formed with complete rays. Narrow and very small strip of the embryonic fin-fold still persist at dorsal and ventral aspects of the caudal fin base and also anterior to anal fin. Body uniformly silvery white in colour. Fins free from chromatophores except at the base of anal and caudal. Post larva, 15 mm stage (Fig; 39 b). All the fins possess adult characters in every respects. Pigment cells uniformly spread on the dorsal half of the body, head and opercles; but crowded near the lateral line.

Economic Importance

Uses: This mullet contributes a minor fishery in the Hooghly- Matlah estuary (West Bengal) and it forms an important fishery in the Kayamkulam lake (Kerala) from July to February.

Miscellaneous Information

 The Mahanadi estuary (Orissa) and is abundant from September to November. It attains a length of 47 cm. Capable of adapting to freshwater. Cultivated in brackishwaters in India, but does not breed in confined waters. A costly fish when freshly caught.
