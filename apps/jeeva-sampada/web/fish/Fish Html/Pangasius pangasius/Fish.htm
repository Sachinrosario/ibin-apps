HABIT & HABITAT: Warm Water


Distribution :Freshwaters of UP,Bihar,Darjeeling,Assam,Orissa,MP,Chennai,WB, Rohini & Rapti river (UP) & Gangetic estuary.

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate and compressed. Head slightly granulated above; occipital process reaches to basal bone of dorsal fin; snout fairly prominent. Eyes large, about 3.5 times in length of head, visible from ventral surface of head. Mouth fairly wide. Teeth villi form on jaws and palate; vomero- palatine teeth in a crescentic band, composed of four patches .

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY: Unlike some bagrid and sisorid catfishes, where genital papillae are present especially during the breeding months, the pangasid males and females are not usually evidenced externally, except during breeding months when mature males ooze milt by a slight pressure on the abdomen and females show bulging abdomen with wide genital opening. Besides, David (1963) has expressed that females possess larger body proportions with regard to head length, pre dorsal  length, pre anal length, body depth at dorsal and anal fm, and diameter of eye in the total length; height of fins, length of spines and barbels, showing tendency to increase at a faster rate in females than those in males. Of these characters only two (pre dorsal length both in adult and juveniles, and diameter of eye in adult stage) are statistically different between male and female at 5% level. The structural differences in the male and female gonads appear right from the 7 cm fingerling stage, when the testis developed with uneven outlines and irregular lobules, while the ovary has plane and smooth outlines (David, 1963).

 

Breeding season:  Pangas is a freshwater seasonal spawner. Their natural spawning season in the northern India is within June to August. They do not ordinarily spawn in ponds, but breed in flooded rivers and adjoining areas. They perform a long spawning migration but do not ascend into small streams or tributaries far from the main rivers. They breed early in the monsoon within the inundable channel over the muddy islands of the main river (David, 1959). With the rise of the water level of river the well grown larvae and fry are washed downstream, drifted into the fast currents of the flooded river and ultimately move into the tidal zone, where they spent 3 to 4 years, and after maturing they once again seek the freshwater to repeat the cycle. This may be the possible reason tor non- availability of the embryonic egg into the river. There are diversified views regarding spawning location of pangas. According to Pantulu (1962) this species spend their adult life in the rivers and migrate to estuaries for spawning. David (1963), whereas, believes that pangas attains maturity in the estuary, then migrate.. and breeds in the freshwater, and the young ones drifted into the tidal stretch of the river



Breeding techinique: Induced breeding of pond-reared pangas of 5-6 years age, with an average weight of 4.5 kg females and 2.8 to 3.2 kg males were successfully made in Thailand. Three fractional doses of fresh pituitary glands (of donar species Clarias batrachus having 333g in weight) at the rate of 2, 6 and 12, suspended in 1 cc of 0.8% saline solution, were administered intra-muscularly to the females at 6 hours intervals. Males were given 2 glands injections. Four hours after the third injection to the females, the ovarian eggs were stripped off and inseminated with milt in plastic trays. The fertilised eggs were then transfered in the hatching containers provided with aquatic weed (Ceratophyllum demersum) as egg-adhering plant. The eggs were hatched out within 27-33 hours at 26.5-31.0oC. The ovulation was with 100% fertility (Boonbrahm, 1968).

 


Fishing methods:Unlike many other fishes the catch of P. pangasius is not dependent greatly upon gear selectivity. However, since larvae and fry are drifted by the strong current through the mid-stream, their collections are made by towing 1 mm diameter ring-net by a power boat, but majority of larvae is crusted due to pressure of water and accumulation of debris. Juveniles are caught generally in the fixed bag-nets known as 'Bheen nets' (David, 1954) and circular 'Beshadi' nets (David, 1963). Larger sized specimens are captured by drifting-gillnets (the 'Tiar') and the wide meshed 'Gochail' nets operated from boats in the Ganga. Shore seine (Maha jal) also gives considerable catch. Large sized maturing and mature specimens are captured just prior to the monsoon months in stake-nets tied across the canals that connect various estuarine creeks when the breeders try to ascend the main river for spawning. Hooks and lines with baits as crickets, cockroaches, chunks, offal from slaughter houses, etc. are used for fishing. 'Sirki' or large basket traps laid across the low-lying river channels is used to fish pangas in good numbers.

 



BIOLOGY:  Pangas is lurid in appearance (Hamilton, 1822) and often grows to about 120 cm (Weber and De Beaufort, 1913) or more. It possesses a keen sense of smell. It has non-predatory habit, rather exhibits foul feeding habit usually gorging on molluscs, carcases or even vegetable matters. For extensive feeding on molluscan shells, pangas can be safely utilised as a biological control agent, consequently preventing investation of diseases, such asSchistosomiasis for which molluscs are known vectors (Jayaram, 1978). In other words, the serious pests of molluscs and helminths can be effctively controlled by culturing this species in ponds. It probably feeds at night. Being a bottom feeder it can best be caught by paste-baiting. Although primarily a carnivore, the alimentary canal is fairly long, with a bag-shaped stomach. The last portion of the canal is also wide and scale-like (Hora, 193 8b). The food habits of pangas correlate with the digestive enzyme. The amylase is either absent or very faintly present in the stomach, but it is always detected from the intestine in a weak quantity. Invertase activity is mild in the stomach but strong in the anterior intestine. The activity of proteolytic enzyme is fairly high both in the stomach and intestine. Lipase quantum is appreciable in the intestine. pH is 6.3 in the stomach, and ranges from 5.9 to 6.4 in the intestine (Ghosh and Saigal, 1981).Pangas possesses a hydrophysial cavity. This interesting feature of pangas is comparable to that of the primitive teleosts, the eel and trout, which retain the structure similar to that of Acipenser in a degenerate condition (Sathyanesan, 1960). Pangas is very hardy in nature, and can withstand long-ranged temperature and salinity and high turbidy. It can also thrive well in low dissolved oxygen level in swamps and raw sewage-fed/oxidised/jute-retted ponds, and thus it is found to be very much suitable to even culture in low-lying fellow waters. Pangas has fluviatile anadromous in nature and shows migratory cycle between fresh water and saline water. For its high adaptability, the fish if once spawn in fresh water, its life cycle can be curtailed without materially affecting its growth and related bionomical activities (David, 1963).

Stages of development:The larval and the post larval stages of pangas can be distinguished easily from those of other known catfishes by the help of: its strickingly large head and a greatly spread caudal fin. Boonbrahm (.1968). has given the 10comotary and the feeding behaviour of pro larva. The newly hatched larvae move repeatedly in vertical distribution. The two- day-old larvae, with well developed maxillary barbels, move horizontally and bit at anything in their paths. They have well developed mouth and shown cannibalism. The larvae of three-day-old and onwards do accept nicely the artificial feed. Economic Importance

Uses: It is a food-fish but its flesh is not graded among that of good varieties of fishes. A good fishery exists in the Gangetic estuaries during July-August.It feeds on foul and decaying animal and vegetable matter. Because of its dirty feeding habits, this fish is not much liked. Since it attains a good size, it affords a good sport to anglers.
