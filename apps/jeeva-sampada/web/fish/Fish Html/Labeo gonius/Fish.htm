MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES:Body elongate, its dorsal profile more convex than the ventral. Snout slightly projecting beyond mouth, devoid of lateral lobe, studded with numerous pores. Eyes moderate, not visible from underside of head, the diameter 4.5 to 5 times in head. Mouth narrow and subinferior; lips thick and fringed, with a distinct inner fold in their circumference. Barbels two .

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:. L. gonius is heterosexual. The sexual differentiation for male and female is only possible during maturing in March-April onwards, on the appearance of secondary sexual characters. Like other India major carps, the roughness on the pectoral fins and on the scales, oozing of milt on slight pressure on the abdomen near vent, relatively longer pectorals, etc. of milters; and smoothness of pectorals and bulging abdomen with extrovert swollen vent of spawners are the common identifying characters. The pectoral fin, when extended backwardly and dorsaly, is reaching the lateral line scale 16th or 17th in female and 18th or 19th in male breeders (Chaudhuri, 1959a).

 

Breeding season:L. gonius breeds once only during spawning season and that all the ova are shed in a single spawning act. The natural spawning season is in July-August (Khan, 1934), July in BudhaNala, Ludhiana, Punjab (Ahmad, 1936), June-July in Bengal (Mookerjee and Ganguly, 1949), June-August in Orissa (Alikunhi, 1957), June to August with a peak in July-August at Agra (Chondar, 1970c), from April to August with a peak in May in Assam (Parameswaran et al., 1974), and from June to September with a peak in July-August-early September in Udaipur region (Unnithan, 1978) coinsiding the south-west monsoon. Since the monsoon rain breaks earlier in Bangladesh and in the north-east region of India, the gonius and other Indian major carps are found to breed in Chittagong (Bangladesh) from April onwards (Hora, 1945; Ahmad, 1948a) and in north-east Indian region from May onwards. L. gonius breeds in rivers, nalas, lakes, bundhs and 'bheels', where shallow flood plains suitable as spawning grounds are available. The grassy fields adjacent to Baretaghat nala which is connected with the river Betwa at Jhansi (Uttar Pradesh) (Ray etal., 1968); the Keetham lake and the tail-end of the Agracanal falling into the lake (In Chondar, 1977 mimeo);theNawpukri, a largetank(c 70h) connected with the river Dikhow in Sibsagar (Parameswaran etal, 1974); and the lakes of Udaipur region (Unnithan, personal communication) are some of the known breeding grounds. The abrupt rise in water level in the river which filled the dry nala; the heavy local rains on-rushed to the nala inundating the adjacent fields; and the sudden dilution in the concentration of soluble salts (bi-carbonates, chlorides, etc.,), combinedly affected the breeding. In the process of oogenesis, the cyanophils of the proximal part distal is of the pituitary gland controls the maturation of the ovary. The glycoproteinaceous contents, which remains in poor concentration during resting phase, is increased during maturation phase. Neurosecretory material also plays some role in oogenesis. These interpretations have been given by Joshi (1988) for maturation in gonius.

Breeding techinique:  The maturation is  advanced by hypophysation of gonius with the addition of extra putuitary hormone in induced breeding. The successful induced breeding of gonius was first achieved by Central Inland Fisheries Research Institute (Anon, 1965). The artificial inducement has also been referred by Parameswaran et al (1974 Ms), Chondar (1977 Ms), Sharma et al (1978). The natural breeding was also taken between male L. gon;us and female L. ca/basu in the hapa by pituitary hormone treatment at Jaysagar Fish Farm (Assam) in 1977 (Chondar, 1977). L. gon;us responds to a moderate dose offish pituitary gland, such as, a knock-out dose @ 6-8 mg/kg of spawners in favourable weather condition, or by 2 injections @ 3-4 mg and 6-8 mg/kg to the females, and I injection @ 3-4 mglKg to the males. According to Joshi (1981) the most suitable doses are 4 mg/kg in the first and 8 mg/kg in the second injections to the female and 3-4 mg/kg to the males. The latency period is 4-8 hours.

BIOLOGY:L. gonius is the slowest growing fish among Indian major (?) carps. Although Day (1878) recorded the maximum .Gonius is domesticated in nature and can produce a large number of eggs at a time, its fecundity is as high as rohu. The number of 2-day-old spawn is counted as 551-590/ml (Prasad, 1970). As a whole, gonius has less demand for quick perishability offlesh, insipid taste, numerous small intennuscular bones, non-availability of pure seed in adequate quantity in all regions. However, as a substitute of mrigal, where necessary only, gonius can be included in the composite culture. Prasad (1970) suggested to replace the heavy population of slow-growing gonius form reservoir by stocking of hybrid fimbriatus-gonius, a variety superior to parent species. Gonius is not so hardy in nature and can not tolerate salinity. The information on the parasitism of this species is lacking.

Miscellaneous Information

 This carp which attains a length of 1.5 m, is found in the waters of north India, Assam and Orissa, and along the east coast up to Krishna river. The species spawns during south-west monsoon months. It does not normally breed in ponds. It is cultivated in ponds along with other carp species. Artificial breeding successful through hypophysation.
