MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body stout and rather deep. Head fairly large and conical, its length less than body depth. Snout depressed and fairly pointed, devoid of lateral lobe, studded with pores. Eyes moderate, not visible from underside of head, the diameter about 3.3 times in head. Mouth inferior; lips thick and conspicuously fringed, both lips with a distinct inner fold. Barbels two pairs .

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:1 Sexuality. L. calbasu is heterosexual. Separation of sex can be made from the secondary sexual characters, which appeared only during breeding season. The roughness of the pectoral fin, sandy texture on the scale, larger size of the pectoral fin and the freely oozing milt by slight pressure on the abdomen are the identifying morphological characters .

 

Breeding season:. Kalbasu is a short seasonal breedEtand breeds normally in the nature once in a year in lacustrine habitat during rainy months coinciding the south-west monson (Qasim and Qayyum, 1962; Natarajan, 1971). Breeding is subjected to the favourable conditions of the monsoon floods and other associated factors. Breeding takes place in the river in a sandy stretch after a short migration of 15-25 km (Natarajan, 1971). The breeding season is also varying in differerent localities coinciding monsoon floods of those areas/regions. However, the breeding mainly occurs during the first half of the monsoon period in general. In West Bengal (India) and Bangladesh, the peak breeding is found in June, though the season extends from May to July. In the river Halda (Chittagong, Bangladesh), the breeding prevails just during a flood in the temperature of 79°F (Mookerjee and Majumdar, I 944b), while Raj (19 I 6) reported the breeding ofkalbasu in cold water too in Madras. Khan (1924) recorded the natural spawning in kalbasu in Punjab waters during July and August. In south India, the breeding commences by 'the end of May with the commencement of south-west monsoon and extends to the end of October, and the maximum spawning is seen in the first half of the season during night and the early hours of the morning (Chacko and Kuriyan, I 949a). The spawning season in Assam commences from May, since monsoon starts here earlier. In Bhabanisagar reservoir the breeding period is rather long from May to August, the peak becoming in May-June. In Godavari river system, the peak breeding month is the June (Rao and Rao, 1972) and in the Loni reservoir in July (Pathak, 1975). In Nagarjuna reservoir, the breeding takes place during July-September with high degee in August, and during breeding season females are more in number than males (Vinci and Sugunan, 1981).

BIOLOGY:L. calbasu is of special biological interest being one of the constituents of the compatible-complex of the major carps species employed in aquaculture practices. There is a commoner liking for its attractive appearance, sporting nature, and excelent eating. It has comparatively less intermuscular bones. Though attaining a maximum length of 90 cm, it is a relatively slow-growing fish. It is a bottom dweller and predominantly a herbivorous, although it takes considerable quantity of benthic fauna. Adults prefer to be in the mud or in some hiding place during day time and are found to come occassionally near the edge of the pond during the night to prey on insects larvae and to devour remnal/ts of the cooked food particles deposited from washing of the household utensils cleaned usually in the village ponds. Sometimes they come to the surface to ingest algae growing upon weeds and other aquatic vegetation. They are often seen to rest in a vertical position at the time of taking food either from the bottom or from the surface of water (Mookerjee and Majumdar, I 944b). The relative fecundity of kalbasu is highest among the major carps. Kalbasu is a seasonal breeder and spawn once in a year in the lotic environment during monsoon. The period of incubation is usually 15 hours (Mookerjee and Majumdar, I 944b). But the eggs are found to hatch even within 13-14 hours depending upon the temperature of water, the larva may sometimes takes about 24 hours to come out of the eggs. Similarly, the development process in the larva becomes rapid or slow due to the temperature of water. Economic Importance

Uses: It is an important foodflsh and at several places is referred to as the 'Black Rohu'.It is an important game-fish in the tanks where it is stocked and is cultivated along with other species. It thrives better in tanks and lakes than in running waters; can tolerate slightly brackish water also.It can be taken on small fly-spoon.

Miscellaneous Information

 Of wide distribution in India, it is one of the major Indian carps. It does not normally breed in ponds; induced bred by hypophysation. It is essentially a bottom feeder. It attains a length of 90 cm.
