HABIT & HABITAT: Warm  Water


Distribution :Fresh water of East Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Darjeeling District in West Bengal, Assam and Orissa.

Back to Top

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES:Body elongate and compressed. Eyes small. Mouth wide, its gape extends posteriorly to beyond eyes. Barbels two pairs; maxillary pair long, extend posteriorly to well beyond origin of anal fin, the mandibular pair much shorter, about as long as snout.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:Sexual dimorphism in male and female of all stages and in all season can be easily marked from the structure of pectoral spine which is well developed broad and strongly denticulated in male ,and weekly developed ,narrow and feebly serrated in female.

 

Breeding season:.  Boal breeds once in a year in monsoon during May-August with a peak in June-July in the eastern states; July in Punjab (Ahmad, 1934, I 944a); June-September with a peak at July-August in V.P. (Qasim and Qayyum, 1961); June-August in north-western states (Das, 1964); July-August in the upper river part of the Tungabhadra reservoir (David et al., 1979) in India. It breeds generally in the inundated marginal areas of rivers and reservoirs, but also seen to breed in large tanks after heavy showers followed by a run-off flow of water into the tank. The low grassy banks of the Budha Nala (Punjab) with slightly varying levels when inundated to depth of six inches to about a couple of feet and an optimum water temperature of c 80°F provided excellent spawning environment to boal (Ahmad, 1944a). Its natural spawning has also been observed at bed level 905 MSL of the river Bhavani with rapids and pools and pebbles and occassional sandy patches at the bottom (Ranganathan et al., 1964).



Breeding techinique:  Artificial breeding of boa first has been succesfully conducted by Ahmad (1944a) and Central Inland Fisheries Research Institute (1985). Pressed out of the instantly caught fully matured female and male breeders the ripe ova and the spermatic fluid are mixed well in a china clay dish for 5 minutes before adding water in it. 80% of the eggs fertilised were transfered to hatching trays, which were moored in the shallow running water of the Budha Nala (Ahmad, 1944a). In the CIFRI techniques the breeders are induced with injection of pituitary extract and LHRH formulations, 'Hoe 766 vet' and 'progesteron'. The injected breeders are kept in hapas pitched in the pool made temporarily in a rivulet by errecting dyke. Two doses offish PG extract (at the rate of3-5 mg and 12-20 mg/kg respectively in the first and second injections) are administered to the female at an interval of 6 hours; and a single dose at 3-5 mg/kg to males during the second injection to the female. Hoe 766 vet (containing 0.525 mg Buserelin acetate corresponding to 0.500 mg Buserelin antimicrobial mixture; 10 mg Benzyl alcohol) at a dose of 0.3 ml (150 g) kg-' followed by the final dose of progesterone at 0.2 ml (100 g) kg-I are administered to females, while males receive a single dose of 0.1 ml (50 g) kg-' Hoe 766 vet.

 


Fishing methods:Rod and lines with live-bait and other baits are used for fishing of W. attu. Boal shows much greediness for live- baits. Fry, spoon and spinning baits are mostly employed for fishing in rivers; and worms and occassionally flour paste are used in tanks. Thomas (1897) advocates the use of wire in preference to gimp or gut for fishing boal. Laicy (1905) suggests the best fishing of boal in warm season and warm days. Smaller sized fish (c 2-7 kg) can be caught with a stout pike or salmon rod and light tackle spinning with a small fish or a 1 Yz inch spoon at any time of the day (Dhu, 1923). Boal gives excellent sport with such light tackle.

 



BIOLOGY:W attu is commonly known as 'Freshwater shark' for its dwelling primarily in freshwater, highly predaceous in feeding habit and destructive in nature. It may even make fierce attempt to bite at the time of handling. The voracious feeding habit of boalleads to certain morphological and anatomical adaptations in the digestive tract. The large gasping mouth, spacious buccal cavity with patches of sharp teeth, pronounced bag-shaped stomach with greatly folded internal wall providing large space when expanded for ingestion of big-sized food, and short intestine are some characteristic features of highly carnivorous feeding habits accepting large prey as food (Kapoor, 1953). The backwardly directed large sharp teeth in two broad bands arranged in formidable rows are homodont and polyphyodont types. The same are used for holding fast the prey but not mastication. The teeth-like gill rakers also work as additional grip, and once the prey is caught it is difficult to escape out of their mouth. A medium sized carp can easily be consumed by them. For such feeding activity they show very sluggish movement and live most of the time at the bottom. This frequent slowness seems to discover the presence of food to deficient sight (Thomas, 1897). In other words, their sight plays a very little part for searching food. Rather their tactile feelers are the main agency to feel their way in the muddy waters (Beavan, 1877). W. attu possesses well developed nervous system with oval-shaped leptobasic hypophysis. Boal is a hardy fish and can live out of water for a considerable period. It can tolerate salinity even up to 10 ppm. It has a habit of jumping out and falling back with a splash when following shoals of fishes. As regards infection and diseases the various types of parasitic attack is encountered with boal. An osteogenic fibroma has been detected in the connective tissue of pond-reared specimens. A list of common diseases often come across in boal is given here. The extremely voracious carnivore W.  attu feeds predominantly on fish, though variety of items including even limbs of human being (Alikunhi, 1957), and wild rat (Rahman, 1981) have rarely found in the stomach. Boal has preference for alive fish, and to capture this food they select advantageous position in natural water as to swim rapidly after the prey (Menon and Chacko, 1958). Boal appears to be a mid-feeder in the deep pools.

Miscellaneous Information

This catfish inhabits large rivers, tanks and lakes. It is one of the largest, voracious and predatory of the local catfishes which thrives well in rivers and tanks also, especially in jheels with grassy margin. It is fished in large numbers, being caught in large scoopnets, long.lines and hooks. The fish prefers muddy tanks subject to periodical flooding from a nullah or river. It is esteemed as food by the poorer classes. Abundant during the warm season, considerable quantities are sold round the year in all parts of India. It is a pre monsoon summer breeder.This catfish is a good sport and can be easily taken on a hook with dead bait. It also takes live-bait well. In West Bengal and Bihar it is captured by spearing from a boat. It is very destructive to other more valuable food-fishes such as the major carps. A tank where this fish exists, usually contains no other good fish. In order to have ~ther fishes in the tanks and reservoirs, it is very necessary to eliminate this fish. It grows to about 2 m and weighs more than 45 kg. It is rather sluggish and stays at the bottom of water in search of food. handled it bites strongly as it has a huge mouth with formidable jaws, armed with bands of conical teeth. Due to its rich oil content it is liked by many. It is also offered to Goddess Kali by the Hindus.
