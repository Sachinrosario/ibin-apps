HABIT & HABITAT: Warm Water


Distribution : Fresh water of West Bengal, Eastern Himalayas, U.P., Bihar,Himachal Pradesh,Bombay, Northern India and Punjab.

Back to Top

MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body streamlined, its depth about equal to length of head. Snout blunt, often with pores. Mouth broad; upper lip entire, lower lip most indistinct. Barbels a single short pair of rostrals only. Pharyngeal teeth in three rows, 5.4.2/2.4.5 pattern. Gillrakers 40 to 49 on first arch. Dorsal fin as high as body. Pectoral fins shorter than head. Caudal fin deeply forked. Lateral line with 40 to 45 scales; lateral transverse scale-rows 6-7/5-6.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY: Mrigal is heterosexual. Male and female sex can be differentiated only during breeding season, when male oozes milt on slight pressure on the abdomen near the vent and the female shows bullged and soft abdomen with extrovert swollen reddish vent. The pectoral fins, in male, during breeding season, measured bigger than anals, while in female these are equal to anals (Mookerjee, 1938). In identical sized male, mrigal has pectoral fins better developed and largre than anals (Khan and Hussain, 1945). Pectoral fins in mature male become rough during breeding season and reach to 10th or 11 th lateral line scale (Chaudhuri, 1959). According to Chakravarty and Singh (1963) the pectoral and pelvic fins of male are larger than those of female, and the height of the dorsal fin in male measures more than that in female. The relationship between the right pectoral fin and the fish length of both the sex are for: Males, Pectoral length = -8.74 + 0.10877 fish length, and Females, Pectoral length = -0.370 + 0.12890 fish length.

 

Breeding season:The natural spawning of mrigal occurs in river, reservoir and in bundh-tanks where fluviatile conditions previl. Spawning takes place once in a year during south-west monsoon in India, Bangladesh and Pakistan. The spawning season in different regions of this sub-continent extends from April to September (Table 6.9), though the hypophysed mrigal is reported to have bred in December in Tuticorin (South India) during 1983 (CIFRI Report, 1984).

Fishing methods:. Mrigal alongwith other carps fishing in ponds and tanks is done mainly by dragnet and castnet throughout the year. Gillnet, scoopnet, etc. are mainly used for fishing in deeper region, and dragnet in the shallower zone of the river and reservoirs. The fishing in the river remains suspended during monsoon months due to strong current, increased depth of water and declaration of closed season for breeding.

 



BIOLOGY:C. mrigala is a very fast growing big sized cyprinid. For herbivorous feeding habit and other cultural qualities mrigal has been extensively utilised as a nice compatible member for six species compostie fish culture in practice in India. Their competitors are the feeders on vegetable debris, microscopic plants and detritus, notably L. calbasu and L.fimbriatus. Mrigal grows up to a salinity level of 14% and the salinity tolerance of fry and fingerlings is almost same as in rohu. Fingerlings (65-70 mm) thrive well in temperature ranging from 18.3-37.8°C, but become lethal below 16.7°C and above 39.5°C (Mookerjee et al., 1946c). For culture some weed growth is better as the adult mainly subsists on blue-green and filamentous green algae, pieces of higher aquatic plants, decayed vegetable matter, mud, detritus, etc. Mrigal hatchlings consume artificial feed better than by that of rohu and catla. Mrigal is a local and medium range distance migrant for feeding and breeding purposes. They are hardy in nature, but large scale mortality often encountered by them in ponds and tanks due to parasitic infections and diseases. The infestations are more liable to occur in ponds for overcrowding fish populations.

Economic Importance

Uses:  mrigal Thehas been transplanted into waters of peninsular India for aquaculture. It is a fine fish of elegant appearance. It is an important major carp of India and quite a game fish. Because of successful transplantations, mrigal has now spread over whole of peninsular India

Miscellaneous Information

 In natural waters mrigal is known to attain a length of99 cm and a weight of 12.7 kg from two of the most important rivers of its occurrence in India, Ganga and Yamuna. In natural open waters, it breeds during south-west monsoon in shallow pockets in marginal areas, and in bundhs. The depth of water at breeding grounds is 0.5 to 1 m. Mrigal is a detritus eater .
