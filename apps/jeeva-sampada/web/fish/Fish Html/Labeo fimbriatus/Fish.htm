MORPHOLOGICAL FEATURES: Body elongate, its dorsal profile more convex than the ventral. Snout blunt and fairly swollen, slightly overhangs mouth, devoid of lateral lobe, studded with minute pores. Eyes moderate, not visible from underside of head, the diameter 3.7 to 4.5 times in head. Mouth moderate and subinferior; lips thick and fringed, continuous, and having an inner fold above .

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY:L. fimbriatus is heterosexual. Like other carps sex recognition by external features is possible in maturing and mature stages only, when the sexual characters are developed. The identifying characters for male and females of L.fimbriatus are same as detailed in C.reba, except the length of the pectoral fin, which is extending upto 11- 12th lateral line scale in males, and 9-1 Oth L.I. scale in female.

 

Breeding season:L. fimbriatus breeds in river once in a year for a short period during rainy months, June to September (Alikunhi, 1957), July to September with a peak in August in Narmada (Karamchandani et al., 1967; Bhatnagar. 1972), June to September with a major spawning activity in August in Godavari (Rao, 1974). Breeding normally coincides with the flood season in the Narmada river (Bhatnagar, 1972), but also observed even when the monsoon fails and extreme summer conditions prevail (Alikunhi, 1957). The certain resultant hydrological conditions emerged from the riverine flood are the main natural factors responsible for spawning in the river. The minimum size of fimbriatus at which females start actual spawning is 421-430 mm for Narmada river (Bhatnagar, 1972) and 336 mm for Godavari (Rao, 1974). The strain of spawning is recorded to be greater in the case of males (Bhatnagar, 1972). In the river Godavari the breeding is confined to the upper stretches of the river commencing from Bhadrachalam and extending upstream beyond Dummagudem (Rao, 1974). Like other major carps the spawning grounds are the shallow pockets of the river bed or shallow inundated stretches adjoining the river.



Breeding techinique: The artificial breeding of fimbriatus in confined waters has also been successfully done by inducement with fish pituitary hormone (Anon, 1964 ; Naseem and Alikunhi, 1970) or with copper sulphate (Giridhar and Rao, 1976). One set of fimbriatus has been reported to breed by injection of CuSO4 solution (concentration 10 mg/ml distilled water) at the dose of 0.5 mg and 1.0mg/kg in the first and second injection to the female, and only one injection to the males, and produced 1.75 lakh spawn from a 1.5 kg spawner.

BIOLOGY:The fringe-lipped carp, the L. fimbriatus, is a moderately slow-growing fish with herbivorous feeding habit and high fecund. In south India they are locally known as "Rohu" of the Narmada river for general appearance and taste and also for preference among the commoner like rohu. L. fimbriatus has relatively more flesh for deep body (deeper than rohu and kalbasu) and less bones for small head and appendicular spines. For timid nature they do not leap much over the net during fishing, rather get gilled easily for smaller head when pass through the net. They have fairly great tolerance for temperature but less tolerance for salinity. Like adult the fingerlings are also hardy and thus the seed can be transported over a long distance.

Economic Importance

Uses: Very common in brackishwaters of the coastal regions of Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Orissa. This species is extensively cultivated in ponds and tanks where they breed naturally.

Miscellaneous Information

 This fish is the commonest major carp in Tamil Nadu. In the wild state it attains a length of 23 cm in the first year and 31 cm in the second; maximum length reached is 91 cm. This carp does not normally breed in ponds. It is cultivated along with other carp species. Predominantly a herbivore, feeding on diatoms, blue-green and green algae.
