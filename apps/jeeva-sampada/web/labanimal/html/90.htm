Background :

A recognized agricultural pest in Africa (South of Sahara)

 and is reservoir of plague in South Africa. In wild it was also

 implicated with epidemiology of Lhasa fever outbreak in

 1972. It is found in bush, shurb and cultivated area near

 human habitates. Anatomically, it resembles rat (gal baldder

 absent) and intermediate in size between rat and mouse it is

 nocturnal and shows peak activities at dusk and down. It is

 omnivorous and lives on grains and grass seeds. It travels

 long distances for food  and can swim in water. It uses

 deserted burrows of oher rodents. It can also dig own burrow

 in soft soil.

Wild pais from Baragwanath, South Africa were bred at

 Medical Research Centre, Johannesburg in 1946. To Walter

 Reed Institute USA in 1946. To N is 1954 and 1959. To

 Argonne National Laboratory, USA in 1964. To institute of

 Experimetal Gerontology, Neitherlands in 1967. To Justus

 Leibeg University in 1970.

        

Characteristics :

Mastomys was initially used for routine diagnosis and

 exoeromental work on plague. It also came to be used as a

 standard test animals for evaluation of vaccines. Now,

 extensively used in Protozoological and Helminthological

 research. It is also excellent laboratory animal for studying

 bilharziasis. Also used in endocrinology particularly for

 studying the hormonal effects on the prostate gland. Now

 increasingly being used for studying autoimmunity.
