 Biology/ Ecology / Behaviour

The adult female lay eggs right in the middle of an aphid colony, the larvae are pale brown or grey and start preying after emergence. The range of preys is very large and among them mites, thrips, mealybugs, moth eggs and larvae are the most common. A developing larva can destroy more than 200 aphids. At 25.5ºC constant it takes about 21-24 days from egg to adult but remarkably C. carnea shows a good flexibility to temperature fluctuations even in field condition. Even if the temperature reaches very low levels for few hours, the larva can survive and start again feeding as soon as a suitable condition returns.

 Appearance

Egg: Eggs are oval shaped, pale green.                                                                                                                                                                                       

 

 

 

 

Larva (Male/Female):

 

The larvae, which are very active, are gray or brownish and alligator-like with well-developed legs and large pincers with which they suck the body fluids from prey. Larvae grow from <1 mm to 6-8 mm.
 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

                                                                                                                                                               

Pupa (Male/Female):                                                                                                                                                                    

 

 

 

 

 

Adult (Male/Female): Adult green lacewings are pale green, about 12-20 mm long, with long antennae and bright, golden eyes. They have large, transparent, pale green wings and a delicate body. Adults are active fliers, particularly during the evening and night and have a characteristic, fluttering flight.

Miscellaneous Information

The predator was reared on eggs, nymphs and adults of the mealybugs infested on pumpkin in the laboratory at a temperature of 26-280 C and relative humidity of 65 to 70 per cent.

Egg; Chrysoperla eggs were obtained from Pest Control of India, Bangalore, Twenty eggs of the Chrysoperla were kept individually in glass vials (7.5 cm x 2.5 cm) and plugged with cotton. Incubation period was recorded. After hatching the larvae were fed only with the mealybug eggs. Fresh eggs were offered to the predator once in three days. Developmental period of each instar of the predatory grub was observed. Pre-pupal, Pupal periods, fecundity and adult longevity were also recorded.

Nymph; The newly hatched predatory larvae were fed individually with 10-15 days (2 nd instar), Old mealybug nymphs until predation. second instar nymphs were offered to the predators once in three days. Observations on the developmental period of different stages of the predators were recorded.

Adult: Freshly hatched predatory grubs were fed only with female adult mealybugs individually. Mealybugs were supplied to the predator once in three days until pupation, Observation were made on the duration of different developmental stages of the predator.

Longevity and oviposition: Newly emerged male and female Chrysoperla adults were collected and kept in pairs (10) separately and fed with 50 per cent honey in glass vials (7.5 cm x 2.5 cm). To record  the oviposition each pair was transferred to fresh vials and daily supplied with the citrus mealybugs to facilitate egg laying. Number of the eggs laid by the predator was counted using binocular microscope. This procedure was continued till all the adults died. Observation was also made on pre-oviposition, ovipositions and post-oviposition periods and on the longevity of adults. The total number of egg laid per female was computed in the end. The study on the longevity and oviposition was conducted on ten pairs of adults Chrysoperla considering each pair as one replicate.

Feeding potential of Chrysoperla: Experiments were conducted to determine the rate of consumption of different stages of the citrus mealybug by the predatory larvae. After hatching each predatory larva was confined in a glass vial (10 cm x 2.5 cm) and provided with a known number of preys. Observation was taken on the number of preys eaten at every 24 hours. Surviving preys were counted and removed. Fresh preys were offered to the predatory larvae in each instar and also the total number consumed in its larval development were later calculated. The feeding potential studies were conducted with 20 predatory larvae where each predatory larva was considered as one replicate.
