 Biology/ Ecology / Behaviour

The Indian bee builds many parallel combs in the cavities and hollows of trees, caves and such other hidden sites; the combs being parallel to the direction of entrance in the plains and at right angles to the entrance in cold regions. It is mild and capable of being domesticated and is commonly reared in South India. The annual yield of honey is 2 to 5 kg per colony. A queen can lay 350-1000 eggs per day.

A race of this species is found in the hilly tracts of South India. The hill bee is larger in size and darker in colour than the plains race and has the habit of moving away from the broods even at slight disturbance (2).
