 Biology/ Ecology / Behaviour

Gypsy cuckoo bee (Bombus bohemicus Seidl) are vary large social bees with hairy bodies covered in black and yellow. They can fly with such large bodies look thick and heavy. The hairs on their bodies help them conserve heat when flying. This is one scary bee, especially the ones that are mostly covered in black. Bombus bohemicus  can conserve heat by shivering their flight muscles. Only the queens and workers can do this. This adaptation allows them to survive in colder temperatures and higher latitudes. Therefore, they have first access to high elevation flowers like the fireweed (Epilobium). They are one of the few insects that use muscle thermo genesis. The workers are the daughters of the queens and the males are drones. They only produce during the mating season, which is at the end of summer. However, their colonies can’t survive the cold season, they have annual colonies. The queens hibernate and they will build a new colony when spring arrives. When the queen awakes from hibernation, she will search for her new nest site that will be spherically shaped and have a single exit. The nest will be in the ground and it’s usually an abandoned mouse nest or some other preexisting hole in the ground. It will provide good insulation for the colony. In the middle of the nest, the queen makes pollen paste to lay several eggs in then she seals it with wax. She then has to incubate her eggs and while she waits she feeds on her honey pot, which is a wax cup filled with nectar. When the larvae hatch, they consume the pollen paste. Full-grown larvae spin cocoons to allow metamorphism. Thus, a new colony is born. The inside of their nest is not like that of a honeybee. There are no honeycombs but instead there is a mound of capsule like cells (3).

Adult (Male/Female):

It has a long shaggy coat, dull yellow collar, and no yellow band on the abdomen. It has a white tail. In the females (Queens) there are two yellow patches before the white tail, forming a faint broken band of yellow but the yellow fades rapidly. There are a few pale yellow hairs at the rear of the abdomen. The species has prominent callosities, which do not meet in the middle. Males are similar female but the white or yellow tail ends with some black hairs (2).
