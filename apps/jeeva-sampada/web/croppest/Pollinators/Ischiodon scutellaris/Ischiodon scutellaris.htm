 Biology/ Ecology / Behaviour

The Ischiodon scutellaris's eyes are glabrous. Antenna with segment 3 only twice as long as broad. Abdomen flat or slightly convex dorsally; slightly, but distinctly margined on tergites 2 to 5. Lateral mesonotal margin bright yellow, distinctly demarcated from dark dorsum. Lower lobe of squama bare above. Anterior flat portion of mesopleuron bare, with only microscopic hairs and not with long hairs. Metasternum bare. Sternopleural hair patches separated posteriorly. Abdominal tip in ventral view symmetrical in female, asymmetrical in male (1).

Common Hover Flies may sometimes confused with bees or wasps because of their mimic colour. Their bodies are medium to slender. On their abdomen there are the yellow-black wasps pattern and the narrow waist mimic pattern. Common Hover Flies visit flowers as bees and wasps. They are usually seen hovering or resting on flowers. The flies feed on nectar and are the pollinators of plants as well. Common Hover Fly rests on stick during the evening. This Hover fly hovering among the hibiscus plants searching for aphids. Their larvae are the predator of aphids. After mating, female Common Hover Fly look for the good site to lay her eggs. Usually somewhere near the aphids feeding place. Aphid-eating hover fly larvae are legless and maggot-like. They are creamy-white to brown in colour, the above picture shows the larvae is searching its prey in a aphids colony. They pupate on the stem of plants which is dark brown in colour. They turn into adults in about a week. Adults look for mate and start the new life cycle (2).
