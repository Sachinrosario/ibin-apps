1. English name: Termites

Scientific name: Odontotermes obesus W

(Termitidae: Isoptera)

2. Distribution: Throughout India.

3. Appearance:

  1. a.Eggs: The queen termite lays one egg per second or 70 000 to 80000 eggs in 24 hours. 

  2. b.Larvae: 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Greyish white, wingless social insects. Their colony organisation is based on the caste system. In a colony there are numerous workers, soldiers, one queen, a king and a good number of complementary or the colonizing forms of true but young males and females. 

4. Nature of injury: Attack seedling underground parts.  

5. Symptom of damage: Seedlings fade away.

6. Alternate hosts: Sugarcane

7. Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: In the rainy season, when atmospheric conditions are favourable, the colonizing forms leave their parent colony. As they are weak fliers, they do not travel a long distance unless aided by wind. As a rule, a particular species swarms at about the same time of the season. The numbers of swarm comprise individuals of both sexes. They are attracted to light, where they mix with individuals of the neighbouring swarms. A great majority of them fall a prey to many types of predators and only a few individuals survive in the end. The survivors sooner or later fall down and cast their wings and mate before or after shedding them. Both the male and the female participate in the early operations of forming a nest by excavating small burrows or galleries or the nuptial chambers. The eggs laid at first are only a few and are looked after by them and the newly hatched nymphs are fed by the parents themselves which then taken over all the brooding, feeding, cleaning and other duties. During the first season, the reproductive castes are usually not produced and the queen gradually grows in size, and the number of eggs laid also increases.

The eggs hatch after about one week during the summer and within six months the larvae develop to form soldiers or workers, as the case may be, the reproductive castes, when produced matures in 1-2 years. The queen is capable of laying many millions of eggs during her life, which is very difficult to estimate, but probably 6-9 years is the approximate span.

Nest of many termites grow fungus gardens in the centre, near about the ‘royal chamber’. The fungus grows into a comb like structure and is fed to the royal pair and the larvae.

8. Other species with similar biology: Eutermes hoemi

9. Management:

Application of chlorpyriphos will give satisfactory control for a short period.

 

Termites in Groundnut - Odontotermes obesus (Ternitidae: Isoptera)

 

Nature of injury: When the crop is at the seedling stage, termites eat away the tender roots and parts of stem under the soil.

 

Symptom of damage: If the roots are eaten, the plants turn yellow, but if the stem is damaged the plants wilt and can be easily pulled out. The intensity of attack increases in soil moisture depleted areas. Pods and kernels are also damaged.

 

Management:

Cultural:

a. Repeated ploughing reduces termite population but the shallow cultivation carried out by small-scale farmers is unlikely to be effective this way.  Harvesting the groundnuts as soon as they  mature and early removal of produce from the field will reduce the termite damage to pods.  

b. Fumigate: Locate the active termite mound and introduce one or two Al-phosphide  tablets inside the mounds and close the mouths of the mounds with wet soil.

d. Pour 60ml Chlordane chlorpyriphos 20 EC/18 Lit of water/mound. Pour the solution through the hole and close the hole.
