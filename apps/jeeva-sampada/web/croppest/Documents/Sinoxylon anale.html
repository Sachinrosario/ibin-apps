4.

Nature of injury: The damage is done only to dormant grapevine. The adult beetle constructs a circular hole, extending to the centre of the stem and then makes longitudinal gallaries and forms a number of exits. The damage is always found about one foot above the ground level. Both adults and grubs cause damage by feeding inside the vine-stem.

5.

Symptom of damage: All the plant parts above the point of attack dry up completely. The pest prefers the late-sprouting varieties.

6.

Alternate hosts: Shorea robusta, Ouglinia dalbergioides, Dalbergia sisso wooden packings, etc.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The adult is sturdy and a very lively beetle that walks slowly and flies rarely. It is typically dark brown and measures 4.25 mm in length and 1.8 mm in breadth. The diagnostic three bladed antennae and a pair of spines on the posterior elytral extremity are present. The full-grown larvae of the beetle are thick yellowish-white curved grubs, often found feeding along with the adult beetles when the infested vines are split open. The pest is active on dormant grapevine. After the winter sets in, the adult beetles bore into the living woody stems and branches. Eggs are laid in galleries constructed by the adult beetles inside the attacked portions. The grubs after hatching continue feeding inside. The activity of the pest is accomplished with a peculiar cracking noise, followed by the ejection of a dusty material from the exits of the feeding galleries. The multiplication of the pest continues in the dead vines throughout the year.
