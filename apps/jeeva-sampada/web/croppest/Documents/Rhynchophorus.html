1. English name: Red Palm Weevil

Scientific name: Rhynchophorus ferrugineus Olivier

(Coleoptera; Curculionidae)

2. Distribution: Throughout India.

3. Appearance: It is a reddish-brown cylindrical weevil, with a long curved snout. The male weevil has a tuft of hairs along the dorsal surface of the snout, whereas the female is without it.

  1. a.Eggs: Females lays the eggs in scooped out small cavities as many as 276 eggs, which are oval and white.  

  2. b.Larvae: Apodous light yellowish grub with a red head becomes full grown in 36 to 78 days and pupates in fibrous cocoon inside the trunk itself.  

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): It is a reddish-brown weevil has 6 dark spots on the thorax, cylindrical weevil, with a long curved snout. The male weevil has a tuft of hairs along the dorsal surface of the snout, whereas the female is without it. 

4. Nature of injury: The larvae feed on soft tissue of the trees and often cause very severe damage, especially when a large number of them bore into the soft growing parts. Oozing out of brown liquid from holes indicate the early infestation by the pest.

5. Symptom of damage: Cut injuries found on the trunk crown. With occurrence of leaf rot disease the infestation of the weevil increases. Central stout shows sign of wilting.

6. Alternate hosts: All other palms

7. Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The weevil lays 200-500 eggs in its life span of 3-4 months. The eggs hatch in 2-5 days and the soft whitish grubs on hatching feed on the soft tissues and tunnel into the trunk of the trees. The full fed grubs are yellowish in colour and measure about 65 mm in length. The adults capable of flight and is diurnal in habits. The weevil is incapable of causing direct damage, but in the early stage it is harmful.

9. Management: The incidence of the pest can be suppressed by avoiding wounds on the trees and by disposing of felled trunks or tree stumps. Infested portion should be scooped out and dressed with tar.
