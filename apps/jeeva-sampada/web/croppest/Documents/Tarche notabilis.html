1.

English name: The Cotton Semilooper

Scientific name: Tarche notabilis Walker

(Lepidoptera; Noctuidae)

2.

Distribution: Throughout plains of Pakistan and India.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Green rounded eggs found singly on the underside of the leaves. 

  2. b.Larvae: The full-grown caterpillars are about 40 mm long and dark green and have six pairs of black and bright-yellow spots on the back. As they move, they bend the body forming half loops and hence the name semilooper. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): They are handsome stoutly built white moths, with prominent grey and brown spots on the wings and measure 30-32 mm across. 

4.

Nature of injury: The caterpillars feed on cotton leaves and skeletonize them altogether. In years of heavy infestation, the plants may be completely denuded of leaves.

5.

Symptom of damage: Skeletonized leaves.

6.

Alternate hosts: Sonchal, Brinjal, Okra etc.,

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The pest is active from April to October and passes the winter as hibernating pupa. The moths appear in March-April and are active at night. They lay green rounded eggs singly on the underside of leaves. The eggs hatch in 2-4 days and the caterpillars start feeding voraciously right from the beginning. They are full-fed in 9-16 days and then descend to the ground where they construct earthen cells at a depth of 3 cm to pupate inside. They emerge as moths in 5-14 days. The moths are short lived. The life cycle completed in 16 to 34 days. By October - November, they complete 4-5 generations.

8.

Other species with similar biology: Anomis flava Fabricius

9.

Management:

Plough up the fields soon after cotton harvest or by growing clovers in rotation with this crop.
