1.

English name: The coffee stem – borer / The coffee white borer

Scientific name: Xylotrechus quadripes Chevrolat

(Coleoptera; Cerambycidae)

2.

Distribution: South India

3.

Appearance: Dark brown longicorn beetle having a number of whitish marks on the thorax and upper wings.

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Found in cracks and crevices on the plant stem. They generally hatch in about 10 days. 

  2. b.Larvae: When full fed ¾ inch long and has a pale yellow colour; inform it is stout and swollen towards the head end and tapering towards the tail when about to pupate, it makes an emergence hole on the surface and then changes into pupa in a pupal chamber made close to the surface to help it to come out easily on assuming adult stage.  

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Beetle has white cross bands and dark brown elytra. 

4.

Nature of injury: The white larva bore into the coffee stem and kills the young plant and making the older ones appears quite unhealthy. The branches tunnelled by larvae wilt and break easily and the young plants often succumb to the injury.

5.

Symptom of damage: The branches tunnelled by the larvae will wilt and break easily. The young plants occasionally die.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The adult is blackish brown beetle and is 1.25 cm in length with prominent antennae. There is a characteristic pattern of yellowish bands on the elytra. A female beetle lays about 100 eggs in 3-4 weeks in cracks and crevices of the trunk bark or of the primary branches. They hatch in about 10 days. Only one generation completed in a year. There are two are distinct periods during which the adults emerge, one occurring in April-May and the other during September-December. The emergence in the autumn is greater and results in heavy egg laying.

Management:

All measures have to be directed to prevent the beetle from laying eggs on the stem.

Proper scraping of the stem s once or twice during the egg-laying season with strong brushes would destroy the eggs and just hatched grubs.

Proper disposal of the dead wood often containing the beetle grubs.

Adult beetles can be also collected and destroyed.

Identify the affected plants after cessation of rainfall.

Ridged develop on plant, uproot such plants and burn and destroy them. Don’t keep it as such in garden.

Smoothen the stem by swabbing with carbaryl (2%)- before the rainfall and after the rainfall.

Maintain optimum shade in field. Late rains cause death of pupal or late pupal stages.

Emergence activity increased with sunny days.
