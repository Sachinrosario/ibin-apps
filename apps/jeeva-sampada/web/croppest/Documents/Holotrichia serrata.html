White grub - Holotrichia serrata / H. reynaudi  (Coleoptera – Scarabaeidae)

 

 

Nature of injury: The damage becomes evident only when the entire plant dries up due to the grubs feeding on fibrous roots. The grubs eat away nodules, the fine rootlets and may also girdle the main root. The beetles at night feed on foliage and may completely defoliate trees like neem, Eugenia, Pithaealobium etc. immediately after emergence from the soil during April/May rain.

 

Symptoms: Entire plants dry up in patches. When such plants are pulled out they come out easily.

 

Management:

1. Cultural practices:

 a. Deep ploughing: Ploughing 4-5 times during the month of June exposes the larvae to birds and natural enemies, which results in considerable reduction of larval population.

  1. b.Seed treatment: 8 ml of chlorpyriphos (20EC) / kg of seeds (Take precaution to avoid consumption of treated seeds). To be practiced only in endemic areas. 

  2. c.Control of second instar larvae by ploughing the topsoil repeatedly 3 or 4 times 1 month after receipt of rain. Birds will also pick up larvae. 

2. Mechanical:

  1. a.After receipt of the summer rain during April-May follow collection and destruction of adults feeding on neem or eugenia trees near the field from 10 pm by beating tree branches.  

  2. b.Attract the emerged beetles to the planted neem branches treated with carbaryl 0.04% in the field and destroy them. 

  3. c.Remove the larvae mechanically along the plant rows by digging.
