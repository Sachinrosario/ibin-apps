1. English name: Black-headed Caterpillar

Scientific name: Opisina arenosella Meyrick

(Lepidoptera; Xyloryctideae)

2. Distribution: Throughout the peninsular India.

3. Appearance:

  1. a.Eggs: About 125 scale-like eggs in small batches on the underside of the tips of old leaves. 

  2. b.Larvae: Greenish brown with dark brown head and prothorax, and a reddish mesothorax. The larval period lasts for about 40 days. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): The moth is small greyish white in colour, 10-15 mm in length and 20-25 mm across wings. 

4. Nature of injury: The larvae feed on green matter and simultaneously construct a silken gallery, in which it feeds. As a result of numerous galleries made by the caterpillars while feeding, the foliage dries up.

5. Symptom of damage: The infested trees can be recognized from the dried-up patches in the fronds. In severe cases the whole plantation presents a burnt up appearance from a distance.

7. Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The freshly laid eggs are creamy white and turn pink on the completion of embryonic development. The incubation period lasts about 3-5 days in the summer and 10 days in the winter. The young larva is about 1.5 mm in length and grows up to 15 mm and pale grey in colour with black head. The larvae feed on green matter and simultaneously construct a silken gallery, in which it feeds. As a result of numerous galleries made by the caterpillars while feeding, the foliage dries up.

9. Management: Promptly cutting and burning the first-attacked fronds.

Release of parasites such as Bracon brevicornis, Elasmus nephantidis, Trichospilus pupivora.
