1.

English name: The spotted Boll-worms

Scientific name: Earis insulana (Boisduva) and E. vitella (Fabricius) or E. fabia (Stoll)

(Lepidoptera; Noctuidae)

2.

Distribution: India, North Africa, Pakistan and other tropical countries.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Crown shaped, sculptured and deep sky blue in colour. 

  2. b.Larvae: The full-grown caterpillars are 20 mm long, dull green and have tiny stout bristles and a series of longitudinal black spots on the body. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): E. insulana months are completely green and measures about 25mm across the wings. E. vitella moths are of the same size and have a narrow light longitudinal green band in the middle of the fore wing. 

4.

Nature of injury: When the cotton plants are young, the larvae bore into the terminal portions of the shoots, which wither away and dry up.

5.

Symptom of damage: Young plants wither away and dry up. Later on, they cause 30-40 percent shedding of the fruiting bodies. The infested bolls open prematurely and produce poor lint, the lint stem attacked bolls will not be clean.

6.

Alternate hosts: Bhendi (Hibiscus esculantus), hollyhock, H. cannabinus etc.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The pest breed practically throughout the year but during the winter only the pupae are found hiding in plant debris. The moths that appear in April live for 8-22 days and lay 200-400 eggs at night, singly on flower-buds, brackets and tender leaves of okra or cotton plants. The hairy parts of the plants are preferred for oviposition. In warm weather, the eggs hatch in 3-4 days and the caterpillar s pass through six stages, in 3-4 days and the caterpillar s pass through six stages, becoming full-grown in 11-16 days. They pupate either on the plants or on the ground among fallen leaves and the moths emerge in 4-9 days. The life cycle is completed in 17-29 days during the summer. In the winter, eggs hatch in about one week and the pupal stage is greatly prolonged, occupying 6-12 weeks. Several overlapping generations are completed in a year. The roots of cotton plants sprouting in early spring and the fruits of okra left neglected in the field are the two important sources of early infestation and multiplication of this pest.

8.

Other species with similar biology: Earias fabia, S.

9.

Management: Removal and destruction of shoots and buds attacked by spotted bollworm. Avoiding cultivation of bhendi (Hibiscus esculantus), which is an alternative host of spotted bollworm, in the off-season. The fumigation of sowing seed with carbon disulphide or methyl bromide against pink bollworm. Early removal of cotton stalks at the end of the season to prevent continuous breeding of the pests.
