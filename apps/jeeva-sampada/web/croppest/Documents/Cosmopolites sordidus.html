1.

English name: The Banana Weevil or The rhizome weevil

Scientific name: Cosmopolites sordidus Germere (Coleoptera; Curculionidae)

2.

Distribution: India, South East Asia, Australia, Hawaii Islands, Tropical and south Africa and tropical America.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: The egg is elongate oval, about 1/12 inch long and pure white. 

  2. b.Grubs: About 12 mm long, creamy white, stout, fleshy, legless grub with the body distinctly curved and swollen in the middle. The mandibles are dark brown and well developed. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Adult weevils are about ½ inch long and, hard-shelled and have a pronounced snout. The newly emerged adult is reddish brown but soon becomes uniformly black in its moist habitat, the banana corm. Elytra is curved. 

4.

Nature of injury: The seedlings are killed as a result of larva bore corm tissue and approach the growing point. The tunnelled corms some times break, but since the weevils do not attack the roots they do not cause toppling over.

5.

Symptom of damage: Central growing shoot dries up, leaves wither and drop.  Young infested plants turn yellow and ultimately die.  Heavily infested plants are readily recognized with more ‘blown down’ than the healthy ones. In limited infestation, detection is difficult.  Backward plants or plants showing premature withering of the leaves should be suspected and their corms from harvested should also be inspected because they are a favourite harbourage of weevils.  Adults feed on the plants from April to October

6.

Alternate hosts: Nil. This pest is very specific to Musa genus.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The female weevil bites a small hole in the corm, lays a single egg and continues this process throughout the year. A female can lay 10-50 eggs during its life of few a months. The larvae emerge from the eggs in about a week and bore into the corm, where they feed, making a tunnel. When full-grown in 2-6 weeks, these larvae pupate in the same tunnel. The pupal stage lasts about a week. The adults, on emergence, remain in the soil for some time feeding on the underground parts of the plants. Later on they visit the growing - point for oviposition.

8.

Other species with similar biology: Odoiporus longicollis

9.

Management:

Infested plants should be dug and destroyed

Collect the rhizomes from the healthy gardens.

 Less susceptible varieties can be chosen.

Treatment of rhizomes take out the roots and top most layer, dip in dung slurry and sprinkle 8-10 gm of

Do not continue the crop for more than 2 years.

Examine the rhizomes for any kind of infestation.

Spray the crop with 10 ml of dimethoate (30 EC) per 10 lt of water

Apply granular insecticides like carbofuran or phorate @ 1 Kg/ ha

Water suckers and pseudostems from which banana bunches have been harvested should be removed regularly.
