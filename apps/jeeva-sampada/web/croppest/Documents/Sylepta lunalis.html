1.

English name: The Grapevine Leaf-roller

Scientific name: Sylepta lunalis Guenee

(Lepidoptera; Pyralididae)

2.

Distribution: Southern India and Punjab.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Creamy white oval. 

  2. b.Larvae: Green in colour. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Dirty brown in colour. 

4.

Nature of injury: Only caterpillars cause damage. The caterpillars (1-3 instars) feed on the lower epidermis of the leaves and skeletonize them. The grown up larvae (4th and 5th instars) roll up the leaf margins towards the mid-rib, there being one caterpillar in each roll.

5.

Symptom of damage: Skeletonized leaves.

6.

Alternate hosts: Different species of family Vitaceae.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The caterpillar is green in colour and measure 26 mm in length. The adults are dirty brown with white spots present on the fore and hind wings. The wingspan of the male moth is 22-24 mm., whereas that of the female is 25-28 mm. The abdomen is light brown, the terminal segment being dark brown. The body is covered hairs and has a brownish-black head. The pest is active from August to October. The Moths lay creamy white oval (1.4 mm across) eggs on the lower surface of the leaves. The number of eggs laid by a female varies from 98 - 120, the oviposition period being 1-3 days and the oviposition period 2-3 days. The eggs hatch in 2-3 days. The first instar larva is tiny, dirty green and measures 3.5 mm in length.  There are five larval instars and pupation takes place inside the leaf rolls. Later on the pupa falls down to the ground on fallen leaves and debris. The pupal stage lasts 6-7 days. Peak incidence of attack especially during August.
