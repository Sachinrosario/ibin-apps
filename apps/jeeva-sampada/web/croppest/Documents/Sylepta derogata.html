1.

English name: Cotton Leaf-roller

Scientific name: Sylepta derogata Fabricius

(Lepidoptera; Pyraustidae)

2.

Distribution: Widely distributed in the Orient and Africa. In India cotton growing belts.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Smooth, flat and pale white in colour. 

  2. b.Larvae: The caterpillars measure about 25-30 mm in length when full grown. The larva is glistering green in colour with dark brown head and prothorax. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Moths are yellowish white with black and brown spots on the head and the thorax, and they measure, about 28-40 mm across the spread wings. There are series of dark-brown wavy lines on the wings. 

4.

Nature of injury: The larvae feed on green matter by scraping the cotton leaves and in years of serious outbreaks, cotton plants may be completely defoliated.

5.

Symptom of damage: Defoliation, rolling of the leaves.

6.

Alternate hosts: Okra, Althaea officinalis, Abutilon indicum and some forest plants.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The leaf roller is active from March - October and passes the winter as a full-grown caterpillar among plant debris or in the soil. The hibernating larvae pupate by the end of February and the moths emerge during March. The moths are active at night when they mate and lay 200-300 eggs singly on the under side of the leaves. The eggs hatch in 2-6 days and the young caterpillars at first feed on the lower surface of the leaves. The larvae grow through seven stages and are full-fed in 15-35 days. They pupate either on the plant, in side the rolled leaves, or among plant debris in the soil. They emerge as moths in 6-12 days and they live for about a week. The life cycle is completed in 23-53 days and the pest posses through 5 or 6 generations.

Management:

Systematic handpicking of rolled leaves in the beginning of the season helps in reducing the pest population.

Infested fields should be irrigated and ploughed with furrow turning plough any time during November to February with a view to bury down the hibernating caterpillars.

Insect resistant varieties Viz., 10 F should be encouraged.

Avoid excessive use of fertilizers containing nitrogen.

Bio control agents viz., Trichogramma sp. (egg parasite); Elasmus indicus, Brachymeri tachardidae Cam., Microbracon lefroyi D. (larval parasite) and Xanthopimpla sp. (Pupal parasite) should be encouraged.
