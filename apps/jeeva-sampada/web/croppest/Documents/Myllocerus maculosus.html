1.

English name: The Cotton Grey Weevil / The ash weevil

Scientific name: Myllocerus maculosus Desbrocher

(Coleoptera; Curculionidae)

2.

Distribution: Throughout India.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Ovoid light yellow. 

  2. b.Larvae: The grubs are white, leg less, cylindrical and are about 8 mm in length. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): The weevils with greyish, white elytra with dark lines and are 3-6 mm long. 

4.

Nature of injury: Both the adults and grubs cause damage. The grubs feed underground on the roots of cotton seedlings and destroy them. One grub can destroy 9 seedlings in 40 days. The adults feed on leaves, buds, flowers and young bolls.

5.

Symptom of damage: Wilting and dry up of seedlings.

6.

Alternate hosts: Ragi, Bajra, Maize, Sorghum, Guava, Arhar, Groundnut etc.,

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The pest is active from April to November and passes winter in the adult stage, hidden in debris. The weevils appear in April-may and lay ovoid light yellow eggs in the soil. A female lays on an average 360 eggs over a period of 24 days. The eggs hatch in 3-5 days in May-September and the young grubs feed on the roots of cotton and other plants. They pupate in soil inside earthen cells, forming creamy-white pupae, which change into adults in about one week. The adults live for 8-11 days in the summer and 4-5 months in the winter. During the active period, the life cycle is completed in 6-8 weeks and pest probably breeds 3-4 times in a year.
