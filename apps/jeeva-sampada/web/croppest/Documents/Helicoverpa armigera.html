English name: Cotton boll worm -  Helicoverpa armigera. F

Helicoverpa in Red gram

 

Nature of injury: The young caterpillars feed on the tender parts of the plant. Later it bores in to the pods by thrusting its head in to the hole with the rest of its body outside.

 

Symptoms: Dropping of flowerbuds/flowers with circular hole on calyx pods with holes.

 

Management:

Cultural methods:

  1. 1.Deep ploughing of the field immediately after harvest/ before sowing to exposes pupae to sunlight or birds 

  2. 2.Growing of early maturing open flowering varieties that escape the pest attack 

  3. 3.Sow the crop before 15th July to avoid pest load  

  4. 4.Apply recommended dose of N fertilizer. 

  5. 5.Crop rotation with non hosts is recommended.   

  6. 6.Outer cropping reduces the pest infestation. 

 

Mechanical control:

  1. 1.Setting up of bird perches  

  2. 2.Hand picking of grown up caterpillars and destroy them completely 

  3. 3.Set up pheromone traps- 6-8 traps/ha ie., prior to flower bud formation. Change the lures once in 15 days  

 

Biological control:

  1. 1.Spray HaNPV @ 250 LE/ha, mix in 750-1000 lit of water and spray 

  2. 2.Spray NSKE 5%  

 

Chemical control:

1. Spray application of endosulfan 35 EC @ 0.07 % or quinalphos 25 EC  0.05 % or chlorpyriphos20 EC @ 0.04 % or dust with malathion 5 @ 20-25 kgs/ha.

 

 

Helicoverpa in Cotton

Appearance:

  1. a.Eggs: Shining greenish yellow eggs, spherical shape and beautifully sculptured are laid singly on the tender parts of the food plant. 

  2. b.Larvae: Caterpillar is the larva of a stout built yellowish brown moth.  

 

Nature of injury: The young caterpillars feed on the tender parts of the plant. Later it bores in to the pod and cause serious damage.

Symptom of damage: Premature opening of bolls

 

Alternate hosts: Redgram, Bengalgram, Groundnut, Sorghum, Tomato, Opium, Peas, Ganja etc.

 

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: Full-grown caterpillar is elongated and cylindrical growing to about an inch and half in length and green in colour. Pupation takes place in an earthen cocoon in the soil near the plant; pupa is dark brown in colour with a sharp spine at the anal extremity. Peak incidence of attack is seen in the winter months.

 

 

Management:

Deep ploughing before taking up the crop and immediately after the harvest.

Because in the field may harbour larvae and even pupa found in soil.

Shredding of cotton stem farmers pull out the plant and use as fuel source- serve for larval emergence. So shred plant at once and incorporate in soil.

Sowing tolerant varieties in endemic areas.

Seed treatment with H2SO4- 80-120 ml / Kg of seed for 6-10 minutes.

Early sowing sow during early May.

Harvested fruits kept in field accumulated covered mosquito net so emerging larva will not escape; but if some natural parasite are there, it comes out.

Destroy weeds viz., Sida sp, Hibiscus sps, etc.

Installing pheromone traps at 5-8 / hectare.

Effective threshold level: 10 male moths / hectare – start control measures.

Natural enemies: Trichogsaroma evanescens

Chrysoperla sps.

NPV: at 200 –250 larval equivalent / hectare.

Minimise use of pyrithroids as they developed resistance.

Chemicals: Methomyl 24L – 0.08 to 0.1 % 4.12 ml / Litre.

Carbaryl 50WP – 0.5 % - 10 g / Litre.

Collect all dropped squares and bolls and destroy.

 

 

 

Helicoverpa in Sorhgum crops

Nature of injury: The young caterpillars feed on the tender parts of the plant. Later larva feeds on the grains and damaged ears could be easily spotted in the field by their chalky appearance.

 

Symptom of damage: The larva infests the sorghum head where it feeds on the developing grain, accumulation of broken grain and excretory pellets on the ground of infested plant.

 

Management:

1. Deep ploughing before taking up the crop and immediately after the harvest to expose the pupae.

2. Sowing of tolerant varieties in endemic areas: Open earhead types fairly tolerant for the pest compared to compact earhead type.

3. Early sowing (during May)  helps in reducing the pest population effectively.

4. Harvesting and threshing of the crop is preferred in pest infested areas.

5. Installing pheromone traps at 5-8 /ha

6. Effective threshold level: if 10 male moths/ha is noticed then go for control measures.

7. Natural enemies: Trichogramma chelonis, Chrysoperla sps. also help in reducing the pest population. The parasitoids can be obtained and released at recommended dosage depending on the strain

8. Dust the earheads with malathion 5D, endosulfan 4D or carbaryl 10D at 20-25 kg/ha.
