1.

English name: The Dusky Cotton Bug

Scientific name: Oxycarenus hyalinipennis Costa

(Hemiptera; Lygaeidae)

2.

Distribution: India

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Yellowish and cigar shaped. 

  2. b.Nymph: The young nymphs have a round abdomen and as they grow older they resemble the adults except for being smaller and having prominent wing pads in place of the wings. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): The adults are 4-5 mm in length, greyish brown and have dirty white transparent wings. 

4.

Nature of injury: Nymphs and adults suck the sap from the immature seeds, which may not ripen, may lose colour and get crushed in the ginning-factories.

5.

Symptom of damage: Staining the lint and lowering its market value.

6.

Alternate hosts: Okra, hollyhock and other malvaceous weeds.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The insect is active practically throughout the year, but during the winter, only adults are found in the unginned cotton. The cigar shaped eggs are laid in the spring on Hibiscus and then on okra and finally on cotton during the monsoon. Initially, they are whitish, then turn pale and finally become light pink before hatching. The eggs are laid in the lint of half opened bolls, either singly or in small clusters of 3 to 18 each. The egg stage lasts 5-10 days and the nymphs, on emerging, pass through seven stages, completing the development in 31-40 days. The life cycle lasts 36-50 days and the numbers of generations are completed in a year.

8.

Other species with similar biology: Oxycarenus lactus Kirby.

9.

Management:

At time of boll opening, spray crop with carbaryl – 0.2 %

Quinolphos- 0.05 %; Endosulfan 35EC 0.07 %.
