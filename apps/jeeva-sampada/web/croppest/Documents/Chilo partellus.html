The maize stem borer - Chilo partellus  (Lepidoptera; Crambidae)

 

Nature of injury: Young larvae of maize stem borer scrape the green matter from the leaves by nibbling, and cause pinprick like holes arranged in parallel lines on leaf blades.  Later, they eat their way into the central shoot when plants are young and cause formation of dead hearts.  They form tunnels inside the stalk and cut circular exit holes.  

 

Symptom of damage: In later stages, they eat their way into the central shoot when plants are young and cause formation of dead hearts. Red mining in the midrib, presence of ‘holes’ in the leaves, bore hole at the base of the stem are common symptoms of damage.  

 

Management:

  • Remove all the grasses particularly wild sorghum as it serves as an alternate host for its perpetuation.  

  • Remove “deadhearts” affected plants and destroy them in order to kill the pest 

  • Set up light traps till midnight to attract and kill the moths.  

  • Ploughing up the infested fields during winter and burning the stubble can suppress the pest. 

  • Collect the stubbles after harvest and burn them to destroy diapausing stage 

  • Mix 8 kg of quinalphos 5 G or carbaryl 10 G with sand t make up the total quantity to 20 kg and apply in the leaf whorls. 

  •  Spray endosulfan 35EC at 0.07 %  or Dust endosulfan 4 D @ 20 kg/ha
