4.

Nature of injury: Both the nymphs and adults suck up the plant sap from the under surface of leaves. Vigour of the plants is impaired to a great extent.

5.

Symptom of damage: Leaves show symptoms of ‘hopper burn’ such as yellowing, curling bronzing and sometimes drying up and these symptoms are expressed differently depending on how the different varieties react to the toxic saliva of the insect.

6.

Alternate hosts: Brinjal, Potato, Lady’s Finger, Potato, Sunflower etc.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The female leafhopper inserts about 15 eggs inside leaf veins and the incubation period ranges from 4-11 days. The nymphal period occupies 7-21 days depending on the weather conditions.

Management: Spray application of Methyl parathion 0.025 %, dimethoate 0.03 %, phosalone 0.05 %, Monocrotophos 0.04 %, Phenthoate 0.05% etc. affords protection.
