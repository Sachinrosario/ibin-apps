1.

English name: The Grapevine Thrips

Scientific name: Rhipiphorothrips cruentatus Hood

(Thysanoptera; Heliothripidae)

2.

Distribution: Wherever grapes are grown in India.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Dirty white, bean shaped and can be spotted as little bright specks when a leaf is held against a strong light. 

  2. b.Larvae: Reddish active creatures, which become yellowish-brown, as they grow older. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): The adults are minute, being 1.4 mm long, blackish brown, with yellowish wings. 

4.

Nature of injury: Both adults and the nymphs cause damage by sucking cell sap from the leaves.

5.

Symptom of damage: Plants suffer owing to the constant feeding of a large number of insects. The attacked leaves show a whitish hue at first, acquiring a withered appearance and then turning brown. The leaves ultimately curl up and drop off prematurely. Fruits developed will be of poor quality.

6.

Alternate hosts: Rose, Jaman (Sizygium cuminii  Skeels).

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The pest breeds throughout the year, except in winter when it is found as pupa in the soil at a depth of 8-18 cm under the host plants. The adults appear in march and lay eggs on the under surface of the leaves by making small slits in the plant tissues, placing one egg in one slit. A female on an average, lays 50 eggs which are dirty white, bean-shaped and can be spotted as little bright specks when a leaf is held against a source of strong light. The eggs hatch in 3-8 days and the nymphs appear as reddish active creatures, which become yellowish brown, as they grow older. They feed on the underside of the leaves by rasping the surface and sucking the cell sap that oozes. They are full-fed in 9-20 days and during the season of active breeding they pupate of the leaves. The pupae possess power of locomotion and crawl away when disturbed. In 2-5 days they change into adults, which also feed like the nymphs. The females can reproduce with or without fertilization, the fertilized eggs giving rise to females and unfertilised ones to males. The insect hibernates as pupa in the soil from December to March and during the active period many generations are completed.
