1.

English name: Banana aphid

Scientific name: Pentalonia nigronervosa Coq. (Hemiptera: Aphididae)

2.

Distribution: India, Sri Lanka, Philippines, Pacific Islands, Australia, West Indies, Central and South America, Mauritius, Sierra Leone, Egypt.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Parthenogenetic development  

  2. b.Adults (Male / Female): Alate adults have very prominent dark brown to black wing venation: these alate adults are usually produced after 7-10 generations and they migrate to new host plants. 

4.

Nature of injury: This pest is important as the vector of bunchy top virus and cardamom mosaic (‘Katte’) disease.

5.

Symptom of damage: Bunchy tops.       

6.

Alternate hosts: Alpina purpurata, Heliconia sp., Taro, Tomato, Torch ginger.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: Found on the banana plants by pulling back leaf sheaths around the base of the pseudostem, when colonies of brown shiny wingless aphids can be seen, always accompanied by ants which play an important role in virus transmission. It does not feed on the banana fruit itself, but on young shoots and suckers. It is most often found at ground level on the base of the plant.

Young ones are given birth by alate and apterous females by parthenogenetic development. An aphid is capable of producing 32 to 50 nymphs in its lifetime.

9.

Management:

Destroy bunchy top affected plants.

Spray application of Phosphomidon 0.05 %, oxy demeton 50 EC, dimethoate 30 EC or methyl demeton may be useful.

Destroy alternate hosts large cardamom and colocasia.

Ratoon and intercrops which act as additional hosts and facilitate aphid population build up, should not be taken.
