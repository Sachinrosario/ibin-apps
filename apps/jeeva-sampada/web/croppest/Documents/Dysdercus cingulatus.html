1.

English name: The Red Cotton Bug.

Scientific name: Dysdercus cingulatus (Fabricius)

(Hemiptera; Pyrrhocoridae)

2.

Distribution: Widely distributed in India.

3.

Appearance: The bugs are elongated slender insects, crimson red with white bands across the abdomen. The membranous portion of their forewings, antennae and scutellum are black.

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Spherical, yellow eggs in soil in a loose mass. 

  2. b.Nymph: The newly hatched nymphs are light and in all the stages provided with a central row of black spots and row of white ones on each side. They feed often gregariously on fully exposed bolls and consequently quite conspicuous. The nymph passes through five moults and the nymphal period is 42-49 days. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Medium sized reddish bug having white bands on the abdomen and black markings on wings. 

4.

Nature of injury: Both adults and the nymphs feed on cell sap of cotton. The bugs suck cell sap from leaves and green bolls of cotton.

5.

Symptom of damage: Heavily attacked bolls open badly and the lint is of poorer quality. The seed produced may also have low generation and less oil. The bugs also stain the lint with their excreta or body juices as they get crushed in the ginning factories.

6.

Alternate hosts: Hollyhock, Wheat, Maize, Pearl Millet, Clovers etc.,.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The insects are active throughout the year, and passes winter in the adult stage. In the spring, the bug becomes active and lays, on an average, 100-130 eggs in moist soil or in crevices in the ground. The eggs are spherical and bright yellow and are laid in clusters or in loose irregular masses of 70-80 eggs each. They hatch in 7-8 days and a young nymph has a flabby abdomen, but as they grow older, they become more slender and develop black markings on the body. There are 5 nymphal stages and the development is completed in 49-89 days. In summer, the life of an adult is very variable, but in winter it may live up to 3 months.

Management:

Since the pest feeds gregariously, the infested leaves or bolls can be shaken in water and drowned.

Plough the fields to expose the eggs.
