1.

English name: The bud Worm

Scientific name: Phycita infusella Meyrick

(Lepidoptera; Phycitidae)

2.

Distribution: Throughout India.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Small rounded and hatch in about 7 days. 

  2. b.Larvae: In the caterpillar stage, it makes characteristic webbings around the terminal young growth of the leaves where it feeds. The full-grown caterpillars are 13-16 mm long, are greenish, having black head and with faint brown stripes on the rest of the body. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): The adult is greyish- yellow moth, speckled with fuscous, and measures 20 mm in wing expanse. The front wings are faint yellow at the base with red wavy lines, whereas the hind wings are white with brown marginal lines. 

4.

Nature of injury: Webbing of leaves and feeding by the caterpillars check further growth of the plants.

5.

Symptom of damage: Webbing of leaves. The attacked leaves wither away, dry up and drop off. The flowering and the fruiting are very poor and reduction in yield is considerable.

6.

Alternate hosts: Okra and other allied crops.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The pest is active from April to October and passes winter as a hibernating caterpillar in debris on the ground. The moths appear in April and lay small rounded eggs on tender leaves of cotton or other host plants. The eggs hatch in about 7 days and the young larvae start feeding on buds or young leaves. As the larvae grow, they web together the terminal leaves with silken threads and feed inside. They are full fed in 21 days and make silken cocoons in the midst of twisted leaves. The pupal stage lasts 11 days and the moths on emergence live for a few days only. The life cycle is completed in 5 to 6 weeks and the pest completes 4-5 generations in a year.

Management:

Spraying chemical pesticides when caterpillars are young and vulnerable. Since larvae well be protected within the webbed leaves, they are hard to be hit by any insecticide.
