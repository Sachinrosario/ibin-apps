1.

English name: The spotted boll worm

Scientific name: Earias fabia

2.

Distribution: All over India.

3.

Appearance:

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs:  Sculptured shining bluish eggs are laid singly on the tender parts of the plant. 

  2. b.Larvae: Full grown larva is about two third s of an inch in length and at this stage it generally comes out of burrow. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): Upper wing is pale white with a broad greenish band. 

4.

Nature of injury: Larva bores down the top shoot.

5.

Symptom of damage: Drooping and fading of top shoots. Attacked bolls show holes generally plugged with excreta.

6.

Alternate hosts: Lady’s finger, hollyhock, Hibiscus cannabinus. Etc.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: Full grown larva is about two thirds of an inch in length and at this stage it generally comes out of burrow, and selecting a suitable spot on the plant and prepares a boat shaped cocoon of dirty brown silk and changes in to the pupa inside the same. The cocoon is found on the surface of bolls, bracts and occasionally in cracks in the soil near the plant. One life cycle takes about 25 days.

8.

Other species with similar biology: Earias insulana (vittella)

9.

Management:

Removal and destruction of shoots and buds attacked by spotted bollworm. Avoiding cultivation bhendi (Hibiscus esculantus), which is an alternative host of spotted bollworm, in the off-season. The fumigation of sowing seed with carbon bisulphide or methyl bromide against pink bollworm. Early removal of cotton stalks at the end of the season to prevent continuous breeding of the pest.
