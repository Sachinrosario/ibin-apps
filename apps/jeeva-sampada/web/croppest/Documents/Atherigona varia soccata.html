Sorghum shoot fly - Atherigona varia  soccata (Anthomyidae; Diptera)

 

Nature of injury: The pest attacks and feeds on the young seedlings when it is in the six-leaf stage.  Maggots migrate to dorsal surface get into leaf sheath, move down and bore into stem portion and cut off the growing shoot.  

 

Symptom of damage:  as a result of larval feeding the central leaf wilts and later dries up, giving the typical ‘Deadheart’ symptom.  The ‘Deadheart’ can be easily pulled out and at the base, emits a bad smell.  The young whitish yellow maggot feeds only on the decaying tissue. Normally the damage occurs from 1 week to about 1 month crop..  If the attack occurs a little later, plants may produce side tillers that may also be attacked.  Late sowing during the rainy season the crop subject to attack severely.

 

Management:

1. The more promising control measures are the adjustment of sowing dates and high seed rate.  Sorghum should be sown as early as possible after the onset of monsoon, i.e. before the end of June as late sown crop suffers heavily.

2. Remove and destroy the shootfly infested seedlings.

3. Use of higher yielding shoot fly resistant cultivars like M35 - 1, CSH-7, 8.

4. Setting up of fish meal traps @ 5 traps per acre from the first week of germination reduces incidence

5. In extremely high level of incidence Spraying endosulfan 35 EC at 0.07%, 15-20 days after sowing.
