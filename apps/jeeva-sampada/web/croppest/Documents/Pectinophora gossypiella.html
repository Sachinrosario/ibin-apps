1.

English name: The Pink Boll-Worm of Cotton

Scientific name: Pectinophora gossypiella Saunders or Platyedra gossypiella Saunders.

(Lepidoptera; Gelechiidae)

2.

Distribution: Africa, America, Australia and Asia.

3.

Appearance: They are pink and are found inside the flower buds, the panicles and the bolls of cotton or the fruits of okra and other allied plants.

  1. a. 

  1. a.Eggs: Whitish flat striated eggs found singly on the underside of the young leaves, on new shoots, on flower buds and on the young green bolls. 

  2. b.Larvae: On emergence are white at first, but turn pink, as they grow older. 

  3. c.Adults (Male / Female): In the adult stage, the insect is a deep-brown moth, measuring 8-9 mm across the spread wings. There are blackish spots on the fore wings and the margins of the hind wings are fringed. 

4.

Nature of injury: Damage is caused in various ways. Enters through developing boll, owing to the attack of this pest, there is excessive shedding of the fruiting bodies. The entrance holes get closed up as the boll matures. It feeds on seeds and moves to adjacent locule by making a hole through the septum.

5.

Symptom of damage: Excessive shedding of fruiting bodies. The infected flowers buds shed prematurely. A typical rosette shaped bloom examined will contain the larvae, the infestation results in the seeds being destroyed. In addition to retardation of lint development and weakened lint and staining of the lint both inside the boll and in the gin. Infected bolls open prematurely and expose it to invasion by sporadic fungi.

6.

Alternate hosts: Fruits of okra and other allied plants, hollyhock etc.

7.

Biology / Ecology / Behaviour: The yearly life cycle begins with the emergence of moths in the summer.  The emergence takes place at two distinct times: in May-June and in July-August. The females lay whitish, flat eggs singly on the underside of the young leaves, on new shoots, on flower buds and on the young green bolls. The eggs hatch in one week and the caterpillars, on emergence, are white at first, but turn pink, as they grow older. Soon after emergence, the larvae enter the flower buds, the flowers, or the bolls. The holes of entry close down, but the larvae continue feeding inside, on the seed kernels. They become full-grown in about two weeks and come out of the holes for pupation on the ground among fallen leaves, debris, etc. within one week, the moths emerge to start the life cycle all over again. By October-November, four to six generations are completed.

Management:

Destruction of off-season cotton sprouts and the alternative host plants, or the burning of the debris from the cotton fields. Deep ploughing with a furrow tunnelling plough by the end of the February is also helpful in reducing the carry over of this pest to the next season.

 

The fumigation of sowing seed with carbon disulphide or methyl bromide against pink bollworm.

Periodical spray application of carbaryl 50 WP @ 2.5 kg / ha

Monocrotophos @ 1.25 kg / ha
