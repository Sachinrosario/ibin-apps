 Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
A tree attaining in the Eastern Himalayas a height of 60 m and also in Afghanistan to Bhutan 2800-4000 m. It is generally found in forest areas the Himalayas, Nepal, Sikkim, Bhutan, kashmir, Afghanistan (Hindu Kush), Tibet; from 1800 to 4200 m; introduced in Europe in 1822. It is rustic, but suffers sometimes from the cold in the north of France; fears the late frosts and the dry summers; all especially wet grounds; slow growth at the rather fast beginning then;
Biotic Zone: Himalaya.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES :
An evergreen, pyramidal tree growing to 50 m or more at a slow rate , upper branches horizotal; bark dark grey, deeply grooved. Leaves linear, to 4 cm, flattened, with incurved margin, apex rounded and notched; young branchlets with hairy grooves. Crown broadly conical. Branches horizontally spreading. Bark dark gray, rough and scaling in scraps; large buds of 1 cm, globulous, brown, bleached by resin; furrowed, brown dark, a little pubescent branches, Shoots red-brown, deeply grooved, pubescent in the grooves. Buds large, globose, resinous. Needles on the upper side of the shoot arranged in several ranks, leaving a V-shaped depression between them, 2-6 cm long, with emarginate apex; upper surface dark green and glossy, with 2 broad stomata bands beneath. Cones cylindrical, 14-20 cm long and about 7 cm thick, violet-purple when young, later brown; seed scales 1.5-2 cm wide; bract scales concealed. Wood is soft, white. The inner zone of each annual ring is soft and spongy; weight is 29 lb per cubic foot.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY

Breeding System :

Phenology :
The flowering occurs during April - May. The scented flowers are monoecious (individual flowers are either male or female, but both sexes can be found on the same plant) and are pollinated by wind and the seeds ripen from October to November. Fruits will be cones from 12 to 18 cm (24 cm reach) out of 7 broad; coniferous tree; cylindrical; initially purple crimson then brown with maturity; 1,5-2 cm broad scales; bractées included; granulates of 1 cm.

Pollinators:
Pollination occurs due to wind.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination : 
The seed remains viable for up to 5 years if it is well stored. Germination is often poor, usually taking about 6 - 8 weeks.

Cultivation:
The plant prefers light (sandy), medium (loamy) and heavy (clay) soils and can grow in heavy clay soil. The plant prefers acid and neutral soils. It can grow in full shade (deep woodland) semi-shade (light woodland) or no shade. It requires moist soil.
Cultivation: Seed - sow early February in a greenhouse or outdoors in March. Germination is often poor, usually taking about 6 - 8 weeks. Stratification is said to produce a more even germination so it is probably best to sow the seed in a cold frame as soon as it is ripe in the autumn. The seed remains viable for up to 5 years if it is well stored. When large enough to handle, prick the seedlings out into individual pots and grow them on for at least their first winter in pots. Plant them out into their permanent positions in late spring or early summer, after the last expected frosts.(2 ) Alternatively, if you have sufficient seed, it is possible to sow in an outdoor seedbed. One report says that it is best to grow the seedlings on in the shade at a density of about 550 plants per square meter , whilst another report says that they are best grown on in a sunny position.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Leaves,Bark,Wood.

Uses:

  1. Resin mixed with oil of roses is used externally for headache and neuralgia.
  2. Infusion is used in the treatment of hoaeseness and coughs.
  3. Juice of the fresh leaves is used as a family medicine in fever which acts as antiperidic for infants dose 5-10 drops in water or in mother's milk.
  4. In Bengal it is given as a tonic after parturition.
  5. In Jhelum, twigs and leaves are used as fodder and litter for cattle.

Other Uses :
  1. The leaves are astringent, carminative, expectorant, stomachic and tonic. They are used in the treatment of asthma, bronchitis etc.
