Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
Picrorhiza is a creeping plant native to the mountains of India, Nepal, Tibet and Pakistan. Also known as Kharbagehindi, found in the Himalayan region growing at elevations of 3,000-5,000 meters.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
The plant is a perennial woody herb with greyish brown, cylindrical, irregularly curved roots - 5-10 cms long. It consists of long leaves and five-lobed, flat, oval, and sharply serrated, The leaves are 5-10 cms long with rounded tip, spatulate and serrate. The flowers, which are pale blue or reddish-blue in color, white or pale purple and borne on a tall spike. The largest part of the plant is the rhizome, which can grow as long as 10 inches. The rhizome is used medicinally. manual harvesting of the plant takes place October through December. Kutki prefers a moist habitat and sandy loam soil.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
At the beginning of October, the plant turns yellowish and becomes ready for pereniation. After December, it remains under snow and at the beginning of summer when the snow melts, the plant regenerates from the dormant part. The natural regeneration takes place by seeds and rhizomes. The regeneration of this species is better in the shady and moist areas instead of open areas. The plant may be cultivated at higher altitudes in the Himalayas by both seeds and rhizome cuttings. Rhizome cultivation is considered faster than seed cultivation. The plant easily regenerates from the underground propagules left during harvesting.

Phenology:
Flowering occurs during June-August and fruiting occurs from September onwards.

Harvesting Practices:

Bional Hepavit 40 capsules
A blend of nutrients with milk thistle, Picrorhiza kurroa and artichoke extracts specially formulated to help maintain a healthy functioning of the liver and gall bladder.


Chemistry:
Picrorhiza contains a number of active ingredients, including acetophenone derivatives (which have antiasthmatic properties), iridioids and cucurbitacins. Kutkin is the active principal of Picrorhiza kurroa and is comprised of kutkoside and the iridoid glycoside picrosides I, II, and III. Other identified active constituents are apocynin, drosin, and nine cucurbitacin glycosides.3,4 Apocynin is a catechol that has been shown to inhibit neutrophil oxidative burst in addition to being a powerful anti-inflammatory agent,5 while the curcubitacins have been shown to be highly cytotoxic and possess antitumor effects.
Kutkin, a bitter glycosidal principle, was reported by Rastogi et al. They also isolated D-mannitol, vanillic acid and some steroids.Kutkin was later shown to be a stable mixed crystal of two C-9 iridoid glycosides-Picroside I and Kutakosid. Apocynin has been isolated from the plant. Picroside II has been isolated and shown to have hepatoprotective activity. With the help of preparative HPLC, larger Quantities of picrosides have been isolated, permitting precise structure identification and biological experiments.

Other uses:

  • The bitter rhizomes of picrorhiza have been used for thousands of years in India to treat people with indigestion.
  • It is also used to treat people with constipation due to insufficient digestive secretion and for fever due to all manner of infections.
  • It has been used in the traditional Indian systems of medicine to treat liver troubles and bronchial problems.
  • Androsin, a phenolic glycoside isolated from P. kurroa, has been attributed with anti-asthmatic properties, probably through the inhibition of the platelet activating factor, which is responsible for bronchial obstruction.
  • Its extracts have also been shown to possess anti-allergic activity.
