 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
An errect, perennial, grass like sedge, indigenous to west Asia and Africa, recorded as occuring scattered from Punjab to Nilgiri hills. The plant is cultivated in the Southern parts of Europe for its edible tubers.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers, Roots, Shoots.

Uses

  1. They are cooked as vegetable or roasted, ground to powder and used as a substitute or adultereant for coffee and cocoa. 
  2. An account of its high oil content, chufa flour made out of tubers and mixed with wheat constitutes a high calorie food.
  3. In Spain, the freshly gathered tubers are cleaned, presseda and separated emulsion of juice and oilcooled and consumed as drink.The tubers are also used as feed for swine and cattle.
  4. In North-Western Provinces the root is used as food.
  5. The toasted roots have been used as a substitute for coffee and yield a preparation resembling coffee.
  6. In China the shoots of this plant are used in making huts and mats.

Harvesting Practices
It yields a crop of about 400kg per acre in 4-6 months

Produce and Products
mats

Chemistry
 Analysis of tubers gave the following results: moisture-14.15; oil-25.85; albuminoids-5.21; starch-22.72; digestible carbohydrates-24.79; fibre-5.83; and mineral matter-1.48%. The tubers yield a yellow oil, chufa oil (230-28%), with pleasant taste and odour.

Miscellaneous Information
There are five or six distinct forms of this plant, of which two occur in India, viz., forma tuberosa (sp. Rottb.) in Madras and forma hindustanica in Northern India.
