HABIT & HABITAT
Actinodaphne hookeri (Pisa) is a small evergreen tree found in the warm and moist forests of India. A moderate sized tree found in the evergreen forests of the Western Ghats, in the Konkan and north Kanara. It is commonly met within Matheran and Mahabaleshwar. It is also found on the east-coast forest of Orissa. The genus comprises of 50 species of evergreen trees or shrubs, distributed chiefly in the East Indies, Japan and North America. Nearly 24 species are found in India (1, 2, 3).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
It is a moderate sized tree with spreading branches, attaining a girth of 30 and over. The outer bark is brown, hard and woody. The inner bark is fibrous and greyish. Leaves coriaceous, in 2 whorls of 3 each, 10-18 by 4.5-6.3 cm., penninerved, elliptic.lanceolate, finely acuminate, the mature leaves glabrous above and usually clothed with thin tomentum beneath, the young leaves densely silky with long tawny hairs, base usually acute; main nerves 6-10 pairs; petioles 1.3-2.5 cm. long, silky-pubescent (1, 2, 3, 4).

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers dioecious, yellow from the naked branches, the males in clusters of about 8, the females umbellate or subracemose on very short stout peduncles; pedicels clothed with tawny hairs, those of the female flowers longer than the male. Perianth densely clothed with tawny hairs; segments elliptic or ovate. Stamens 9 in the male flowers, reduced in the female to strap-shaped antherless staminodes of the same number as the male stamens or less; filaments thickly covered with whitish hairs, the interior row of 3 with 2 somewhat reniform glands at the base of each filament, the glands in the male flowers slightly stalked, those of the female sessile. Style rather thick; stigma large, mushroom- shaped, oblique. Berry 8-13 mm. long, ellipsoid, red when ripe, seated on the cup-shaped enlarged perianth-tube (1, 2, 3, 4).

Economic Potential and Uses

The seed contains about 48% of reddish brown oil with iodine value, about 10.9 and is mainly lauric acid oil. Hence, it is an suitable substitute for coconut oil in soap making. The systematic cultivation and proper exploitation of kusum and Pisa may be helpful in reducing the use of coconut oil in soap manufacture. The seed kernelis a much better source of lauric acid than palm kernel oil or coconut oil. Lauric acid finds several industrial application especially for the preparation of sodium laury sulphate, a valuable detergent, in some respect superior to ordinary soap (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6).

Uses

The flowers are particularly valued for honey.

Harvesting Practices
The seeds are small in size and their collection is difficult when they fall on the ground. The branches bearing fruits are cut and the seeds are then plucked off. The Pisa seed is surrounded by pulp with high moisture content. It needs to be depulped and dried immediately to avoid fungal infestation (1, 2, 5).

Other Uses
A cold infusion of the leaves is mucilaginous and is used in urinary disorders and in diabetes. The oil of the seeds is used as an external application to sprains.
