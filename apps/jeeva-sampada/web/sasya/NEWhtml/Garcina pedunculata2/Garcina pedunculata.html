 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Deciduous tree up to 20 m tall;It is found in West Bengal, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur and Meghalaya.
Availability :  
Specimen examined:   West Bengal: s. loc., 13.05.1932, K. Biswas, 463 (CAL); Assam: Harjuli, 23.12.1874, J.L. Lister, 112 (CAL); Darrang- Panbore Camp, Batasipur, Nov., 1952, G.M. Nath, 1117161 (DD); Nagaland: Piphenias, 667 m, 04.03.1882, H. Collett, 194 (CAL).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Trunk fluted with short spreading branches; bark brown or grey, smooth, spongy, exuding gum. Leaves obovate-oblong, 10-40 x 5-15 cm, acute or obtuse at apex, undulate, membranous or sub-coriaceous. Male flower: pale green, c 1 cm in diam., bracts in pair. Sepals 4, concave, fleshy. Petals 4, oblong-ovate. Stamens indefinite. Female flowers: solitary, terminal, similar to male flower, yellow or pale-green, c 2 cm across; staminodes 20-30 in 4 fascicles. Fruit berry, yellowish, fleshy, 7-11 cm in diam. Seeds 8-10, reniform with fleshy aril.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Male Flower: Pale green, in 8-12 flowered panicles, sepals orbicular, fleshy, inner part narrower; petals narrower, but scarcely longer, oblong; stamens 4- angled, stipitate mass, anthers 4-gonous, 2-celled ; Female flower: Solitary larger than male, terminal on thick 4-angled peduncled; stamens 20-30, filaments in 4-bundle; ovary globose, stigmatic rays 8-10, spreading  ; Fruit: Large yellow the size of small melon; Seed: 8-10, large, reniform.

Floral and seed biology:
September – July.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
A new compound – 2,4,6,3’,5’-pentahydroxybenzophenone (fig. 1) isolated from heartwood along with 1,3,5,7-tetrahydroxyxanthone, 1,3,6,7-tetrahydroxyxanthone biflavanone GB-la and talbotaflavanone (fig. 2) (volkensiflavone). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

 A new polyisoprenylated benzophenone – pedunculol (fig. 3) along with garcinol and cambogin isolated from pericarp and structure of new compound elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for constipation, griping stomachache, indigestion (Fruit, Jain and Borthakur, 1980); Edible (Fruit, Arora, 1981).
