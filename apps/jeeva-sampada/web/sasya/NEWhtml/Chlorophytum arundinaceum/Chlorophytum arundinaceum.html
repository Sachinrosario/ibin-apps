 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Herbs; perennial; root tuberous, clustered found to occur in Eastern and Western Ghats, Central to Eastern Himalayas and extended to North-Eastern region ascending up to an elevation of 1200 m. It is also available in Myanmar and Bhutan.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs; perennial; root tuberous, clustered. Stem stout, truncate at base. Leaf at first short with very broad amplexicaul base while in flower, oblanceolate, 15-75 x 2.5-6.0 cm, obtuse or acute to acuminate at apex, narrowed into a petiole, outer distinctly petiolate, inner sub-sessile. Racemes dense; scape as long as leaves, often branched at apex; bracts green, scarious, lanceolate-acuminate, 5-more nerved; pedicels jointed in the midrib. Flower bracteate, 2-many, star-like. Perianth white, tepals 0.9-1.2 cm long, narrowly oblong and obtuse. Anthers about twice as long as the filaments. Style declinate. Fruits 1-3 at each node, globose, ca 8 mm in diam., emarginate at both ends. Seeds 1-6 in each cell, black, suborbicular, compressed.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence:  In lax raceme ; Flower: White, pedicellate, bracts scarious ; Perianth: White, segments 6, free, spreading, 3-5 nerved ;Androecium: Stamens 6 adnate to the base, anthers twice as long as filaments ; Gynoecium:  Ovary 3-locular, ovules 4, style filiform, stigma minute; Fruit: Capsule white, broad 2-lobed at the tip and base, cells 3-4 seeded ; Seed: Sub-orbicular, flat and black. Flowering & Fruiting : June-August. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Tubers constitute the drug commercially known as ‘safed musli’. The drug is considered a valuable nervine and general tonic for strength and vigour. Fried powdered of root is used in apathae of mouth and throat.


Chemistry:
A new galactoglucan isolated from fruits, shown to consist of galactose and glucose units. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Two new compounds – 4-hydroxy-8,11-oxidoheneicosanol and pentacosyl docosanoate – along with stigmasterol, its glucoside, nonacosane and tetracosanoic and triacontanoic acids isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Acid hydrolysate of butanol-soluble fraction from roots found to contain stigmasterol, tigogenin, neogitogenin and tokorogenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

A new bibenzyl glycoside (fig. 1) isolated from roots and characterized as 4,2’,4’,-trihydroxy-2-xylopyranosyl-bibenzyl. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

A new acylated glucoside, arundinoside B, isolated from the roots of Chlorophytum arundinaceum has been characterized as 1-O-(b-D-3-benzoylglucopyranosyl) octacosanol (fig. 2) by spectral and chemical evidence. (Tandon and Shukla, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0532.

Isolation and characterization of a new tokorogenin (fig. 3a) based saponin arundinoside A (fig. 3b) has been described. The saponin structure was established as 1b-O-{b-D-xylopyranosyl (1à3)-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à3)-b-D-glucopyranosyl (1à3)-b-D-glucopyranosyl} –25-R.5-b-spirostan-1b,2b,3a-tri ol (fig. 3c). (Tandon aand Shukla, 1997). MAPA, 1997, 9703, 1831.
