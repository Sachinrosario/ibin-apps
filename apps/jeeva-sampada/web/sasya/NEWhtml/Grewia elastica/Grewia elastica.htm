Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Medium sized deciduous tree found in subtropical regions like East Indian hills and parts of Western Ghats. It is distributed in the sub-Himalayan range from the Indus eastwards to Assam and Meghalaya, ascending 1500 m; also in Indo-Gangetic plain, Bihar, Orissa and Madhya Pradesh. A common associate of sal in the sub-Himalayan tract. Plant of subtropical climate with hot dry summers in its natural distributional range. It can grow on variety of soils, is a light demander and frost and drought hardy. Suitable for field edges. Frost and drought hardy.


Availability :

Largest supply from the south zone – chiefly Coorg and Kanara forests. Moderate supplies from North zone. Small supplies from central zone.


Conservation/ Threat Status :

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Deciduous tree with greyish white bark. Leaves obliquely oblong-ovate or elliptic, acuminate, crenate, serrate. Flowers yellow, in axillary cymes. Drupe globose, dark brown or blackish when ripe.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Fresh leaves appear during April-May when flowers also appear. In central India the flowering is a little earlier than in the north. Ripening of fruit takes place from August to November, which may extend to January in some regions.


Pollinators:


Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by entire transplanting. Seedling growth is moderate to fast. It coppices well. It can be raised by planting stumps or nursery raised seedlings.

15-19 seeds generally weigh a gram. The ripe fruits are depulped to get seeds for nursery sowing, which is done during March-April in well-prepared nursery beds in lines 25 cm apart. The seedlings attain plantable height in 3-4 months. The entire plant with earth ball or after removing the container is planted during July-August in pits (30 cu cm) spaced at 3 m X 3 m. normally the leaves and lower branches are pruned before planting to reduce transpiration. Stumps are prepared from about 15 month old seedlings and planted either in crowbar holes or in pits as stated above.

Miscellaneous Information

Ornamental plant. 

Wood: Sapwood pale yellowish white to yellowish grey, distinct from heartwood which reddish brown to brown with darker coloured streaks. Moderately hard to hard and moderately heavy to heavy. Specific gravity 0.67 – 0.88 air dry, 44-47 lb. /c ft. Dull. Fairly straight grained and medium to medium-coarse-textured.

Quite often it is possible to distinguish wood of G. elastica due to its slightly coarser texture and usually streaky appearance due to denser and darker latewood bands, which show rather prominently on longitudinal surface.

Wood develops surface cracks and end splits, also liable to warp.
