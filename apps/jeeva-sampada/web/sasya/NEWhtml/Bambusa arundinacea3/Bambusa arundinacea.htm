Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

The plant is very common is moist deciduous forests and along streams and water courses, and forms impenetrable thickets, extending over vast areas. Though it prefers moist localities, it is not particular about the soil and tolerates even clarify and inundated soils. The best growth is found on alluvial soils along streams. It is cultivated in the plains of N.W. India and on the hills of Andhra Pradesh, T.N. and Karnataka.
Availability :

Distributed throughout the moist parts of India, upto an altitude of 1,250m, particularly near river bank; cultivated in the plains of N.W. India and on the hills of Andhra Pradesh, T.N. and Karnataka.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

- A raceful, spinous bamboo.
- Rhizomes short, stout, knotty.
- Culms, dense, reaching 24-30m in height and 15-17 cm in diameter, green, hollow, purplish green when young, turning golden yellow, with prominent nodes and long internodes, lower ones rooting, often sub-angular, flexuous.
- Flowers in large panicles, sometimes occupy the whole culms.
- Caryopsis oblong 5-8mm long, grooved on one side.
- Monocarpic.

In India, bamboos are found naturally as an under storey in many types of forest in almost all the states, except Jammu and Kashmir where they are cultivated. Their distribution in India is largely governed by rainfall, temperature, alt. & soil type. They form an important constituent of many deciduous and evergreen forests.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
It flowers gregariously once in 35-40 years. It dies within one or two years after flowering. All populations from the same seed, irrespective of locality, starts flowering at the same time. The factors which determine gregarious flowering are not clearly known. Most probably the stimulus for flowering lies in the underground rhizome. In general, flowering is more prominent on dry, coarse, well-drained soils.

The gregarious increase in seed production is reported to increase the rodent population, resulting in destruction of cultivated crops in the succeeding year. Flowering is associated with floods; the long tunnels burrowed by rats into the mud-banks along the rivers, cause weakening of the dykes, resulting in inundation of the land around.

Seeds soaked in water for 6 hr. germinate and survive better than dry seeds (Rathinam, Madras Agric. J. 1981, 68, 521).

Floral and seed biology:
Seedling Germination :
The bamboos need warm temperature to grow successfully. Temperature from -5o to 46o is suitable for their cultivation. The bamboos can be grown on denuded wastelands and watersheds along the eroded riverbeds, hill slopes. The soils of bambusa arundenacea forests contain slightly higher amounts of iron and Al, and lower quantities Si, K and Ca. It grows on all types of soils, having adequate moisture but prefers moderately heavy soils.

Bamboos regenerate naturally from seeds with case, with the onset of monsoon. Although weeds and shade affect regeneration heavily, many seedlings persists and die back for several years until they become strong enough to overtop the weed. Within 3-4 years, the clumps establish themselves, and in about 6 hrs or so a homogenous crop of almost equally spread young clump is formed.

Bamboos are propagated artificially through seeds, division, offsets, marcotting, stem - or rhizome cuttings or by layers and are ready for exploitation in 10-12 years. The good seed germinates and the seedlings are reared and transplanted in the field, but seedling years are scarce.

Seeds are sown directly at a depth of 20-30 cm in pits, 3-5m apart, before the onset of monsoon. One kg of seed/ha is required. Post monsoonal sowing eliminates the weeding. The seeds are generally sown in the nurseries before the monsoon (May-June), however, sowing after the monsoon (Sep-Oct) is reported to be equally efficient.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
FOOD & FODDER - Young shoots are pickled as also used as vegetable. In India, the seeds of B. arundinacea are extensively eaten by the poor famines. When mixed with honey, they area delicacy. Seeds are pickled and candies and used for making beer.

TIMBER - used as timber particularly in the humid tropics where houses are made from them. They are preferred because of their easy availability cheapness, strength, straightness smoothness and lightness combined with hardness and facility and regularity with which they can split.

PAPER PULP - Bamboos occupy a very important place in paper making. In China and Japan, the finest handmade paper is produced from bamboo. India is reported to be the leading country in producing the largest volume of pulp, principally from Dendrocalamus strictus and Bambusa arundenacea.
It is also in demand for cardboard industry.
Bamboo pulp is suitable for preparing printing paper of good quality. It is used for the production of bond-paper, ledger paper, art-paper, facial tissue paper, paper for air mail, newsprint etc. The unbleached pulp can be used for the manufacture of cable insulations, industrial saturating and to well packing.

Parts Used:

Culm, young shoots, leaves


Harvesting Practices:
Generally, 3 year old culms are harvested.
After gregarious flowering, all the over-matured bamboo clumps are felled to avoid the hazards of fire; their immediate disposal for utilization is not possible, it is best to chip them after extraction and give suitable preservative treatment

Produce and Products:
Tabashir or banslochan
Paper pulp
Bamboo ply


Chemistry:
The shoots are rich in phosphorus like asparagus, kale and spinach, but low in Ca & Fe. Shoots contain HCN. A toxic constituent, laxiphyllin has been isolated from the shoots. The young, tender shoots may be poisonous to both humans and cattle, as they contain benzoic acid and acyanogenic glucoside.

Buds contain estrogen and estrogen like substances. Young shoots possess insecticidal properties and are lethal to mosquito lavae.

Hollow internodes accumulate a substance, called tabashir or banslochan, used medicinally.

Root contains a cyanogenic glucoside. It alsocontains albermenoids.

Processing Techniques:
Preparation of edible products.
The outer covering of leaves or sheaths is removed and the tender portions are cut into rings or pieces of suitable sizes. The pieces are then steamed to remove the HCN.

PAPER PULP - First 3 methods are used in commercial pulp production. Separation of cellulose fibers from other organic components such as lignin, pectin, starches, fats and resins.

Commonly used commercial processes for the digestion of the raw material are:
i) The soda process using NaOH
ii) The sulphate process which employs a mixture of NaOH and sodium sulphide.
iii) The 'sulphite process' using an aq. solution of Ca or Mg sulphite, with a small concentration of free SO2.
iv) The cold soda process.
v) The 'neutral sulphite semi-chemical process' using a mixture of sodium sulphite and sodium carbonate.


Other uses:

Young tender shoots-pickles, steeped in oil for direct consumption or for making curries; used for birth control (In North Lakshmipur forest division (Assam) have insecticidal properties, poultice of young shoots is applied to ulcers for the dislodgement of worms, hollow internodes accumulate a substance called tabashir or banslochan which is used medicinally.

Leaves - are emmenagogue, anthelmintic, astringent, febrifuge, given to cattle to check diarrhoea, remedy for cough and cold, used as fodder in hilly areas during the lean winter months, used in Ayurvedic system of medicine as blood purifier, in leucoderma and inflamatory conditions, infusion of the leaves is used as an eye-wash, given internally for bronchicitis, gonorrhoea and fever. The dealcoholized extract of leaves have anti-bacterial activity against Bacillus subtilis (Ehrenb) Cohn, micrococcus pyogenes lehmann and Newmann var. aureus Hucker Sern. Staphylococcus aureus Rosenb., Aerobacter aerogenus (Kruse) Beijer, Salmonella typhi (Schroter) & S. paratyphi (Kayser) Castell & Calm.

Culms - construction of houses, ceiling, flooring, frames of mud-built houses, scaffolding, partitions, household items, furnitures, boxes, ornamental vasen, mats, hats, wicker work, boxes, arrows, fish traps, walking sticks, rafters, ladders, tent poles, shaft for tongas, for artificial limb, splints; building boards of good strength, possessing resistance to water, termites and fungi which can be utilised for light partitions, ceiling boards, moulded furnitures such as desks and dressing tables, drawers, sewing-machine covers, travelling-requisites, ward robe, flat mats etc., good for making bamboo-ply.

Ornament - the living bamboos are planted in the garden as hedges, borders and screens and also in mass plantings, in groves and in isolated clumps.

Conservation of soil - Bamboos can be grown on steep hill slopes for controlling landslides, where other economic crops do not grow. Valuable as a wind break.
