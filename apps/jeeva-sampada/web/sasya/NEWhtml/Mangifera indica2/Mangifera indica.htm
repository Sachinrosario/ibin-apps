Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A large evergreen tree, found in Tropical and sub-tropical areas of high rainfall like Western Ghats, West Bengal and Assam (Wet Tropical). Mango occurs wild or semi-wild nearly throughout India, in tropical and semi-tropical hilly forests, particularly near nullah and ravines. Large, evergreen tree. Trees need less care when they are fully mature. Young trees need protection from frost. Suitable for growing in groves, house compounds and roadsides. Commonly cultivated for its fruits throughout the country.

 


Availability :Occupies 60% of the total area under fruits. The total area under mango is estimated at 2 million acres. The largest availability is in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar ranks second and other important mango growing areas are distributed in West Bengal, Madras and the west coast.


Conservation/ Threat Status : Status: Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - Least concern

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Trees are of moderate height (20 m) and spread (10 m) but vegetatively propagated trees are usually round and 8-10 m height. Long tap root with surface feeders mostly in the sub soil. Strong trunk with usually upright branches, bark of chocolate colour which is cracked in a symmetric manner, diameter may be 50 cm to 150 cm. Leaves alternate, spirally arranged, petiolate, entire, coriaceous, lanceolate, glossy on upper surface and hairy on the lower surface, emit aromatic, resinous odour when crushed. Small leaves are usually red but they turn green on expansion and become lush green. Petiole 1-5 cm and leaf may be 10X15 cm long.

Inflorescence is terminal much branched panicles, yellowish to reddish in colour. Flowers are many in number about 1,000 to 5,000 in one branch. Hermaphrodite or male, borne within the same panicles. Sweet scented ovate, cancave, pubescent sepals usually 5 but may be 3-7, free yellowish green. Petals 5, cream coloured, oblong, subacute, reflexed, glabrous, larger than the sepals. Stamens 5 with pink anthers but only 1 or 2 are fertile. Ovary sessile, one celled. Fruit fleshy drupe, green to golden yellow with a reddish blush, juicy, size is variable, 5X14 to 10X20 cm. Shape conical to oblong. Mesocarp may be fibrous or without fibre but is sweet and scented. Seed is in the middle of mesocarp, variable in size, two fleshy cotyledons. It may be flattened or slightly bulging out in the middle but has two papery layers which are of green colour, inside the hard seed coat is the seed which is also edible after roasting. Size 4-6X10X2 cm. Sometimes, the seeds are polyembryonic with 2-12 embryos.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Trees start bearing fruits after 7 years of planting while the vegetatively propagated trees start after 3 years of planting. Flowering is during January - February.


Pollinators:

Plant is cross pollinated.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Fresh seeds meant for sowing are known as seedstones which are collected from ripe fruits. Grown by entire transplanting (grafted).

Fresh seedstones are sown 3 to 5 cm deep in soil during rainy season. Generally 3 seeds are placed at one place. Only the vigorous one is retained while others may be discarded. Trees along with earthballs may be transplanted during rainy season at a spacing of 6-8 m. The plants are also propagated by air layering with application of hormones.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diseases:

Oidium mangiferae Berthet - Powdery mildew, affects blossoms in North, West and South India.

Colletotrichum gloeosporiodes Penz. - Anthracnose, serious disease in moist climate.

Bunchy top or abnormal inflorescence - common disease. But the causal organism not known. Widespread disease but particularly serious in hot plains of north India. A mite Eriophyes has been found associated. Two other mites Tyrophagus castellanii Hirst and Typhlodromus asiaticus Evans have also been reported.

Black tip or necrosis - Fruit disease noticed in the vicinity of brick kilns.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, Fruits, leaves, flowers and seeds.

Uses:

Wood used as fuel. But considered to be bad fuel. Hard charcoal of high calorific value is obtained from mango wood. Calorific value of sapwood is 4,610 kcal/kg of drywood.

Used for packing cases and other domestic uses. Also used for plywood, shoe-heels, furniture, floor and ceiling, window frames, tea chests, match-boxes and splints, bush backs, oar blades and agricultural implements. Timber is used for floor boards, tea chests, etc.



Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:
  Dried slices, amchur, chutney, pulps, jams & jellies, squash, crush & syrup, preserve.

Other uses:

Fruits - Fruits are edible. Unripe, fully developed mangoes used for pickling. Ripe mango fruit is considered invigorating, refreshing and fattening. The juice, along with aromatics, is recommended as a restorative tonic. It contains vitamnins A and C and is useful in heat apoplexy.

Seeds are also eaten after roasting. Ground seed is a source of flour

Leaves - Leaves have medicinal value. Ash of burbt leaves is a household remedy for burns and scalds. Leaves are masticated to give tone to the gums. Fumes from the buring leaves are inhaled for relief from hiccups and affections of throat.

Blossoms - Fragrant flowers were formerly used in preparing an otto, Am Attar. Dried mango flowers are astringent. They are given for diarrhoea, chronic dysentry, catarrh of the bladder and gleet.

Bark - The bark contains tannin and may be used for tanning. It contains resinous matter. The abrk yields a colouring matter which produces beautiful, light, yellow shades on cotton, silk and wool. In conjunction with turmeric and lime, the bark dyes cotton a bright rose-pink. Bark is astringent. It is used in diphtheria and rheumatism.

Gum - Stem exudes gum resin which is a substitute for gum arabic.

Seed kernel - The kernels are used as human food in certain parts of India in times of scarcity. They are sometimes roasted or boiled for eating. Also converted into flour after soaking in water and eliminating the astringent principles.
Seed kernel - Used as cattle feed.
Bark - Used to cure dysentery.
Fruits - Tukhmis variety is used as anti-cancerous.

Miscellaneous Information

 Shade tree as well as tree for afforestation at various sites.

The mango tree tends to live to great age. Giant trees, over 100 years old, have been recorded in West Bengal, Bihar, Bombay and Punjab. A tree in Chandigarh (Punjab) has a trunk 9.6 m in girth and a crown spreading over an area of 2,250 sq. meters and yields annually c. 450 maunds of fruit.

Mangoes are grouped under two broad categories: seedling types (wild and cultivated) and horticultural clones, propagated by budding or grafting. Somatic embryogenesis in the cv Amrapali has been obtained.

Wood: Wood yellowish-white to greyish-brown sometimes with a pinkish tinge, moderately hard, moderately heavy, average weight 690 kg/m3 (43lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; medium to coarse-textured with straight to somewhat interlocked-grain.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings distinct to indistinct. Pores large to moderately large, distinct to the eye, few to moderately numerous, usually solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3 or more, partly filled with tyloses; vessel lines conspicuous on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues aliform, forming eyelets round the pores occasionally with lateral extensions joining adjacent and often also as a distinct line delimiting growth rings. Rays fine to moderately broad, indistinct to the eye, but under the lens, as numerous, closely-spaced lines.
