HABIT & HABITAT
 A small, much-branched evergreen tree or shrub, found throughout the greater part of India , from the Gangetic plains eastwards to Assam and southwards to Kerala and in Nicobar Islands .

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 It is also grown in gardens. Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruits, Roots, Flowers , Bark, Leaves.

Uses

1.The fruits and roots are reported to be used by Santals as an antidote for coloured urine.

2.The ripe fruit is eaten by Santals and the leaves are used as fodder for hurfaloes.

Chemistry
Leaves contain ixoral and beta-sitosterol,leaves and flowers gave two flavonoids-rutin and kaempferol-3-rutinoside whereas the stems gave a flavone glycoside, seed oil gave capric,lauric,myristic,palmitic,stearic,arachidic,behenic,oleic and linoleic acids.

Other Uses

1.       The wood is brown, hard and heavy (wt., 57-66 Ib./cu. ft.), close-grained and smooth. It takes a good polish and is suitable for engraving and turning. Though available only in small sizes, it has been reported to be used for furniture and building purposes. It is also used as fuel : the twigs burn freely and are employed for torches. The flowers are pounded in milk and given for relieving whooping cough.

2.      A decoction of the bark is used for anaemia and general debility.
