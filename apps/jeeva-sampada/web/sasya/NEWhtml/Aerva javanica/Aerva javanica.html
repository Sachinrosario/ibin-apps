 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
 Herbs or undershrubs, erect or scandent, 0.6-0.9 m high, hoary tomentose.  It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra Gujarat and Rajasthan.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
     Stem terete, branched, covered with a thick, easily detachable stellate tomentum. Leaves alternate, ovate-lanceolate or linear-oblong, 2.0-6.5 x 0.3-2.0 cm, obtuse or slightly retuse or acute at apex, greenish white, densely tomentose, sessile or subsessile. Flowers in terminal, cylindrical, wooly, 2-15 cm long, paniculate spikes, unisexual, greenish-white; bracteoles braoadly ovate, acute, white, hyaline. Outer tepals or perianth ovate, rather more than 1.5 mm long in male flowers and obovate-spathulate, 2.5 mm long in female flowers; inner tepals smaller. Utricles orbicular-ovoid, compressed, very thin. Seeds rounded, 0.8 mm in diam., brown to black, shinning.

Chemistry:

Kaempferol-3-galactoside (255-7°), Kaempferol-3-rhamnogalactoside (190-92°), b-sitosterol, a-amyrin were isolated from ether extracts of Aerva javanica whole plant. (Aiyar et al, 1973).

Flavone glycosides chrysin-7-O-b-galactoside was isolated from the roots of A. javanica, and their structure was identified on the basis of spectral data and hydrolysis by acid and enzyme. (Garg et al, 1979).

Flavone-aervanone was isolated and its structure was elucidated as 8-C-b-D-galactosyl-7, 4’-dihydroxy flavanone from Aerva javanica. (Garg et al, 1980).

Hentriaiontane (m.p. 60°), nonacosane (64°), nonacosanol (82.5°), tritriacontane (72°), tetra-triacontane (72.6°), sitosterol (136°), oleanolic acid (308°) have been isolated from the fresh leaves of Aerva javanica. (Usmanghani et al, 1982).

b-sitosterol glucoside was isolated from ethylacetate extract of the A. javanica fresh leaves. (Usmanghani et al, 1983). C.A.1983,99: 19666n. Flavanol, isohamnetin 3-o-(4’’-P-coumaroyl-a-rhamnosyl (1®6) galactoside has been isolated from Aerva javanica along with its unacylated derivative, its kaempferol analogue and various common kaempferol quereitin and isohamnetin glycosides. (Saleh et al, 1990).

10-methyl-canthin-6-one and 10-hydroxy-canthin-6-one were isolated from Aerva javanica. (Ammar et al,1997). CA,1997,127: 15440h.

Three saponin glycosides (I,II,III) were isolated, I afforded b-sitosterol and glucose, II yielded lupeol, galactose and glucose, III afforded oleanolic , galactose and glucose, rhamnose with this lauric, myristic, myristoleic , palmitoleic, stearic, oleic and linoleic acids  were also  identified. (Abdelwahab et al ,1997)

Carbohydrates arabinose, rhamnose, xylose, galactose, glucose, nannose and mannitol were identified in A. javanica. (Aboutabl et al, 1999) C.A. 104799,130, 1999.

Lupeol, Mixture of myristic, palmitic, steraic, oleic acids, methyl acid esters,b-sitosterol, betulinic acid, phytol,quercetin, quercetin-3-O-rutinoside, quercetin-3-O-xylosyl (1®2) rhamnoside and shikimic acid were isolated (Hesham and  Sahar, 2000) C.A. 2000, 132: 134744y.


Pharmacology:
A.javanica induced diuresis, urine output observed with the fresh plant extract was elevated, flowers were found to be most effective. (Udupihille  and  Jiffry, 1986).


Ethnobotany:
Rheumtism medicine ( Seed, Singh and Pandey, 1980 )Stuffing pillows (Seed, Vishnu Mittre,1981)
