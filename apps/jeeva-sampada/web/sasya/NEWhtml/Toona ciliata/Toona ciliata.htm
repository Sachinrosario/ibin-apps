Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large deciduous, fast growing tree found in humid west Himalayan region, subtropical climate like Himalayan foothills and Central Indian Hills, subtropical regions like East Indian hills and parts of Western Ghats, tropical medium rainfall areas of the Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical). Distributed in the sub-Himalayan tracts and outer Himalayas up to an altitude of 4,000 ft. and in Assam, Bengal, Chota Nagpur, Western Ghats and other hills of the Deccan peninsula.

Prefers deep, rich, moist loamy soils. Moderate light demander, requires side shade and full overhead light. Needs protection from sun and considerably frost hardy. Sensitive to fire and cannot withstand drought. Superficial root system.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large deciduous tree, with spreading crown. Bark smooth when young but thick, exfoliating in woody scales in old trees. Leaves abruptly-pinnate, leaflets opposite or alternate, 4-15 pairs, lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate, acuminate, entire or slightly undulate, glabrous, base inequilateral, the upper side usually larger rounded, the lower smaller acute.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers white, honey-scented, in large drooping terminal more or less pubescent panicles nearly as long as the leaves. Buds ovoid or oblong. Pedicels short, slender. Calyx divided nearly to the base, lobes orbicular-ovate, ciliate. Petals broadly elliptic, obscurely veined, ciliate, keeled at the base inside. Stamens 5, each inserted on one of the orange-coloured hairy lobes of the disk. Staminodes 0; filaments subulate; anthers oblong, cordate, rounded and apiculate at the apex. Ovary hairy, ovoid or subglobose. Seeds are winged, light, carried by wind.

Phenology:

Leafless from December end to mid March. May extend to the end of March, new leaves start appearing. Flowers appear during March - May. Seeds ripen during March - May.

Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by entire transplanting. Seed propagation is also practised. Best time for plantation is July. Young plants need protection from sun.

Under natural conditions germination takes place soon after seed fall early in the rainy season. Clearing the ground around seed bearers is beneficial for natural regeneration.

Seeds are collected in May, off the trees, and not from ground, as it has been observed that the percentage of germination in collections from the ground is low. Seeds are sown in raised nursery beds.

 

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:Wood used as fuel. Calorific values : sapwood – 5,168 kcal, 9,303 Btu; heartwood – 5,113 kcal, 9,204 Btu.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, pale pinkish-grey to pinkish-brown. Heartwood light brick-red turning deep brownish red on exposure. Wood soft to moderately hard, light to moderately heavy, average weight 515 kg/m3 (32 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; rather lustrous, and usually with a characteristic cedary smell when freshly cut; straight-grained, medium coarse and somewhat uneven-textured.

A semi-ring-porous to ring-porous wood. Growth rings distinct, delimited by large and more numerous earlywood pores. Pores in the early wood large, clearly visible to the eye sometimes filled with dark brown gummy deposits; transition from early wood to late wood gradual; latewood pores small, distinct under the lens, solitary or in short radial multiples, often filled with dark brown gummy deposits; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues usually indistinct, but sometimes just visible under the lens, as inconspicuous and often incomplete sheaths round the pores, and also faintly delimiting growth rings. Rays moderately broad to fine, the former just visible to the eye, distinct under the lens, rather few and somewhat widely-spaced.

Wood yields timber.

Wood is used for furniture, ceilings, floors, boarding, doors, panels, frames, windows, footboards, carriage roofs, bent rims, cigar cases, tea boxes, cordite cases and plywood. Also used as ornamental timber. Suitable for matchboxes, planking. Employed in well-construction, boats, masts, oars, toys, carving, musical instruments, carts, railway carriages. Wood is used for furniture and building.
