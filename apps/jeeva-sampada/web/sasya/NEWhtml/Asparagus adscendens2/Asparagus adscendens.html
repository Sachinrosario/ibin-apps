DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Suberect, prickly shrubs with white tuberous root. Stem tall, suberect, terete, smooth, white, much-branched; branchlets ascending, grooved and angled; angles minutely scabrid; spines stout, 1-2 cm long.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Cladodes in dense tufts of 6-20, filiform, 1-5 cm long, soft, suberect or curved. Racemes 2-5 cm long, many-flowered; pedicels jointed above and below the middle; bracts minute. 

Inflorescence: Simple or branched racemes often bearing cladodes at the top; Flower: White, numerous, pedicel jointed ;Perianth: Petaliod, 6, partite, campanulate or spreading  ; Androecium: Stamens 6, inserted at the base of perianth segments, shorter than the perianth;Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled, ovules 2 or more in each cell; Fruit: Berries subglobose, more or less 3-lobed, red when ripe; Seed: 1-3 shining, black, spherical. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowers 2 cm in diam.. Perianth segments spreading. Berries 6-8 mm in diam., 1-seeded. It flowers between July-february. Flowering & Fruiting: October-April. 

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Tuberous root are used as demulcent and tonic. It is useful in diarrhoea, dysentery and general debility.


Chemistry:
·         b-sitosterol, sarsasapogenin and diosgenin isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·         Asparanins A (fig. 4a) and B (fig. 4b), asparosides A (fig. 3a) and B (fig. 3b) along with sitosterrol-b-D-glucoside isolated from fruits and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·         Asparanins C (fig. 4c) and D (fig. 4d) and asparosides C (fig. 3c) and D (fig. 3d) isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

·         Adscendins A (fig. 1a) and B (fig. 1b), adscendosides A (fig. 2a) and B (fig. 2b) isolated from leaves and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

·         Isolation of two new glcosides from roots and their characterization as 3b-O-b-D-(2’-O-tetracosyl) xylopyranosyl stigmasterol and 3b-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl (1à2)-a-L-arabinopyranosyl stigmasterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·         Acid hydrolysate of butanol-soluble of roots contain saponins of stigmasterol and sarasasapogenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
