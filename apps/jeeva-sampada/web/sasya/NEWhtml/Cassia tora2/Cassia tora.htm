DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  A small weed, subshrubby, upto 75-150 cm, branchlets glabrous, leaves upto 4 cm, leaflets 3 pairs, obovate, 1.5-3.0 cm by 1.0-1.5 cm, thin, coriaceous, glabrous above, pubescent below, base oblique, cuneate, rotund, margin entire, apex, obtuse apiculate, petiole upto 2 cm; racemes terminal, coxymbose, bracteoles two, linear, flowers 1.5 cm across, golden yellow, pods short, stipulate, flat, compressed, upto 9 mm, 4-6 mm across, seeds 20-30, oblong, longitudinal.

Plant is distributed throughout the India. Annual fetid herbs ;  Stem: Aerial, erect, herbaceous ; Leaves:  rachis grooved, more or less pubescent, with aconical gland between each of two lowest of  pairs leaflets, stipulate, linear, subulate, caudacous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   

Inflorescence: Axillary racemes; Flower: Usually in sub-sessile pairs in the axils of the leaves, the upper crowded ; Calyx:  Sepals glabrous, divided to the base, ovate, acute and spreading; Corolla: Petals 5, pale yellow ;Androecium: Stamens 10, the three upper reduced to minute staminodes , the remaining seven perfect, unequal; Gynoecium:  Ovary sessile, at the bottom of the calyx, ovules many, style short stigma many; Fruit: Pods obliquely septate, puberulous, subtetragonous, much curved when young,  not reticulate, the sutures very broad; Seed: 25-30 , rhomobohedral with long axis in the direction of pod .


Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : November-December.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant thrives on wide range of soils but well drained loams  with pH ranges between 6-8.5 are best for the growth.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers. Where the soil fertility is low to medium the crop generally needs 50 –100 kg nitrogen,20-50 kg phosphate and 20 kg of potassium  oxide during  the period of growth .

Water management: Crop requires periodic  irrigation particularly if rains are irregular during the period of growth.

Harvesting period / Method: Leaves are harvested when they becomes mature. Pods are harvested when still green.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Leaf-laxative; leaf and seed-in skin diseases; root antidote for snakebite.


Chemistry:

Leaves contain chrysophanol aloe-eurodin, rhein and emodin, seeds gave a glycoside-rubrofurasin-6ß-gentiobioside, chrysophanol, physcion, emodin, aloe-emodin, rhein, aloe-emodin monoglucoside, physcion diglucoside, chrysophanol diglucoside and chrysophanol triglucoside, plant contained glucose.

·         A sterol, mp. 137°C and myricyl alcohol, mp. 87°C were identified. (Rastogi and Mehrotra,1960-1969)

·         Isolation and structure of a new rubrofusarin glycoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra,1960-1969)

·         From the seeds of Cassia tora, a new naphthalene glycoside was isolated and characterized as 2-acetyl-3- O-beta-D-apiofuranosyloxy-8-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyloxy-1,6-dimethoxynaphthalene (cassitoroside). (Choi et al., 1995) MAPA 1996, 9602-0953.

·         From the roasted seeds of Cassia tora L., a new naphthopyrone glycoside was isolated and characterized as 10-((beta-D-glucopyranosyl-(1®6)-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl)oxy)-5-hydroxy-8-methoxy-2-methyl-4H-naphtho(1,2-b)pyran-4-one(isorubrofusarin gentiobioside). Along with isorubrofusarin gentiobioside, alaternin and adenosine were isolated and identified. (Lee et al., 1997)

·         From the seeds of Cassia tora, an anthraquinone glucoside was isolated and characterized as alaternin 2-O-beta-D-glucopyranoside. (Lee et al., 1998)

·         The 1H and 13C-NMR spectra of alaternin, aurantioobtusin, chryso-obtusin, obtusin and 2-glucosyl obtusifolin isolated from the seeds of Cassia tora have been assigned based on HMBC, long-range HECTOR, fully 1H-coupled 13C-NMR, deuterium isotopes experiment and comparison with the model compounds. (Choi et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9702-1029.

·         Carbamoylethylation of Cassia tora gum was carried out with acrylamide in presence of sodium hydroxide under different reaction conditions. Variables studied were concentration of sodium hydroxide, acrylamide, Cassia tora gum as well as reaction temperature and time. The nitrogen content, carboxyl content and total ether content were determined. The optimum condition for preparing carbamoylethyl Cassia tora gum (%N=3.24) comprised concentration of acrylamide (1.12 mol), sodium hydroxide (1.25 mol), Cassia tora gum (0.197 mol) at 30degreeC for 1 h. Rheological properties of carbamoylethyl Cassia tora gum solutions showed non-Newtonian pseudoplastic behavior regardless of the %N. At a constant rate of shear the apparent viscosity of carbamoylethyl Cassia tora gum solutions increase with the increase in %N of the product.(Sharma et al., 2003)

·         Ethanolic extract of seeds of Cassia tora L. and its fractions were investigated for hypolipidemic activity on triton induced hyperlipidemic profile. Ethanolic extract and its ether soluble and water soluble fraction decreased serum level of total cholesterol by 42.07, 40.77 and 71.25%, respectively. On the other hand ethanolic extract, ether soluble fraction and water soluble fraction increased the serum HDL-cholesterol level by 6.72, 17.20 and 19.18%, respectively. Ethanolic extract, ether fraction and water fraction decreased triglyceride level by 26.84, 35.74 and 38.46%, respectively. The reduction in LDL-cholesterol level by ethanolic extract, ether soluble fraction and water soluble fraction were 69.25, 72.06 and 76.12%, respectively. (Patil et al., 2004)


Pharmacology:

·        Radical scavenging principles on 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl(DPPH) radical were isolated from the seeds of Cassia tora L. Assignments of the 1H- and 3C-NMR data showed the active components to be an anthraquinone, alaternin and two naphthopyrone glycosides, nor-rubrofusarin-6-beta-D-glucoside(cassiaside) and rubrofusarin-6-D-gentiobioside. Alaternin showed more potent radical scavenging effect than the others. (Choi et al., 1994)

·         Emodin is an active constituent of cassia tora, was administered to rabbits by i.v. bolus resulted in serum profile which could be well described by a two-compartment model to perform pharmacokinetics in which emodin was foun to be highly bound (99.6%) to serum protein. (Liang et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9603-1444.

·         The antifungal activity of the deaklcoholized extract of leaves of Cassia tora was determined on five fungal organisms. The crude leaf extract significantly inhibited their growth when tested by turbidity and spore germination methods in a concentration dependent fashion. The effects produced by the extract were compared with a standard antifungal grisofulvin. (Mukherjee et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9703-1615.

·         Water extracts from Cassia tora L. (WECT) roasted at 150, 200 or 250 degreeC for 5 min were evaluated for their antimutagenicity against various mutagens by Ames test using S. typhimurium TA98 and TA100. No toxicity or mutagenicity was found in the WECT at a dose of 0.25-5 mg per plate. WECT had a very marked and dose-dependent inhibition effect on the Aroclor 1254-hepatic S9-mediated mutagenicity of 2-amino-3-methylimidazo(4,5-f)quinoline and benzo(a)pyrene. The antimutagenicity of WECT decreased with an increasing roasting temperature in the following order: unroasted>150 degreeC>200 degreeC>250 degreeC. The results presented herein suggest that the antimutagenicity of WECT was due to a desmutagenic action, but not a bioantimutagenic action. (Wu and Yen, 1999)

·         The antioxidant properties of water extracts from Cassia tora L. (WECT) prepared under different degrees of roasting were investigated. (Yen, 2000)

·         Leaves of Cassia tora Linn. were evaluated for their hepatoproctective activity using in vitro technique. For this purpose, ethanolic extract of the leaves and its benzene, ethyl acetate and butanolic fractions were taken. It was observed that the butanolic fraction of ethanol extract showed a considerable degree of protection against CCl4 challenge. (Upadhyay et al., 2000)

·         The leaves of Cassia tora Linn. (Family: Caesalpiniaceae) were soxhlet extracted with methanol. The spasmogenic effects of the extract were evaluated on guinea pig ileum, rabbit jejunum and mice intestinal transit. Antinociceptive activity of the extract was also evaluated in the mice. The extract also significantly (P<0.05) reduced the nociceptive response of mice to increased force (g). The effects were dose-dependent. The studies suggest that the use of C. tora, traditionally, as a purgative and in the treatment of other ailments is justifiable. (Chidume et al., 2002)

·         The growth-inhibitory activity of Cassia tora seed-derived materials against seven intestinal bacteria was examined in vitro, and compared with that of anthraquinone, anthraflavine, anthrarufin, and 1-hydroxyanthraquinone. The active constituent of C. tora seeds was characterized as quinizarin, using various spectroscopic analyses. (Lee, 2003)
