DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Sheath of corm closely reticulate, basal spathes embracing the scape 2-valved. A small bulbous perennial, 6-10 inches high, cultivated for its large, scented, blue or lavender flowers. The flowers have trifid, orange-coloured stigmas which along with the style-tops yield the saffron of commerce.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers violet, autumnal appearing with the leaves, throat of perianth bearded. Anther yellow. Style arms orange-red, subclavate tips, entire or lobulate. It flowers between May- October.
CIMAP: Flower: Stalkless, deep blue violet, funnel shaped ;  Corolla: petals narrow-oblong, long slender corolla tube ;Androecium: Stamens 3,  ; Gynoecium:  Ovary inferior 3-chambered, with many ovules, style 3-lobed, orange, stigma brick red ; Fruit: Capsule spillting longitudinally; 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : October. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:  The reported chromosome numbers - 22,40.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated by young cormlets.

Soil type: Plant prefers well drained clayey loam for cultivation.

Nutrient requirements:  Plant responds well to the application of manure and fertilizers.

Water management:  It requires irrigation during dry periods after 15-20 days.

Harvesting period / Method: Stigma are harvested when the flowers open in morning.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used: Stigma and Style- anticatarrhal, stimulant, stomachic, antidote for snake-bite, febrifuge, in melancholia, liver diseases.

Uses:
 Saffron is bitter; anthelmintic, tonix, laxative; improves taste; used in bronchitis, throat problems, headache, vomitting, scabies, skin diseases, biliousness and diseases of brain (Ayurveda). Leaves are used in fractures and pain in the joints. Saffron is used in kidney, liver, spleen and brain diseases; good in scabies; improves blood (Yunani).


Chemistry:
Carotenoid glycosides – crocin-1 (fig. 1a), crocin 2 (fig. 1b) (b-monogentiobioside-b-D-monoglucoside ester of a-crocetin), crocin-3 (fig. 1c) (b-monogentiobioside ester of a-crocetin) and crocin-4 (fig. 1d) (b-D-monoglucoside ester of monomethyl-a-crocetin) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Mono-b-D-gentiobiosyl and mono-b-D-glucosyl esters of crocetin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Crocetin-di-)b-D-gentiobiosyl) ester (crocin), crocetin-(b-D-gentiobiosyl)-(b-D-glucosyl) ester and crocetin-di(b-D-glucosyl) ester isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Synthesis of Picrocin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

b-sitosterol, ursolic, oleanolic, palmitic, palmitoleic, oleic, linoleic and linolenic acids identified in bulbs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Astragalin, helichrysoside, kaempferol, kaempferol-3-O-b-D-glucosyl(1®2)-b-D-(6-acetyl)glucopyranoside and kaempferol-3-O-b-D-glucosyl(1®2)-b-D-glucopyranoside isolated from petals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Two new glycosides – crosatosides A (fig. 2) and B (fig. 3) isolated from pollen along with kaempferol-3-O-b-D-glucoside and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Four new compounds, crocusatins F (1), G (2), H (3), and I (4a), together with 21 known compounds, were isolated from an aqueous extract of the stigmas of Crocus sativus (saffron). The structures of 1-4 were established by spectral methods. The tyrosinase inhibitory activities of all 25 compounds isolated were evaluated in vitro using mushroom tyrosinase. (Li and Wu, 2002a).

 Five new naturally occurring monoterpenoids, crocusatins-A -E, a new lactate, sodium (2S)-(O-hydroxyphenyl)lactate and eighteen known compounds were isolated and characterized from the pollen of Crocus sativus L. The tyrosinase inhibitory activities of these compounds were also discussed. (Li and Wu, 2002b).

Saffron contains crocins. Volatile constituents are 3, 5, 5-trimethyl -4-hydroxy- cyclohexanon-2-ene safranal, 3, 5, 5- trimethyl -1, 4- cyclohexadione,3,5,5-trimethyl-1,4- cyclohexadione-2-ene, 3, 5, 5-trimethyl -2-hydroxy-1,4- cyclohexadione-2-ene, 2, 6, 6-trimethyl-4- hydroxy-1-cyclohexen-1-carboxaldehyde, 2, 4, 4-trimethyl-3-formyl-6-hydroxy-2,5-cyclohexadien-1-one and iso-phorone. Saffron also contains crocetin and picrotoxin. It is rich in riboflavine and also contains thiamine.

Pharmacology:

Effect of plant extract on macromolecular synthesis was examined in three human cell lines – A 549 (derived from lung tumor), WI-38 (normal lung fibroblasts) and VA-13 (WI-38 cells transformed in vivo by SV 40 tumor virus). Malignant cells were more sensitive than normal cells to inhibitory effect of extract on both DNA and RNA synthesis but protein synthesis was not affected in both types of cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Crocin suppresses the effect of tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-alpha on neuronally differentiated PC-12 cells. PC-12 cells dead from exposure to TNF-alpha show apoptotic morphological changes and DNA fragmentation by which crocin inhibits neuronal cell death induced by both internal and external apoptotic stimuli. (Soeda et al., 2001)

Crocusatin H, crocin-1, and crocin-3 from stigma showed significant tyrosinase inhibitory activity. (Li and Wu, 2002a).

Aqueous and ethanolic extracts of saffron stigma and petal have an antinociceptive effect, as well as acute and/or chronic anti-inflammatory activity. (Hosseinzadeh and Younesi, 2002).
