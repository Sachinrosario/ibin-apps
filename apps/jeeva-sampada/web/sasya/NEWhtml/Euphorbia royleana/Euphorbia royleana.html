DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Succulent shrub or tree. Stem round, woody, with 4-5 angled whorled branches. Leaves at the branch apex, alternate, obovate, mucronate at apex, entire at margin, subsessile, deciduous, glabrous. Cyathia in cymes, borne above the axils of fallen leaves, with marginal appendages and glands. Styles 2 lobed. Capsules trigonous.

 REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Cymes from the long peducles from the crown rom root stock, appearing after the leaves that have fallen, 3-many flowered; Flower: Bracts at fork ovate-lanceolate, monoecious, combined in an inflorescence of many male florets surrounding female floret ; Male Flower: a stalked stamen without floral envelop ; Female Flower: Ovary 3-celled on ultimately exerted stalk in the center of greenish involucres, ovules solitary in each cell, style 3, free; Fruit: Capsule of 3 two valved compressed cocci with styles combined in the middle ; Seed: Broadly ovoid, subacute, smooth. Flowering & Fruiting : April – May  .

Floral and seed biology: Fl. & Fr.: April – May.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.  
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=30.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Acrid milky juice possesses cathartic & anthelmintic properties.


Chemistry:
Taraxerol, and ellagic acid, mp. 360°, isolated from plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

2,3-dimethoxyellagic acid, succinic acid and an unidentified compound, mp. 181°C, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

A new triterpene –epitaraxerol (fig. 1) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Ellagic acid, euphol, taraxerol, sitosterol, m-hydroxybenzoic acid, 7-hydroxy-3,4-benzcoumarin, 7-methoxy-3,4-benzcoumarin, 2',7-dihydroxy-3,4-benzcoumarin isolated both from latex of plant and shilajit. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structure elucidation of cycloroylenol (fig. 2). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

a-amyrin, octacosanol, hexacosanol, tetracosanol, docopsanol, b-sitosterol, stigmasterol and campesterol isolated from latex. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

 


Pharmacology:

Ethyl acetate fraction (EA) from the residue of an 85 percent ethanol extract of Euphorbia royleana latex exhibited a significant antipyretic effect in hyperthermic rats and rabbits. The oral LD50 was more than 2 g/kg in rats and mice. Analgesic and antipyretic activities have been attributed to the inhibition of the prostaglandins. (Bani et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9802-0778.

The hydrosoluble fraction of Euphorbia royleana latex (AER), administered by gavage at doses of 50-200 mg/kg, showed dose-dependent anti-inflammatory and anti-arthritic effects in different acute and chronic test models in rats and mice. (Boe et al, 2000).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine to stop bleed, on burns, cuts (Latex, Jain, 1984); Ear complaints (Latex, Vir Jee, 1984); Skin disease (Latex, Purohit et al, 1985); Wounds (Latex, Gaur, 1980)

Any Other  

Dilute aqueous solutions of the latex of Euphorbia royleana used for killing fishes. (Singh and Singh, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 131007v.


Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:
Resin from latex caused pronounced inflammation of mammalian skin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
