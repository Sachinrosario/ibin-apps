Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Unarmed large shrubs or small trees found throughout the plains of India. Prefers clayey soil. Drought resistant over long periods.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large erect shrub or small tree, unarmed. Leaves bipinnate; main rachis slender, channelled, pubescent, ending in a weak spine; pinnae 3-6 pairs, shortly stalked, their rachises pubescent, ending in a weak spine. Leaflets 10-15 pairs, rather distant, membranous, glaucous, linear-oblong, acute, glabrous or nearly so above, finely pubescent beneath, base oblique. Flowers in dense globose heads; peduncles often geminate, slender, pubescent, elongate and slightly thickened fruit. Calyx long, tubuloso-companulate, membranous; teeth short, triangular. Petals spathulate-oblong, indurated at the tip, whitish. Stamens much exserted, anthers not gland-crested. Ovary shortly stalked, slightly pubescent. Pods 5 6, straight, flat, obliquely triangular at the apex, narrowed at the base into a stalk, glabrous. Seeds 15-20.

Floral and seed biology:

 

Seedling Germination: Seeds germinate rapidly with high percentage. It regenerates and spreads readily from self-sown seeds, but natural viability of seeds is low (c. 10%). For cultivation purposes, the seeds are scarified before sowing; steeping in hot water at 700 – 800 for five minutes also facilitates germination. In starting fresh plantations, it is usual in the Philippines to inoculate the soil with nodule organisms (by mixing soil from old plantations of L. glauca) before planting. It can be propagated by cuttings (2-5 cm. diam.) and stumps.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, leaves, twigs and seeds.

Fuel: Wood burned as fuel. Wood used for making charcoal.  Source of charcoal & energy (1 million barrels oil p.a. from 12,000 ha)

Wood may be used in paper manufacture in combination with long-fibered pulps. Used for pulp.


Harvesting Practices: Coppices vigorously and the growth of coppice shoots is more rapid than of seedling plants.

 

Produce and Products:

Chemistry:

Calorific value of the wood: 7,250 cal/g.

Processing Techniques:

Difficult and slow to season. Should be converted soon after felling. Sapwood and heartwood should be treated separately. Sapwood liable to split and discolour, dries quickly than heartwood.

Wood can be kiln dried in 8-10 days, in addition to initial steaming, one intermediate steaming and one more towards the end for 2-3 hrs are necessary. Presence of gum makes kiln drying difficult.

 

Other uses:

Planted for filling forest gaps, as windbreak and for checking soil erosion. Grown as shade and cover plant in Plantations of tea, coffee, cocoa, rubber, cinchona, teak and sal. It shades its leaves regularly and enriches top soil with organic matter.

Leaves and twigs rich in nitrogen and potassium salts and can be used after composting. Powdered seeds also make useful manure.

Tops, leaves, pods and seeds of the plant relished by the cattle, sheep and goats. Leaves can be used with alfalfa leaf and meal in poultry rations. Seeds can be used for dairy animals as concentrates.

When consumed in excessive quantities, all plant parts toxic to monogastric animals like horses, pigs, rabbits and chickens.

Young shoots eaten as vegetable in some countries. Dry seeds eaten after parching.

Plant reported to be a worm repellent and also a fish poison.

The shiny seeds used in fancy baskets, purses and ornaments. Seeds - Flat brown seeds are used as beads.

Miscellaneous Information

Seeds germinate on variety of soils and dominating vegetation. Source of green manure.
Forage is toxic to non-ruminants causing hair loss. Wood is hard, strong, medium textured and close grained.
