 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, hoary pubescent herb. It is found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh.


Availability :  


Specimen examined:  Jammu & Kashmir: Pahalgam, 2400 m, 19.09.1954, K.M. Vaid, 118055 (DD); Himachal Pradesh: Deosai, 4334 m, 10.08.1905, A. Meebold, 861 (CAL).
Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant is rare in wild.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves broadly ovate, decompoundly pinnatisect; segments pectinately pinnatifid, puberulous on both surfaces. Rachis simple. Heads 15-20-fid, broadly hemispheric, nodding subsecund, distant in slender panicled racemes. Involucral bracts hoary pubescent with a green disk and broad scarious margins.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: Heads 6-20 flowered, short to long peduncled, nodding, hemispheric in racemose panicles, involcral bracts sparsely to densely hoary tomentose rarely glabrous;  Receptacle small conic naked with scarious margins; Outer florets female fertile, corolla obscurely lobed, gradually expanding to the base; Disc florets, yellow, hermaphrodite, tubular, limb 5-lobed, sparsely glandular ; Fruit: Achenes  oblanceolate, slightly curved to the base and truncate at the top ; Flowering & Fruiting : July- September. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds and basal cuttings.

Soil type: Easily grown in a well drained circumneutral or slightly alkaline loamy soil, preferring a warm sunny dry positions.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
New coumarin hemiterpenes- lacarol (fig. 1a), desoxylacarol (fig. 1b) and methyllacarol (fig. 1c) isolated from leaves and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Four new coumarin derivatives- neoartanin (fig. 4a), neoartaninepoxide (fig. 4b), neoartenindiol (fig. 4c) and isosabandin (fig. 3) isolated from leaves of Chinese plant; neoartanin, lacinartinepoxide (fig. 2a) and lacinartindiol (fig. 2b) isolated along with lacinartin, desoxylacarol and methyllacarol from leaves of Japanese plant; structures of new compounds determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Four stereoisomers- artedouglasia oxides A (fig. 5a), B (fig. 6a), C (fig. 5b) and D (fig. 6b) isolated and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Isolation of umbelliferone and scopoletin from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Lacarol, quercetin, isorhamnetin,butein, isoliquiritigenin, 5- hydroxy-7,8-dimethoxycoumarin and 5,6-dimethoxy-7-(2’,3’-dihydroxy-3’-methyl) butoxycoumarin isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

A major component – (3S,4S,7R,9R)silphiperfol-5-en-3-ol (fig. 8a)– along with its isomers (3S,4S,7S,9R)silphiperfol-5-en-3-ol (fig. 8b) and their corresponding ketones as minor components as well as silphiperfol-5-ene (fig. 8c), (3R, 4S, 7R, 9R) silphiperfol-5-en-3-ol (fig. 8d), (3R, 4S, 7S, 9R) silphiperferol-5-en-3-ol (fig. 8e) and (5S, 9R) shilpiferol-6-en-3,5-dione (fig. 7) as trace components isolated from essential oil. Structures of new compounds determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Analysis of essential ol of plants from Kashmir region indicate existence of two chemotypes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

The sesquiterpene fraction of the essential oil from Artemisia laciniata from the Kashmir region of India was analysed and was found to contain mainly oxygenated derivatives. This main constituent, about 4 percent of the total oil, is silphiperfol-5-en3-ol A. Besides many other minor compounds possessing this rare skeleton, the biogenetically related presilphiperfolan –9a-ol was isolated. (Weyerstahl, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9805-3125.


Pharmacology:
The extracts of aerial part A. laciniata reduced the mutagenicity of AFBI. (Park et al, 1996). MAPA, 1998, 9801-0208.
