Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Erect tree in tropical and subtropical climate. This is found up to 1,300 m elevation throughout much of mountainous India. On rocky hills, ravines and denuded wastelands that are too dry for agriculture. Common in dry deciduous forests in India and the open grasslands of Sri Lanka. It is not formally cultivated; natural regeneration supplies the current demands for its wood. The trees have been tested outside their native habitat.

Commonly found in the forests of the sub-Himalayan tract and Sivalik hills and in the hills throughout India ascending to 1,200 m. This can grow on dry, rocky hills, ravines and denuded wastelands that are too dry for normal agriculture. Some Rajasthan forests where it grows receive 600 mm, but they have long periods of water deficit because of irregular rainfall and high evapo-transpiration (generally exceeding the precipitation). In this area the monsoon failed each year from 1965-1969 causing the trees to die back, but they have since resprouted with healthy vigor. In Himalayas, it is gregarious forming almost pure crops, or is found mixed with Bauhinia semla Wunderlin, Buchanania lanzan Spreng., Ougeinia oojeinensis (Roxb.) Hochr. Terminalia alata Heyne ex Roth, Wendlandia exserta DC. and Pinus roxburghii Sarg. In Madhya Pradesh, it is abundant in miscellaneous forests. It occurs in the forests of Chota Nagpur in Bihar, in eastern and western Maharashtra; in Belgaum and Kanara and the eastern forests of Coorg in Karnataka; in abundance in Madurai and Tirunelveli in Tamil Nadu and in many parts of Andhra Pradesh and Kerala up to 1,200 m.

Tree is found at its best in Madurai, Coimbatore and Salem districts of Tamil Nadu, Chandrapur district of Maharashtra and North and South Kanara districts of Karnataka and in Uttar Pradesh.

Drought resistant, but sensitive to abnormal and prolonged drought. It avoids very moist and very dry regions. Frost hardy. Susceptible to fire and browsing damage. It is a slow growing, light demanding which can withstand some shade in early stages. It grows on a variety of geological formations including sandstone, limestone, metamorphic rocks, trap and laterite. it, however, grows best in alluvial soils. It avoids swampy and badly drained soils and requires good drainage. It prefers a maximum shade temperature of 38-470 C and a minimum 0-150 C.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Moderate to large sized deciduous tree. Stem deeply fluted towards the base; bark thin, smooth, greenish or greyish-white, spotted all over with paler greenish or greenish yellow depressions, exfoliating in thin rounded flakes, which leave shallow depression. Young parts glabrous or silky-pubescent. Leaves alternate or sub opposite, elliptic or oblong-elliptic, obtuse or very often shortly cuspidate, glabrous when fully grown, pale dull glaucous-green, base usually rounded, midrib prominent, pink, main nerves 6-10 pairs, arching, prominent on the lower side, the veins between them reticulate.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers minute, greenish yellow, sessile, in small dense heads; peduncles 1 or more from the same axil, branched, not much longer than petioles. Calyx tube pubescent; teeth short and broadly triangular.

Fruit small, several crowded in globular head, the nucleus nearly plano-convex, yellowish brown or reddish brown, glabrous or more or less pubescent, winged down two longest sides beaked with the persistent calyx which is as long as or sometimes longer than the nucleus, wings with entire margin.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling germination: The trees are easily propagated by seed. In India seedlings are planted on staggered contour ridges and also in pits filled with weathered soil.

In natural reproduction of this species is not yet fully understood. The seedlings come up naturally and often in abundance in dense thickets, on newly exposed well-drained ground on stony hillsides and on land-slips as well as on alluvial ground. Reproduction is always best in years of drought, and where the drainage is perfect, a dense crop of saplings appears. The tree coppices and pollards well in most localities. Pollarding is more favourable for production of tanning-material as it results in profuse branching and production of numerous shoots, twigs and leaves.

Tree seeds abundantly every year but the fertility of the seeds is very poor. for propagating from seed, the fruits should be collected when the flower heads commence to break up. Best results are obtained from seeds collected after dry years when a fertile crop of seeds is obtained. Germination is successful when sowing is done in heaps of loose earth and rubble or in irrigated raised beds with soil mixed with a large quantity of coarse sand. Too much of water and calcium are harmful to the growth of the plants. The average germination is only 0.12 per cent. Light and moisture favour successful germination of the seed. In continuous light, germination takes place in about 48 hours. The optimum temperature for germination is 350 C. Germination commences about twenty days after sowing and seedlings can be transplanted without any difficulty.

The growth of the seedlings only moderate, a height of 10-20 cm being ordinarily attained by the end of the first season. The seedlings are very intolerant of weeds. They suffer a little from frost which causes withering of leaves. They require protection from fire and excessive grazing to ensure good growth.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood used as fuelwood. Calorific value of sapwood 4986 cal. and that of heartwood 4909 cal. It also makes excellent charcoal.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, sapwood lighter in colour than the heartwood, olive grey to pale brown, very wide. Heartwood chocolate or purplish-brown, very small. Wood hard, heavy to very heavy, average weight 935 kg/m3 (58 lb/ft3), at 12 per cent moisture content; fine textured with slightly interlocked grain. Specific gravity 0.97 – 1.08 air dry. Somewhat straight to interlocked-grained, occasionally showing ribbon-like bands. Medium to nearly fine-textured. Rather close and even-grained.

A diffuse porous wood. Growth rings present, but inconspicuous. Pores small, indistinct or barely visible to eye, moderately numerous to numerous, mostly solitary and in short radial multiples of 2-3, occasionally more, also with some tendency towards tangential or oblique grouping; vessel lines inconspicuous on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues as thin sheaths or eyelets round the pores, often also confluent connecting tangentially or obliquely arranged pores. Rays very fine, not visible to eye, but seen under the lens as fine, numerous, closely-spaced lines. Vertical gum ducts of traumatic origin may occasionally be present in short or long tangential rows.

The timber is in universal use wherever obtainable and is highly esteemed for use as cart-axles and shafts, poles and for ploughs and tool handles of all types. In mines, it is used as side props in shafts and in horizontal galleries. In Tamil Nadu, it is used for building houses, for houses, for furniture, agricultural implements and also poles for carrying heavy loads. It is also used for making batons and picker arms and for bobbins and shuttles for textile industry. The timber can be made into durable plywood but veneers made from it are liable to crack and split in seasoning. Wood has the potential for paper pulp.

The timber is recommended as a substitute for teak and sal after suitable seasoning and preservative treatment.

Other uses:

Silk - Tasar silk worms are fed on the leaves.

Fodder – Leaf fodder to feed buffaloes and other cattle.

Gum – Tree produces a light coloured and valuable gum, known as gum gatty or Indian gum) that is twice as viscous as gum arabic. Gum used for sweetmeats, calico printing, dyeing, sizing of paper and pharmaceuticals. It is used in India and exported to Europe and North America.

Dye - Leaves yield a black dye, which is commercially useful.

Tannin - Leaves, bark and twigs contain 12-20%tanin, used in tanning industries.

Bark - It is medicinally useful. It is bitter and astringent. Particularly useful in chronic diarrhoea.

Pulp – recently the wood has been recognized for paper pulp.

Leaves - They are used as fodder leaves produce a black dye which is used in tanning.

Fruit - Fruits are medicinally important against diarrhoea and dysentry.
