Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

medium sized, handsome, evergreen tree, native of south-western India, where it occurs in the forests of western ghats from Konkan southwards to Tinnevelly hills up to 5,000 ft. It is also fairly common in the evergreen forests of upper Assam and in the hill districts, ascending to 3,500 ft. in Garo hills.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

medium sized, handsome, evergreen tree with smooth, yellowish grey bark, often flaking off in small pieces. Young parts clothed with rufous stellate pubescence. Leaves equally or unequally pinnate, rachis rusty-puberulous when young, afterwards glabrous, leaflets 2-5 pairs, shining above, red and conspicuous when young, coriaceous, oblong or ovate-lanceolate, more or less oblique, shortly acuminate, glabrous and reticulately veined, base cuneate, main nerves 10-15 pairs, prominent beneath.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers white, small, in clusters on the branches of terminal and axillary large much-branched puberulous panicles, pedicels short, pubescent. Calyx 5-6 lobed, lobes obtuse, densely pubescent. Petals 5-6, linear-spathulate, pubescent, about as long as the calyx. Disk pubescent. Stamens 6-10, included in the female, exserted in the male flowers; filaments hairy at the base. Ovary 2-3 lobed; style long, stigma 2, recurved. Fruit supported on the persistent calyx, edible, when young, globose, reddish, tubercled. Seed solitary, enveloped in a fleshy pale subacid aril, black, shining.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology: Flowers in March – May. Fruits ripen in June – September.

Floral and seed biology:

Longan is grown from seed. Vegetative propagation, where practised in S. China, is by inarching, though other forms of grafting are possible. It is a vigorous tree and is less tender to frost and drought than litchi. It needs generous manuring.

Other uses:

Fruit about the size of cherry and the pulpy aril is eaten. Fruit considered nutrient, stomachic and anthelmintic. Aril used as refrigerant in fevers. Dried aril is said to be used as tonic in China and Indo-China.

The leaves and flowers considered as medicinally useful.

In Bengal and other areas cultivated as an ornamental and shade tree. Bark - It is a source of tannin.
