DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, annual or perennial, much branched, herbs, 30-100 cm tall. Leaves ovate to lanceolate, 6-10 x 3-5 cm, acute at apex, cordate at base, dentate at margin, pubescent beneath
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers white, extra axillary 3-8 flowered umbellate-cymes. Fruits about 5-8 mm across, globose, black or red when ripe smooth, shining. Seeds many discoid, yellow, minute. It flowers and fruits between August-January.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Fruit is used in heart and eye disease, leucoderma, dysentry, hiccough, vomitting, asthma, bronchistis, fever, urinary discharges; Bark  is used in ear eye and nose disease. It is good for neck ulcer,chronic fever. Leaves areused for headache and disease of nose Seeds are useful in gonorrhoea. The juice in doses of six to eight  ounces in given to treat chronic enlargement of the liver. Young shoots are give in skin diseases. The juices of leaves are used in inflammation of kidney and bladder. The herb is native remedy for local applications to anther are pustules. Ripe fruit mixed with honey is used in case of pulmonary tuberculosis.


Chemistry:

·        Detection of solasonine and solamargine in leaves, glucoalkaloids from immature fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960 – 1969).

·        Solasodine type compounds from leaves an fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960 – 1969).

·        Identification of tigogenin in berries. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960 – 1969).

·        Solasodine and slasodiene obtained as aglycones of steroidal glycosides of berries; tigogenin and diosgenin also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Solasonine, solamargine and b-solamargine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Solasodine content in stems,leaves and berries 0.02, 0.03 and 0.7% respectively; stems and leaves contained a-solasonine and a-solamargine and berries in addition contained b-solamargine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Leaves contained highest tigogenin content (0.5%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Dry fruits contained chlorogenic (0.06), caffeic (0.02), traces of neochlorogenic and isochlorogenic acids and caffeoylglucose (0.01%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Diploid, tetraploid and hexaploid varieties of plant analysed for solasodine and diosgenin; diploid showed presence of these compounds (0.65 and 1.2% respectively). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Solasodine (0.02%) present in roots of Nepalese plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·        Tigogenin isolated from roots and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·        Fatty oil isolated from seeds contained palmitic, stearic, oleic and linoleic and acids along with linolenic acid (72%) predominating. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·        Uttronin A, uttrosides A and B isolated from stems and roots and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·        a-carotene isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·        Five steroidal glycosides – SN-O, SN-1, SN-2, SN-3 and SN-4 – isolated from immature berries; first four characterised as 26-O(b-D-glucopyranosyl)-22-methoxy-25D-5a-furost-3b,26-diol-3-O-b-lycotetraoside, desgalactotigonin, solamargine and solasonine respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·        Uttronin B, mp. 2180, isolated from roots and stems and its structures solasodine content in leaves and fruits of plant grown in Pakistan. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·        Six new steroidal alkaloids –SN-a, SN-b, SN-c, SN-d, SN-e and SN-f-isolated from immature berries, structures of first five alkaloids determined; solmargine and solasonine also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985 – 1989).

·        Isolation of solasodine, 12b-hydroxysolasodine, N-methylsolasodine, solanocapsine, tigogenin and tomatidenol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985 – 1989).

·        A new alkaloid –23-O-acetyl-12b-hydroxysolasodine – isolated and characterised. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985 – 1989).

·        Two new quercetin glycoside isolated from leaves and characterised as quercetin-3-O-[galactosylrhamnosyl (1-2)]-b-glucosyl (1-6)-b-galactoside and quercetin-3-O-a-rhamnosyl (1-2)-b-galactoside; quercetin-3-glucosyl (1-6) galactoside, -3-gentiobioside, -3galactoside and –3-glucoside also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985 – 1989).

·        Isolation of a-solamargine and a-solasonine from green unripe fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990 – 1994).

·        Two new steroidal saponins, named nigrumnins I and II, together with two known saponins were obtained from the whole plant of Solanum nigrum L. On the basis of spectroscopic analysis (1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, 1H-1H COSY, TOCSY, HMQC, HMBC and FAB-MS), nigrumnin I was established as (25R)-5a-spirostan-3b-ol 3-O-bD-xylopyranosyl-(1à3)-[a-L-arabinopyranosyl-(1 à2)]-b-D- glucopyranosyl-(1à4)-[a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1à2)]-b-D- galactopyranoside, and nigrumnin II was elucidated as (25R)-3β,17a-dihydroxy-5a-spirostan-1 2-one 3-O-β-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à3)-[a-L-arabinopyranosyl-(1à  2)]-b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1à 4)-[a-L-rhamnopyra- nosyl-(1à 2)l-b-D-galactopyranoside . ( Ikeda et al., 2000)

·        By bioactivity-guided fractionation, three known steroidal glycosides, b 2-solamargine,  solamargine, and degalactotigonin, were isolated from Solanum nigrum (Hu et al., 1999)

 

Pharmacology:

·        Alcoholic extract reduced levels of alkaline phosphatase, SGPT, B.S.P. clearance test values highly significantly, SGOT and thymol turbidity significantly an incresed body weight of treated rats significantly. Increase in RNA concentrationof liver was significantly more than that of DNA. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985 – 1989).

·        The presence of plant extracts of Solanum nigrum and Cichorium intybus in the reaction mixture containing calf thymus DNA and free radical generating system protect DNA against oxidative damage to its deoxyribose sugar moiety. The effect was dependent on the concentration of plant extracts. However, the effect of Cichorium intybus was much pronounced as compared to the effect of Solanum nigrum. These studies suggest that the observed hepatoprotective effect of these crude plant extracts may be due to their ability to suppress the oxidative degradation of DNA in the tissue debris. (Sultana et al., 1995)

·        Crude powder ethanol and petroleum ether extracts of S. nigrum fruits revealed moderate to good hepatoprotective action against CCl-4- and paracetamol-induced liver damage in rats as evidenced by enzymatic and histopathological examination. The crude drug powder exhibited better effects vs the extracts. Inhibition of lipid peroxidation and free radical scavenging activity is suggested as a possible mechanism of action. (Nadeem et al., 1997)

·        The ethanol extract of the fruit of Solanum nigrum L. (Solanaceae) was studied for its neuropharmacological properties on experimental animals. On intraperitoneal injection, the extract significantly prolonged pentobarbital-induced sleeping time, produced alteration in the general behavior pattern, reduced exploratory behavior pattern, suppressed the aggressive behavior, affected locomotor activity and reduced spontaneous motility. (Perez et al., 1998)

·        Toxicological studies on three ethanol extract preparations of Solanum nigrum L. leaves were made on Biomphalaria alexandrina. Extract (A), made by soaking leaves powder over night in cold 70% ethanol, has the highest activity, (LC50 3.37 mg/L within 24 hr). This extract also showed larvicidal activity against larvae of two mosquito species, Aedes caspius and Culex pipiens, (LC50 51.29 and 125.89 mg/L within 24 hr, and 21.38 and 38.11 mg/L within 48 hr, respectively). Sunlight, pH, and turbidity did not affect the activity of this extract, but the molluscicidal activity seems to be correlated with the increase of temperature. The concentrated extract (1000 mg/L) can be stored at room temperature for six months without any change in its activity, but diluted solutions of this extract lost their activity after four weeks. (Ahmed et al., 2001)

·        Anti inflammatory activity of Solanum nigrum. (Sarma et al., 2001)

·        The 50% ethanol extract of the whole plant of Solanum nigrum was tested in vitro for its cytoprotection against gentamicin-induced toxicity on Vero cells. Cytotoxicity was significantly inhibited as assessed by the Trypan blue exclusion assay and mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity (MTT) assay. The test extract also exhibited significant hydroxyl radical scavenging potential, thus suggesting its probable mechanism of cytoprotection. (Kumar et al., 2001)

·        Glycoprotein of Solanum nigrum Linn. (SNL glycoprotein) was isolated and identified using SDS-PAGE. SNL glycoprotein's antioxidative effects were tested in vitro and its cytotoxicity effects were tested using MCF-7 cells. One glycoprotein was isolated from SNL fruits, the other from stems and leaves. SNL glycoprotein is reactive with oxygen radicals, such as the -OH and O2- in vitro. Moreover, the SNL glycoprotein's radical scavenging activity is sensitive to the superoxide anion radical and the hydroxyl radical. In the MCF-7 cell, SNL glycoprotein I had a cytotoxic effect at 1 mug/mL and SNL glycoprotein II at 100 mug/mL. When MCF-7 cells were treated with SNL glycoproteins, nitrite oxide production was induced. (Lee and Lim, 2003)
