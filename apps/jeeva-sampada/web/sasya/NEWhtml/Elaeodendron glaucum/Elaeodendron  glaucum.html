 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
Moderate-sized, deciduous tree, or occasionally only a shrub, occurring throughout the hotter parts of India and in Ceylon.  Along the outer Himalaya, it ascends to 6,000ft.

   
Availability :  
Found in Konkan region, in Deccan near Poona, Sattara and Kamatki ghats, Belgaum,, Dharwar forests near Devilkope, Talbot, Kanara, Gonda forests and also Malayan Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A graceful small tree with leaves 2.5 to 6 by 1 to 2.5 inches, extremely variable, elliptic, acute or acuminate, often twisted at the apex, crenate-serrate or sub-entire, glabrous, base acute or rounded; petioles 0.5 to 0.75 inches long.  Flowers numerous, in divaricate axillary or extra-axillary paniculate dichotomously branched cymes; pedicels slender, glabrous; bracts small, ovate, acute.  Calyx very deeply divided; lobes unequal, orbicular, with membranous margins.  Petals long, oblong, obtuse, distant.  Stamens much shorter than the petals; anthers roundish.  Disk thick and fleshy.  Drupes obovoid, 1/3 to 1/2 inch long, apiculate, gray or blackish bark. Leaves opposite, elliptic cariaceous and small, yellowish brown,  flowers in axillary or extra-axillary dichotomous cymes.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
bark, wood and leaves

Uses

  • The plant yields a gum called 'Fumrasi'.  The gum obtained is a colourless to reddish brown gum that occurs in the form of round tears having a diameter of 1.5cms rough or cracked on the surface  It readily dissolves in water and produces a clear, pale yellow adhesive. This is a high grade gum. It is tasteless and forms a sherry-coloured solution with water.
  • Used in Tanning skins and hides.

Chemistry
'Fumrasi', occurs in roundish tears about half an inch in diameter, rough or cracked on the surface.  It is tasteless and forms a sherry-coloured solution with water.

Other Uses
  1. Powdered leaves used to relieve headache, as a fumigatory in hysteria.
  2. Cold water extract of crushed roots is used as an emetic.
  3. Bark used to dye cotton fabrics ochre-red and in native medicine it is said to be a virulent poison.
  4. Fresh bark paste with water applied to swellings.
  5. The root is a specific against snakebite.
  6. Wood used for cabinet work, combs and picture frames.  It is also employed  for fuel.
