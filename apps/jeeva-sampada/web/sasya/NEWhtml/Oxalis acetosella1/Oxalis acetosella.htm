 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
An herb found in temperate Himalayas from Kashmir to Sikkim at altitudes of 2,400-3,600 m. Generally it can be seen in Woodland, Sunny Edge, Dappled Shade, Shady Edge, Deep Shade, Ground Cover and Forests. 
Distributed to Europe, North Asia, North Africa and North America.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A small delicate, stem less perennial herb, 5-15 cm. high, with a long or short horizontally creeping, reddish knotty scaly rhizome. Leaves radical, trifoliolate with elongated petiole: leaflets broadly obcordate. Fruit is a capsule, 8 mm. long, egg-shaped with 2-3 seeds in each cell. 

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers solitary, white or pale rose, veined with purple, yellowish at base. The flowers are hermaphrodite (have both male and female organs)  and Cleistogamy (self-pollinating without flowers ever opening). The plant is self-fertile. The flowers lies  on elongated slender peduncles.

Breeding System :

Phenology :
It will flower from April to May and there is another report of flowering  during May-October. 

Pollinators:
This is known to be pollinated by bees and flies. 

Seed Biology :

Seedling Germination :
Seed - best sown as soon as it is ripe in a cold frame. Prick out the seedlings into individual pots when they are large enough to handle and plant them out in late spring or early summer. Division in spring. Very easy, larger divisions can be planted out direct into their permanent positions. We have found that it is better to pot up the smaller divisions and grow them on in light shade in a cold frame until they are well established before planting them out in late spring or early summer.

Cultivation :  
The plant prefers light (sandy), medium (loamy) and heavy (clay) soils. The plant prefers acid, neutral and basic (alkaline) soils. It can grow in full shade (deep woodland) semi-shade (light woodland) or no shade. It requires moist soil.
 Prefers moist shady conditions and a humus rich soil in shade or dappled sunlight.  Dislikes very heavy and wet soils. Plants are hardy to about -25°c. A dainty woodland carpenter growing well in a woodland or wild garden. When well sited the plants can run aggressively and also self-sow. The plant flowers in early spring, but does not produce much fertile seed at this time. Most of the fertile seed is produced from cleistogamous flowers during the summer.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Leaves, Flowers. 

Uses
  1. The leaves of the plant possess a refreshing sub acid flavour and are used as a substitute of sorrel (Rumex acetosa Linn.); they are used in salad and in parts of Europe as a spring vegetable. 
  2. Leaves - raw or cooked. A delicious lemony flavour, the leaves make a refreshing, thirst-quenching munch and are also added to salads, soups, sauces etc. This leaf should be used in moderation.
  3. Flowers - raw. A decorative addition to salads.
  4. The dried plant can be used as a curdling agent for plant milks.

Chemistry
The leaves contain large amount of potassium oxalate (0.86% in leaf juice). They are rich in vitamin C (183 mg./ 100 g.). The milk of cows feeding on O. acetosella yields butter with difficulty when churned (Ediin, 51 ;Uphof, 260; Medsger, 165; Wehmer, I, 591 ; Hocking, 158; Chem. Abstr., 1947, 41, 5584; Singh & Kohli, Indian vet. J., 1956, 32, 278).

Other Uses
  1. The plant possesses refrigerant, diuretic and anti-scorbutic properties. It is used in liver and digestive disorders, febrile diseases, urinary affections, catarrh, gonorrhea and hemorrhage; it is used also to remove cancerous growths from the lips. In England, an antiputi-escent gargle is concocted against m with the leaves and petals of the plant. The plant used in homeopathic medicine. It is poisonous if taken in large doses.
  2. The juice of the leaves removes iron mould stains from linen.  Plants can be grown as a ground cover in woodland or under the shade of shrubs. They should be spaced about 45cm apart each way.
  3. Anodyne; Antiscorbutic; Astringent; Diuretic; Emmenagogue; Expectorant; Febrifuge; Irritant; Stomachic.
    The fresh or dried leaves are anodyne, antiscorbutic, astringent, diuretic, emmenagogue, expectorant, febrifuge, irritant and stomachic. A decoction is used in the treatment of fevers, both to quench the thirst and allay the fever. Externally, the leaves are crushed and applied locally to dispel boils and abscesses, they also have an astringent affect on wounds. When used internally, some caution is advised due to the oxalic acid content of the leaves, the plant is contra-indicated for people suffering from gastritis or a calculus condition.
