DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Large trees up to 20 m tall; branches with prominent leaf scars. Leaves paripinnate, 25-50 cm long, crowded at ends of branches; leaflets 3-6 pairs, opposite or alternate, ovate-oblong, 5-20 x 2.5-12 cm, coriaceous, dark green.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in axillary panicles, c 1 cm across, greenish-white. Staminal tube cup-shaped. Fruits c 7.5 cm long. Capsules ovoid, smooth, black when ripe, woody. Seeds c 5 cm long, compressed, winged.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Bark is anthelmintic, refragerant, aphrodisiac, laxative; used for the tereatment of sore throat, fever, cough, asthma, blood impurities, ulcers, leprosy and dysentery (Ayurveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933). Bark is used in bowel as astringent and also used in fever.


Chemistry:

·         Lupeol, sitosterol and methyl angolensate isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         In addition, deoxyandirobin isolated from wood and bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Quercetin-3-O-L-rhamnoside and 3-O-rutinoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Two new tetranortriterpenoids (I to II) isolated from bark and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Another new tetranortriterpenoid – febrifugin – isolated from heartwood and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Naringenin, myricetin, dihydromyricetin and quercetin isolated from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         A process for isolation of methyl angolensate and deoxyandirobin developed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·         Febrinins A and B isolated from heartwood and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·         Three new tetranortriterpenoids – epoxyfebrinin B, 14, 15-dihydroepoxy febrinin Band febrinolide – isolated from fruits and their structures elucidated; deoxyandirobin, 17b-hydroxy-6a-acetoxyazadiradione, methyl angolensate and sitosterol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·         Seed oil showed presence of linoleic, linolenic, oleic, palmitic and stearic acids, lupeol and sitosterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Pharmacology:

·        Antiplasmodial activity of Soymida febrifuga. (Simonsen et al., 2001)
