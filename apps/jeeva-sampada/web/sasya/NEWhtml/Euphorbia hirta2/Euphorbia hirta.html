 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
 Annual, erect or decumbent pubescent herb with milky latex. It is found to occur throughout the hotter parts of India at 200-1370 m. Very common, in grassy fields, roadsides, waste places, gardens, agricultural fields.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual, erect or decumbent pubescent herb with milky latex. Leaves opposite, short petiolate, stipulate, oblong or elliptic-lanceolate, acute at apex, oblique at base, serrulate at margin, sparsely hairy above, long hairy beneath; stipules subulate. Cyathia in axillary cymes. Involucres sparsely hairy without; glands 4 each with a minute limb. Male flowers: 4-6, anthers dehiscing longitudinally. Female flowers: styles 2-fid. Capsules globose, appressed hairy. Seeds ovoid, 4 angled, brownish, rugolose.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Cyathium aggregated in axillary clusters, each cyathium  consists of 5 involucre of bracts, fused   to form of  cups  On the inner side of each bracts a gland is present. In  the center of cyathium, there is single   female flower surrounded  by many male flowers;  male flower  represented bya single stamen,which is seprated from the pedicle by a joint,dithecous,introrse ;  female flower  represented by gynoecium raised on long pedicle,tricar pellary,syncapous,ovary superior,trilocular,style absent  or  short,stigma 3,each is bifurcated ;  fruit  regma spiltting into 3 cocci ; Flowering & Fruiting: Most of the part of the year.

Floral and seed biology: Fl. & Fr.: Throughout the year, chiefly during July to November.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.  insects  . Seeds are dispersed by   wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Plant decoction is used as an ingredient of medicines for cough and asthma. Plant juice is used in dysentery and in diseases of urinogenital tracts. It is applied to children affected by worms, bowels, cough. It is also prescribed in gonorrhoea. Santals use the root to allay vomiting and the plant to nursing mothers when the supply of milk is deficient. Leaf juice is applied in eye troubles. This herb is also used as astringent, to cure chronic diarrhoea, dysentery, ulcers, oedomas, phlegmons, apathae; used as tonic, narcotic, antiasthmatic.

The leaves of Euphorbia hirta were analyzed for their nutritive value and antinutritionalfactors. Protein-content of the leaves ranged between 2.60% and 3.42%, while fiber and mineral (ash) contents were 1.15% - 7.73% and 1.48% - 2.86% respectively. Methionine was the most limiting amino acid in Euphorbia hirta. The highest chemical score was recorded for Euphorbia hirta protein. Euphorbia hirta leaves contained the highest concentration of calcium (175 mg%), Copper (14.7 mg%), iron (45.8mg%) and lead (26.4 mg%). (Wallace et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 18, 320822q.

A chemotaxonomic study of Euphorbia hirta based on their flavanoid profile reported. Twenty two flavanoids (13 flavanols, 8 flavones and 1 flavanone) were isolated from different species using paper chromatography and thin layer chromatography techniques. The identification was done by UV spectroscopy. (Aqueveque et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 16493x.

The n-hexane extract of the aerial parts of Euphorbia hirta and its main constituent triterpenes, b-amyrin, 24-methyl-enecycloartenol, and b-sitosteol. (Martinez et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 189454j.


Pharmacology:

A hygroscopic base and plant extract showed antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Plant extract exhibited antidiarrhoeal activity in experimental models of diarrhoea induced by castor oil, arachidonic acid and prostaglandin E2, but not in model of magnesium sulphate provoked diarrhoea. It delayed transit in small intestine when this was accelerated in castor oil but not in normal condition. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Quercitrin (50.0 mg/kg) showed antidiarrhoeic activity against castor oil and PGE2-induced diarrhoea in mice. It also delayed rat small intestinal transit when this was accelerated with castor oil. However, when administered intralumnally it did not modify fluid transport across colonic mucosa either in normal condition or when this transport was altered by PGE2 or Sodium picosulphate. Quercetin increased colonic fluid absorption only in presence of secretagogue compounds such as PGE2 and sodium picosulphate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Ingenol triacetate was potent inducer of nerve growth factor production in L-M cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Lyophilised aqueous extract of Euphorbia hirta L. (Euphorbiaceae) has been evaluated for analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory properties in mice and rats, in order to complete its activity profile, after the confirmation of the existence of a central depressant activity particularly expressed by a strong sedative effect, associated with anxiolytic effects. This study leads us to the conclusion that this plant extract exerts central analgesic properties. Such a dose-dependent action was obtained against chemical (writhing test) and thermic (hot plate test) stimuli, respectively, from the doses of 20 and 25 mg/kg and it was inhibited by a naloxone pretreatment, a specific morphinic antagonist compound. An antipyretic activity was obtained at the sedative doses of 100 and 400 mg/kg, on the yeast-induced hyperthermia. Finally, significant and dose-dependent anti-inflammatory effects were observed on an acute inflammatory process (carrageenan-induced edema test in rats) from the dose of 100 mg/kg. On the other hand, plant extract remained inactive on chronic processes such as Freund's adjuvant-induced rheumatoid arthritis, after a chronic treatment during fourteen days at the daily dose of 200 or 400 mg/kg; however, if inefficacy was observed on rat backpaws edema and on loss of weight, the aqueous extract reduced the inflammatory hyperalgia. (Lanhers et al, 1991).

The antidiarrhoeic activity of the Euphorbia hirta whole plant was investigated. The lyophilized decoction demonstrated antidiarrhoeic activity in experimental models of diarrhoea induced by castor oil, arachidonic acid, and prostaglandin E2. It showed no activity when magnesium sulphate was used to provoke the diarrhoea. The lyophilized decoction delayed small intestinal transit when this was accelerated by castor oil but not in normal conditions. A flavonoid, quercitrin, with antidiarrhoeic activity was isolated from this crude drug. (Galvez et al, 1993).

Aqueous extracts of Euphorbia hirta L was investigated for their influence on prostaglandin biosynthesis (PG I2, PG E2, PG D2) which strongly reduced the release of prostaglandins I2, E2, and D2. Additionally Euphorbia hirta extracts exerted an inhibitory effect on platelet aggregation and depressed the formation of carrageenin induced rat paw oedema. The chemical nature of the active principle of Euphorbia hirta could be characterized as (a) compound(s) of medium polarity in the molecular weight range of 1000 to 3000 Da. (Hiermann and Bucar, 1994).

Fraction of Euphorbia hirta (50 chloroform : 50 methanol) was evaluated for male antifertillity effects at a dose level of 50 mg/kg body weight. The sperm motility and density of cauda epidymal and testis sperm suspensiondeclined significantly, leading to 100 percent negative fertility. The weights of sex organs were lower than control. The androgen parameters decreased after the treatment. The testes showed degenerative changes in histoarchitecture. The sertoli cell area was reduced by 53 percent. The intertubular space increased and the number of normal tubules declined. Euphorbia hirta changed the internal milieu of the reproductive organs including antifertility in male rats. (Mathur et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9602-0827.

The methanol extract obtained from the leaves and stems of Euphorbia hirta inhibited the activity of angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) by 90 percent and 50 percent at 500 microg and 160 microg respectively. Intraperitoneal administration of 10mg/ 100 mg body wt of the extract significantly (p less than 0.05) decreased the amount of water consumed by rats. This effect lasted 2h. (Williams et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9801-0246

The invention relates to a compound or group o compounds present in an active principle derived from plants of the species Euphorbia hirta and to pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds. Extracts from these plants have been found to show selective cytotoxicity against several different cancer cell lines. The compounds are useful in effective treatment of cancers, particularly malignant melanomas and squamous cell carcinomas. (Aylward, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 205113p.

Euophorbias hirta is used locally in Africa And Australia to treat hypertension and edema. The diuretic effect of Euphorbiahirta leaf extracts were assessed in rats using acetazolamide and furosemide as standard diuretic drugs. The water and ethanol extracts (50 and 100 mg/kg) of the plant produced time-dependent increase in urine output. Electrolyte excretion was also significantly affected by the plant extracts. (Johnson et al, 1999).

EtOH and CH2Cl2 extracts from E. hirta, showed a significant chemosuppression of parasitaemia in mice infected with P. berghei berghei at orally given doses of 100-400 mg/kg per day. (Tona et al, 1999)a.

Whole plant aqueous extracts from Euphorbia hirta with other plant extract act as antidiarrhoeic agents by a triple pronounced antibacterial, antiamoebic and antispasmodic action. (Tona et al, 1999)b.

The hexane extract and the triterpenes exerted significant and dose-dependent anti-inflammatory activity in the model of phorbol acetate – induced ear inflammation in mice. Some dual and triple combinations of the triterpenes were tested as anti-inflammatory activity than the individual tritepenes alone. (Martinez et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 189454j.

Three major extracts from some traditional preparations, based on Euphorbia hirta and with other plants, used as antidiarrhoeal agents were investigated for their putative antiamoebic and spasmolytic activities in vitro. Results indicated that both biological activities are concentrated in the polyphenolic fraction, and not in the saponin or alkaloid containing fractions. The most active was found to be the polyphenolic extracts. (Tona et al, 2000).

The methanol extract of Euphorbia hirta and other plant was studied against dysentery causing Shigella spp. using the vero cell line. This extract was found to be non-cytotoxic and effective antibacterial agents. (Vijaya et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9603-1530.
