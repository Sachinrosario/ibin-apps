DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves long, petiolate, digitately 5 rarely 7 foliate; leaflets slightly tomentose above, canescent below, lanceolate, acuminate at apex, attenuate at base, petiolulate, subentire. Cymes compact, subsessile, forming long spiked verticilasters, bracts minute or absent. Calyx tomentose, campanulate, teeth triangular, obtuse. Corolla lilac, 3 times long than calyx, tube subexerted, throat inflated, mouth glabrous. Drupes spherical, obtuse, slightly longer than calyx.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurvedic system of medicine seeds are used as stomachic, abortifacient, douretic, alexiteric and biliousness. It is useful in “Kapa” and “Vata”. It is also used in pruritus, itching, burning sensation and thirst. In Unani system the seeds are bitter astringent and used in dropsy, pain, inflammation and enlargement of spleen.


Chemistry:

·        Casticin, mp.186oC, characterized as 5, 2’-dihydroxy-3,6,7,4’-tetramethoxyflavone. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra 1960- 1969)b

·        Oils of V. agnus castus obtained by hydrodistillaion of the leaves, flowers and fruits, were analyzed by capillary GC.  The leaf oil was rich in 1,8-cineole+ limonene (22.42 – 30.92 percent), sabinene (15.00 – 19.16 percent) and alpha-pinene (4.32 – 6.48 percent) the flower oil contained 1,8 – cineole + limonene (12.18 – 18.43 percent), sabinene (9.34 – 9.99 percent) and alpha – pinene (2.01 – 12.00 percent), while the fruit oil contained 1,8 – cineole + limonene (20.73 – 23.36 percent), sabinene (16.09 – 22.28 percent) and alpha – pinene. (6.60 – 19.73 percent) (Kustrak et. al., 1994). MAPA-9501-0452.

·        Essential oil obtained by hydrodistillation of leaves, inflorescences and fruits of Vitex agnus castus. Yields ranged from 0.8 to 1.8 percent on dry weight basis. Eighty – five compounds were identified by GC-MS. The ratio of hydrocarbon: oxygen-containing components were nearly 1:1. 1,8 Cineole, alpha-terpineol sabinene, beta caryophyllene, beta-selinene and cis-beta-farnesene were the main components in the oils. (Senatore, et. al., 1996).

·        Vitex agnus castus is used against disorders of the female sexual organs and as an anaphrodisiac. The essential oil of the fruits was investigated by GC and GC-MS. The yield of essential oil obtained by hyddrodistillation was 0.71 percent. 73 compounds were found in the oil, of which 58 constituents, representing 87 percent of the oil were identified. The main components were sabinene beta phellandrene, beta caryophyllene and germacrene B. (Zwaving and Bos, 1996). MAPA 9701-0575.

·        Four new flavonoids, luteolin 6-C-(4”-methyl-6”-O-trans-caffeoylglucoside), luteolin 6-C-(6”-O-trans-caffeoylglucoside), luteolin 6-C-(2”-O-trans-caffeoylglucoside), and luteolin7-O-(6”-p-benzoylglucoside), together with four known ones 5, 4’-dihydroxy-3,6,7,3’-tetramethoxyflavone, luteolin, artemetin and isorhamnetin, were isolated from the root bark of Vitex agnus castus. The structures were elucidated by spectroscopic means. (Hirobe et al., 1997).

·        The content of total polyphenols and tannins in the leaves, flowers and fruits of V. agnus collected from various plants of Croatia were determined by spectormetric method according to Schneide. The investigation results showed the variety of total polyphenol content b/w plant parts (leaves 7.36 – 13.20 percent, flowers 9.00 – 10.64 percent and fruits 6.92 – 13.24%) The highest tannin content was found in leaves but it was similar in flowers and fruits (0.24-2% resp . 0.24-1.60%). (Antolic and Males, 1997). CA-128, 59076 u.

·        The content of total polyphenols and tannins in the leaves, flowers and fruits of Vitex agnus castus were determined by spectrometric method according to Schneider. The investigation results showed the variety of total polyphenol content between plant parts (leaves 7.36 – 13.20 percent, flowers 9.00 – 10.64 percent and fruits 6.92 – 13.24 percent). The highest tannin content was found in leaves. (0.68 – 3.00 percent), but it was similar in flowers and fruits (0.24 – 2.00 percent, respectively 0.24 – 1.60 percent. (Antolic and Males, 1997). MAPA-9805-2972.

·        Quantitative spectrometric analyses of flavinoids, tannins and total polyphenols in leaves, flowers and fruits of Vitex agnus castus L. f. rosea from three locations in Croatia are exported. The results point to high variability of analytes content not only as a function of plant part but also sampling site and sampling stage, content of flavonoids in leaves, flowers and fruits was found to be 0.18-0.32, 0.09-0.14 and 0.05-0.08% respectively. The tannins content in leaves ranged from 1.12 to 2.68% in flowers from 0.88 to 2.28% and in fruits from 0.80 to 1.16% whereas the content of total polyphenols varied in leaves (7.12% - 10.76%) and fruits (4.88 – 8.72%), but not in flowers of (08-8.48%). (Males, Zeljan, 1998). CA 130, 49828t.

·        The major components of leaf oil of Vitex agnus castus are 1,8- cineole , sabinene and (E)beta farnesene . The chemical composition of oils from Berin, Nigeria Italy and Croatia were qualitatively compared. (Moudachirou, et. al., 1998). MAPA 9806, 3604.

·        The essential oil of leaves and flowers of Vitex agnus castus f. rosea was obtained by hydrodistillation. Its quantification was done gravimetrically. Its composition was investigated by GC and GC-MS. The oil was composed of monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes. The main constituents of monoterpenoid fractions were limonene, cineole, sabinene and alpha-terpineol, while the main sesquiterpenes were beta-caryophyllene,beta gurjunene, cuparene and glubulol. (Males et al., 1998).

·        The composition of essential oils was investigated of leaves and flowers of Vitex agnus castus f. rosea. The essential oil yields ranged from 0.33-0.52%. The oils were composed of monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes. The principal constituents of the terpenoid fractions were limonene, cineole, sabinene and a- terpineol, while the major sesquiterpenes were b- gurjunene, cuparene and globulol. (Males et al., 1998).

·        Vitex agnus castus L. was extracted with carbon dioxide in a high pressure apparatus with 5 L extractor vessel volume. The effects of the extraction parameters (pressure, temp.) on the yield and recovery of the active compounds were determined by 32 full factorial designed experiments. Fractionation of extracts was carried out by releasing the separation. Pressure at two stages. The extracts were separated into essential oil rich “oil” and fatty/waxy products. (Simandi  et al., 1999). CA 1365, 362322b.

·        Variation in essential oil yield and composition of Cretan Vitex agnus castus was carried out. (Sorensen et al., 1999). CA-132:170829p.

·        The essential oils of fresh leaves, flowers, and fruits of V. agnus castus, growing in north Brazil, were obtained by hydrodistillation and their volatile constituents were identified by GC – MS. The most abundant constituents were 1, 8 – cineole (E)-beta- farnesene, sabinene, alfa pinene, alfa terpenyl acetate, beta – caryophyllene, and bicyclogermacrene, Their compositions were compared with oils from Europe and Nigeria. (Zoghbi et al., 1999). CA.132, 141660w.

·        Use of carbon dioxide in supercritical fluid extraction from chaste tree was made. (Prechl et al., 2000). CA 134, 120747f.

·        Mature and immature fruits of Vitex agnus castus L. were chosen to investigate different parameters such as comminution, maturity, distillation period and extraction method influencing the essential oil yield and composition. The effect of the comminution and the maturity of the plant material showed highly significant differences in yield and composition of the essential oils obtained, as well as the distillation duration from one to five hours and the method applied (hydrodistillation and simultaneous distillation extraction. The results showed that many different essential oil qualities can be obtained from the same plant material according to the parameters employed in its extraction. Entire fruits hydrodistilled for one hour yielded oil much richer in monoterpene hydrocarbons and oxygenated compounds whereas the best combination to obtain an oil rich in less volatile compounds is by SDE of comminuted fruits for five hours. For mature fruits the main components varied as follows due to the parameters studied: sabinene16.4-44.1%, 1,8-cineole 8.4-15.2%, beta-caryophyllene 2.1-5.0%, and trans-beta-farnesene 5.0-11.7%. (Sorensen and Katsiotis, 2000).

·        Quantitative high performance liquid chromatographic analysis of casticin in the fruits of Vitex agnus castus was carried out. (Hoberg et al., 2001). CA-135, 157793q.

Pharmacology:

·        Seed oil showed progesterone like effect in mature female rats. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra, 1960- 1969)a

·        In vitro prolactin but not LH and FSH release is inhibited by compounds in extracts of Agnus castus. (Jarry et al., 1994). CA 122:97074a.

·        V. castus extracts contain do-paminergic compounds which are inhibitory to pitutary prolactin release and therefore beneficial for the treatment of premenstrual martodynia. (Wuttke et al., 1995). CA 125: 67387z.

·        V. castus extracts contain do-paminergic compounds which are inhibitory to pitutary prolactin release and therefore beneficial for the treatment of premenstrual martodynia. (Wuttke et al., 1995). CA 125: 67387z.

·        Male volunteers received thrice daily oral administration of the V. aguns extracts, and basal and TRH-stimulated prolactin secretions were measured at various times during the day. The circadian pattern of prolactin secretion was not altered by the drug. However, th lowest dose tested (ext. from 120 mg of the plant) stimulated the extent of both the basal and TRH-stimulated prolactin secretion, whereas two higher doses (extracts form 240 and 480 mg of the plant) inhibited these parameters. Various side effects were produced (allergic, gastrointestinal, sensory, psychic), but these were generally transitory and of low intensity. (Merz et al., 1995). CA 125:26141w.

·        The premenstrual tension syndrome (PMTS) scale, treatment with Vitex agnus castus produced a reduction in score points from 15.2 to 5.1 (-47.4 percent). (Lauritzen et al., 1996). MAPA: 9803-1422.

·        The effective administration of ethanol seed extracts of Vitex agnus castus against mastodynia and symptoms related to female cycle disorders with concomitant hyperprolactinemia has been documented. (Odenthal, 1998). 130: 61020 f.

·        The study of Vitex agnus castus as double –blind, placebo was carried out of controlled in two parallel groups (each 50 patients). Treatment phase lasted 3 consequent menstrual cycles (2 x 30 drops/day = 1.8 ml of VACS) or placebo. Mastalgia during at least 5 days of the cycle before the treatment was the strict inclusion condition. For assessment of the efficacy visual analogue scale was used. Altogether 97 patients were included into the statistical analysis (VACS: n = 48, placebo: n = 49). Intensity of breast pain diminished quicker with VACS group. The tolerability was satisfactory. It have been found that VACS to be useful in the treatment of cyclical breast pain in women. (Halaska  et. al., 1998).

·        Vitex agnus castus was studied and is proposed that the diterpenes are one fraction of the biological active compounds of BNO 1095 which contribute to the clinical efficacy in pathophysiol. Conditions with elevated prolactin. (Christoffel et al., 1999). CA 133:217598 a.

·        Vitex agnus and other plant extracts used for breast enhancement in human females by strengthening connective tissues and encouraging the growth of new cells. (Ernest et al., 1999).

·        The pharmacological effects of EtOH extracts of V. agnus castus fruit (esp. Ze 440) and of various extract fractions of different polarities were evaluated both by radioligical and binding studies and by superfusion experiments. (Meier  Berger, 2000).

·        The pharmacological effects of ethanolic Vitex agnus-castus fruit-extracts (especially Ze 440) and various extract fractions of different polarities were evaluated both by radioligand binding studies and by superfusion experiments. A relative potent binding inhibition was observed for dopaminer D2 and opioid (micro and kappa subtype) receptors with IC50 values of the native extract between 20 and 70 mg/mL. Binding, neither to the histaminer H1, benzodiazepine and OFQ receptor, nor to the binding-site of the serotonin (5-HT) transporter, was singinficantly inhibited. The lipophilic fractions contained the diterpenes rotun-difuran and 6beta, 7beta-diacetoxy-13-hydroxy-labda-8, 14-dien. They exhibited inhibitory actions on dopamine D2 receptor binding. While binding inhibition to mu and kappa opioid receptors was most pronounced in lipophilic fractions, binding to delta opioid receptors was inhibited mainly by aqueous fraction. Standardised Ze 440 extracts of different batches wee of constant pharmacological quality according to their potential to inhibit the binding to D2 receptors. In superfusion experiments, the aqueous fraction of a methanolic extract inhibited to D2 receptors. In superfusion experiments, the aqueous fraction of a methanolic extract inhibited the release of acetylcholine in a concentratio dependent manner. In addition, the potent D2 receptor antagonist spiperone antagonised the effect of the extract suggesting a dopaminergic action mediated by D2 receptor activation. (Meier et al., 2000).

·        The efficacy of the complex medication Phyto-Hypophyson L in female, hormone-related sterility. A randomized, placebo-controlled clinical double-blind study of Vitex agnus was done. In women with sterility an oligomenorrhea, a treatment with Phyto Hypophyson L can be recommended over a period of 3-6 months. (Bergmann et al., 2000).

·        Treatment of cyclical mastalgia with a solution containing a Vitex agnus castus extract: Results of a placebo-controlled double-blind study. (Tschudin and Huber, 2000).

·        A multicentric noninterventional trial (open study without control) to investigate the efficacy and tolerance of a drug in a large number of patients under routine medical conditions was performed for a new solid preparation from an extract of a the fruit of Vitex agnus castus in 1634 patients suffering from premenstrual syndrome (PMS). A specific questionnaire was developed for determining the effect of Vitex on psychic and somatic complaints, on the four characteristic PMS symptom complexes depression, anxiety, craving, and hyperhydration (DACH), and on single groups of symptoms. After a treatment period of three menstrual cycles 93% of patients reported a decrease in the number of symptoms or even cessation of PMS complaints. (Loch et al, 2000).

·        Efficacy of Vitex agnus castus L. extract Ze 440 in patients with pre-menstrual syndrome (PMS) was studied and found that a symptomatic relief occurs rather than the response being dependant on duration of syndrome. (Berger et al., 2000). CA 135: 87094m.

·        The review covers medicinal application of the fruit of V. agnus especially for gynecology and hormonal uses. (Irsy Gabriella, 2001).

·        The n-hexane extract shows activity in the tracheospasmolytic bioassay was fractionated by solvent extraction and from the major active fraction two compounds were isolated and identified as viteosin-A and vitexicarpin. These compounds blocked spontaneous contraction of isolated male guinea pig trachea induced by histamine; however only vitexicarpin was active in model using sensitized guinea pig trachea stimulated by ovalbumin up to minimum dose of 1.3 x 10 (-5) M. The result suggests that vitexicarpin is able to block effects of histamine released from sensitized mast cells possibly by stabilizing the mast cells membrane function. (Alam et al., 2002).

·        Vitex agnus-castus used as a viable option of phytoestrogens. (Russell et. al., 2002).

·        Evaluation of estrogenic activity of vitex agnus castus extracts for the potential treatment of menopausal symptoms. Methanol extracts of chasteberry (Vitex agnus – castus L.) showed significant competitive binding to estrogen receptors alpha (ER alpha) and beta (ER beta). (Liu et al., 2001).

·        Both oils were found to be effective, although the leaf essential oil appears to have a broader range of actions as it also addresses psychological aspects. ( Lucks  et al., 2002).

 

Toxicology

·        Antimicrobial activity of the etheric and ethanolic extract of Vitex agnus castus was studied by the dilution method. The extracts were active against all tested micro-organisms: Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus faecalis (6.5-20 percent extracts), Salmonella species, Escherichia coli (10 – 20 percent), Candida albicans, C.tropicalis, C. pseudotropicalis, and C. krusei (10.40 percent). The extracts also inhibited the growth of dermatophytes and moulds species. High toxicity against the mycelial growth of Trichophyton metagrophytes, Epidermophyton floccosum, Microsporum canis  and  M. gypseum  in dilutions in the range from 1.5 to 12 percent and Penicillium virdicatum was found. (Pepeljnjak, et. al., 1996). MAPA 9703-1618.

·        A crude extract was pepared with ethanol from dried ripened Vitex agnus castus fruits growing in Israel (Vitex extract). Cytotoxicity of the extract against human uterine cervical canal fivroblast (HCF), human embryo fibroblast (HE-21), ovarian cancer (MCF-7), cervical carcinoma (COLO 201), and small cell lung carcinoma (Lu-134-A-H) cells was examined. After culture for 24 h (logarithmic growth phase) or 72 h (stationary growth phase), the cells were treated with various concentrations of Vitex extract. In both growth phases, higher growth activity of cells and more cytotoxic activity of Vitex extract were seen. The cytotoxic activity against stationary growth-phase cells was les than that against logarithmic growth-phase cells. (Ohyama, et. al., 2003).
