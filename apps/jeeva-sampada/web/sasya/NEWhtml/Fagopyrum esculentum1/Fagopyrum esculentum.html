HABIT & HABITAT
It is a herbaceous plant and found in  Higher altitudes (2000-12000 ft.) of Himalayas, from Kashmir to Sikkim, Khasi hills and Manipur and  Nilgiris.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An annual herb, 3-5 ft. high, with alternate hastate, acute leaves, 1-3 inches long; flowers in axillary or terminal cymes, pinkish white, fragrant, dimorphic and highly selfsterile; fruit a 3 cornered achene, 0.2-0.4 inches long with keeled edges, varying in colour from silvery grey to brown or black.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
      Inflorescence: Axillary and terminal cymes ; Flower: Pink or white, pedicellate and bracteate ; Perianth: Segments 6, inner accresent, entire ; Androecium: Stamens 6, filaments short, anther oblong; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-gonous, ovule solitary, style 3, stigma fimbricate ; Fruit: Nut trigonous, with acute angles, light browned polished ; 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : January. 

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
 Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome numbers -16, 32.

Chemistry:
CIMAP:   The later flowering cultivars produced a higher content of rutin than the earlier flowering cultivars in the summer cropping. The difference in the cumulative solar radiation received in the two seasons seems to explain this increase in the rutin content of the buckwheat flour. The rutin content of early flowering cultivars, which are suitable for long day conditions, is pos. correlated with yield in the summer cropping. The rutin content in the intermediate and late summer ecotype cultivars, which are suitable for short day conditions, show no correlation with yield in the late summer cropping. There are wide intervarietal variations of rutin contents in buckwheat flour and the rutin content increases under long day conditions1; Six flavonoids have been isolated and identified in buckwheat grain. These are rutin, orientin, vitexin, quercetin, isovitexin and isoorientin. Rutin and isovitexin are the only flavonoid components of buckwheat seeds while hulls contains all six identified compds. The total flavonoid concn. in the seeds was 18-8 and in the hulls 74 mg/ 100 g of dry matter. Dehulling the grain by using different temp. regimes resulted in drastic redn. of the total flavonoids concn. in the grain (by 75% of the control) and smaller but significant (15 –20 %) redn. in the hulls2; Buckwheat was stored for the first 25 days at low temp. then stored at room temp. (30˚ ) and changes in the colour, pH, free fatty acids content were obsd. There was little change in the colour, but the decrease and free fatty acid increase were obsd. The cold storge was effective in maintaining the fragrance3.
NBRI: Presence of rutin, quercetin, and 4 unidentified flavanoids by PC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
Kaempferol-3-rhamnosylglucoside, p-cumaroylquinic and feruloylquinic acids isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
L-2-(2-furoyl)alanine (fig. 1) isolated from seeds and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Two new amides -N5-(2-hydroxybenzyl)-allo-4-hydroxyglutamine (fig. 2a) and N5-(4-hydroxybenzyl)-L-glutamine (fig. 2b) -isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
A new piperidine derivative -fagomine (fig. 3), mp.176° -from seeds characterised as 3,4-dihydroxy-2-hydroxymethylpiperidine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.
Quercetin (0.0021), quercitrin (0.0047), hyperin (0.17) and rutin (0.006%) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.
Isolation and characterization of eriodictyol-5-O-methyl-7-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-galactopyranoside from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Two new dihydroflavanol glycosides - aromadendrin-3-O-galactoside and taxifolin-3-O-xyloside - obtained from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Brassinolide and casterone isolated from pollen. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Isolation of Fagopyritol A1, A2 and B3 from buckwheat Compounds which include two or more of Fagopyritol A1, Fagopyritol A2, Fagopyritol B1, Fagopyritol B2, Fagopyritol B3, and D-chiro-inositol at least one of which is an isolated Fagopyritol A1, isolated Fagopyritol A2, or isolated Fagopyritol B3, are also disclosed. The fagopyritols can be used to prepare pharmaceutical compounds, the administration of which can be used to treat diabetes. (Obendorf and Horbowicz, 1998). C.A. 199, 130, 12459x.

NBPGR:  Leaves contains 3.5-4.7 per cent, while fruits yield about 1.3 per cent rutin.  Also contain fagopyrin, flavanol, fagomine, alanive, rhamnodiastase, enzymes hydrolyse and various flavanone glycosides.

Pharmacology: Perfusion of quercetin (2 - 20 ppm) in rats and guinea pigs decreased permeability of dermal capillary walls. It was about 1.3 times as active as rutin. It showed a transitory vasodilator effect. At 40 ppm. It lowered tonus of smooth musculature and showed about five times the spasmolytic activity of rutin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Rutin (100 and 200 mg/kg) and quercetin (100 and 200 mg/kg) when orally administered for 30 days produced dose-dependent increase in biliary secretion and increased faecal excretion of bile acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.
Rutin and quercetin increased survival time of mice inoculated with NK/Ly ascites tumor cells; increase was maximum when mice were given rutin (2.0 mg) twice daily for 8 days. Luteolin, morin and pelargonidin also inhibited growth of ascites tumor cells in vitro. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Quercetin protected mice from lethal Meningo virus infcction when given orally 12 hr before and up to 56 hr after inoculation. Necrotic lesions in central nervous system, salivary and lacrimal glands, thymus, pancreas, kidneys and spleen from both normal and immuno-deficient animals were less severe and developed later in quercetin-treated mice than in placebo-treated ones. Quercetin enhanced graft-vs.-host reaction, but failed to affect humoral antibody response of mice to sheep red blood cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Quercetin inhibited aggregation of human platelets in the same manner as calmodulin inhibitors. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.
Quercetin inhibited both phasic and tonic components of anaphylactic smooth muscle contraction of ileum from guinea pigs sensitised to egg albumin, in concentration- dependent fashion. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.
Study of effect of quercetin on calcium release in rabbit skinned muscle (sarcoplasmic reticulum) fibres indicated that quercetin increases rate of tension development in muscle fibres not only by inhibition of calcium influx but aIso by significant stimulation of calcium efflux and, possibly, by increase in Ca2+ sensitivity of a Ca2+ induced calcium release mechanism. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.
Both quercetin and naringenin inhibited the dextransucrase which produces a-1,3-rich (insoluble) glucan to a higher degree than the dextransucrase which forms a-1,6-rich (soluble) glucan, thus, quercetin strongly inhibited adhesive glucan, formation by Streptococcus mutans on human dental plaque. Naringenin, hesperetin and chrysin showed only partial suppressive effect. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.
A study of effect of quercetin on human basophil histamine release, triggered by six different stimuli, showed that it (5.50mM) exhibited predilection for inhibiting histamine release stimulated by IgE-dependent ligands. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Quercetin and its 3-arabinorhamnoside (5.0 mg/kg) stimulated nonspecific immune response of mice infected with Salmonella typhi. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.
Interferon detected in serum 18 hr after administration of single dose (0.1 g/kg) of quercetin or quercitrin in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.
Quercetin (2.0-32.0 mg) produced dose-dependent positive inotropic effects and reduced heart rate in isolated rabbit heart. Responses to quercetin significantly inhibited by propranolol, potentiated by theophylline and attenuated by imidazole. Responses were altered by changes in concentrations of calcium, sodium or potassium chlorides; indicating that the quercetin-induced positive inotropic effects were due to increase in availability of intracellular calcium. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.
Quercetin when protected against oxidative degradation by ascorbate, exhibited anti- polio virus activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.
Quercetin inhibited replication of herpes simplex virus in RK-13 cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)h.
Effects of feeding quercetin and flavone on hepatic drug-metabolising enzymes studied in rat; 14-days administration of flavone (0.25%) in feed, increased liver weight and hepatic cytochrome P450. It also increased hepatic mono oxygenase and GSH transferase activities; UDP glucuronyltransferase activity was increased by both quercetin and flavone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)i.
Quercetin reduced concentrations of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) and leukotriene B4 (LTB4) in pleural exudate induced by carrageenin given intrapleurally to rats. Leukocyte migration in exudate was also reduced; reduction of eicosanoids and leukocytes in exudate was dose-related. Quercetin also reduced LTB4 synthesis in cells stimulated with ionophore A-23187. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)j.
Quercetin markedly inhibited (IC50 32.0 -55.0 mM) growth of human gastric cancer cells in vitro DNA synthesis was suppressed to 14.0% of control level by incubation with quercetin (70.0 mM) for two days. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Quercetin (0.01- 2.5 mM) exhibited dose- related inhibition of proliferation of ovarian cancer cell line OVCA 433. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Quercetin inhibited cell proliferation and DNA formation by two human tumor cell lines (HepG- 2, Caco- 2) and a nontumor human small intestinal epithelial cell line. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Quercetin inhibited DNA synthesis in mouse leukaemia L7712 cells (IC50 23.1 mg/ml). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.
Quercetin at 28.0 mg/m1 in vitro caused 50% mortality in human carcinoma of larynx (Hep-2) cells, but inhibition of sarcoma-180 (S-180) cells was less. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.
Quercetin caused significant decrease in ulcer index in acute gastric ulcer (both co1d- restraint and pylorus-ligated models) with respect to control group in rats; significant difference was found in histamine secretion. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.
Quercetin, rutin or kaempferol(25.0 -100.0 mg/kg, i.p.) inhibited in dose-dependent manner, gastric damage produced by acidified-ethanol in rats. Platelet-activating factor (PAF) present in mucosa, was found to be inhibited suggesting that protective effect of these substances is mediated by endogenous PAF. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.
At 10.0 mM, flavone inhibited electrically-induced contractions by 50.5% whereas kaempferol and quercetin showed very weak inhibition (17.3 and 13.0% respectively) in guinea pig isolated ileum. At higher concentrations (30.0 mM - 100.0 mM), they showed inhibition in a concentration- dependent manner. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.
Quercetin (1.0 mM -100.0 mM) inhibited in a concentration-dependent manner concentrations induced in vascular smooth muscle of rats by noradrenaline, high concentration of KCl and Ca2+. This vasodilator effect was observed whether quercetin was administered before or after inducing concentration.
Quercetin, chrysin, luteolin and 7,8- benzoflavone inhibited ethoxyresorufin deethylase activity in human and rat liver microsomes. In man, compounds without hydroxyl groups are more powerful inhibitors than polyhydroxylated flavonoids (7,8-benzoflavone>chrysin>luteolin>querceti~morin) and human microsomes are more sensitive than rat microsomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.
Quercetin, its 3-O-glucoside and isoquercetin, at 0.1 mg/ml, inhibited DNA polymerase of hepatitis B virus by 42.0 to 51.0% and, therefore, may be useful for treatment of hepatitis B. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)j.
Quercetin inhibited aldose reductase with IC50 34.4 mM. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)k.
Quercetin (6.25 to 50.0 mg/kg, i.p.) significantly reduced intestinal transit in mice and this effect was antagonised by yohimbine and phentolamine. Quercetin (12 .5 to 50.0 mg/kg) also reduced intraluminal accumulation of fluid and diarrhoea induced by castor oil and these effects were antagonised by yohimbine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)l.
Quercetin (200.0 mg/kg, i.g., 14d) decreased activities of Na+-K+-exchanging ATPase of rat brain plasma membrane and heart sarcolemma and Ca2+-Mg2+-ATPase of heart sarcolemma membrane. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)m.
Experiments were done to find whether buckwheat extract ameliorates the real injury induced by ischemia-reperfusion, in ischemic-reperfused control rats, the activities of antioxidant enzymes in the renal tissue and blood and renal parameters deviated from the normal range, indicating dysfunction of the kidneys. In contrast, when buckwheat ext. was given orally for 20 consecutive days before ischemia and reperfusion, the activities of the antioxidant enzymes superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase were higher, while thiobarbituric acid-reactive substances in serum and renal tissue were lower, In the treated rats than in the controls. Decreased urea N and creatinine in serum demonstrated a protective effects against the renal dysfunction caused by ischemia and recirculation. Also, buckwheat extract had a protective effect on cultured proximal tubule cells subjected to hypoxia-reoxygenation, probably by preventing O free radicals from attacking the cell membranes. (Yokozawa et al, 2001). C.A. 201, 134, 361168y.
Buckwheat seed components were evaluated for antioxidant and free radical-scavenging activities using solvents of different polarities to isolate components from hulls and groats. Components extracted from buckwheat hulls were pro-oxidant in canola oil. Antioxidant activity of extracts from buckwheat groats increased when more polar solvents were used for extraction. The highest activity was observed for the methanolic extract. Radica1-scavenging activity of buckwheat extracts was analyzed with DPPH (2,2-diphenyl-2-picrYl-hydrazyl). This activity increased when the more polar solvents were used for extraction, with the highest activity observed for the methanolic ext. It was also observed that the radical scavenging effectiveness of extracts was concentration dependent. Analysis revealed the presence of tocopherols in the hexane extract, while methanolic extracts were rich in phenolic acids and flavonoids. (Przybylski et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 124096h.
Water extract of Fagopyrum esculentum exhibited antioxidant activity. (Zielinski and Kozlowska, 2000). C.A. 2000, 132, 119322m.
Antioxidant activity of methanolic extract evaluated according to b-carotene bleaching method expressed as AOX (Dlog A470/min), AA (percent inhibition relative coefficient) ranged from 0.05, 53.7, 0.009 and 51.7 to 0.26, 99.1, 0.46, and 96.93 respectively and found to be significant. (Velioglu et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 289463t.
Juice of buckwheat leaves was expected to show antihypertensive and antiatherosclerotic effects. (Otani, 2000).
