Taxonomic Details

Correct Name : Euphorbia hypericifolia, Linn.
Family : Euphorbiaceae
Synonyms : Chamaesyce hypericifolia, Euphorbia indica, Euphorbia parviflora.
Common Names :
Sanskrit: Dugdhika
Hindi:  Dudhia, Hakshardana, Dudhikalave, Dushi
Marathi: Dhaktidudhi, Dudhmogra, Dudhi
Misc:
Punjab: Hazardana

Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Herb, commonly found thorughout the hotter parts of India and occuring up to 4,000 feet on the Himalaya. Also found in jute fields, Forests, rice fields, Road sides, Sal forest, Sugar cane fields, Tea gardens, Undergrowth forests, Vegetable fields, Waste land and Wheat fields,
BioticZone: Deccan and carnatic, North Circars, W. to E. slopes of ghats.
Misc: Baghnadi, Balod, Chilpi, Corbett National Park, Kabirchabutra, Khami to Pandaria, Khondra, Khuria, Lamni, Rajnandgaon and Simga.
Country: India.
Zone: Central, North East and South.
State: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Tamil Nadu and Tripura.
District: Balaghat, Bilaspur, Chattarpur, Damoh, Dharmapuri, Durg, Hoshangabad, Indore, Nimar East, Raigarh, Raipur, Rajnandgaon, Rewa, Sagar, Salem, Satna, Seoni, Sidhi, South Arcot, Surguja, Tikamgarh and Tiruchi.
Talluk: Pachmarhi,

Availability :  
Common throughout hotter parts of India & Ceylon.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A small slender annual herb with serrulate leaves. Inflorescence is cyme with axillary branchlets. Ovary and capsule glabrous and hispid. Seeds smooth or with shallow transverse pits.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Leaves, Plant

Uses

  1. An infusion of the dried leaves is a remedy in dysentery, diarrhoea, menorrhagia and leucohhhoea. 

  2. It affects the system as an astringent and feeble narcotic.

  3. It is given with milk to children in colic.
