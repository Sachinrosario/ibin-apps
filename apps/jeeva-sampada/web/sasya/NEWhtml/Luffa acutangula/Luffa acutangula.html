HABIT & HABITAT:
Monoecious, climbing herb. Stem 5-angled, angles sharp, glabrous, often scabrid. It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Tripura, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Daman & Diu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tendrils usually 3-fid. Leaves reniform-orbicular, 8-20 cm long and broad, palmately 5-7 angled or sublobate, cordate at base, scabrous above; petioles 5-12.5 cm long, angular, scabrid. Male flowers in axillary, 12-20-flowered raceme, 10-15 cm long, yellow. Petals spreading, obovate, yellow with green hairy veins. Stamens 3. Female flowers solitary; ovary ribbed. Fruits clavate, 5-30 cm long, tapering towards base, longitudinally ribbed with 10-angles. Seeds black, elliptic, compressed, corrugated on sides.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine fruit is sweet, cooling and it is used as anthelmintic and antipyretic. It cures biliousness, asthma, bronchitis. Leaves are antibilious and antipyretic and cures bronchitis. In Unani system of medicine fruits cure biliousness, spleen diseases, leprosy, piles, fever, haematuria, syphilis and bronchitis.


Chemistry:
Amarin identified as cucurbitacin B. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Curcubitacins B and E and oleanolic acid isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

A new compound – amarinin (fig. 1) isolated from seeds and its structure elucidated as 2-deoxycucurbitacin B. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation and structure elucidation of seven new oleanane-type triterpene saponins – acutosides A (fig. 2), B, C, D, E, F and G (fig. 3a- 3f) from herb. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Another two heptaglycosides – acutoside H (fig. 4a) and acutoside I (fig. 4b) isolated from seeds and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.


Pharmacology:
Amarinin inhibited growth of second leaf sheath of rice both in the absence and presence of GA3. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Two trypsin inhibitors, LA-1 and LA-2, have been isolated from ridged gourd (Luffa acutangula Linn.) seeds and purified to homogeneity by gel filtration followed by ion-exchange chromatography. (Haldar et al, 1996)


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for convulsion, cramp (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarfader, 1970); Fever (Fruit, Sharma and Malhotra, 1984); Jaundice (Fruit as snuff, Venugopal and Raghunathan, 1982); Madness, scabies, syphilis, tetanus (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarfader, 1970); Snake-bite (Tendril, Srivastava and Varma, 1981); Edible vegetable (Fruit, Shah, 1984).
