HABIT & HABITAT
Dioecious, evergreen tree with pendent branches. It is found to occur throughout Tropical India. Wild and cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves short petiolate, alternate, dark green, oblong or elliptic -oblong, acute, obtuse or shortly acuminate at apex, serrulate at margin. Male flowers: crowded in axillary clusters; sepals oblong, obtuse; stamens many, filaments connate at base. Female flowers: 1-3 in each axil; sepals broadly elliptic, obtuse. Ovary minutely hairy; styles 3; stigma crescent shaped. Drupe ellipsoid, white, tomentose; stone rugose.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plant is fragrant, cooling, pungent; aphrodisiac, laxative, diuretic. It is good for eyes. It is also useful in biliousness, thirst, burning sensation, erysipelas, elephantiasis, causes "vata " & "kapha". Decoction of leaves, fruits, stones of fruits are given in cold and fever.


Chemistry:

 Isolation and characterisation of new triterpenes -putranjivadione (fig. 1a) and roxburgholone (fig. 1b) from bark; characterisation of former as friedelan-3,7-dione and latter as 3a- hydroxyfriedelan- 7-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
A saponin -putranjivoside (fig. 2), mp. 215°- from seed coat, characterised as 3b-L-arabino-L-rhamno-D-glucoside of oleanolic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Two new triterpenoids -putranjivanonol (roxburgholone) and putranjic acid (fig. 3), mp. 220° -from stem bark; structure of putranjic acid determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
Putrolic acid from stem bark identified as putranjic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.
New triterpenes -putrone, putrol, putranjivic acid (fig. 4) besides b-amyrin, its palmitate and stigmasterol isolated from leaves; structure of putranjivic acid elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.
Putranjivic acid characterised as 3,4-sec-friedelan-4(23)-en-3-oic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.
A new seco acid - putric acid - from stem bark characterized as 2-hydroxy-3,4-sceofriedelan-3-oic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
A new biflavonoid - putraflavone - from leaves characterized as 7,4''-dimethylamentoflavone. Amentoflavone, 4'''-O-methylamentoflavone and 4''',7-di-O-methylamentoflavone isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Structures of putrone and putrol revised. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
Putranosides A (fig. 5a), B (fig. 5b), C (fig. 5c) and D (fig. 5d) isolated as methyl esters from seed coat and theu structures elucidated; putranoside D established as b-D-glucopyranosyl(l®28) ester of [a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®3)]-b-D-xylopyranosyl(1®4)-b-D-glucuronopyranosyl (1®3)oleanolic acid; in putranoside C xylose was absent and in putranosides A and B glucosyl unit attached to 28-COOH in putranosides C and D respectively was absent. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.
Leaves yielded putranjiva saponin A (fig. 6a) (a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®2)-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-b-D-glucuronopyranosyl(1®3)-oleanolic acid, saponin B (fig. 6b) (a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®2)-b-D-glucopranosyl(1®3)[b-D-xylopyranosyl(1-4)]-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-oleanolic acid, saponins C (fig. 6c) and D (fig. 6d) were b-D-glucopyranosyl(l®28) esters of saponins A and B respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.
Revised structure of Putrol (fig. 12). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Pharmacology:  

Essential oil from seed kernels was toxic against Helminthosporium oryaze at MIC of 500 ppm. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Y-spur is an Ayurvedic formulation containing herbs like Putranjiva roxburghii with other plants which was found to increase the sperm count. (Mathur et al., 1999). MAPA 1999, 9905-3002


Ethnobotany: Medicine for antifertility, cold, fever (Fruit, Sharma et al, 1979); Ornamental beads (Seed, Vishnu Mittre, 1981)
