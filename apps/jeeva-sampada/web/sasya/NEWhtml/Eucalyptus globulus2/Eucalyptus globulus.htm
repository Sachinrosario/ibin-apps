HABIT & HABITAT
It is large tree attaining a height of 300 ft. or more In India, found in Nilgiris and palni hills, Shimla and Shillong from 1350 to 2350 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 A large tree attaining a height of 300 ft. or more, with a clean straight bole under forest conditions, but often tending to branch freely when grown in the open.  Bark deciduous in long thin strips or sheets.  Leaves on juvenile shoot opposite, sessile, cordate-ovate, covered with a bluish white bloom; adult leaves alternate, lanceolate 6-12 in long and 1-2 in broad.  Stems of seedlings and coppice shoot quadrangular.

UTILIZATION:
Parts Used:
 Essential oil from leaves- antiasthmatic, antiseptic, antirheumatic, antidropsical, antispasmodic, disinfectant, mosquito repellant, febrifuge, carminative, diaphoretic, rubefacient, stimulant, expectorant, in chronic bronchitis, migraine and other forms of neuralgia, diphtheria, ague, ulceration of throat, skin diseases; leaf-vermin repellent, in phthisis; root-purgative.

The average recovery of both schizandrin A and tanshinone IIA are 98.31% (CV = 2.1%) and 99.15% (CV = 1.1%) respectively. The method is simple and rapid and its reproducibility is satisfactory. (Wang et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 122, 299198e.
A number of aspects quality were determined by the chem. of woods. Bands in the near IR (NIR) spectra of woods arise from the vibrations of bonds in the chemical components of the woods and can be used as sensitive monitors of changes in their chemistry. Principle components analysis (PCA) can compares most of the variations analysed at the 700 points in the NIR spectrum into a few components enabling pattern of relationships to be discerned which might otherwise be overlooked. This paper shows how PCA analyses of the NIR spectra of wood meals can be used to discriminate between woods from eucalypts, between woods from different eucalypt species. The chem. significance of the classification is also discussed. (Schimleck et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 331897g.
More than 70 components in the residues of Eucalyptus globulus leaf oil were detected and among them 48 compounds were identified with GC and GC-MS. The main components were identified as a-terpineol (27.92% and 5.5% in different samples), aromadendrene (17.3% and 26.6%), alloaromadendrene, guaienes, guriuenes, epiglobulol, ledol, diepopalustrol and others. (Zhao et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 275369p.
Antifouling agents containing isopentylphloroglucinols (fig. 13) is useful for fish nets, ship nets, ship bottoms, marine constructions, etc., contain (R1, R2 = H, CHO, CO2H, C2-10 alkoxycarbonyl; R3 - R5 = H, C1-9 alkyl, C2-10 alkylcarbonyl; R6 = sesquiterpene residue). Isopentylphloroglucinols may be extracted from Eucalyptus plants. (Eito, 1997). C.A. 1999, 130, 62406y.
The phenolic compounds extracted by solvents showed antioxidant activity. Under the best conditions, the antioxidant activity coefficient was 64% of the value found with a synthetic antioxidant (BHT). (Cruz et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 285621m.
The chemical structure of lignin from plantation Eucaluptus globulus are presented. (Evtuguin et al, 2001).
Steryl glycosides (SG) and acyl steryl glycosides (ASG) in eucalypt (Eucalyptus globulus) wood has been investigated. These compounds were analyzed as their trimethylsilyl ethers by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry (GC/MS) using a 15 m length high-temperature capillary column with a thin film, and identified on basis of their mass spectra and relative retention times comparing with those of authentic standards. Significant amounts of SG were identified in eucalypt wood whilst only traces of ASG could be detected. (Gutierrez and del Rio, 2001).
Four ellagic acid rhamnosides were isolated from the stem bark of Eucalyptus globulus. Their structures have been established on the basis of the analysis of their 1H NMR, 13C NMR, HMBC, IR and MS spectral data. The HMBC data of these compounds were most useful for their structure determinations, with these bring determined to be 3-O-methylellagic acid 3'-O-alpha-rhamnopyranoside, 3-O-methylellagic acid 3'-O-alpha-3''-O-acetylrhamnopyranoside, 3-O-methylellagic acid 3'-O-alpha-2''-O-acetylrhamnopyranoside, 3-O-methylellagic acid 3'-O-alpha-4''-O-acetylrhamnopyranoside, respectively. (Kim et al, 2001).

Pharmacology: Euglobals Ia1, Ia2, Ib, Ic, IIa, IIb, IIc, III, IV, V, VII and IVb showed granulation-inhibiting activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.
N-tritriacontan-16,18-dione showed marked antioxidant activity in water/ alcohol system and was more effective than a-tocopherol and BHA; it had no antioxidant activity in oil system. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.
4-hydroxytritriacontan-16,18-dione showed strong antioxidant activity in a water alcohol system. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.
Anti-tumor-promoting activity of euglobals Ia1, Ia2, Ib, Ic, IIa, IIb, IIc, III, IVa, IVb, V and VII was tested in vitro on 12-O-tetradecanoylphorbol-13-acetate (TPA)-induced Epstein-Barr virus early antigen (EBV-EA) activation test system. Euglobal-III showed strong inhibitory activity, followed by euglobals Ib, IIa, Ic, Ia1 and Ia2. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.
Eucalyptus globulus seed reduced edematous swelling of ear inhibited the proliferation of granuloma, blood capillary permeability of mice, and the cotton pellet granuloma implanted in rats. It had anti-inflammatory effects in rats with carrageenan-induced edema. (Jiao et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 212140v.
The microbicides, useful for disinfection of poultry eggs, medical facilities, meat-procesing facilities, etc. and as additives for feed, wet tissues, diapers etc., contain extracts of Eucylaptus globulus leaves with polar solvents and chitosan. The extracts may be prepared by removing essential oils from Eucylaptus leaves upon defatting with nonpolar solvents or steam distillation and then extracting the defatted leaves with polar solvents. The extracts contain a dihydrochalcone derivative (I) as main microbial component. An acetone extract of leaves of Eucylaptus (Eucylaptus globulus etc.) was fractionated with some steps to give eucalyptin, 8-desmethylaucalyptin, and I. An EtOH extract of Eucalyptus globulus leaves and chitosan showed synergistic antimicrobial activity against Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus subtilis and Escherichia coli. The extract and chitosan also inhibited growth of Propionibacterium acnes, Trichophyton mentagrophytes, Aspergillus niger and Penicillium citrinum. Application of the microbicides to chicken eggs, beef, wet tissues, and disposable diapers were also shown. (Takahashi, 1998). C.A. 2000, 133, 1748b.
Eucalyptus globulus is used as a traditional treatment of diabetes. Eucalyptus in the diet (62.5 g/kg) and drinking water (2.5 g/L) reduced the hyperglycemia and associated weight loss of streptozotocin-treated mice. An aqueous extract of Eucalyptus (AEE) (0.5 g/L) enhanced 2-deoxy-glucose transport by 50%, glucose oxidation by 60% and incorporation of glucose into glycogen by 90% in mouse abdominal muscle. In acute, 20 min incubations, 0.25 - 0.5g from the colonal pancreatic b-cell line (BRIN-BD11). (Gray and Flatt, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 153015m.
Crude methanol extracts of Eucalyptus globulus gave IC50 value of 78 mug/mL. (Tahir et al, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9904-2563.
Ethanol extract of the Eucalyptus globulus leaves (extract A) and its EtO Ac-solublefraction (extract B) containing most of macrocarpals were examined for the cariostatic effects in vitro. The extract A and B inhibited the growth of cariogenic bacteria at concentrations of 6.25 - 50 and 3.13 - 25 mug/ml, respectively, but their antibacterial activities on the intestinal bacteria were relatively low. The extracts A and B strongly inhibited the glucan synthesis, especially adhesived-insoluble glucan synthesis by GTase prepared from Straptococcus sobrinus. (Osawa et al, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9805-2927.
Essential oil of Eucalyptus globulus shows antibiotic property. (Tkachenko et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 133: 63573z.
--The composition of lipophilic extractives in the chloroform solution fraction of the acetone extract from Eucalyptus globulus wood has been examined. The lipid extract was fractionated by solid-phase extraction on aminopropyl-phase cartridges into four different fractions of increasing polarity. The total lipid extraction and the resulting fractions were analyzed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry using high temperature capillary column. The main compounds identified included sterol esters, fatty acids, steroids ketones, hydrocarbons and triglycerides. Minor compounds such as fatty alcohols, mono- and diglycerides, waxes and tocopherols were also identified among the lipids from Eucalyptus globulus wood. (Gutierrez et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 21068n.
50% ethanol extract of Eucalyptus globulus shows inhibitory collagenase activity. As a result fractionate an active substance with higher inhibitory effect. And, with the examination by TLC and UV- spectra. It was found that the active substance was polyphenol compounds with chelating effect. (Kyotani et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 133968n.
Twelve compounds with lipid peroxidation inhibitory activity were isolated from the stem bark of Eucalyptus globulus. Their structures were assigned as a new aromatic monoterpene (1) and eleven known compounds, pinoresinol (2), vomifoliol (3), 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenol 1-O-`-D-(6'-O-galloyl)glucopyranoside (4), Me gallate (5), rhamzin (6), rhamnetin (7), eriodictyol (8), quercetin (9), Taxifolin (10), engelitin (11), and catechin (12) on the basis of U.V., mass, and NMR spectroscopic analysis. these compounds except vomifolil significantly inhibited lipid peroxidation in rat liver microsome. (Yun et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 115060b.
The effect of essential oil of Eucalyptus globulus on lipid peroxidation in simple in vitro systems were carried out. (Dessi et al, 2001).
The antioxidant and antimicrobial activities of ethyl acetate extracts obtained from acid hydrolysates of several lignocellulosic materials (Eucalyptus globulus wood) were evaluated. (Cruz et al, 2001)
