HABIT & HABITAT
A moderate to large sized evergreen tree with a dense crown, sometimes attaining a height up to 80 ft. and a girth of 7 ft. On well-drained soils a girth up to 15 ft. has been recorded in Mysore forests, the girth of heartwood being not more than 4 ft. In Ceylon, where growth and development are better than in south India, ebony logs (without sapwood) up to 7 ft. in girth have been recorded.
 In general it is found in dry evergreen forests, evergreen forest, Forests, ravines, semi evergreen forest, Slopes, tree farm.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  The sapwood of Diosyprus ebenum is light yellowish grey to grey, often streaked with black. The heartwood is jet black, rarely with a few dark or light brown or golden streaks. It has a metallic luster when smoothed and works to a flinty surface without any characteristic odour or taste. It is heavy (sp; gr., 0.85-1.00 plus; wt., 72 lb./cu. ft. for the black portion and 55 lb./cu. ft. for the light portion), straight, grained or somewhat irregular or wavy grained, fine and even textured. It resembles the Indian ebony from Diospyrous melanoxylon and Diospyrous tomentosa, but is heavier and harder. Growth rings are not apparent and vessels and rays are very small. Male flowers- axillary umbellate clusters, calyx lobes obscure, corolla 1cm across, tubular to salveform.
Female flowers- solitary, calyx lobes ovate, corolla 3mm across, lobes acute, berries globose.
Wood is Grey, with irregularly-shaped masses of jet black ebony near the centre, frequently with lighter coloured streaks.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination :
The tree is reported to seed, occasionally, twice a year in Ceylon. Good seed years are not regular and the seed is liable to weevil attack. Propagation is by seed. Seedlings endure a fair amount of shade, but after they have established themselves, overhead light is beneficial. The rate of growth is slow; the recorded average girth figures in Ceylon are: 18 in. at 25 yrs.; 36 in. at 75 yrs.; 45 in. at 135 yrs.; and 72 in. at 200 yrs. A girth increment of 0.5 in. per year has been reported in some parts of Mysore.

Cultivation:

CLIMATE AND SOIL: Diospyros ebenum
It is found sparsely scattered in the dry evergreen forests of Deccan and Karnatak extending westward to north Coimbatore, South Kanara and Coorg, and southward through Malabar and Cochin to Travancore. It prefers well-drained rocky soil and grows well on sandy loam with good subsoil drainage and also in soils with a fair amount of clay. It is never gregarious but is found scattered in mixture with Chloroxylon swietenia, Manilkara hexandra (Roxb.) Dubard Euphoria longan Steud. Albizzia odoratissima. Vitex altissima Linn.f., Berry a cordifolia and other species of Diospyros.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruits, Wood.

Uses

  • The fruit of Diosyprous ebenum is edible.
  • The gum from the ebony tree is used in medicine for removing obstruction from any cause in the vision.

Harvesting Practices
Yeild:
Diospyrous ebenum is perhaps the best ebony yielding tree and the only one which yields jet black heartwood without streaks or markings, the true EBONY of commerce. The yield of ebony cannot be predicted by the age of the tree. Generally it decreases in volume from the base upwards, but its occurrence is often erratic and, in some instances, interrupted by light colored wood. The proportion of black wood to the gross volume of trees varies from 14 to 35%, the average being less than 25%.

Other Uses

  1. The plant is astringent, attenuant, lithontriptic and a fish poison. Exploited for fine cabinet work , musical instruments, inlay work, rulers in Europe, and in China for pipes, carved articles and images.In India for turnery works, walking sticks, umbrella handles and fancy articles.It can be tried for flooring and other ornamental work.
  2. Ebony has been used from time immemorial for ornamental carving and turnery, and for special purposes in decoration. It is used for veneers, in laying, musical instruments, sports goods, mathematical instruments, piano keys, and caskets. It is of such outstanding appearance that certain woods, especially pear wood, are stained black or 'ebonized' and used as substitutes. The timber of D. melanoxylon is often passed off as true ebony in India.
  3. The colouring matter of ebony does not appear to have been identified. Some workers to a gum-like substance attribute the black colour.

Economical Potential to the country
D. ebenum is not very common in India, nor do the trees, where they occur, attain any large size. Only limited supplies of ebony are available in the areas of their occurrence. The annual production in Mysore is reported to be 500 - 1,000 cu. ft. and in Travancore-Cochin, about 25 tons. Ebony is available from Ernakulam; Chalakudy and Trivandrum depots at prices varying, according to Quality, from Rs. 350 to Rs. 800 per ton.
