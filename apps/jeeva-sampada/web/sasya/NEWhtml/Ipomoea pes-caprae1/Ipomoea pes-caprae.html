DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Prostrate or twining perennial herb; roots long, with thick, brown bark; stems cylindrical, glabrous. Leaves 3.8-6 x 5-7.5 cm, broader than the long, cuneate or truncate at base, deeply bilobed, fleshy, parallel-veined, glabrous. Flowers rose-purple or pinkish purple, solitary or 2-3 together, 2.5-11.5 cm long; bracts lanceolate, caducous. Two outer sepals smaller than the inner. Corolla infundibuliform, brilliant rose-purple with deeper colour in the tube; filaments hairy at base. Capsules ovoid, glabrous, 1.3-1.6 cm long. Seeds villous.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine the herb is cooling and is used as astringent to the bowels. It is used in diarrhoea, pains and vomiting.


Chemistry:  

Seed extract showed presence of ergoline and clavine alkaloids; total alkaloids 0.009%. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Malic, citric, maleic, tartaric, C, D and E (fig. 1) present in plant extract, of which pescaprosides A, B succinic and fumaric acids isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Quercetin-3-glucoside, -3-galactoside and -3-(6''-O-acetyl)-b-D-glucopyranoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).
Five fatty acid glycosides - pescaprosides A, B, and E isolated. Pescaproside E characterized as O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®3)-O-[a-L-rhamnpyranosyl (1®4)]-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®4)-O-b-D-fucopyranosyl(1®2)-Oa-L-rhamnopyranosyl-11-oxyhexadecanoic acids having its nonanomeric hydroxyl groups esterified with methylbutyric acid and lauric acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Isolation of 2-hydroxy-4,4,7-trimethyl-1(4H)napthalenone, (-)mellein, eugenol and 4-vinylguaiacol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
b-damascenone and E-phytol isolated as active compounds from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Pharmacology:  

2-hydroxy-4,4,7-trimethyl-1(4H)-napthalenone, (-)mellein, eugenol and 4-vinylguaiacol inhibited prostaglandin synthesis in vitro; eugenol and 4-vinylguaiacol were most active, with IC50 9.2 and 18.0 mM respectively. Inhibitory effect on prostaglandin formation may partly anti-inflammatory activity of plant extract. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.
b-damascenone and E-phytol exhibited antispasmodic activity in isolated guinea pig ileum with potency in the same range as that of papaverine. Effectiveness of plant extract in treatment of dermatitis, such as that caused by toxic jellyfishes, is possibly due to presence of b-damascenone and E-phytol which interfere with contraction of vascular smooth muscle cell. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.
The antinociceptive effects of the MeOH extract and two of its subfractions thereof (EtOAc and aqueous) produced considerable antinoceptive activity against two classical models of pain in mice. The MeOH extract had a caalcd. ID50 of 33.8 mg/kg, i.p., in the writhing test and it also inhibited both phases of pain (neurogenic and inflammatory) in the formalin test, with ID50 values of 37.7 and 12.5 mg/kg, i.p., for the 1st and 2nd phases, respectively. Preliminary phytochemical analysis suggested the presence of steroids, terpenoids, alkaloids and flavanoids. The findings support, at least in part, the popular use of Ipomea pescaprae for pain treatment. (Maria et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 565c.
The study describes the isolation and identification of several constituents from Ipomea perscaprae (L.) R.Br. a medicinal plant frequently employed in including inflammation and pain. (Krogh et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 49262z.


Ethnobotany:  

Medicine for headache (Part not mentioned, Bhargava, 1981); Rheumatism (Leaf, Shah et al, 1983); Symbolic garland of stem placed on hut of woman in confinement, keeps evil away (Mehra et al, 1975)
