Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A large tree introduced in North India. Occurs in tropical medium rainfall areas of the Indo –Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical). Common in most parts of India. it is rare in Karnatic, but fairly plentiful in Tirunelveli and on the west coast. It iextends northwards to the sub-Himalayan tract, where it is distributed along the banks of streams. It is common in Chota Nagpur, Orissa and in the Northern Circars.

Prefers loose, moist, fertile alluvial loam along river-bank. Found in the areas with annual rainfall 1000 - 1750 mm, with temperature in the range of -10 C and 480 C, on altitudes up to 1200 m. Superficial root system. Moderate shade bearer. Seedlings sensitive to frost and drought. Moderate shade bearer. Grows on variety of soils.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

A large tree with a very thick trunk and horizontally spreading branches. Bark thick, smooth, greenish-white, flaking off in large flat pieces. Leaves usually subopposite, oblong or elliptic-oblong, obtuse or subacute, pale dull green above, pale brown beneath, shallowly crenate-serrate in the upper part or sometimes throughout, base rounded or cordate, often unequal-sided. Main nerves arcuate, 10-15 pairs, veins reticulate, pellucid, petioles with 1 or usually 2 prominent glands at the top immediately below the leaves.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers sessile, in short axillary spikes or in terminal panicles; bracteoles linear-lanceolate, shorter than the flowers, caducous. Calyx glabrous, mouth broadly companulate; teeth triangular. Ovary quite glabrous. Disk clothed with yellowish or reddish hairs. Stamens much exserted. Drupe ovoid- or obovoid-oblong, fibrous-woody, glabrous, dark-brown, with 5 hard projecting wings striated with numerous curved veins.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Nearly evergreen. New leaves appear in hot season, before all the old leaves have fallen. Flowering is during April - July. Fruits ripen during February - May. Fruiting starts when tree is 6-7 years old and thereafter, every 3rd year is a good fruit year.

Floral and seed biology:


Seed Germination & Propagation: Natural regeneration is satisfactory. Germination takes place early in the rainy season and may actually commence with the erly pre-monsoon showers. Seeds does not readily germinate if exposed to the sun. If fruits are partially buried by rain or otherwise, germination is much more successful, whereas the success is even greater on bare soil with a considerable amount of moisture in it. Grown by entire transplanting. Seed propagation is also practised. Best time for plantation is July.

Stumps are also used for multiplication. Entire transplantation and seed sowing is also practised. Plant can be successfully raised along the field crops. It cannot be propagated by using branch cuttings. Air-layering also has been tried. Seeds are germinated by soaking them in water for 96 hours followed by covering with moist gunny cloth in shade.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel. Excellent firewood. Calorific value: sapwood – 5,030 cal; heartwood – 5,128 cal. Makes very good charcoal. Suitable for producer gas.

Wood: Sapwood is reddish white, the heartwood is brown to dark brown, streaked with darker, blackish lines. The wood is somewhat lustrous, more or less irregular- and interlocked-grained, coarse-textured, very hard, strong and moderately heavy (sp. gr. 0.74; wt. 816-865 kg. cu.m.).

Used for carts, agricultural implements, water troughs, boat building and various purposes. It is used as mine-props in the Kolar Glod Fields and for solid cart-wheels in Chota Nagpur. Suitable for plywood (tea chests) and other structural purposes, such as house building, water troughs, oars, masts, transmission poles and tool handles. It can be used for conversion to sleepers and for jetty-piles.

Also yields timber.

Yields rayon grade pulp in mixture with other hard-woods. Timber is used for construction.

Tannin - Bark and fruit employed for tanning. Stem bark used as tannin material.
