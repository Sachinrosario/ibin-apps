DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Procumbent, hispid herbs; branches 10-40 cm long. Leaves obovate, spathulate, oblong or elliptic, 1.0-4.5 x 0.5-1.8 cm, acute or obtuse at apex, narrowed at base, scabrid or ciliate at margin, pubescent or nearly glabrous, subsesile; stipules scarious with a few long thick bristles longer than sheath. Flowers 2-6 in a whorl within the stipular cup, sessile or subsessile; bracts lanceolate-subulate, hyaline. Calyx hispid, 4 mm long; tube narrowly campanulate, teeth as long as tube, linear-lanceolate, very acutely pointed, reflexed hairy. Corolla pale blue or white, 4-5 mm long; lobes oblong, 1-2 mm long, acute, bristly on back near the tip. Capsules ellipsoid, 3.5-5.0 mm long, rounded at apex, obtuse at base, crowned with calyx teeth. Seeds 2-3 mm long, dark brown or black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: Roots are used in antidotes to snake bites, antifertility, appetite, bleed in child birth, check miscarriage, gum troubles and toothache. Leaves are also useful in eye diseases, scabies, skin diseases, stomach complaints, ulcer and wounds.


Chemistry:
Isorhamnetin isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979). 3-Keto-olean-12-en-29-oic acid and olean-12-en-3b-ol-29-oic acid were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for antidote to snake bite (Root, Goel et al, 1984); antifertility (Root, Tarafder, 1983); appetite (Root, Mukherjee et al, 1986); bleed in child birth (Root, Tarafder, 1984); body ache (Whole plant, Goel et al, 1984); check miscarriage (Root, Taafder, 1984); Eye disease (Leaf, Saxena et al, 1981); gum trouble (Root, Srivatava and Varma, 1981); scabies, sin disease (Leaf, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); stomach complaints (Leaf, Kumar et al, 1980; Whole plant, Tribedi et al, 1985); stimulant (Seed, Mukherjee et al, 1986); ulcers (Leaf, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); wounds (Leaf, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); Edible as vegetable (Leaf, Pal and Banerjee, 1971; Ball, 1967)
