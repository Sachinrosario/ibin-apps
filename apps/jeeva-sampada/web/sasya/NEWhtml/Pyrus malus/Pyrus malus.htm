Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
A juice and wine is prepared from fruit (Barwal, 1991)

Production of apple cider, i.e. apple juice that has undergone alcoholic fermentation, holds great potential for utilization of unmarketable surplus fruits.

Materials and Methods

Seven prominent apple varieties of Kashmir viz. Red cider, Kesri, Red Delicious, American, White dotted Red commonly known as Maharaji, Trail and Khuru were procured from local Jammu market and used for the studies. Juice was extracted from the fruits by the conventional method using a hydraulic press after crushing in a fruit mill. It was strained through a muslin cloth and used for the fermentation studies. In order to study the influence of clarification and pasteurization on product quality, some lots were made after clarification using pectionol enzymes and some other ones after pasteurization of clarified/ unclarified juice. Juice was sulphited with potassium metabisulphite 12 hours before fermentation in order to incorporate desired dose of sulphur dioxide into it.

The yeast culture collection maintained in the Regional Research Laboratory, Jammu, a strain of Saccharomyces cerevisiae which was evaluated best in our earlier experiments, was used for inoculation of juice.

The culture was rejuvenated in pasteurized apple juice by incubating at 250C for 24 hours. It was further multiplied in steps to get 5% volume of juice to be fermented. The actively growing yeast starter was added to the sulphited and the fermentation was allowed to proceed at 16-200C in containers plugged with cotton. It was considered complete till no further fall in brix for 2 consecutive days was observed. It took 20-22 days to reach dryness.

At the end of fermentation, the eider was syphoned off. It was filled in bottles, sealed and pasteurized at 680C for 30 minutes and allowed to cool to room temperature. 15 days old cider obtained from different varieties was subjected to chemical and organoleptic evaluation. Cider blends from different varieties in various proportions were also evaluated in a similar way.

Quality of raw material

The data on juice yields and chemical composition of 7 apple varieties is presented in Table. The juice yield is important as it affects the economics of production whereas sugar-acid-tannin balance determines the flavour of juice. In addition, sugar content is directly related to the alcohol formation in the final product. Maharaji variety which constitutes the biggest bulk of the total apple production in the State, yields maximum juice i.e. 72 percent of the total fruit weight but the juice is of poor quality because it is very acidic (0.70 percent) with low sugar content 11.02 percent and hence is a prohibitive factor in its palatability. On the other hand Red Delicious is a sweet variety having 13.25 percent Sugar and 0.20 percent acids. Values for sugars and acidity in other varieties varied between 11.4-12.63 percent and 0.30-0.56 percent respectively. The brix values have been found to be slightly lower in all the varieties than what has been reported earlier for these fruits pH values ranged from 3.3 to 4.3. Maharaji, Kesri and Trail had lower pH whereas Red Delicious and American had pH higher than 4.0. pH is a controlling factor in microbiological changes occuring during fermentation and juices of high pH are prone to undergo cider disorders of bacterial origin. Bitterness and astrigency in cider are imparted by phenolic compounds and juice lacking in these is fortified by additional tannins. Oxidation and condensation of these compounds during fermentation can, however, effect the colour and flavour of cider. Trail, American and Kesri varieties were fairly rich in natural tannins having 160-230 mg percent whereas 55-75 mg percent tannins were found in Red Delicious, Khuru and Maharaji.

Cider preparation is a common process of utilising culled apples. Cider is made by fermentation of apple juice. The astringency of apple tannins and brisk taste of acid give cider two of its important flavour elements. The product of normal alcoholic fennentation containing between 0.5 and 8.0% alcohol by volume is called hard cider or fermented cider (Johnson and Peterson, 1974). For sparkling cider, fermentation is stopped at about 3.5% alcohol, thus retaining part of carbon dioxide in solution. Cider can also be artificially carbonated to make a refreshing beverage.

Apples were cut into small pieces and crushed in a blender. The juice from pulp was extracted manually, filtered using two layers of muslin cloth, clarified by centrifugation, weighed and stored at 180C in 20 L. plastic cans until required for use.

The test cultures were subcultured 3 to 4 times prior to use. One hundred fifty milli-liters of diluted apple juice (1:3) fortified with 1% yeast extract was dispensed into side arm flasks and sterilised at 1150C for 20 min. A loopful of the 24 hr test culture from agar slans was transferred to this medium and incubated at 300C. the inoculum rate used was 10.0% and its absorbance was 0.6 which was measured by double cell photoelectric colorimeter using blue filter.
