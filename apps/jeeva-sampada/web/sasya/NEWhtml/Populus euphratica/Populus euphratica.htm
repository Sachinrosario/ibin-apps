Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A middle-sized gregarious tree. Occurs in southern Punjab plains on the banks of the Sutlej and along its feeders in the western Himalayas, ascending to an altitude of 4,000 m in Ladakh and western Tibet. The species occurs in North Africa and extends through Middle East to Central Asia.

The tree is typically gregarious, occurring pure or associated with the other species. It reaches in elevation tot he upper limits of tree growth, and is found even in the hottest parts in the plains, where it thrives under the mitigating influence of river inundations. It is a light demander, frost hardy.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A middle-sized gregarious tree. Buds slightly pubescent, not viscid. Leaves most variable in shape, those of seedlings, young trees and young shoots linear or oblong, entire and ovate, rhomboid or orbicular, often as broad or broader than long, dentate or more or less cut or lobed, with a rounded cordate or cuneate 3-5 nerved base, the narrower leaves sometimes entire.

 

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers pedicellate in both sexes; catkins glabrous.

Male flowers on long slender pedicels. Disk membranous, suborbicular, with 6-8 (usually 8) irregular acute teeth. Stamens 8-12; filaments filiform; anthers oblong, quadrangular, longer than the filaments.

Female flowers :Disk membranous, caducous, tubular, with 8-12 linear segments. Stigmas 2 3- (commonly 3-) valved. Pedicels shorter than the capsules. Seeds narrowly ellipsoid, acute at both the ends, enveloped in a mass of silky hairs.

Phenology:

Flowers in February. Nearly leafless in January.


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Natural reproduction is through root suckers and seeds. The seedlings spring up on fresh alluvial soil after the floods recede.

Artificial propagation through seeds and cuttings has not been very successful. The tree coppices well and stands pollarding for a long time. The rate of growth of the tree is fast.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Ganoderma lucidum (Leyss.) Karst. – root rot

Corticium salmonicolor Berk. & Br. – stem canker followed by twig blight and die-back

Botryodiplodia palmarum (Cke.) Petrak & Syd. – stem canker

Melampsora populnea – rust

Cladosporium herbarum Fr. – velvety, blackish

The tree is attacked by number of defoliators, borers and gall-forming pests.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Wood used as a fuel. Calorific value of Sapwood - 5,019 cal., 9,035 Btu; heartwood - 5,008 cal., 9,016 Btu

Wood: Sapwood white and broad; heartwood reddish, often almost black at the centre, straight to irregularly interlocked-grained, medium fine- but somewhat uneven textured, moderately hard and light, 481 kg/ c m.

Wood used for planking, well-curbs, lacquer-work, turnery, match-box and splints. Suitable for plywood, cricket bats, shoe heels and bobbins.


Parts Used:


Harvesting Practices:

Fairly durable under cover and in contact with water, but liable to insect attack in green state.

Produce and Products:

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:

Seasons well without difficulty. Takes 5-7 days for kiln drying.


Other uses:

Leaves used as fodder for sheep, goats and camels. Twigs chewed and used for cleaning teeth in Punjab.

Bark reported to be a vermifuge.
