REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flower- Usually scarlet red, sometimes yellow, 3.7-5.0 cm long, and as much across mostly solitary or 2-4 together Epigynous; Calyx: Persistent; Corolla petals 5-7, obovate; Androecium: Stamens numerous and in many whorls, filaments usually short, inflexed in bud, anthers dithecous, introrse and dehiscing longitudinally; Gynoecium the carpels are arranged in two series with many ovules in each locule, the placenta in upper series of carpel is parietal and in lower it is axile, style long ;Fruit globose crowned by persistent calyx, with coracious woody rind and anterior septate with membranous wall ; Seed angular with fleshy testa which is red, pink or whitish; 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
 Plants are pollinated by insect and seeds are dispersed by wind, water, birds or animals.

Genetic variability:
There are two sub-species of P. granatum – (i) Chlorocarpa found in Trans-Caucasus region and (ii) Porphyrocarpa found in central Asia. These sub-species have been established on the basis of colouring of ovary, a stable feature that is retained when they are reproduced by seeds. In India several varieties of P. grantum exists such as Alandi, Bendana, Dholka, Kabul, Kandahari, Musket, Red, Paper Shell, Poona, Spanish, Ruby, Vellodu etc.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation : Plant is propagated through seeds and also vegetatively by stem cuttings.

Soil type: Plant prefer calcareous soil, containing lime nodules, and tolerate soil alkalinity to a greater extent than most other plants.

Nutrient requirements : Farmyard manure mixed with fine soil, old cattle manure required every year and also application of ammonium sulphate, wood ash and lime along with farmyard manure required every year at the time of fruiting.

Water management: Plant respond well to irrigation, copious and regular irrigation is required at the time of fruiting.

Harvesting period / Method: Normally the plants starts fruiting in about 4 years of transplanting and in about 6-7 months from the time of flowering. The fruits are harvested when the skin turns slightly yellow and fruit gives out a metallic sound when tapped. Usual harvesting season is October-December.

Chemistry: 

Pelletierine (fig. 1) from root bark characterised as (-)3-(2-piperidinyl)propan-2-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
2-propenyl-3,4,5,6-tetrahydropyridine isolated from leaves as unstable hydrocarbon, mp. 112°C. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Absolute configuration of pelletierine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
Sitosterol and ursolic acid isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Two tannins - punicalagin (fig 3) and punicalin (fig. 2) isolated from peels and their structures determined; hydrolysis of punicalagin yielded ellagic acid and punicalin, the latter yielded glucose and a tetralactone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Pectin isolated from fruits contained mannose, galactose, rhamnose, arabinose and glucose in ratio of 1: 1.3: 2.1: 4.4: 6.2; principal sugar acid was galacturonic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
Ellagitannin - granatin A - isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).
Isolation of cyanidin-3-glucoside and -3,5-diglucoside, delphinidin-3-glucoside and -3,5-diglucoside from seed coat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Punicafolin (fig 5) isolated from leaves and characterised as 1,2,4-O-galloyl-3,6(R)-hexahydroxy-diphenoyl-b-D-glucose granitin A, granitin B, corilagin, strictinin, 1,2,4,6-tetra-O-galloyl-b-D-glucose and 1,2,3,4,6-penta-O-galloyl-b-D-glucose also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
A new hydrolysable tannin-2-O-galloyl-4,6(S,S)-galloyl-D-glucose (fig 4) - isolated and characterised ; structures of punicalin (fig 6) and punicalagin (fig 7) revised. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Determination of punicic (33.3), nonadecanoic (5.9), heneicosanoic (5.0), tricosanoic (4.9) and 13-methylstearic (1.5), 4-methylalluric (0.5%) acids in seed oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.
Four new alkaloids - A1, A2, A3 and A4 - isolated from root bark along with pelletierine, isopelletierene, pseudopelletierine, methylpelletierine and methylisopelletierine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Pelletierine, N-methylpelletierene, pseudopelletierine, seldridine, 2-(2'-hydroxypropyl)-piperidin-1-ene, 2-(2'-propenyl)-piperidin-1-ene and norpseudopelletierine isolated from bark of plant from Yogoslavia. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
1,2,3-tri-O-galloyl-b-glucopyranose isolated from leaves and characterised; in addition brevifolin, brevifolin carboxylic acid, corilagin, ellagic acid, granatin B, punicafolin, 3,6(R)hexahydroxydiphenoyl-(a/b)-glucopyranose, 1,2,6-tri-O-galloyl-b-glucopyranose, 1,4,6-tri-O-galloyl-b-glucopyranose and 3,4,8,9,10-pentahydroxy-dibenzo[b,d]pyran-6-one isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
The chemical studies were carried out on the pericarpium of P. granatum and Tamin lilee compounds were isolated. (Olkal and Dinc, 1993). C.A. 1995, 122, 16944j.
Total phenols, ellagic tannins and gallic and ellagic acid were determined in the peel of the Tunisian Pomegranate variety Chelfi. The ellagic tannin content was prominently less than the amount of total phenols, which led us to look for the presence of the condensed tannins. (Ben et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 299705e.
The EtOAc and BuOH fractions of the peels of the Kazaki variety contained 2.9 and 6.8% total polyphenols and 74 and 89% tannins, respectively. The main polyphenol was identified as grantin B, pelargonidin 3-O-glucoside, quercetin, quercemeritrin, gallic acid and ellagic acids, punicalin, punicalagin and corialgin were isolated and identified from Physicochemical properties. (Mavlyanov et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 356971f.
Juices of pomegranate contained pelargonidin-3-O-glucoside, a pelargonidin 3,5-di-O-glycoside, a cyaniding 3-O-glcoside, a delphinidin 3-O-glycoside and a delphinidin 3,5-di-O-glycoside. The amount of sugars in the juices ranged 15-21% and the level of organic acids 0.9-3.0%. (Mavlyanov et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 356971f.
Leaves of Punica granatum contain the new gallotannins, 1,2,4-tri-O-galloyl-b-glucopyranose and 1,3,4-tri-O-galloyl-b-glucopyranose together with the hitherto unknown ellagitannins, 1,4-di-O-galloyl-3,6-(R)-hexahydroxydiphenyl-b-glucopyranose and brevifolin carboxylic acid 10-monopotassium sulfate. (Hussein et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 158982x.

Pharmacology: 

Fruit extract exhibited high activity against Micrococcus pyogens var. aureus, Escherichia coli and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. It also showed activity against intestinal pathogenic bacilli such as Salmonella typhosa, S. monterideo, S. schottmuelleri and Shigella paradysenteriae. Aqueous extract of roots completely inhibited activity of Mycobacterium tuberculosis - 607. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.
Tannin from the pericarp of Punica granatum showed effective antiviral activity against genitial herpa virus (HVS-2) in cell culture. The tannin inhibited viral replication, demonstrated a killing effect and blocked viral adsorption to target cell. (Zhang et al, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 249811v.
Screening of seeds of Punica granatum has revealed the presence of ursolic acid and b-sitosterol along with a long straight-chain hydrocarbon, nonacosane. The presence of estrogens and glycoside have also been detected. (Ahmed et al, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 325122p.
Presence of compounds possessing antioxidant activity from peel and seeds of pomegranate with peel as an enriched source of the antioxidants exhibiting higher activity as compared to seeds. (Singh et al., 2002).
Pharmaceutical preparation of drugs containing Punica granatum with other plants used in treatment of AIDS. (Hozumi et al., 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 134473.
Collagenase inhibitor for therapeutic use and for manufacturing skin cosmetics is extracted from plants such as pomegranate fruits. The plant materials are dried and powdered and extracted with water to give precipitate containing collagenase inhibitors. (Kawakami, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 266082e.
The polyphenols of the peels of the pomegranate had marked antitumor and antiviral action. (Mavlyanov et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 356971f.
The antibacterial and antiviral effects of dietry pomegranate possible therapeutic role against HIV. (Lee and Watson, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 81184v.
The gastroprotective effect of tannic acid and the aqueous extract of Punica granatum fruit peel was investigated in the rat against ethanol-induced damage. Punica granatum extracts gave 100% precipitation of ovine haemoglobin in vitro. The acid content of the stomach was significantly increased by Punica granatum (368%). (Gharzouli et al, 1999). MAPA 9905-3051.
Punica granatum showed high antibacterial activity. (Meera et al, 1999). MAPA 9905-3190.
Extracts (petroleum ether, chloroform, methanol and water) of Punica granatum fruit rind were tested for in vitro trypsin activity using commercially available enzyme (porcine pancreatic type II trypsin) and substrates (benzoyl-D, L-arginine-4-nitroanilide) and the activity was compared with standard trypsin inhibitor from hen egg white (ovomucoid). The methanolic extract was found to be most active and the activity was comparable to that of the reference standard. (Samiulla et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-05-2537.
Punica granatum peel extracts were tested for hepatoprotective activity against CCl4 in rats. Among these, acetone and methanolic extract, showed significant activity, lowered the serum enzymes like GOT and GPT in rats intoxicated with CCl4. (Asockson et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-05-2426.
Succesive petroleum ether, chloroform, methanol and water extracts of fruit rind of Punica granatum were tested (in vitro) for their antibacterial activity. The methanolic extract was found to be most effective against all tested microorganism. (Prashanth et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-05-2606.
Punica granatum showed good activity on Staphylococus aureus and was inactive against the other standard strains. (Holetz et al., 2002).


Ethnobotany: 

Medicine for anthelmintic (Root + Stem, Bhalla et al, 1982; Shah et al, 1981); Checks bleeding of child birth and miscarriage (Leaf, Tarafder, 1984); Cholera (Fruit + Leaf, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Cooling (Leaf, Bhattacharjee et al, 1980); Cough (fruit + Seed, Shah and Joshi, 1971); Diarrhoea (Fruit, Dar et al, 1984; Shah et al, 1981); Dysentery (Fruit + Leaf, Bhalla et al, 1982; Rao and Jamir, 1982); Eye complaints (Leaf, Bhalla et al, 1982); Pimples (Seed, Shah and Joshi, 1971); Stomachache (Fruit, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Edible (Fruit, Bhargava, 1959); Tannin (Fruit rind, Sarin, 198); Yellow dye (Fruit rind, Vishnu Mittre, 1981); Symbolic offering (Fruit, Agarwal, 1981)

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

Any other:
