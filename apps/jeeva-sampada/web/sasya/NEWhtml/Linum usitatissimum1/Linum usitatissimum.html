Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Linseed is an annual herb belonging to the family Linaceae and the genus Linum that has 100 species. It has been grown from ancient times for fibre (flax) and for its seed, which is rich in oil. The oldest regions of cultivated flax are reported to be in Asia and on the Mediterranean coast.Linseed is extensively distributed in the countries of the temperate zone as well as in those of the tropical zone. The major linseed-growing countries are Argentina, the USSR, India, the USA, Canada, Pakistan and Australia. India accounts for about 1.9 million hectares, with a seed production of 4.98 Iakhs of tonnes and occupies the third rank among the linseed-producing countries.

In India, Madhya Pradesh leads in yield and acreage, followed by Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra, Bihar, Rajasthan, Karnataka and West Bengal also grow linseed in large areas. Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh together contribute to the national linseed production to the extent of about 70 per cent (1).

Linseed can be grown on different kinds of soils, except the sandy and badly drained heavy clays or clay loams. It does well on the deep clayey black soils of central and peninsular India and on the alluvium loams of the Indo-Gangetic plains (1).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Erect glabrous annual herb, tall, corymbose-branched above, terete, grayish-green, leaves gray-green, alternate, linear-lanceolate, numerous, flat, 2–3.5 cm long, 2–4 broad, acute to acuminate, gradually and slightly narrowed at base, glaucous (2). The plant is an erect annual with single stem, forming a short taproot, linseed may grow to a height of 120 cm, but varieties grown for oil production are usually considerably shorter. The stems are thin and wiry, the degree of branching depending upon plant type and density, the leaves are unstalked, small, sessile, narrow and pointed and dark or greyish-green in colour (3).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers borne in loose, terminal, leafy racemes or open cymes, on long erect pedicels, 2–4 cm long; sepals five, long-acuminate, ovate-oblong, 5–7 mm long, entire, glandular, margins serrate, about half as long as the petals, petals blue, or white or pale pink, 1 cm or more long, obovate.

Stigmas linear, decurrent along the inner side of styles; fruit a 5-celled capsule, on long erect pedicels, globose-ovoid, 7–10 mm high, about 7 mm across, surrounded by the persistent calyx, indehiscent (5).

Breeding System :
Linseed is predominantly a self-pollinated crop. The flowers are produced near the top of the stems, each being separately stalked, while the flowering period may last for some weeks, individual flowers are short-lived. Linseed flowers are commonly bright blue, but may be pale blue or white, while most flax cultivars bear white flowers (3).

Phenology :
Main season for growing linseed is October- November. Plant to comes to maturity 110-130 days after sowing. Flowering starts around 50-60 days after sowing. Flowers are short lived and each flower is succeeded by a rounded capsule in which the seeds develop.

Pollinators:
Linseed is mainly a self-pollinated crop (4).

Seed Biology :
When mature, the seed most often has a brown colour. With a smooth and glossy surface, it is a oblong and flat, with a more or less curved beak. It develops in a capsule with 5 loculi with 2 compartments each and one seed in each compartment. The seed is made up of an embryo, Covered with an oily albumen and protected by teguments.

The latter have five cell layers. From the outermost to the innermost layer:

-- Transparent mucilagenous layer,

-- Greenish layer of parenchymatous aspect,

-- Fibrous layer with thick yellow membranes,

-- Thin and colorless, irregular layer of broken cells,

-- Monocellular red layer which gives the seed its colour if it is lacking, the seed is greenish or yellowish, and if it is atrophied, the seed is dark brown in colour (11).

 

Propagation is usually by seed and it can also be propagated vegetatively from stem cuttings. Seeds up to 10 per capsule, yellow or light brown to dark brown, shining, varying in size, weighing from 3–12 gm per 1,000 seeds, flat, oval, one end rounded. About 30-40 kg of seeds are required for one hectare (5).

Cultivation:

Fields of linseed are prepared as for other crops. Frequent ploughings are given to retain moisture and to check weeds in the field during the rainy season and a fine weed-free seedbed is prepared for sowing. BHC 10% or Aldrin or Chlordane 5% dust at the rate of 26-30 kg per ha is mixed within soil during the last ploughing before sowing to protect the seedlings against white-ants and the early damage from cutworms (1).

The main season for sowing linseed is October to November, depending upon the cessation of the rains. The crop is grown either broadcast or is drilled in lines 20 to 30 cm apart: In the case of broadcasting, which includes utera sowing also, the seed-rate is kept at 40 kg per ha. For line sowing, the seed-rate varies from 20 to 30 kg per ha, depending upon the seed size. Thin sowing gives a good number of tillers, but for fibre and seed production, thick sowing is desirable.

The application of fertilizers and irrigation increases the yield of the crop. Under rainfed conditions, the fertilizer dose including 30 kg of N and 15 kg of P per ha is given. If irrigation is available, two to three irrigations are sufficient. Under irrigated conditions, the linseed crop requires 60 kg of N and 30 kg of P per ha.

The deep placement of fertilizer at sowing in the case of the rainfed crop gives better results. Under irrigated conditions, nitrogen is applied in two splits, half the dose as basal and the other half at the first irrigation, 40 to 50 days after sowing. The utera crop is fertilized at the rate of 10 kg of N per ha applied at the time of sowing. The rainfed crop generally requires no interculture. When the crop is irrigated, one weeding becomes essential (1).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

1. Linseed fly (gall midge) (Dasyneura lini)

Symptom: The orange coloured fly destroys the flower bud while laying eggs flower in folds of the calyx. Maggote larvae feed on tender parts of the flower, thus the capsule is not formed.

Control measures: The growing of resistant and early-maturing varieties. Early sowing is recommended. The spraying of Carbaryl, Phosphomidon (0.03%) or Endosulfan (0.05%) at 15-20 days interval is suggested.


2. Linseed Caterpillar (Laphygma exigua)

Symptom: The moth lays eggs on the under side of the leaf. The newly hatched larva is pale green, with black head. It feeds on the outer epidermis of the leaves.

Control measures: The hand picking of caterpillars. Ploughing the fields after harvesting the crop to destroy pupae. The spraying of Malathion or Endosulfan or any contact insecticide.


3. Cutworm (Agrotis spp)

Symptom: Caterpillars cut the plants near the roots under the soil.

Control measures: Apply to the soil BHC 10% or Aldrin 5% or Chlorodane at 25-30 kg/ha.


4. Aphid (Myzus persicae sulz.)

Symptom: Aphids congregate in small columes. Plants stunted.

Control measures: Spray Fenithion 0.1%.


Diseases:

1. Rust (Melampsora lini)

Symptom: The appearance of bright yellow or orange pustules on both surface of leaves, surrounded by necrotic zone, later reddish brown to black pustules appear on the stem.

Control measures: Sowing of rust-resistant varieties. Early sowing. Seed treatment with Agrosan GN at 2 g/kg seed before sowing.


2.Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum)

Symptom: This disease can attack the plants at any stage of their growth. It attacks the roots. Plants attacked by this disease gradually wither and die.

Control measures: Sow resistant varieties. A crop rotation is recommended. Seed treatment with Agrosan GN at 2 g/kg seed before sowing.


3.Powdery mildew (Oidium lini)

Symptom: The disease appears in the form of whitish powder on older leaves. This powder then spreads all over the aerial parts, except the petals. Plants look as if covered with white talc powder.

Control measures: Use of resistant varieties. Early sowing. Spray Morestan or Benlate 0.1 % immediately after the appearance of the symptoms and repeat the spraying at 10 days intervals.


4.leaf spot (Alternaria lini)

Symptom: Buds fail to open. Minute black spots appear on the calyx on the young leaves, the infection is seen at the base, producing dark brown spots, spreading to the stem. Leaves dry up and get twisted.


Control measures: Sow the crop in well-drained fields. Seed treatment with Agrosan GN at 2 g/kg seed before sowing (1, 16).

Economic Potential and Uses

Linseed occupies a greater importance among oilseeds, owing to its various uses and special qualities. In India, it is grown mainly for seeds, used for extracting oil. The oil content of the seed varies from 33 to 47 per cent. Linseed oil is an excellent drying oil used in manufacturing paints, resins, printing ink, soaps, wood treatments, varnishes, oilcloth, waterproof fabrics and linoleum and as an edible oil in some areas (1, 13).

Internally, oil given as a laxative. Linseed oil mixed with an equal quantity of lime water, known as carron oil, is applied to burns and scalds. Oil mixed with honey is used as a cosmetic for removing spots from the face. In veterinary medicine, oil is used as a purgative for sheep and horses. Linseed-cake is used as feed for livestock and is prized for its high protein content. Cake is prepared for feeding by grinding to linseed meal or by making pellets suitable for feeding in outdoor feed lots or on the range (5).

Uses

Whole seed is used in the baking and confectionery industries. Linseed is used in making paper and plastics. Linseed straw produces fibre of good quality and also has application in biomass energy burners. Linseed straw from seed varieties is used in the manufacture of upholstery tow, insulating material, rugs, twine, and paper. Some of the better quality straw, produced in the more humid sections or under irrigation, is used in manufacture of cigarette and other high-grade papers (5). Flax straw is rarely fed to livestock. If mature and of good quality, it is about equal to oat or barley straw in feed value. It can be used safely as the only roughage for cattle. The fibers are digested like other fibrous materials and do not accumulate in the stomach to form indigestible balls. Linseed tea used for colds, coughs, irritation of the urinary tract (when honey and lemon juice may be added) and a jelly formed by boiling seeds is often given to calves. Linum contains the anticancer agents 3'-demethylpodophyllotoxin, podophyllotoxin and b- sitosterol.

Harvesting Practices
The crop was harvested in Feb-Mar before the capsules are dry. Plants are cut loose to the ground with a sickle or uprooted by hand. They are allowed to dry in the field for a day or two, then thrushed and seeds seperated from chaff from winnowing. The average yield of a pure crop varies from 210 to 450 kg per ha of seed under rainfed cultivation. The crop in northern India generally gives higher yield than in central and peninsular India. The irrigated crop may yield 1,200 to 1,500 kg per ha.

Chemistry

Crude linseed oil contains 0.25% phosphatides, the component fatty acids being 11% palmitic, 11% stearic, 4% hexadecenoic, 34% oleic, 20% linoleic, 17% linolenic, and 3% unsaturated C20–22. There is some wax in the crude oil containing 18.7% stearic acid, 32.5% cerotic acid, 43.1% ceryl alcohol, and 7.0% hydrocarbons. The essential amino acids are (g/16 g N) 8.4 g arginine, 1.5 histidine, 2.5 lysine, 1.5 tryptophan, 5.6 phenylalanine, 2.3 methionine, 5.1 threonine, 7.0 leucine, 4.0 isoleucine, and 7.0 valine (5).

Processing Techniques

The seeds are usually prepressed to remove more than half of the oil. The cake is tempered with steam, flaked, and hexane extracted to obtain residual oil. Before crushing, the seed is rolled into meal and heated after moistening in a steam-jacketed trough fitted over the expeller. Since crude oil contains FFA, pigments, wax and mucilage, it needs to be refined. Settling in tanks for up to three weeks allows about 10% of the oil to separate and settle as foots. Subsequently, the decanted oil is warmed rapidly to 110°C to further deposit 0.5% of matter. Chilling may be useful to remove the remaining wax. Filtration and sulphuric acid treatment remove most of the mucilage. This can be followed by alkali refining to remove FFA and treatment with 3% highly acid activated earth to lighten the colour to acceptable standards (6).

Other Uses

Linum finds its way into folk remedies for boils, bronchitis, burns, cancer, carbuncles, cold, conjunctivitis, corns, coughs, diarrhea, gonorrhea, gout, inflammation, intoxication, labor, rheumatism, scalds, sclerosis, sores, spasms, swellings, tumor. Seeds are considered emollient, demulcent, pectoral, diuretic, and astringent. Crushed seeds make a good poultice, either alone or with mustard, lobelia seed added in the poultice for boils. Sometimes seed are roasted and used in a poultice. Sometimes employed as an addition to cough medicines. Considered astringent, cyanogenctic, demulcent, diuretic, emollient, expectorant, laxative, suppurative and vulnerary (5).

Miscellaneous Information

Important hints for getting maximum yield:

(a) Clean, fine, weed free seed bed should be prepared by ploughing during the rainy season.
(b) 10% B.E.C. or 5% Aldrin or Chlordane at the rate of 25 to 30 kg/ha be mixed with the soil at the last ploughing before sowing.
(c) Early sowing escapes the attack of many diseases and pests Under utera, sowing be done at the dough stage of paddy.
(d) The application of 30 N+15 1 for the rainfed and 60 N+30 P for the irrigated crop and 10 N kg per ha for the utera sowing is recommended.
(e) Use the optimum seed rate of 30 to 40 kg per ha and a closer row spacing between 20 and 30 cm and 40 kg seed per ha for utera sowing.
(f) Cultivate the recommended varieties.
(g) Carry out plant protection measures, if needed
