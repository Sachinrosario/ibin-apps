Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large tree found in subtropical regions like Himalayan foothills , from Kumaon eastwards to Assam up to an altitude of 1,500 m. and in Andaman Islands and Central Indian hills. Occurs in mixed deciduous forests, outer Himalayas from Kumaon (Uttar Pradesh) extending eastwards to Assam and Upper Burma.

It grows on a wide range of geological formations and thrives best on well-drained, light alluvial soil and also on silt. The plant is a strong light-demander. It is frost resistant.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Middle-sized deciduous tree. Bark brownish grey when young, dark brown, rough, splitting in square flakes in mature trees. Leaves ovate or ovate-cordate, shortly acuminate, finely serrate or serrulate. Flowers small in spikes. Fruit long, cylindrical, yellowish white, sweet or insipid.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Natural regeneration is not common. Grown by entire transplanting, direct sowing or branch cutting. Best time for planting is July.

Stump planting is also practised.

Growth rate varies according to locality. It is rapid in first few years, but falls off gradually.

It coppices and pollards well..

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, Fruits and leaves.

Wood used as fuel.

Largely used in Darjeeling, Assam for making oars, stocks, spokes, poles, shafts of carriages and carts, yokes, furniture and planking. It is suitable for low grade plywood and for panelling, carving and turnery, tea-boxes and toys. It is also used to make tennis rackets. Wood used for house building.

Miscellaneous Information

Tree is planted as soil stabilizer. Grows well on plains and foothill areas. Suitable for gardens, homesteads and marginal lands.

Wood: Sapwood white or yellowish white; heartwood bright yellowish or golden brown. Wood is light to moderately heavy. Specific gravity is 0.95, wt. 38 lbs. per ft3, straight grained, medium coarse- and even-textured, moderately hars to hard, strong and elastic. Durable under cover.
