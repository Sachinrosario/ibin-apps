Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

An unarmed monoecious palm. Planted near sea-coast where it grows better than inland. Widely distributed in the tropics.

A well distributed rainfall ranging from 1,270-2,550 mm per annum is required. In average annual temperature between 27-320 C is the optimal requirement. It can be grown on variety of soils, including coastal sands and alluvial volcanic, lateritic or clay soils, provided drainage is good with adequate soil aeration. Porous soil behind the beaches is best suited for the growth of coconut. It can be grown under situations such as backwater areas of the sea coast, paddies, field bunds, embankments, irrigation canal bunds, river banks subjected to inundation. The palm prefers a light, well-drained soil permitting free root development and aeration, and does best on alluvial and sandy soils near the coast.It is cultivated throughout the hot, damp regions of India, particularly in coastal regions of Orissa, Bengal, Gujrat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An unarmed monoecious palm bearing a crown of large, pinnate leaves. Trunk 40-80 ft. high, stout, annualate, thickened at the base, with a mass of rootlets. Leaves 6-15 ft. long; leaflets equidistant, 2-3 ft. long, linear-lanceolate coriaceous; petioles stout.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

The palm is monoecious. The female flowers are relatively few, 1 in. long and globose, borne at the base of the panicle. Male flowers are numerous, small and sweet-scented, borne towards the top of the panicle.

Spadix 4-6 ft. long, stout, androgynous, at length drooping, simply panicled. Branches bearing scattered female flowers (often between 2 males) towards their bases and numerous males above. Lower spathes 2-3 ft. long, oblong, hard, splitting lengthwise.

Male flowers unsymmetric. Sepals small, valvate. Stamens 6; filaments subulate. Anthers linear erect. Pistillode minute or 0.

Female flowers larger than the male, 1 in. long, ovoid, supported by broad bracteoles. Perianth accrescent. Sepals round, concave, imbricate. Petals shorter than the sepals, convolute, with imbricate tips.

Fruit 8-12 in. long, 3-gonously obovoid or subglobose, green or yellowish. Pericarp thick, fibrous. Endocarp bony, with 3 basal pores indicating the 3 cells of the ovary. Albumen lining the endocarp, the cavity large, filled with a sweet somewhat milky fluid known as coconut milk.

Phenology:

Flowering begins when the plants are four to five years old and continues for more than 50 years. It flowers all year round and the fruit takes about 9-12 months to ripen fully.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Coconut palm is propagated by seedlings raised from fully mature fruits. Seeds are selected from high-yielding stock with desirable characters. Seedlings are transplanted in pits ideally of dimension 3ft. X 3 ft. X 3 ft.

Other uses:

Mesocarp - Fibrous pericarp yields coir which is used for ropes, mats.

Nut - Oil from the nuts is an important article of trade, used for burning, cooking, the manufacture of soap, etc.

Fruit - The ripe fruits - coconuts are largely exported as an article of food. Used as food and for drinking. Copra is edible and used for extracting oil, which is used for making soaps, in cosmetics and refuge cake is used for stock feed. Dried copra is used in confectionery. Water of fruit is sweet, cooling and used in urinary disorders and it is aphrodisiac.
Tender coconut milk is used as medium in plant physiology experiments.
Leaves are used for weaving, thatching.
Inflorescence - Inflorescence axis is tapped for toddy, which is used to make jaggery. Toddy when fermented gives rise to arrack, palm wine and fermented further to vinegar. Young buds are consumed as vegetable.

Miscellaneous Information

The sap is drawn regularly from the trees and allowed to ferment and used thus as a beverage or afterwards distilled to make arrack. It is a plantation crop.
Plant is an important source for honey bees.
Dwarf cultivars are highest yielding.
Somatic embryogenesis and plants from zygotic embryos have been obtained.
Plantlets from leaf tissue cultures have been obtained.
