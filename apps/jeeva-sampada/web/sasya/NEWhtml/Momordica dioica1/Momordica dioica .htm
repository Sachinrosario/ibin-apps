Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
A perennial, dioeciously climber with tuberous roots found throughout India from Himalayas to Ceylon, up to an altitude of 5000 feet. The plant is sometimes found growing wild and is common in hedges. It is a creeper. Widely found in forests and plains. Tribal people of Orissa cultivate this plant in their backyards. It is also found to grow in the semi-forest or fallow lands. Now it is cultivated in the fields and kitchen gardens of Assam, Bangladesh, South India, Andaman's and the northern parts of western parts.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES 
There is a closely related species (M.Cohinchinenesis) which bear very large fruit having the shape and size of a duck egg. The fruits are heavily seeded, i.e., not so fleshy but very nutritious. Its root is tuberous and the separate plants bear male and female flowers. Flower colour is blue flowers solitary, yellow; fruits ovoid or ellipsoidal, shortly beaked , densely echinate with soft spines; seeds slightly compressed, irregularly corrugated.  Teasel gourds require stakes or supports for climbing and spreading of the vines. Stems glabrous; leaves broadly ovate, entire, deeply 3-5 lobed.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY


Breeding System :

Phenology:
The flowering occurs during the month of March- December.

Pollinators:
Bees
For the pollination and fruit setting presence of male plant at least at a ratio of one male plant per 100 female plants is necessary. The plants grown from seeds produce male plants in great numbers. In other words, the sex ratio is very wide, males disappointingly exceeding the female.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination:
The plants are grown from seed or tuberous roots. The tubers remain dormant in winter and sprout in summer. The seeds dispersed from the ripe fruits of the plants germinate and grow to seedlings all around in May-June. So each plant grow only one tuber. In November the vines dry out. Then they should be removed. The fleshy roots may remain in the field or dug out for storing. The plant can also be propagated by shoot cuttings. 20-25cm. Long shoots are put into the sand medium for rooting. They root within 2 weeks. By this process female plants are obtained more in number.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:
Teasle gourd is affected by fruit rot (Pythium aphandermatum) during the rainy season and leaf spot (Cercospora mormordicae Mcrae). Fruit rot is controlled by spraying plants with Bordeaux mixture or chestnut mixture.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Tuber and fruits.

Uses: 

  1. In Philippines the tender twigs are used as pot herbs.
  2. The mucilaginous tasted root are used in the form of electuary in cases of bleeding piles and in certain bowel-affections.
  3. Juice of the root is a remedy in the Konkan for inflammation caused by contact with urine of the House Lizard.
  4. The root of the male creeper is used in ulcers caused by snake bite and root of the female plant is used as an expectorant.
  5. The unripe fruit is used as a vegetable and given as a delicacy to patients recovering from fever.

Harvesting Practices
Hand picking of fruits.
Storage: Fruits can be stored for 3-5 days under cool and shady conditions; under cold storage they can be stored longer. (4 weeks at 32 - 350 F, and 85 - 90%R.H.).

Other Uses
  1. Healthcare Properties:
    Abortion: Grind together the tuber of Momordica dioica with the epiphyte that grow on Baranga tree to make a paste. Take this paste orally once. It works for 3 to 4 months of pregnancy.
    Diarrhea (pediatric): Grind together the tuber of Momordica dioica with the bark of Deosandha to make a paste. Prepare eight small tablets of this paste. Administer orally two tablets a day for four days to the small children.
    Fever: (In pediatric): Grind together the tuber of Momordica dioica with the bark of Devasandha to make a paste. Prepare eight small tablets of this paste. Administer two tablets daily for five days.
    Poison: Extract juice from the tuber of Momordica dioica. Take one teaspoonful of this juice times a day to get recovery from poison.
    Rheumatism: Extract juice from the tuber of Momordica dioica. Take one teaspoonful of this juice orally with a glass of country liqueur (prepare from the flowers of Madhuca longifolia).
    The roots, which often weigh 1 lb or even more astringent and contain traces of an alkaloid. The roots of the female plant are larger than those of the male and are preferred for medicinal use. They are applied in bleeding piles, bowel affections and urinary complaints. The root is pasted and applied over the body as a sedative in fevers.
