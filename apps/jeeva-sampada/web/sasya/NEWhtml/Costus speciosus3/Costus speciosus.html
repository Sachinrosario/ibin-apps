An erect 1.2-2.7 m high herbs, found to occur throughout India from the Central and Eastern Himalayas ascending to c 1350 m. Srilanka,Malaya Islands, Nepal,Indo-china, Malaysia to New guinea, Taiwan.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An erect 1.2-2.7 m high herbs. Rootstock tuberous. Stem sub woody at base. Leaves subsessile, oblong or oblanceolate-oblong, 15-30 x 3.5-7.5 cm, apex acute or acuminate, often cuspidate, base rounded, glabrous above, silky-pubescent beneath. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers white, numerous in very dense, 5.0-12.5 x 3.8-7.5 cm spike; bracts ovate, 2.0-3.2 cm long; acumiante at apex, bright red. Calyx 3.2 cm long; oobes deltoid-ovate, 4-6 mm long, cuspidate. Corolla tube as long as the calyx; lobes ovate-oblong, apiculate; lateral lobes 3.5 x 1.3 cm and dorsal 4 x2 cm; lip orbicular, white with a yellow centre, concave, plicate. Stamens 3.8-4.5 cm long; connective petaloid, 0.8-1.3 mm braod. Capsules globosely 3-gonous, ca 2 cm in diam., red. Seeds black.
CIMAP:
    Inflorescence: Dense fid spike; Flower: Bracteate, complete, hermaphrodite ; Calyx: Calyx segments ovate, cuspidate ; Corolla: Corolla segments white obling, lip sub-orbicular; Androecium: Lateral staminode , filaments connate, anthers linear ; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled, ovary superior, ovules many, superposed, style filiform, stigma semilunar ; Fruit: Capsule globose, red, crowned with persistent calyx ; Seed: Obovoid . 

Floral and seed biology:
August - February.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: Root is pungent, bitter; useful in bronchitis, fever, dyspepsia, inflammation, anaemia, rheumatism, lumbago (Ayuveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
Other uses:
Purification and characterization of a b-glucosidase which converts furastanol glycosides to spirostenol glycosides from Costus speciosus. (Inoue and Ebizuka, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 302993a.

Rhizomes contained diosgenin (2.6%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Saponin from seeds yielded three genins and glucose on acid hydrolysis; major genin was diosgenin; seed fat contain palmitic (26.9), stearic (8.3), oleic (40.5), linoleic (20.9), arachidic (1.7), gadoleic (0.7) and behenic (1.0%) acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Pharmacology:
Chloroform extract of rhizomes found to be very potent ecbolic agent exerting its action on muscle cells isolated estrinised uterus of rat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Total saponin mixture showed significant anti-inflammatory activity, significantly increased uterus weight of spayed rats and produced proliferative changes in uterus and vagina. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Saponins showed oestrogenic activity in spayed rats, significantly increased uterine weight and uterine glycogen concentration and produced proliferative changes in uterus and vagina. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)h.

Saponin mixture showed spasmodic effect in uterus of varying degree on isolated ileum and isolated tracheal chain preparations. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)i.

Alkaloidal frction from rhizomes showed papaverine-like smooth muscle relaxant, antispasmodic, cardiotonic, hydrocholeretic, diuretic and CNS depressant activities in laboratory animals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)j.

Methyl 3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)-2E-propenoate inhibited growth of Aspergillus niger, Cladosporium cladosporides, Colletotrichum gloeosporides and Gloeosporium mangiferae. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)h.

Costus speciosus showed significant hypoglycemic activity.( Mosihuzzaman et al, (1994).

 Costus specoisus rhizome (24.9%) possessed significant blood glucose – lowering activity. (Nahar et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 319349j.
