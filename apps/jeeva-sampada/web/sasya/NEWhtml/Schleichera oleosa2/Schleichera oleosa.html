Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
The tree is typical of mixed deciduous forest often of somewhat dry type. Kusum is a tree of dry forests in many parts of India, Burma and Sri Lanka. The absolute maximum shade temperature in its natural habitat varies from 35-47.5 o and the absolute minimum from -2.5-17.5 o, the rainfall varies from 75-250 cm. In central India it occurs scattered in mixed forest, chiefly near banks of streams. It is also common in siwalik range. It thrives best on light, well-drained, gravelly or loamy soils although it occurs in sandy and loamy soils. Tree prefers slightly acidic soils (5, 6, 7, 8).

Availability : 

Status : Wild

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Tree is medium sized to large growing upto 32.3 m in height and 2.4-3.7 m in girth, usually with a clear bole of 6 m. bark grey or brown, reddish inside, exfoliating in small, round, irregular flakes of 8 mm thickness. Tree bears leaves and flowers early in the spring. Leaflets elliptic or elliptic-oblong, coriaceous, 5-25cm X 3-10cm., 2-4 pairs. Berries smooth or slightly prickly, globose or ovoid, hard-skinned, 1.25-2.5 cm x 1.1-1.8 cm. (6, 7, 8).  Calyx cup shaped, sepals five, stamens 5-8, exceeding the calyx. Ovary 3-4 celled (3, 6, 7, 8). One-two seeded; seeds brown, irregularly ellipsoidal, 1.0-1.5 cm X 0.8-1.25 cm., slightly compressed, oily, enclosed in a succulent aril which dries up on the seed.(6, 7, 8)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Tree bears flowers early in the spring. Flowers are minute, yellowish green, some trees producing only male flowers mostly bisexual, fascicled on simple or compound drooping racemes which are 6-15 cm long and crowded near the end of branches.

Breeding System :

Phenology :
The old leaves are shed in January-February and the young ones appear in March-April. The racemes of greenish yellow flowers appear with young leaves and the fruits ripen in June-July (7, 8).

Pollinators:

Seed Biology :
Fresh seed is expected to give about 50-60 per cent germination. In nursery the seeds are sown in lines about 15 cm apart with aspacing of about 25 cm between the seeds. Seeds weigh 1,410-2,190 per kg (6, 7, 8).

Seedling Germination :
The seed germinates readily on being supplied with adequate moisture. Germination is epigeous. The primary root of the seedling is very long, moderately thick, tapering with numerous, short to moderately long, fibrous lateral roots distributed down the main root and the stem is errect, green or pink and tomentose. The hypocotyl arches slightly, soon straightening and raising the fleshy unequal cotyledons above ground (7, 8).

Cultivation:

Natural regeneration of the tree is through seeds and root-suckers. The fruits fall towards the end of the summer or at the beginning of the rainy season. Under natural conditions the germination of the seed and establishment of seedling appear to depend largely upon the quickness of the seeds becoming covered by the earth and debris; if not, they perish due to exposure to the sun. The seeds are viable only for a short period; they can be stored for one year in gunny bags and two years in sealed tins. Germinating capacity of the seed is reported to be 58 per cent in Uttar Pradesh. If the weed growth is excessive, the growth of the seedlings is retarded.


Artificial regeneration is done by direct sowing of fresh seed in thoroughly prepared soil or by stump-planting. In West Bengal, fresh seeds are sown direct, 7.5 cm. apart, at the rate of 2-3 seeds per stake. The gaps are filled up with surplus seedlings. The nursery raised seedlings do not stand transplanting well. For obtaining stumps, the seeds may be sown in the nursery 5.0-7.5 cm. apart immediately after their collection. The stumps are prepared from the seedlings in the next transplanting season by which time the seedlings become 0.7-1.27 cm. in diameter. The stump consists of 4.0 cm. shoot and 23 cm. root portion, the rest being discarded. With regular heavy showers of monsoon the stumps may be transplanted in crow-bar holes or in cubical pits (30 cm.). Plants may also be raised in pots and transplanted during subsequent rains. The seedlings are frost-tender in early stages, and are to be protected. Stump-planting appears to give better results in moist climate as on the west coast. Regular weeding and protection from grazing are essential for the first few years. Line-sowings along with field crops may prove successful. The growth of the seedlings is believed to be slow to moderate. Trees raised by sowings showed an average of 7.2 m. height and 36.8 cm. girth at the age of 16 years, the largest being 58 cm. in girth, corresponding to 3.6 cm. girth increment. The growth of the coppice-shoots is fast during the first few years, becoming slower afterwards (6, 7, 8, 9) .

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

A large number of insect pests have been reported damaging different parts of the tree. The larvae of a number of insect pests belonging to families Geometridae, Limacodiadae, Lycaenidae, Noctuidae, Saturnidae and Sphingidae defoliate the trees. Aspidiotus orientalis, Laccifer albizziae and L. lacca feed on the sap of the branches. Serinetha augur is commonly found on fallen, dry as well as fresh fruits and also occasionally in leaves and ripening fruits and the bug is responsible for large scale damage to the seeds as it feeds on their oily content. The nymphs of Dysdercus cingulatus are also found on the fruits along with Serinentha augur. Most important pests attacking the tree is lac insect, lacifera lacca. Occurrence of Leptocoris augur (Fabr.) (Heteroptera – Coreidae) was also recorded on the Schleichera oleosa (6, 8, 15).


Diseases:

Rosellinia bunodes and Polyporus weberianus cause stem blight and yellow cork rot respectively. Daedalea flavida and Hexagonia apiaria cause white spongy rot. Irpex flavus causes white fibrous rot and leaf blight caused by Pestalotiopsis dichaeta (6, 8, 16).

Economic Potential and Uses

Schleichera oleosa seed oil is also known as Kusum seed oil, which is yellowish brown, semi-solid fat with a very high acid value. When allowed to stand, a lighter-coloured solid fat separates and settles down. It is rich in oleic acid and arachidic acid. The oil is used for illumination and in medicines, soaps, textile lubrication and in the preparation of fatty acids. The kusum oil has long been used for hair dressing. It is used for culinary and lighting purposes. Oil is used for the cure of itch, acne, burns and other forms of skin troubles. It is also used for external massage in rheumatism.The soap has a hard consistency and possesses excellent emulsifying and cleaning properties. It is highly resistant to rancidity and has a roughening action on the skin. The cake may be used as soil manure and as a cattle feed. For edible purposes, the oil should be properly refined and deodorized to remove cyanogenetic glucosides. The oil is a common adulterant of mustard oil. Seed and seed oil, presumably due to the presence of hydrocyanic acid, induce symptoms similar to irritant poisons, producing giddiness, dilatation of pupils, and in fatal cases, syncope and death (6, 10, 11, 17).

Uses

Raw fruits are pickled. The ripe one possesses whitish pulp of acidic taste and are often eaten during summer. Seeds are eaten raw or roasted. Flowers yield dye. Young shoots are eaten, they are also lopped for fodder. The timber or wood is used for oil and sugar mills, rice poulders, pestles, axles, agricultural implements such as yokes, ploughs, harrows and for turnery articles (6).

Harvesting Practices
Fatty oil obtained from the seeds is known in commerce as Macassar Oil. The fruits are generally collected during May-July and the seeds removed. The collection is conveniently done by climbing up the trees and cutting off the fruit-bearing branches. On an average a full grown tree yields about 28-37 kg. of fruits in one season and an adult can collect 19-28 kg. of fruits which yield 14-21 kg. of depulped seed (6).

Back to Top

 

Produce and Products

Fruits are pickled. Oil is extracted from the seeds, which has diverse industrial application. Wood is used in the manufacture of agricultural implements. The finest quality lac is obtained from this tree. Dye and resin is obtained from the flowers and tree respectively.

Processing Techniques

The seed contains up to 54% kernel, while the kernels contain up to 57 % oil. Oil extraction involves cleaning of the seeds, depulping, drying, decortication and finally oil removal by expellers, hydraulic press or solvent. For depulping, the fruits are kept in a heap for 2-4 days. During this period the pericarp decays, and is rubbed off with hand. The depulped seeds are washed and dried (10).


Back to Top

 

Other Uses .

Bark contains tannin which is astringent and used in external application for itch, pain in the back and inflammation and ulcers. A maceration or infusion of the bark is prescribed in malaria. Powdered seeds are applied to remove maggots from ulcers and wounds of animals. Seed oil is used in curing fungal infection, goiter, haemoroids and scabies (6, 13).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

It is estimated that out of a total annual potential of 90,000 tonnes of seeds, 30,000 tonnes of oil and 58,000 tonnes of oil cake. Only 9,000 tonnes of seeds and 3,000 tonnes of oil are being utilized. This implies that we are not exploiting the resources on commercial scale (14).
