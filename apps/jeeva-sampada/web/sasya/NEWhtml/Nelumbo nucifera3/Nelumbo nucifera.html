HABIT & HABITAT
It is an aquatic herb found throughout India. Very common in ponds, tanks, dep pools,  etc. N. China, Persia, Mmalay Islands, Japan, Tropical Australia.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Aquatic, perennial, stoloniferous herbs with milky latex. Leaves orbicular, glaucous above, pruinose, partly flat, floating, with weak petioles, partly shallowly cup-shaped, on rigid petioles raised above water; larger leaves 35-60 cm in diam; pedicels and petioles to 2 m long, with sharp warts. Flowers far projecting above water, erect, fragrant, 15-25 cm in diam. Sepals 2, early caducous. Petals many inserted at base of receptacle,, pink, white at base, sometimes entirely white. Receptacle obconic, spongy, with flat apex, during anthesis yellow, later green, at last black-brown, 6-10 cm in diam. Nutlets 1.5-2.5 cm long, usually empty. Seeds exarillate. It flowers and fruits between April and January.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Parts used:  Rhizomes and seeds are  edible. Leaves are used to serve as food plates. Flowers are used in the Hindu temples for religious purposes. This is the sacred Indian Lotus flower,“ Padma “, devoted to Brahma. Rhizomes are given to Children in diarrhoea and dysentry. Young leaves petioles and flowers are eaten as vegetables.
NBPGR: Flower- astringent, in diarrhoea, cholera, diseases of liver and heart; filament – in bleeding piles and menorrhagia; rhizome in piles, dysentery, dyspepsia, and skin diseases including ring worm; young leaf in fever; seed used for local application in leprosy and other skin affections; pistil-antidote in snake poisoning, demulcent, antiemetic.
Chemistry:
NBPGR:   Flowers yielded quercetin, luteolin and their glycosides and kaempferol glycoside.  Leaves gave quercetin iso-quercitrin and leucoanthocyanidin.  More interesting constituents from the leaves however were the benzyl tetrahydroisoquinoline, aporphine and 6a, 7-dehydroaporphine type of alkaloids.  Thus basesroemerine, nuciferine, anonaine, pronuciferine, N-nor-nuciferine, liriodenine, nor-nuciferine, armepavine, N-methylcoclaurine, dehydronuciferine, dehydroroemerine, dehydroanomaine and N-methylisococlaurine have been obtained from the leaves, seed oil consisted mainly of linoleic, linolenic, palmitic acid oleic acid, with a small quantity of myristic acid.
