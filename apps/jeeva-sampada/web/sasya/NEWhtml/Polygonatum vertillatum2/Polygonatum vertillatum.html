HABIT & HABITAT
Herbaceous plant and grows upto0.5-1.0 m tall, found in Temperate Himalaya; Kashmir,N. Kumaon to Sikkim  from 1200-3500 m. TIBET, EUROPE, N. ASIA, AFGHANISTAN.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stems 0.5-1.0 m tall, slender or stout, sometimes as thick as middle finger, angled and grooved, glabrous. Leaves 4-8 nately whorled, linear or linear –lanceolate, 7.5- 15.0 x 0.4-2.3 cm, acuminate at apex, membranous, often ciliolate at margins, green above, glaucous beneath. Peduncles 2-3 fid, whorled, 0.6-1.7 cm long; pedicels 0.5-1.5 cm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Perianth variable in size, companulate, 0.4 - 0.8 cm long, greenish or lilac, constricted at the middle; tube slender. Berry c 0.6 cm in diam., 6-10 seeded. Seeds globose. April- September.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  The root stock is sweet, cooling, emollient, diuretic, aphrodisiac, galactagogue, appetising and tonic.  It is useful in vitiated conditions of pitta and vata, burning sensation, fever, strangury, seminal weakness, anorexia and general debility.


Chemistry:
Plant yielded triterpenoid glycosides named sapindosides A,B,C,D and E4 derived from hederagenin. Related compounds named mukurozi-saponins E1, G, X, Y1, Y2- derived from hederagenm again were also obtained from the pericarp other constituents from plant are flavonoids-quercetin, kaempferol, apigenin and rutin.
Isolation of lysine, serine, aspartic acid and threonine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
Four saponosides A, B, C and D isolated from fresh rhizomes. Acidic and enzymic hydrolysis showed that saponoside D was a protoglucoside of dioscin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
Diosgenin (1.5%) isolated from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Pharmacology: The lectins from root stocks of Polygonatum multifloruma and Polygonatum verticullatum were purified by affinity chromatography on thyroglobulin-sepharose, purified lectins were obtained from fresh raw material of Polygonatum multiflorum and Polygonatum verticillatum, 340 mg/kg and 120 mg/kg respectively. The both lectins do not contain carbohydrates. Amino acid analysis has shown that the both lectins had high content of asparaginic acid/ the lectin from Polygonatum verticillatum almost 28%. Ion-exchange chromatography permitted separating the lectin from Polygonatum multiflorumi into 8 isoforms, that did not differ inmolecular weight. Purified lectins interacted with sialic acid and did not interact with other monosaccharides. Purified lectins agglutinated, but did notagglutinate human, cow, sheep and frog erythrocytes at all. Lyophilized lectins are amorphous white powders,that are well soluble in distilled water. (Antoniuk, 1993)


Ethnobotany: Medicine for aphrodisiac (Root, Gupta, 1981); Appetite (Bulb, Malhotra and Balodi, 1984); Nervine tonic, tonic (Root, Rhizome, Uniyal, 1968, Gupta, 1981); Edible (Bulb, Arora, 1981)
