REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Stipes sub-erect, polished, blackish, glabrous ; fronds bipinnate with a short terminal pinna and numerous erect-patent lateral ones on each sides, the lowest slightly  branched again, the base cuneate; sori rounded or obreniform, placed in roundish sinuses of the   crenations.

Chemistry:
  • Extraction A. capillus-veneris with petroleum ether chromatography over acidic Al2O3 and elution with petroleum ether containing increasing amounts of Et2O gave the following compounds- a mixture of esters C28H54O2 m.p. 85-90 °, saponified to acids, C16H32O2 m.p. 85-95 °, a ketone C25H40O or C26H42O, m.p. 222-224 °, a diol, C30H52O2 m.p. 243- 245 °.   (Berti et al., 1960), 2146. C.A. 1961, 55, 24933 f.
  • A. noritopene-adiantone (Fig. 1) (Berti et al 1963).  
  • Adiantoxide a compound of C30H50O or C29H48O, m.p. 229-231 °, [ a]D30 + 46.8 ° has been isolated from the fern Adiantum capillus-veneris.. (Berti, 1963).
  • The structure of adiantoxide a triterpenoid epoxide with a new type of carbon skeleton whose molecular formula C30H50O which was confirmed by mass spectrometry (Fig. 2).   These skeleton of the epoxide was biogenetically derived from hopane through several 1,2-shifts of Me group and H atoms. (Berti et al.,1964).
  • On the basis of several chemical and physical observations proved the structure and stereochemistry of a triterpinoid epoxide from Adiantum capillus-veneris which has been identified as 3 a -4 a - epoxyfilicane (Fig. 2) (Berti et al., 1969).
  • Silica gel column chromatography and several elution of EtOAC- soluble portion of   fresh fronds of A. capillus-veneris and A. cuneatum revealed 11 fractions for A. capillus-veneris. Fraction 1 contained no flavanoids, fraction 4 contained astragalin and isoquercitrin and fraction 6 & 7 contained both nicotiflovin and kaempferol-3-glucuronide, fraction 9 contained rutin and fraction 10 and 11 contained querciturone in A. capillus-veneris. (Akabori and Hasegawa, 1969). C.A. 1969, 71, 120515 j.
  • Astragalin nicotiflorin, isoquercitrin, rutin, kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside sulphate, 1-caffeylglucose and sulphate esters of 1-coumarylglucose and 1-coumarylgalacotose isolated from fronds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).
  • Kampferol-3-sulphate was isolated from an ethanolic extract of fresh fronds of A. capillus-veneris (Fig. 3). The isolated compound was electrophoretically highly mobile (towards annode), strongly suggestive of a sulphate derivative and structure was established by chemical and spectroscopic methods.   (Imperato, 1982).
  • A flavanoid was isolated by ethanolic extract of fresh fronds of A. capillus-veneris L was quercetin 3-O-(-6”malonyl )-d-galactoside (Fig. 4). (Imperato. 1982)
  • Diacylglyceryl-O-4’ (N, N, N-trimethyl)-homoserine isolated from fronds of A.capillus-veneris and identified by chemical and spectral analysis (Sato, 1983), C. A. 1983, 99, 155269 n.
  • Sterols in extract of dried leaves of A. capillus -veneris   were purified by TLC, acetylated and separated by HPLC.   b-sitosterol, stigmasterol and campesterol were identified on the basis of 1HNMR & mass spectral data. from leaves. (Marino et al.   1989), C. A., 1990, 112, 73892 h.
  • Ethyl acetate extract of fronds of A. capillus veneris contain Daphnortein. Hydrolyzed mucilage extracted from fronds of A. capillus veneris revealed the presence of galacturonic acid, galactose, glucose, xylose and rhamonose. Janin content of fronds showed the presence of resorcinol, phloroglucinol, methyl phloroglucinol and pyrocatechol. El-Tantawy, et al. 1994) CA, 1995, 123, 237630 t
  • Fronds of Adiantum capillus – veneris contains six hopane triterpenoid alcohols viz. pteron – 140en, 7 A-ol (Fig. 5.1), fern-9 (11) –en-3A-o1 (Fig. 5.2), fern-7-en-3A-o1 (Fig. 5.3), adian-5 (10)-en-3A-ol (Fig. 5.4), adian-5-en-3A-ol (Fig. 5.5) and fern-9 (11)-en-28-ol (Fig. 5.6) were isolated along with many other known triterpenoids from fresh fronds of A. capillus veneris. Their structure was elucidated by detailed 2-D-NMR analysis.    (Nakane, et al 1999).

Pharmacology:

  • Plant extract showed antiviral activity against veslcular stomatitis virus in monkey cell culture. (Husson et al. 1986), C. A. 1986, 105, 108018 n.
  • Alcoholic and aqueous extracts as well as the isolated mucilage shows antidiabetic and diuritic activity. (Et-tantawy, et al.1994), CA, 1995, 123, 237630 t.
  • Cosmetics and food contain extracts of 28 plant such as Adiantum capillus veneris  Jambosa vulgaris and Piper aduncum as inhibitors of histamine release from mast cells, the inhibitors controlling inflammation and allergy etc. The inhibitors are inhibitors of hyaluronidase. (Ogura, 1996), C. A. 1996, 124, 2984565.
  • Distillates of certain plants such as buchu and henna are used for the treatment of dry hair and sealp. (Greff, and Brambilla 1996). C. A.,1997, 126, P108626 t.
  • The alcoholic extract of A. capillus veneris tested positively against E. coli , Staphylococcus epidermidis and Bacillus thurigiensis and varied with the degree of its aqueous dilution. The growth inhibitory effect of undiluted extract was more pronounced in Staphylococcus epidermidis than in E. coli and Bacillus thurigiensis. The aforesaid extracts also produced positive stahl’s test (1969) for the presense of alkaloids. (Kumar and Kaushik 1999). B. A., 2001, (108(7), 109026.
