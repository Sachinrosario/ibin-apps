HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect tree reaching a height of 13-17 m. young stem clothed with Grey or brown silky hairs. It is found to occur in Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Tripura, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.
Availability :  
Plains in India, ascending up to 1300 m., Myanmar.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves: alternate, petioles 7.5-15.0 cm. long, trifoliate, leaflets 10-20 cm. Long leathery, lower surface covered with dense silky hairs. Flower: racemes, 15 m. long flowers bright orange-red, thickly clothed on the outside with silvery small hairs. Pods: 15-20 cm. by 3.7-5.0 cm., narrowed suddenly in to a stalk longer than calyx.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Large racemes ; Flower:  Bracteate, bracteolulate, narrow pedicillate,  hermaphrodite, irregular, complete ; Calyx:   Sepals 5, dark, olive green, densely velvety outside, clothed with silky hairs within, teeth short, the upper 2 connate and the lower 3 equal, deltoid ; Corolla: Petals 5, much exerted clothed with silky hairs, orange or salmon coloured, keel semicircular beaked; Androecium: Stamens 10, diadelphous, anthers uniform, filaments free; Gynoecium:Ovary sessile, shortly stalked, ovules 2, style elongate, incurved, beardless, stigma terminal, very small; Fruit: Pods stalked, thickened at the sutures, reticulately veined, argenteo-canescent; Seed: 

  Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : Febuary-March .
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind .

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
Sum-Febrifuge in backache, phthisis, haemorrhagic affections, ulcers, sore throat, eye troubles, leaf and flowers – tonic, aphrodisiac diuretic, leaf – in boils, pimples flatulence, worms, piles, flower-depurative in orchitis, swellings, dysmenorrhoea, seed-laxative, anthelmintic, gum, flower and leaf-astringent, gum and flower anti diarrhoeal
BSI:
In Ayurveda system of medicine roots are used in defect of eyesight. Barks are used as laxative, appetiser, aphrodisiac and also use in bone fractures, disease of anus, dysentery, piles, hydrocele, ulcer and tumour. Leaves are useful in eye and gum diseases, dysentery, stomatitis, cough, and cornial opacity. Flowers are used in leprosy, strangury, skin diseases and eye diseases.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:
Seeds contain 8-lactone of n-heneicosanoic acid, palasonin, its L-b-phenylalanine derivative, a-anryrin, b-sitosterol and b-sitosterol glucoside, flowers contain flavonoids-butrin, iso-butrin, palasitrin, butin, butein, coreopsin, iso-coreoprin, mono spermoside, iso-monospermoside and sulphurein, soft resin gave jalaric esters I and II and laccijalanic ester I and II.  Plant also gave cantharic acid, proanthocyanidins, L-b
-Phenyalanine derivatives and aleuritic acid.  Roots contain glucose, glycine, a glycoside and an aromatic hydroxy compound.

Pharmacology:

Alcoholic extract of seed prevented pregnancy in rats at a dose of 300 mg/kg on days 1 to 4 of pregnancy. At higher dose it was toxic. Flowers also showed encouraging anti-implantation activity in rats at a dose of 300 mg/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Palasonin and its sodium and piperazine salts showed anthelmintic activity, minimum lethal concentrations being 1.0 and 0.75 mg/kg respectively against Ascaris lumbricoides of human origin. A single oral dose of sodium salt (25 mg/kg) or piperazine salt (20 mg/kg) was completely effective against Toxocaria canis in dogs. LD50 values (s.c.) for sodium and piperazine salts were 7.5 and 20 mg/kg respectively in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

Active principle of seed is an alkaloid which has physostigmine – like action as shown by specific atropine blockade, potentiation of acetylcholine responses and antagonism of curare action. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Flower extract exhibited antihepatotoxic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

Seeds given orally effective in roundworm and thread worm infestations but ineffective in case of tapeworm. Nusea, vomiting, dizziness, general weakness and pain in abdomen regularly observed as side effects. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Extracts of seed coat (0.12 g/kg, p.o.) administered daily from day one post-coitum for ten days prevented pregnancy in female rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.

Alcoholic extract of flower at low doses whereas ether and water extracts at higher doses showed anti-estrogenic activity in immature mice; ethyl acetate extract containing butrine and isobutrine exhibited poor activity. Significant inhibition of uterus weight gain and vaginal epithelium cornification also observed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Methanolic extracts of Butea monosperma was studied on uterotropic and uterine peroxidase activities in ovariectomized rats. It was observed that these two parameters were highly correlated in response to treatment with these test materials and also to estradiol. It was suggested that the uterine peroxidase assay could be utilized as a biochemical parameter in the screening of new antifertility agents for their estrogenic/antiestrogenic properties. (Johri et al., 1991)

Ethanolic extract of B. monosperma flowers was administered at 150 mg and 600 mg per kg doses for 24 days to adult male rats. The testes of treated rats showed vacuolization of seminiferous tubules, exfoliation of germ cells in the tubular lumen and leydig cell hypoplasia. (Chauhan and Mathur, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9602-0793.

The petroleum ether extract of flowers of Butea monosperma exhibited anticonvulsant activity. Acetone soluble part of petroleum ether extract of B. monosperma flowers. The fractions protected animals from maximum electro shock, electrical kindling and pentylenetetrazole-induced convulsions in mice. The fractions also inhibited convulsions induced by lithium-pilocarpine and electrical kindling. The fractions antagonised the behavioral effects of D-amphetamine and potentiated the pentobarbitone-induced sleep. The fractions raised brain contents of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) and serotonin. These fractions were found to be anxiogenic and general depressant of central nervous system. (Kasture et al., 2000).

The anticonvulsive principle of B. monosperma was found to be a triterpene (TBM) present in the n-hexane:ethyl acetate (1:1) fraction of the petroleum ether extract. TBM exhibited anticonvulsant activity against seizures induced by maximum electroshock (MES) and its PD(50) was found to be 34.2+/-18.1 mg/kg. TBM also inhibited seizures induced by pentylenetetrazol (PTZ), electrical kindling, and the combination of lithium sulfate and pilocarpine nitrate (Li-Pilo) TBM exhibited depressant effect on the central nervous system. After repeated use for 7 days, the PD(50) (MES) of TBM increased to 51.5+/-12.1 mg/kg. (Kasture et al., 2002)
