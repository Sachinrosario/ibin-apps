DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Woody, bushy shrubs up to c 1.8 m high, glabrous or nearly so, unarmed; buds resinous. Leaves elliptic-oblong or obovate-oblong, 4.5-7.0 x 1.5-2.5 cm, obtuse or subacute at apex, obtuse, acute or sometimes cordate at base, coriaceous, glabrous, shinning; nerves 12-18 pairs; stipules connate, truncate or mucronate.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers subsessile, 1-3 together. Calyx c 1 cm long, densely pubescent; teeth triangular, 2-3 mm long. Corolla white at first, soon changing yellow; tube 3.0-3.7 cm long, pubescent outside; lobes oblong, 2.5-3.1 x 1.0-1.5 cm, obtuse. Fruits oblong or ellipsoid, 2.0-3.5 cm long, with a stout beak, smooth.
 CIMAP:      Inflorescence: Cymose; Flower:Polygamous ; Calyx: White, limbshortly tubular, subulate ; Corolla: Corolla tube glabrous, pubescent, lobes obtuse oblong ;Androecium: As many as corolla lobes, anthers sessile, linear included in the tube ; Gynoecium:  Ovary 1-celled, style stout, stigma clawed, ovule numerous; Fruit: Often large, ovoid, globose; Seed: Many, embedded in planceta . Flowering & Fruiting : March-September.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Gum obtained from the plant is used internally in dyspepsia and in veterinary medicine. It is employed to keep off files from sores. Drug is considered antiplasmotic and carminative. Powdered gum-resin is said to have diaphoretic and expectorent properties and used in guinea-worm. It is also used as an astringent for cleansing foul ulcers and for allaying irritation of the gums and checking diarrhoea during teething of children.


Chemistry:

Oleanoic aldehyde, sitosterol, D-mannitol, erythrodiol and a new compound – 19a-hydroxyerythrodiol (fig. 2) isolated from stem bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Gardenin and 5-demethyltangeretin, mp. 170°C isolated from gum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Two new flavones – 3’,4’-dihydroxywogonin (fig. 1a) and 3’,4’,5’-trihydroxywogonin (fig. 1b) isolated from gum in addition to isoscutellarein, 4’-hydroxywogonin, apigenin and demethoxy sudachitin and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Another flavone isolated from gum and characterized as 3’,5’-dihydroxy-4’-methoxywogonin (fig. 1c). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine as antiseptic (Gum, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1981); Bone fracture (Part not mentioned, Goel et al, 1984); Constipation (Gum, Tribedi et al, 1982); Stimulant (Gum, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1981); Veterinary anthelmintic, repels flies from wounds (Leaf, Pal, 1980); Wound (Gum, Jain, 1965); Edible (Fruit, Maji and Sikdar, 1982; Jain, 1963; Jain and De, 1964; Jain, 1964; Jain, 1963; Goel et al, 1984; Pal and Banerjee, 1971; Nagar, 1985; Seed, Jain, 1964; Shah, 1984; Nagar, 1985).
