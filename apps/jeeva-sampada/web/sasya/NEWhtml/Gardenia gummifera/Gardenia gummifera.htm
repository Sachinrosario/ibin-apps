Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:Fuel. Calorific values: 4,543 cal., 8,178 Btu.

Wood: Wood yellowish white, somewhat lustrous with smooth feel. Hard, heavy. Specific gravity 0.74, 48 lb. / c ft. straight grained, fine and even textured. Liable to fine end-splitting during seasoning.

Wood suitable for turnery work. Used for combs, tool handles, rulers, penholders and other small articles. Substitute for boxwood.


Harvesting Practices:

Should be felled soon after monsoon and the material should be protected from dry winds.

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques: Can be seasoned without difficulty.

Other uses:

Tender shoot apices with golden yellow shining exudates. This is antispasmodic, expectorant, carminative, diaphoretic and anthelmintic. It is given to children in nervous disorders and diarrhoea due to dentition and rubbed on gums to allay irritation. It is also used to clean foul ulcers. Decoction of resin is used in fevers. In dyspepsia attended with flatulance, the resin has been used with advantage. Externally, it acts as an antiseptic and stimulent. The resin is extensively employed in veterinary medicine to keep away flies from sores, for destroying maggots in wounds and as sheep wash. The resin is called as Dikkamalyor combee.

Miscellaneous Information

The leaf buds and the young shoots of this species yield a resinous exudation, known as DIKAMALI or CUMBI GUM. The resin is secreted freely in the form of tears. The shoots and buds are broken off with the tears of resin attached and marketed either in this form or after agglutination into cakes or irregular masses. The resin is transparent, greenish yellow, with a sharp pungent taste and peculiar offensive odour.
