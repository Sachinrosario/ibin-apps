Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Bentinckia are handsome, moderate to tall, solitary, slender palms with brown ringed trunks, a conspicuous crownshaft and arching pinnate leaves. Leaflets are long, tapering and alternate, with bifid tips. Superficially they resemble Archontophoenix palms, but with longer crownshafts and the leaves having a more drooped habit. The inflorescence arises below the crownshafts, is enclosed by two bracts in bud, is well branched, and bears both male and female flowers in triads. Fruit is round to egg-shaped, and black or purplish when ripe.
This Palm naturally grows on the slopes of moist hills.
Availability :

This species occurs in the border areas of Tamil Nadu and Kerala, Western Ghats of southern India, from 1000-1500 m (3300-5000 ft) elevation, and in the central mountains (Palni Hills).
Conservation/ Threat Status : Endemic (Western Ghats), Bentinckia condapanna is now rare because its succulent 'cabbage' has been prey to both elephants and humans.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Solitary slender stemmed monoecious palm; erect, to 8 m long, about 20 cm in diam, near base; crownshaft cylindrical about 1 m long. Leaves pinnate, 1-1.5 m long, ascending to spreading in all direction; leaflets linear, acuminate, deep green in colour, to 80 cm long, to 4 cm broad at middle, reduplicately folded near the point of attachment; midnerve conspicuous on upper side; tip of the leaflets bifurcating into long narrow lobes. Inflorescence infrafoliar, decompound; prophyll and peduncular bract large bicarinate, 25-30 cm long, fall off after emergence of flower branches; peduncles bracteate, divided upto 4th order. In male flowers petals slightly purple in colour, twice longer than sepals; female flowers ovoid to globose; staminodes 6, minute. Fruit ovoid, 1.4 x 7-8 mm with perianth persistent in ripe fruits; seed ovoid, 6 mm long; endocarp brittle; endosperm white, homogeneous.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Male and female flowers are separate, occur on the same tree. It propagates through seeds. No more information is available on breeding system of the species.

Phenology: Fl.: April-September & Frts.: November-March. Flowers & fruits are on a much-branched peduncle. The fruits are bright chocolate coloured ovate-globose, little compressed in the middle.
