HABIT & HABITAT
 Annual, prostrate or climbing, monoecious herb. Stems angled, more or less pubescent; young parts villous. It is found to occur in Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Tripura, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Lakshadeep.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves orbicular or cordate, 5-10 x 5-10 cm, acute or mucronate at apex, cordate at base, dentate along margin, deeply divided into 5-7 lobed, pubescent or subglabrous on both surfaces; petioles channeled, pubescent. Tendrils slender, pubescent. Male flowers: solitary, lemon yellow, 5-10 cm long, furnished with a reniform or orbicular, foliaceous bract near middle of the peduncle; calyx 8-10 mm long, pubescent; corolla veined. Female flowers: peduncles 4-10 cm long, bractetae, slender; staminodes 3; ovary fusiform; stigma bifid. Fruits long, fusiform, beaked, ribbed with numerous triangular tubercles, 3-valved. Seeds compressed, margin corrugate, sculptured on both faces.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine root is used in opthalmia and prolapsus vaginae. Fruit is bitter, anthelmintic and antipyretic. It is used in biliousness, blood diseases, aneamia, urinary discharges, asthma, ulcers and bronchitis. In Unani system of medicine fruit is astringent to the bowels. It is used in syphilis, rheumatism, spleen troubles and opthalmia.

Pharmacology:

Lectin (I), agglutinated human type-O red blood cells at concentration of about 2-4 mg/ml, while the other lectin (II) did not show agglutination even at 40 mg/ml. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)l.
A partially purified factor was both cytostatic to BHK-21 cells and inhibitory to vesicular stomatitis virus plaque formation in dose-dpendent manner. It inhibited both RNA and protein synthesis in intact tissue culture cells and also protein synthesis in cell-free wheat germ system but DNA synthesis was slightly stimulated. It was also cytostatic for both BHK-21 andleukaemic cell lines for at least 120 hr. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)m.
a-Momorcharin and b-momorcharin induced midterm abortion in mouse. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.
Momorcharaside B inhibited DNA and RNA synthesis in S180 tumor cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.
Administration of mixture of compounds (IV and V) (50.0 - 12.5mg/g) to mice reduced micronucelated polychromatic erthrocytes induced by mitomycin C, a known mutagen, by 80%. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.
Momordica anti-human immunodeficiency virus [HIV] protein (Molecular weight, 30 kd), MAP30 may be useful as nonspermicidal protection against sexually transmitted diseases.( Schreiber et al., 1999).
a- momorcharin from Momordica charantia is a unique component of momorcharin with anti-HIV activity, and markedly inhibited HIV-1 replication in acutely but not chronically HIV-1-infected T-lymphocytes. (Zheng et al., 1999).
The effect of momordin Ic was also attenuated by pretreatment with capsaicin (75 mg/kg in total, s.c.). These results suggest that the inhibition of gastric emptying by momordin Ic is relative to serum glucose and, at least in part, mediated by capsaicin-sensitive sensory nerves and the central nervous system.( Matsuda et al.,1999).
Antioxidant activity of conjugated octadecatrienoic fatty acid (9 cis,11 trans,13 trans-18:3), alpha-eleostearic acid, of karela seed (Momordica charantia), fed to rats for 4 wk. The growth pattern of rats and the effect on plasma cholesterol and high density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol and peroxidation of plasma lipid, lipoprotein, eryhrocyte membrane, and liver lipid were measured. (Dhar et al., 1999).
Effect of Momordica charantia, a bitter vegetable popularly known as Karolla, on fasting and post prandial (2 hours after 75 gm oral glucose intake) serum glucose levels were studied in 100 cases of moderate non-insulin dependent diabetic subjects. Drinking of the aqueous homogenized suspension of the vegetable pulp led to significant reduction (p < 0.001) of both fasting and post-prandial serum glucose levels. This hypoglycaemic action was observed in 86 (86%) cases. Five cases (5%) showed lowering of fasting serum glucose only. (Ahamad et al, 1999).
The activity of bittergourd fruit juice was tested on STZ treated RIN cells and isolated islets in vitro. It was found that feeding with bittergourd fruit juice caused reduction in STZ-induced hyperglycemia in mice. It markedly reduced the STZ-induced lipid peroxidation in pancreas of mice, RIN cells and islets. Further it also reduced the STZ-induced apoptosis in RIN cells indicating the mode of protection of BF juice on RIN cells, islets and pancreatic beta-cells. (Sitasawad et al., 2000).
Flavonoids from Momordica charantia (bittergourd) exerted the beneficial action by eliciting potent hypolipidaemic and hypoglycaemic activities. Moreover these flavonoids were effective in raising the haemoglobin levels in rats. (Anila and Vijayalakshmi, 2000)
Dietary bitter melon resulted in a consistent decrease in serum glucose levels in rats fed cholesterol-free diets, but not in those fed cholesterol-enriched diets, although no dose-response was noted. Addition of cholesterol to the diets as compared to those without added cholesterol caused hypercholesterolemia and fatty liver. Bitter melon had little effect on serum lipid parameters, except for high density lipoprotein (HDL)-cholesterol; HDL-cholesterol levels tended to decrease by dietary cholesterol, while they were consistently elevated by dietary bitter melon both in the presence and absence of dietary cholesterol, indicating an antiatherogenic activity of bitter melon. In addition, bitter melon exhibited a marked reduction in the hepatic total cholesterol and triglyceride levels both in the presence and absence of dietary cholesterol; the reduction of triglyceride levels in the absence of dietary cholesterol was in a dose-dependent manner. (Jayasooriya et al., 2000).
Preventive role of water-soluble constituents of Momordica charantia fruit during carcinogenesis, which is mediated possibly by their modulatory effect on enzymes of the biotransformation and detoxification system of the host. (Ganguly et al., 2000).
Effect of P(2)' site tryptophan and P(20)' site deletion of Momordica charantia trypsin inhibitor II on inhibition of proteinases. (Kamei et al., 2000)
Ethanol extract of the fruits showed significant activity against HCl-EtOH induced ulcerogenesis in indomethacin-pretreated rats and diethyldithiocarbamate-induced ulcer models. (Gurbuz et al., 2000).
Thai bitter gourd protein (MRK29) was isolated from Momordica charantia ripe fruit and seed. MRK29 inhibited the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase with 50% IR at the concentration of 18 micrograms/ml. MRK29 was concentrated in the 30-60% salt precipitated fraction, at which the concentration of 0.175 microgram/ml exerted 82% reduction of viral core protein p24 expression in HIV-infected cells. MRK29 might have modulatory role on immune cells, because it increased 3-fold TNF activity. (Jiratchariyakul, et al., 2001).
The treatment of diabetic rats with M. charantia fruit extract over a 10-week period, plasma (LPO) product and malonedialdehyde (MDA) levels close to normal. In addition, karela juice also exhibited an inhibitory effect on membrane LPO under in vitro conditions. These results suggest that M. charantia fruit extract exhibits hypolipidemic as well as hypoglycemic effects in the STZ-induced diabetic rat. (Ahmed et al., 2001).
The in vivo effect of the administration of extracts of Momordica charantia on certain biochemical parameters of Sprague-Dawley rats was investigated. It was observed that there was an increase in muscle and liver protein levels, while there was a reduction in the levels of brain protein, muscle and liver glycogen. The activities of plasma L-alanine transaminase and alkaline phosphatase were reduced. The L-aspartate transaminase and adenosine triphosphatase activities were slightly elevated in whole plant extract treated rats while the L-aspartate transaminase was unaffected by the ethanol extract but reduced the adenosine triphosphatase activity. (Araba, 2001)
Aqueous extracts of Momordica charantia prevented hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia induced by a diet high in fructose. (Vikrant et al., 2001).
Antidiabetic effect of Momordica charantia is derived, at least in part, from a decrease in insulin resistance because of the increase of GLUT4 protein content in the plasma membrane of the muscle. (Miura et al., 2001).
Antimutagenicity and chemopreventive activity of an 80%-ethanol extract of bitter melon (Momordica charantia Linn.) against the formation of azoxymethane (AOM)-induced aberrant crypt foci (ACF) was investigated. (Chiampanichayakul et al., 2001)
Alpha-glucosidase inhibitory activities were found in aqueous methanol extracts of the seeds of. (Matsuur et al., 2002).
Charantin inhibited cell-free translation in a rabbit reticulocyte lysate system with an IC50 of 400 nm, a potency lower than that of the previously reported small ribosome-inactivating protein gamma-momorcharin (IC50 = 55 nm) which also exhibited an abundance of arginine and glutamate/glutamine residues. Charantin reacted positively in the N-glycosidase assay, yielding a band similar to that formed by the small ribosome-inactivating proteins gamma-momorcharin and luffin S. (Parkash et al., 2002)
The effects of chronic treatment with hot water extract of Bitter Melon (Momordica charantia L.) or Ginger Rhizome (Zingiber offifinale Rosc) on spontaneous mammary tumorigenesis were examined in SHN virgin mice. In mice given free access to extract of Bitter Melon (0.5%) or Ginger (0.125%) in drinking water, the development of mammary tumors was significantly inhibited. Furthermore, the former inhibited uterine adenomyosis with a common pathological background to mammary tumors and the latterinhibited mammary tumor growth. While the mechanism of the effects of these natural products remains to be clarified, there were no adverse effects of chronic treatment with these agents as estimated from body weight, food and water intake and various plasma component levels as well as external appearance. Thus, these natural products, popular in Japan as foodstuffs, also appear to have a health benefit. (Nagasawa et al, 2002).
The alteration in hepatic and skeletal muscle glycogen content and hepatic glucokinase, hexokinase, glucose-6-phosphate and phosphofructokinase levels in diabetic mice were partially restored by Momordica charantia. (Rathi et al., 2002).
Bitter Melon reduces adiposity in rats fed a HF diet. BM appears to have multiple influences on glucose and lipid metabolism that strongly counteract the untoward effects of a high fat diet. (Chen et al., 2003)
Bitter melon could stimulate the increase of natural killer (NK) cells percentage and P-glycoprotein level on the membrane in blood samples from cervical cancer patients who ingest bitter melon. The results showed an increased percentage of NK cells in patient control and patient treatment groups. (Pongnikorn et al., 2003).
