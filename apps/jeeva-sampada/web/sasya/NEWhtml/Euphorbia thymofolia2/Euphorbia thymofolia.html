HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennial, prostrate, diffuse herb found to occur in  India, ascending to l893m. Very common in waste places, fallow lands and roadsides. Tropical Asia, Bhutan, Srilanka and all hot countries except Australia.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial, prostrate, diffuse herb. Stem hispid, pinkish or green. Leaves opposite, elliptic, acute at apex, oblique at base, crenulate at margin, pinkish or green, glabrous above hairy beneath, shortly petiolate. Involucres in alternate, axillary clusters, hispid, 4-lobed; lobes with minute glands, glands with minute limb. Male flowers: anthers dehiscing vertically. Female flowers: ovary pubescent; styles 3, stigma 2 partite. Seeds 4 angled, brown, transversely furrowed.

 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Cymes from the long peducles ,3-many flowered; Flower: Bracts ovate-lanceolate, monoecious, combined in an inflorescence of many male florets and female floret ; Male Flower: a stalked stamen without floral envelop ; Female Flower: Ovary 3-celled on ultimately exerted stalk in the center of axillary and campanulate involucre, ovules solitary in each cell, style 3, short, 2-fid, free; Fruit: Capsule obtusely keeled pubescent, of 3 two valved compressed cocci  ; Seed: Quadrangular, bluntly  pointed, with 5-6 numerous furrows . Flowering & Fruiting : More or less throughout the year.

Floral and seed biology: Fl. & Fr.: Throughout the year.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Leaves and seeds are used as stimulant, astringent, anthelmintic, laxative, particularly for bowel complains of children. Plant extract is used as a cure for ringworm. The whole plant is used to stop diarrhoea and dysentery. Roots are used by santals as a remedy for amenorrhoea.


Chemistry:
Compounds A, mp. 187°C, B and C isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

12-deoxyphorbol-13,20-diacetate, 12-deoxy-4b-hydroxyphorbol-13-dodecanoate-20-acetate, n-hexacosanol from whole plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Epitaraxerol, hexacosanol, euphorbol, 24-methylenecycloartenol, 12-deoxy-4b-hydroxyphorbol-13-dodecanoate-20-acetate, 12-deoxy-4b-hydroxyphorbol-13-phenylacetate-20-acetate, 12-deoxyphorbol-13,20-diacetate and quercetin-3b-galactoside isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Isolation of a new hydrolysable tannin – isomallotinic acid (fig. 1) and its structure determination along with isolation of 2,3-di-O-galloyl-D-glucose, 1,2,3-tri-O-galloyl-b-D-glucose, 1,3,4,6-tetra-O-galloyl-b-D-glucose, 2,3-(S)4,5,6,4’,5’,6’-hexahydroxydiphenoyl-D-glucose, pendunculagin, 1-desgalloyleugeniin, eugeniin, rugosin B, corilagin, geraniin, bixanin, casuariin and desgalloyl-stachyurin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).


Pharmacology:

Compounds A and C inhibited growth of Escherichia coli and Bacillus subtilis but had no effect on B. circus and Staphylococcus aureus. Compound B did not inhibit growth of any of these four organisms. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Ethyl acetate extract (0.45 mg/ml) and chloroform extract (0.75 mg/ml) inhibited growth of Escherichia coli and Shigella flexneri in vitro. Ethyl acetate extract was also active against S. flexneri in vivo; its administration in agar suspensions, 1.5 mg/day for 3 days cured 80% of infected mice which rose to 100% by 6.0 mg schedule. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Euphorbia thymifolia in medicated form were used on scalp for strengthening the skull bone of children by means of which they could carry head-loan in adult age. (Banerjee and Pal, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9503-1875.


Ethnobotany:

Medicine for atrophy (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Blood dysentery (Whole plant, Jain, 1984); Blood purification (Whole plant, Mishra and Sahu, 1984); Cooling (Whole plant, Maheshwari et al, 1980); Dysentery (Leaf, Singh et al, 1987; Whole plant, Jain, 1984; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Eye disease, pain in breasts (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Ringworm, skin disease, snake bite (Whole plant, Mishra and Sahu, 1984); Sores (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Spermatorrhoea (Whole plant, Maheshwari et al, 1980; Maheshwari et al, 1981); Veterinary stomach disease (Whole plant, Chaudhuri and Pal, 1976)

Any Other 

Efficacy of antiviral principals (AVPs) from coconut, Euphorbia thymifolia, in preventing the infection of  cowpeas by tomato spotted wilt virus was completely lost when an enzyme trypsin was added to them as revealed by 1.66 – 3.20 percent inhibition of lesion formation over control as against 88.9 to 100 percent inhibition of lesion formation over control in AVPs alone sprayed plants. (Manickam and Rajappan, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9902-1108.

Euphorbia thymifolia plant extracts chemical solution namely copper sulphate and copper acetate (1000 ppm) were found to suppress virus symptoms in treated green gram (Vigna radiata) plants. (Manickam and Rajappan, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9904-2542.
