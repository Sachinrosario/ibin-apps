HABIT & HABITAT
Moderately-sized to large deciduous tree 12-28 m in height. Found throughout the greater part of India and the Andaman islands. An Indo-Malaysian species whose native range extends from north-western  India  to Sri-Lanka, Myanmar, Indo-China and Taiwan. It is found up to an altitude of 1500 m in sub-Himalayan Sal (Shorea robusta) dominated forests  of  the  hills and plains, particularly in dry  locales. Often planted as an avenue tree and sometimes for making field boundaries.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Plant has a bark which is Grey or whitish-smooth, exfoliating in irregular rounded plates, leaves imparipinnate, 25-45 cm long, crowded at the base of  branches. Leaflets 7-9 oblong-ovate 7.5-15 cm x 2.5-5 cm. Flower small, yellowish or purplish , unisexual, male fascicled in slender compound recemes, female in simple pubescent recemes, fruit red, compressed, reniform, 1-seeded.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests: 

Botrychid beetle (Heterobostrychis aequalis Waterh.) and a few other borers of felled timbers. Placaederus obesus Gahan a cerambycid beetle. The larva of this insect pupates inside the wood in wide galleries. Others are Pachydissus sp. Xyleborus, porvulus  Eichoff and Glenea mullltiguttata Guar .

Diseases: 

Fungal infection: Rust(Phacospora odinae Mundkur) brown leaf rust (Cerotelium lanneae (V.Hoehn.) Arth.) and leaf spot (Meliola geniculata Syd & Butler).

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
bark, wood, Leaves

Uses
Bark of stem and branches is the source of Jhingan gum.  

  • This gum is used in confectionary and as mucilage for making ink.
  •  Used in calico-printing, paper and cloth sizing, inferior varnishes and inks, plastics and white washing, confectionary, flocculating agents for clarification of sugarcane juice.
  • The gum exuded from the cut bark is given in the treatment of asthma and as a cordial to women during lactation.


Harvesting Practices
Gum is tapped in North India  from March till the onset of rains by making shallow cuts in the bark. The yield is about 5 kg/tree in the first year, decreasing to 1kg after 5 Years.  Therefore, after tapping for five years, the plant should be given rest until wounds get healed and the tree has regained vitality. Gum occurs in round tears or colorless angular fragments, like gum arabic.

Produce and Products
Jhingan gum

Other Uses
  1. Wood: Making furniture, plough shafts, combs, brushback, troughs , boats, brown paper.
  2.  Bark is astringent, acrid, sweet, thermogenic, stomachic and anodyne lotion for bruises, wounds, sores, ulcers, sore eyes, asthma, gout, ulcerative, stomatitis, sprains dysentery. Used by women during lactation, stimulant emetic.
  3. Leaves are useful in elephantiasis, inflammations, neuralia, sprains and bruises.
  4. In Ayurvedic, the bark and resin are used to treat wounds , cardiac diseases, diarrhoea and diseases of the nervous system.
