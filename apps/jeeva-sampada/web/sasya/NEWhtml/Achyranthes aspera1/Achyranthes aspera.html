HABIT & HABITAT
It is a 90-120 cm tall under shrub, with quadrangular branches having fruits with spines pointing downwards in major parts of the young twigs.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Erect 0.3-0.9 m high; stem stiff, not much branched. The leaves are opposite, thick, pointed, broadly ovate or orbicular, 8 -12.5 cm  long and 5-8 cm broad. Leaves are acuminate, base cuniate, petiole short, pubescent from a coat long simple hairs. Stipules dull  green, often tinted with purple. A.  aspera along  with  A. bidentata  occurs commonly as a weed in the  drier wastelands, up to 2000 m elevation. Flowers greenish white & numerous in rachis, in elomgate terminal spikes. Bracts 3 mm ovate, bracteoles 3 mm long broadly ovate & concave. Perianth 4-6 mm, stamens 5 in number. Seed subcylindric & brown.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers shining dull green. Bracts and  bracteoles spinescent with stiff perianth. The stamens alternate with five  oblong staminodes, all united at the  base.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Plant, Seeds, Root.

Uses
1. The plant is mainly used against renal dropsy and bronchial affections, and sometimes as an abortifacient. 

2.Decoction of the whole plant is considered a good diuretic, while the decoction of powdered leaves with honey or sugarcane juice is useful in the early  stages of diarrhea and dysentery.  

3.A mixture of  powdered  root with pepper powder is used in  cough, while the twigs containing seeds  are considered as a good  expectorant. Ash of root with honey is used for treating cough  and  for  external application on piles. 

4.The use of twigs as an abortifacient appears to  be mechanical but effect of plant juice  in quickening  labour pains indicates   that it may have some physiological  function. The ash of the plant is considered a good expectorant, diuretic and antispasmodic and used in bronchial troubles and dropsy.  

5.Root is used for treatment of pyrrohea, cough, fever, leprosy and enlargement of liver and spleen.

Produce and Products
The well-known Ayurvedic preparations are "Apmarga Kshara" (ash of  the plant) and "Apmarga Taila", while  in Unani medicine, the ash of the plant  is   used   in   "Kushta   Sam-ul-far",  "Kushta Shangraf" and "Kushta Har- tal Warqi".

Chemistry
Defatted seeds yield about 2 per cent  saponins(A and B), which have been identified as a-L-rhamnopyranosyl  (1 -4) p-D-glucopyaranosyl (1 — 4) p- D-glucopyranosyl (1-3) oleanolic acid and p-D-galactopyranosyl (1 -28) ester of saponin A [1,2]. Ecdysterone has been isolated. Betaine is the basic constituent identified besides achyranthine alkaloid.

Other Uses

  1. A saponin has shown cardiac stimulant activity. 
  2. Achyranthine, an alkaloid contained in the plant, showed decrease in blood pressure and heart rate, dilates blood vessels and increases the rate and amplitude of respiration in experimental animals (dogs, frogs). Achyranthine has diurec- tic and purgative activities in rats. 
  3. Total extract of A. aspera is shown to be hypertensive, cardi-otonic and relaxant of intestinal muscles. Benzene-soluble fraction of the plant has exhibited 100 per cent abortifa- cient activity in the rabbits in a single dose level of 50 mg/kg. The drug (total alcoholic extract) was reported to be neither estrogenic nor antiestrogenic orandrogenic. The drug is reported to
    be non-toxic and non-teratogenic .

  6. Ash of root with honey is used for treating cough  and  for  external application on piles. 
  7. The use of twigs as an abortifacient appears to  be mechanical but effect of plant juice  in quickening  labour pains indicates   that it may have some physiological  function.  
  8. The ash of the plant is considered a good expectorant, diuretic and antispasmodic and used in bronchial troubles and dropsy; used as an alkali in dyeing.
  9. Root is used for treatment of pyrrohea, cough, feverleprosy and enlargement of liver and spleen.
