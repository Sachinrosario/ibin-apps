DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem long, greenish-yellow, fleshy, glabrous, sometimes dotted with red. Flowers solitary or in umbellate clusters of 2-4 or in short raceme; pedicels short; bracts ovate-oblong, fleshy. Calyx lobes deltoid, 3 mm long, fleshy. Corolla white, deltoid, reflexed, scales at base of corolla tube. Stamens at throat of the corolla tube. Capsules depressed, globose, glabrous. Seeds 2-4, black, glabrous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence:  Solitary or in umbellate clusters of 2-4 or in short racemes; Flower:  Pedicellate, bracteate, complete, hermaphrodite; Calyx:  Divided almost upto the base, lobes slightly unequal, obtuse, glabrous, fleshy; Corolla: Campanulate, deltoid, acute, reflexed, scales almost at the base of corolla tube ;Androecium:  Stamens 5, inserted in the throat of corolla tube, filaments scarcely any, anthers exerted beyond the top of corolla tube; Gynoecium:  Ovary imperfectly 2-celled, ovules 2 in each cell, ovoid style simple, stigma 2 distinct, large and fleshy; Fruit:  Capsule depressed, globose, glabrous; Seed: 2-4, large, black, glabrous.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : January- February. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine the plant is used as astringent to the bowels and tonic. It is also used in eye and heart diseases, biliousness and ‘Kapha’. In Unani system of medicine the plant is used as tonic, anthelmintic, purgative, diuretic and to purify blood. It is also used in jaundice, pain in muscles and joints, headache, paralysis, spleen diseases and vomiting. Seeds are diuretic and sedative. It is used in liver, spleen diseases, chronic fever, hiccough. It purifies blood and clean to bowels.


Chemistry:
Dulcitol, luteolin, quercetin and a glycoside of luteolin, m.p. 318°C, isolated from stem. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Antiviral substance containing protein, M.W. 14,000-18,000, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

A triterpenoid – maragenin (fig. 1) – which is insect growth regulatory factor, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of scoparone, melanettin, quercetin and hyperoside from plant parasitic on Bougainvillea spectabilis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Cuscuta reflexa contained mainly caffeic acid bepsides. The phenolic patterns proved to be stable accumalation of caffeic acid depsides in the haustorial regions, were correlated to an enhanced activity of phenylalanine ammonia-lyase. (Loeffler et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 122, 261093d.

Hydroxyoleanene alkanoates (fig. 2) were isolated from Cuscuta reflexa and their structures determined. (Anis et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 220378x.

A new natural product stigmast-5-en-3-O-D-glucopyranoside tetraacetate was isolated along with known compounds. Stigmast-5-en-3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside, stigmast-5-en-3-yl-actate and b-sitosterol. (Anis et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 90768c.

Nine compounds including a coumarin, a-amyrin, a-amyrin acetate, b-amyrin acetate, oleanolic acid, oleanolic acetate, stigmasterol and lupeol were reported. (Anis et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 149021j.


Pharmacology:
Alcoholic extract (0.5, mg) caused rise in tone and amplitude of perfused frog heart but 2 mg caused immediated systolic arrest. Extract (1 mg/kg) administered i.v. in dog caused fall in blood pressure (60 mm) leading to tachphylaxis on repeating administration 3-4 times. Studies on smooth muscles suggested that extract had direct depressant action on rabbit duodenum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
