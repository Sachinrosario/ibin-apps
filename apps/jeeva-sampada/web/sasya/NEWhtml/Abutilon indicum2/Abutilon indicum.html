DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, annual or perennial herbs or undershrubs, upto 3 m high. Stems minutely hoary, tomentose, diverestly branched. Leaves ovate to sub-orbicular, cordate at base, acute or acuminate at apex, crenulate-dentate, 7 or 9-nerved, wooly tomentose, 2-18 cm long. Stipules linear, deflexed, 0.4 cm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plants yields a cordage fibre. It is also used as febrifuge, anti-inflammatory and in Urinary  troubles. Bark is used as astringent and diuretic. Root is used as nerve tonic and in piles
Chemistry:
  • Flavanols and anthocyanins present in flower petal of A. indicum were Gossypetin – 8 and 7- glucosides and cyanidin-3- rutinoside respectively. (Subramanian & Nair 1972)
  • The petroleum ether extract of A. indicum was fractionated. Fraction A. was saponified. The unsaponified matter was found to contain n-alkane mixture (C22-C34), an alkanol fraction and b-sitosterol. The marc was extracted with alcohol 50% and the extract fractionated into Fractions B, C and D. The residual plant material was extracted with and the extract fractionated into Fractions E and F. In Fraction B, vanillic, p-coumaric, p-hydroxybenzoic, caffeic and fumaric acids, and in Fraction C, p- b-D-glucosyloxybenzoic acid (Fig. 1a) and gluco-vanilloyl glucose (Fig. 1b) were isolated and identified. In fractions D and F, fructose, galactose, glucose, leucine, histidine, threonine, serine, glutamic acid and aspartic acid were identified by co-chromatography. In Fraction E, a mucilage, the acid hydrolysate showed galactose and galacturonic acid. (Gaind and Chopra, 1976).
  • Amino acids, glucose, fructose and galactose isolated from leaves. (Baxi and Parikh,1981, C. A. 1981, 95, 165633 h).
  • b-pinene, caryophyllene, caryophyllene oxide, cineole, geraniol, geranyl acetate, elemene, eudesmol, farnesol and borneol identified in oil. (Jain and Sharma 1983, C. A. 1983, 98, 86304 k).
  • Eudesmol (22.0), geraniol (13.0) and caryophyllene (11.6%) identified in essential oil of flowers. (Geda and Gupta, 1983, C. A. 1983, 99, 128145 n)
  • Gallic acid isolated. (Rastogi & Mehrotra, 1985 - 1989).
  • Alantolactone and isoalantolactone are the two sesquiterpene lectones which were isolated from plant A. indicum. (Sharma and Ahmad, 1989).
  • A water-soluble galactomannan has been isolated from the seeds of Abutilon indicum containing- D-galactose and D-mannose in 2:3 molar ratio. Acid catalyzed fragmentation, periodate oxidation and methylation showed that the seed gum has branched structure consisting of linear chain b -D (1 ® 4) linked mannopyranosyl units, some of which are substituted at O-6 by two a -D (1 ® 6) galactopyranosyl units mutually linked glycosidically as end groups. (Singh et al., 1997, 203-205., CA, 1997, 128:125804 h.)  

Pharmacology

  • Fixed oil from roots shows significant analgesic activity. (Rastogi & Mehrotra, 1985 – 1989).
  • Gallic acid at 0.1g/kg, i.p. showed analgesic activity in rat within30-45 min. (Rastogi & Mehrotra, 1985 - 1989).
  • Methanolic extract of A. indicum was used to measure the uterine peroxidase activity of overiectomized rats, which have been shown to possess antifertility activity. (Johri et al., 1991).
  • Abutilon indicum shows antimicrobial activity (Metha et al., 1997, B.A., 1997, 104: 102771).
  •   Eugenol isolated from this plant shows significant analgesic activity at the doses of 10, 30 and 50 mg/kg body weight and it also exhibit 21.30, 42.25 and 92.96% inhibition of acetic acid induced writhing in mice. (Ahmed et al., 2000).
  • The liquid leaf extract of A. indicum along with Allium cepa shows positive effect on paracetamol    and carbontetrachloride induced hepatotoxicity. (Porchezhian and Ansari 2000).
  •   Abutilon indicum exhibited maximum antifungal activities towards inhibiting the mycelial growth as well as spore germination of pathogen. (Pyricularia grisea cav). (Kamalakannan et al.,, 2001).
