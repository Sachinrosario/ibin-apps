HABIT & HABITAT                                                                        
Annual or Perennial hairy herb, 30-120 cm. high, found in temperate Himalayas from Punjab eastwards to Assam, ascend- ing up to an altitude of 3,600 m., and in Nilgiri hills.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Erect herb with leaves long-stalked, cordate, downy : flowers pink, crowded, in clusters. Biennial herbs, 50-100 cm tall. Stem sparsely stellato-velutinous. Leaves simple, alternate. Stipule ovato-lanceolate, stellato-puberulous; petiole 2-8 cm, subglabrous, puberulous in adaxial groove; leaf blade reniform or round, 5-11 ´ 5-11 cm, palmately 5-7-lobed, lobes triangular, obtusely acute, margin crenate, very sparsely strigose or subglabrous both adaxially and abaxially. Flowers 3 to numerous fascicled in axils of leaves, very short-pedunclate to nearly sessile. Bracteoles 3, filiform-lanceolate, 5-6 mm, ciliate. Calyx cup-shaped, 5-8 mm in diam., 5-lobed, broad triangular, sparsely stellato-strigose. Corolla slightly longer than sepals, whitish to reddish; petals 5, 6-8 mm, apex retuse, claw glabrous or sparsely hairy. Staminal column 4 mm, hairy. Style branches 10-11. Fruit flat globose, ca. 5-7 mm in diam., mericarps 10-11, dorsally smooth, 1 mm thick, reticulate on both sides. Seeds reniform, ca. 1.5 mm in diam., glabrous, purple brown.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Entire plant, Leaves , Flower (But poisonous to sheep).

Uses
  1. The leaves and tender shoots of the herb are boiled and eaten as a vegetable in Assam, where it is grown as a cold weather crop. 
  2. The plant is used as animal feed as such or after ensilage. Malvalic acid has been identified in the oils from leaves and seeds . 

Harvesting Practices
Leaves harvested in February.

Produce and Products
Oil, Fibre.

Back to Top

Chemistry
The plant contains 0.5-2.5% total oxalate (calculated as oxalic acid) and possibly an alkaloid (Webb, Bull. Conn. sci. industr. Res. Aust., No. 232, 1948, 103 ; Chem. Abstr., 1954, 48, 14037 ; Webb, Bull. sci. industr. Res. Org. Aust., No. 241, 1949, 34).

Other Uses
  1. The plant is considered to possess hechic, emollient and resolvent properties. A decoction of the plant is given in dysentery. 
  2. The leaves are applied to inflamed sores and wounds as a cooling and healing salve and flowers are given as a pectoral and diaphoretic.
  3. The plant apparently shows no antibacterial properties. The stems yield a fibre and are used for making brooms M. tinu'ricaniim Ton', syn. M. spicnium A. Gray is a weed, up to 60 cm. in height, found in various parts of India. It is said to be poisonous to sheep.
  4. The root of M. verticillata is used for whooping cough in Indo-China. 
  5. The ash of dried leaves is given in scabies 

  6. It is used for anti- bacterial ,vegetable and medicinal purposes.
