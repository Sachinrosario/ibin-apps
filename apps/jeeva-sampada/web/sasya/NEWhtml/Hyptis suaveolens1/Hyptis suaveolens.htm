Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
Strong scented Herb sparingly naturalized in relatively dry, disturbed sites, 150-300 m. Distributed in Tropical America, now widespread as a weed. Very abundant in open, wasre places at low and medium altitudes throughout the philippines.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Strong-scented aromatic annual grows to 1.5 m tall, coarse, erect, branched, more or less hairy squary stems.
Leaves; ovate to obovate, 3-5 cm long and 2-4 cm wide, the margins serrulate, lower surface densely hairy; petioles up to 3 cm long.  Flowers axillary, long-stalked Calyx hairy, 5 mm long in flower, 10 mm long in fruit, ribbed, striate with erect, stiff teeth.    Corolla blue, strongly zygomorphic, bilabiate; stamens 4, declinate, and about 8 mm long, with a limb 5 mm in diameter.
Fruits:The immature fruits are green, when maturing change cafe clear; of 0,5-0,6 cm in length. Nutlets about 1.2-1.5 mm long, slightly notched at the end, seeds are flat, black and mucilaginous.

Chemistry:
The seed contain 77%-80% linoleic acid, and little or no linolenic acid. 1,8-cineole, spathulenol, (E)-caryophyllene and biclyclogermacrene were the principal constituents of essential oil from seeds. Cluster Analysis which allowed three groups of essential oils to be distinguished with respect to the content of alpha-terpinene/terpin-4-ol/globulol/epi-a-cadinol, o-ocymene and beta-bourbonene/germacrene B/(E)-caryophyllene. The acidic polysaccharide from the seed-coat mucilage of Hyptis suaveolens is a highly branched L-fuco-4-O-methyl-D-glucurono-D-xylan for which a structure is proposed having a 4-linked beta-D-xylan backbone carrying side chains of single 4-O-methyl-alpha-D-glucuronic acid residues at O-2 and 2-O-L-fucopyranosyl-D-xylopyranose units at O-3. The structural analysis involves base-catalyzed beta-elimination of uronic acid residues from the methylated glycan followed by degradation using a modified Svensson oxidation-elimination sequence.

Other uses:
· Fever associated with cold.
· Flatulence: Decoction of herb as tea.
· Boils: Crush leaves and apply on affected area.
· Headache: Crush leaves and apply on forehead or temples.
· Pounded fresh material applied as poultice may be used for treatment of snake bites.
· Decoction of fresh material may be used as external wash for dermatitis, eczema.
· A bedbug repellant from the intense pungency of the leaves.
