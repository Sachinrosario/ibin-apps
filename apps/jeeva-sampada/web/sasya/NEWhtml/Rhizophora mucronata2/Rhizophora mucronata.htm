Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large evergreen glabrous shrub or small tree found in salt marshes and tidal creeks along the coast from Sind to Kanara. Common and often gregarious along the intertidal banks of creeks and channels in sheltered mangrove areas under estuarine influence.

In India, this species is prominent in the Sunderbans, and other east coast mangrove forests and Andaman and Nicobar Islands and is less known on the west coast. A species of old world tropics distributed from East Africa to the Pacific Isles through S. Asia, S. E. Asia and N. E. Australia.

The plants are found only in tropics and subtropics. They are very sensitive to frost.

Trees are suited to coastal and estuarine zones.

The plants are best in moist tropical areas where annual rainfall is over 1,000 mm.

The plants can survive in highly saline, poorly drained soils that are irregularly inundated by the tide. They grow best, however, in areas that are regularly flushed by the tide or in areas with freshwater seepage. Regular flushing with seawater or freshwater appears to be necessary to avoid excessively high salinities, which arise through evaporation and would otherwise restrict growth. Consequently, attempts to impound mangrove areas within levees often reduce productivity and sometimes lead to death of trees. Soil in mangrove swamps is usually deep, black mud that is almost continuously water logged, but mangroves also will colonize sand and carbonate soils.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large evergreen glabrous shrub or small tree up t 20 m tall; with many upwardly growing branches, young branches thick, conspicuously marked with the scars of fallen leaves and stipules; stem-base supported by numerous branched stilt-roots. Leaves 3-8 by 1¾-4 in., elliptic, acute, usually mucronate, glabrous, bright green above, paler, minutely rugulose and dotted with black dots beneath, base tapering; stipules glabrous, minutely rugulose, caducous.

Aerial roots descend from the branches, and "prop" or "stilt" root spring out from the sides of their trunks and arch downward deep into the soft, black mud. These roots function as aerators, while the subterranean, absorptive roots, and even the lower parts of trunks, are submerged in salt water, conditions that would completely destroy other forest trees.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers pedicelled in axillary, usually 3- (rarely 2-) flowered cymes slightly longer than the petioles; peduncles stout, ¾-1½ in. long from axils of leaves of the same year; pedicels short, rugose. Calyx lobes pale-yellow, coriaceous, triangular-oblong, subacute, glabrous, reticulato-rugose, keeled within, reflexed in fruit. Petals white, shorter than the calyx-lobes, oblong, obtuse, thick and fleshy, densely villous on the margins and less so on the inner faces. Stamens 8, anthers linear, acute. Fruit ovoid-conical, surrounded at the base by the persistent calyx-lobes; protruded radicle cylindric, sometimes reaching 2 ft. long before it falls from the tree.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Seeds germinate while still attached to the tree; the fully formed, spindle like seedling falls from the tree, spearing into mud beneath, or floating until it finds lodgement, develops roots, becomes upright and sprouts leaves at the apex.

Studies reveal that direct seedling method result in about 90 per cent survival. Air layering has become important.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as firewood by coastal people of tropical and sub-tropical countries. Good fuel with high calorific value. Calorific value: 4,888 cal. It is reported that 5 t of wood equals the calorific value of 2-3 t of coal.

Makes excellent quality charcoal. It burns steadily, giving off intense heat without sparking.

The exhausted mangrove wood may be pressed in a hydraulic press to remove moisture and used as fuel in boilers.

Wood: Sapwood is light red; heartwood dark red to reddish brown, sometimes marked with darker bands, giving an ornamental appearance, straight grained and very fine and dense textured, tough, strong, very hard and heavy. Weight 833-169 kg/cu. ft. Specific gravity: 0.81. 0.9% of ash. Durable. If cut on the quarter, the wood presents an attractive silver-grain.

Timber is good for heavy construction. The logs are used as piles for temporary bridges, buffers, mine- and pit-props, brake blocks, stakes for fish traps, tool handles, and mallet handles. Young trees are used as posts in huts, for reinforcing mud-walls, and for frames of houses located in and about the swamps. Baskets for sifting the grain are made from the split stems. Wood chips useful for pulp and rayon. Timber is used for building but also for pulp and rayon.

Other uses:

Tannin - Bark supplies a useful tanning material. It is one of the richest and cheapest sources and is much exploited in leather industry. Both bark and wood are used for the commercial production of solid tan-extract, which is generally referred to in the leather trade as 'cutch'. High tannin content, low cost of its tan, low loss of tannin due to fermentation of tan liquor and a relatively high fixation value makes it a very useful tanning material. Wood extract is better than that of the bark extract.

Leaves, unripe fruits and ripe fruits contain tannin.

Mangrove extract - It has been found to be very good for maintaining oil-well drilling muds within a desired range of flow, preservation of fishing nets and sails, softening of water in boilers, making ion-exchange resins and for the preparation of plywood adhesives.

Spent bark of mangrove after the extraction of tannis can be exploited as a source of furfural. Its ash is composed mainly of lime and calcium carbonate and may be useful as a fertilizer in place of ground lime.

Medicinal - Plant parts are medicinally useful.

Fruit - Fruit is sweet and is eateen. The juice is made into a light wine.

Food and fodder - Young shoots are cooked and eaten as a vegetable. Shoots and fruits are a poor quality fodder.

Bark - Bark is used in tanning.

Miscellaneous Information

 The plant exhibits vivipary.

One of the trees first to occupy newly formed saline mud flats.

Used as wind break.

Like other mangroves, this is the feeding and nesting place for many sea birds and home for other wildlife.

Plant is over exploited for making charcoal.
