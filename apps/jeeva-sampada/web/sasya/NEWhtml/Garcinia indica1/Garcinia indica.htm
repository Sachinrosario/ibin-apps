HABIT & HABITAT                                                                           
Kokam is a slender evergreen tree with drooping branches. Kokam is one of the most important oil seed tree. Found cultivated in the WestCoast . Kokam is found to grow widely in tropical rain forests of the Western Ghats from Konkan southwards i.e., Goa, South Kamataka, Coorg, Wyannad, the lower slopes of Nilgiri hills and Kerala. It is common in the coastal districts (Ratnagiri and Sindhudurg) ofMaharashtra. It is also reported to flourish in evergreen forests of Assam, Megha- laya, West Bengal and Surat district of Gujarat.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 A slender tree of moderate height with drooping branches, branchlets black in colour, bark dark brown, smooth, young twigs cylindrical, with four elevated lines, glabrous. Leaves opposite on long petioles, blade oblong-lanceolate or oblong-oval, 2-4 inches long, obtuse or acute at the apex, tapering at the base, entire, thin, glabrous, bright green, reddish when young. Leaves are red in colour when young, Flowers are small. Male flowers are borne in terminal pedunculate cyme, in clusters of 3 to 7; pedicels are ¼ inch long and filaments are short. The bisexual flowers are solitary, stamens are 10- 18 coherent in 4 bundles alternating with petals. Seeds may be as many as 5 to 7 since there are 5 to 7 cells in the ovary. The fruit is usually globose. Colour of fruit is purple and seeds are embedded in red acid pulp.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Breeding System :
Kokam is an important but underutilized fruit crop commonly grown by seeds. Variability exists in yield and quality of fruits. There are no well defined cultivars. Kokam is dioecious, but highly variable in sex forms. Correlation studies have indicated that higher fruit rind, seed and pulp yield are the economically important characters in kokam. Khanvilkar et al. (1987) concluded that seedling types need due consideration for selection of promising genotypes for better yield. The genotypes like KK-87, KK-155 were found to be promising in respect of yield and fruit number. Considering the economic importance of this fruit, research efforts are required to identify an ideotype in kokam

Phenology :
In kokam, flowering starts from October-November and continues upto February. Fruits are ready for harvest during the months of March to June.

Seed Biology :
Seeds large ½- ¾ inch long, oblong-reniform in outline, very blunt at each end, roundede on the back, thinner on the inner edge, somewhat compressed, testa rather thin, soft, veined, brown, embryo homogenous. The embryo is remarkable in being, though of large size, almost absolutely without cotyledons; it consists merely of the thickened radicle, at the apex of which is a slight depression indicating the plumule whence a small bundle of fibres runs down the center of the axis.

Seed Germination:
Seed
The most common method of propagation of kokam is by seed. However, due to its dioecious nature, about 50 per cent seedlings turn out to be males. Only female trees produce fruits and hence seed propagation is not advocated. Other methods of propagation are Vegetative propagation, Inarch grafting, Soft wood grafting, Side grafting

Economic Potential and Uses

Uses
 Harvesting Practices
The fruit colour changes from greenish to reddish at maturity. Fully matured fruits are plucked carefully by hand. No single criterion for judging maturity correctly has been worked out. On the basis of changes in growth attributes i.e., physical indices (weight, volume, length, diameter and colour) and quality parameters (moisture, TSS, sugar, acidity, pH ascorbic acid and tannin) maturity standards for kokam fruits have been fixed (Nair, 1986).
Seedling kokam trees require 7 to 8 years to bear while grafted plants start bearing at the age of 4 to 5 years. The number of fruits increase with advance in age and size of the tree. It has been reported to yield 60 to 80 kg fruits i.e., about 10-15 kg of seeds/ tree (Anonymous, 1956). Khanvilkar el al. (1986, 1987) evaluated seedling types for fruit yield and reported that yield of kokam varied from 15.48 to 48.56 kg and 1134 to 3304 fruits by weight and number, respectively at 10th year of age. The genotypes viz., KK-87, KK-76, KK-155, KK-153, KK-149 and KK-201 were found to be highly productive in terms of yield and number of fruits. From properly cared plantation, about 30 to 50 kg fruit yield can be obtained from 15th year onwards.

Processing Techniques
The oil is extracted from the seeds by boiling. The seeds are first sun dried for some days. They are then bruised and boiled in water; when the oil collects on the surface, and on cooling concretes into a solid cake; this is subsequently purified by melting and straining, and then moulded by hand into cakes or balls. The quantity of the concrete oil obtained will be one tenth of their weight.

Other Uses :

1.       The fruit of G. indica is anthelmintic and cardiotonic and useful in piles, dysentery, tumours, pains and heart complaints. A syrup from the fruit juice is given in billious affections.

2.     From ripe kokam fruit rind, a refreshing drink called amrit kokam is prepared. It is useful as antidote against bilious infections.

3.     Dried, ripe kokam rind known asamsole is used in curry in place of tamarind.

4.     The seed yields a valuable edible fat known in commerce as kokam butter, has food and non-food applications and possesses export potential.

5.     The oil is ordinarily extracted by crushing the seed into paste and boiling in water to give a solid fat, which is greasy to feel, and whitish yellow in colour.

6.     Kokam butter is regarded as nutritive, demulcent, astringent and emolument. It is used effectively against chaffed hands and cracked heels during severe winter.

7.      The cake left after extraction of oil can be used as manure. The oil is widely used in cosmetic industries, in chocolate and confectionery preparations, in pharmaceuticals and medicine, manufacture of soap, candles and ointments, for sizing of cotton yam, suppositories and cocoa butter substitute.

Miscellaneous Information

Storage and postharvest technology:
Low temperature was better for storage of mature green and ripe fruits than at ambient temperature. Different products like dried ripe kokam rind, kokam syrup, extraction of fat from seed, dried matured green kokam and amrit kokam could be successfully prepared.
Work on processing of kokam fruits for juice extraction revealed that heating the kokam crushed at 60°C yielded maximum juice recover)' (71.6%), TSS (14.8%) and total anthocyanin (73 mg/100 ml). Further studies on utilization of kokam pomace indicated that 50 per cent water treatment to kokam pomace gave greater amount of water extract yield (49.6%) and TSS (7.8%). This water extract could be supple- mented to original kokam juice in order to increase its final juice yield.

Soil and Climate:
Lateritic soils with high organic matter as well as red sandy and light coastal sandy soils are suited for the growth of kokam plants. Kokam requires, tropical climate and as such coastal areas having warm climate with high humidity receiving annually over 250 cm of rainfall are most suited for its culti- vation. Low temperature is not favourable for its growth and fruiting.

Propagation:
Seed
The most common method of propagation of kokam is by seed. However, due to its dioecious nature, about 50 per cent seedlings turn out to be males. Only female trees produce fruits and hence seed propagation is not advocated. Seedlings have some disadvantages such as slow growth, very long pre-bearing age of 7 to 10 years and show too much variation for not being true to type. For raising seedlings, fruits are collected from early maturing, heavy yielding, elite trees having bold sized fruits. After extraction, seeds are washed thoroughly in water and dried for 3 to 4 days. Seeds are sown in small polythene bags (5x7 cm). For early germination, seeds may be soaked in water for about 2 days. Seeds germinate in about 12-15 days. After one year, the seedlings are planted at site.

Vegetative propagation
Vegetative propagation has got many advantages over seedling propagation. To ensure female trees, uniformity in yield and quality of fruits, vegetative propagation should be followed in kokam. Different grafting techniques like inarching, side grafting, softwood grafting have been tried with considerable success.

Inarch grafting
Inarching seems to be the most successful method of vegetative propagation in kokam. It is reported that inarch grafting was successful on 10 to 18 months old, vigorous seedlings when done in the month of December-January.
For grafting purpose, about 3-4 months old upright growing shoots from scion plants, should be selected. If horizontal growing scion shoots are used, the grafts have straggling habit with top heaviness on one side. About 90 per cent success is observed in this method.

Soft wood grafting
The soft wood grafting technique consists of grafting by wedge method on terminal new growth of the stock. For soft wood grafting, more than 22 weeks old seedlings and 5-6 months old scion sticks are suitable. . Graft union occurs within 2 months if the take is perfect. April-May was found to be the best period for grafting and about 80 per cent success.

Side grafting
This seems to be the best method of propagation for kokam since it is ideal for establishing in situ plantations. Beside, this technique can be successfully utilized for preparing plants anywhere since the scion sticks can be transported to any place for grafting. Also this is an ideal method for converting old and inferior seedling trees into productive ones of a desired type through top working. Experiments conducted at Konkan Krishi Vidyapeeth, Dapoli revealed that male trees can be converted into female trees by side grafting in the month of August-September with 70 per cent success.
