DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, annual herb or shrubs, 1-2 m high, glabrous; stem and branchlets purplish with small prickles. Leaves polymorphic, 3-5 palmalifid to palmaliparlite, 4-11 x 0.5-2 cm, cuneate at base, lobation up to 3/4 of lamina, middle lobe longer; lobes lanceolate, ovate or oblong, acute at apex, serrate, glabrous, midrib reddish-purple or green with a nectary beneath; lower leaves unlobed; petioles 2-8 cm long, green or reddish-purple; stipules linear, 1.0-1.8 cm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers axillary, solitary, nearly sessile or in racemes; pedicels purple, jointed near base, 4-7 mm long. Epicalyx segments 8-12, adnate to the base of the calyx, linear or lanceolate to oblong eliptic, green or purple, shorter than the calyx, persistent, usually finally appressed. Calyx fleshy, red, muricate or setose, campanulate 1.5-4.0 cm long; lobes 3-nerved, usually smooth or with few bristles, green or purple, persistant. Corolla yellow with a purple centre; petals 4-5 cm long. Staminal column shorter than petals. Capsules ovoid, 1.2-2.0 cm long, densely strigose. Seeds reniform, large, black-brown, closely covered with stout stellate hairs.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Part used:Stem yields the “Rozella hemp”. Of commerce, which is used for sacking cordage, The fibre is used as a substitute for jute. The fleshy calyses are edible as vegetables and also used for the preparation of jelly and soft drinks.

NBPGR: Seed-aphrodisiac; leaf, seed and calyx-diuretic, antiscorbutic; calyx-digestive, choleretic, hypetensive, laxative, antibilious, in heart and nerve diseases, calcified arteries; mucilage-emollient; fruit and calyx-internally antiseptic; leaf and calyx-sedative.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:  Flower gave a glucoside of gossypetin named gossytrin, hibiscitrin, gossypitrin and sabbaritrin.  Flowers also gave flavanols, gossypetin – 8 – glucoside and gossypetin-3- glucoside; antocyanins-cyanidin-3, 5-digluconide and cyanidin-3- (2-glucosyl) rutinoside.

NBRI: Extract of flowers contained reducing sugars, glycosides, acids, an alkaloid and resin. It acted as antispasmodic in intestine and uterus muscle preparations. Decoction may be used as hypotensive agent since it lowered blood pressure without producing side-effects. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

New glycosides – gossytrin (fig. 1), mp. 182°C – isolated from flower petals and structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Gossypetin-7-glucoside, 8-glucoside, cyaniding-3,5-diglucoside and cyanidin-3(2’-glucosyl-rutinoside) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Delphinidin-3-sambubioside (cannabinin), delphinidin-3-glucoside (myrtillin), cyanidin-3-sambubioside (fig. 2) and cyaniding-3-glucoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Cholesterol, campesterol, stigmasterol, b-sitosterol, a-spinasterol and ergosterol isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Detection of myristic, palmitic,palmitoleic, stearic, oleic, linoleic,sterculic, malvalic and cis-12,13-epoxy-cis-9-octadecenoic acids in seed oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Alcoholic extract of flowers inhibited in vitro angiotensin I-convertingenzyme and to lesser degree elastase, trypsin and a-chymotrypsin; in vivo angioprotective effects in rats were apparently due to presence of flavones and anthocyanins. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

A pigment isolated from calyxes and its structure established as delphinidin-3-O-b-D-xylapyranosyl(1®2)-b-D-glucopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Determination of linoleic (43.2), oleic (24.7), palmitic (17.2), sterculic (2.1), linolenic (1.2%) and myristic, palmitoleic, epoxyoleic, stearic, malvalic, dihydrosterculic and eicosenoic acids (<1.0% each) in seed oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Sepals contained delphinidin-3-O-sambubioside, cyaniding-3-O-glucopyranoside, quercetin, myricetin, hibiscetin, hibiscitrin and O-coumaric and p-coumaric acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.


Pharmacology:
Hibiscus sabdariffa extract had no effect on paw edema but had an inhibitory effect on yeast induced pyrexia and a significant effect on the hot plate reaction time. Among the phytoconstituents found in plant, flavanoids, polysaccharides and organic acids may be mainly responsible for their pharmacological activities.

Chloroform and Ethyl acetate of the flowers of H. sabdariffa  protect rat hepatocytes from t-BHP-induced cytotoxicity and genotoxicity by different mechanisms.( Tseng et al,  1997)

Hibiscus sabdariffa  tea lowers high blood pressure.( Haji and Haji, 1999)

Roselle calyx extract decrease in serum creatinine, cholesterol, and glucose in the treated rats compared with the control as well as a significant increase in serum uric acid was observed. The serum proteins (albumin and total protein) in the treated rats when compared with the control groups was not changed significantly. (Onyenekwe et al, 1999)

A roselle (Hibiscus sabdariffa Linn.) tea extract was found to have high inhibitory activity against porcine pancreatic alpha-amylase. Hibiscus acid and its 6-methyl ester were respectively isolated as active principles from the 50% methanol and acetone extracts of roselle tea. (Hansawasdi et al, 2000).

The methanol extracts of Hibiscus sabdariffa (p < 0.01) showed a significant dose dependent relaxant effect (IC50) = 350 microM) on rat ileal strip comparable to the effect shown by nifedipin and papaverine as reference compounds. Similarly, the extract when administered intraperitoneally significantly (p < 0.05-0.01) reduced the intestinal transit (13%-35%) in rats (IC50, = 250 microM). The extracts (40% +/- 04%) and nifedipin (51% +/- 05%) also potentiated the diarrhoea inducing effect of castor oil (IC50 = 350 microM). It is postulated that these effects are possibly generated by constituents such as quercetin and eugenol via a Ca2+ channel modulated mode of action. (Salah et al, 2002).
