Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Olea is a genus of evergreen trees and shrubs distributed in the warm temperate and tropical regions of world. About 6 species ocuur in India. O.europea, common oilve has been introduced and grown in some parts of India.

A small evergreen tree, native of western Asia, 8-10 m. high, is widely cultivated for its fruits, which yield the Olive Oil of commerce. The tree is relatively slow growing but longlived, some specimens reportedly being over 1000 years old. The tree is extensively grown in countries bordering the Mediterranean and has also been introduced into many other parts of the world. For proper growth the plant needs deep fertile soil and a temperature averaging to 130 not dropping to below -100. Locations satisfying these requirements are found in Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Nilgiris. An annual rainfall of 100-120 cm has been found sufficient for olive provided it is well distributed throughout the year. Grown successfully on many types of soils, but prefers light, deep soils and does not do well on poorly drained, heavy and impermeable soils. Thrives best on calcareous sandy soils (2).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A small to moderate sized evergreen tree with small coriaceous leaves, grey above and white or silvery beneath. The dark green leaves are 1-3 inches long. They are ablong or lanceolate, entire and oppositely arranged on the stem. Bark grey, thin, smooth, exfoliating in narrow strips. The wood is resistant to decay and if top dies, a new trunk will often grow from the roots. The fruit, which ripens from Sept-Dec, ia a black ovoid drupe about 0.3-0.5 inches long with a bony endocarp (3).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
The small flowers are in trichotomous panicles, whitish containing both the sexes and are essentialy autogamous in nature. Most of the flowers are imperfect and they cannot bear fruits. They give off pollen which is usally carried from flower to flower by the wind. In olives, flowers are borne on paniculate inflorescences arising in the leaf axils of oppositely arranaed leaves, borne on the previous season's shoots. However occasionally they may arise from dormant buds of one to two year old shoots. In areas where olive trees fail to put forth their growth flush autumn, inflorescence may arise from axillary buds borne by one year old shoots. A typical olive flower consists of four-lobed gamosepalous calyx, four corolla, yellowish white in colour, alternating with sepals, stamens usually- two, single carpel, ovary superior, two-celled, with one syncarpous style and syncarpousovary with one or two ovules in each locule. In olives, the size of panicle and number of flowers borne on a panicle are influenced by the genetic constitution of the tree and environmental conditions prevailing during the period of bud development/ontogeny of different floral parts. Principally olives bear two types of flower Viz.,

--Complete or bisexual flowers, containing functional stamen and pistil.

--Incomplete or unisexual flowers containing functional stamens but aborted pistil.

However, in arid areas with frequent periods of moisture stress during late winters or spring, intermediary flowers containing rudimentary but non-functional pistils may develop (12).

Breeding System :
It is both self and cross pollinated. In olives peak time of anthesis has been found between 0.00 hrs to 0400 hrs. practically, 11.25 to 29.11 per cent of anthesis was observed between 0600 to 0800 hrs. Occurrence of lower temperature coupled with a moderate relative humidity conducive for anthersis of olive flowers. The peak period of dehiscence of anthers was observed between 0200 hrs to hrs 0060. The pollen grains released during early morning hours can be conveniently used for pollination immediately after their dehiscence from pollen sacs. The increased time lag between these two processes will tend to lower down the extent of pollen viability. Peak period of stigma receptivity varied between one day before to one day after anthesis. Thus pollination should be carried out within specified period in order to obtain satisfactory fruit set in controlled cross pollination (12).

Phenology :
The leaves are shed in Jan-Feb and the leaves appear before the old ones fall (4). The time of onset of flowering in olives is influenced by environmental conditions prevailing between time of panicle emergence until beginning of flowering. Flowering occurs during the month of March-April whereas in cooler climates it is in the month of May. A shift in the time of flowering has also been noticed at the same location from year to year. The duration of flowering varied between 6 to 13 days in mid hills of Himachal Pradesh (12). It was also found that prevalence of cool weather due to a light shower prior to flowering tend to delay the time of flowering whereas occurrence of such conditions during blooming resulted in extended duration of flowering. Thus, time and duration of flowering depends upon the cultivar and season.

Pollinators:
Although olive flowers are hermaphrodite in nature but most of the cultivars are self in compatible, only a few cultivars are self pollinating, but the production can be increased by cross pollination. The olive flowers are generally pollinated by wind. Honey bees though visit the olive flowers to collect the pollen but are not essential for pollination.

Seed Biology :
Fruit is one seeded drupe, globular, oblong or even crescent shaped, usally considered a drupe and is composed of a hard endocarp and fleshy exocarp. The fruit passes through straw colour and pink to the red that preceeds black on ripening (5).

Seedling Germination :
Seeeds germinate 15-20 days after sowing provided environmental conditions are favourable. The growth of the seedling is very slow, on an average it grows only 3 inches per annum. Germination process continues for a period exceeding one year and complete germination takes even two years. Slower rate of germination is due to hard seed coat. A long tap root is developed at an early stage. The seedlings are sensitive to drought and requires good conditions for its growth and establishment. Exposure of olive seeds for one month at a temperature of 100 C followed by a period two months at 200 C was found to be optimum for improving germination (4, 12).

Cultivation:

Field should be ploughed well and leveled properly and pits measuring 2 x 2’ should be dug before plantation. Pit should be filled with well rotten F.Y.M mixed with surface soil and silt in the ratio of 1:1:1. Generally olive is planted at 5 x 6 or 6 x 6 m from plant to plant and row to row distance according to the variety and fertility of soil. After planting the tree, irrigate them immediately.

There are two methods of propagation namely Sexual and Asexual propagation.

i) Sexual: Olive propagated by seed gives the great deformity of progeny from parent tree. This technique is used only for genetic improvement of the species and for the production of seedling to be used as rootstock.

ii) Asexual: This method include grafting, cutting and root suckers and ovules for propagation.

a. Grafting: This method is successfully and economically applied for olive propagation particularly for those cultivars with self rooting problem. One year old seedling with stem diameter of 6-8 cm are suitable for grafting. High percentage of grafting success can be obtained when plants are grafted during February through pen grafting technique.

b. Cutting: Olive can be successfully propagated from cuttings under mist. Higher rooting success can be obtained from the cuttings of 10-15 cm length of last year growth treated with 3000 ppm (temperature ranging from 20-28oC with relative humidity 90%) (6).

c. Suckers: This method of propagation is followed in old trees, which become uneconomical and are to be cut. The plant is cut down at the ground level. The suckers arise and root under the mound of soil before being separated and planted in the field. The plants produced by this method remain juvenile for a longer period, coming into bearing late.

d. Ovuli (knotts): Sometimes rounded, smooth-barked growths at the base of trunks of olive known as ovuli are cut off in winter, stratified in sand until spring, then planted deep in rich nursery beds, where shoots arise from them and are trained to one stem for planting in the orchard. The ovuli is the protuberance formed at the collar region of olive plant where a build up of sap or slowing down of circulation occurs, this causes hyper nutrition of cambium cells, which proliferate actively and determine the extorflexion of the parenchymatus tissue, of which the ovuli is almost exclusively constituted.


Fertilizer: Manuring and fertilization encourage the vegetative growth and fruiting of the tree. Application of NPK depends upon the soil condition, fertility of soil and age of the tree. The olive tree needs more nitrogenous fertilizer than phosphorous and potash. Potash and phosphoric fertilizer should be incorporated in the soil before planting of trees at the rate of 200 kg and 300 kg per hectare respectively. It should be mixed thoroughly with soil with deep ploughing. Application of these fertilizers should be repeated after every 3-4 years. The best time of nitrogen fertilizer is before the flowering after fruit setting and at the stone hardening. The schedule of nitrogen fertilizer is given below:

Pruning and training: The main task of pruning is to modify the vegetative growth and regulate its production efficiency. It should also aim at lowering the bearing head, removal of non-bearing wood and proper spacing of shoots. This is attained by thinning out and light heading back of shoot. Olive bears fruit on one year old branch and pruning is generally practiced after every two years (13).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

The common olives pests are olive fruit fly (Dacus oleae) , olive moth, twig cutters, Bark beetle, Woolly aphid and scale etc. olive moth, twig, cutter, thrips and scales can be controlled with malathion at 2 ml/l. Activities of olive fly starts during August and cause severe damage to the fruit. Rogor at the rate of 1-2 ml/l is considered effective to save the fruit from damage of olive fly. Wooly aphid suck the sap of tree and excrete the white sticky secretion which results in stop of the plant growth. Dimecron/Karate at 1-2 ml/1 lit of water can be used effectively against this insect.

Olive scale (Parlatoria oleae) is one of the more common and serious pests in California. Oleander or ivy scale (Aspidiotus hederae), greedy scale (Aspidiotus camelliDe) and California red scale (Aonidiella aurantii) also attack the olive tree. Black scale (Saissetia oleae) is being controlled by a parasite in a few districts (5).

Diseases:

The common diseases in olive plant are trunk decay, sooty mould and peacock spot. The bacterial knot appears in the form of numerous irregular tumors and galls resembling canker. Olive knot (Bacterium savastonei) enters through wounds and produces warty growths along the branch. Care should be taken to prevent the disease being spread during pruning operations. Two fungus diseases which may be serious are peacock spot (Cycloconium oleaginum) and Verticillium rot (Verticillium alboatrum). The peacock spot is characterized by zoned ring spots on the leaves which start in January in the lower part of the tree and may result in complete defoliation. Olive trees are sometimes weakened by lesion nematodes particularly citrus nematode (Tylenchus semipenetrans) and root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne sp.) (6, 13).

Economic Potential and Uses

Olive oil is obtained from ripe fruit by expression, the yield ranges from 15 to 40%. If the fruit is not fully mature, the yield of oil is poor and its taste is bitter. Olive oil is a non- drying oil varying in colour from pale yellow through greenish yellow to greenish brown. Olive is good source of edible oil, is used chiefly as a salad oil and is also used for table purpose for pickles. Before using for table purposes, the fruits are given lye treatment containing 1.5% to sodium hydroxide dependmg on the cultlvar and stage of matunty, which helps in removal of a bitter substance known as 'oleuropein'. After removing the bitterness of glycosides, good pickle is prepared from the fruits (9).

The olive has not only nutritional and medicinal value but also is free from cholesterol having polyunsaturated fatty acids. It is considered useful in regulating high blood pressure and thus is safe for the patients suffering from heart or other ailments. Vegetables can be fried in the oil without any risk of fat accumulation in the arteries. Olive oil is good human skin and massaging with olive oil is useful in muscular pains and provides warmth during cold weather (13). It is also used in food preservation, textile industry and cosmetic preparation, manufacture of soaps, lubricants and sulphonated oils, the oil is demulcent, emollient and laxative. along with variety of other purposes. The press cake after oil extraction is used as fertilizer. Stones are of interest to a few special industries, moulded products, plastics and furfurol manufactures (6&7).

Uses

Both green and ripe fruits are edible. Fresh fruit may be pickled, stuffed or made into various edible preparations. Olive oil is the best of lubricants, and the carbolized oil is used for chafing and upon hands and instruments in surgery, and in vaginal examinations, and in introducing bougies or catheters. If a stream of warm oil be forced into the urethra in spasmodic stricture just in advance of the catheter, the dilation may be made satisfactorily, and the catheter may be introduced when that act was previously impossible. Olive oil is used to protect the mucous surfaces of the esophagus and stomach when poisoning has occurred from the caustic alkalies. It also forms a neutral innocuous soap with the alkali and can subsequently be removed. The leaves of the olive tree are used medicinally for their hypotensive qualities. The press cake has a limited shelf life. It can be used for animal feeding. The stones are of in moulded products, plastics and furfural manufacture (8).

Harvesting Practices
The fruit progressively increases in oil content until it turns black, an indication of ripeness. At that point the weight of oil per fruit remains stable (5).

Methods of harvesting

(1) Where the trees are closely planted and the olives are at too great a height to be reached with ease, they are left to fall. Only poor quality oil can be extracted.

(2) Beating the tree: common in many regions but injurious to both tree and fruit.

(3) Hand picking is recommended for oil olives, the fruit being dropped to the ground onto cloths.

(4) Harvesting table olives should be done by hand, the picker carrying a receptacle for the fruit.

Mechanical Harvesting: In extensive olive groves, fruits cannot be harvested efficiently by manual methods. Therefore, different types of mechanical shakers have been developed.

(i) Small shakers: These shakers with finger-like arms covered with rubber sheet are used for milking type of harvesting. They are run either by batteries or by electrical motors of different powers depending on their prototypes. Small shakers are useful for harvesting olives from young trees having thin branches and they can be moved from one place to other even on a slopy land where heavier machines are difficult to operate.

(ii) Mechanical shakers: A movable mechanical arm having a mechanism for adjustment is mounted on the tractors. This arm is used to clamp the limbs firmly and then shaking is done by mechanical means resulting in the detachment of ripe fruits from the tree. Mechanical harvesting by shakers is most useful for extensive olive growers having old trees which otherwise are difficult to harvest.

Harvesting by Abscission Inducing chemicals: The mechanical harvesters are generally used after loosening the fruit by chemical sprays, i.e., by ethrel or ethylene producing chemicals. Addition of glycerin in the ethephon solution, under arid conditions, reduces the rate of evaporation, thus reducing the condition of inadequate uptake of ethephon. Spraying of ethephon or alsol each at 1000, 1600 or 2000 ppm was found to reduce the fruit removal force (13).

Processing Techniques

Pre-treatment

(1) Fruit is separated from leaves and other trash and then washed.

(2) Olives are crushed in edge runner or other type mills.

Extraction

(1) Olives are pressed in open hydraulic presses or occasionally pot presses or screw presses.

(2) European practice -relatively low pressure open presses are used and pressing is conducted in two or three stages with segregation of the oil from each stage.

(3) First pressing yields virgin olive oil, seldom exported in its natural state but forms the basis of edible export types of the finest qualities. Separation of on from aqueous material that is expressed, is carried out by prolonged settling.

(4) The residual pulp is subjected to a second pressing with hot water, yielding an oil of higher free fatty acid content.

(5) This oil is subjected to a refining process of decolorization, deacidification and deodorization and is commercially known as refined olive oil. Largely used to blend with Virgin oils for edible export types.

(6) Pomace may be pressed a third time.

(7) The residue from pressing, which contains 8.10% oil, is commonly solvent extracted with a hydrocarbon solvent to produce an oil that is saleable as an edible oil after refining.

(8) If carbon disulphide is the solvent used, the oil produced is known commercially as sulphur olive oil or olive oil foots, and is non edible (5, 11).

Other Uses
Olive oil or sweet oil is a nutritive and laxative for children. It may be given whenever irritating substances are retained in the intestinal tract and when convulsions are present from gastro-intestinal irritation. It is now generally used internally and externally in the treatment of appendicitis, and it is a most efficacious remedy. It is an excellent agent in dysentery, whether of infants or adults. Olive oil is exceedingly valuable in the treatment of sprained, bruised or contused parts, applied warm on absorbent cotton and kept hot. It acts as nutrition to the part, diffuses the heat and is markedly soothing in its influence (8).

Through proper planning and Scientific management of olive plantation, olives can be revenue generating source.

Miscellaneous Information

Olive oil enjoys a privileged position amongst edible vegetable oils. There are many reasons for this status:

-Historically, it is the oldest oil known to man, it was used in antiquity and is therefore several thousand years old.

-Its production zone is identical to the territory once covered by the Greco-Roman civilization and it thus continues to thrive in our regions, it is this civilization which promoted olive groves and production and consumption of olive oil throughout the Mediterranean basin, which has remained the olive tree's natural area.

-Olive oil is the juice of a fruit and as such it is consumed nearly entirely in its virgin state. Today this fact makes it a distinctive product.

-Production of olive oil requires special equipment unsuitable for crushing of any other oil seed.

-Olive oil is a relatively exclusive and expensive oil (compared to other dietary oils and fats) and therefore undeniably plays an important social role in its production zones.

For all these reasons, the olive and its oil have become the object of special attention on the part of various national, European and international organizations (10).
