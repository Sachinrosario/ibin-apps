Herb, 0.3-1.2 m tall, it found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Meghalaya, Tripura, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Tropical Himalaya.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herb, 0.3-1.2 m tall; stem fleshy, glabrous or with upper parts glandular hairy. Leaves sessile, oblong, spathulate, 7.5-12.5 x 3-8.5 cm, obtuse at apex, crenate or cuneate at base, glabrous. Flowers yellow in dense flowered cymose-panicle, c 2 cm long. Calyx 6-10 mm, glabrous. Corolla 1.3 cm long, lobes broadly lanceolate. Fruits follicle, 1 cm long. glabrous.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Leaves are used in cholera.


Chemistry:
A new glycoside – patuletin-3,7-di-O-rhamnoside – isolated from leaves and flowers; patuletin, quercetin, quercetin-3-O-glucoside-7-O-rhamnoside, kaempferol and its 3-O-rhamnoside also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).


Pharmacology:
Leaf extract found toxic to rats, rabbits, dogs and sheep; LD50 inmice and rat 230 and 560mg/kg, i.p., respectively. It was nontoxic orally in doses upto 2 g/kg in mice and rat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Leaf extract decreased spontaneous motor activity; a dose of 20.0 mg/kg increased pentobarbitone sleeping time (83.0%); conditioned avoidance response in rats was impaired at 100.0 mg/kg. A dose of 25.0 mg/kg produced marked hypotensive effect in anaesthetized dogs, which was not blocked by either atropine or promethazine, and negative inotropic effect on intact dog heart which was responsible for hypotensive action. It caused dose-dependent inhibition of contractions induced by acetylcholine, histamine and barium on guinea pig ileum but was devoid of any effect on frog rectus abdominis muscle. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).


Ethnobotany:


Medicine for boils (Leaf, Jain, 1984); Burns, cholera, wounds (Leaf, Sharma et al, 1979).
