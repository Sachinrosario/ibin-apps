 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
Roots consisting of a fascicles of several small ovate-oblong bulbs common and cultivated throughout India.


Availability :  
Specimen examined:
Uttar Pradesh: Bunda, 10.05.1901. A. S. Bell, 457 (CAL); Assam: Ganhali, 10.03.1902, A.C. Chatterjee, 482451 (Acc. No.) (CAL).  


Conservation/ Threat Status : Plant grows wild as well as commercially cultivated.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Roots consisting of a fascicles of several small ovate-oblong bulbs. Leaves basal only fistular, shorter than the scapes. Scapes 30-60 cm long, tapering from the swollen base. 

Inflorescence:  Dense heads; Flower: bulbils enclosed by thin membranous spathe, pedicles 4-5 times longer than that of flower, spathe composed of 2-3 reflexed valves; Perianth: 6 in number in whorls of three each, imbricate, ; Androecium: Stamens 4 arranged in two alternate whorls of three members each, always opposite to the tepals, , connate with each other and the perianth at its base, anthers dithecous, basifixed, introrse and opening by longitudinal slits; Gynoecium: tricarpellary, syncarpous, trilocular superior ovary with numerous ovules in two rows in each ovule,style with distinct stigma, a nectariferous disc is present below the ovary; Fruit: Capsule. 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Umbels globular, dense with flowers only. It flowers and fruits between March July.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and bulbs.

Soil type: It grows well in wide variety of soils but well drained clayey loam are best suited for its cultivation.

Nutrient requirements: Finely powdered fish manure, sulphates of ammonia and potash and nitrate of soda and super phosphate are required.

Water management: Periodic irrigation ater 5-10 days are  highly recommended for the crop.                                       

Harvesting: Plant is harvested when the fot the bulbs when the leaves turn yellow and the bulbs are exposed above the ground.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 It is used to cure earache, a small piece being placed in the meatus. It is also fried in butter and preserved in honey as an aphrodisiac.
Chemistry:
·        Quercitol, its 3,4’-diglucoside and 7,4’-diglucoside (fig. 1) isolated from bulbs and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Thiosulphinates – MeS(O)Sme, MeS(O)SPr, PrS(O)SPr, cis- and trans-propylsulphinothioic acid-S-propyl esters together with cis- and trans-2,3-dimethyl-5,6-dithiabicyclo[2.1.1]-hexane-5-oxides identified by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Phytochemical analysis of the polar extracts from bulbs of shallot, Allium ascalonicum Hort., led to the isolation of two new furostanol saponins, named ascalonicoside A1/A2 and ascalonicoside B , respectively. On the basis of 2D NMR and mass spectrometry data, the structures of the novel compounds were elucidated as furost-5(6)-en-3a,22a-diol 1b-O-b-D-galactopyranosyl 26-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à2)-O-b-D-glucopyranoside), its epimer at position 22 , and furost-5; 20(22)-dien-3b-ol 1a-O-b-D-galactopyranosyl 26-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à2)-O-b-D-glucopyranoside) This is the first report of furostanol saponins in A. ascalonicum. High concentrations of quercetin, isorhamnetin, and their glycosides were also isolated and described. (Fattorusso et al., 2002)

Pharmacology:

·        The peptide designated ascalin manifested a unique specific anti-fungal activity. It inhibited mycelial growth in the fungus Botrytis cinerea but not in the fungi Mycosphaerella arachidicola and Fusarium oxysporum. Ascalin inhibited HIV-1 reverse transcriptase with an IC50 of 10 muM, much more potently than Allium tuberosum anti-fungal protein and other anti-fungal proteins. (Wang and NG, 2002)
