Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
 It is a small to moderate-sized, deciduous, glabrous tree with thorny branches, growing to a height of 10 m and 0.6 to 1.6 m in girth. It is a thorny tree commonly found in dry deciduous forests and cultivated in many parts of India for its fruit.Wood apple is reported to be native of South India and generally cultivated in both peninsulas. It is one of the very hardy trees found grown all over the plains of northern, central, eastern and southern regions of India , especially in the semi-arid and arid-regions. More common in Deccan , Maharashtra , Karnataka, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh, also occurs in the western Himalaya upto an elevation of about 500 m. Ceylon , India ,Java,

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 The trunk is short, cylindrical with a symmetrical crown of foliage. The bark is dark grey coloured, rough, thick and longitudinally furrowed. Spines are axillary and stout, 2.5 to 5 cm long. Leaves are odd pinnate, 15-25 cm long, petiole and rachis flat, often narrowly winged. Leaflets 3 to 9, opposite, sessile or short petiolate, 2.5 to 3.8 x 1.3 to 2.5 cm, obovate with crenate tip and cuneate base, glabrous, coriaceous, stiff and entire.Flowers are small, numerous, pale greenish in colour with red tinge and appear in terminal or axillary panicles, male and female flowers often in the same panicle.The fruit is 5.0 to 8.0 cm in diameter, usually globose with a woody brownish pericarp, filled with a dark brown sub-acid pulp when ripe.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
 The flowers are mainly staminate and hermaphrodite. Ovary, style and stigma are present in both hermaphrodite and male flowers, but rudimentary in the latter. Both perfect and staminate flowers have 10 to 12 stamens of equal size. The anthers are basifixed dehiscing through a slit between the two pollen sacs of each lobe. The fresh pollen grains are dark yellow (Karale, unpublished). Size of pollen grains ranged from 28.4 to 36.1 microns. Sucrose (20%) was found better medium for pollen germination, addition of 0.5 to 1% agar or 15 ppm GA to sucrose slightly improved germination (Singh et al., 1989).

Breeding System :Wood apple is a highly cross pollinated crop.There is no systematic work on the breeding and cultivar improvement in wood apple.

Phenology :
In wood apple, numerous small flowers are borne on terminal or axillary panicles, mainly on new shoots. Emergence of panicles commences in the middle of February and continues upto 3rd week of May. Opening of flowers starts in the 2nd week of March. There are other reports stating flowering occurs during February-October (01) and also during
March-April (02).

Pollinators:
Wood apple is a highly cross pollinated crop. Pollination is done by insects and unpollinated flowers failed to set fruits. No evidence of bud pollination or parthenocarpy was noticed (Karale, unpublished).
Seed Biology :

Seed Germination:
a) Seed
It is usually propagated by seed. Freshly extracted seeds should be used for raising seedlings. Seeds sown fresh usually show a high percentage of germination (66%). Pre-treatment of the seed is not necessary. About 31000 plants can be raised from a kilogram of seed (Troup, 1921). Seedlings are transplanted a year later. Polyembryony to the extent of 63 per cent is observed in wood apple (Karale, unpublished). Polyem-bryonic seedlings are vigorous in growth and breed true to type.
b) Vegetative propagation
It can be vegetatively propagated through cuttings, budding and also by inarching.
c) Budding
Budding can be done in late summer or early monsoon. Budded plants are dwarf, precocious and prolific in flowering and fruiting. It would be advantageous to go for insitu budding in this crop under arid conditions.
d) Inarching
Bhore (1986, personal communication) attempted propagation of wood apple by inarch grafting and obtained more than 70 per cent success.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:
Larvae of insects are found in the pulp of the fruits. Insects also damages woodand defoliate.

Diseases:
Since this plant is not under regular orcharding, there is no record of any serious problem but the pests and diseases affecting citrus/bael may cause damage to this crop also.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruit, Leaves Bark, Timber

Uses:

·        The ripe fruits contain sour-sweet, aromatic pulp which is about 70 per cent of total weight. Wood apple pulp contain significant amount of proteins. The fruit is a rich source of riboflavin, acidity is about 2.3 per cent and sugars 7.2 per cent.

·        The leaves are eaten by sheep and goat. Acidic pulp is eaten by children.

·        The pulp is sweetened with gur or sugar and eaten fresh.

Harvesting Practices
Flowering in wood apple takes place from February to May and ripe fruits are avai- lable from October to March. In fact, the period of flowering and fruiting is governed by climate and the period of moisture availability. The stage of harvest is determined by the purpose of using the fruit. For chutney, immature but fully developed fruits are preferred, however, for squash and jelly, fully matured fruits are desirable.
Proper care is required for harvesting of fruits. A minor crack on the rind can cause spoilage during storage. At maturity the grip of the fruit stalk is loosened and the fruit is detached from the tree without any efforts which otherwise is difficult in unripe fruit. The stem end of the fruit is vulnerable to fungal infection.
Yield
A vegetatively propagated tree starts bearing at 3 years. However, it takes about 10 years for a tree to provide optimum production. Yield varies considerably but a grown up tree can produce about 200 to 250 fruits per annum. Fruits can be graded on the basis of size and marketed.

Produce and Products
Wood apple syrup, chutney, pectin, snuffboxes, gum, agricultural implements, naves of wheels, oil crushers, house building, ornamental carving, shoe lasts, penholders, rulers and similar domestic articles.

Processing Techniques
Wood apple syrup-Fruits are de-shelled by hand and the pulp with seed are mixed with required quantity of water and boiled. Two extractions from the pulp are made. Brix is raised to 13° by addition of sugar from its original brix. The prepared juice after boiling for 30 minutes is filled into cans while it is still hot. The cans are sealed and stored at room temperature for more than 1 Vz years (Swamy et al., 1977).

Other Uses

1.The timber of this tree is largely used for preparing agricultural implements, naves of wheels, oil crushers, house building, ornamental carving, shoe lasts, penholders, rulers and similar domestic articles. T

2.The transparent gum that exudes from the trunk is used as a substitute for gum arable for making artists water colours, dyes and varnishes.

3.The hardy dry shells of the fruits are made into snuffboxes.

4.The leaves have an aroma of aniseed and yield 0.73 per cent essential oil.

5.The pulp of fruit is applied externally as remedy for bites of venomous insects and retiles. The fruits are considered as tonic, refreshing, astringent and anticarbutic, used in the treatment of diarrhoea and dysentry. The importance of wood apple fruit lies in its curative properties, which make the tree one of the useful medicinal plants of India .

6.Its medicinal properties have been dealt within Chark samhita and Sushruta samhita, two early medical treatise in Sanskrit. It has a great demand from native system of medicine such as Ayurvedic. Fruit is used as stomachic and stimulant while leaves are carminative. Bark is prescribed for biliousness. Pulp is applied externally as a remedy for bites of venomous insects and reptiles.

7.Though its nutritional economic importance has not been widely recognized, this fruit can be exploited for regular cultivation in dry areas, provided supported by processing industry as it makes excellent chutney, can be used as adjunct in jelly and made into squash. Being rich in pectin, it makes good jelly. Pulp contains 2.66% pectin on fruit weight basis..

Miscellaneous Information

Storage and post-harvest technology:
In general, wood apple fruits have a good postharvest storage life because of its hard outer shell and it can withstand transport and marketing hazards.
A good quality stiff jelly of attractive colour can be prepared from the pulp. Similarly, wood apple toffee can be prepared by boiling the pulp with the sugar. It is also possible to prepare syrup and chutney from wood apple.

Species:
The genus Feronia belongs to the family Rutaceae. It is a monotypic genus of the Indo-Malayan region represented by the only species Feronia limonia (syn. F. elephantum) and has 2n=18 chromosomes.

Cultivation
Planting
Regular planting does not exist. However, if done, it should be planted at a distance of 8 to 10 m. The pits of 45 cm3 are dug and filled with top soil mixed with 10 to 15 kg farm yard manure. Usually, work of digging of pits is completed before the onset of monsoon. The best time for wood apple planting is July to August. The transplants are taken out along with whole root system in a ball of soil and are planted in the centre of the pit.

Training and pruning
Trees are allowed to grow freely on central leader with well spaced branches in all directions.

Irrigation
Wood apple is a crop of dry region and once the plants are established, they hardly need any irrigation. Nevertheless, conservation of run off rain water in root zone willenhance the productivity of this crop.

Manuring and fertilization
Manuring is not practiced but it will be benefitted, if manured at the rate of about 25 kg FYM of compost per tree in the beginning of monsoon. It will help in increasing fruit size and quality.

Cultivars:
There are no named or standard cultivars of this fruit. Some sour and sweet types have been observed. Shete etal. (1999) reported the physico-chemical data on fifteen local strains of wood apple from Rahuri ( Maharashtra ). The weight of the fruit, pulp content and rind thickness varied from 173 to 540 g, 58.0 to 75.93 per cent and 0.2 to 0.5 cm, respectively and the total soluble solids, acidity and total sugars ranged from 14 to 18.5 per cent, 1.04 to 4.5 per cent and 4.08 to 8.47 per cent, respectively among the genotypes described. It appears that the organoleptic quality of wood apple pulp depends upon the brix :acid ratio. Higher values make the fruit pulp more palatable. The evaluation suggested that the superior strains/genotypes No. 5, No. 9, No. 10 and No. 15 hold promise for future cultivation. The Marthawada Agricultural University (MAU), Parbhani, Maharashtra has released a type called HB-10 having large-sizedfruits. Average weight of fruit is 350 g with weight of pulp 224 g.

Soil:
It can grow in a wide variety of soils including degraded soils of arid region and can tolerate salinity to certain extent. The tree shows a marked preference for black cotton soils for its optimum growth and fruiting. It can be grown successfully in marginal lands.

Climate:
It is mostly grown in dry regions of subtropical and tropical regions of the country. It does not grow well above 1500 m. The mature plants can tolerate low temperature (0-15°C) as well as a temperature as high as 47.5°C (Troup, 1921). The warm season appears conducive for the initiation of floral buds. The tree sheds its leaves and the branches are bare for a short period in the cold season during January and the flower- ing starts in February-March. It possesses great tolerance to drought.
