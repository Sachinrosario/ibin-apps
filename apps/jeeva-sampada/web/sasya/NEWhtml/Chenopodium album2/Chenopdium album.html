HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual erect or ascending herb, c 20-75 cm high, milly green or reddish, simple or much branched; It is found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Tripura, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem stripped; young parts clothed with white powder like vesicles. Leaves variable, lower leaves long petiolate, ovate-rhomboid, dentate, serrated or lobulate at margin; upper ones short, petiolate, entire, acute or obtuse at apex. Flowers c 0.2 mm across, sessile, 5-merous, arranged in panicular clusters or often in a leafy terminal paniculate spike. Tepals connate, concave, with a strong midrib. Fruits globose, 1.5 –2.0 mm across. Seeds 1.2-1.5 mm in diam., compressed, keeled, smooth, shining, and blackish-brown.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence:  In clustes forming complex or lax paniculate mealy spikes; Flower: Minute, hermaphrodite, complex ; Perianth: Sepals 5, oblong-lanceolate, keeled, cloding over the thin membranous utricle; Androecium:  Stamens 5, hypogynous, anthers 2-celled, filaments free, sometimes intervening stamonides; Gynoecium: Ovary depressed, one celled, ovule solitary, style 0, stigma 2,; Fruit:  A membranous utricle; Seed: Orbicular, compressed with acute margin, smooth shining . Flowering & Fruiting : November - January.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:   In Ayurveda system of medicine plant improve apetite and used in biliousness, abdominal pain, eye diseases, throat troubles, piles, blood, heart and spleen diseases.

Plant-laxative, anthelmintic.

Chemistry:
b-Ecdysone, polysone and polypodine B isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Plant contained saponins (8.0%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of cryptomeridiol and 8a-acetoxycryptomeridiol from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

A new phenolic amide (Fig. 1) isolated from roots and its structure determined as N-trans-feruloyl-4-O-methyldopamine and confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

The content of oxalic acid and calcium in Chenopodium album was analysed with high content of oxalic acid with a range of values from 360-2000 mg/100g of fresh weight. (Guil et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 32165y.

Ascorbic acid contents of fresh leaves was 220.97 to 377.65 mg and b-carotene was 19.00 to 24.64 mg/100g, DW. The percent loss of ascorbic acid ranged from 2.03 to 8.77 and 45.15 to 66.9 while lower losses (0.0 to 1.75 and 1.63 to 2.84) of b-carotene were observed in leaves stored in the refrigerator and at 30°C, respectively. A markedly greater reduction in ascorbic acid and b-carotene were observed in leaves stored in dried, blanched and cooked leaves. The study data suggest that storage of leaves in refrigeration, drying in oven, blanching for short time and cooking in pressuer cooker results in better retention of these vitamins. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9801, 0586.

The nutritive value and chemical composition of Chenopodiumalbum were studied. The analysis showed that C. album contains sufficient amount of carbohydrate 4.33%, fiber 9.032%, protein 1.024%, fat 0.216%, mineral matter (ash) 3.138% and water 82.26%. the concentration of macro and micro element was found to be Na 29.30 mg, K 71.798 mg, P 3.14 mg, Ca 123.88 mg, Fe 0.344 mg, Zn 0.172 mg and Mn 0.86 mg per total ash sample of Chenopodium album. Chenopodium album contains vitamin C (2.17 mg), folic acid (9.135 mg), thiamine (1.029 mg) and niacin (0.88 mg). Ribose glutamic acid, lysine, alanine and asparagines were identified by thin layer chromatography from the water extract of Chenopodium album. (Dahot and Soomro, 1997). C.A. ----, 129, 272989w.

Nutritional (ascorbic acid, dehydroascorbic acid and carotenes); antinutritional and toxic components (oxalic acid, nitrate and erucic acid) were determined. Ascorbic + dehydroascorbic acids contents were very high in several species, especially in Chenopodium album, (155 mg/ 100 g). (Guil et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9803, 1770.

From aerial path of Chenopodium album, kaempferol, kaempferol 3-O-b-glucoside, kaempferol 3-O-b-diglucoside, Kaempferol-3-O-arabinoglucoside, quercetin and quercetin 3-O-xylosylglucoside were isolated and identified. (Bylka and Kowalewski, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 125813k.

Chenopodium (Chenopodium album) leaves have mineral elements (Na, K, Ca, Mg, P, Fe, Cu, Zn and Mn), fatty acids, vitamin C, carotenoids and oxlic acid contents. (Guerrero and Isasa, 1997). C.A.1998, 128, 12746x.

Total b-carotene content was analysed by HPLC. (Nambiar and Seshadari, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 123996q.

Quercetin-3-rhamnoglucoside a flavanoid was identified from Chenopodium album. (Gonzalez et al, 1998). C.A. 2000, 132, 119854r.

IR-spectra from 400-4000 cm-1 of plant ash were presented. (Janczak et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 168382u.

Vegetable and the products of Chenopodium album were prepared as per standard methods and analyzed for their ascorbic acid and b-carotene contents. The ascorbic acid content of the products from bathua was 6.25 and 12.46 mg/100g DW and their b-carotene content was 0.68 and 1.14 mg/100g DW. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1999). C.A. 2000, 133, 251477v.

The isolation and spectral data of three saponins from roots of Chenopodium album are reported. One of them is seco-glycoside analogous to compounds that were previously found in species belonging to Caryophyllales. (Lavaud et al, 2000).
Chenopodium with other plant was analysed for proximate composition, minerals and vitamin content, in vitro bio-availability of mineral, in vitro protein digestibility and two anti-nutrients, i.e. oxalic and phytic acid. Results showed crude protein, crude fat, minerals, crude fibre, carbohydrate, energy, calcium, iron and iodine content was in the range of 1.74 to 4.93%, 0.23 to 1.38%, 0.46 to 3.02%, 0.88 to 5.02%, 1.46 to 14.46%, 15 to 17 Kcal, 19 to 355 mg, 1.22 to 6.2 mg and 0.5 to 5.16 mg respectively, b-carotene, thiamine, riboflavin, niacin and ascorbic acid content ranged between 169 to 3020 mg, 0.03 to 0.1 mg, 0.2 to 0.24 mg, 0.07 to 0.87 mg and 3.26 to 173.13 mg per 100 g, respectively. (Raghuvanshi et al, 2001).

Root: ecdysteroids, ß-ecdosyne, polypodine B; shoot: ascorbic acid, ß-carotene, ß-sitosterol, caffeic acid, campesterol, catechins, ferulic acid, gallocatechin, imperatorin, n-triacontanol, P-coumaric acid, scopoletin, spinasterol, stigmasterol.
Pharmacology:

Saponins showed b-haemolysis against buffalo and sheep blood and g-haemolysis against human blood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Cryptomeridiol showed significant growth promoting activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Phenolic amide at 0.01 mM showed attracting activity toward zoospores of Aphanomyces cochlioides. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Chenopodium album shows a very high efficiency for leukocyte binding. (Mavrina et al, 1992).

Chlorophyllase (I) was purified from mature leaves of Chenopodium album and its ezymic properties were also investigated. (Tsuchiya et al, 1997). C.A. 997, 127, 216849x.

Chenopodium album was evaluated and following parameters like chemical percentual analysis (AOAC), gaseous chromatography fatty acid determination, and antinutrient searching (by chemical, enzymatic and immunological method) were performed. Protein quality was determined by the following indexes: net protein utilization (NPU), true digestibility (tD) and biological values (BV). (Escudero et al, 1999).

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

 Chenopodium album was characterized by various methods including crossed immuno electrophoresis (CIE), Crossed radioimmuno electrophoresis (CRIE) and SDS-PAGE immunoblotting for pollen allergens profile. (Wurtzen et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 254404y.

Chenopodium album was found to be the most prevalent sensitizing allergens, with frequencies of 53.6%, 52.7% and 50.9%, respectively, among the sensitized subjects (corresponding to 23.7%, 23.3%, and 22.5%, respectively) for the entire population. (Ezeamuzie et al, 1997).

Pollen grains of Chenopodium album are the main allergy causing bioparticle. (Singh and Kumar, 2002).

 Che a 1, a relevant allergen from chenopod pollen, is structurally related to the Ole e 1-like protein family, but exhibits significant differences on its polypeptide sequence that could explain its different antigenic behaviour and limited cross-reactivity. (Barderas et al, 2002).

Miscellaneous Information
Other information:
The low molecular weight 2S albumin protein of Chenopodium album seeds has been isolated and characterized with respect to its subunit structure by SDS-PAGE and antigenic homology with low molecular weight seed storage proteins of several other phylogenetically related species by immunoprecipitation and western blotting. These studies revealed the existence of antigenically homologous proteins of similar molecular weights in seeds of some other members of Chenopodiaceae and Amaranthaceae. (Dey and Mandal, 1993).

Bathua (Chenopodium album) were stored in polyethylene bags and without packing for 24 to 48 hours in a refrigerator at 5 or 30°C in polyethylene bags. The fresh leaves were also dried (oven and sun); blanched (5, 10 or 15 min.) and cooked in open pan and pressure cooker. The processed leaves were analyzed for total and extractable calcium and zinc content. The Ca and Zn content of these leaves varied from 970 to 2230 and 10.50 to 12.30 mg/100g DM and the percentage HCl-extractability was 80.34 to 83.04 and 82.43 to 83.90 respectively. Non-significant improvements of drying and storage were observed on total Ca and Zn content and HCl-extractability while blanching and cooking resulted in significant improvement in HCl-extractability of these two minerals. Thus cooking and blanching are good ways to improve the HCl-extractability of Ca and Zn. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9906, 4035.

Bathua (Chenopodium album) commonly available as green leafy vegetables. Their leaves are rich source of ascorbic acid and b-carotene. They are consumed in cooked form in commonly prepared products viz. soup, vegetable, raita, parotha, pakora and cutlet. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1999) C.A. 2000, 133, 251477v.
