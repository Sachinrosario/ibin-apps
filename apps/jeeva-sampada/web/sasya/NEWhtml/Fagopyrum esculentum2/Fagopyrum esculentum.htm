Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
An annual herb, native of Central Asia, cultivated in various countries as a food or fodder crop. In India, grown in temperate parts of Himalayan ranges and also on south Indian hills. Often raised as a vegetable in lower reaches of mountains.

The crop can be grown on a variety of soils, but it thrives best on well-drained sandy or loamy soils.  It does not grow well on poorly drained,  heavy or acidic soils.

Moist cool climate is ideal for its optimum growth. A monthly average temperature of 18 to 30° C seems to be satisfactory.  

Availability :  
 The plant is found in India, Central Asia, Europe, France, Russia and USA.

Buckwheat is said to be a native of Central Asia and is cultivated in various countries as a food or fodder crop.  In Europe, Russia, USA and France it constitutes one of the main food crops.  In India, it is grown as a grain crop in the higher altitudes (600-3650m) of the Himalayas (from Kashmir to Sikkim) and in the Nilgiris, but more often as vegetable crop in the lower mountain ranges.

Geographic regions of cultivation/wild population:Numerous varities have been distinguished based on the size, shape and colour of the fruit. The notched or wing-fruited  variety is often considered as a distinct species  and designated F. emarginatum Meissn. Trials in India  of the two types, Darjeeling and Chinese, have shown that the chinese type matures earlier.


Status : 

An endangered species. It is included in the Red data book (IUCN) as it is an overexploited species .

CONSERVATION CONCERN:

NBPGR, Simla is currently conserving the  germplasm of more than 10,000 collections of different crops including 561 collections of Fagopyrum esculentum, F. tatarium, F. tatarium var.Himalianum .Under natural conditions  at temperature –20 degree celsius to +20. The seed remains viable for 4-50 years with a germination  percentage ranging  from 65-85%. At National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources, New Delhi, a  large number of germplasm of this species and related species are conserved in the national repository for long term storage at –20°C.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

Herbaceous erect annual; stem hollow, angular, smooth with swollen nodes. Stem's colour ranges from green to red but turns brown with age. Leaves-alternate, triangular, blades hastate or cordate, acute; Inflorescence is axillary and terminal cyme with more or less densely clustered flowers. Each flower consistes of 5 partite persistent perianths, white, pink or red. 8 stamens which are alternated at the base by 8 glands which secrete nectary. Ovary is one-celled and contains a single erect ovule; Style is tripartite, each with a knob like stigma. Fruit - dry, one0seeded, three-sided nut called achene. Faces of the nut are glabrous, somewhat polished and generally convex and the angles more or less acutely keeled.

The three-sided achenes resemble miniature nuts from the beech tree (Fagus). This resemblance led to the German name "buchweizen" (beech-wheat) which became corrupted to the present name of buckwheat. The nutritious achenes are produced in flower clusters that arise from the axils of triangular-ovate leaves.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY

Breeding System :


Complete outbreeder, show self-incompatiblity. Flowers are dimorphic, flowers with stamens shorter than the style are known as “pin” flowers, while those in which the stamens are longer than the style are called “thrum” flowers. Inter-morph compatible and intramorph incompatible. (Only pollinations involving thrum x pin or vice-versa result in seed set, thrum x thrum and pin x pin do not result in seed set)

Phenology :
Fl.&Fr.: May - Sep.

Pollinators:

Seed Biology :

Seed is brown, grey-brown or black, marked with darker spots and lines. Calyx persistent.  Seed (grout)  which lies free inside the pericarp or hull has a pale testa and is triangular like the fruit. Endosperm white and opaque contain much starch than cereal grain endosperms and in the form of round or polygonal grains. Embryo is embedded in the centre of the endosperm and possess 2 cotyledons which are folded in the form of letter “s”.

The seed remains viable for 4-50 years with a germination  percentage ranging  from 65-85%

Seedling Germination :
Seeds remain viable for several years. Old seeds produce weak plants  which are slow growing.


Cultivation:

The crop being a short duration, medium-sized annual and sensitive to water-logged situations and a poor competitor with weeds, thorough preparation of the land so that it is free of weeds is necessary.  It requires a seed rate of 60-80kg per ha.  The seeds are line sown with a spacing of 20cmx10cm.  The most favourable temperature for germination is to be 35 °C.  In the crop sown in between December and January, there are chances of delay in germination due to frost.

Planting
Planting depth: 1/2 - 1.5 inches
Seeding Method

Drilled: Seed at 48-70 lb./A (1-1.4 bu/A)

Broadcast: Seed at 60-96 lb./A (1.2-1.5 bu/A)

Mix: with sorghum-sudangrass hybrids, sunnhemp
Soil improvement

Poor for loosening subsoil

Excellent at releasing P and K

Very good at loosening topsoil
  • Buckwheat attracts beneficials such as hover flies, predatory wasps, minute pirate bugs, insidious flower bugs, tachinid flies and lady beetles. To provide beneficial insect habitat, allow buckwheat to flower for at least 20 days (to allow minute pirate bugs to breed next generation).
  • Buckwheat is sensitive to herbicide residues from previous crops. To check for stunting or mortality, sow and water a small test plot.
To prevent buckwheat from becoming a weed in following crop, kill it within 7-10 days after flowering begins, before the first seeds harden and turn brown
  • Tolerates acid/low fertility soils
  • Minimum broadcast seeding rates of 60 lbs. pure live seed/acre;
  • pH range from 4.5-6.5;
  • Approximate growing time 30 days;
  • Approximate dry matter yield 1.5 tons/acre;
  • Approximate N content 18 lbs./T dry matter;
  • Add 20 lbs. of nitrogen/ton dry matter at plow down;
  • Optimum planting period year round at elevations between 0-4000 ft.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES
Pests: 

Some importance pests are Bronchid (Acanthecelids obtectus), cutowarm (Cirphis sp.), Aphids (Myzus persicae), grain moth (Cephitinea sp.), and storage beetle (Mycetophagus sp.) which damage the crop and can be controlled with the periodic treatment of any pesticides during the crop season.  

Diseases: 

Several fungi have been reported to attack.

1)       Sphacelotheca fagopyri at Keti and Septoria polygonicola at Ootacamund in the Nilgiris. Cercospora fagopyri (leaf blight) , Erysiphe polygoni, Ascochyta italica (Brown leaf spot), Puccinia fagopyri and Sclerotinia libertiana

2)      Viral diseases - Brazilian  curly top of tobacco and tomato transmitted by the vector Agallia albidula , Aster yellow virus, Mosaic virus.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts used
Roots, leaves, grains, husk,  hulls 

Uses

  • Dye :  Dried leaves and flowers contain rutin and they form promising commercial source of rutin. Which is used as a medicine in the treatment of increased capillary fragility with associated hypertension, prophylactic against gangrene due to frost bite.
  • A yellow crystalline colouring matter may be extracted from the leaves identical with rutin, and also with ilixanthin.  This dye yields on mordanted cotton bright yellow shades. 
  • Hulls also yields a dye used for  textile fabrics and  printed fabrics.

Harvesting Practices
Harvesting of grains is done during October –November in North-Weston Hills of India, and in December-January in Eastern  Hills.  In Nilgiri hills harvesting is done in August- September  and in April in Palni hills. Ripening of seeds is not uniform and delayed harvesting results in seed shattering. Due to an indeterminate growth habit, flowers , green grains and mature grains are present on the plants at the time of harvest

Produce and Products
Flour, dye, rutin (dried leaves and flowers)

Processing Techniques

Rutin - Solvent employed for the extraction of rutin are hotwater, dilute isopropyl alcohol or  ethanol. Hot isopropyl alcohol is an efficient solvent. By distillating off the alcohol from the extract and straining the fat a watery solution is obtained from which crude rutin crystallises on standing and cooling. Dissolved  in boiling water, treated with silica gel to remove the red pigment, and rutin crystallises from the filtrate. About 78% of rutin in the meal is recoverable by this process.

Dye: It may be obtained by adding acetate of lead to a decoction of the leaves; filtering while hot and adding acetic acid, when the yellow crystals will be precipitated.

Other Uses 
  • Roots are astringent, sweet, cooling, aphrodisiac and diuretic and are useful in diabetes, strangury, fever and vitiated conditions of vata and pitta.
  • Excellent crop for soil improvement, particularly for lands, newly opened or of poor soil which do not support any other cereal crops.
  • By its spreading habit and vigorous growth, it smothers weeds and forms an excellent cover.
  • Acts as soil binder preventing erosion during heavy rainfall.
  • Green manure, flour for making bread, pancakes and porridge.
  • Leaves and young shoots are boiled and eaten as spinach.
  • Grain and various by-products are used for feeding farm animals. The seed yield a hard, bitter and unpalatable bread which is said to be eaten in plains during the bart or fast-days. The hulled achenes or groats are used in several brands of hot and cold breakfast cereals. The seeds are ground into flour which is used for pancakes, noodles and breads. In Russia, a nutritious porridge called "kasha" is made from buckwheat flour.
  • Husk, rouphage, hulls - fuel and bedding for farm animals and for packaging.
  • Perisperm – fuel in producer gas plants.
  • Leaves are anodyne and are useful in rheumatalgia.
  • In conjuction with certain miotics is of value in glaucoma and in combination with dicumarol in the treatment of retinitis.
  • Biomass/organic matter source (Dry Matter: 2,000-3,000 lbs/ac/yr) ; Very fast establishing cover ;Quick regrowth ;Weed suppression ; Insectary plant ;Topsoil conditioner ; Phosphorous scavenger ; Low fertility crop.
