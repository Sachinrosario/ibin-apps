DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stout, bushy, glabrous shrubs or small trees, 0.7-2.5 m high; bark thin, smooth, greyish-yellowish; young branches terete, glabrous. Leaves membranous, oblong to elliptic-lanceolate, 7.5-18.0 x 2.5-7.0 cm, acute or acuminate at apex, tapering to a rounded base, glabrous on both surfaces, lateral nerves 8-10 pairs; petioles 0.3-1.2 cm long; stipules connate, triangular, acute, thin, deciduous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers in terminal sessile, pubescent corymbose cymes, white, odorous; pedicels 3.0-5.5 mm long, densely pubescent; bracts broad, membranaceous, the lower cupular. Calyx campanulate, 3 mm long, densely pubescent; tube narrowly campanulate; teeth triangular, 0.8-1.2 mm long, acute. Coralla tube 1.0-1.3 cm long; lobes linear-oblong, 5-8 x 1.2-2.5 mm, subacute. Fruits globose, 0.5-1.4 cm in diam., black, smooth.

Uses: The root is bitter and generally used in visceral obstruction.
Chemistry:
The fatty acid compn. of the seed oil of Pavetta indica Linn., the constituent amino acids of the seed protein, the sugars present in the seed and in vitro antimicrobial activity of the seed oil against some gram +ve, gram -ve bacteria and some fungi. (Srivastava et al, 1999).


Pharmacology:
A comparative study of the efficacy of Pavetta indica and Osbeckia octandra in the treatment of liver dysfunction is done. (Thabrew et al, 1987).
