HABIT & HABITAT:
Small or medium sized tree, c 20-25 m, it is found in Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Daman & Diu.

Commonly found in the forests of sub Himalayan tract and Siwalik hills and in the hills throughout India ascending to an altitude of 1000m. also found in Pakistan and Sri Lanka. in dry deciduous forests of India, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.  Ascending to 1000m.; but not found in the Trans-Ganga plains.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:


Small or medium sized tree, c 20-25 m. tall, bark grayish white, smooth; branches silky, pubescent. Leaves alternate or sub-opposite, elliptic or oblong-elliptic, 4.5- 11.0 x 6.5 cm, obtuse or very offen shortly cuspidate at apex, glabrous, chartaceous, rusty below, base usually pink. Flowers sessile in small, dense axillary and terminal heads, 3.5-4.0 mm across, yellow. Calyx tube pubescent, persistent, triangular, yellow. Stamens exerted. Ovary densely tomentose. Fruits small, crowded in a head, 3-4 mm broad, yellowish brown, winged down the two longer sides, beaked with persistent calyx. Seeds 1-seeded.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Inflorescence: Small dense globose heads; Flower: Yelow, sessile, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Calyx tube pubescent, teeth short, broadly triangular; Corolla: Petals absent Androecium: Stamens 10, biseriate, exserted, anthers small, cordate ;Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 1-celled, ovules 2, pendulous from the top of the cell, style filiform, thickened at the base, villous, stigma simple; Fruit: Small, several, crowded in globular head, glabrous, more or less pubescent, beaked with persistent calyx, wings with entire margins ; Seed:Solitary, ovoid. Flowering & Fruiting: May-July. Flowers are pollinated by insects and seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.  

Floral and seed biology:

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine root is pungent and increases biliousness. Bark improves taste and appetite and also useful in anaemia, urinary discharges, piles, skin disease and erysephales. Leaf juice is given in purulent discharges from the ear. Fruits are astringent to bowels. In Unani system of medicine bark is used in liver complaints, chronic diarrhoea, ophthalmia and eyesores.

Plant: in toothache, spleen enlargement, dysuria diarrhoea, cold, cough, cholera, excessive perspiration, piles, urinary disorders, in scorpion sting and snake bite; Bark : bitter, astringent; Gum (called Gahtti or Indian gum) : as tonic after delivery. gives stable oil-in-water emulsion and patended for incorporating oil-sol, vitamin preparations have an excellent emulsifying properties.  


Chemistry:

Bark: quinic acid, shikimic acid, steroids, 3,3’, 4 -tri-flavellagic acids and two glycerides; Heart wood: ellagic acid and its derivatives, gallic acid, quinic acid, shikimic acid, quercetin, myricetin and steroids; Plant mucilage: aldobiouronic acid, aldotriuronic acid; Gum: calcium salt of ghattic acid. Leaf: - Quinic acid, shikimic acid.  
A glucoside (C12H28O) was isolated from aqueous extracts of Anogeissus latifolia leaves. Its m.p. 158° and Mol. Wt.  356. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Gallotanin, gallic acid, chebilagic acid and trigallic acid (mp.224) were isolated from the leaves of Anogeissus latifolia. (Reddy et al, 1964)

A dextro-rotatary leucocyanidin in 0.5% yield has been isolated from acetone extract and ellagic acid and3,3,4-tri-o-methyl ellagic acids in 0.5% and 0.7% yields respectively from methanol extract of the bark. From sapwood both ellagic acid and trimethylellagic acid have been isolated (0.01% & 0.4%). Further quercetin , myricetin  and trimethylellagic acid were also isolated from Anogeissus latifolia heartwood. (Reddy et al, 1964)

Two glycosides of elllagic and flavellagic acids have been isolated from the bark of Anogeissus latifolia and their structures established as 3,3’-Di-O-methylellagic acid – 4’ - b - D – xyloside (fig. 1) and 3,4,3¢ – tri –O- methyl flavellagic acid – 4’ – b – D – glucoside (Fig. 2). (Despande et al, 1956)

3,3’,4-tri-O-methylflavellagic acid  (mp.280-1°C)was isolated from bark of Anogeissus latifolia. (Reddy et al, 1974)

Quercetin-3-O-b-D-galactopyranosyl  (1à4) -O-a-L-rhamnopyranoside  (mp.272-74oC) was isolated from the roots of Anogeissus latifolia.(Chaturvedi and Saxena, 1985)

A flavone glycoside luteolin –7 ­- O – b – D – galactopyranosyl – (1 à 6) – O – b –D – galactopyranoside was isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

A triterpenoid saponin 3 – O – acetyl – olean –12 – ene –28 – oic –O –b –D –glucopyranosyl – (1à4) –O -b  -D- xylpyranoside from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
