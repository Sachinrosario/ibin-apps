HABIT & HABITAT
Stem 30-60 cm long, stout, scandant by the stout, simple or branching roots and usually found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Orissa, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Southern India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem 30-60 cm long, stout, scandant by the stout, simple or branching roots. Leaves spreading, recurved, coriaceous, linear-oblong, 5-25 x 0.4-1.2 cm, entire, apex praemorse with usually 2 unequal rounded lobes and an acute one in between.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers 6-10, 5 cm across racemes; peduncles 15-25 cm long; bracts ovate, 3 mm long, acute; pedicellate. Sepals yellow, tessellated with brown lines and white margins; lateral sepals obovate, 1.5-2.5 x 0.7-1.6 cm, base subcuneate with more or less waved margins; dorsal sepal obovate-oblong. Petals yellow with brown lines and white margins, shorter than the sepals; lips 16 mm long, bluish dotted with purple; spur straight, conicle obtuse, 6 mm long. Capsules broadly oblong, 7.5-9.0 x 1.5 cm, sharply winged.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The root is bitter; laxative, tonic to the live and the brain. It is good for bronchitis, biles, lumbago, toothache, boils on the scalp, lessens inflammation and heals fractures (Unani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933). Root is used in dyspepsia, bronchitis, rheumatic pain, diseases of abdomen, hiccough etc. (Ayurveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).


Chemistry:
·        b- and g-siotosterols and a long chain aliphatic compound,mp. 85°, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Roots yielded tetracosyl ferulate and b-sitosterol glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·        From the whole plant of Vanda tessalata, a new phenanthropyran derivative was isolated. Its structure was elucidated as 3,7-dihydroxy-2-methoxy-9,10-dihydrophenanthropyran on the basis of spectroscopic data. (Anuradha and Prakasa,1998). MAPA 9905-3237, BA 105(14) 193772.

Pharmacology:

PHARMACOLOGY 

·        Petroleum ether, chloroform and methanol extracts of roots showed 54.3, 42.1 and 21.9% anti-inflammatory activity respectively at a dose of 0.5 g/kg in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

·        The alcohol extract of the flower of Vanda tessalata was found to stimulate morning behaviour of male mice. The extract (50 or 200 mg/kg) also increased mating performance in the mice. The alcohol extract was devoid of any conspicuous toxicity. (Kumar et al, 2000). MAPA 2001-01-0230.


Ethnobotany:

Medicine for abscess (Root, Joshi et al, 1980); Antifertility (Root, Tarafder, 1983); Bone fracture (Whole plant, Goel et al, 1984; Maheshwari et al, 1980; Maheshwari et al, 1981); Dyspepsia (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Earache and pus (Leaf + aerial Root, Nagendra and Abraham, 1984; Jain and De, 1966; Bhattacharjee et al, 1980; Maheshwari et al, 1986; Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Eye disease (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Night blindness (Whole plant, Tribedi et al, 1985); Rheumatism (Root, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973; Sharma et al, 1979); Sores (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Ornamental anklets of Snatal girls (Leaf, Jain,  et al 1987)
