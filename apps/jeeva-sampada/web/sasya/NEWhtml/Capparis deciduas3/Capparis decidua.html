HABIT & HABITAT:
Much branched shrubs or small tree, up to 7 m tall; it is found in Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Branches slender, scrambling or suffruticose, glabrous. Stipular spines straight, yellowish, rarely absent; cataphylls to base. Leaves present in young twigs, deciduous, fleshy, linear, spathulate or linear-oblong, 5-10 x 2-4 cm, acute or spiny at tip. Flowers showy, coral pink, brick-red, orange-red or yellowish, in corymbose raceme or fascicle, 1.5-2.5 cm across. Sepals unequal, tomentose, adaxial sepal saccate, hooked, other petaloid. Petals almost equal; anterior pair triangular, ovate, included within the hooded sepal. Stamens 10-16. Fruits berry, ovoid to subglobose, many seeded glabrous, scarlet-red when ripe. Seeds 1-4, reniform, 4-5 mm in diam.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Many flowerd corymbs, from older branches or from short lateral shoots, pedicle slender ; Flower: Bracteate, pedicillate, hermaphrodite, hypogynous, tetramerous, hypogynous, cyclic; Calyx:  Sepals outer pubescent, ciliate, subvalvate, the lower sepal very saccate, acuminate, ovate-oblong, concave; Corolla: Petals red, oblong-narrow, imbricate ;Androecium: Stamens many, inserted on te torus at the base of a long gynophore ; Gynoecium: Ovary stalked, 1-4 celled, ovules many, on 2-6 parietal, stigma sessile ; Fruit:  Berry globular, size of small cherry, glabrous beaked ; Seed: many, embedded in pulp, embryo convolute. Flowering & Fruiting : November - March.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by cattles.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine bark is used as analgesic, diaphoretic, anthelmintic. It is used in case of cough, asthma, ulcers, boils, piles, vomiting and inflammations. Fruits are used as astringent to the bowels; destroy foul breath, urinary purulent discharges and good in cardiac troubles. In Unani system of medicine the plant is used as carminative and tonic. It is also used in rheumatism, hiccough, cough and asthma.


Chemistry:
Capparine, m.p. 236°C, capparilline, m.p. 188°C and Capparinine, m.p. 229°C, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

n-Pentacosane, n-triacontanol, b-sitosterol and 1-stachydrine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Fruits, leaves, bark and roots claimed to relieve a variety of ailments such as toothache, cough, asthama, intermittent and rheumatism; also useful in cardiac troubles and biliousness. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A glucoside-glucocaparin is composed of methyl isothiocyanate and glucose isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Arabinose, galactose and polyphenols are present in plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A new spermidine alkaloid  capparidisine (fig. 1a)was isolated from root bark and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation and structure determination of capparisine (fig. 1b) from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Capparisinine(fig. 1c) isolated from root bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

A new spermidine alkaloid  isocodonocarpine was isolated from root bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Two other spermidine alkaloids 14-N-acetylisocodonocarpine and 15-N-acetylcapparisine was isolated from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.


Pharmacology:
Capparidisine showed dose-dependent depressant effect on heart rate and coronary flow in isolated rabbit heart. Maximum fall in coronary flow observed at 1.0 mg/ml. Contraction of heart rate increased at 2.0 ng dose and then upto 128.0 and 32.0 ng a dose-dependent fall was observed in force of contraction and heart rate respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Alloxan-induced diabetic rats were treated with insulin (i.p.) or with Capparis decidua powder as a hypoglycaemic agent mixed with diet. The effect was assessed on lipid peroxidation (LPO) and the antioxidant defense system in rat tissues. The increased levels of blood glucose in diabetes produce superoxide anions and hydroxyl radicals in the presence of transition metal ions which cause oxidative damage to cell membranes.  C. decidua may have potential use as an antidiabetic agent and in lowering oxidative stress in diabetes. (Yadav et al, 1997).

Antidiabetic treatment with powdered fruit of Capparis decidua decreased alloxan induced lipid peroxidation (LPO) significantly in erythrocytes, kidney and heart. Erythrocyte superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity decreased while the kidney and heart SOD increased in diabetic animals. These alterations in SOD were counteracted by insulin as well as with powdered fruit of C. decidua. Increased catalase (CAT) activity in erythrocytes, liver, kidney and heart with C. decidua treatment indicate that the treatment may neutralize H2O2 toxicity by its increased decomposition by CAT. Result shows that treatment with C. decidua lowers alloxan induced LPO and alters SOD and CAT enzymes to reduce oxidative stress. (Yadav et al, 1997)
