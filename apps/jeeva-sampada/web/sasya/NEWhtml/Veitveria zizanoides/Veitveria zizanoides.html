DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial, tufted herbs. Roots fibrous, aromatic. Culms up to 1.5 m tall with rhizomatous rootstock. Leaves linear, up to 15 cm long, margin ciliate; sheath glabrous; ligules minute. Panicles c 30 cm long; spikes 5-7 cm long, purple.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Sessile spikelets lanceolate, acute. Lower glume c 3.5 mm long, 5-nerved; upper glume c 4 mm long, spinulose, awnless. Lower lemma c 3.5 mm long, empty; upper lemma c 3 mm long, 3-nerved, mucronate. Caryopsis c 2 mm long, broader at base. August - November.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Infusion of the root is given as a febrifuge and in bilious complaints. It is regarded as stimulent, diaphoretic, stomachic and refrigerant. Paste of the pulverised roots in water is also used as a cooling external application in fever.

·        Increasing the value of the hydrocarbon fraction of Bourbon vetiver essential oil (BVEO) was attempted by oxidation and reduction reactions to obtain odorous sesquiterpenic alcohols. Oxidation of this sesquiterpenic fraction of BVEO with m-chloroperbenzoic acid afforded three new oxidized products, including a zizaene monoepoxide and two diepoxides derived from zizanene and valencene. Reduction reaction of sesquiterpenic epoxides of BVEO led to three sesquiterpenols, including two zizanol epimers and an epoxialcohol derived from valencene, which have been synthesized in high yields (85-100%). Structural determination of these derivatives followed from one- and two-dimensional NMR spectroscopy and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry techniques. (Bombarda et. al., 1996).

Three of these were prepared for comparison purposes, e.g. via ozonolysis or hydrocarbon starting from I. (Weyerstahl, et. al., 1997).

Pharmacology:

·        Zizanal and epizizanal exhibited insect repellant activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Khusitoneol exhibited juvenile hormone activity against mustard aphid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Alcoholic extracts of Vetiveria zizanioides was pharmacologically screened for cardiac activity on isolated rabbit heart, and showed significant negative inotropic activity with negative chronotropic effects. (Sajid et. al., 1996).

·        An antifungal composition containing pulverized roots of Vetiveria zizanioides alongwith other constituents is useful for treatment of human nails fungus. (Bindra et. al., 2001).

 

Ethnobotany:

·        Ethnobotanical uses of Vetiveria zizanioides for dyspepsia and ear ache for vanda tesseuata. (Satapathy and Brahman, 1994-1995).

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

·        The essential oils obtained from Vetiveria zizanioides were found to be toxic for Aedes aegypti larvae causing mortalities of over 50 percent at a concentration of 50 mg/L. (Chantraine et. al., 1998).

Growth inhibition of fungi by various essential oils containing Vetiveria zizanioides was determined by direct contact in broth and agar media and compared with the fungistatic action of their vapors using the micro-atmospheric method. Tests were performed on eight cellulolytic mold strains that often contaminate archive and museum reserves. (Delespaul et. al., 2000).
