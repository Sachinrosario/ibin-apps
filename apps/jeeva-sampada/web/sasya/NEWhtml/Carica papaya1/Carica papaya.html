HABIT & HABITAT
Soft wooded, short lived tree, 2-10 m tall, stem usually unbranched, rarely branched with large leaf scars. It is found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Tripura, Assam, Maharashtra, Laccadives, Andaman & Nicobar Island.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves large, glabrous, palmatefied and palmi-nerved, 25-75 cm wide; petiole long, hollow, forming a round tuft at top of the stem. Male flowers arranged in long drooping panicles. Female flowers arranged in short clusters. Sepals free or slightly connate. Corolla fleshy, white, yellow or light yellow. Corolla of bisexual flowers 3.0-3.5 cm long. Ovary 1-celled; stigma sessile, 5-lobed, lacerate. Fruit pepo, ellipsoid, 10-25 x 7.0-5.0 cm, succulent, indehiscent, yellow green, orange inside. Seeds numerous, black, enclosed in sweet mucous pulp and covered with a loose hyaline skin or arillus; testa thick, bitter.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Male Flower: Long drooping panicles, stamens 10 on the mouth of corolla tube, 2-seriate corolla, anthers oblong; Female Flower: Short clustered, pendulous, stigma reniform, ovary free, 1-5 celled with short style Fruit: Berry the one formed from perfect flower is more elongated; Seed: Shining black, smooth, rounded . Flowering & Fruiting : July- September. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Propagation: Plant is propagated by seeds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine ripe fruits are astringent to bowels and use as aphrodisiac and in biliousness. It is also useful in insanity. In Unani system medicine ripe fruits are used as stomachic, digestive, carminative and diuretic. It is also used in inflammation, enlargement of spleen, removes urinary concretions, haemoptysis, bleeding piles, wound of urinary tract and skin diseases.

The pectin mol. mass declined and the soly. of pectin in cyclohexan-diaminotra cetic acid and Na2Co3  solns. increased during ripening. The mol. mass decreased and the soly. of hemicellulose in KOH increased during ripening water sol. uronic acid increased 6-fold during ripening as the yield of cell wall material declined. The loss of high mol. mass pectins decreased throughout while the demethylation rate was greater in early in ripening changes in pectin mol. size did not parallel loss of fruit fiemness during early ripening. The pectin fractions were mainly composed of rhamnose, glucose, xylose galactose, mannose and arabinose, in decreasing order of conc. Non cellulosic glucose and xylose were the main neutral sugars in the hemicellulose fraction followed by mannose and galactose and traces of rhamnose and arabinose. These results suggested that pectin hydrolysis and the modification of hemicellulose both were involved in papaya fruit softening.
