HABIT & HABITAT
Small to medium sized tree, and found in India: Commonly cultivated and planted in gardens and along roadsides. Wild in sub-Himalayan tract at altitude 700-1000 m.  China, Pakistan, Nepal, Iran, Turkey, Myanmar.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Small or medium sized trees up to 0.3 m high; young parts tomentose, glabrous on old. Leaves bi-or tripinnate, 30-90 cm long; leaflets 3-11, opposite, lanceolate, thin, leathery oblique at base, toothed along margins, 2.5-5 x 1-2.5 cm, glabrous on both surfaces.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in long peduncled, axillary panicles, fragrant. Staminal tube 20-30 toothed, dark purple. Drupes ellipsoid-globose, fleshy, yellow when ripe, 1.2- 1.8 x 0.8-1 cm. Seeds 4, ellipsoid.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The root is astringent, anthelmintic and used to treat tumours, pain in heart, vomitting, leucoderma, blood impurities. It cures ulcers, headache, uterine pain after delivery, fever, burning sensation,  urinary discharges, lung complains etc. (Ayurveda). Leaves and seeds are used to treat heart and spleen diseases. It strengthen teeth, stops inflammation, cures scabies and dry skin eruptions. Seed-oil is a brain tonic, used in earache, piles, spleen and liver disorders; purifing blood. Flowers and leaves relieve nervous headache (Unani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
NBPGR:  Bark-antidiarrheal, fish-poison, in skin diseases, leaf-antilithic, diuretic, antitumour; leaf and flower-emmenagogue, febrifuge, sedative, in headaches, leaf, fruit and stem-bark-antileprotic, in scrofula; fruit, root bark, flower and leaf-deobstruent, resolvent, alexipharmac; seed oil-antirheumatic, insecticidal.

Chemistry:
NBPGR:  Stem-bark gave 6b-hydroxy–4-Stigmasten-3-one, 6b-hydroxy-4-campesten-3-one and 4,5-dihydroxy flavone-7-0-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1®4)-b-D-glucopyranoside; fruit and heart wood yielded a bitter principle bakayanin and lactone bakalactone, respectively; leaves gave quercitrin, rutin, tetranortriterpenoids-salannin and vilasinin.
