 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
 Branched, evergreen, perennial tree, Cultivated in many parts of India or grown in garden as an ornamental plant, China, Myanmar.
Availability :  


Specimen examined:

Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant is wild, found in Burma, China and introduced in Ceylon.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Branched, evergreen, perennial tree. All parts glabrous. Leaves lanceolate, 3-nerved, 7-10 cmlong, coriaceous. Flowers small, arranged in axillary cymose panicle. Perianths silky about 3 mm long. Fruits drupe, ellipsoid, glabrous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary and terminal  panicles; Flower: Pale yellowish, complete, hermaphrodite, small; Perianth: With oblong lobes and short tubes, segments 6 ; Androecium: Stamens 9, fertile, introrse 4-celled anthers; Gynoecium:  Ovary superior,  surrounded by cup shaped base, hypogynous, one chambered with single ovule, style slender; Fruit: Black succulent somewhat ovoid berry  sealed on somewhat enlarged perianth tube . Flowering & Fruiting : April-May. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Bark is used  in case of inflammation, pregnancy, headache, piles; it also  enriches blood. The bark is used  as a substitute for Ceylon Cinnamon (Uanani).


Chemistry:
Detection of cinnamaldehyde and eugenol in bark oil and benzyl benzoate in leaf oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Leaves and bark oil contained cinnamaldehyde, 71.3 and 82.0%, respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

New diterpenoids – cinncassiols A, B and C – isolated together with insecticidal cinnzeylanin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Cinncassiol A (fig. 1) and its glucoside (fig. 1a), cinnzeylanine, cinnzeylanol, anhydrocinnzeylanin and anhydrocinnzeylanol isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Isolation and structure elucidation of cinnacassiol C1, m.p. 204°C. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

Cinnacassiol D4 (fig. 2) and its glucoside isolated from bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

Coumarin, trans- cinnamic acid, b-sitosterol and protocatechuic acid isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

Recent development of chemistry of essential oils of cassia [Cinnamomum cassia]. (Lawrence, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9502-1108.

Simultaneous microsteam distillation/solvent extraction is an efficient method of extracting semivolatile flavor and fragrance compounds from Cinnamomum for subsequent separation by series coupled-column gas chromatography using, pentane as extraction solvent. (Jayatilaka et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 122, 158893r.

The essential oil contents of bark of C. cassia var. macrophyllum were determined and as a result 36 compounds were identified in the oil by GC-MS. (Ruan et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 245493g.

Cinnamomum cassia was analysed at 15% moisture level for bark oil, bark oleresin and leaf oil percentages. Bark oil ranged from 1.2 to 4.9 percent, leaf oil 0.4 to 1.6% and bark oleoresin 6.0 to 10.5%. Cinnamaldehyde was the major constituent in both leaf and bark oils. (Krishnamoorthy et al, 1999). MAPA 2000-04-2450.

The chemical constituents of essential oil from ramulus of Cinnamomum cassia were studied. The essential oil was obtained by steam distillation, and its chemical constituents were examined by means of high resolution capillary gas chromatography and gas chromatography-mass spectrometery. The H.P. Ultra-1 Quartz capillary column (25m x 0.2 mm, 0.33 mm) was used. Forty four from 50 separated constituents of essential oil was identified by MS, and determined by areanormalization method. The content of 9 components from 44 compounds was > / % of cinnamic aldehyde was 62.4%. (Ma et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 56421d.

A HPLC method for the determination of cinnamaldehyde in C. Cassia was established. Cinnamaldehyde was separated on a Kromasil C18 column with a mobile phase of MeCN – 0.1% H3PO4 solution (48:52) and UV detection at 285 nm. The caliberation curve was linear in the range .004-0.02 ml ml-1; with a correlation coefficient of 0.9996. The average recovery was 100.7% with RSD 0.3%. (Luo et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 340341p.

Cinnamaldehyde (I) was determined in Cinnamon oil by GC with a chromosorb WAW column, 10% PEG – 20M as the stationary phase and a hydrogen flame ionization detector at 190°C. Volumetric constituents were identified by GC-MS with a programmed temperature gas chromatograph at (120°C for 5 min., increase of 5 per minute to 150° for 7 min.) and the ratio of mass to electrical charge of 10: 425. The caliberation curve was linear within the range of 2-10 mg I/ml. The average recovery for I was 99.98% with RSD of 1.3%. (Li and Yuan, 1999, Li and Yuan, 2000).

The cosmetic containing C. cassia shows antiaging and antiwrinkle effects. (Seibu, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 242430t.

Determination of cinnamic acid in Cinnamomum cassia after compatibility in coptis chinensis. (Xu et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 134, 183594z.

C. Cassia plant extract is used in make-up composition with other plant extracts. (Seibu, 2001). C.A. 2001, 134, 242430t.


Pharmacognosy:
Microscopial method for the identification of Cinnamomum Cassia. (Park et al, 2000). MAPA 2000-05-2745.

Pharmacology:

Plant exhibited anticomplement activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)h.

Aqueous extract administered orally to rats with nephritis prevented increase of protein level in urine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)i.

Cinnacasiol C1, cinnacassiol D4 and its glucoside exhibited anti-allergic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)j.

Effect of Cinnamon (Cinnamomum cassia) on the peroxide value of cake. (Zain and Hussin, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9501-0124.

Cinnamomum cassia with other plants shows degeneration of the testes was milder, and the blood levels of testosterone and oestradiol were higher indicating beneficial effects on the histomorphlogy of the tests. (Zheng et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9603-1506.

Cinnamomium cassia oil is used in cough and in breathlessness. (Kulkarni, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9605-2715.

2’-Hydroxycinnamaldehyde, which inhibits farnesyl-protein transferase (FPTase), has been isolated from the stem bark of Cinnamomum cassia. The biologically active agent called as 2’-Hydroxycinnamaldehyde present in the extract has been purified by silica column chromatography and HPTLC. The structure of the isolated compound was elucidated on the basis of 500 MHz NMR experiments. (Kwon et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0427.

The compositions contain bovine, sheep or swine thymus glands extracts as main ingredients, including Cinnamomum cassia extracts; also calcium, Vitamin B1, Vitamin B2, Vitamin B6, Vitamin B12 and vitamin E. the composition is useful in treating systematic erythematosus. (Itakura et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 166777z.

The growth-inhibiting activity of Cinnamomum cassia (Blume) bark-derived materials toward five intestinal bacteria was examined using an impregnated paper disk method and compared with that of tetracycline and chloramphenicol, as well as four commercially available compounds. (Lee and Ahn, 1998).

Antiallergic interleukin – 4 formation inhibitors contain extracts of Cinnamomum cassia. (Kondo et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 184689x.

Two kinds of cinnamaldehyde derivative, 2'-hydroxycinnamaldehyde (HCA) and 2'-benzoxy-cinnamaldehyde (BCA), were studied for their immunomodulatory effects, which were screened as anticancer drug derived from stem bark of Cinnamomum cassia. (Koh et al, 1998).

Cinnamomum cassia was included in the hot drugs. The effect of hot drug on in vitro enzyme activities were measured and compared with the in vivo effects. In vitro MAO-A activities were increased by hot drugs. (Hwang et al, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9905-3067.

The extract of Cinnamomum cassia help in stimulating human lymphocytes to proliferate. (Shan et al, 1999).

The antignawing activity of the compound was compared with that of four common available compounds: cinnamyl alcohol, trans-cinnamic acid, eugenol, and salicylaldehyde. Cinnamaldehyde and cinnamyl alcohol exhibited potent repellant activity at 5% and 2.5% solutions, respectively, of these compounds in ethanol. Little or no activity was observed in 1% solution,. As a naturally occurring repellant, cinnamaldehyde and cinnamyl alcohol could be useful as a new preventive agent against various kinds of damage caused by rodents. (Lee et al, 1999)b. C.A. 1999, 131, 99877z.

2'-Hydroxycinnamaldehyde (HCA) was isolated from Cinnamomum cassia Blume (Lauraceae) and 2'-benzoyloxycinnamaldehyde (BCA) was prepared by the reaction of HCA and benzoyl chloride. HCA and BCA strongly inhibited in vitro growth of 29 kinds of human cancer cells and in vivo growth of SW-620 human tumor xenograft without the loss of body weight in nude mice. HCA prevented adherence of SW-620 cells to the culture surface but did not inhibit oncogenic K-Ras processing, implying its antitumor mechanisms at the cellular level. (Lee et al, 1999)a.

C. cassia is considered to be hot drug which help in increasing MAO – A (i.e. a flavoprotein found exclusively in the mitrochondrial outer membrane) in vitro. (Hwang et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 102719c.

Extract of Cinnamomum cassia was found to be effective against HIV-I. (Premanathan et al, 2000).

Extract prepared from the bark of Cinnamomum cassia prevents glutamate-induced neuronal death in cultured cerebellar granule cells. (Shimada et al, 2000).

Cinnamomum cassia showed insecticidal and fumigant activity against Mechoris ursulus. (Park et al, 2000).

The protective effect of a water extract of Cinnamomum cassia bark on glutamate-induced neuronal death by MTT assay and its action on 45Ca2 influx using cultured rat cerebellar granule cells is reported. In a dose-dependent manner, the extract significantly protected against glutamate-induced cell death and also inhibited glutamate-induced influx. The bark of Cinnamomum cassia has been suggested to have a protective effect on glutamate-induced neuronal death through the inhibition of Ca2 influx. (Shimada et al, 2000). MAPA 2001-01-0289.

Cinnamomum cassia is evaluated for the antimutagenic effect against two mutagens, viz. benzo[a]pyrene (B[a]P) and cyclophosphamide (CP). The antimutagenic properties of C. cassia were examined by the Ames test. (Sharma et al, 2001).

The antifungal effects of C. cassia with these volatile components were studied. (Sun, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 235916y.

Extract obtained from C. cassia is useful active ingredients as a-glusidase. The plant extract is also useful for manufacturing the anti-diabetic agents. (Kusu, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 16939e.

C. cassia was extracted with H2O/ action (7:3) mix and the extracts were worked up to give various fractions. The fractions were subjected to passive cutaneous anaphylaxis test with rats. Epicatechin was identified in the active fraction. (Park et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 308669y.

Extracts was prepared from cinnamon (Cinnamomum cassia) with other plant to evaluate their antimicrobial activity on common food borne microorganisms. As a result mixed extract was of promising potential for incorporation into various food products for which a natural antimicrobial additive is desired. (Mau et al, 2001).


Ethnobotany:
Cinnamomum cassia is used as a flavoring spice with some established medicinal properties. (Sharma et al, 2001).

Miscellaneous Information
Other inforamtion:
The methanol extract of C. cassia was screened for prolyl endopeptidase (PEP) inhibitors as a result it inhibit more than 70% of PEP activity at a concentration of 40 ppm. (Lee et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 70669q.

Amylase inhibitors (I) are prepared from defatted Cinnamomum cassia Blume by extraction with EtOH solution. The I are safe and free of off-flavor and bitterness. (Iida et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 126, 237700m.

Cinnamomum cassia bark powder suppressed garlic odor in breath for 120 min. (Oniki et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 278023x.
