Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Small tree with umbrella-shaped crown. Occurs in semi & lava plateau & Deccan islands, humid to semihumid and Western Ghats and Karnataka plateau. Common on the coast of South India, Andamans, Bangaldesh, Myanmar and Sri Lanka. Cultivated as an avenue tree in Chennai, Mumbai, etc., as far inland as Chandigarh. Wild along the beach and tidal forest.

It can grow everywhere including saline soils, except in hilly areas, but prefers light and porous soils. It thrives in moist and warm situations, but can withstand temperatures as low as -40.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

Small tree with an umbrella shaped crown. At once recognized by its tulip-like yellow flowers with a purple centre, its heart shaped poplar like leaves and the black turban shaped capsules, up to 12 m high. Bark grey, inner bark fibrous. Leaves poplar like, 8-15 X 6-10 cm, heart shaped, entire, pointed at the tip. Petiole 22.5-10 cm; stipules awl-shaped, deciduous.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers axillary, solitary or two together, bisexual, bell shaped, 5-7.5 cm across, pedicels 5-7.5 cm long. Calyx cup shaped, covered with minute, stalked scales. Petals 5, yellow with crimson or deep chocolate centre, resembling yellow crepe paper.

Fruit a globose capsule, 2.5-4 cm across, covered with minute stalked scales, calyx persistent, black when ripe. Seeds 1 cm long, ovoid. Flowers change colour from yellow to purple in 24 hours.



Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Although evergreen in moist areas, it sheds many leaves in February in less moist areas. The yellow withering leaves resemble flowers, giving the impression from a distance that the tree is in full bloom. Flowers throughout the year, particularly at beginning of the cold season. Fruits April-May.


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:


Seed Germination & Propagation: Propagated from seeds and cuttings. Seed retains its viability even after a prolonged exposure to saline water. Best time for plantation is July. It is mostly planted in gardens in Chennai and Mumbai but is capable of growing everywhere except in the hills.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Several defoliators, sapsuckers, leaf-miners and borers on dead wood have been recorded.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel.

Wood: The sapwood is white with a pale-yellowish or pinkish tinge, darkening on exposure. Heartwood reddish brown to chocolate or even purplish brown with beautiful sharply defined and darker streaks. the timber, known as "SEYCHELLES ROSEWOOD", is straight- to somewhat interlocked-grained, even- and medium-textured, tough, hard and heavy (sp. gr. 0.54-0.88; wt. 640-897 kg./cu.m.). Wood is water-resistant.

Timber is suitable for gun-stocks, carts and carriages, wheel-spokes, boat building, particularly knee and joints, rafter and reefers, agricultural implements, staffs, cabinets, all kinds of tool-handles, shuttles, beams, furniture, clubs, paddles and musical instruments. Also suitable for turnery. Hardwood is used for gunstocks, wheel frames.

Other uses:

Tannin & Dye - Tannin is present in the wood and bark (7%). A fine red colouring matter is also present.

Dye - Flowers and capsules yield a yellow dye, soluble in water. It is capable of producing exquisite, though somewhat faint shades of brownish yellow and brown on tasar, mulberry silk and wool produces.

Bark - The bark yields a strong fibre which is used for cordage, fishing-lines and coffee-bags and for caulking boats.

Fodder - Leaves as fodder.

Food - The young buds and leaves have a pleasant taste and along with the flowers are eaten either raw, cooked or fried in butter.

Seeds - Seeds yield deep red coloured oil. Seed oil used as illuminant.

Gum - Tree reported to yield a brown, pitchy, shiny gum which does not dissolve but swells up in the water.

Medicinal - Leaves and bark decoction are medicinally useful. Root is used as tonic. Flowers and fruits also medicinally used. Seeds yield a deep-red, thick fatty oil which is also used in cutaneous diseases. Compound oil from bark and capsule is medicinally useful.

Miscellaneous Information

 
It is suitable for avenues as its umbrella shaped crown provides shade.

Suitable for coastal tracts - beach and sandy areas. This is ornamental tree, planted as soil stabilizer and wind break. Planted as a roadside tree.
