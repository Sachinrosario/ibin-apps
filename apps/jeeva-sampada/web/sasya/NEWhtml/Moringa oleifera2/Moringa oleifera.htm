Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A middle-sized tree. Indigenous to North-West India. Occurs in subtropical region of Himalayan foothills and Central Indian hills, tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plain and the Peninsula (moist tropical), dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical).

Grows on alluvial land in or near sandy beds of rivers and streams. Plant does not withstand waterlogging, frost and drought. It grows in all types of soils, except stiff clays and thrives best under the tropical insular climate of S. India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A middle-sized tree with corky bark, soft wood, pungent roots and tomentose young parts. Leaves usually 3- pinnate, rachis slender, thickened and articulated at the base; pinnae and pinnules opposite, deciduous, their rachides very slender, articulated and with a gland at the articulations; ultimate leaflets lateral elliptic, terminal obovate and slightly larger than the lateral ones; nerves obscure. Flowers white, in large puberulous panicles. Calyx-lobes linear-lanceolate, reflexed, puberulous outside. Petals spathulate, veined. Stamens 5, sterile, alternating with 5-7 antherless ones; filaments villous at the base. Ovary oblong, villous; style cylindric. Pods reaching 18" long, 9-ribbed. Seeds 3-angled, the angles winged.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowering during January-April whereas fruits ripen from April-January.

Leaves turn yellow and fall in December-January. New leaves are produced in February-March.


Pollinators:



Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by branch cuttings and direct sowing. Cuttings are preferred. Plants raised from seeds produce fruits of inferior quality. Cuttings of fairly large size, planted to sizeable trees within a few months.Best time for plantation is July.

Tree coppices very well.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diplodia sp. - foot rot, observed in Madras.

Eupterote mollifera Wlk. - Hairy caterpillar, cause defoliation

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, Leaves, Roots, Whole plants and Seeds

Wood is used as fuel, but it is a low quality fuel.

Wood: Specific gravity of wood is 0.3-0.4. Wood is soft and brittle. Wood is used for paper pulp.

Produce and Products:

Other uses:

The scraped root furnishes

Food - Fruits and flowers edible. Used as vegetable. Tender fruits are used as vegetable. They are cut into slices and used in culinary preparations. They are also pickled.

Roots - Roots are used as condiment or garnish.

Fodder - Leaves and twigs used as fodder.

Medicinal - Plant parts are medicinal.

Seed - Oil from seeds is edible and used in cosmetics. Dried seed powder is used as water purifier.

Gum - Stem exudes a gum.

Miscellaneous Information

 
Ornamental tree often cultivated in hedges and homeyards. Fast growing in foothill areas and plains. Grows in a wide range of rainfall (750-2500 mm) and temperatures (10 - 480 C).
