HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennial, dioecoius, strong, climber or ascending tree; roots tuberosus; stems angular, robust, glabrous. It is found in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Tripura, Nagaland, Rajasthan, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tendrils simple, angled, stout, glabrous. Leaves suborbicular, cordate at base, 10-18 cm in diam., glabrous on both surfaces, margin near the base furnished with umbelicate glands, divided to the middle or to the base into 3 or rarely 5 lobes; lobes ovate-oblong or lanceolate, acute or acuminate, diverging, margins entire or faintly toothed; petioles stout, sulcate, glandular at middle and at apex. Flowers whitish yellow, pale yellow or white, tinged with yellow. Male flowers: solitary, peduncles angularly furrowed, more or less pubescent; bracts embracing the flower, broader than long, cordate at base, pubescent or scabrid; calyx hirsute; lobes oblong-lanceolate, acute; corolla pubescent outside and more or less so inside. Female flowers: peduncles with a small bract at middle. Fruits ovoid, 10-15 cm long, red, fleshy, densely covered with raised points c 3 mm long. Seeds numerous, ovoid, compressed, black, sculptured

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: In Ayurveda system of medicine unripe fruit is sour and is astringent to the bowels. In Unani system of medicine seeds are used in cough and chest complaints. It stimulates urine discharges.


Chemistry:
Oleanolic acid and a sterol isolated; a saponin fraction hydrolysed to give a new triterpene – momordic acid (fig. 1). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

A sterol glycoside fraction from tubers showed haemolytic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Columbin isolated from roots of Bangladesh plant collected in june. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Three new momordins I, II and III isolated from roots and characterised as oleanolic acid 3-O-a-l-arabinopyranosyl(1®3)-b-d-glucuronopyranoside, its 28-O-b-glucopyranoside and 3b-hydroxy-lla ,12a -epoxyolean-28,13-olide- 3-O-a -l-arabinopyranosyl(1®3)-b-d-glucuronopyranoside respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Isolation of two momordica saponins I and II from seeds and their characterisation as 3-O-b-d-galactopyranosyl( 1® 2)-[a- l-rhamnopyranosyl(1®3) ]-b-d-glucuronopyranosido-28-O-b-xylopyranosyl(1®3)-b-d-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-[b-d-xylopyranosyl(1®4)]-a-l-rhamnopyranosyl(1®2)-b-d-fucopyranosides of gypsogenin and quillaic acid respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Oleanolic acid glucoside and oleanolic acid arabinoglucoside isolated from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

Chondrillasterol isolated from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Ten new saponins -momordins Ia, Ib, Ic, Id, Ie, IIa, IIb, IIc, IId and IIe - isolated from roots and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.

A trypsin inhibitor, MCCTI-1, with a molecular weight of 3479 Da as determined by mass spectrometry, was isolated from Momordica cochinchinensis seeds with a procedure involving extraction with 5% acetic acid, ammonium sulfate precipitation, ion exchange chromatography on CM-Sepharose and reverse-phase high performance liquid chromatography. (Huang et al, 1999).

A new triterpenoid ester 3,29-di-O-(p-methoxy)benzoylmultiflora-8-ene-3 alpha,29-diol-7-one from the seeds of Momordica cochinchinensis Spreng has been isolated. Its structure was elucidated by spectroscopic evidence and confirmed by X-ray crystallography. (De Shan et al, 2001)


Pharmacology:
b-Carotene from Momordica cochinchinensis  is a good source of provitamin A carotenoids. Severely anemic children might particularly benefit from routine consumption. (Vuongle et al ,2002).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine Fruit, cough (Seed, Puri, 1983); Edible as vegetable (Fruit, Arora, 1981).
