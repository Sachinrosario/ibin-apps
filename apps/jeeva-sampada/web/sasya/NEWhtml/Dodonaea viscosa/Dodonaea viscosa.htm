Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Evergreen shrub or small tree. Found as undergrowth in Pinus roxburghii Sarg. Forests and in dry miscellaneous forests ascending to 6,500 ft.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

 

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A shrub rarely a small tree, with erect twiggy branches often angled; young parts scurfy-puberulous. Leaves subsessile, simple, more or less viscid with a yellowish resinous exudation, 1.5-4 by 0.25-1.5 in. (the breadth very variable), oblanceolate, subacute or shortly apiculate, glabrous, shining, tapering much towards the base.

Flowers unisexual or polygamo-dioecious, inconspicuous. Flowers greenish yellow, small, in short few-flowered axillary spreading cymes; pedicels slender, nodding. Sepals oblong, about equalling the stamens. Anthers oblong-linear, very large. Ovary pilose, style long. Capsules membranous, compressed, slightly broader, with a wide marginal wing notched at the base and apex, viscid, orange-brown. Seeds black, lenticular or sub-globose, compressed, exalbuminous.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diseases:

A virus disease, similar to the spike disease of sandal (Santalum album Linn.) is known to affect the tree. Affected plants show a characteristic bushy appearance. Leaves diminish in size and the internodes are shortened. Flowering and fruiting cease. The diseased condition is characterised by an accumulation of sugars, starch and non-protein nitrogenous constituents and by a deficiency in calcium.

Other uses:

Leaves used in the treatment of wounds, swellings and burns. Used as febrifuge and sudorific in gout and rheumatism. An embrocation of the leaves applied to sprains and bruises.

Bark employed in astringent baths and fomentations.

Leaves and twigs used as manure in Madras.

In Peru, leaves are chewed as a stimulant and are used as an adulterant of coca leaves.

Seeds said to be edible.

Seeds are said to be edible and the fruits were once used as a substitute for true hops (Humulus lupulus Linn.) in making yeast and beer.

Miscellaneous Information

A good hedge plant. It has been used as a sand binder and also for reclaiming marshy lands. The leaves are not browsed by cattle and the plant lends itself to topiary work. Cultivated ornamental with winged fruits. It contains molluscicidal saponins.

Wood: Wood is dark brown, close grained, hard and heavy. 75-80 lb. c ft. It is fairly resistant to termite attack
