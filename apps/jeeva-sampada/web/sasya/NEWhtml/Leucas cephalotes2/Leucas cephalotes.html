HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, stout, annual, branched, hairy herbs up to 1 m. tall, with 4-angled, grooved stem, found in  moist shady places preferably on sandy soil. It is found to occur in Kashmir, Punjab, West Bengal, Assam, Rajasthan, Gujarat and plains of Tamil Nadu, ascending upto 1800 m. in the Himalayas. Found more or less throughout India as a weed in cultivated fields, wastelands and roadsides.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, stout, annual, branched, hairy herbs up to 1 m. tall, with 4-angled, grooved stem. Leaves ovate to ovate-lanceolate, 3-10 x 1-3 cm, acute at apex, cuneate at base, crenate-serrate along margins, membranous, appressed hairy on both surfaces; petioles short.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:   CIMAP: The plant is useful in bronchites,  jaundice, asthma, leucoderma. The fresh juice is useful in external application of scabies. The flowers are used in the form of a syrup for coughs and colds. Leaves are edible as pot herb (Ayurveda). NBPGR:   Plants used as insecticide, antipyretic, flowers are used in cold, leaf-antirheumatic, in psoriasis, scabies and chronic skin eruptions.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:  Plant gave oleanolic acid, ursolic acid and β-sitosterol.  Seeds contain oleic, linoleic, linolentic, palmitic and stearic acids; unsaponifiable powder part gave β-sitosterol and ceryl alcohol.

NBRI: The whole plant (shrub), used medicinally in India, was extracted with 95% alcoholic and the residue from solvent evaporation extracted with 1:1 hexane Et2O; from this was obtained a glycoside [m.p. 296° [a]D-30° (C 1.0, pyridine)] of b-sitosterol, the free sterol itself being obtained separated. The alcoholic water soluble extract showed an alkaloid present in traces but no essential oil could be obtained at any stage. (Bahadur and Sen, 1969). C.A. 1970, 72, 82922a.

The seed oil contains an allenic acid which has been characterized as octadeca-5, 6-dienoic (laballenic) acid (Fig. 1) on the basis of oxidative degradation and spectral studies L. cephalotes has 28% of allenic acid. (Sinha et al., 1978).
