Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A very tall, erect dioecious palm. Native to India. Ideal for semi arid areas, excellent for coastal areas. Found throughout tropical India, especially along the peninsular coast and in West Bengal and Bihar. It has run wild in many parts of India and has also been cultivated, chiefly in the dry or sandy localities of Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Orissa, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal. Several large groves occur in some of these areas. It grows in isolated patches in other states such as Assam, Gujarath, Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Very tall, erect, magnificent dioecious (rarely hermaphrodite) palm, 20-23 m in height and 1.0-2.2 m in girth, sometimes up to 31 m in height, with a fine crown of 30-40 large leaves. Trunk blackish grey, with a dense mass of long rootlets near the ground, often swollen above the middle, when young covered with dry leaves or the bases of petioles, when old marked with the black narrow scars of the petioles. Leaves fan-shaped, palmately divided, 3-5 ft. in diameter, stout, semi-terete, 60-80 segments, shining, folded along the midrib, linear-lanceolate, petioles 2-4 ft. long, semiterete, the margins with hard spinescent serratures.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers dioecious. Spadix very large simply branched, sheathed with numerous open spathes.

Male flower: small, spikes 1-3 at the ends of the branches, cylindric, densely clothed with imbricating bracts; numerous minute second spikelets concealed by the bracts so as to appear immersed in the spike, the flowers coming to the surface one by one they successively open. Stamens 6, filaments connate with the corolla into a stalk.

Female flower: larger, globose perianth fleshy, greatly accrescent. Sepals imbricate. Petals smaller convolute. Staminodes 6-9. Ovary 3-4 celled.

Fruit a large sub-globose brown drupe 6" in diameter or more, with 1-3 obcordate fibrous pyrenes, enclosed by a large perianth.

Seeds oblong, 3 lobed at the top; albumen uniform, hollow.

Floral and seed biology:

Grows naturally, and no particular cultivation is necessary. It requires no artificial irrigation or manuring. It is generally propagated by direct sowing. Polyembryony and twin-seedlings have been reported in the palm.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood used as fuel. Fibres from the petiole and its base also used as fuel.

Wood: Wood of the trunk is very hard and durable, and resists salt water. It comprises an inner soft and light brown portion with fibro-vascular bundles and outer black and hard portion. The central portion is chiefly composed of cellular tissues which often decay in old palms, resulting a hollow tube. With age the wood becomes harder.

Timber is heavy (wt. 838-1139 kg/m3), strong, durable and very hard but poor in toughness.

Wood is largely used for pillars, posts, poles, foot-bridges, fencing, well-sweeps, rafters , etc. Wood used for water pipes and channels, and. Split wood halves used as water channels. The wood also used in boat construction.Timber resists salt water. It is used for rafters.

Harvesting Practices:


The exploitable age is probably 50-60 years; less in some localities. It is usual to cut only those trees which are past tapping. Tapping does not injure the wood. The timber of the female tree is reported to be superior to that of the male.

Other uses:

Sap - Jaggery obtained by boiling the juice immediately. A considerable quantity of jaggery is consumed as food by the poorer classes in India.

Candy - Sometimes candy is also prepared. It is prepared directly from the sap.

Sugar - About 8% by weight of brown sugar can be recovered from the palm sap.

The tree yields a dark, vitreous gum of the insoluble type. It is reported to be used as emollient in indurations.

Beverage - This palm is extensively tapped for the sweet sap which is fermented into a country liquor.

Neera - Neera (a sugar exudate) used as a drink and converted into palm sugar or jaggery, as well as vinegar.

Molasses - Molasses, a waste product, is nutritious and rich in sucrose, minerals and vitamins. It can be consumed directly too. It is utilized in the preparation of golden syrup, confectionery syrup, fruit preserves and cattle feed.

Fruits - Fruits soft, tender and relished in summer. Tender shoots of 2-3 month old palm are edible. Sometimes flour is made from them. Fruit roasted and eaten.

Food - The flesh of the young roots eaten. Young seedlings are also eaten and yield a good flour when ground.

Leaves - Large fan shaped leaves used for thatching and in manufacture of fancy and utility articles such as fans, mats, hats, bags, umbrellas, buckets, receptacles for liquids, baskets, flutes, toys, plates, leaf rope, fencing and raincoats. Sometimes they are bleached, coloured or painted to improve the quality and colour of the product. Also made into olas or writing paper sheets, the writing being done upon them with a stylus.Leaves used for thatching and olas or writing paper. (Perhaps the oldest form, a stylus used for writing. Leaf base fibre used for making brushes. Split leaves used for weaving mats, baskets, etc.

Fibre - It is the heaviest fibre that can be handled satisfactorily in all methods of manufacture, such as twisted in wire, pegged, stapled, pitch-set and wire-drawn. From the base of leaves fiber is collected and used in the manufacture of brushes of different shapes, brooms, ropes, twines, fencing, sandals, reapers, cradles, doormats, mats for [packing, cushions, different types of baskets, hats, etc. Split leaves are woven into mats, baskets, etc. also employed for lacing of cots and chairs, tying up faggots, cattle-yokes, and for other agricultural purposes. The stem or tar fibre is used for making fishtraps. The fibrous web at the base of young leaves is used for straining toddy and to make torches.

Paper pulp - The petiole, devoid of coarse and short edges, is a cheap and potential raw material for paper pulp.

Midribs - Midribs of the leaf are used in the manufacture of brooms.

Root, spathe, green leaves, bark, flower possess medicinal value.

Seedling is also edible.
Yields odiyal flour.
