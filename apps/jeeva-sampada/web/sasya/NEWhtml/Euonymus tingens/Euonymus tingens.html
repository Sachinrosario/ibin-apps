DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Small trees, up to 2 m high; branchlets short, thick. Leaves ovate to ovate lanceolate, 2-7 x 2-3 cm; acute at apex, cuneate at base, dark green above, pale beneath, serrate.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers in 3-7-flowered, dichotomously branched, condensed cymes. Sepals 5, ovate, ciliate.  Petals 5,  ovate. Fruits globose, size, colour, Capsules, 3-5-angled. Seeds oblong.
CIMAP:
    Inflorescence: Peduncled cymose; Flower: Hermaphrodite, regular ; Calyx:  Calyx 4-5 fid, spreading, ; Corolla: Petals 4, shortly clawed, many orbicular, coarsely crenulate, yellowish with purple veins ;Androecium: Stamens 4-5, inserted on the disk, anthers broad, 2-celled; Gynoecium: Ovary sunk in the disk, 3-5 celled, ovules 2 in each cell, attached to inner angle ; Fruit: Capsule 3 lobed, coriaceous; Seed: Covered by aril, 1. Flowering & Fruiting : April- June.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Root and bark are used in dysentery and headache. Roots and leaves is useful in diseases of the eye. Red and yellow powder of inner bark are also used in vermilion. 


Chemistry:
An orange red benzoquinone derivative – tingenone, mp. 171°C – isolated from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Tingenone (fig. 1) and 20-hydroxytingenone isolated from nark; crystal structure of tingenone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Oleanen-3,2’-diol isolated from Euonymus    tingens  along with some known triterpenes guttpercha dulcitol and dimethyl terephthalate. (Banskota et al, 1995) MAPA, 1996, 9603-1559.
