DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herb, 60-100 cm long, glaucous, with characteristic aromatic smell. Stem terete, striate, polished, almost solid. Leaves divided into filiform segments, 1.0-3.0 cm long, sessile. Stamens much longer than petals. Ovary oblong. Fruits variable in length, c 0.4-0.6 cm, oblong with strong ridges and a conspicuous, vittae between whole length of carpel.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine seeds are used as laxative, aphrodisiac, stomachic, appetiser, anthelmintic and alexiteric. It also cures “tridosha”, eye diseases, burning sensation, fever, thirst and wounds. It is useful in dysentery, biliousness and leprosy. In Unani system of medicine seeds are used as carminative, galactagogue, diuretic and stimulant. It is also useful in chest, spleen, kidney diseases, headache, menorrhoea, cough and asthma. Roots are used as purgative and leaves as diuretic.
Parts used: Fruit-anticholerin, antigonorrhoeic, stimulant, stomachic, carminative, emmenagogue, galactogogue, expectorant, anodyne, aphrodisiac, cardiotonic, diaphoretic, restorative, vermicidal, in aerophagia, amenorrhoea, enuresis, hernia, nausea.

The extractable amount of oils from fennel was analyzed for their carotenoid, tocopherol and fatty acid content. Beside application of traditional methods of extraction, supercritical CO2 was used alone or with propane to prepare the oils. The result revealed greatly significant differences between extraction methods in the recovery of biologically active compounds of plant oils. The SFE-prepared fractions and final products are of special interest from the therapeutics and human nutrition points of view. (Then et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 242581c.

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

Essential fennel oils produced by supercritical CO2 extraction resp. steam distillation from raw material were analyzed by HPLC and GC for trans-anethole, cis-anethole, fenchone, estragole, p-anisaldehyde, limonene and a-pinene. It was shown that CO2 extracts corresponds to a large extent in their composition with steam-distilled oils of the same batch. However they contained considerably less p-anisaldehyde and cis-anethole, which are formed from trans-anethole in the presence of light and heat and are thereof considered as an indicator for inferior quality. Cis-anethole has been reported to be about 15 times more toxic than trans-anethole. These results offer evidence that fennel CO2 extracts are if better quality and of less toxicity than fennel steam-distilled oils. (Ehlers et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 251413w.

PHARMACOGNOSY

Any Other

The deodorant was prepared by extracting fennel with a mixed solution of methanol and water or ethanol and water. (Kim et al, 1997). C.A. 2000, 133, 198441a
The repellent activity of materials derived from the methanol extract of fruits from Foeniculum vulgare against hungry Aedes aegypti females was examined using skin and patch tests and compared with that of the commercial N,N-diethyl-m-toluamide (deet) and (Z)-9-octadecenoic acid. The biologically active constituents of the Foeniculum fruits were characterized as (+)-fenchone and (E)-9-octadecenoic acid by spectroscopic analyses. Responses varied according to compound, dose, and exposure time. In a skin test with female mosquitoes, at a dose of 0.4 mg/cm(2), (+)-fenchone and (Z)-9-octadecenoic acid exhibited moderate repellent activity at 30 min after treatment, whereas deet provided >1 h of protection against adult mosquitoes at 0.2 mg/cm(2). (Z)-9-Octadecenoic acid was a more potent repellent agent than (E)-9-octadecenoic acid. (+)-Fenchone and (E)-9-octadecenoic acid merit further study as potential mosquito repellent agents or as lead compounds. (Kim et al, 2002).
The insecticidal activities of materials derived from the fruit of fennel, Foeniculum vulgare, against adults of Sitophilus oryzae, Callosobruchus chinensis and Lasioderma serricorne were examined using direct contact application and fumigation methods. The biologically active constituents of the Foeniculum fruits were characterized as the phenylpropenes (E)-anethole and estragole, and the monoterpene (+)-fenchone, by spectroscopic analysis. Responses varied with insect species, compound, dose and exposure time. In a filter paper diffusion test, estragole at 0.168 mg cm-2 caused 91% mortality to S oryzae adults within 1 day after treatment (DAT), whereas (+)-fenchone and (E)-anethole gave over 90% mortality at 2 and 4 DAT, respectively. Against C. chinensis adults, all test compounds revealed potent insecticidal activities at 0.021 mg cm-2 at 2 DAT. Against L serricorne adults at 0.105 mg cm-2, (E)-anethole gave 100% mortality at 1 DAT, whereas 90 and 60% mortality at 4 DAT was achieved with estragole and (+)-fenchone, respectively. In a fumigation test, the compounds were much more effective against adults of S. oryzae, C. chinensis and L. serricorne in closed cups than in open ones, indicating that the insecticidal activity of test compounds was largely attributable to fumigant action. As naturally occurring insect-control agents, the F vulgare fruit-derived materials described could be useful for managing field populations of S oryzae, C. chinensis and L. serricorne. (Kim and Ahn, 2001).
