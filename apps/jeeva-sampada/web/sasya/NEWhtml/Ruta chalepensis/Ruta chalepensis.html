HABIT & HABITAT
It is a erect glauscent herb, Known to be cultivated in many parts of the country. And it also occurs in MEDITERRANEAN region, Alantic Islands, S. Europe.  
Availability :

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, glaucescent herb, 60-70 cm high; stem more or less angular. Leaves pinnatisect, oblong, 2.5-8.5 cm long; leaflets heteromorphic, obovate, oblong-lanceolate or elliptic, 5-15 x 2-3 mm, obtuse or rounded at apex, margin entire or obscurely crenate, subsessile. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in dichasial or often in lateral cymes develop in a helicoid fashion, glabrous. Sepals 4(5), foliaceous, broadly ovate acuminate at apex, entire or obscurely crenate along margins, green. Petals 4(5), spathulate, obtuse, hooded at apex, gibbous adaxially, margins irregularly fimbriate. Capsules 4-5 lobed, 6-8 mm across, lobes acute, dehiscing inwardly at apex. Seeds 1-2 per locule, ca 2 mm long, angled, pitted brown, it flowers and fruits between Sept-April.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 The whole plant is bitter, laxative, tonic, digestive, diuretic, emmanagogue and antiphrodisiac. It yields an essential oil with properties similar to that of ‘Garden rue Oil’. Decoction of leaves is used in fever. In large doses it acts as an acronarcotic poison. The essential oil is used in hysteria, ammenorrhoea and epilepsy. Leaves are used in salads and as flavouring agents.


Chemistry:

·        Rutamarin, kokusaginine, xanthotoxin, chalepensin and skimmianine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Chalepensin, chalepin and coumarin isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Isoimperatorin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new alkaloid – taifine – isolated and characterized as 9-ethyl-7-methoxyfuro [2,3-b]quinolone -4.(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Detection of 2-undecanone (38.1), 2-nonanyl acetate (20.9), 2-nonanone (17.2) and 2-decanyl acetate (5.6%) in essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Two new furoquinolone alkaloids – isotaifine and 8-methoxytaifine-isolated and their structures  elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Bergapten, isopimpinellin, xanthotoxin, chalepensin, heliettin, rutamarin, kokusaginine, skimmianine and arborinine isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Detection of 2-undecanone, 2-tridecanone, elemol, b-eudesmol and chalepensin in Saudi essential oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new coumarin-angustifolin-isolated from aerial parts along with scoparone, 6, 7,8-trimethoxycoumarin and an alkaloid graveolin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        In addition, skimmianine, kokusaginine, graveoline, chalepin and chalepensin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Four shikimate metabolites – moskachans A, B, C and D – isolated from aerial parts and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        In addition, psoralen, bergapten, xanthotoxin, benahorin, isoimperatorin, imperatorin, heraclenol, angustifolin, 7-demethylrutacultin, escoparone, 6,7,8-trimethoxycoumarin, methyl nonyl ketone, b-sitosterol, its glucoside and graveoline identified. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Chalepin and rutamarin synthesized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Isolation of new alkaloid-chaloridone – and a new coumarin – rutalpinin – from roots and determination of their structures. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Crystal structure of angustifolin determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        A new olefinic derivative – 8-(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl) oct-1-ene – along with piperonylacetone identified in essential oil of Malaysian plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Isotaifine synthesized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Steam distilled essential oil from aerial parts of Ruta chalepensis was analysed by Ge and Ge/Ms. Twenty four compounds were identified representing 93.4% of the oil with 2-undecanone (66.49%) and 2-nonanone (16.24%) as major constituents. (Baser et. al., 1996).

·        Ruta chalepensis contained palmatic acid, linoleic acid. (Husain and Devi, 1999).

·        The roots of Ruta chalepensis, collected from the northen Saudi desert yielded two new quinoline alkaloids namely, 2-[6’-C2H-benzo[d]1’’, 3’’-dioxolen-5’’-yl) hexyl]- hydroquinolin-4-one and 2-[6’-(2H-benzo[1] 1’’, 3’’-dioxolen-5’’-7L)-hexyl]-4-methoxy quinoline. (Sayed et. al., 2000).

·        The essential oil of aerial parts of Ruta graveolens was obtained by ydrodistillation with a 0.74% yield on a dry weight basis. Thirty-eight components were identified by GC and GC-MS analyses. 2-Ketones predominated in the essential oil, with undecan-2-one (46.8%) and nonan-2-one (18.8%) as the main constituents. The essential oil and some of its constituents were tested for their allelopathic activity in vitro on radish germination and radicle growth in light and darkness. The essential oil and some of its minor constituents were effective and dose-dependent inhibitors of both the germination and radicle growth; 2-ketones are not active. The possible allelopathic activity of rue essential oil and some its isolated constituents is reported. (De Feom et al., 2002)

·        Conjugate additions of o-iodoanilines and methyl anthranilates to acetylenic sulfones. A new route to quinolones including first syntheses of two alkaloids from the medicinal herb Ruta chalepensis (Back et al., 2003)

·        Bioassay-directed isolation of antifungal compounds from an ethyl acetate extract of Ruta graveolens leaves yielded two furanocoumarins, one quinoline alkaloid, and four quinolone alkaloids, including a novel compound, 1-methyl-2-[6'-(3' ',4' '-methylenedioxyphenyl)hexyl]-4-quinolone. The (1)H and (13)C NMR assignments of the new compound are reported. Antifungal activities of the isolated compounds, together with 7-hydroxycoumarin, 4-hydroxycoumarin, and 7-methoxycoumarin, which are known to occur in Rutaceae species, were evaluated by bioautography and microbioassay. Four of the alkaloids had moderate activity against Colletotrichum species, including a benomyl-resistant C. acutatum. These compounds and the furanocoumarins 5- and 8-methoxypsoralen had moderate activity against Fusarium oxysporum. The novel quinolone alkaloid was highly active against Botrytis cinerea. Phomopsis species were much more sensitive to most of the compounds, with P. viticola being highly sensitive to all of the compounds. (Oliva et al., 2003)

Pharmacology:

·        Chalepensin and chalepin showed antibacterial activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Coumarin gradually reduced canine obstructive lymphoedema by 20.0-30.0% over 8 mo, it reduced excess water in oedematous limbs. As a secondary event, fibrosis in skin is also reduced, presumably by an increase in collagenase activity of mononuclear phagocytes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

·        Dried leaf infusions of Ruta chalepensis L. (Rutaceae), 'rue', 'ruda', were found to cause perinatal changes in mice, at daily doses of 0.16, 0.80 and 1.60 g/kg, administered po from 1 to 14 days post coitum. Significant decreases in the appearance time of physical signs, righting reflex and cliff avoidance together with minus scores in string test and swimming ability were observed. Moreover, histological studies showed progressive angiogenic development on placenta blood supply and weakness at blood barrier in brain, thymus and pery-lymph vestibule. We found out that the results tend to confirm the embryotoxic effect of the plant and its harmful use. (de Sa et al., 2000).
