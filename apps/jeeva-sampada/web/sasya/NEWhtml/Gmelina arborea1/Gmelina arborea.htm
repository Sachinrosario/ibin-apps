Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Moderate sized unarmed deciduous tree. Moist forests found in tropical regions with high rainfall areas of Western Ghats, West Bengal and Assam (wet tropical), Tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical).

Found upto 1,000 m. The tree withstands up to 520 C, but can be severely injured by frost. It grows satisfactorily on sites that receive rainfall from 750mm to 4,500 mm annually. At least some provenances have drought tolerance: in East Africa, gmelina is grown where the dry season is 7 months long. Also, gmelina is one of the plantation species that can be grown in the northern Philippines with its 6-month dry season.

Gmelina is fairly adaptable and survives well on a wide range of soil types: acid soils, calcareous loams, and lateritic soils. Light demender, moderately frost hardy, intolerant of excessive drought. It cannot withstand waterlogging and it gets badly stunted on very thin soils, impermeable soil layers, highly leached acid soils, or dry sand. It produces best in moist, base-rich, well-drained alluvium. Suitable for village commons.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Moderate sized, unarmed, deciduous tree. In well-thinned plantations under optimum conditions it may reach heights of about 30 m and stem diameter over 60 cm, but it is more usually about 20 m with a clear bole of 6-9 m. In the open, however, it develops low, heavy branches, a wide crown, and a strong bark greyish yellow, rather corky. Branchlets and young parts clothed with fine white mealy pubescence. Leaves 4-8 by 3-6" broadly ovate, acuminate, entire, glabrous above when mature, stellately fulvous-tomentose beneath, base cordate or sometimes truncate and shortly cuneate. Petioles 2-3" long, cylindric, puberulous, glandular at the top. Flowers appearing with or sometimes before young leaves, usually in small cymes of about 3 flowers arranged along the branches of densely fulvous-hairy panicle reaching 12" long; buds clavate, angular; bracts linear-lanceolate. Calyx broadly companulate, densely fulvous-hairy, teeth 5, small, triangular, acute. Corolla brownish-yellow, densely hairy outside, 5-lobed, 2-lipped; upper lip rather more than 3/8" long, deeply divided into 2 oblong, obtuse lobes; lower lobe nearly 1" long, 3-lobed, the middle lobe projecting forward, ovate, subobtuse, with irregularly crenulate margin, much longer and broader than the obovate rounded lateral lobes. Drupe ovoid or pyriform, smooth, orange-yellow when ripe.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Grown by entire transplanting and direct sowing. Gmelina is very easy to handle and simple to establish. It can be propagated by seed, cuttings or by budding or grafting. Tubed seedlings are widely used, but in several countries direct seedling has proved cheap and quick if rainfall is reliable. A spacing of 2m X 2m has been recommended for fuelwood plantations in Malaysia. Gmelina can be established among agricultural crops (the taungya system) such as peanuts (groundnuts), cashew, tobacco, corn and beans. It then benefits from better manufacturing and care. However, it grows too rapidly for longer-term crops such as cassava.

Seeds are soaked in water 24 hours (not necessary if there is irrigation or daily rainfall). Seeds lose viability within a year. Weeding is essential during first year; gmelina cannot compete with aggressive grasses or vines. Tree well adapted as coppice.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Whole plants, bark, fruits, flowers, leaves and root.

Uses:

Wood burns quickly.Charcoal made burns well without much smoke but leaves much ash. It is a firewood crop, output is 30 m3/ ha/ yr. Wood useful for particle board, plywood cases, pit props, matches and sawn timber for light construction, general carpentry, packing and furniture.Timber used for construction work, planking, furniture, cabinet work, venetial blinds, panelling, carts, lacquered boxes, boat building, agricultural implements, turnery, toys, artificial limbs, guns, rifles, musical instruments, picture frames, stethoscopes, tight cooperage, carriages, printing blocks, shafts, axles, jute bobbins, loom parts, callipers, mine props, etc.

Wood gives paper pulp. This gives average yields of paper with properties superior to those obtainable from most hardwood pulps. Wood also suitable for tea chest plywood, paper making, match sticks, match boxes. Yields utility timber.


Harvesting Practices: Coppice rotation for fuel is 5 years.

Produce and Products: Timber

Chemistry:


Processing Techniques:

Wood seasons well without cracking or warping but is slow to dry both in the open and in the kiln. green conversion and open stacking with crossers under cover are recommended.

 

Other uses:

Flowers provide high quality honey because of abundant nectar.

Leaves good fodder. Some plantations have been destroyed by browsing livestock. The palatability of the foliage may be a drawback for establishing gmelina woodlots near villages.

Fruits, flowers, leaves, root and bark used in native medicine for treatment of cough, headache, stomachache, laxative, nerve tonic and blood diseases.

Tree casts dense shade.

Leaves are sometimes used as feed for eri silkworms and also considered to be good fodder for cattle.

Miscellaneous Information

In vitro rooted shoots have been obtained through axillary bud culture. Multiple shoots and hardened plantlets have been obtained from single node explants of mature tree.

Wood: Wood creamy white to pale yellowish-grey or buff turning to yellowish-brown on exposure, with roe and mottle figuring. Soft to moderately hard, strong, light to moderately heavy, average weight 515 kg/m3 (32 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; lustrous with a smooth feel; medium coarse-textured with straight to wavy grain.

A diffuse-porous to semi-ring-porous wood. Growth rings distinct to indistinct, when distinct delimited by large and somewhat numerous pores and a fine line of soft tissue. Pores large to moderately large, fairly distinct to the eye, few to moderately numerous, mostly solitary and in short radial multiples of 2-3, tyloses abundant; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly paratracheal, vasicentric forming a light-coloured thin sheath round the pores, prominent under the lens and often also as a fine line delimiting the growth rings. Rays broad to moderately broad, distinct to eye, few, rather widely spaced.
