HABIT & HABITAT:

A large ornamental shrub or small tree found mostly in the coastal regions of Deccan Peninsula, eastern parts of India and the Andaman Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small tree; bark grayish with vertical furrows, young branches terete, pale when dry. Leaves elliptic or ovate, subacute or shortly and bluntly acuminate, glabrous, dark green and polished above, paler beneath, usually attenuated but sometimes rounded at the base, margins slightly revolute; midrib prominent; lateral nerves obscure. Flowers numerous, in umbellate cymes from the axils of fallen leaves on the old wood; peduncles several together, elongating in fruit, ultimate pedicels very slender; buds pyriform. Calyx companulate before expansion (saucer-shaped when expanded), limb truncate or 4-toothed; teeth, when present, small, triangular. Disk without or with very faint rays. Berry globose, purplish-black, crowned, though rather inconspicuously with the calyx limb, 1- (less commonly 2) seeded.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, Fruits and leaves.

Fuel: Wood yields charcoal of good quality.

Wood used for house posts and rafters, light axe handles, pestles, combs, walking sticks and decorative work. It may be used as a substitute for boxwood.

Other uses:

Fruits pulpy, astringent and edible. The leaves possess cooling and astringent properties and given in leucorrhoea and gonorrhea. A lotion prepared from them, used in eye troubles. The leaves and bark applied to bruises. The leaves yield a yellow dye, which gives beautiful light shades; in combination with myrobalans and sappanwood it produces a bright red tinge much used for dying mats.
Ornamental tree.

Miscellaneous Information

Wood: Sapwood greyish brown, turns dark brown on exposure; heartwood small, darker than sapwood. Hard, heavy, 62 lb./ c ft., fine textured, close grained and durable.
