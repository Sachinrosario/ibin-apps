Preparation and preservation of Almond Syrup

Almond syrup is a popular beverage in North India and other hot and dry places. It is considered a tonic which quenches the thirst and refreshes the body. In places like Delhi and the Punjab, this is very much in demand.
Selection of the raw material: Almonds of good quality, which do not give kernels of bitter taste, are selected and the shell removed by breaking with a hammer.

Preliminary treatment: Soak the almond kernals in almost boiling water for some time and remove the loosened peel.

Recipe: Sugar, 40 lb; almond kernels, 1,1/2 lb.; citric or tartaric acid, ¼ oz.; essence (Kewra or rose), cardamom, saffron, as desired.

Preparation of milk of almond: Grind the peeled almond kernels very finely in a mortar and pestle using small quantities of water during the grinding which is thereby facilitated. Strain the thick paste of almond kernels through a coarse muslin cloth and get milk of almond. Repeat the processes of grinding and straining 2-3 times.

Preparation of sugar syrup: Mix the sugar with r/3 its quantity of water and boil until the temperature of the boiling syrup reaches 2210F. Allow the syrup to cool.

Mixing of the ingredients: Mix the milk of almond with the sugar syrup prepared in the manner described above and boil the mixture till the temperature reaches 223-2240F. Allow the syrup to cool.

Colouring and flavouring: Add the desired additives viz., essence (Kewra or rose or both), saffron, cardamom when the syrup is sufficiently cooled down and fill the finished product in thoroughly cleaned and sterilized bottles.

Preservation: Place the filled bottles in hot water (2000F) for 25-30 minutes or add sodium benzoate (dissolved in a small quantity of water) at the rate of 1,1/2 oz. Per 100 pounds of the finished product and pack in bottles which should then be made air-tight.

Storage and uses: Keep the filled bottles in a cool dry place. Dilute the squash 5-6 times with ice cold water for use as a refreshing drink.

Almonds (Prunus amygdalus, Batsch) are extensively cultivated in USA, Spain, Italy, Iran, Morocco and Portugal. In India, almonds are mostly cultivated in Kashmir hilly areas of Himachal Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh.

The fully mature almond kernels are considered as a high quality ingredient in the manufacure of confectionery and bakery products. The kernels may be processed as raw, dry roasted or roasted in oil or converted into powder, almond butter, almond oil and almond paste. The almond paste is generally prepared by grinding the blanced, peeled kernels with requistic quantity of water and sugar. The paste finds numerous applications in bakery products, confectionery and in the preparation of refreshing beverages.

Ready-to-serve (RTS) Beverage: The samples of almond paste at different storage intervals were made into RTS beverages by diluting with chilled water to 15-160Brix. The scoring pattern for the sensory attributes of the RTS beverage made from pastes stored for different periods, indicates only a very slight change in the sensory quality of the beverage owing to storage effect. The beverage was, however, found to be relished well by the panelists thoughout the storage period.
The milky appearance and mild almond flavour and taste of the beverage was imparted by the immature almond kernals component of the paste. The paste samples containing increased proportion of the kernel therefore found to receive higher score in respect of colour and flavour. These paste samples containing 30 and 35% almond component were, however, generally liked most as evident from the taste and overall acceptability score.

The green almonds may thus be advantageously preserved and stored as kernal paste for future use in the event of early harvest for climatic economic reasons.
