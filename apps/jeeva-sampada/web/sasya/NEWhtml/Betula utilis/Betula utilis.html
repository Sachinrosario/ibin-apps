HABIT & HABITAT:
Small deciduous tree or shrub, it is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Assam, Sikkim at altitude 2334- 4667 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Bark white with conspicuous long horizontal lenticels, inner layers pink, peeling off in papery flakes; twigs dotted with yellow resinous drops; young leaves and shoots silky. Leaves ovate, 5–10 cm long, sharply irregularly serrate, sticky when young with yellow resinous scales. Flowers arranged in catkins and spikes. Fruits narrow, nut with wing.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Male and female borne on separate spikes ;  Male Flower:  Pendulous cylindrical catkins, 4lobed perianth, 2-12 stamens, reddish ; Female Flower: with a naked ovary of 2 fused carpels with two long thread like styles ; Fruit: Nutlets with 3 lobed much longer bract ; Seed: 1, winged. Flowering & Fruiting : April-May. Flower are  pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
In Ayurveda system of medicine bark is used as tonic, pungent, alexiteric. It is also useful in convulsions, bronchitis, diseases of blood, ear and leprosy. In Unani system of medicine bark is used in earache.


Chemistry:
Betulin, lupeol, oleanolic acid, acetyl oleanolic acid and leucocyanidin from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Lupeol, betulin, oleanolic acid and betulic acid isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Isolation and structure of karachic acid (fig. 1) from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.


Toxicology:
Atmospheric surveys carried out in different parts of India reveal that Betula utilis is important source of inhalant allergens particularly in the coastal areas of the country. (Singh and Kumar, 2002).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine antiseptic (Bark, Malhotra and Balodi, 1984); burns, cuts (Resin, Shah, 1982; Shah and Joshi, 1971); Contraceptive (Resin Shah, 1982; Shah and Joshi, 1971); Ear complaints, hysteria, Jaundice (Bark, Malhotra and Balodi, 1984); Wounds (Bark, Negi et al, 1985); veterinary aliment canal disease (Part not mentioned, Uniyal, 1968); Bridge-making (Twigs, Koelz, 1979); broom, plates thatch (Whole plant, twigs, Bark, Rawat and Pangtey, 1987); writing purpose ancient times (Bark, Rawat and Pangtey, 1987); symbolic in religious ceremonies (Whole plant, Rawat and Pangtey, 1987)
