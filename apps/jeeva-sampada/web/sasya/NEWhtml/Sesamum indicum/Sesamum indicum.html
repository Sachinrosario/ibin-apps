Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Sesame is an herbaceous annual which has a wide distribution covering tropical Africa, Madagascar, Arabia, India, Sri Lanka, tropical Australia and a few of the eastern islands of the Malayan Archipelago. Africa is considered to be the primary centre of origin of this crop because of the presence of its diverse wild species in that continent. India is the secondary centre of origin other than Japan. India ranks first, both in the area and production of sesamum in the world. The annual area under cultivation in India is about 2-5 million hectares (45 per cent of the world hectarage) and the total production is nearly 52 thousand tonnes. Sesame is distrubuted throughout India. Sesamum is grown on 21 lakh hectares in only eight states, viz. Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and Orissa. Among other states only Karnataka has a sizable area under sesamum. In the remaining states it is grown only on a small area and hence is a very minor crop there.

Sesamum grows in the plains and at elevations up to 1,200 m. It cannot stand frost, continuous heavy rain or prolonged drought. It is grown in sandy-loam to heavy black soils. Most of the crop is confined to lighter soils and its cultivation in heavy soils is limited to certain regions. The pH of sesamum growing soils ranges from 5.5 to 8.2 (1).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Sesamum is an erect and annual herb. The plant is simple or branched. Stems of early cultivors are unbranched, late maturing cultivors show pronounced branching, stems square, longitudinally furrowed, green, occasionally purple. The leaves are opposite below and alternate above. Leaves at the top are lanceolate where as the leaves below are ovate, toothed or lobed with varying shapes. Leaves hairy on both sides, margins ciliate, exstipulate, ovate, sometimes palmately lobed or palmately compound; petiole about 5 cm long, lamina 8-15 x 6-10 cm, coarsely serrate. Upper leaves alternate or sub-opposite, lanceolate, entire or with 1-few coarse teeth, petiole 1-2 cm long; lamina 5-13 x 1-3 cm (11). The flowers are borne in the leaf axils in two’s or three’s and often solitary and are variously coloured starting from pure white to purple or to deep violet (3).

Flowers are zygomorphic, in axils of upper leaves, solitary, occasionally two or three together. Peduncle short, about 5 mm long with an extra-floral nectary on either side at base, yellow or black, representing aborted flowers, each subtended by a linear bract, about 1 cm long. Calyx 6-8 mm long, glandular hairy, 5-partite to near base, lobes narrow, acuminate. Corolla tubular campanulate, 3-4 cm long, widening upwards, 2-lipped, 5-lobed with middle lower lobe longest, pubescent outside, white, pink or purplish with yellow or purple blotches, spots and stripes on inner surface. Stamens didynamous, inserted near base of corolla, 2 longer stamens 1.5-2 cm long, 2 shorter stamens 1.0-1.5 cm long; filaments slender, basifixed, whitish in colour, anthers lying against inner upper lip of corolla, awl-shaped, pale yellow, with short white or brownish beak. Ovary superior, sessile, many-ovuled, often densely hairy, of 2 united carpels becoming 4-8-locular by intrusive growth of parietal placentas, style slender, cream coloured, 1.5-2 cm long, stigma bifid, hairy, lying between 2 pairs of anthers. Nectiferous disc surrounding ovary. Sepaloid flowers with abnormal internal organs have been recorded (11). The fruit is a capsule, erect and oblong. The capsule contains numerous small ovate seeds (3). The testa may be smooth or reticulate and may be white, yellow, reddish-brown or black. The seed is dicotyledonous, albuminous and oleaginous.

Seeds are small, numerous, 3 x 1.5 mm, ovate, smooth or reticulate, white, yellow, grey, red, brown or black. 100 seeds weigh about 0.3 g. Contained in an errect oblong, obtusely tetraganous capsule (2)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY


Back to Top

Breeding System :
Sesame is mainly self-pollinated crop, though cross-pollination takes place to a limited extent of 5-6 per cent. The anthesis takes place between 2 a.m. to 4 a.m., in the morning and the maximum dehiscence is between 3 a.m to 4 a.m. The flowers open between 5 a.m. to 8.a.m. It takes nearly 2 hours for the corolla to open after the dehiscence of the anthers. The stigma becomes receptive, at the same time as the dehiscence of the anthers and remains so till 8 a.m. Fertilization takes place in about 6-12 hours after pollination (3).

Phenology :
In early mansoon areas, crop is sown in the month of April- May and in other areas June-July. The plants flower in about 25 days after sowing. When the flowers are solitary in the leaf axils the opening of the flowers is in an acropetal succession or racemose, when they are in clusters of two or threes the opening is in a cymose order.

Back to Top

 

Pollinators:
Pollen is usually shed before the flowers open. The flowers open early in the morning and at the same time the bifid stigma separates and becomes receptive and is copiously covered with pollen from the stamens. Consequently the great majority of the flowers are self-pollinated. The corolla and stamens are shed 15-20 hours after the flowers open. Bagged flowers set seeds. The flowers are visited by insects so that a negligible amount of cross-pollination can occur (11).

Seed Biology :
Seeds are small, numerous, 3 x 1.5 mm, ovate, smooth or reticulate, white, yellow, grey, red, brown or black. 100 seeds weigh about 0.3 g. Contained in an errect oblong, obtusely tetraganous capsule (2). Before sowing, seeds are treated with fungicides to control seed borne diseases. Seed requirement for one hectare is around 3-5 kg. Seeds should be sown to a depth of 2-3 cm. Spacing adapted is 25-35 cm between the rows and 10-20 cm between the plants in the row.

Back to Top

 

Seedling Germination :
Soaking of seed in water is helpful for uniform germination in dry land condition. The seeds should be placed shallow to get proper germination and plant stand. Seedlings appear above the ground in about 3-10 days.

Cultivation:

Sesamum grows in the plains and at elevations up to 1,200 m. It cannot with stand frost, continuous heavy rain or prolonged drought. In India, seasmum is grown in three seasons, viz. kharif, semi-rabi and summer. Kharif sesamum is grown both as pure and mixed with other crops, whereas the semi-rabi and summer crops are taken as pure. The kharif crop occupies over 70 per cent of the area, whereas the semi-rabi and summer crops occupy 20 per cent and 10 per cent area respectively.


The kharif sesamum is sown in June-July with the onset of the monsoon and is harvested in December-January. The kharif and semi-rabi crops are entirely rainfed, whereas summer crop is grown under irrigation. The yield of the kharif crop is poor, whereas those of the semi-rabi and summer crops are high, as they are grown in rich soils and under better management. The preparatory cultivation for the kharif crop is usually not thorough. For the semi-rabi and summer crops, the land is ploughed and harrowed repeatedly to secure a clean and fine seedbed. Except in Maharashtra and Gujarat, where line sowing is practiced, in all other states it is broadcasted. The recommended spacing for the line-sown crop in different regions, however, range from 25 to 35 cm between the rows and 10 to 20 cm between the plants in the row. The seed being small, it is often mixed with sand to ensure its even distribution and the drill is operated rather shallow to avoid deep sowing. After sowing, the seed is covered lightly with a brush harrow. The seed-rate varies from 3 to 5 kg per hectare. The seed should be treated with Captan or Thiram at the rate of 3 g per kg of seed to control seed-borne diseases.

The rainfed crop is weeded and hoed once or twice and the irrigated crop is weeded often. Wherever the weed control with mechanical means is a problem, chemical weed control with pre-emergence application of Lasso at the rate of 3 litres/ha is recommended. The crop is generally recommended for different regions as 25-30 kg of N, 20-30 kg of P2O5 and 0-20 kg of K2O per hectare (1).

The varieties differ not only in respect of the season of growth and the time of maturity but also in the number of capsules formed in the leaf axils, the habit of growth (Single-stemmed or branched) and the size, colour and oil content of the seeds. MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

1. Gallfy (Asphondylia sesami)

Symptoms: Maggotes feed inside the flower and produce galls. The damaged flower does not produce a capsule.

Control measures: Spray the crop at bud initiation with 0.03% Phophomidon @ 750 litres per hectare.


2. Hawk moth (Acherontia styx)

Symptoms: Caterpillars feed voraciously on the leaves and defoliate the plants.

Control measures: Spray with 0.25% Carbaryl @ 600 litres/ha when the pest starts appearing.


3. Leaf roller and capsule borer (Antigastra catalunalis)

Symptoms: Caterpillars web and feed on the tender leaves in the early stage

They feed inside the flower and the pods in the later stages of the crop.

Control measures: Spray with 0.033% phosphomidon or 0.25% Carbaryl @ 750 litres/ha two or three times (1).


Diseases:

1. Bacterial blight (Xanthomonas sesami, Pseuomonas sesami)

Symptoms: Spots appear from their 4-6 leaf stage of the crop and continue till maturity.

Control measures: Spray Streptocycline 500 ppm two or three times from the initiation of the disease.


2. Cercospora leaf spot (Cercospora sesami)

Symptoms: Small, brown, irregular spots are formed on the leaves and result immature defoliation.

Control measures: Spray the crop with 0.3 % Zineb from the initiation of the disease two or three times at 10 day interval.


3. Phyllody Mycoplasma

Symptoms: All floral parts are transformed into leafy green structures. Affected plants donot bear healthy capsules.

Control measures: Control insect vectors with insecticidal sprays to check the spread of the disease (1, 17).

Economic Potential and Uses

The sesamum seed is a rich source of edible oil. Its oil content generally varies from 46 to 52 per cent. Sesamum oil is used as a cooking-oil in southern India. It is also used for anointing the body. 'Sesame oil is also used for manufacture of perfumery oil as it does not itself possess any strong odour, especially after it is kept for sometimes. Sesame oil have thc property to, absorb the fragrant essence of swect flowers. The oil having lower grades is used in soap making industries. The oil of higher grades is used for medicinal purposes and for manufacture of vanaspathi. It is also used as Industrial paints (4).

Sesame oil is widely used as an ingredient of confectionery and for making margarine. It can be used in the manufacture of cosmetics, perfumes, insecticides and pharmaceutical products. Small quantities are used in the preparation of scented hair-oils and also in therapy as a vehicle for fat-soluble substances. The oil is used also in the formulation of antacids, ointments, injectable as a vehicle for fat-soluble substances. The oil is highly stable and does not go rancid and hence is blended with other oils to increase their stability. The oil is also used by tanners for oiling up tanned cow hides and goat, sheep skins. The oil is as well digested and absorbed as any other vegetable oil or fat. In respect of aiding calcium and phosphorus retention, however, the oil is reported to be superior to groundnut oil (9).

Sesamum-cake is a rich source of protein, carbohydrates and mineral nutrients, such as calcium and phosphorus. The cake is edible and is eaten avidly by working classes. It is also a valuable and nutritious feed for milch cattle (1).

Uses

The seeds may be eaten fried, mixed with sugar or in the form of sweetmeats. Sesame seeds is also used by Hindus in the religious function. The stalk is used for fuel. Dehulled seeds are used as an ingredient of hot curry preparations in some parts of India. In the Middle East, preparations such as Tehinah and Halawa made from dehulled seed are popular. The seeds are used also in bread to which they impart an agreeable flavour. Sesame seed is an ingredient of porridges, soups, confections and other food preparations consumed in Africa. In Venezuela, a beverage is prepared from toasted and crushed seed with milk and sugar. In USA and in European countries sesame seed is used as a garnish for bread rolls and bakery products (9).

Harvesting Practices
Sesamum is harvested well before the plants are completely dry in order to prevent loss of seeds due to dehiscence of the capsules. When the plants have reached physiological maturity, viz. when flowering has ceased and leaves turn yellow and start drooping and capsules are still green or turn slightly yellowish, the plants are cut at the base with a sickle. The harvested plants are carried to the threshing floor where they are piled in circular stacks with the root end outwards. The stacks are about 1-2 m. in diameter at the base. The capsules are allowed to dry in this way for about a week. The stacks are then opened daily and spread out to dry during the day and restacked during the night. This is done till all the capsules dry and dehisce. By inverting the bundle of plants and shaking them, most of the seeds are emptied on to the threshing floor. A light beating is given with a stick if necessary to open the few capsules that might not have yet shattered. The seeds are collected from the threshing floor and cleaned thoroughly. Winnowing and cleaning completes the process (9, 10).

The average grain yield of the kharif crop grown unmixed is 200 to 500 kg per hectare and the average yields of the semi-rabi crop and of the irrigated summer crop are 300 to 600 kg per hectare.

Processing Techniques

The most popular method of oil extraction from sesame seed in India is by ghani, which is basically a large pestle and mortar. The mortar is an inverted cone and the pestle a heavy baulk of timber seated at the bottom of the cone. The pestle is inclined at an angle so that it crushes the meal against the sides of the mortar during rotation. The timber baulk is pulled against thc side of the mortar by weight. and is rotated by a pole .~ inserted through it at right angle. The ghani is driven by one or two bullocks. In earlier days, it was made from wood, but nowadays may also be of wood-lined concrete or cast iron. The Burmese hsi-zon is similar to the Indian ghani. Oil extraction by ghani consists of cleaning the seeds to remove dust and dirt particles and other contaminants, and de hulling to obtain seed kernels,which are then directly used in the ghani. In many parts of India, water or jaggery (brown sugar) is added to sesame seed to facilitate oil extraction. Following extraction, the oil is removed from the ghani, allowed to settle, skimmed, and sometimes strained through a cloth before sale. Sometimes the residual meal is double-pressed to obtain more oil.

The most serious drawback of the village ghanis is their comparative inefficiency of production of oil, production which is considerably lower than that from power mills, particularly screw presses. The yield of sesame oil from traditional Indian ghanis is only about 40-45%, while that from Burmese hsi-zon is about 33%, thereby leaving behind a higher percentage of oil in the cake. Other major drawbacks include inefficient operations, thereby resulting in low outturn, the resulting oil contains more moisture and suspended and mucilaginous impurities and the unhealthy sanitary conditions under which the ghanis work (13,14).


Pretreatment

In the parts of India where decuticling is practiced, the seeds are soaked and the cuticle removed

manually.

Extraction

  1. Seed is readily processed in conventional oil mill machinery, continuos screw press, hydraulic press, prepress and by solvent extraction.

  2. In Europe and Asia it is usua1 to express the oil in three stages

  • First pressing is made cold and the oil after filtration is ready for use.

  • Second pressing is made of the heated residue, which is subjected to

high pressure and yields a highly coloured oil, which is refined and used for edible purposes

  • Third expressing under the same conditions as the second, gives an oil of inferior quality which is generally used as soap.

(3) Expeller pressed oil is alkali refined, bleached, deodorized and hydrogenated (2).

Other Uses
Sesamum seeds are considered emollient, diuretic, lactagogue and a nourishing tonic. They are said to be helpful in piles, a paste of the seeds mixed with butter being used in bleeding piles. A decoction of the seed is aid to be an emmenagogue and is also given in cough. Combined with linseed, the decoction of the seeds is used as aphrodisiac. A plaster made of ground seeds is applied to burns, scalds, etc. and a Poultice of seeds applied to ulcers. Powdered seeds are used in amenorhoea and dysmenorrhoea. Fresh leaves are useful in catarrhal affections of kidney and bladder, externally they are applied in ophthalmic and cutaneous complaints. A decoction of leaves and roots is employed as a hair wash to promote hair-growth. The mucilaginous juice of the plant is used to destroy hair lice (9).

Export Potential

The export and import trade in sesamum seed or oil has not been very spectacular. The quantity and value of the oil exported during 1967-68, 1968-69 and 1969-70 were respectively: 160,361 kg., Rs. 805,776, 115,321 kg., Rs. 609,107; and 1,792 kg., Rs. 10,867. Sesame seeds were mostly exported to countries like France, Iran, Quatar, Bahrain., UK, etc.

In 1968-69, 23,198 kg. of sesamum seeds worth Rs. 47,860 were exported whereas in 1969-70 the exports rose to 141,242 kg. worth Rs. 255,545. Exports in oilcake have been rather more compared with those in oil and seeds. Exports of sesame oilcake during 1967-68, 1968-69 and 1969-70 were 8,137, 5,476 and 4,735 kg. valued at Rs. 3,299,655, 3,284,822 and 2,848,705 respectively (9).

Miscellaneous Information

Sesame seed has a special significance for human nutrition on account of its high content of sulphur amino acids, particularly methionine. The main limitation in the use of sesame seed and meal is the presence of cuticle (husk) which contributes dark colour and bitterness to the cake, the husk has also a high crude fibre content and cntains calcium oxalate.

The most satisfactory method of getting an edible sesame flour is to use dehulled seed for oil extraction. Several wet processing methods and mechanical treatments have been tried. A lye treatment process for removal of hull has been reported. The process consists of

  • Precleaning of the seed

  • Hot lye treatment of the seeds for 1 minute

  • Washing with excess cold water and scrubbing in a suitable equipment to separate the seed coat

  • Drying the dehulled seeds

  • Extraction of expeller and

  • Solvent extraction.

This yields a flour with 56 per cent protein. The crude fibre and oxalic acid contents are significantly decreased. Since the process for dehulling involves use of water and removal of water from the wet dehulled seeds, the drying cost is considerable. In addition, the loss of dry matter from the seed in the form of hull and soluble pigments also adds to the cost. It is estimated that the cost of the dehulled seed would be about 30-40 per cent higher than that of the commercial seed. The quality of the oil is not affected by the lye treatment (13, 15).
