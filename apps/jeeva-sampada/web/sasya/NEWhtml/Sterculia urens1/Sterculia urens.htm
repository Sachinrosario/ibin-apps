Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large tree. Usually occurs in dry, rocky forests. tropical Himalaya from Ganga eastwards, E. and W. Peninsulas, South India, Sri Lanka and Myanmar. Also near the coast on rocky soil in the Konkan and North Kanara.


Availability :

Limited supply as tree found scattered in the forest, chiefly in central India and the Deccan.


Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large tree with irregular gnarled trunk. Young parts more or less pubescent. Trunk erect, straight. Bark white, smooth, papery, the outer surface thin, peeling off, the inner coat fibrous and netted, scaling off like birch bark. Leaves crowded at the ends of the branches, shallowly palmately lobed, glabrous or nearly so above, velvety beneath, lobes 5, caudate-acuminate, base cordate. Petioles terete, velvety-tomentose.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers yellow, numerous, small, the hermaphrodite or female mixed with many males, in much-branched glandular-pubescent terminal panicles appearing before the leaves at the ends of the branches. Calyx companulate, hairy on both surfaces, lobes oblong, acute, as long as the tube, with a small hairy gland at the base of each lobe.

Male flowers: Staminal-column short; filaments 10.

Hermaphrodite flowers: Carpels usually 5, on a short stout gynophore; style short, thick, hairy, stigmas 5. Stamens in a ring round the carpels.

Follicles 4-6, ovoid-oblong, 1 in. long, densely pubescent, the pubescence mixed with stinging hairs. Seeds 3-6, oblong, black.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:Wood reported to be a good fuel. Calorific value : sapwood 5,194 cal., 9,350 Btu.; heartwood 5,744 cal., 10,339 Btu.

Wood: Sapwood greyish white to pale pinkish buff, 4-6 cm wide. Heartwood distinct, reddish brown, soft to moderately hard. Light to moderately heavy. Specific gravity 0.41 - 0.67 air dry. Rather dull with a distinct oily feel and somewhat pungent odour when freshly cut. Straight grained, even and coarse textured. Very perishable but not liable to warping and cracking. Sapwood susceptible to stain and should be converted immediately after felling in the dry season.

Timber used for toys, guitars, inferior packing cases and boarding and occasionally for dugouts, planking. Wood suitable for cement-barrels, cheap matchboxes and splints, low-grade pencils and for moulded picture and slate frames.

The quarter-sawn pieces of heartwood may be used for small panels and inlay work in better-class furniture.

Other uses:

Seeds eaten like pulse after roasting or cooking.

Bark reported to yield fiber, used for cordage. Also said to be employed to cover the indigenous water-bottles made from the wild types of Lagenaria siceraria.

The leaves possess good nutritive value as fodder and rich in vitamin A. Tender roots reported to be eaten after cooking.

Glue made from the proteins of seed can be used as an adhesive in making plywood.

Miscellaneous Information


Plant is a source of karaya gum or Indian tragacanth, which is a substitute for tragacanth in cosmetics and ice creams. Used in medicine.
