DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stems 0.2-1.7 cm tall, branched, furrowed; young parts densely hairy. Leaves elliptic-lanceolate to obovate, short acuminate at apex, amplexicaul at base, wooly beneath, upper surface pubescent. Heads yellow, many in racemes, 4-5 cm across, stout; peduncles hairy. Involucral bracts multi-seriate, densely hairy; outer ovate, 1.5 x 0.8 cm, tips recurved, densely wooly outside, tubercled within. Ray florets ligulate, 2-3 cm long; ligules c 1.5 cm long, hyaline, conspicuously nerved. Disc florets c 1.5 cm long. Achenes 0.2 cm long. Pappus up to 8 mm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Roots are aromatic and often employed as an adultarant of “Kuth”. It is also used as an expectorant and resolvent. Roots contain inuline (10%) and an essential oil containing alantolactone which is strongly anthelmintic, expectorant and diuretic. Seeds are aphrodisiac. It is also a good cure for rheumatism.


Chemistry:

  • Alantolactone, isolantolactone and dihydroisolantolactone  from roots ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969)a.
  • A germacranolide-inunolide (fig.1)from  root oil.( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969)b.
  • b-sitosterol, octadecanoic acid and D-mannitol isolated ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)a.
  • Improved process for isolation of alantolactone developed.( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984)a.
  • (-)dammar-20,24-dien-3β-yl acetate isolated from roots .( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984)b.
  • alloalatolactone isolated from  roots  and characterized.( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984)c.
  • Two new sesquiterpene lactones-inunal  and isoalloalantolactone- isolated and characterized ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989)a.
  • Aplotaxene (heptadeca-1,8,11,14-tetraene)(22.0%) and phenylacetonitrile (2.0%) determined in  essential  oil ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989)b.
  • Stereochemistry of inunolide and dihydroinunolide revised.( Rastogi and Mehrotra  1984-1989)c.
  •  Isolation  of  four oxygenated alantolides-I,II,III and  isoinunal- from  roots together  with alantolactone, isoalatolactone, isoalantolactone and telekin.(Rastogi and Mehrotra 1984-1989)c.
  • Three new oxygenated alantolides- I,II, and 4(15)-aepoxyisotelekin- isolation and  their  stereostuructures determined ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994)a.
  • The roots of Inula racemosa gave b-sitosterol, daucosterol and isoalantolactone. The structures were determined by a combination of spectral methods (IR, EIMS, 1H and 13C NMR, DEPT, COSY, NOESY and HETCOR). All isolates were subjected to antifungal tests. Isoalantolactone, a major sesquiterpene lactone of I. racemosa, was found to be active against the human pathogenic fungi, Aspergillus flavus, A. niger, Geotrichum candidum, Candida tropicalis and C. albicans at concentrations of 50, 50, 25, 25 and 25 mug/ml, respectively. (Tan et al., 1998)

Pharmacology:

  • Alcoholic extract of the root of I. racemosa lowers blood glucose and enhances liver glycogen without increasing plasma insulin in rats. There is also no increase in degree of degranulation of the beta cells of pancreas. In similar conditions it does not show any effect on activity of adrenal gland. However, thyroid gland undergoes activation at the later stage (delayed response). It appears that hypoglycemic response of I. racemosa is not due to enhanced secretion/synthesis of insulin; the action may be at the peripheral level by potentiating the insulin sensitivity. (Tripathi and Chaturvedi, 1995)
  • The powder and different extracts of the roots of I. racemosa were tested for their hepatoprotective activity against CCl4-, paracetamol- and rifamipicin-induced hepatotoxicities in rats. Powdered roots, total aqueous extract and methanolic extract showed significant hepatoprotective activities, comparable with those of silymarin. (Rao and Mishra, 1997)
  • Alcoholic extract of root of Inula racemosa, was studied for its antiallergic effect in experimental models of type I hypersensitivity, viz. egg albumin induced passive cutaneous anaphylaxis (PCA) and mast cell degranulation in albino rats. (Srivastava et al., 1999)
  • In an attempt to find the possible regulation of steroid diabetes by Inula racemosa extract, relative efficacy of I. racemosa (root) extracts (400, 600, 150 and 500 mg/kg. b. wt./day, respectively, for 15 days) in the regulation of dexamethasone-induced hyperglycaemia was studied in mice. Simultaneously, levels of thyroid hormones (thyroxine and triiodothyronine) were estimated by radio-immunoassay (RIA), to reveal the possible mediation of the effects of the plant extracts through thyroid hormones. While the administration of dexamethasone (1 mg/kg b.wt.) induced hyperglycaemia in mice, Inula racemosa was able to ameliorate the drug induced hyperglycaemia by 24%. These effects were comparable to the standard corticosteroid-inhibiting drug, ketoconazole (Keto-ZoleTM). The lack of increase in hepatic lipid peroxidation and decrease in Superoxide dismutase or catalase activity suggest the potential use of  plant extract in the regulation of corticosteroid-induced hyperglycaemia. (Gholap et al., 2003)
 

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for gastro-intestinal  rheumatism (root, Gupta et al., 1981). Incense (root, Koelz, 1979).
