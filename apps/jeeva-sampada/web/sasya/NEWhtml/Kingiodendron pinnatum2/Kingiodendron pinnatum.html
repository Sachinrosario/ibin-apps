 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A large  evergreen tree, found in the  semi-evergreen to evergreen forests of Western Ghats from South Canara to Kerala.

The species occurs in typical rain forest climate, with the mean annual rainfall varying from 3000mm to 4000mm, but is  also recorded at places with annual rainfall up to 9500mm.  The mean annual temperature in its natural habitat is about 27.1°C, the absolute maximum being 37°C and the absolute minimum being 16.5°C.  This species occurs in tropical wet forest with crystalline rocks, usually gneiss or granite.  The typical soil, where this species occurs, has coarse sand of 49 percent, fine sand of 10 percent, silt 8 percent, clay 22 percent and moisture 5 percent and organic matter  6 percent with a pH of 5.5.

Availability : 
It is endemic to the Western Ghats of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.  in Karnataka, locally abundant in Hassan, Coorg, Dakshina Kannada and Chikmagalur districts.  In Kerala recorded all along the Ghats.  In Tamil Nadu, occasionally recorded in higher altitudes of Kanniyakumari and Tirunelveli districts.


Status : 

Endangered as per the Red list.
It's Red List Status has been assessed as "Vulnerable(Globally)".

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

It is a large, handsome, evergreen tree, attaining a height upto 40 50m and a girth of 1 to 4m.  Straight and unbranched.  Brk dark brown mottled with green and reddish inside.  Exudes a sticky resin when cut.  Leaves alternate, pinnate, leaflets 5 to 7, oblong, acuminate, rounded, margin entire, shining, glabrous, distinctly stalked.  Flowers small in dense cylindrical, panicled recemes, bisexual, dull yellow, mildly fragrant.  Pods dark brown, glabrous, turgid, woody, compressed.  Seed 1, brown ellipsoid, flat and hard.

Seed Biology :


Seeds are separated from the ripe pods, after drying in the sun,  can be stored up to a year. 

Seedling Germination :
It can be propagated using seeds.

Ripe pods are collected from May to July and also in October.  Seeds are separated from the ripe pods, after drying in the sun and can be stored up to a year.  Fresh seeds are dibbled into raised beds under shade, along contours, preferably aligned North-South to provide partial shade.  Germination percent is 85.

Cultivation:
Three to six months old nursery raised seedlings can be transplanted to the field.  Planting of 1 year old nursery stock is recommended.  Care should be taken to protect the plant against any fungal and pest attacks.  About 80 percent success is obtained in establishment of this species in the field.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Wood

Uses

  • The oleo-resin is dark to reddish brown in colour and has a characteristic odour and taste;  it is used locally as wood varnish after diluting with turpentine; an oil is obtainable by steam-distillation of this resin; it also has certain medicinal properties.    
  • Oleoresin is used in the treatment of gonorrhoea, dressing for sores of elephants.


Harvesting Practices

Oleo-resin (balsam)- Tapping is done  by boring  a hole of about 2 cm diameter .  The hole reaches the pith about 0.9m above ground and slopes downwards from the pith of the bark. The exudate is collected in a tin through a metallic lip. When the  flow stops the hole is plugged with wood and the tree is given rest for 10 yrs. A healthy  tree of 2.4 m girth yields 55 liters of oleo-resin, the maximum yields reported being 180 liters. Tree yielding a thinner form of Oleo-resin usually give more than those  yielding the thicker form. Thicker is considered superior  to thinner oleo-resin.

Produce and Products
Oleo-resin, oil

Other Uses
  1. Wood is suitable  for  turnery and for rotary veneer work. It  is primarily used  for beams, rafters, battens, ceiling boards, flooring and furniture.  Used for cordite cases, bowls, and croquet balls and is suitable for billiard tables and cue handles.  Selected stock of the timber is used for cabinet  work and ornamental veneers, ship-building, plywood.
  2. Oil  also has medicinal uses.
