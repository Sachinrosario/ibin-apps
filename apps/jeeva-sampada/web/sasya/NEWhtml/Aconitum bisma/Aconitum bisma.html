Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
Herbaceous plant, found in Alpine Himalaya, 2700-3200 m in moist Abies and Rhododendron forest. Uttar Pradesh (Garhwal), Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh (Kameng, Lohit, Siang).
Availability :  
Specimen examined:
Uttar Pradesh: Mussoorie range, G. King, s. n. (CAL); West Bengal: Phullalong, Darjeeling, 3834 m, 06.10.1870, s. legit, 13410 B (CAL); Sikkim: Kopup, 4334 m, 14.09.1945, s. legit, 393 (DD).
Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant is rare, found in alpine Central Himalayas.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs, 40-150 cm high; stems erect, simple, hollow, glabrous; roots paired, tuberous. Leaves obcordate to reniform, up to 15 cm in diam., deeply 5-lobed, stalk 4-10 cm long.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in 10-20 cm long loose panicles, blue or bluish-white. Sepals greenish blue or bluish white. Petals head S-shaped, 5-6 mm long, glabrous; spur bulbous. Follicles oblong, 2.5-4.0 cm long, glabrous. Seeds black.
Inflorescence: Panicles ; Flower: Greenish blue, regular, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Sepals 5, petaliod, posterior vaulted, the rest flat, 2 anterior narrow; Corolla: Petals 5, 2 posterior clawed, limb hooded and enclosed in helmet; Androecium: Stamens many, anthers adnate, dehiscing laterally; Gynoecium: Multicarpellary, free, 1-celled, stigma simple ; Fruit: Follicles glabrous ; Seed: Blackish, ovoid, winged, lamellate. Flowering & Fruiting: Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
Floral and seed biology:
 It flowers between June - October.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  Extract from the roots of this plant is used medicinally.
Chemistry:
  • The chemical study of the roots of Aconitum palmatum under the name Vakhma’, reveals ‘the presence of a large number of alkaloids from which five diterpene alkaloids have been isolated and characterized. The first water insoluble base named Vakognavine having the molecular formula C35H41O10N, mp. 2980,the ether soluble alkaloid was Palmatisine (C22H25O6N or C24H27O7N mp.285 °. The chloroform soluble alkaloid named Vakatisine (C22H23O4N), mp.308 ° and the two water soluble bases have named Vakatisinine (C22H33O4N),mp.308 ° and Vakatidine (C22H33O2N), mp. 259 °. (Singh and Singh. 1965; David and Edward, 1968).
  • Absolute configuration of Vakognavine. (Pelletier, 1971).
  • Crystal structure of Vakognavine, C34H37NO10 m.p. 298 ° Rf 0.95 at 21 ° (Fig. 1a) a highly oxygenated N, C (19) Seco-diterpene alkaloid with modified atisine type skeleton isolated from root tubers of Aconitum palmatum was established on the basis of X-ray crystallographic studies carried on Vakognarine hydrochloride (Fig. 1b) and spectroscopic data. (Singh et al., 1974).
  • Four diterpenoid alkaloids-15-Deacetylvakognavine (C32H35NO9) m.p. 224.5-226.5 °C (Fig. 2a), Palmadine (C31H35NO5) m.p. 269-271 ° (Fig. 2b), Palmasine (C29H33NO4) m.p. 252-254 °C (Fig. 2c) and 6-Acetylheratisine (C24H35NO6) m.p. 156-158 °C (Fig. 2d) isolated along with Vakognavine, Heteratisine, Isoatisine, and Hetidine; structure of new compounds elucidated. (Jiang and Pelletier, 1988).
  • Two new diterpenoid alkaloids vakhmatine (Fig. 3a) and vakhmadine (Fig. 3b) have been isolated from A. palmatum, together with the known alkaloids atisine and hetisine, their structures were established by 2D NMR and nOe studies; (Jiang and Pelletier, 1991).
