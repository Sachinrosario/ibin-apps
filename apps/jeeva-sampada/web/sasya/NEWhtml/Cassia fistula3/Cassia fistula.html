HABIT & HABITAT
Deciduous tree, 10-20 m tall with spreading crown, it is found in Jammu Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Bark smooth, pale grey when young, dark brown when old; branchlets pubescent. Leaves 23-40 cm long; leaflets 4-8 pairs, ovate-oblong, 5.0-12.5 x 2.5-6.0 cm, acute to subacuminate at apex, rounded or subcuneate at base, bright green and glabrous above, silvery pubescent beneath when young. Stipules deltoid, 1-2 mm long, acute, pubescent; petioles terete, 7-10 cm long, glabrous; petiolules 4-6 mm long. Flowers in lax pendulous raceme, yellow 24-40 cm long, 4-5 cm across; pedicels 1.5-6.0 cm long, 1-3 together; bracts ovate, 6-8 mm long, acute, puberulous, caduceus; bracteoles linear, twisting, 5-7 mm long, acute, caducous. Sepals ovate-oblong, 7-9 x 4-5 mm, obtuse, pubescent, finally reflexed. Petals obovate, 2-3 x 1.0-2.5 cm, thinly pubescent. Stamens 10. Ovary and style subglabrous or pubescent; stigma sublateral, punctiform. Pods cylindrical, 30-60 cm long, 2.0-2.5 cm across, indehiscent, pendulous, brownish black. Seeds ovate, 8 cm long, immersed in a dark coloured, sweetish pulp.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
April-February.
Floral and seed  biology:
Inflorescence axillary or extra axillary, pendant, racemose raceme; Flower bracteate, pedicillate, complete, zygomorphic, hypogynous, pentamerous and cyclic; Calyx sepals 5, polysepalous, quinquncial, old sepal anterior, petaloid; Corolla petals 5, polypetalous, ascending imbricate, yellow; Androecium stamens 10,in whorls of 5 each,the anterior 3 are reduced to staminode, polyandrous, dithecous, dorsifixed, introrse; Gynoecium monocarpellary, syncarpous, ovary superior, unilocular, placentation marginal, ovules many, style short and stigma capitate; Fruit is legume.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal: 
Plants are pollinated by birds and seeds are dispersed by wind and animals.
Genetic variability:
The basic chromosome count is 14.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type:   It can grow on wide variety of soils i.e. on poor shallow soil  ,on dry slopes of Himalayas, trap,granite and sandstone soil or even frequently on khurda laterite.

Nutrient requirements:  Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers.

Water management: It requires annual rainfall between 450-3000 mm or even more but it flourishes best with the rainfall between 750-1,900 mm.

Harvesting period / Method: Leaves matures for harvesting in about 60 days, pods are harvested when still green, plant is harvested when the tree reaches a minimum age of 30 years.

Tocophetols, obtained after sapon. of the seed oils increased from 0.12-60%. The oil content increased from 0.48-4.0%1; The seasonal and ripening stage variations of sennoside content in leaves and pods of 2 cassia fistula populations from Mexico were studied between Nov. 1986 and Oct. 1987. For both populations, a peak of sennoside content in leaves was obsd. in June when new leaves appeared soon after the rainy season had begun. The highest percentages of sennosides in pods were recorded at the mid stage of fruit maturation when the pods were plae brown in colour. There was a relation between age of trees and sennoside content. The population with older indivisuals contained lower levels of sennosides in their leaves and pods with respect to the other population with the younger trees. In the leaves, both these antraquinone  glycocides remained high in the mature and old leaves in the months from Jan. to Apr. when the contents of mature pods were low. In the developing green pods the contents were high compared to the older ones, while the young leaves had lower glycosidal content compared to their mature stage. In general, leaves were a richer source of these glycosides5.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine root is used in skin diseases, leprosy and syphilis. Powder or decoction of bark is given orally in leprosy, jaundice, syphilis and heart diseases. Leaves are also useful in ulcer and rheumatism. Flowers cure flatulence. Fruits are useful in leprosy, heart diseases and abdominal pains. Seeds cure biliousness. In Unani system of medicine leaves are used in inflammation against different lesson. Fruits are used as abotifacient and antipyretic. They are also useful in chest complaints, throat troubles, eye diseases, rheumatism and asthma.

·        The total phenolic, proanthocyanidin, and flavonoid contents, and the   antioxidant activities, of fresh vegetative and reproductive organs of   Cassia fistula harvested at different stages of growth were determined using the Trolox equivalent antioxidant capacity (TEAC) and ferric-reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assays. The antioxidant activities were strongly correlated with total phenols (TEAC r = 0.989; FRAP r = 0.951) in all organs studied, and with proanthocyanidins (TEAC r = 0.980; FRAP r = 0.899) in reproductive organs including fruits. The antioxidant activities of reproductive parts were higher than those of   the vegetative organs, with the pods having highest total phenolic, proanthocyanidin, and flavonoid contents and antioxidant potentials   (TEAC = 992 +/- 0.4 micromol/g dry weight; FRAP = 811 +/- 23 micromol/g   dry weight). (Luximon et al., 2002)

Compound 1 showed anti-microbial activity. (Yadava and Verma, 2003)


Pharmacology:

·        The methanol extract of Cassia fistula was investigated for its effect on a cough model induced by sulphur dioxide gas in mice. It exhibited significant antitussive activity when compared with control in a dose dependent manner. The antitussive activity of the extract was comparable to that of codeine phosphate, a prototypes antitussive agent. The C. fistula extract (400, 600 mg/kg, p.o.) showed maximum inhibition of cough by 44.44% and 51.85% with respect to control group. (Bhakta et al., 1998)

·        Oral administration of aqueous extract of seeds of Cassia fistula to mated female rats from day 1-5 of pregnancy at the doses of 100 and 200 mg/kg body weight resulted in 57.14% and 71.43% prevention of pregnancy, respectively, whereas 100% pregnancy inhibition was noted at 500 mg/kg bw. In the uterine bioassay test carried out in immature bilaterally ovariectomized female rats, aqueous extract of seeds of Cassia fistula (100 mg/kg bw) increased the uterine wet weight (p<0.05) and luminal epithelial cell height (p<0.001) but did not induce premature opening of the vagina. This suggests a mild estrogenic activity of the extract. However, when the extract was administered conjointly with estradiol valerate (EDV, 0.1 mg/kg bw), it significantly (p<0.001) prevented the estrogen-induced uterotrophic effect, thus showing an antiestrogenic nature of the extract in the presence of a strong estrogen.  (Yadav and Jain, 1999)

·         Methanol extract of buds of C. fistula was   examined for its antipyretic action on normal body temperature and yeast-induced pyrexia in rats. The extract showed significant activity in both the models at doses of 200 and 400 mg/kg. At a dose level of 200 mg/kg, the extract caused significant lowering of normal body temperature up to 3h. At 400-mg/kg doses, it caused significant lowering of body temperature up to 6h after administration. In the model of yeast-provoked elevation of body temperature, the extract showed dose dependent lowering of body temperature up to 4h at both the dosage levels tried. The results obtained are comparable to those for   paracetamol, a standard antipyretic agent. (Bhakta et al., 2000)

·        Effects of methanolic extract (ME) of Cassia fistula seed on the growth   of Ehrlich ascites carcinoma (EAC) and on the life span of tumor bearing mice were studied. ME treatment showed an increase of life span, and a decrease in the tumor volume and viable tumor cell count in   the EAC tumor hosts. Cytological studies have revealed a reduction in the mitotic activity, and the appearance of membrane blebbing and intracytoplasmic vacuoles in the treated tumor cells. Improvement in the hematological parameters following ME treatment, like hemoglobin content, red blood cell count and bone marrow cell count of the tumor bearing mice have also been observed. (Gupta et al., 2000)

·        Hepatoprotective activity of the n-heptane extract of Cassia fistula leaves was investigated by inducing hepatotoxicity with paracetamol in rats. The extract at a dose of 400mg/kg body wt. exhibited orally, significant protective effect by lowering the serum levels of transaminases (SGOT and SGPT), bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase  (ALP). The effects produced were comparable to that of a standard hepatoprotective agent. (Bhakta et al., 2001)


PHARMACOGNOSY:

·        Pods of Cassia fistula (Fam. Leguminosae) is medicinally important   for its laxative property. The pods are often adulterated by the pods of Cassia javanica. An attempt has been made to distinguish the two on the basis of macromorphology, micromorphology, histochemical study and physical pharmacognostic study. (Dutta and De Bratati, 1997)

Microscopy, seasonal   variations and spectrophotometric estimation of hydroxy-anthraquinone   glycosides of culitivated Cassia fistula Linn. The total glycoside contents ofthe morphological parts of this species at different stages of growth are also presented. The study shows that anthraquinone glycosides are concentrated in the leaves (1.75%) and flowers (1.58%) at peak flowering. Notable seasonal variations were observed in the cultivated species. Hydroxyanthraquinones reached peak levels druing the months of September (1.08%) and October (2.20%). There was a significant drop (P < 0.05) in glycoside content during most part of the rainy season. (Abo et al., 2001).
