Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

medium sized to large deciduous tree with large rounded crown. Found in mixed deciduous forests, usually of a somewhat dry type, often growing on rocky and sandy soil. dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical).

Drought and frost hardy, but suffer under severe conditions. Reported to be unaffected by excessive monsoons and found in water logged or low-lying clayey and shallow soils in Mysore. A strong light demander.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A deciduous tree with thick, dark-colored cracked bark, the inner bark red, milky; short trunk; branches numerous, spreading, forming a thick shady head. Leaves clustered near the ends of the branches, coriaceous, hard and firm, elliptic or elliptic-oblong, shortly acuminate, pubescent or tomentose when young, at length glabrous, base rounded or acute; main nerves 10-14 pairs, apart with closely reticulate veins between; petioles at first pubescent, ultimately glabrous or nearly so; stipules subulate, densely pubescent, very caducous. Flowers in dense fascicles near the ends of the branches, below the terminal leaf bud, pedicels drooping, fulvous- or rusty-pubescent or tomentose. Calyx divided nearly to the base, segments ovate, subacute, rusty-tomentose, usually 4 (rarely 5), the two other subvalvate and enclosing the others. Corolla cream colored, fleshy, rugose when dry, lobes 7 14 (usually 8 9), ovate-lanceolate, acute, erect. Stamens 20 30 (usually 24 26); anthers in 3 series, acuminate, hairy at the back. Ovary hirsute, style 1" long or more, hairy at the base. Berry 1 2" long, fleshy, ovoid, and greenish. Seeds 1 4.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Biology: Natural reproduction takes place by seeds which germinate early in the rainy season, soon after falling. Exposure renders them liable to fungus attack and radicles may dry up or be eaten by insects; for germination to take place the seeds should become covered with earth or debris. Natural seedlings are thus found chiefly in small hollows into which earth is washed at the commencement of rains. Subsequent growth is slow, but is favoured by abundant light.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diseases:

Tree is affected by rust Scopells echinulata (Niessl) Mains syn. Uromyces echinulatus Niessl. The tree is also subject to white spongy rot (Polystictus steinheilianus Berk. & Lev.), heart rot of stems (Fomes caryophylli [Racib.] Bres.) and root and butt rot (Polyporus gilvus Schw.).

Pests:

The leaves are eaten by caterpillars of Achaea janata Linn., Anuga multiplicans Wlk., Bombotelia nugatrix Guen., Metanastria hyrtaca Cram. And a few species of Rhodoneura. Two species of Acrocercops mine the leaves and blotch them. Newly felled trees and green logs are attacked by the pin-hole borers, Xyleborus spp. and Polygraphus bassiae Beeson, The bark is destroyed by white ants (Odontotermes obesus Ramb.) and the bark borer, Xyloctomus scolytoides Eichh. The sapwood of dead tree is damaged by ghoon borers, like Schistoceros anobioides Waterh. and Xylicis tortilicornis Lesne.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, Flowers, Berries and Seeds.

Fuel: A good fuelwood. 

Mahua timber used for constructional purposes. Used for carriages, furniture, turnery, sports goods, musical instruments, oil and sugar presses, ship building, boats, bridges and well construction.


Harvesting Practices: Coppices vigorously and the growth of coppice shoots is more rapid than of seedling plants.

Back to Top

 

Produce and Products:

Mahua oil and Mahua butter.

Chemistry:

Calorific value of wood: sapwood – 4,890-4,978 cal, 8,802-8,962 Btu, heartwood – 5,005-5,224 cal, 9,010-9,404 Btu.

Processing Techniques:

Green conversion of logs, followed by immersion in water and subsequent drying of planks under cover in fairly close piles, recommended.

 

Other uses:

Corollas of mahua flowers, commonly called as mahua flowers, are edible. Berries eaten raw or cooked.

Seeds yield a fatty oil known as Mahua Butter, Mowra fat, Illeppe Butter or Bassia Fat.

Mahua oil used mainly in the manufacture of soaps, particularly laundry soaps. Used for edible and cooking purposes in some rural areas. Oil sometimes used as an adulterant to ghee. Refined oil used in manufacture of lubricating greases and fatty alcohols. Also used for candles, as a batching oil in jute industry and as a raw material for the production of stearic acid. Mahua cake used as manure.

Mahua flowers used to prepare distilled liquors. Flowers also used to prepare vinegar. Syrup prepared by extracting flowers with hot water. Flowers used to feed livestock.

Flowers of medicinal use.

Miscellaneous Information

Wood: Sapwood reddish white to brownish white; heartwood light to dark red or reddish brown, dull. More or less straight or shallowly interlocked grained, coarse to even textured, very strong, hard and heavy. Specific gravity 0.95 – 0.97, 61-62 lb./ c ft., refractory, liable to split and crack.
