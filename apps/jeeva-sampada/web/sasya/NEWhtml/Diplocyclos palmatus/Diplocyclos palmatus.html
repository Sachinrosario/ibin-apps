HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual or perennial, much branched, slender, grooved, glabrous herb. It is found in Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves membranous, ovate-suborbicular, 8-15 cm in diam., 5-7 palmatipartite, base cordate; lobes oblong or elliptic-lanceolate, margin sinuate, distantly denticulate, scabrid above, glabrous beneath. Tendrils 2-fid. Male flowers: 3-6 in fascicles, peduncles, glabrous. Corolla white or greenish yellow, hairy within; lobes ovate-oblong, acute. Female flowers: solitary or few or many; ovary globose. Fruit subsessile, globose, 1.3-2.5 cm across, smooth, bluish green or yellowish green, streaked with broad vertical lines. Seeds ovate, 5-6 x 2.5-3 mm, yellowish brown.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
In Ayurveda system of medicine the plant is hot and pungent. The leaves are applied in inflammation.
Chemistry:


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for ague, asthma, carbuncle, cholera, colic, costipation, convulsion, (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Eye infection (Leaf, Goel et al, 1984); Fever, headache, paralysis, phthisis, post-natal complaints (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); promotes fertility in women (Seed, Jain, 1963; Jain, 1965; Saxena and Dutta, 1975); small pox (Whole plant, Saxena and Dutta, 1975); Sores, spleen complaints; syphilis, tumor (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); snake bite (Part not mentioned, Shah and Joshi, 1971); Veterinary (Part not mentioned, Sen et al, 1985); Fodder (Whole plant, Goel et al, 1984).
