Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A moderate-sized to large evergreen tree in western part of temperate Himalayan regions. Gregarious. Massive root system. It grows on a variety of geological formations including micaceous sandy soil. Suitable for deep soil, moist localities on marginal lands and village commons. Moderate light demander.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A moderate-sized to large evergreen tree. Bark grey to greyish brown, silvery when young, peeling off in rounded flakes. Leaves oblong- or ovate-lanceolate, with white or grey tomentum beneath.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Male spikes densely hairy and clustered. Female flowers usually sessile; acorns single or in pairs. Nut ovoid, brown with a grey pubescence near the apex.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Natural reproduction is by seeds. Seeds are produced in abundance. As the seeds are eaten by birds and animals even before they ripen, their survival is difficult, unless the ground below is well worked so that the falling acorns get covered by soil and are hidden.

Artificial propagation is done by direct sowing or by transplanting seedlings raised in nursery. The growth of the seedling is somewhat slow and in the first few years, the plant withstands a fair amount of shade, thereafter it requires full sunshine for best development.

Seedlings and saplings killed by severe frost. Seedlings sensitive to drought. Suffers severely from fire.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood extensively used for fuel. Excellent firewood. Calorific value : sapwood - 4,633 cal., 8,339 Btu.; heartwood - 4,566 cal., 8,221 Btu. Gives excellent charcoal.

Wood: Light russet light greyish brown, with darker streaks along the grain, straight or somewhat wavy grained, medium fine- but uneven textured, very hard, moderately strong and moderately heavy. Average unit weight at 12 per cent moisture content 865 kg/m3. Specific gravity 0.74, 750 kg/ c m. Liable to split, warp severely. Very difficult to season.

Wood diffuse-porous to ring-porous. Growth rings usually indistinct but distinct when ring-porous. Pores rather large or medium-sized to small, the former visible to the eye, and distinct under the lens; exclusively solitary, arranged in long oblique, radial or flame-like groups, tyloses present; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surface. Soft tissues abundant, distinct only under the lens, diffuse to diffuse-in-aggregates or as numerous fine interrupted lines forming a net work with rays. Rays of two types, extremely broad and very fine; the former very few and widely spaced prominent to the eye on all the three surfaces, showing up as conspicuous flecks on the radial surface; the later visible only under the lens, numerous and closely-spaced.

Wood occasionally used for building and agricultural implements. Suitable for tool handles.

Other uses:

Bark extensively employed for tanning purpose.

Leaves used as cattle fodder in the hills. Also provide material for rearing tasar silk worms.

Long-lived ban forests important for soil conservation and flood prevention. Tree used for afforestation, rehabilitation and stabilization.

Acorns medicinally useful.
