Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Native to the Indo-Malaysian region and Australia, found throughout the hotter parts of India and the tropical Himalayas from Nepal eastwards to Arunachal Pradesh and Manipur in Peninsular India, common on the eastern ghats.
Availability :


Conservation/ Threat Status : found wild in India but is not cultivated.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

- An evergreen tree, upto 10m in height and 60 cm in girth.
- Bark dark brown, fibrous, pinkish brown inside.
- Leaves variable, oblong lanceolate, lanceolate or oblong elliptic, coriaceous, glossy green, 5.0-18.0 cm x 2.5-4.0 cm.
- Inflorescence many - flowered, terminal or axillary, narrowly spicate or racemose.
- Male flowers pale green, with offensive odour, sessile, united into sample or branched panicles.
- Female flowers pedicelled in racemes.
- Drupes globose or ovoid, 9-10 mm in length, green turning red, finally bluish violet, juicy when ripe, single seeded.
- Seeds ovoid or ovoid oblong, slightly oblique.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fruits.


Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:
Fruits - season fish and meat preparations in the Philippines, for jellies & jams, fruit juice converted to syrup for topping ice creams, as a base for beverages, converted into brandy or wine by distillation after fermentation.
Leaves - eaten with rice, boiled leaved in syphilitic ulcers, are diaphoretic, leaf extract in digestion.
Stem and root bark - poisonous, cyanophoric properties.
Stem bark - made into rope.
Timber - made into pulp for cardboards.
