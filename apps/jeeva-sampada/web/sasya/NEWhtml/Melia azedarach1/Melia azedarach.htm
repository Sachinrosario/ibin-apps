Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Medium tree indigenous to Baluchistan and Kashmir, cultivated all over India. Found in Subtropical regions like Himalayan foothills and Central Indian hills, East Indian hills and parts of Western Ghats, tropical regions with high rainfall areas of Western Ghats, West Bengal and Assam (wet tropical), occurs in tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plain and the Peninsula (moist tropical), dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical). Suitable for growing around fields and on marginal lands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A moderate sized deciduous tree with cylindrical bole. Bark dark gray with shallow longitudinal furrows. Leaves bi-pinnate or occasionally tri-pinnate. Leaflets opposite or alternate, ovate or lanceolate, acuminate, obtusely serrate, sometimes lobed, glabrous on both surfaces, slightly inequilateral at the base; petiolules short, slender. Flowers fragrant, lilac, glabrous, slightly ribbed outside, faintly pubescent within, acutely 20-toothed; anthers sessile, glabrous, apiculate, one between each pair of teeth. Ovary glabrous, 5-celled. Drupe ellipsoid-globose, 4-seeded.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Under natural conditions, the plant regenerates freely from seeds during rains. Artificial propagation is possible by direct sowing, transplanting seedlings from the nursery or by cuttings and root suckers. The plant grows fast in the early stages.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

The tree is prone to attack by Fomes senex Nees & Mont.

Ganoderma lucidum – root rot from Bihar

Corticium salmonicolor – stem & twig canker and girdling of trees

Cercospora leucosticta – leaf spot

Dendrophthoe falcata – mistletoe, killing branches

Helixanthera ligustrina – mistletoe, killing branches

Loranthus corynitis Spreng. – mistletoe

Loranthus umbellifer Schilt.f. - mistletoe

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel: Wood used as fuelwood. 

Wood used for toys, cigars and ammunition boxes and packing and museum cases. Suitable for sportsware, roofing material and agricultural implements. Useful for furniture, ornamental plywood, turnery and musical instruments. It is a timber tree. Timber is used for construction.

Other uses:

Tree lopped for fodder. Occasionally eaten after boiling with vegetables.

Leaves used as green manure.

The leaves, bark and fruits accredited with insect-repellent properties.

Fruits contain glucose and starch and may be employed for the production of alcohol. A kind of whisky reported to be made from the fruits. Stones of fruits used as beads in necklaces, rosaries.

Fruits considered poisonous to man and animals though sheep, goats and birds have been observed to eat the fruit without apparently any ill effect.

Various parts of tree reported to possess therapeutic value:

Bark & leaves - Bark and leaves are medicinally useful. Also are insecticidal - antifeedant triterpenoids.
Fruits - Fruits are used as beads.
Leaves - Leaves are used as vermifuge, diuretic.
Roots, root bark, stem bark, seeds and heartwood are medicinally important.

Miscellaneous Information

6-8 year-old plantation yields 10-25 m3 annual biomass per ha.
Plant is cultivated as an ornamental. 

Wood: Sapwood yellowish white; heartwood red, turns reddish brown with age, mostly straight grained and coarse textured. Tough, moderately hard, moderately heavy. Specific gravity 0.56, 29-53 lb./ c ft., durable, lustrous with dry feel. It is easy to work, turn, peel and finish.
