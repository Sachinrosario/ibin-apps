HABIT & HABITAT
Medium  sized evergreen tree, and occurs in INDIA: In moist deciduous and semievergreen forests of Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Manipur, Maharashtra and Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Also widely cultivated and planted in garden throughout India. Sri lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Middle sized evergreen trees, up to 12 m tall with umbrella shaped canopy. Leaflets 4-6 pairs, oblong-lanceolate to oblong-elliptic, 6-18 x 3-6 cm, acute to acuminate at apex, entire margin, cuneate at base; petiolules c 5 mm, basal glands apprently absent.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence compact, small corymb; bracts small, ovate to obovate, 1-6 x 1-3.5 mm, persistent; bracteoles erect, oval to obovate, 2-7 x 1.5-4.0 mm, acute at apex, embracing the pedicel. Calyx tube 10-17 x 1-2 mm; lobes oval to obovate, 7-10 x 5-9 mm, rounded at apex. Pods oval to oblong, 4.5-15.0 x 2.0-4.5 cm, compressed, slightly turgid, apiculate.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Bark is astringent and anthelmintic. It is used to cure dyspepsia, thirst, burning sensation, blood diseases, tumours, abdominal enlargement, colic, piles, ulcers, bloody discharges from uterus and in urinary discharges (Ayurveda).
Stem-bark-astringent, antileucorrhoeic, antibilious, uterine sedative, in menorrhagia and other menstrual disorders in general haemorrhoids, dyspepsia, colic, pimples, leaf-blood purifier, in stomachache; flower-uterine tonic, anti diabetic, anti syphilitic; seed in urinary discharges; stem-bark and flower-antibilious, in haemorrhagic dysentery.
BSI: In Ayurveda system of medicine the bark is a strong astringent, refrigerant, alexiteric, anthelmintic, uterine, sedative, demulcent, emollient and use for the treatment of dyspepsia, thirst, diseases relating to blood, burning sensation, biliousness, effects of fatigue, tumours, enlarged abdomen, colic, piles, ulcers, menorrhagia, bone fracture, haemorrhagic dysentery, haemorrhoid and for beautification of the complexion (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933). Leaf is used as medicine in heart diseases. Bark and flowers are used in tonic, urinogenital diseases including menorrhagia and menstrual complaints.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:  Flower gave β-sitosterol; flavonoids and flavone-glycosides – quercetin, kaempferol –3-0-β-D- glucoside, quercetin –3-0-β-D- glucoside and apigenin –7-o-β-D- glucoside, anthocyanins – pelargonidin –3, 5-diglucoside and cyanidin –3, 5-diglucoside; bark yielded catechol and sterols 24-methylcholest –5-en-3 β -ol, 24-ethyl cholesta –5, 22-dien –3 β -ol, a wax containing n-alkanes, esters and free primary alcohols.
