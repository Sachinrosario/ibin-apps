 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A tall, climbing shrub, of the North-Eastern Himalayas, Assam and Burma; occasionally cultivated in the Deccan and elsewhere in India, but  only experimentally, except in Burma where it assumes some importance; distributed through Sumatra, Java and China.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Leaves pinnate, ovate-cordate, membranous; flowers small in clustered cymes; follicles about 7.5 cm long, narrow, tapering; seeds ovoid.

The plant grows readily from layers, slips or cuttings and the plant is permanent like nerium.  So that the plantation once formed will continue for a number of years; and if are allowed to draw a comparison between the leaves of this plant and the those of Wrightia tinctoria, the quantity of the colour they may yield will be in a larger proportion than that from the common indigo plant.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Leaves, bark

Uses

  • The leaves of this climber yield dye like Indigo.  
  • M. tinctoria is cultivated in Northern India, being a native of Sylhet and Burma.  The leaves yield more and superior indigo to the Indigofera tinctoria.
