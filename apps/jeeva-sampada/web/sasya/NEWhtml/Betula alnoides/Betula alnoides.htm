Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Medium sized tree found in temperate Himalayan regions. Suitable for broken marginal lands. Occurs in the temperate and sub-tropical Himalayas, the Khasi and Naga hills and the mountains of Manipur. This is a low level birch of the Himalayas. It often occurs sporadically in high forests mixed with oak and other broad leaved species, as well as other conifers. It tends to become gregarious whenever conditions are favourable for its natural reproduction.

Not a pronounced light demander and grows, as a rule, on more sheltered and moist situations.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A moderate to large-sized tree, with a straight bole up to 30 m in height and 1.2-1.5 m in girth. Bark grey or greyish brown, shining with horizontal oblong lenticels, peeling off in horizontal rolls. Leaves 7.5-15.0 cm long, base cuneate, rounded or cordate.

 

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Tree with catkins of flowers. The male catkins are laid down in autumn as large buds at the end of the years’ growth, the female further back, on leafy branches. In the axils of each leaf of the catkin there are 3 flowers. The bracts of the lateral flowers (a, b) occur, but no bracteoles. In the male, the bracteoles a, b are joined to the bract itself. Each flower has two stamens and a perianth, often reduced from the typical 4 leaves to the 2 median leaves, or even to the single anterior leaf. The stamens are divided into halves nearly to the base; the lateral ones are absent. In the female the bracteoles a, b are free from bract at the time of fertilization, but afterwards they unite with it to form the 3-lobed woody scale under the fruit (or rather the tissue beneath them grows up, carrying up all together). No perianth.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Can be grown by entire transplanting and also by direct sowing.

For artificial regeneration freshly collected seeds are broadcast in shaded nursery beds in summer. Germination is about 15 per cent and is complete in five weeks. Seedlings, 60 cm in height are transplanted preferably in cold weather. Stripping off the leaves before planting is beneficial.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel and also for making charcoal.

Wood: Wood is white or grey, moderately hard, straight-grained, medium fine- and even-textured. It is generally distinctly differentiated into off white sapwood and light greyish brown or grey. It is moderately heavy (wt 510-664 kg/cu m)., strong and fairly durable timber.

Used for plywood, furniture and tool handles. For shoes.

Owing to high cost of extraction and unsteady supplies it does not appear to be very economical for general plywood manufacture. The wood is good in drying and holding glue, and is one of the best Indian woods for aircraft plywood. The timber is suitable for interiors of cabinet work, for turnery, spools, pill boxes, etc. excellent for handles of screw drivers, planes, saws, etc. light clean veneer can also be obtained from it.

Wood yields 69.9 per cent of unbleached pulp, suitable for the manufacture of wrapping paper. The pulp after bleaching can be used for the production of cheap grade writing and printing paper.

Other uses:

Food - Inner bark constitutes an important article of food in the mountain tracts of Manipur. After sun-drying, it is either eaten like biscuits or is made into flour and cooked as an article of food.

Bark - The bark is bound around the earthen vessels as a protection.

It is also planted for shade in tea plantations.
