Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Calophyllum inophyllum is a medium sized to tall growing evergreen tree found in evergreen forests of south India (Western Ghats from North Kanara in Karnataka to Kerala) and grows widely in the littoral forests of Sunderbans and Andamans Islands, Burma, Ceylon and along the east and west coasts of Indian Peninsula, specially in the coastal tract of Orissa, Karnataka and Maharashtra. It is essentially littoral species, growing near the sea shore on sandy places, along river banks, near water resources, and in gardens. It prefers the relatively sheltered, well-drained sites on sandy beaches and dune systems along the coastline. Although it will persist on the drier parts of the coastline, it grows more vigorously and exhibits greater tolerance to exposure on the higher rainfall areas of the tropical coast (1, 2, 25).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
It is a large to moderate sized, evergreen tree that can grow to a height of 15 metres. It has a multi- branched crooked trunk and pale grey bark with shallow longitudinal grooves. The branches or stems are long and smooth and are square in cross-section when young. The stems exude a sticky yellow sap when cut. The simple stiff leaves are arranged opposite each other and are made conspicuous by the densely packed parallel veins extending from the midrib to the leaf margin. These glossy leaves are 10-20cm long, 6-10cm wide, dark green, smooth and leathery. Trees with straight bole of 30 ft. The wood is reddish-white to reddish brown and moderately heavy. 4 ft in girth. The fruit is green and round, turns yellow at maturity, and 3.75 cm in length. The green, globe-shaped fruit are succulent and have smooth, thick skin. They are up to 5cm in diameter, contain only one seed, and hang from a long stalk. (2, 3, 4, 5)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
The showy, white, fragrant flowers 2-3 cm across with numerous yellow stamens are borne in clusters on short stalks in the axils of the upper leaves. Flowers are of two kinds, male and hermaphrodite, sepals 4, petals 4, white; stamens yellow in four bundles covering the bright pink ovary in hermaphrodite flowers (2, 3, 4, 5, 6).

Breeding System :
Calophyllum inophyllum is a cross-pollinated in nature.

Back to Top

 

Phenology :
Flowering occurs throughout the year but the main flowering periods are in January and July.

Pollinators:
Pollinaton is caused by insects. Flowers begin to open at 3-4 am and are wide open at sunrise. The fragrance attracts a wide variety of pollinating insects.

Seed Biology :
The round seed is about the size of a walnut. It is made up of pulp, a hard or soft, seed coat and a single kernel, which is about 2.5 cm in diameter.

Seedling Germination :
Twin seedlings are reported from germination studies under nursery conditions of seeds of Calophyllum inophyllum collected from a 15 year old tree in [Tamil Nadu] India. Four twin seedlings were recorded out of a total of 45 germinated, on the 21st day. The seedlings died after 4 days when separated and replanted. The abnormal development is due to polyembryony not reported before in this species. After germination, seedlings grown to a height of 40-50 cm in 6 months (24, 25).

Cultivation:

Calophyllum is usually propagated from seed. Nursery-raised seedlings in 150 mm pots are transplanted into protected dunal areas when about 45cm tall. Flowering occurs throughout the year but the main flowering periods are in January and July. Seeds germinate easily and seedling growth may be rapid for several years. After this, growth often slows down and subsequent annual increments become smaller. It can be raised without difficulty from seed provided the seed is sown soon after ripening.

Economic Potential and Uses

The oil is mainly used for lighting purposes in villages and for varnishing country boats. It is also used for massaging rheumatic patients and in ointments for wounds. The oil is of excellent quality for soap making, particularly laundry and medicated soaps but is unsuitable for edible purposes because of the presence of toxic non fatty constituents. It is used as illuminant. Refined oil is injected intra-muscularly to relieve pain in leprosy. Seed oil is suitable for general skin and cosmetic purposes. The oil's unusual absorption, its mild and pleasant aroma, and its luxurious richness make it ideal for use in lotions, creams, ointments and other cosmetic products. Oil absorbs readily, leaving skin feeling smooth, plump and soft. The oil adds a glow to skin, without any residual greasiness or oiliness. It is also used as hair oil and in application to boats to avoid fungal growth. C. inophyllum oil had the greatest antifeedant effect and the greatest growth disruptant effects against brown planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens) and the green leafhopper [Nephotettix virescens] . Apart from this, oil is known to have insecticidal properties against Spodoptera litura and Holopeltis antonii (Tea mosquito bug). Seeds of C. inophyllum contain several known coumarins, including the potent HIV [human immunodeficiency virus] reverse transcriptase inhibitors costatolide and inophyllum P. The cake left after oil extraction is used as manure and feedcake. Oil cake is also used in the control of lesion nematode (Pratylenchus zeae) in Rice and Meloidogyne incognita root knot nematode in associstion woth a fungus Fusarium solani. (2, 7, 8, 9, 10, 14, 15, 17, 20, 21, 22, 25, 26).

Uses:

The fruits are also used to make a brown dye. If the trunk is cut it exudes a gum which solidifies. The fragrant flowers are used in garlands.The wood is used for posts, beams, furniture, railway carriage, cooperage and crane shafts. It is employed in ship building especially for keels and for pulley blocks. It is also used for fishing boats and cabinet work. Because the timber does not smell bad, it was also carved into food containers (2, 9).

Harvesting Practices
There is organised method for harvesting instead fallen seeds are manually collected from the forest floor. When the fruits of the tree are collected and cracked open, the kernel inside contains no apparent oil. But when the kernel dried for a month or so, it turns to deep chocolate-brown colour and becomes sticky with a rich, pleasant-smelling oil (8).

Produce and Products

Oil is extracted from the seeds which is valued more for its medicinal importance. Timber from the tree is empolyed for wide variety of uses. Other plant parts also have medicinal value.

The Calophyllum seed oil contains three basic classes of lipids: neutral lipids, glycolipids, and phospholipids. The oil also contains a unique fatty acid called calophyllic acid, and a novel antibiotic lactone and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory agent called calophyllolide. The fatty acid composition of seed oil: Palmitic acid 16.8%, Stearic acid 9.7%, Linoleic acid 23.8 %, Linolenic acid 0.28% and Oleic acid 49.7%. From root bark a new xanthone named caloxanthone D and from root heartwood, another new xanthone, caloxanthone E, in addition to four known xanthones were isolated. (2, 11, 12, 14, 18, 19).

Processing Techniques

On a commercial scale oil is obtained from the seed by expression or by extraction by solvents the former being a common practice in the villages. There are three traditional methods of expressing oil from seed. (1) Boiling method (2) Chappa method and (3) Bullock Ghani method. In boiling method the depulped seed is boiled in water for some time and then dried. Thereafter it is roasted on a hot pan until the shell become crisp and friable. The whole seed is crushed into powder and it is boiled in water for some time after which the oil floating at the top water is separated by decantation. In the Chappa method the powdered kernel is steamed over a vessel of boiling water and pressed between the two limbs of chappa. In bullock ghani method the powdered kernel is sprinkled with little hot water and pressed in a Bullock-Ghani (13).

Other Uses
The gum, bark, leaves, roots, flowers and the oil extracted from the seeds are used in traditional medicine for a wide range of ills. The oil is used for massages, together with coconut oil and flower fragrances. Seeds and leaf extracts of calophyllum possess larvicidal properies.
Specifically, calophyllum seed oil possesses a unique capacity to promote the formation of new tissue, thereby accelerating wound healing and the growth of healthy skin. This process of forming new tissue is known as Cicatrization. For this reason, oil is applied liberally to cuts, scrapes, burns, insect bites and stings, abrasions, acne and acne scars, psoriasis, diabetic sores, anal fissures, sunburn, dry or scaly skin, blisters, eczema and herpes sores and is also used to reduce foot and body odour. Oil is also applied to the neck to relieve sore throat and is massaged into the skin to relieve neuralgia, rheumatism and sciatica. It is employed by Polynesian women for promoting healthy, clear, blemish-free skin and is also used on babies to prevent diaper rash and skin eruptions (7, 8, 9, 23).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

It grows slowly and starts for fruiting after about 10 years growth. A mature tree yields about 40-50 kg of dry fruits which may yield about 17-18 kg of oil. The oil has multiple uses, including medicated soaps, as a hair oil, preventing fungal growth on wooden boats and medicinal purposes (25).
