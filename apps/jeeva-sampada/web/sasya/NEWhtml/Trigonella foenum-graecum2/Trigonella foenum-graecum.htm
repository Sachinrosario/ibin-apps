Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Annual herb. Fenugreek, unique among the spices, is the dried seed of Trigonella foenum- graecum L. and belongs to the family of pulses (Fabaceae). It is cultivated all over the world as a leafy vegetable, condiment and as an important medicinal plant.

Climate and soil
Cultivation both in tropical as well as in temperate regions; mostly in North India as a winter (rabi) season crop and in Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu as kharif crop for seed production. Fenugreek requires moderately cool climate for proper growth and yield, with a moderate or low rainfall of 500-700 mm per year (3). The crop is vulnerable to frost damage at flowering and early grain formation stage. Cloudy weather and high humidity favours incidence of aphids and powdery mildew at grain formation stage, which affects yield and quality. During harvest season there should be dry and moderately cold weather condition. Grows successfully in almost all types of soil having good drainage and rich in organic matter. Best suited is fertile well drained medium black loamy soil such as clay loam and sandy loam, the pH of soil ranges from 6.0 and 7.0.

Availability :

Native of South Eastern Europe and W. Asia, now cultivated widely in India. The other producing countries are Argentina, Egypt and Mediterranean countries (S. France, Morocco and Lebanon). Its original home is the region bordering the eastern shores of the Mediterranean extending to Central Asia. An independent centre of origin exists in Ethiopia. Cultivation for seeds is usually confined to the cool regions, mostly irrigated tracts. Wild in Kashmir, Punjab and upper Gangetic plains (2). Rajasthan, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh are the major producing states. Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Haryana, Maharashtra, Punjab are the other producing states, also cultivated in many other parts of India as a pot-herb.
Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild and cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An erect, stiff, strongly scented, annual herb, up to 60 cm tall. Stem is tender, round, terete, and green to purple with negligible secondary branches. Leaves alternate, trifoliate, stipules triangular, small, adnate to the petiole; petiole 1-4 cm long; rachis short; leaflets obovate or oblong, upper part of the margin denticulate. Plant has tap root system with lateral branches. Nodules are formed on the lateral branches.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers solitary axillary, bluish white-yellow, subsessile, 12-15 mm long; calyx companulate, finely pubescentm, tube 4.5 mm long; standard hood-shaped. Obovate, clawed, pale yellow; stamens diadelphous (9+1); pistil with sessile ovary, glabrous style and capitate stigma.

Fruit:
A straight to sickle-shaped pods, glabrous, beak 2-3 cm long, containing small deeply furrowed 10-20 small, hard, yellowish-brown seeds.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Hybridisation is practiced for adaption to stress, increase number of double pods per plant, etc.

Seed Biology:
Seeds are flat, oblong rhomboidal, 3-5 mm x 2-3 mm with a deep furrow dividing it into two unequal lobes, hard, brownish-yellow having a groove across one corner giving hooked appearance.


CULTIVATION ASPECT:
Seed germination:
Takes 4-5 days.

Seedling development:
First leaf is simple, second usually trifoliate, matures after 3-5 months after planting.

Local cultivars :
Indian fenugreek comes in several well-known cultivars such as 'Desi' and 'Champa' (1), Kasuri, Pusa Early Bunching, UM-5 etc.

Released varieties:
CO-1, CO-2, Rajendra Kanti, RMt-1, Lam Sel.1, Hisar Sonali, RMt-303, Guj Methi-1, Hisar Suvarna, Hisar Madhavi, Hisar Mukta, Pant Ragini, Pusa Early Bunching and Rajendra Khushba.

Chromosome number:
2n = 16

Selection of planting material:
Seedlings raised from the seeds are used as planting material.

Planting:
Field preparation: Field is ploughed two to three times to obtain friable and well aerated tilth, immediately planked to break clods and levelled to conserve soil moisture. If soil moisture is low one pre-sowing irrigation should be given before tillage. Apply endosulfan powder 4% or methyl parathion powder 2% @ 25 kg/ha before ploughing to control termites and insects. Similarly 10-15 kg FYM or compost/ha has also to be incorporated into soil during field preparation.
Seed rate: 10 to 20 kg (depending upon spacing) 20-25 kg/ha, (Gujarat, Bihar and Rajasthan), 30 kg/ha (Andhra Pradesh) and 15-20 kg/ha (Uttar Pradesh).
Sowing time: Fenugreek is cultivated throughout the year. Sowing periods recommended are first week of October to first week of November (Gujarat), middle of October (Bihar), September to December (Uttar Pradesh), first week October-November (Rajasthan) Mid of October to first week of November (Haryana), middle of November and first week of October (Andhra Pradesh)
Sowing method: Seeds are soaked in water up to 6-8 hours before sowing. Seeds are drilled or dibbled in lines at depth less than 5.0 cm or broadcasted.
Spacing: The sowing should be done 30 cm apart in rows with a plant-to-plant spacing of 10 cm at a depth less than 5.0 cm (line sowing). The spacing recommended for different states are Gujarat 30 x 10 cm, Bihar 20 x 10 m, 25 x 10 cm 25 cm row spacing in Rajasthan, 30 x 10cm Haryana, 15 x 10 cm/22.5 x 10 cm (Tamil Nadu) 15 x 10 cm girth 30 x 10 (Uttar Pradesh).
Intercropping: with coriander, sesame or chickpea.

Management after planting:
Immediately after sowing the fenugreek field should be irrigated. Crop requires 6-7 irrigations in light soils and 4 - 5 irrigation in heavy soils at IW/ CPE ratio of 1.0. Irrigation should be imposed 30, 70, 90, 110 days after sowing. Application of 20 kg N and 40 kg P2O5/ha at the time of sowing is essential. Treatment of seeds with Rhizobium cultures (R. meliloti) gives better crop stand, is recommended to growers in all states. Application of FYM @ 20 kg N and 40 kg P2O5/ha at the time of sowing are recommended.

Two weeding and hoeing should be required, first weeding and hoeing should be done at 10-20 DAS and at the same time thinning should be done, second weeding and hoeing at 50 DAS. Application of weedicide Pendimethalin @ one kg/ha pre-sowing is recommended for Haryana, In Rajasthan pre-plant application of Fluchlorin @ 0.75 kg/ha, in 500-600 Lit. Water supplemented with hand weeding once in 50 days after sowing.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:
Aphids - Acryrthosiphon pisum, Aphis craccicora and Ropholosiphum maidis
Mites - Petrobia latens, Tetranychus cucurbitae.
Symptoms: Aphids suck the sap from tender parts and flowers. Crop turns yellow and shrivelling of grains and reduction in yield and quality. The infection is more severe on young leaves. Mites also attack the crop.
Control: Spray Endosulfan (0.07%) or Dimethoate 0.03% Phosphamidon or 1.0% Malathion 50 or Monocrotophos (0.1%) 400-500 Lits/ha.

Leaf early caterpillar and pod borers (Spodoptera litura)
Control: Spray 0.05 Quinalphos or Endosulfan @ 0.075% or Monocrotophos 0.04%.


Diseases:

Root rot: A complex disease caused by Rhizoctonia solani and Fusarium sp.Symptoms: Appears after the initiation of pod formation, yellowing and drying of leaves resulting in premature drying of plants and reduction in yield.
Control: Seed treatment with Thiram or Captan @ 2 to 3 g/kg of seed, application of neem cake @ 150 kg/ha combined with seed pelleting with antagonists like T. viride, T. harzianum (Talc formulation 4 g/kg of seed) followed by soil application of neem cake 150 kg/ha.

Powdery mildew: Caused by Erysiphe polygoni and Levellilulla taurica
Symptoms: White powdery mass on leaf in the initial stage and later on petiole, stem, pods and whole plant with whitish powder. Deteriorates quality and reduces yield and size of grains.
Control: Dusting with 300 mesh sulphur (25 kg/ha) or spraying wettable sulphur or Dinocap (0.25%) or Karathane (0.1%) twice at flowering stage and 15 days interval commencing from 40 DAS @ 400-500 Lits. solution/ha.

Cercospora leaf spot: caused by Cercospora sp.
Control: Two sprays of Carbendazium (0.05%)/ Manacozeb (0.02%)/ Zineb (0.3%).
Downey mildew: Caused by Peronospora trigonella.
Symptoms: At initial stage yellowish spot appear on the upper surface and a whitish growth on the lower surface and at advent of infection the leaves turns to yellow and sheds.
Control: Spray 400-500 Lits/ha of 0.2% blitox/difolatan/fytolan. Repeat the spray after 10 days, if needed. Spray of Zineb (3g/Lit) 15 days interval is also recommended.

Rust: Caused by Basidiomycetous fungi Uromyces anthyllids.
Control: Cultural operation like good field sanitation, destruction of infected plant parts, crop rotation will minimize the infection. Spray Mancozeb 0.2% or Bayleton 0.05%.

Damping off: Caused by Rhizoctonia solani
Control: Spray Carbendazim (1g/Lit) cultural operation like crop rotation and removal of diseased plants. To minimise the source of infection seed treatment with Benomyl (3g/kg seed) or Carbendazim (3 g/kg seed) is recommended.

Nematode Induced Diseases
Affected by nematodes (3).

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Fresh green leaves are used for vegetable and spice, seeds as condiments, flavouring agent in curries and pickles and whole plants as fodder. Basically bitter spice but in appropriate quantity adds the taste. Used mainly in curry powder and in culinary preparation and also used medicinally as a digestive and to promote lactation in both women and in cows, contains diosgenin. Ground seed is used in curry powders, blend, pickling, mayonnaise. Seed or extracts commonly used in syrups, baked foods, condiment, chewing gums and meat seasoning and as an emollient and flavouring agent in pharmacy. Most of the produce is consumed domestically. It has high nutritive value having high protein content. Boiled or parched seeds mixed with honey are eaten as a snack. In West fenugreek and its oleoresin are used to prepare artificial maple syrup and are also used in substitutes for caramel, vanilla, butterscotch, rum and liquorice. Roasted seeds are a substitute for coffee. Chopped leaves add to bitter taste to salads. Fenugreek also grown as a green manure and a cover crop. seeds are a source of dye in India and of industrial galactomannan mucilage. Oleoresin extracted is used in perfumery, cosmetics and hair tonics (3).

Parts Used:

Dried seeds and green.

Harvesting Practices:
Crop should be harvested when it starts turning yellow and majority leaves starts falling. Delayed harvesting result in shattering. Harvesting is done by cutting the plants with sickles or uprooting the plants, early morning. Crop raised in November-December comes to harvest in February-March (about 70-120 days), yields 1200-1500 kg/ha. Leaf cutting yields about 800-1000 kg/ha. in Gujarat, fenugreek grown for leaves is harvested by clipping leaves and young shoots, and allowing the crop to regrow until the next harvest (3).

Post Harvest Handling
After harvesting, crop is dried for 4-6 days and then seeds are separated by beating, followed by winnowing. Fenugreek comes to harvest with in about 70-120 days. Seeds are cleaned and dried under sun and stored in paper lined gunny bags. Seeds meant for sowing purpose can be stored at 9% moisture level after treatment with Captan 2 g/kg in 700 gauge polythene bags for higher viability.
Various grades are: Special, Good and Fair (seeds) Standard (Powder).

Produce and Products:
Fenugreek seed, ground powder, oleoresin.

Medicinal uses
Leaves are refrigerant, aperient and are given internally of vitiated conditions of pitta. A poultice of leaves is applied for swellings and burns. Seeds are bitter, mucilaginous, aromatic, carminative, tonic, thermogenic, galactagogue, astringent, emollient and anaphrodisiac. Good for fever, vomiting, anorexia, cough, bronchitis and colonitis. Externally in the form of poultices they are used for boils, abscess and ulcers. An infusion of seeds is good cool drink for smallpox patients. Powdered seeds find application in veterinary medicine. An aqueous extract of seeds possesses antibacterial property (2). Reduces fever, stimulates digestive process and general metabolism, relive colic flatulence, diarrhoea, dysentery, dyspepsia, dropsy, liver enlargement, spleen enlargement, rickets and gout; particularly useful to cure disturbance after delivery cure diabetes. In Ayurvedic medicines particularly for gastritis, urinary and digestion problems. Diosgenin, an important precursor of oral contraceptives and corticosteroids, has been reported to be present in seeds. Traditionally fenugreek is used to help control blood sugar levels in the case of diabetes, protein deficiency and anaemia. A preparation made out of fenugreek, raw rice and jaggery is given to pregnant women in most Indian village households, apparently to stimulate digestion and to ward of stomach disorders. Taking this preparation during the heavy monsoon season (month of Ashadam) is practiced still by people in rural India. Used to treat oral ulcers and chapped lips. In China used for nephrosis, abdominal pain, hernia and arthritis (3). In ancient time, used to reduce fevers and soothe intestinal inflammation. Tribesmen mixed the crushed seeds to a paste & applied the poultice to battle wounds, boils & other 'open' disorders of the skin. In Egypt it was given to improve lactation for women. Ground fenugreek with cottonseeds was given to increase lactation in cows. In Turkish family life used by women to sweeten their breath & banish body odours. They took a mixture of seeds & honey to improve their allure. In middle ages, used a fenugreek was used for curing boldness & now used in hair tonics in Java. One of most important uses of fenugreek is in the breaking up of mucus in the body. An infusion of seeds, mixed with onion juice & lemon juice relieves inflammation of mucus from intestinal packages, organ & blood streams & dissolves fat in kidneys (4).

Chemistry:
Important chemical constituents are Anethole and methyl chavicol. Volatile content of seed is very low (0.02%) but it is extremely odourous. Fatty oil content is about 7%. Bitter taste is due to presence of alkaloids trigonellene and choline. Quality of fenugreek is judged on the basis of its size, colour, luster, trigonelline and diosgenin content. Seed contains very small quality of essential oil (< 0.02 %). It is also rich in minerals.
Nutritional value of fenugreek*
Carbohydrate - 44.1 g, protein - 26.2 g, fibre - 7.2 g, water - 13.7 g, food energy - 333 (cal.), minerals - 3.0%, thiamine - 0.34 mg, riboflavin - 0.29 mg, niacin - 1.1 mg.
*Chemical composition of ground spice (100 g edible portion)

Fresh leaves contain per 100 g: water - 87.6 g, protein - 4.6 g, fat - 0.2 g, carbohydrates - 4.8 g, fibre - 1.4 g, ash 1.4 g. straw is a medium-protein roughage suitable for maintenance of dairy cattle. Seed contains lysine (6.5%) but poor in sulphur-containing amino acids. They also have trypsin and chymotrypsin. Percentage of diosgenin is 0.5-2%. Weight of 1000 seeds is 10-20 g. Chemical compositon of seed oil resembles that of cod-liver oil and is rich in phosphates, lecithin and nucleoalbumin. In US the regulatory status 'generally regarded as safe' (GRAS) has been accorded to fenugreek (GRAS 2484) and fenugreek oleoresin (GRAS 2486). Maximum level of oleoresin permitted in food products is 0.05% (3).
