DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herb, up to 1.2 m high. Stem and branches usually 6-angled, more or less hairy, usually rough on the angles. Leaves ovate, elliptic or lanceolate, 4.0-7.5 x 2.0-3.5 cm, acuminate at apex, usually rounded at base, more or less hairy above; main nerves 6-8 pairs; petioles 0.5-1.5 cm long. Flowers in trichotomous cymes, in large lax divaricate pubescent panicles; bracts 2, opposite, often very unequal, longer  and shorter 0.4-0.6 cm long, both linear, acute, mucronate with white membranous margin; bracteoles 4, similar to bracts but shorter. Calyx lobes 5, shortly connate, valvate, linear to lanceolate-subulate, 0.2-0.3 cm long, acute with ciliolate margin. Corolla pink, 1 cm across, 2-lipped; tube cylindric, 0.4 cm long, twisted; upper lip elliptic-oblong, 0.3-0.6 x 0.1-0.3 cm, obtuse, entire; lower lip 3-fid, deflexed. Stamens 2. Capsules ellipsoid, 1.0-1.2 x 0.3 cm, acuminate, hairy with a basal solid beak. Seeds orbicular, 0.2-0.3 cm in diam., papillose.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The whole plant macerated in an infusion of rice is said to be a good antidote for snake bite.
A yellowish brown essential oil obtained by steam distillation of the plant, shows tuberculostatic activity in vitro. It inhibits the growth of various strains of Mycobacterium tuberculosis.


Chemistry:
Petunidin-3-rhamnoglucoside was isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).The isolation and spectral data of the new 14-methyl-tritriacont-14-en-15-ol (1) and 35-hydroxynonatriacontanal (2) from the aerial parts of Peristrophe bicalyculata are reported. (Singh et al, 2000).

Ethnobotany:
