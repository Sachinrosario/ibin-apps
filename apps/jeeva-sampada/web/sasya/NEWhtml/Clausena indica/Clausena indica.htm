DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  This is a small tree growing from 3 to 6 meters in height. The leaves are 20 to 30 centimeters long, with 7 to 11 leaflets which are ovate-lanceolate, and 5 to 11 centimeters long. The panicles are 15 to 20 centimeters long, are terminal, and in the upper axils. The flowers are greenish white, fragrant, 5-parted, and about 8 millimeters in diameter. The fruit is nearly spherical or ovoid, almost 1 centimeter in diameter, and whitish when mature.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Leaves.

Uses: 

The leaves when crushed are aromatic. According to Bacon, the alcoholic extracts have a very strong anise like odor. He adds that it is possible that this plant could be use locally in preparing “anisado” which is an alcoholic beverage of the Filipinos.

Chemistry
The oil extracted from the leaves and states that from its distillation a colorless oil is obtained. This oil resembles the fresh leaves in odor and possesses the following constant: Specific gravity, 30°/30°   = 0.963; N 30°/D = 1.5235; and saponification number 3.6. The oil is inactive. It has a faint odor of anise or anethol, which, together with the constant, identifies it almost with certainty as methyl chavicol. The volatile oil contains methyl chavicol to extent of 90 to 95 percent.

 Reyes conducted a photochemical study of Clausena anisum-olens and reports that preliminary test made on aqueous extracts of the leaves showed the presence of tannins and saponins. The volatile oil of the leaves yielded 1 to 2.03 percent; having the following constants; ND30° = 1.5175 to 1.5177; AD 30° = inactive; d 30/30 = 0.9597 to 0.96391; acid number = 1.0809 to 2.2311; ester number (before crystallization) = 3.2689 to 4.3082; (after acetylization) = 5.3485 to 25.1461. The oil showed the presence of methyl chavicol in great amounts. The volatile oil produced narcosis upon dilution with air of 1:450,000 in frogs of varying weights.

Other Uses
A decoction of the roots and fruit is prescribed for coughs with fever, and, of the leaves, for nausea during conception. Guerrero states that the leaves, stuffed into pillows and placed under the head, have a soporific effect, and that they are also used in baths for rheumatism.
