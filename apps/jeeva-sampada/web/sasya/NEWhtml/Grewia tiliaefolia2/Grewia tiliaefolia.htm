Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Moderate sized to large tree found in the sub-Himalayan tract from Jumna to Assam and in central, western and southern india. Moist climates with hot dry summer and mild winter. A light demander and can withstand frost.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

Availability: Largest supply from the south zone – chiefly Coorg and Kanara forests. Moderate supplies from North zone. Small supplies from central zone.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Moderate sized tree. Bark gray or dark brown. Leaves stipulate, ovate with oblique base, acuminate, crenate-dentate.Flowers small, umbellate, buds obovoid-oblong or subglobose, tomentose, peduncles 3 or several, axillary, thick, about equalling the petioles, pedicels 3 5, divergent, shorter than the peduncles; bracts beneath the pedicels linear-lanceolate. Sepals oblong, subacute, pubescent outside, glabrous within. Petals about half as long as the sepals, oblong or spathulate, entire or notched, gland about 1/3rd of the length of the petal, densely white-villous on the margin. Torus rather long, ribbed glabrous, with 5 obscure villous teeth at the apex. Ovary villous; style longer than the stamens, stigma somewhat irregularly 5-lobed. Drupes size of a pea, black, globose or 2 (rarely 4-) lobed, stones 1 2-celled.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Pericladium tiliacearum Mundk. & Thirum. – infects petiole and stem

Puccinia tiliaefolia Ramakr. & Sund. – Leaf rust in Ambalavayal (Kerala)

Ganoderma applanatum – heart rot

Myriangium parasiticum Mont. & Berk. – Stem infection from Maharashtra

Phyllosticta sedgwickii da Costa & Mund. – Leaf spots from Dharwar and Karwar (Karnataka)

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Whole plants, Bark, leaves, fruits and roots.

Uses:

Fuelwood, Dhaman wood used wherever available, for shafts, poles, frames, panels, masts, oars, tool handles, agricultural implements, bent parts of carts and carriages, spokes, felloes, horizontal bars, etc.

Ornamental timber suitable for furniture. Used for picker arms in textile mills and for shuttles, rabbet tubes, bobbins, etc. Used as side props in mine shafts and galleries. Also used for cooperage, golf shafts, billiard cue shafts, cricket stumps and bails.


Harvesting Practices:

Produce and Products: Timber

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:

Green conversion, immersing the stock in water for about six weeks, followed by seasoning under cover gives the best result. kiln seasoned stock retains the original brightness for an indefinite period.

Other uses:

Stem bark applied externally to remove irritation from cow-itch. Also employed in dysentery. Wood, in powder form, used as an antidote to opium poisoning.

Leaves used as fodder.

Bark yield a fiber used for cordage. It has been tried for paper making material.

Fruits - Fruits are edible.

Root - Root paste is used to cure syphilys

Miscellaneous Information

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood. Sapwood greyish brown, distinct from heartwood which reddish brown to brown with darker coloured streaks. Moderately hard to hard and moderately heavy to heavy, average weight 785 kg/m3 (49 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content. Specific gravity 0.67 – 0.88 air dry. Dull, sometimes with a faint smell mildly suggestive of teak. Fairly straight grained and medium to medium-coarse-textured.

A diffuse porous wood, with occasional semi-ring-porous tendency. Growth rings usually distinct, demarcated by dense, darker latewood fibres. Pores small to moderately large, just visible to eye, moderately few to moderately numerous, mostly solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3, often filled with tyloses or chalky white deposits; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly vasicentric, as thin sheaths round the pores, just visible under the lens, sometimes also as fine broken lines in between the rays, indistinct or barely visible even under the lens. Rays fine to moderately broad, the later visible to eye, few, widely-spaced. Ripple marks occasionally faintly visible.

Liable to surface cracking and end splitting during air seasoning. Conversion of logs soon after felling and proper stacking in shade give good results. Easy to work and finish. Can be peeled.
