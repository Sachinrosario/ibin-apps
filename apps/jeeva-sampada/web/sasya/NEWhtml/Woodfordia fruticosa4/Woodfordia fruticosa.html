Chemistry:

·        EIlagic acid, polystachoside, myricetin-3-galactoside and pelargonidin-3, 5-diglucoside isolated from leaves and flowers. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra 1970- 1979)b

·        Cyanidin-3, 5-diglucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970- 1979)c.

·        Octacosanol, b-sitosterol and chrysophanol-8-0-b-D-glucopyranoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970- 1979)d.

·        Quercetin-3-rhamnoside, naringenin- 7-glucoside, kaempferol-3-glucoside, hecogenin and meso-inositol isolated (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980- 1984)a.

·        Graded hydrolysis of gumyielded mixture of neutral oligosaccharides, polyuronic acids, galactose and arabinose (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985- 1989)b.

·        Norbergenin isolated and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980- 1984)c.

·        Lupeol (0.023), ursolic acid (0.022), b-sitosterol (0.008), betulin (0.004), betulinic acid (0.001 %) and oleanolic acid present in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985- 1989)a.

·        Three new dimeric hydrolyzable tannins-woodfordins A, B and C – isolated from dried flowers and their structures established; oenothein B also obtained. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra 1990- 1994)a

·        Quercetin-3-0-(6’’-galloyl)-b-D-galactopyranoside, quercetin-3-O-(6’’-galloyl)-b-D-glucopyranoside. Quercetin- 3 -O-a-L-arabinoside, quercetin- 3 -0- xylopyranoside, myricetin -3 -0-( 6’’-O- galloy1)- b-D-galactopyranoside and myricetin-3-0-6"-0-galloyl)-b-D-galactopyranoside and myricetin- 3-0-arabinopyranoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra 1990- 1994)b

·        Two new trimeric tannins -woodfordin D and oenothein A -isolated from dried flowers and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990- 1994)c.

·        A new tannin monomer -isoschimawalin A -and five oligomers -woodlordins E, F, G, H and I -isolated from dried flowers and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990- 1994)d.

·        Two new antitumor trimeric hydrolysable tannins woodfordin D(5) and oenothein A(13), were isolated  from the dried flowers of Woodfordia fruticosa and their macrocyclic  structures, which have a novel  constituent unit (woodfordinoyl group) connecting the monomers the have been elucidated on the basis of spectral and chemical evidence. (Yoshida et al., 1991).

Three polygalacturonase inhibitors were isolated from dried flower W. fruticosa. These polygalacturonase inbitors are useful for preservation of plant, fruit and vegetable. These are also useful for preservation of plant disorders. (Tsuji et al., 1994). CA 125: 32089b.

Pharmacology:

·        Extract of flowers possessed significant abortifacient activity in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970- 1979)a.

·         The impact of W. fruticosa flowers on the immunomodulatory activity and alcohol and sugar contents of the Ayurvedic drug ‘Nimba arishta’ was investigated by means of model preparation. The use of Woodfordia flowers in model preparations result in a substantial increase of the inhibition of both human complement activity and chemiluminescence generated by zymosan-stimulated human polymorphonuclear leukocytes. The increased biological activity was due to immuno-active constituents released from the Woodfordia flowers. (Kroes et al., 1993).

·        Woodfordia fruticosa is useful in cough. (Aminuddin, et. al., 1995). MAPA 9603-1795.

·        Woodfordia fruticosa is added into the vat as one of the ingredients in almost all the formulations of Asavas and Arishtasthis group and it is supposed to cause ethyl alcohol fermentation. Chemical analysis of Dhatakipuspa gave maximum extractives (32.04%) in alcohol. The alcohol extract showed significant antipyretic and anti-inflammatory activity at a dose of 500 mg/kg body weight in albino rats. (Muzaffer Alam et al., 1995). MAPA 9603-1400.

·        Woodfordia fruticosa is useful in cough. Some of the remedies discussed may be taken up for screening their therapeutic potential on scientific parameters with a view to incorporate them in organized health care programmes. (Aminuddin et al., 1994-1995).

·        Dhatakipuspa (flowers of Woodfordia fruticosa) is added into the vat as one of the ingredients in almost all the formulations of  Asavas and Arishtasthis group and it is supposed to cause ethyl alcohol fermentation. The chemical analysis of Dhatakipuspa gave maximum extractives (32.04 percent) in alcohol. The alcohol extract showed significant antipyretic and anti-inflammatory activity at a dose of 500 mg/kg body weight in albino rats. (Muzaffer Alam et al., 1994-1995).
