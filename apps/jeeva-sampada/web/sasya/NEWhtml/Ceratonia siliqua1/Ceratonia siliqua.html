 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Small, evergreen, dioecious tree, c 10-17 m tall; stem woody with whitish scar-like elliptic spots. It is found in Punjab, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra.


Availability :  
Specimen examined:  Punjab: Hoshiarpur, Apr. 1908, A. Meebold, 5023 (CAL); West Bengal: Indian Botanic Garden, s. legit, 136635 (CAL); Tamil Nadu: Thembirganur, 667 m, 08.07.1912, s.legit, 89 (CAL); Maharashtra: College garden, Poona, 11.08.1902, L.D. Garade, 738 (CAL).  
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant has naturalized in Punjab and in few other regions of the country.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves peripinate; leaflet 2-5 pairs, opposite, elliptic or broadly obovate, 3-5 cm long, rounded at apex, narrowed at base, entire at margin, lateral veins 12-14 pairs, more prominent on lower surfaces, coriaceous, glabrous above, minutely hairy beneath; subsessile. Flowers in axillary raceme, majority unisexual with male and female on different trees, rarely with a few perfect flower on the same tree, scented, white, yellow-green or red, petals absent; calyx small, saucer shaped, lobes-5. Stamens 5, filaments long, disk intra staminal. Ovary stipitate, grey pubescent. Pods 7.5-20 x 1.3 –5 cm, compressed, curved, chocolate in colour. Seeds brown, shining, embedded in pulp, smooth, very hard.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Male Flower: Pedicelled in nearly axillary cymes; peduncles filiform, pubescent, bracts small, subulate, sepals 4, hairy outside, obovate-oblong, concave, petals combined into cyathiform corolla, glabrous within, half the length of sepals, filaments longer than the corolla; Female Flower: in elongate, solitary or twin, axillary racemes, pedicels very short, pubescent or villous, bracts foliaceous, sepals 1 ovate-oblong, villous on outside, petal 1, subrotund, about half the length of sepal, ovary 1, style short, 3-fid; Fruit: Drupe sub-globose, compressed, hairy, red; Seed: Horse-shoe shaped. Flowering & Fruiting : July-September. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: The plant is propagated through seed.

Soil typ : The plant can adapt to a wide range of soil type. However, best soil is sandy, well drained loam but calcarious soil with high lime contant are also suitable. The plant can not with stand water logging and tolerate salinity well.

Nutrient requirements: --

Water management: The plant is xerophyte can survive in dry climate without irrigation. However, requires atleast 350mm annual rainfall.                                      

Harvesting period / Method: Carob harvesting usually carried out at the end of the summer or the beginning of autumn depending upon the cultiver and region. It can be mannual or mechanical.

CIMAP:
The leaf-longevity of the Ceratonia siliqua is mentioned about 2 yr; the raw fiber content increased during leaf growth; thereafter, it remained const. In the leaves having a lifetime of 2 yr, in the spring of the second year an addnl. Increase, although weak, was obsd. The chlorophyll content increased during the leaf growth, then remained unchanged for some time, but began to decrease early. In the leaves of ceratonia the shrinkage was recognized already in the spring of the second year. During senescence, the decrease of chlorophyll ‘a’ was more distinct than that of chlorophyll ‘b’. The total N content decreased during leaf-growth and then remained constant. In Ceratonia leaves, it was resonably high considering that it is an evergreen species. The N-content began to decrease rather early, and intensified during the last months of the life time of the leaves. This was due to the well-known export of N-compounds from senescent tissues. In Ceratonia leaves the N- content increased during the first winter. The content of protein –N in all species showed essentially the same variations as that of total N. Results of tests carried out in Jan. and Aug., revealed that the proline content in the leaves was higher during the drought period than during the cold period1; Total 14C- incorporation immediately after termination of 14CO2 application is a measure of the assimilation rate; it was high and nearly identical in Apr. and May, but low (only about 5% of the Apr. value) in summer and winter. The incorporation of 14C into sol. structural components was highest in Apr. uptake of total 14C and labeling of sol. compounds during the 6 day period of the expts. Show that metabolic rates were very low in summer but high during spring. The turnover of sol. compounds in Feb. was remarkably high regarding the low CO2 fixation rate. The labeling of sol. sugars in leaves decreased in all expts.; the specific activity of starch remained rather stable. The incorporation of 14C into the lipid fraction was weak in all seasons and significant alterations took place only during the growing period. The results do not suggest a specific adaptation of the metab. Of storage compds. to summer drought in ceratonia2.
