Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Mature leaves are used for preparing local liquor called “ Choarak” (Huidrom, 1996). 
Method of preparation “ Chorak” is prepared in apparatus called ‘Bati-bogu’ which is piling of three silver vessels. The bottom of the middle vessel is hollowed and a little below the neck of the same vessel is connected by a small pipe, which is the outlet of wine. The inside end of the pipe is fitted with a big spoon like plate to collect the wine drops inside the vessel and allow to pass down through the down let pipe.

The mature leaves of Allophylus, Antidesma roxburghii and tender leaf of Artocarpus heterophyllus are well sun dried and ground into powder in equal proportion along with few chillies. The powder is mixed with red sugar in the proportion of 5:1 and the mixture is made into paste with rotten boiled rice (rotten boiled means 2 to 5 days after boiling, depending upon the temprature) in the proportion of 1: 15 along with little water. The mixture is made in to rounded plates. This mixture is called “Chuao”. The ‘chuao’ is kept for 2-3 days and again it is exposed to sunlight for one day. Then the ‘chuao’ is kept inside the bottom vessel of the ‘bati-bogu’ and sprinkled some water to wet it completely.

The top vessel is filled up half of its portion with cold water with the intention to liquefy the hot wine vapour around its surface by lowering the temprature. Then the bottom vessel is heated with firewood till boiling. The vopour liberated from the ‘chuao’ is condensed into droplets due to the contact of cold surface of bottom of the top vessel and collected by the spoon-plate and is passed down through the outlet pipe. It is collected in a bottle and transferred to an earthen pot, name ‘Chuak’ for storing. The mouth of the ‘chuak’ is covered with a suitable plant leaf preferably banana leaf. Passing down through this lid, a pipe, named “Chungi” is inserted. Through this pipe wine is consumed by family members (Huidrom, 1996).
