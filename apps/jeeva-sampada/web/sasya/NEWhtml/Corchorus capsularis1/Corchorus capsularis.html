HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, erect, much branched herbs occurs throughtout the hotter parts of India. Cultivated in most tropical countries for the jute of commerce.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual, erect, much branched herbs, 1.0-2.5 m high, glabrous. Leaves oblong, ovate-lanceolate to linear-lanceolate, 5-15 x 1.5-8.0 cm, acute to acuminate at apex, rounded at base, serrate at margin, basal most serration prolonged into filiform processes; petioles up to 4 cm long, pubescent; stipules linear, 6-10 mm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers 1 or 2 in axillary or opposite cymes, subsessile, 8-10 mm across; bracts linear-ovate, 2-3 mm long. Sepals linear-oblong, 4-5 mm long. Petals obovate, 3-5 mm long, notched at apex, yellow. Stamens 20-30. Capsules globose to subglobose, c 1 cm across, depressed at apex, longitudinally grooved, wrinkled, 5-loculed. Seeds cuneiform, c 3 x 1 mm, glabrous, brown.
CIMAP:
 Inflorescence: Short cymes; Flower: Pedicellate, complete, hermaphrodite, yellow; Calyx: Sepals 4-5, free valvate ; Corolla: Petals 4-5, naked at the base, alternate with sepals, valvate; Androecium: Stamens numerous, inserted on very short torus, anther cells two; Gynoecium: Ovary free, 3-5 celled, ovules many in each cell, style short, the apex stigmatose, hollow crenate; Fruit: Capsule subglobose, depressed, broader than longer, ridged, 5-valved ; Seed: Few in each cell, wedge shaped, smooth and brown. Flowering & Fruiting : September-November.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant prefers well drained loam or clayey loam soil with pH 5-7.

Nutrient requirements: Application of ammonium sulphate at the time of active growing period is beneficial.

Water management: Plant requires proper irrigation facilities.                                      

Harvesting period / Method: Plants are harvested before flowering stage.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Leaves are used to treat dysentery.


Chemistry:
Determination of cardiac glycosides in seeds by colorimetry; glycoside mixture contained olitoriside and corchoroside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Veticoside, m.p. 168°C, from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Sucrose, raffinose, stachyose and verbacose isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Erysimoside isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

A new terpenoid – corosin (fig. 1), m.p. 292°C – along with b-sitosterol isolated from roots and structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Pectin, potassium chloride, wax, m.p. 85°C, fructose, and galactose from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

A new glycoside isolated from seeds and characterized as  glucopyranosyl(1à3)-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)-b-D-boivinopyranosyl(1à3)-strophanthidin (Fig. 2). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

A new glycoside – corchoside C – isolated from seeds along with strophanthidin, glycosides A and corchoside B, corchoside C – indicated to be ribose glycoside of strophanthidin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Ursolic acid, oxocorosin and corosolic acid (fig. 3) isolated from roots; structure of corosolic acid was determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A triterpene glucoside and b-sitosterol glucosides from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

A new glycoside (fig. 4) isolated from seeds and its structure confirmed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

A new dammarane glycoside – capsin (fig. 5a) isolated from leaves and its structure and configuration determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation of capsugenin-30-O-glucopyranoside (fig. 5b) from leaves and its characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

A new dammarane triterpene glycoside isolated from leaves and characterized as capsugenin-25,30-diglucoside (fig. 6). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
