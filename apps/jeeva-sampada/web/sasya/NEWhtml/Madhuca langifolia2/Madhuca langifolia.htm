DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Trees; leaves crowded at the end of branches, elliptic or oblong-elliptic; flowers creamish in colour, in dense fascicles near the end of branches; fruits berries, ovoid, 1-4 seeded.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Alcoholic drink is prepared from flowers. (345, Jain 1991)

Methods of Preparation:

Generally two methods are adopted in the preparation of local liquor - 1. Closed process and 2. Tube process.

In closed pot method, generally the earthen pots are used and liquor is collected in the pot, while in tube (Nala) method, a tube is connected to the pot in which droplets are stored and comes out by the help of tube and stored in a pot or cane.

The dried flowers are kept in a pot and some water is added. To this pot, juice of Buchanania lanzan leaves is also added or Ranu tablet is added to help rapid fermentation. The ranu tablets are the mixture of various roots, tubers, stem barks of plants. This mixture is pounded, powdered and dried and tablet is prepared with help of rice flour.

The pot in which mahua and ranu tablet is kept is closed tightly by cloth. After 3 or 4 days when mahua start smelling, then pot is kept on the stove and on this, another pot is placed in which the cold water is filled. The junctions of these pots are tied by cloth. The vapour passes through the middle pot and strikes the bottom of the upper pot in which the cold water is kept. The vapour cools and droplets collect in the middle pot.

In closed system a small earthen pot called dokli is placed in middle pot, in which the liquor is collected. In tube process the vapour passes through the tube, which is connected to the middle pot and collects in cane or another pot. The water of upper pot is regularly changed to keep it cool (Kumar 2001).
