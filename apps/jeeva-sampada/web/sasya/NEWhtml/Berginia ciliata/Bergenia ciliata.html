HABIT & HABITAT:
Availability :  
Temperate Himalaya, from Kashmir to Bhutan between 2300 and 3300 m. and Khasi Hills at 1300 m.
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant grows wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
       A perennial herb with stout rootstock, found in the temperate Himalayas between altitudes of 900 and 3000 m. leaves variable, 5-35 cm. Long, coarsely hairy, sparsely hairy to glabrous, broadly obovate or elliptic; finally or sparsely denticulate or shallowly sinuate dentate; flowers white, pink or purple in long cymose panicles; capsules round.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: Pink, white or purple in spreading or dense clusters, regular, hermaphrodite; Calyx:  Sepals 5, , with marginal bristles; Corolla:Petals 5 ;Androecium: Stamens equaling the petals in number; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, frequently fused at their bases and tapering into the stigma, with flat cup shaped receptacle encircling the ovary, style long, ovules many; Fruit: Capsule spillting along the inner edges of carpels ;

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : March-July.   
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind. 
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 34.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Rhizomes and root taste bitter act as astringent, diuretic, tonic, also used in fever and applied to boils and ophtalmia.  It is an important drug for dissolving kidney and bladder stones.  The flowers are pickled by Gorkhas and Rhizomes are used to cure fever and swollen joints.  Leaves are kept on under cap for relieving fever.  Roots are also consumed in preparing local liqueur.


Chemistry:
  Roots contain afzelachin, bergenin (a C-glycoside),b -sitosterol, gallic.

Four new phenolic compounds 6’-O-protocatechuoylarbutin, 11-O-p-hydroxybenzoylbergenin, 11-O-protocatechuoylbergenin and 6’-O-p-hydroxybenzoylparasorboside, were isolated from the rhizome of Bergenia ciliata together with (+)-afzelechin, (+)-catechin, quercetin-3-O-b-D-xylopyranoside, quercetin-3-O-a-L-arabinofuranoside, eriodictyol-7-O-b-D-glucopyranoside, arbutin, 6’-O-p-hydroxybenzoylarbutin, bergenin (fig. 1), 4-O-galloylbergenin, 11-O-galloylbergenin, p-hydroxybenzoic acid and protocatechuic acid. (Fuji et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 347184d.

Analysis for the mineral elements Na, Mg, Al, K, Ca, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Sr, Mo, Ag, Cd and Ce in Bergenia lingulata was carried out using inductively coupled plasma (I.C.P.). Atomic absorption spectrophotometry and flame photometry. Mineral elements Na, Mg, K, Ca, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Sr, Ag and Ce are present in Bergenia lingulata. (Saily et al, 1994).

Four compounds have been isolated from the Bergenia lingulata rhizomes, among which one is new 4(4’-b-D-glucopyranosyloxy-1’-benzoyloxy)-6-methyltetrahydropyran-2-one named as paashaanolactone (Fig. 2) and the structure was established by detailed spectroscopic studies. (Chandrareddy etal, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9901, 0448.

A reverse-phase HPLC method with an optimized extraction procedure for the quantitative estimation of bergenin and (+)-afzalechin was developed. The method was sensitive, precise and gave more than 99% recovery of the active constituents. The rhizomes of the plant contained higher concentrations of bergenin (0.907% wt./wt.) and (+)-afzelechin (0.168% wt./wt.) than other parts of the plant. (Reddy et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 200979r.

A simple and reproducible high-performance thin-layer chromatography method was developed for the simultaneous determination of bergenin and gallic acid in B. lingulata. Water and methanol were used as the extraction solvents. The concentrations of bergenin and gallic acid in both of these solvents were almost the same. The method involved separation of the components by TLC on a precoated Silica Gel 60 F254 plate with a solvent system of EtOAc- HCO2H-HOAc-H2O (100: 11: 11: 27). The sensitivity of the method for bergenin was 0.30 mg whereas for gallic acid it was 0.25 mg. The proposed method is precise and sensitive and can be used for the detection, monitoring and simultaneous determination of bergenin and gallic acid in B. lingulata. (Chauhan et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 136780x.

Bergenin (fig. 1), m.p. 237°, from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

(-)Afzelechin isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Saxin isolated from roots was identified as bergenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Bergenin, its C-glycoside, b-sitosterol and (+)catechin-3-gallate isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).


Pharmacology:
Roots of B. ciliata are extracted with solvent(s) water, MeOH, EtOH, propanol, 1-3-butylene glycol, glycerin, and propylene glycol and the extracts is added to cosmetic preparations. The cosmetic peroxide scavenging activity, elastase inhibiting and collegenas inhibiting activities and improves skin conditioning. (Yamamoto et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 133, 124964b.

The methanol extract of Bergenia ciliata (tested at 200-1000 mg/disc) showed a wide spectrum of concentration-dependent antibacterial activity. (Sinha et al, 2001).

Bergenia lingulata showed the highest antiinfluenzaviral activity with 50% inhibitory dose of 10 mg/ml in an ethnopharmacological screening plants medicine, which were evaluated for antiviral activity. (Rajbhandari et al, 2001). MAPA 2001, 05, 2610.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for dysuria, menorrhagia (Rhizome, Uniyal, 1968)
