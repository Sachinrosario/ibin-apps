Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A medium-sized, evergreen ornamental tree, found  throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 1,300 m in the sub-Himalayan tract.
These trees will survive a minimum temperature of 25º F. Even then, the trees may suffer and the branch tips may die back 2 to 12 inches. If the freezing temperatures only last a few days, the trees are usually not killed and will sprout below the dead parts of the branches. These trees will grow well in moist, well drained, acidic (pH 5.5 - 6.5) soil in a sunny location. They need shelter from wind. The trees may be pruned once the blossoms have died, but only if the tree is growing out of shape.

It flowers well in well-drained soils and adversely affected by cold temperatures.


Availability : 
Purple bauhinia occurs in wild all over Indian sub-continent.  It is very commonly found along the foot-hills of Himalayas from Indus to Brahmaputra region.  This can also be found extensively in all hill ranges of Central and Peninsular India.  

Distributed in the forests of sub-himalyan region to Assam, East Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Madhya Pradesh, Khandesh, Mysore, Andhra , also in drier parts of Western coasts & Burma.


Status : 
Cultivated  throughout India. Frequent in the forest of eastern ghats. The tree is grown on a large scale as an avenue in all hill towns.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES


Bark dark-gray or brown,  wood dark brown, pink to pale-yellow inside, leaves glabrous, shallowly cordate;  flowers varying in colour from white to purple, in terminal and axillary short-peduncled, few-flowered corymbs; pods firm, flat, glabrous, 12-15 seeded. The tree is moderate light demander and can stand frost well. Bears fragrant flowers of varying shades from white, purple, deep rose to lilac. Pedical short, sepals not fully distinct, exceeding the turbinate tube, petal oblanceolate, glabrous, exserted.

Buds narrow-obovoid , apex obtuse, calyx spathaceous, 2.5 x 1 cm tube turbinate, petals 5, equal and similar, obovate, 1.9 x 0.5 cm, narrow at base, obtuse, stamens 3, filaments up to 6 mm, anther oblong up to 6 mm, ovary compressed , grooved 2.5 cm long.

Seed Biology :

12-15 seeds in each pod, Ovoid, Bright brown , glabrous

Seedling Germination :
These trees may be increased by seeds, suckers, or stem cuttings, rooted in a warm propagating case in the greenhouse. To produce trees with only one trunk, prune out the excessive side shoots while they are still young plants.  Can be raised at site by line sowing. Regular weeding is necessary.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES
Pests: 


Diseases: 

The species is heavily parasitized by Dendrophthoea falcata (Linn. f.) Ettingsh.,Hypoxylon ustulatum Bulliard.  Fomes lignosus (Klotzch) Bres. attack the roots. Cercospora bauhiniae Syd & Syd. has been recorded on the leaves.

Other Uses
  1. Roots carminative but the root-bark is poisonous.
  2. Stem-bark is credited with astringent properties and is used in diarrhea.
  3. Decoction of the bark is a useful wash in ulcers.
  4. Bark is also used in the dyeing and tanning industries. The inner portion of the bark yields a fibre.
  5. Flower buds are eaten as vegetable and also pickled. They are reported to  be laxative and anthelmintic.
  6. Flowers contain quercetin, iso-quercitrin, astragalin and pelargonidin-3-glucoside
  7. Leaves are used as fodder.
  8. Seeds yield a non-drying oil.
  9. Heart Wood which is moderately hard is used for - agricultural implements and matches. Also suitable for scantlings and rafters in interior construction work.
