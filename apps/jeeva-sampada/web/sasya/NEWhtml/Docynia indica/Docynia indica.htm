 Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
A moderate sized tree with handsome foliage common in. E. Himalayas, Nepal, Sikkim and Bhutan (4,000-6,000 ft.) in Manipur and Khasi hills (6,000 ft.). A genus of about five species of evergreen or semi-evergreen trees found in Himalayas, China and Assam. Two species have been reported from India. The plant is well adapted for warmer, temperate or sub-tropical regions and could be improved by selection and hybridization into a valuable fruit tree.
Generally it is common in open areas.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY


Breeding System :

Phenology :
Fruits ripen in August-September. There is also report of flowering in December-February.

Pollinators:
These are pollinated by Insects.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination :
Propagation is by seed and possibly by grafting on apple stock.
Seed - best sown as soon as it is ripe in the autumn in a cold frame. When they are large enough to handle, prick the seedlings out into individual pots and grow them on in the greenhouse for at least their first winter. Plant them out into their permanent positions in late spring or early summer, after the last expected frosts.

Cultivation:
Requires a well-drained soil in a sunny position.This species is only hardy in the milder areas of Britain, tolerating temperatures down to about -5 to -10°c.There is some doubt as to whether this species is distinct from D. delavayi.This genus is closely related to the quince, Cydonia oblonga.
