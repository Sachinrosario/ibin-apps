REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary racemes ; Flower: small, hermaphrodite, white,  ; Calyx:  Calyx small, 5-toothed, pubescent, persistent, lobes bradly triangular-ovate; Corolla: Petals 5, narrowed at the base, imbricate, ovate, pubescent outside, tips inflexed ;Androecium: Stamens 10, inserted at the base of an annular crenate disk, alternately long and short, anthers slightly pubescent ; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, 3-celled, surrounded by the disk, ovule 2  each cell, pendulous, style short, stigma 3-lobed ; Fruit: Drupe trigonous, pyrenes heart-shaped ; Seed: Compressed, penduluos, multifid, radicle superior . Flowering & Fruiting : Febuary- April .Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind or birds.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Carbohydrates, glucosides and b-sitosterol from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

3a-hydroxytirucall-8,24-21-oic acid (fig. 1a), its acetyl derivative (fig. 1b), 3-ketotirucall-8,24-dien-oic acid (fig. 1c) and 3b-hydroxytirucall-8,24-dien-21-oic acid (fig. 1d) isolated from resin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A new diterpenealcohol – serratol (fig. 2) isolated from gum resin and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Isolation and structure of b-boswellic acid (fig. 3), its acetyl derivative (fig. 4a), 11-keto-b-boswellic acid (fig. 4b) and its acetyl derivative (fig. 4c) from resin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Detection of p-cymene, d-limonene, terpinolene, bornyl acetate, a-pinene, a-thujene, a-phellandrene, methyl chavicol, a-terpineol in essential oil of leaves by TLC and GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

a-Thujene, p-cymene, b-Pinene, (+)limonene, linalool, terpineol, methylchavicol, terpinyl acetate, cadinene, geraniol and elemol from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Salai gum isolated and found to contain arabinose, rhamnose, glucose, galactose, idose and glucuronic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Digitoxose, rhamnose, xylose, arabinose, galactose and galacturonic acid isolated from gum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Oil from the oleoresin of Indian plant contained high content of a-thujene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation of 4-O-methylglucurono-arabinogalactan from resin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

A new saponin extracted from B. serrata stems was identified as stigmasta-5,24(28)-diene-3b-O-a-D-glucopyranosyl-a-L-rhamnopyranoside. (Gangwal and Vardhan, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 280786u.

Two new triterpenoids 2a,3a-dihydroxyurs-12-ene-24-oic acid (Fig. 5) and urs-12-ene-3a,24-diol were isolated from the gum resin of B. serrata. (Mahajan et al, 1995).

Four major triterpene acids including b-boswellic acid, 3-O-acetyl-b-boswellic-acid, 11-keto-b-boswellic acid were isolated from the gum resin of B. serrata  (Shao et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 72038y.

Boswellia serrata extract comprising 150 mg boswellic acid as a one of the ingredient in treating bone or joint inflammation. (Weisman, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 242324d.

B. serrata gum resin was examined in a double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Bronchial asthma patients were treated with 300 mg 3 x per day for 6 weeks. Improvement occurred in 70% disappearance of symptoms, dysponea, rhonchi, reduction of attack no., increase in forced expiratory volume in 15, forced vital capacity and peak expiratory flow rate, decrease in eosinophilic count and erythrocyte sedimentation. (Gupta et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 7330u.

Commercially available extracts from Boswellia serrata resin used as anti-inflammatory drugs or phytonutrients show paradoxical concentration dependent potentiating and inhibitory actions on 5-lipoxygenase (5-LO) product synthesis in stimulated PMNs. The tetracyclic triterpenes like 3-0x0-tirucallic acid, acted as inhibitors of the 5-LO in the cell-free assay intiate 5-LO activation by a MEK-inhibitor sensitive mechanism and potentiate stimulated product synthesis in intact cells. Because tirucallic acids contribute significantly to the overall biological effects of B. serrata resin extracts, special precaution for standardization is recommended when using b. serrata preparations a drugs or dietary supplements. (Boden et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 327108q.

Gum resin from Boswellia serrata could be effective in the treatment of chronic colitis with minimal side effects. (Gupta et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 236230g.

A rapid and simple high-performance thin layer chromatographic (HPTLC) method was developed for the simultaneous quantitative estimation of the biologically active triterpenoids beta-boswellic acid, 3-O-acetyl-beta-boswellic acid, 11-keto-beta-boswellic acid and 3-O-acetyl-11-keto-beta-boswellic acid from the gum resin of Boswellia serrata.(Krohn et al .,2001)

An HPLC method for the separation of boswellic acids, the active constituents in Boswellia serrata resin has been developed. The first accurate determination of 6 individual acids was possible in the resin as well as in multi-component preparations. By using an acidic mobile phase, raised temperature and a 4 microm Synergi MAX-RP 80 A column the acids could be detected at levels as low as 0.9 microg/ml. (Ganzera)


Pharmacology:
Salai guggal resin reduced cholesterol biosynthesis in rat liver at 100.0 mg/kg but not at 25.0 or 50.0 mg/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

In patients suffering from ulcerative colitis grade II and III the effect of Boswellia serrata gum resin preparation (350 mg thrice daily for 6 weeks) on stool properties, histolopathology and scan microscopy of rectal biopsies, blood parameters including Hb, serum iron, calcium, phosphorus, proteins, total leukocytes and eosinophils was studied. Patients receiving sulfasalazine (1 g thrice daily) served as controls. All parameters tested improved after treatment with Boswellia serrata gum resin, the results being similar compared to controls: 82% out of treated patients went into remission; in case of sulfasalazine remission rate was 75%. (Gupta et al., 1997)

3-O-acetyl-11-keto-b-boswellic acid induced the most pronounced inhibitory effects on DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis with IC50 values of 0.6, 0.5 and 4.1 mM respectively. (Shao et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 72038y.

Method of treatment of lymphoproliferative and autoimmune disorders with a new composition of four boswellic acids including b-boswellic acid, 3-O-acetyl-b-Boswellic acid, 11-keto-b-boswellic acid, and 3-O-acetyl-11-keto-b-boswellic acid which inhibits DNA, RNA and protein synthesis of the target cell without cytotoxic effects. Composition of invention provides advantage of irreversible cytotoxic effects. Composition of invention provides advantage of irreversible cytostatic therapy, equivalent to biological effects of a cytotoxic therapy with killing body cells. (Majeed and Badmaev, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 344638p.

The extract of boswellic acid or derivative thereof is useful in preparing skin care or hair care compositions with the ability to provide a soothing effect to irritated skin. (Eyre et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 28216m.

A composition suitable for use in the oral cavity comprises at least one extract of a Boswellia plant or at least one boswellic acid or derivatives. The extract is useful in preparing compounds or products with the ability to soothe irritation, or treat inflammation in the oral cavity. (Watkins, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 325441c.

Addition of pure beta-boswellic acid, 3-O-acetyl-beta-boswellic acid, 11-keto-beta-boswellic acid or 3-O-acetyl-11-keto-beta-boswellic acid to human leukemia HL-60 cell culture inhibited DNA synthesis in HL-60 cells in a dose-dependent manner with IC50 values ranging from 0.6 to 7.1 microM. These results indicate that beta-boswellic acid and its derivatives (the major constituents of Boswellin) have anti-carcinogenic, anti-tumor, and anti-hyperlipidemic activities. (Huang et al 2000).

Study examined the effect of the Boswellia extract and its single constituent acetyl-11-keto-beta-boswellic acid (AKBA) on leukocyte-endothelial cell interactions in an experimental model of IBD. Ileitis was induced by two subcutaneous injections of indomethacin (7.5 mg/kg) in Sprague-Dawley rats 24 h apart. Rats also received oral treatment with the Boswellia extract (H15) or AKBA at two different doses. Oral therapy with the Boswellia extract or AKBA significantly reduces macroscopic and microcirculatory inflammatory features normally associated with indomethacin administration, indicating that the anti-inflammatory actions of the Boswellia extract in IBD may be due in part to boswellic acids such as AKBA. (Krieglstein et al, 2001)

Boswellic acids were found to be non-redox, non-competitive specific inhibitors of the enzyme 5-lipoxygenase. Gum resin preparation from Boswellia serrata could be effective in the treatment of chronic colitis with minimal side effects. (Gupta et al, 2001)
