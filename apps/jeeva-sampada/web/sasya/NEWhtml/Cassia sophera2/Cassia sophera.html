REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   

Inflorescence: Axillary few flowered racemes; Flower: Bracteate, pedicillate, pubescent,   ; Calyx: divided to the base ; segments membranous, obtuse green ; Corolla: Petals 5, subequal, ovate, obtuse, yellow ;Androecium: Stamens 10, the three upper reduced to minute staminodes , the remaining seven perfect, the 3 longer with larger anthers than the four lateral ones, of which one is reduced to staminodes; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, at the bottom of the calyx, ovules many, style short stigma many ; Fruit:  Pods 3-4, slightly recurved, somewhat turgid, septate between the seeds, not or scarcely torulose; Seed: 30-40, broadly ovoid, acute, compressed, dark brown .


Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : November- Janaurary.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant thrives on wide range of well drained soils from loams to heavy clays but is largely grown coarse, gravelly soil with pH ranges between 6-8.5.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers especially under growth conditions. Where the soil fertility is low to medium the crop nitrogenous fertilizers and also farmyard manure are effective.

Water management: It is rainfed kharif crop requires little irrigation particularly if rains are irregular during the period of growth

Harvesting period / Method: Crop is harvested for leaves and pods, when they are completely mature.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:
Rhamnetin-3-O-b-D-glucoside isolated along with chrysophanol from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

3,5,3’,4’,5’-pentahydroxy-7-methoxyflavone-8-C-L-rhamnopyranoside isolated from leaves and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Two new isomeric anthraquinones (I, fig. 2a and II, fig. 2b) isolated from heartwood along with n-octadecyl alcohol and quercetin and their structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

A new anthraquinone – sopheranin (fig. 1) isolated from heartwood along with b-sitosterol, chrysophanol, physicon and emodin and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Two new anthraquinones – 1,8-dihydroxy-3,6-dimethoxy-2-methyl-7-vinylanthraquinone and 1,3-dihydroxy-5,7,8-trimethoxy-2-methylanthraquinone – isolated from root bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation and structure determination of 1,8-dihydroxy-2-methylanthraquinone-3-neohesperidoside, sitosterol, chrysophanol and physcion also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

From the seed of C . Sophera a new hydroanthracene derivetive named Presengulone was isolated together with physion bianthrone, xanthrone . (Alemayehu et al., 1998)

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine on piles (Leaf, Singh, 1988); or scabies (Whole plant paste, Lakshmanan and Sankaranarayanan, 1988), Edible as vegetable (Leaf, Chaudhuri et al, 1985)
