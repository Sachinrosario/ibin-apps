 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Small, crocked tree with dark scabrous bark; branches drooping. It is found in Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves 1-5 x 2.5-6.3 cm, broader than long, divided into 2-rounded lobes, green and glabrous above, whitish beneath, cordate at base. Flowers in terminal or leaf-opposed raceme, rachis densely pubescent; buds pointed, bracts acute. Calyx pubescent, spathaceous, 6-8 mm long, petals oblanceolate, acute, white or pale yellow. Pods stalked, blunt towards apex, tapering towards base, glabrous. Seeds 12-20, compressed, oblong, black, rounded at apex.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Terminal or leaf opposed racemes; Flower: Bracteate, pedicillate, white, hermaphrodite, buds pointed ; Calyx: pubescent, tube very short, limb spthaceous, at length reflexed; Corolla: Petals 5, sub-equal, imbricate, narrowly oblanceolate, acute, white or pale yellow ;Androecium: Stamens 10, all fertile, filaments densely hairy at the base, free, filiform, anthers versatile, dehiscing longitudinally ; Gynoecium: Ovary pubescent, stalked, ovules many, style long, stigma small,  ; Fruit: Pods stalked, blunt at the apex, tapering to the base somewhat, falcate, glabrous, turgid, not r scarecly veined ; Seed:  12-20, oblong, compressed, rounded at the apex, black .

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine barks and leaves are used as refrigerant, antipyretic, astringent, alexipharmic and vermicidal. They are useful in urinary discharges, thirst, headache, fevers, skin and throat diseases, tumours, blood diseases, chronic dysentery and diarrhoea.


Chemistry:
Lauric, myristic, palmitic, stearic, oleic and linoleic acids identified in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Lauric (0.1), myristic (0.1), palmitic (11.0), stearic (3.6), oleic (20.6) and linoleic (64.6%) acids determined in seeds by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

New dibenzo(2,3:6,7)oxepin derivative – pacharin (fig. 1)– isolated from heartwood and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation of new tetracyclic phenol – racemosol (fig. 2)– from heartwood and its crystal structure determination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Pacharin synthesized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

 Two germplasma of the tribal pulse, Bauhinia racemosa Lamk. Viz., Ayyanarkoil Forest and Mundanthurai Wildlife Sanctuary, was analysed for proximate composition, total (true) seed protein fractions, amino acid composition, fatty acids, minerals and antinutritional factors. Crude proteins, crude lipids, ash and nitrogen free extractives constitutes 19.84%, 9.52%, 3.31% and 60.65%, respectively in Ayyanarkoil Forest germplasm; whereas, in Mundanthurai Wildlife Sanctuary germplasm they constituted 19.31%, 8.94%, 3.81% and 61.30% respectively. The caloric values were found to be 407.64 Kcal (Ayyanarkoil Forest) and 402.90 Kcal (Mundanthurai Wildlife Sanctuary) germplasms. Essential amino acids like isoleucine, tyrosine, phenylalanine and lysine were found to be high in the seed proteins of both the germplasms. The fatty acids, palmitic, oleic and linoleic acids, were found to be relatively higher in the seed lipids of both the germplasms. Both the germplasms seemed to be rich source of calcium, potassium, magnesium, zinc, manganese and iron. Antinutritional substances like total free phenols, tannins, L-DOPA and phytohaemaggulutinating activity were also investigated. (Mohan and Janardhanan, 1994)

The isolation and structural determination of a new tetracyclic 2,2-dimethylchroman derivative, de-O-methylracemosol (fig. 3) is presented. (Prabhakar et al, 1994).

Bauhinia racemosa has albumins and glutelins predominant seed proteins. (Mohan and Janardhanan, 1995). MAPA, 1996, 9602, 0982.

Analysis was carried out on Bauhinia racemosa seed flour; physical and chemical properties were also determined on the oil extracted from the seed by solvent extraction. The analysis indicates that the seed flour contains protein (22.80) total ash (5.20), moisture (18.60), fat (15.53), crude fiber (1.91) and carbohydrate by difference (36.12). The physical and chemical properties show that the extracted oil has sp. gr. 0.89, refractive index 1.46, acid value 5.46, peroxide value 5.63, iodine value 13.52, saponification value 343.73, and unsaponifiable matter 4.23. Generally the results showed that the seed flour and the extracted oil from the seed will be good for industrial purposes. (Amoo and Moza, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 207149v.

 From the leaves of Bauhinia racemosa  kaempferol, quercetin, kaempferol-3-O-rhamnoside and quercetin were isolated. The identity of each flavonoid was determined by spectral analysis. (El-Hossary et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 219771w.


Pharmacology:
Leaves and bark used in skin diseases; decoction of leaves used to treat headache in malarial fever. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Methanolic extract of Bauhinia racemosa (flower buds) was find to decreased the ulcer index significantly. (Akhtar and Ahmad, 1995).

Pharmacological properties of different extracts of Bauhinia racemosa leaves was examined for anti-inflammatory analgesic and antipyretic activity. (El-Kahtib et al, 1995). C.A. 1997, 126, 271832r.

Pharmocognosy:
Morphological, anatomical and phytochemical studies of Kanchnal, the drug identified as the bark of Bauhinia racemosa, was carried out, which shows some of the diagnostic features recorded during this study are: the thick, rough bark, extremely fissured but internally smooth and fibrous, thick cork zone, distinct conducting and non-conducting phloem region, phloem fibers very long with intrusively grown species and arranged in tangential bands tannin and resin in abundance but completely devoid of chlorophyll and aleurone grains. These characters are helpful checking the adulteration or substitution of the drug. (Hashmi and Hussain, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9902, 0869.
