Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Erect branched herb, very common in grasslands, wet fields growing from foothills to 2450 m: even in poor soil (wide amplitude of occurrence), but plants at extremities differ in several features. Low altitude: plants well-branched, to 75 cm and green. High altitude: with increasing altitude plants become smaller, even to 10 cm, and turn purple.Distributed in India and Sri Lanka
Availability :

India (W. Peninsula)
Conservation/ Threat Status : Common 

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Branched herb. Leaves pinnatifid below, 3-fid to linear above, to 4 cm long, progressively reduced upwards. Flowers solitary, axillary, 2-3 per node, forming a lax, terminal leafy raceme. Calyx-tube 5-ribbed, 6 mm; lobes 5, subulate, 1 cm, scabrid, sparsely ciliate. Corolla purple, 1.2 cm wide; tube 1.2 cm, lower lip enveloping the upper lip; lobes 5, broad-ovate, 6 mm, undulate. Stamens 4; filament pairs 4 and 5 mm; fertile lobe oblong, 2.5 mm; sterile one linear, 2 mm. Ovary 2-celled; style 1 cm, hispid. Capsule oblong, 2-4-valved, 7 x 4 mm; seeds striate.Annual with much dissected leaves and fairly large flowers of ornamental value

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
As a ornamental flowering plant.

Parts Used:

plant juice
The juice of the plant is employed by laborers in the Deccan as an astringent to heal sores on the feet caused by exposure to moisture. It stains the skin at first yellow and subsequently black. (Watt, Dict. Econ. Prod. 1. c.) Cooke.
