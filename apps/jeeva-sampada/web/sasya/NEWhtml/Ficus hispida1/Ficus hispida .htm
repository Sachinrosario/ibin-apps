DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES 
A moderate-sized tree or shrub with large opposite leaves,4-12 inch long, and obovoid or turbinate fruits,c. 1 in. long, borne in pairs or clusters on leafless, often trailing, branchlets. The tree is common throughout the outer Himalayas from Chenab eastwards to Bengal, central and south India, and Andaman Islands. It occurs in shady places and along ravines and flowers  and fruits practically throughout the year.

Other Uses

1. The fruits are made into Jam.

2. Immature fruits are eaten in curries.

3. They are considered tonic, lactagogue and emetic.

4. The leaves and twigs of the tree are lopped for cattle and elephant fodder.

5. The leaves are used for poulticing boils.
