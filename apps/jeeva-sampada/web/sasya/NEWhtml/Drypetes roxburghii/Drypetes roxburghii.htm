HABIT & HABITAT

Evergreen trees; branches pendent. Leaves coriaceous, elliptic-oblong. Flowers axillary. Male flowers clustered. Females 1-3 in axils. Ovary pubescent; style recurved; stigma crescent shaped. Drupes ellipsoid, white tomentose. Stone rugose, hard.

Throughout tropical India, wild and cultivated, Ceylon, Burma.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An evergreen tree, 9-12 m high with pendent branches, nearly glabrous except the young shoots; bark corky, pale. Leaves coriaceous, dark green, shining, 6.3-10 x 2.2-3.8 cm, elliptic-oblong, acute, obtuse or shortly acuminate, distantly serrulate; main nerves numerous, slender, with reticulate venation between; petioles 6 mm long; stipules small, triangular, acute, soon falling. Flowers dioecious. Male flowers very shortly pedicellate, crowded in rounded axillary clusters on the main or on short axillary branches. Sepals 2.5 mm long, oblong, obtuse, ciliolate. Filaments 1.5-2 mm diam. Female flowers 1-3 in an axil; pedicles 8-13 mm long. Sepals broadly elliptic, obtuse, concave, ciliolate, 2.5 mm long. Ovary finely silky, 3-celled; styles 3, recurved, dilated into broad fleshy crescent-shaped stigmas. Drupe 1.3-2 mm long, ellipsoid, rounded or pointed at the apex, narrowed at the base, white-tomentose, with pedicels 1.3-2.5 cm long; stone pointed, rugose,

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Uses
The plant is fragrant, cooling, pungent; aphrodisiac, laxative, diuretic; good for eyes; useful in biliousness, thirst, burning sensations, erysipelas, elephantiasis; causes "vata" and "kapha" (Ayurveda).
The leaves, the fruits, and the stones of the fruits are given in decoction in colds and fevers.
The oil from the seeds has been examined by Krishna and Puntambekar (Jour. Ind. Chem. Soc.; June, 1931).
