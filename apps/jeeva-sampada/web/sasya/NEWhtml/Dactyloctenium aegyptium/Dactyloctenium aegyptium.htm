HABIT & HABITAT                                                                          
An annual, very variable grass with erect or creeping culms, 6-18 inch long, rooting arid branching proliferously at the nodes. The grass is common throughout the plains of India and ascends up to 5,000 ft. in the hills. common during the monsoon period on the ridge in depressions and amongst bushes and shrubs and as a weed in cultivated fields and open places often becoming abundant.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 An annual, very variable grass with erect or creeping culms, 6-18 inch long, rooting arid branching proliferously at the nodes. It assumes a prostrate habit on poor dry soils and is a weed in open places and cultivated fields. culms rooting at the base and from the branched niodes. leaves distichous, narrowly linear, flat. spikes 2-6, digitally radiating; rachis keeled. spikelets densely crowded, spreading at right angles to the rachis. lower involucral glume cuspidate; upper awned. floral glumes mucronate or awned. grains obovoid-globose, rugose. It bears, when mature, an inflorescence of 2-6 digitate spikes. Grain reddish, rugose and sub-globose.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Grains.

Uses

  1. The seeds of the grass are eaten in times of scarcity both in India and Africa. They are ground into flour and made into cakes. They have an unpleasant taste and are reported to cause internal disorders.
  2. It is a useful fodder for horses and cattle, but its fodder value is reported to be below average.
