Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A small poisonous evergreen tree in tidal marshes along the coasts of the Presidency. Common in intertidal forest, often extending into muddy seashore; in association with Avicennia officinalis. Species occur along both the coasts from Sunderbans to south of Narmada estuary in the tidal swamps and in the Bay Islands. Distributed in the mangrove forests in India, Sri Lanka, Burma, tropical S. E. Asia, N. Australia and New Caledonia, Indian Archipelago.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Availability: Considerable supplies from Sunderbans, Konkan and South Kanara. Moderate supplies from Andamans.

 

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small poisonous evergreen tree with white highly acrid juice, often 15 m tall, trunk up to 70 cm in diameter at base, bark greyish, lenticelled, wood soft, light. Plant parts with milky sap; root system without a prominent main root, many laterally spreading, superficial, snake like roots producing elaborate pegs from supraterranean bends. Leaves alternate, thickly coriaceous, 2-4 by 1¼-2 in., elliptic, acuminate, entire or shallowly sinuate-crenate, base acute.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers minute, fragrant, yellowish-green; bracts rounded, fleshy.

Male Flowers: Sessile, in numerous catkin-like spikes 1-2 in. long; bracts with 1 flower and several minute bracteoles. Sepals minute, unequal, subserrulate. Stamens long, exserted.

Female Flowers: Pedicellate. Racemes few, ½-1 in. long, the upper flowers usually imperfect. Sepals broadly ovate, acute, subserrulate. Styles free nearly to the base.

Capsules very variable in size, ¼-1 in. in diameter. Seed subglobose, smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:Wood used as fuel. Calorific value: 4,767 cal., 8,582 Btu. Also used to make charcoal.

Wood: Wood is white when first exposed changing to yellowish grey with age and is frequently discoloured with sap stains. The heartwood is not distinct. it is light (24-31 lb/ cu ft.) Soft, spongy, straight grained, fine and even textured, not very durable, 3.18% ash.

Wood used in making paper pulp for boards and in soft-wood industry.

Wood is used for general carpentry and packing cases. also used for toys, legs of bed steads and floats for fishing nets. Suitable for match splints of the second quality.

experimental trails have shown that the wood can be used for power alcohol manufacture. Wood used for incense.

Other uses:

Latex is sometimes used as a caustic in the treatment of obstinate ulcers. Jiuce boiled in oil is applied in rheumatism, leprosy and paralysis.

Latex is a drastic purgative and abortifacient.

Used as a fish poison and as an adjunct to arrow poison.

Leaves are poisonous. The tree is listed among those occasionally poisonous to stock.

Decoction of leaves is given in epilepsy and used as an application to ulcers.

Bark has strong emetic ansd purgative properties.

Root is an ingradient of embrocations used for swellings of hands and foot.

Miscellaneous Information

A common back mangrove tree. Latex causes blinding.

All parts of the plant contain a light yellow acrid latex with a repulsive odour when fresh and produces blisters on skin and cause blindness.
