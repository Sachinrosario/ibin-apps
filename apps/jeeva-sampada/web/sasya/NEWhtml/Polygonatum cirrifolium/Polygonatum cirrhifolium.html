DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem 0.5-1.2 m long, very weak , flexuous, often climbing by the tips of leaves, puberulous or glabrous. Leaves 3-6 nately whorled, 7.5-12.5 cm long, gradually narrowed into the slender revolute tips, membranous, glaucos and costate beneath. Flowers white green or purplish; peduncles 2-4- fid, whorled. Perianth subcylindric. Berry 0.6- 0.8 cm in diam., variable in size and number of seeds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  The leaves of the plant are eaten as vegetable. The plant is used as a tonic and vulnerary. The roots are used for washing purpose. The rootstock is sweet, cooling, emollient, aphrodisiac, galactagogue, depurative, vulnerary, febrifuge, expectorant and tonic. It is useful in burning sensation, agalactia, skin diseases, wounds, ulcers, tuberculosis, fever, and vitiated conditions of pitta, cough, bronchitis and general debility.


Chemistry:  

Two new steroidal saponins - sibricoside A (PS-I) (fig. 1a) and sibricoside B (PS-IV) (fig. 2b) isolated from rhizomes in addition to neoprazerigenin A-3-O-b-lycotetraoside (PS-III) (fig. 2a) and its methyl proto-type congener (PS-II) (fig. 1b); new compounds characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
