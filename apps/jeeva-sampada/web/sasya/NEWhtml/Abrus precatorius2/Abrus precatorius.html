DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves 5-12 cm long; leaflets 10-20 pairs, oblong, 0.5-1.8 x 0.3-0.8 cm, glabrous above, appresed hairy beneath. Flowers pinkish purple, fading white in many-flowered terminal and or axillary, dense racemose 2.5-15 cm long; vaxillum broadly obovate, wings narrowly oblong-falcate, keels curved. Pods 2-4 cm long, beaked, silky appressed hairy. Seeds 3-5, ellipsoid, 5-6.5 x 4-5 mm, blood red, with a black lateral blotch around the hilum.

Economic Potential and Uses
 

UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine fruits, roots and leaves are used as aphrodisiac, tonic and useful in eye disease, lecucoderma, itching, skin diseases, biliousness, asthma, tuberculous gland and head complaints. In Unani system of medicine fruits, roots and leaves are used as brain tonic.

Pharmacology:

  • Reputed for antitumor properties in Ayurvedic medicine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969).
  • Alcoholic extract of seeds showed parasympathomimetic effect on smooth muscle of gunea pig and rabbit and skeletal muscle of frog. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
  • The cytotoxicity of A. precatorius seeds been studied on the chromosomes of the tested of P. picta    and some ciliates. It is observed that the protein fractions of the seeds induce stickiness, spindle derangement, fragmentation of chromosomes and produce morphological and nuclear changes in the ciliates. (Desai and Sirsi, 1971).
  • Aqueous extract showed anthelmintic activity, which was absent in extract prepared by boiling seeds in water. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
  • The amino acid composition of the isolated A-and B-chains of the toxic lectin abrin was determined, the intact lectin contains no free SH-groups whereas the isolated A- and B-chains contain close to one free –SH group each the results indicate that in both toxins the A- and B-chains are connected by a single S-S bond. The B-chains of abrin contain similar amounts of mannose and glucosamine. The A-chain of abrin appears not to be a glycoprotein. The non-toxic abrus agglutinin contain more carbohydrate than abrin. The intact lectin is much resistant to heat, freezing and chemical treatments than the isolated A- and B-chain. The intact lectin are also very resistant to treatment with proteolytic enzymes, whereas the isolated chains are easily digested. Evidence indicating that the toxin and their chains undergo extensive conformational changes upon reduction of the S-S bond is discussed. (Olsnes et al., 1975).   Abrin from the seed of Abrus precatorius is a potent lymphocyte mitogen. The extent of stimulation of BALB/C and CBA strain mouse spleens by this factors is significantly greater than that attained with PHA or PWM. Abrin has been purified to electrophoretic homogeneity, physically characterized, and may undergo conversion from a toxic to mitogenic from. (Kaufman and Mc Pherson, 1975).
  • The major platelet membrane glycoproteins, was solubilized in 1.0% sodium deoxycholate and then sulyected to affinity chromatography on the lectin from Abrus precatorius.  The binding of A. precatorius derived lectin to glycoprotein 1b has been strongly implicated as a necessary step in the aggregation response of platelets to lectin (Clemtson et al., 1977).  
  • Seed extracts of A. precatorius, precipitates some human serum-glycoproteins. These proteins mainly include a2-macroglobulin, haptoglobin, bemopexin, siderophilin, orosomucoid and the three class of immuno-globulins IgA,IgG and IgM. The bemagglutinin of the seeds play a major role in this precipitation but other seed proteins seem to be involved in the phenomenon. (Rouge et al., 1977).
  • Anit tumour proteins abrin-A and abrin-B were isolated from Abrus precatorius. (Lin et al.,1978)
  •   The seed of A. precatorius contains phytotoxin which are among the most potent toxin. (Kinamore et al., 1980)
  •   Abrin A is toxic to cell free protein syntheses and bind D-galactose (Herrmann, and Behnke, 1981).
  • Biological property of abrin A was similar to those of Abrin C. It was toxic to cell free protein synthesis. (Herramann and Behnke 1981)
  •   The ethanol extract leaves of A. precatorius has poisonous neuronal A neuromuscular effects. It inhibited acetylchoine-induced contraction of both toad rectus abdominis and rat phrenic nerve-diaphragm muscle preparation. It also caused flaccid paralysis when injected intravenously into young chicks. (Wambebe and Amosum, 1984).
  • Abrusosides A, B, C and D were not acutely toxic to mice and did not carse mutagenesis in Salmonella typhimurium strain T. M 677, they were 30-100 times sweeter than sucrose.   (Choi et al., 1989).
  •   Steroidal fraction of seeds of A. precatorius caused dose dependent degenerative changes in the testicular weights, sperm count and indirectly effecting the pituitary level by feedback mechanism later stages of spermatogenesis and Leydig cells leading to decrease in production and release of testosterone which result in significant alteration in th testis of rats. It causes decrease in the enzyme activity of 3 a , 3 b , 17 b - hydroxysteroid dehydrogenases, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenases, sorbitol dehydrogenases and leucine aminopeptidase. (Sinha and Mathur, 1990).
  • The methanolic extract of A. precatorius seeds exhibit sperm antimotility properties with the EC50 concentration being 2.29mg/ml. (Ratnasooriya et al., 1991).
  • The boiled seeds of A. precatorius are eaten by the residents of the Andaman Islands in India as tribal pulse. Seeds of A. precatorius are rich in most of the essential amino acids, and they are deficient only in cystine and threonine, when compared to WHO/FAO requirement pattern. It also contains certain antinutritional factors namely free phenols, tannins, trypsin inhibitor activity and haemagglutinating activity. (Rajaram et al., 1992.).
  • The four-chain agglutinins from A. precatorius inhibits mammalian protein synthesis at nanomolar concentration but do not affect plant protein synthesis under the same conditions, therefore should be considered as new class of true Ribosome inactivation proteins RIPs. (Citores et al., 1993).
  •   Plant extract from A. precatorius is used to treat Schistosoma haematobium infections a (urinary schistosomiasis.) It is lethal to adult Schistosomes. (Ndamba   et al., 1994).
  • Hamester submaxillary glycoprotien (HSM –Tn) reacted with A.precatorius agglutinin and precipitated over 81% of the lectin nitrogen added. (Wu et al., 1994)
  • Two galactose specific lectin A.precatorius agglutinin showed a stronger reactivity with IgA-1, mycloma proteins as compared to IgA2. (Wold et al., 1994).
  • Abruquinonnes A, B and D shows inhibitory effects on the platelet aggregation and strong anti-inflammatory and antiallergic effects. (Kuo et al., 1995).
  • Abrin-a, a type II ribosome-inactivating protein from the seeds of A. precatorius. (Tahirov et al., 1995).
  • Abruquinone A, an isoflavanquinone from A. precatorius shows anti-inflammatory effect. (Wang et al., 1995).
  • P1 active glycoprotein with terminal Gal a 1 ® 4 Gal b 1 ® 4 GlcNAc   sequence is one of best glycoprotein receptors for recognizing the toxic lectins (abrin –a). (Wu et al., 1995).

The protein synthesis inhibitory activity of AAG-A (IC (50)=3.5 nM) was weaker than that of ABRa (0.05 nM). (Liu et al., 2000). B.A. 2000, 107(17): 243846.
  • Gunja is attributed to the plant A. precatorius   L. Three forms with red brown and another with white seeds are known to occur in this species and are employed for different therapeutic uses viz. as purgative, emetic, aphrodisac, tonic and also as an abortifacient according to some Ayurvedic literature the seeds are poisonous and should be given to the patients after proper processing (“Shodhan”). A comparative study of various phytochem. Parameters, namely, percentage of successive extractives, total proteins, tannins, total ash and acid insoluble ash of these three forms of the processed with low’s was done TLC and densitometric scanning of successive extractives was also carried out to serve as markers for processed and unprocessed seeds. The percentage of proteins, tannins, alcohol and water soluble extractives decreased in the processed material. besides, their acute toxicity, CNS activity were also studied in albino mice and it was found that white seeds are more toxic as compared to red and brown the toxic effect was reduced with the processing. Further, the “kanji” processed peeds we less toxic than this milk processed ones. (Gautam et al., 1999). B.A. 2000, 132, 83464x.
  • Parallel solution, S-alkylations of a 1-thio- b -D-galactopyranoside derivatives with Michael acceptors and a -chloroketones followed by ketone reduction, reductive aminations and acylations were developed to yield a library of 1-thio- b -D-galactopyranoside carrying small and diverse polar-neutral, hydrophobic, aromatic, cationic or anionic non-carbohydrate aglycon structures screening of the library against a panel of galactose recognizing plant lectins revealed micro mole inhibitors of toxin a of A. precatorius superior to the reference ligands lactose and N-acetyl lactosamine. Such small monosaccharide based inhibitors are attractive lead-molecule for therapeutic development, since they are molecular weight, hydrolytically stable and more hydrophobic then natural oligosaccharides. (Nilsson et al., 1999). B.A. 2000, 132, 334691r.
