DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 A medium sized tree, attaining a height of 50' and girth of 4', leafless only for a very short time. The dark gray bark is thick and is divided into small rectangular plates, resembling crocodile- hide.  Leaves  9- 30 X 3.5-11 cm,  glabrous above, silky pubescent to villose beneath, elliptic, - oblong;  Flowers creamy white, The fruit is a black single -seeded drupe, 1/2" in diameter. The tree is found in dry deciduous forests throughout India and Burma.
The Wood is light gray to grayish- brown, sometimes with a faint yellowish ting and in large trees there is a small amount of dark-brown heartwood.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Leaves, Fruits, oil.

Uses: 

  • The leaves have been reported to be used for fodder in Bombay and in the Punjab, and the bark and the bark and the fruits to furnish a natural varnish. The fruits are eaten by the hill tribes of Central India.
  • The kernels are eaten raw or roasted and form a substitute for almonds, and are much used in the manufacture of sweetmeats.
  • The light yellow oil is sweet and has a pleasant aroma. it is reported to be used as a substitute for olive oil and almond oil.
    The dark coloured ripe fruits are eaten.

Other Uses :
  1. Within its natural habitat it is a useful tree for covering dry hill sides.
  2. The bark comntains 13.4 per cent of tannins and 9.4 per cent of non-tannins and it yields dark reddish- brown leather of somewhat stiff and harsh texture
  3. A pelluloid gum, resembling Bassora gum, exudes from wounds on stems. It is considered efficacious in diarrhoea.

  4. The wood presents no difficulties in seasoning, but being prone to rapid fungal attack, it should be converted soon after felling. It is moderately strong in spite of its light weight, and fairly durable if kept dry and away from white ants. It is easy to saw and work to a moderate finish. It is suitable for match manufacture.
