Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
A genus of shrubs and trees cosmopolitan in the tropics. About fifteen species occur in India. A shrub or a tree c 6 m in height, frequently met with in peninsular India in the eastern and western Ghats up to 1,200 m, in the coastal plains, and in the hills of North-eastern India also in Malabar from Bombay to Coorg; common in Ceylon.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Bark pale yellow, rather smooth, 4 mm thick; leaves elliptic-lanceolate, coriaceous, glabrous and shining, entire or obscurely crenate; flowers light yellow in axillary clusters; capsules 1-2 cm long, aril bright red. The fruits are edible. The tender leaves and shoots are cooked and eaten in curry.
 

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers: bisexual, in axillary or subterrenial racemes, solitary or in small groups subtended by a bract; small, fragrance of jasmine, perianth segments overlapping, sepals 5, petals 5, narrow with frilled tips; stamens 5, ovary stalked with 3 stigmas, ovules many on 3 placentas. fruit: a capsule, splitting into 3-4 parts. seeds 1-6, aril and scarlet, fleshy.

Chemistry
The root contains leucopelargonidin, arabinose, dulcitol and a gutta-percha like substance (m p 59-60°). Presence of p-sitosterol and another sterol ( m p 120- 21°) and two resinous substances having hypoglycaemic activity is also reported. The bark contains tannin and a principle similar to cathartic acid (Basu & Choudhury.CM/r.Sci, 1960, 29, 136; Krishnan & Rangaswami, ibid, 1965, 34, 634; G<.rg & Verma, Indian J appi Chem, 1967, 30, 188; Desai et al, Indian JChem,1971,9,611).

Other Uses  

1.  The root and the rootbark (Trade - Saptrangi) are extensively used in the indigenous system of medicine as an antidiabetic.

2. In recent investigations, the crude aqueous extract of the roots has shown hypoglycaemic activity. The roots are especially efficacious in milder chronic diabetic cases; they are prescribed as a powder or decoction. 

3. They are also frequently used as an adulterant in the drug (Saptrangi) obtained from Salacia chinensis Linn. and S. macrosperma Wght     The wood is mostly used as a building material. It is suitable for making small turnery articles like toys and combs (Indian Woods, I, 46).
