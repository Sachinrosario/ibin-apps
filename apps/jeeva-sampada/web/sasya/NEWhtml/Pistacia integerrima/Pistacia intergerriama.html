HABIT & HABITAT
A moderate sized deciduous tree, found in the Himalayas from Indus to Kumaon, at altitudes of 350-2400 m. It has been observed that the gall formation is more prevalent in lower warmer areas and almost absent at the high altitude.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves are odd or even-pinnate 15-23 cm long and the leaflets are nearly opposite, lanceolate, shining, entire. Flowers are unisexual and dioecious. Drupe is dry and glabrous. The gall formed on the leafstalk by some insects has different shapes and sizes; sometimes twisted, hornlike or circular having purplish brown colour. When broken, the hollow of the hornlike gall is filled with white greyish green dust and often small insects.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Galls are formed on the leaves especially the petioles by the insects and are called as Kakarsingi. Galls are used for tanning and dyeing besides being used in asthma, phthisis and other diseases of the respiratory tract and in dysentery. The oil found in the galls, is used as a carminative. Leaves are used as fodder. The wood is used for construction and beautification purposes.

Chemistry:
The galls contain tannins, a resin and an essential oil. Two isomeric triterpenic acids, a triterpene alcohol and a waxy compound. Two crystalline compounds provisionally named as pistacin and pistacinin have been isolated from the drupes. The galls yield 1.3 percent of essential oil containing a-pinene, camphene dl-limonene, cineole, a-terpeneol, aromadendrone and gummastis, a resinous substance and two crystalline acids.

Dihydroquercetin, (-)eriodictyol and luteolin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).
Oil exhibited central nervous system depressant activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
a-Pinene (21.8), b-pinene (16.2), a-phellandrene (15.5), car-3-ene (11.1%), b- phellandrene, g-terpinene, limonene, a- and b-ocimenes, a- and b-terpineols determined in oil of galls of stem. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Three tetracyclic triterpenoids - pistacigerrimones A (fig. 1a), B (fig. 1b) and C (fig. 2) isolated from galls and characterised as 20(R),24(R)3-oxo-9b- lanosta-1 (2),7 ,24-trien-26-oic acid, 20(R),24(R)3-oxo-9b-lanosta-1(2),8,24-trien-26-oic acid and 20(R),24(R)3-oxo-tirucalla-1(2),24-dien-26-oic acid respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Three more tetracyclic triterpenes - pistacigerrimones D (fig. 3), E (fig. 4) and F (fig. 5) from galls and their characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.
Steam distilled oil from the galls and stem bark of Pistacia integerrima were analyzed by capillary GC and GC/MS. The gall volatiles contained 38 components of which 91% were found to be monoterpenes. The bark oil was found to contain 22 constituents of which 82.3% were monoterpenes. The major constituents of the gall oil were a-pinene, sabinene, b-pinene and limonene. The important components of the bark oil were terpinen-4-ol, a-terpineol and a-pinene. (Ansari et al, 1998).
