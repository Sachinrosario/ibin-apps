DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrubs, 2-3 m high. Leaves broadly ovate or elliptic, 0.5-4.0 x 0.5-2.5 cm, obtuse at apex, rounde or obtuse at base, coarsely dentate at margin, scabrid above, glabrous or sparly hairy beneath.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in axillary or leaf-opposed cymes; peduncles up to 1.5 cm long; pedicels up to 5 mm long. Sepals linear-oblong, 1.2-1.8 cm long, tomentose outside. Petals linear-oblong, c 6.5 cm long, obtuse, usually notched at apex, ciliate at base, white. Androgynophore c 0.5 mm long. Stamens many. Drupes 6-10 mm across, 2-parted, orange-yellow, glossy, glabrescent.
CIMAP:  Inflorescence: Umbellate cymes ;Flower: Purely white, buds oblong, tomentose, pedicels caduceus, bracts two, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Sepals linear-oblong, tomentose outside; Corolla: Petals linear-oblong, usually notched, about 2/3 the length of sepals, glandular; Androceium: Stamens indefinite, inserted on the turbinate often glandular torus, filaments free, anther cells 2, parallel, contiguous, dehiscing longitudinally; Gynoecium: Ovary 4-lobed, glabrous, style longer than the stamens, stigma 4-5 lobed; Fruit: Drupe smooth, orange-yellow, usually of two inseparable halves; Seed: horizontal, muriculate; Flowering & Fruiting time: August-October. 
Floral and seed biology:
It flowers between May- february.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by   wind, birds or animals.             .
 
Genetic variability:

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 The stem is used in cold and cough.


Chemistry:
Triacontan-1-ol, a-amyrin, b-amyrin, b-sitosterol, lupenone, erythrodiol, betulin and tetratriacont-21-ol-12-one isolated from stem bark and leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for cough (Stem, Shekhawat and Anand, 1984); Edible (Fruit, Shekhawat and Anand, 1984; Singh and Singh, 1981; Kanodia and Gupta, 1968).
