Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
A large genus of annual to perinial herbs originating from southwest Asia, poppy was domesticated in Europe in Neolithic times. It was cultivated by the Greeks and cultivation spread to India and China by the 18th century. Presently known to be cultivated for the opium in India, Iran, Turkey, Yugoslavia, Macedonia, Bulgaria, China, Manchuria, and Asia. P. somniferum has been extensively grown in India for its milky juice. During the middle of the eighteenth century Bihar was the chief province of India which produced the best quality and the greatest quantity of opium. At present, poppy cultivation is confined to the States of Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, the present acreage area being one tenth of what it used to be four decades ago. Opium poppy grows in varying climates but cannot endure extreme cold. Thrives in rich, well-manured soil, in hot to warm regions. Deep, warm, moderately moist, medium heavy soils meet the requirements for poppy growing. Soils with neutral or slightly alkaline pH are prefered. It is reported to tolerate annual precipitation of 3.1 to 17.3 dm, annual temperature of 5.6 to 23.5°C and does not do well in the humid tropics (4, 5, 6, 7, 8).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
The poppy is a herbaceous annual, which may grow to a height of 1.5 m. The leaves glabrous or glaucous. Some of which may grow quite large, are often elliptical, sometimes with a few spreading bristles, numerous, ovate to oblong, serrate to dentate-serrate, clasping at base and the lower ones pinnatifid, with wavy or toothed margins and a prominent midrib, the basal leaves form a rosette, while the upper ones clasp the stem. Buds are produced singly at the outer ends of the stems and branches, and remain pointing downwards until the day before they open. The main root is conical, with few laterals. The vegetative portions are light green, often greyish or with a silvery sheen, the stems being cylindrical, slightly ridged, and frequently branched. Fruit a capsule, ovoid to globose, glabrous, 4–6 cm long, 3.5–4 cm in diameter, with 8–12 rayed sessile stigmas; seeds oily, white, dark gray to black, or bluish (4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flower buds are produced singly and point downwards until the day before they open. The flowers are up to 18 cm in diameter with lilac petals which have a thumb print at the petal base which may be pink or blue. Flowers on long peduncles with nodding buds that expand into erect flowers, petals , white to purplish, in varieties also pink, violet, bluish, or red, 5–7 cm long, sepals glabrous, 1.5–2 cm long. Flowers are bisexual, regular, cup-shaped, four- to six-petaled flowers with one superior pistil (female structure) and numerous stamens surrounding the ovary. The two sepals drop off as the petals unfold (4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11).

Breeding System :

Flowers are bisexual favouring self-pollination, before the opening of flowers. Cross-pollination also occurs by insects.

Phenology :
The plants flowers 75-80 days after germination of seeds. They flower in April and May and the capsules are ripe in June to July.

Pollinators:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.

Back to Top

 

Seed Biology :
The ovary develops into a short, many-seeded capsule (800-2,000) that opens in dry weather, permitting the small seeds to escape when it is shaken by the wind. The seeds are small, about 1.25-1.5 mm in long, 0.8-1.1 mm broad, kidney-shaped and grayish blue to dark blue in colour.

Seedling Germination :
The papaver somniferum seeds will germinate in 10-21 days after sowing and do best when the soil temperature is coolish, 60-65 degrees F (or air temperature of 55 degrees F). They will not grow if it is too warm (12).

Cultivation:

Poppy is cultivated as a Rabi crop and often follows a crop of maize or other kharif crops. Land is prepared in September by repeated ploughing and harrowing and brought to a fine tilth. 25-37 cartloads of farmyard manure are added per hectare. Penning sheep and goats on the field is preferred. Application of phosphate and nitrogenous fertilizers has a beneficial effect on opium yield. During the initial period of development, poppy crop need heavy phosphate feeding and less nitrogen potash. But later during the growing period it needs a considerable amount of nitrogenous feed, which increases not only the opium yield but also morphine content. Before sowing, the field is laidout into beds for convenience of irrigation weeding. Seeds mixed with earth or ashes are sown broadcast in October-November at the rate of 3.5 kg/ha. Frequent light irrigation is necessary until the seedlings are fairly established. When seedlings are 5-7.5 cm. high, they are thinned 20-25 cm. apart and weeded.

The cultivated varieties of P. somniferum commonly grown in Uttar Pradesh are:

Teyleah variety or Telia, Haraina, Hariala, or Herera, Sufaid-danthi or Katha Bhabutia, Kutila, Katila or Kotila or Chansura, Ghanghabaha, Chirrah or Bhagbhora, Choura Kutila, Kaladanthi, Karria, Damia, Kalidanthi, or Kalidandi, Subza Kaladanthi or Haraina Kalidanthi, Kalidanthi Baunia, Monoria, Dheri-Danthi, Variegated poppy, Sufaid-danthi Monoria, Monaria Teyleash, Sandpha, or Dhadhua or Bhabhua

Sahbania .

The Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan poppy differs considerably from the Uttar Pradesh poppy. It has a straighter stalk, simpler stem, sharply toothed leaves, of much thinner texture and the usually red or purplish coloured flowers, with fringed petals and rather large sized oblong or ovate-oblong capsules, crowned with broad stigmatic rays. The main varieties grown in that region are:

Bhatphoria or Dhaturia, Galania, Ramzatak, Telia, Ghotia, Chaglia, Kasturi or Tejani (4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 13, 14).

Pests:

Control measures

Cut worm (Agrostis ipsilon, A. spinifera, A. flammatra, A. segetus )

Cutworms feed on the tender leaves of young plants and cut the older ones at the ground level.

Rake 5% Aldrin dust into the soil, dust 4% Carbaryl or 5-10% BHC.

Grey weevils (Myllocerus spp.)

The adults feed on the leaves, first cutting the margins.

Rake 5% Aldrin dust into the soil, dust 4% Carbaryl or 5-10% BHC.

Gujhia weevil (Tanymecus indicus)

Adults are earthern grey weevils, grubs feed on roots, whereas the adults cut growing points or nibble at margins of leaves, severe at seedling stage.

Plough the fields in summer to expose and kill the pupae, mix thoroughly 5% Aldrin or Heptachlor with the 12-25 cm deep layer of soil, for adults dust 5% BHC

Powdery mildew (Erysiphe polygoni)

Slightly discoloured specks from which white powdery spots spread and overrun the leaf, stem and pods.

Economic Potential and Uses

Poppy seed oil is a non-yellowing drying oil forming a hard and lustrous film. The oil is rendered colour less by exposure to the sun. It dries much slower and more uniformly than linseed oil. Poppy seed oil is used in the production of artists' paints. The oil is useful in the preparation of linoleic acid, soft soaps, ointments and emulsions, and compositions for skin care. The oil finds use also as an illuminant. It is used against diarrhoea, dysentery and scalds. Lower grades are used for lubrication. Poppy seed oil is used in Europe as a substitute and adulterant of olive oil. Poppy seed oil is widely used for culinary purposes. It is free from narcotic properties and is used mixed with olive oil, or as a salad oil. It has a high digestibility co-efficient of 96% at a daily intake of 50 g. The hydrogenated oil may also be useful for industrial purposes. The cake or the meal left after extraction of the oil from the seeds is sweet and nutritious and is eaten by poor people. It is readily consumed by cattle and sheep, and may be fed alone or preferably mixed with other feeds (4, 6, 16, 17, 18).

Uses

Opium is the air-dried milky exudation obtained from excised unripe fruits. It is extensively smoked as an intoxicant. Opium is largely used for manufacture of morphine, codeine, narcotine, laudenine, papaverine, and many other alkaloids. It is also the source of the toxic and extremely habitforming narcotic heroin or dimorphine, prohibited in some countries. Seeds contain no opium and are used extensively in baking and sprinkling on rolls and bread. Seeds are a good source of energy. Poppy seeds are more common in India, where they are ground and used as a thickening agent in curries and sauces. They are also sprinkled over cooked noodles, or sweetened with honey and made into a dessert dip or sauce. Dry-fried seeds are added to salads and salad dressings. The crushed and sweetened seeds are used as a fillling in crepes, strudels, pastries etc. Young leaves are edible and eaten raw or cooked. They must be used before the flower buds have formed. Stems used for straw. Lecithin has been extracted from poppy seed meal. Opium poppy is widely grown as an ornamental plant (4, 6, 11, 16, 17).

Harvesting Practices
The poppy crop is usually reaped by hand when the capsules turn yellowish green and the seeds rattle on shaking. Specially adapted grain combines are used on larger farms. If cut by hand before the capsules are fully dry, the crop is stooked in the field. Threshing is done in dry weather. Seed and straw can be seperated by grain husking machines. Seed yields are usually 1.2-1.8 tonn per ha (16).

Produce and Products

Opium drug is obtained from the plant for the pharmaceutical purposes. Leaves are eaten raw or cooked. Seeds are used in the confectionery. Oil is extracted from the seeds which is used for edible purposes and has medicinal and industrial importance. Seed cake is used as animal feed.

Processing Techniques

  • The oil is extracted by pressure or solvent extraction after a preliminary crushing.

  • Cold pressing produces 30-35% of a light yellow oil, which is much appreciated for edible purposes.

  • Hot pressing at a temperature of 1150C produces a dark yellow to reddish oil, which is not edible unless refined (16).

Other Uses
Cosideried as as analgesic, anodyne, antitussive, aphrodisiac, astringent, bactericidal, calmative, carminative, demulcent, emollient, expectorant, hemostat, hypotensive, hypnotic, narcotic, nervine, sedative, sudorific, tonic, poppy has been used in folk remedies for asthma, bladder, bruises, cancer, catarrh, cold, colic, conjunctivitis, cough, diarrhea, dysentery, dysmenorrhea, enteritis, enterorrhagia, fever, flux, headache, hemicrania, hypertension, hypochondria, hysteria, inflammation, insomnia, leucorrhea, malaria, mania, melancholy, nausea, neuralgia, otitis, pertussis, prolapse, rectitis, rheumatism, snakebite, spasm, spermatorrhea, sprain, stomachache, swelling, toothache, tumor, ulcers, and warts. Opium is used as a remedy for cancerous conditions such as cancer of the skin, stomach, tongue, uterus, carcinoma of the breast, polyps of the ear, nose, and vagina; scleroses of the liver, spleen, and uterus; and tumors of the abdomen, bladder, eyes, fauces, liver, spleen, and uvula. The plant, boiled in oil, is said to aid indurations and tumors of the liver. The tincture of the plant is said to help cancerous ulcers. In Ayurvedic medicine, the seeds are considered aphrodisiac, constipating, and tonic; the fruit antitussive, binding, cooling, deliriant, excitant, and intoxicant, the plant is considered aphrodisiac, astringent, fattening, stimulant, tonic, and good for the complexion; in Unani medicine, the fruit is suggested as well for anemia, chest pains, dysentery, fever, but is correctly deemed hypnotic, narcotic, and perhaps harmful to the brain.

The dried latex rolled into pills in combination with other substances has long been valued as a highly effective painkiller. As a sedative it is used to bring sleep to agitated children and suffering patients. It has also been employed to calm hysterics and otherwise mentally or emotionally disturbed patients. Its antidiarrheal properties still make it one of the most powerful agents for the treatment of colic and dysentery, whilst its antitussive action still makes it (Codeine- alkaloid of Opium) one of the most widely used ingredients in all kinds of cough remedies and is invaluable in treating persistent spasmodic coughs. In the past it was much used to treat tuberculosis. As an aphrodisiac it plays an important role in treating sexual problems such as impotency and premature ejaculation (4, 6, 14, 20, 21).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

India produces about half the opium utilized by the world's pharmaceutical industries to produce codeine, morphine, narcotine, thebaine, papaverine, and other medical products. In India, cultivation of poppy for opium was earning considerable amount of revenue to Government during early 90's. Opium was freely sold as an intoxicant within the country and exported for the same purpose to Eastern countries, particularly China. China was a big market for Indian opium, and this resulted in the high acreage under opium poppy in the early part of the present century. The flagrant misuse of opium and its deleterious effects physically, mentally and morally became so widespread, that it became a serious social problem in many countries. As a result of this cultivation of opium poppy in India is controlled by the Government and is now confined to Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan. Negligible quantities are also grown in Jammu &, Kashmir. Cultivation in Himachal Pradesh has been completely banned since 1954-55, as it is difficult control in hilly regions.


Two government factories process the opium. The Ghazipur (UP) factory is about 150 years old, Neemuch (MP) was set up in the 1930's. The headquarters of the Industry is in Gwalior, which was a Native State known for its poppy production (4, 22).
