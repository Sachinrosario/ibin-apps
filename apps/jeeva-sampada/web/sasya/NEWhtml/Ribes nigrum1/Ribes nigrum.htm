HABIT & HABITAT
An erect unarmed shrub up 1.0 -2.0 m. high. found wild from Kunawar to Kashmir at altitudes of 2,100-3,600 m. A large genus of shrubs widely distributed in the cool and temperate regions of Asia, Europe, North and South America. 
BioticZone: Dry inner valleys of kashmir and Temperate Western Himalaya.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Stems rather stout, pubescent when young. with a strong characteristic odour; leaves 3-5 lobed. serrate, 4-8 cm. long; flowers greenish purple in pendulous racemes; berries globose, c. 8 mm. in diam., black, with a strong aromatic smell and taste. The currants and gooseberries are sometimes classified under two different genera or sub-genera Ribes and Grossularia, the former usually unarmed and with flowers and fruits in racemes and the latter often armed with spines or prickles and with flowers and fruits solitary. The fruit is quite large and palatable.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Fruits, Leaves, Seeds

Uses

  • The fruits are strongly aromatic and too acid to be eaten raw. They are frequently made into jelly or jam and are also used to make sauces, pies and wine as well as juice and syrup products. The fruits are also preserved for use by drying.

Chemistry
Black currants are very rich in vitamin C (90-360 mg.; av., 150 mg./l00 g.), and contain good quantities of pectin (0-9-1-7%, as calcium pectate), and also minerals, especially potassium (372 mg./l00 g.), magnesium (17 mg./l00 g.), and calcium (60 mg./l00 g.). The acidity of the fruit is due mainly to citric acid, while malic acid is present in small amounts. Glucose and fructose are the principal sugars, with sucrose as a minor component. The flavonoids identified in the fruits include kaemp-ferol, quercctin, and myricetin. Fruits also contain cyanidin.

Other Uses
  1. Seeds yield 25 per cent of a brownish yellow drying oil (iod. val., 178) with an unpleasant flavour. The buds are used as a flavouring agent. They contain a pale greenish essential oil (0-75%) composed mostly of terpenes. 
  2. Leaves also contain minute quantities of an essential oil to which are attributed their diuretic properties.  Leaves are considered diuretic and are said to be used in place of tea.
  3. A decoction of dried leaves and twigs is said to be a home remedy for coughs.
