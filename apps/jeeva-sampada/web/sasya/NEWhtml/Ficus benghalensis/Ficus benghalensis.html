REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Flower: Minute on the inner walls of the fleshy receptacles which is sessile or  in pair, axillary, globose, or pendulous, with broad basal bracts 3, coriaceous ; Male flower: Rather numerous near the mouth of receptacles, sepals 4, lanceolate, stamens 1 ; Gall flower: Rather numerous near the mouth of receptacles, sepals 4, lanceolate,  style short; Fertile flower : Perianth shorter than the male Fruit: An enlarged hollow cup-shaped receptacles, the inner wall studded with achenes ; Seed: Pendulous .  
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : April-June . 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome numbers -26, 28.

Chemistry:

Isolation of friedelin and b-sitoserol from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
Taraxasterol tiglate from heartwood; quercetin-3-galactoside and rutin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Three new methyl ethers of leucoanthocyanins - delphinidin-3-O-a-L-rhamnoside (fig. 1a), pelargonidin-3-O-a-L-rhamnoside (fig. 1b) and leucocyanidin-3-O-b-D-galactosylcellobioside (fig. 1c) - along with a methyl ether of leucoanthocyanidin isolated from stem bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
20-tetratriaconten-2-one, pentatriacontan-5-one and 6-heptatriaconten-10-one isolated from stem bark and characterized; b-sitosterol-a-D-glucoside and meso-inositol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
GLC of surface hydrocarbons from fresh leaves showed predominant occurrence of lower even-numbered alkanes [C14 (12.9), C16 (16.5), C18 (11.3) and C20 (2.5%)], which was an unusual alkane distribution pattern for a dicotyledonous plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).
A polysaccharide which on hydrolysis afforded xylose, arabinose and smaller amounts of glucose and galactose, isolated from fruits; fractionation of polysaccharide yielded xylan. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Pharmacology: 

Bark showed hypoglycaemic activity in both normal and alloxan diabetic rats; activity was more marked after adequate period of fasting. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
LD50 of plant extract in mice was 9.47 g/kg by i.p. route. In chronic toxicity test, extract produced hepatic damage initially but changes were reversible even on a continued administration. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
A glucoside isolated from bark was ineffective in diabetic animals, but lowered fasting blood sugar of normal animals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.
Oral administration of dimethyl ether of leucocyanidin-3-O-b-D-galactosylcellobioside (0.1 g/kg) for one month to normal and moderately diabetic rats decreased blood and urine sugar, certain lipid components in serum and tissues and glucose-6-phophatase activity in liver; hypoglycaemic response was enhanced when it was given along with low dose of insulin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
A single dose (0.25 g/kg) of dimethyl ether of pelargonidin-3-O-a-L-rhamnoside given to moderately diabetic rats decreased fasting blood glucose by 19.0% and improved glucose tolerance by 29.0%, while administration of 0.1 g/kg/day for one month caused decrease in fasting blood glucose level to almost half that of pretreatment level, and urine sugar level decreased to traces in both groups. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Two flavanoid compounds, viz. 5,7-di-Meether of leucopelargonidin 3-O-a-D galactosylcellobioside and 5,3'-dimethyl ether of leucocyanidin 3-O-a-D galactosylcellobioside obtained from the bark of Ficus bengalensis were evaluated for their antioxidant action in hyperlipidemic rats. The results were compared with the activity of a structurally similar flavanoid, quercetin, a known antioxidant. The Ficus compounds showed significant antioxidant effects which may be attributed to their polyphenolic nature. The methylation of two hyrdroxyl groups in the Ficus flavanoids might have slowed their antioxidant action as compared to quercetin. (Daniel et al., 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 60894g.
Di-Me ether of leucopelargonidin 3-0-a-L- rhamnoside isolated from the bark of Ficus bengalensis (Banyan tree) showed its median ED (ED50) as 100 mg/kg, in demonstrating about 12% hypoglycemic action in normal rats. Compared to a double dose of insulin, a low dose of insulin in combination with ED50 dose of the compound for 30 days equated ill the maintenance of body wt and control of urine and blood sugar and excelled in amelioration of serum cholesterol and triglycerides in alloxan-induced diabetic rats. The results indicate an insulin sparing action of the leucopelargonin, if used in combination with insulin. (Cherian and Augusti, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 135333k.
The extracts of Ficus bengalensis (hanging roots) showed significant inhibitory activity against castor oil induced diarrhoea and PGE2 induced enteropooling in rats. These extracts also showed a significant reduction in gastrointestinal motility in charcoal meal tests in rats. The results obtained establish the efficacy of all these plant materials as anti-diarrhoeal agents. (Mukherjee et al, 1998).
