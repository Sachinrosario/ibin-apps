DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Glabrous or puberulous herbs. Leaves opposite, ovate or lanceolate, 5.0-7.5 x 2.5-3.8 cm, base acute, rounded or cordate, nerves 4-5 pairs, thin, long petioled; petiole 1.2-3.9 cm long. Stipules ovate-lanceolate, bifid.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers in 10-15 cm long pubescent panicles, violet, sessile or shortly pedicelled. Calyx campanulate; teeth short, triangular, acute. Corolla funnel shaped, 1.3-3.0 cm long, usually pubescent; lobes short. Fruits orbicular, 0.6-1.1 cm across, polished, crowned by the conical disk and minute calyx teeth.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: The plant has, diuretic, emmenagogue, aphrodisiac properties and is used for the treatment of liver and stomach troubles, lumbago. The juice of the root is useful in piles, pain in the chest and the liver, inflammation of the spleen. The leaves are tonic, styptic, vulnenery and also used in earache. Leaf juice is considered astringent and given to children suffering from diarrhoea.


Chemistry:
Asperulosiode and four new iridoid glucosides paederoside (fig. 1b), mp. 122°C, paederosidic acid (fig. 2a), scandoside (fig. 2b), mp. 139°C and deacetylasperuloside (fig. 1a) isolated and characteised. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Hentriacontane, hentriacontanol, methyl mercaptan, ceryl alcohol, palmitic acid, sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol, ursolic acid and iridoid glycosides asperuloside, paedweroside and scandoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979) a.

Isolation of epifriedelinol, embelin and b-sitosterol from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994) a.

Leaves contained nonanoic acid, capric, lauric, myristic, arachidic and palmitic aicds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994) b.

Linalool identified as major component in oil from stems, leaves and flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994) c.

NBPGR: Plants –antirheumatic , root and leaf- tonic root- in piles, inflammations of the spleen , pain in chest and liver; root and bark – emetic; leaf-astringent carminative, antidiarrhoeal, antilinthic, diuretic, in herpes; fruit- antiodontalgic.


Pharmacology:
Plant extract showed anti-inflammatory activity stronger than that of acetylsalicyclic aid and weaker than that of hydrocortisone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Butanolic fraction of methanol extract of the defatted leaves of Paederia foetida produced a significant inhibition of granulation tissue formation in cotton-pellet implanted rats. It decreased liver aspartate transaminase activity without affecting serum aspartate transaminase activity. It did not, however, affect adrenal weight and ascorbic acid content significantly, thus ruling out a stimulation of the adrenal-pituitary axis. BMEL antagonised hyposaline-induced haemolysis of human red blood cells and an elevation of rat serum acid phosphatase activity, indicating the presence of a membrane stabilising activity. It also inhibited the elevation of serum orosomucoid levels in rats, suggesting the possibility of the presence of disease-modifying antirheumatic activity. (De et al, 1994)


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for Diarrhoea, dysentery (Leaf, Chaudhuri et al, 1985; Mukherjee et al, 1986; 236); Dyspepsia (Leaf, Mukherjee et al, 1986); Emetic (Root, Mukherjee et al, 1986); Gastric complaints (Seed, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Indigestion (Part not mentioned, Bhattacharjee et al, 1980); Intestinal parasites (Root, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Rheumatism (Part not mentioned, Rao et al, 1981; Mukherjee et al, 1986); Tonic in convulsion (Root, Gupta, 1981); Typhoid (Root, Kumar and Rao, 1987); Edible (Leaf, Arora, 1981; Chaudhuri et al, 1985) .
