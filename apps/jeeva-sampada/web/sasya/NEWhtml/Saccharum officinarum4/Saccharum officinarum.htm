DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tall perennial grass.
Culms of varying thickness, and colour ranging from pale or dark green to dark yellow, red, violet and often striped.
Canes with relatively low fiber and high sucrose content.
Leaves long and narrow or relatively broad, erect or drooping, varying in colour from light to dark green, sometimes with a purplish large.
Inflorescence large and plumose, with main axis glabrous without long hair.
Glumes generally 3, rarely .
Lodicules not ciliate.
Sessile spikelet of the pair blooms first.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Breeding system :the breeding of sugarcane differs from that of many other crop plants in that the parents involved are clones maintained vegetatively and usually characterized by more or less extreme degree of heterozygocity. In countries like Australia and West Indies pure line breeding is emphasized. In contrast to this genetic diversity at the expense of precision and some sacrifice of information is emphasized in Hawaii.

Phenology: The varieties of S. officinarum are almost the sole source of genes control in the elaboration and storage of sucrose in the cane. Inferior or wild canes are repeatedly backcrossed with noble cane to improve their trait is called Nobilization.


Pollinators: Sugarcane is propagated vegetatively by planting sections of immature cane known variously as seed, seed piece or seed sett. Position of can stalks left underground after harvest can be allowed to grow as a satoon crop.

Floral and seed biology:
Seedling germination :

The true seeds of sugarcane are extremely small in size and often poorly developed and inviable. The seed is collected form the arrows of he female parent some three weeks after pollination and germinated in flats or beds soon after. The seeds retain their viability for a short period only but this can be extended to several years by storage at temperatures of 0o-5oC. When the seedlings are 6-12 weeks old, they are transplanted to ground or field nurseries individually or in groups or bunches of 3-15 seedlings. They are again transplanted to the second ground nursery as single plants. At the end of one year selection is made.



MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:

Insect spp. which cause damage to sugar cane are diverse and numerous and their distribution is characteristically limited to definite geographical areas. These include moth borers, termites, white grubs, frog hoppers, scale insects, mealy bugs, Fulgoroidea spp. and related insect spp., locusts and grasshoppers and nematodes.
Rodents also are serious pests of sugarcane.


Diseases:

Sugarcane offers from a large no. of fungus, virus and bacterial diseases in addition to nutritional deficiency diseases.

Fungus diseases -
Ret rot - Collelotricum falcatum Wet.
Use of cane varieties resistant to the disease and use of fungicidal sprays as control measures.
Smut - caused by Ustilago scitaminea Syd.
Wilt - caused by Cephalosporium sacchari Butl.
Yellow spot - Cercospora hoepkii Kriiger.
Brown strip - Helminthosporium stenospilum Drechs.
Downy mildew - Solerosporasacchari Miy.
Eyespot - Helminthosporium sacchari (de Haan) Butl.
Pineapple disease - Ceratocystis paradoxa (de Seynes) Moreau
Rust - Puccinia kuechnii (Kriig) Butl. & P. erianthi Padwet Khan
Root rot - Pythium arrhenomanes Drechsl.
Sclerophthora disease - Sclerophthora macrospora Thir. et al.
Red rot of leaf sheath - Pellicularia rolfsii (Sacc) E. West
Ring spot - Leptosphaeria sacchari (B. de Haan) Butler
Brown spot - Cercospora longipes Butler.
Sooty mold - Capnaduim sp. & Fumago sp.
Banded sclerotial disease - Pellicularia saskii Shirai) Ito.

Virus disease :

Virus disease of sugarcane are chlorotic streak, fiji disease, mosaic, ratoon stunting, streaks, dwarf, grassy shoot, R.13 diseases, ring mosaic, sembur, serch, spike and striate mosaic.

Bacteiral diease :

Bacterial diseases of sugar cane are - Gumming disease, leaf scald, red stripe, bacterial mottle and mottled stripe. Of these only gummosis and red stripe have been reported in India.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Sugarcane juice - gur - it is obtained on concentrating sugarcane juice without prior purification into a solid or semisolid sate. Gur is used for a variety of purposes such as direct consumption by human being, for stock feeding, refining, sweetening milk, tea or coffee and for making sweets.

Khandasari Sugar - Vacuum pan sugar, icing sugar, cube sugar, liquid sugar.

Bagasse - it is the fibrous residue of the cane stalks left after crushing and extraction of juice. Used as a fuel for sugar factory boilers, production of biogass or sludge, used as fuel in producer gas plants, used for making different varieties of pulps suitable for making writing, printing, wrapping, book and bag papers, newsprint, bleached tissue, toweling and glass in and grease proof papers. For manufacture of paper, corrugating, and box boards, particle boards, fiber boards, bagasse can be used in the manufacture of plastics. Among the products made are valie, nonvolate. It may also be used as a cheap and light filler in building materials.
