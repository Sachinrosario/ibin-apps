Taxonomic Details

Name : Tamarindus indica L., Sp. Pl. 34. 1753. 
Synonyms: Tamarindus officinalis Hk.
Family: Caesalpiniaceae
Local Name: Amli, Chinch, Hitamg, Imli, Katara, Tetul
Hindi : Imli
English : Tamarind

HABIT & HABITAT

Large spreading trees; mostly planted as avenue trees and in villages.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Evergreen trees with dense crown; leaves pinnate, leaflets 10-12 pairs, oblong-lanceolate, obtuse, entire, glabrous, flowers in terminal racemes, yellowish-orange, calyx long, lobes 4 ,obliquely oblong, petals long, obvoate oblong pale yellow with red streaks, lower reduced to scales; fruits pods, oblong, slightly curved; seeds 3-8, obovate-oblong, chocolate brown, polished, truncate at both ends.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Seeds are used in preparation of soft drinks. Jain 1991)
Pulp of fruits is used in making drinks. (Das, 1997)
Infusion of dried fruits is employed as a drink in febrile diseases. (Mandal & Basu 1996)

Beverage: formulae for preparing spiced sauces and beverages from the pulp have been reported by Girdhari Lal et al (1958). Tamarind extract as a replacement for phosphoric acid. Citric acid and other acids added to soft drinks is described by Zablocki and Pecore (1995). Beverages containing the extract with a lower pH have improved shelf-life. Tamarind fruit can be used as a raw material for the preparation of wine-like beverages (Benk 1987; Latino and Vega 1986; Sanchez 1985).

The extraction and processing techniques for the preparation of canned tamarind pulp and the manufacture of tamarind (pulp) soft drinks have ben reported by Bueso (1978). Process for making drinks, syrup, juice, liquor and solid extracts based on tamarind were developed by Meillon (1974). Wine and vinegar production from tamarind fruit is reported by Maldonado et al (1975). Studies were conducted on the preparation and shelf life of tamarind and soursop-tamarind soft drinks by Benero et al (1974). Tamarind drinks with 9-12% pulp concentration and 21.50 Brix and soursop-tamarind blended drinks at 10-14% and 150 Brix and 170 Brix were proposed. The drinks were pasteurized at 185-1900F, canned and stored at 850F. Canned tamarind drinks kept well for 1 year and blended drinks for about 10 months, blended drinks of 170 Brix, being preferred in terms of acceptability. Alian et al (1983) have studied bacteriostatic effect of tamarind extract. Extract from tamarind was the most inhibitor against all tested organisms in soft drinks.
