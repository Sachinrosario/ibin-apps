HABIT & HABITAT
 Herbaceous aromatic plant grows upto 2m high. It is found in Western Ghats, widely distributed in  most parts of the Deccan Peninsula, ascending to an altitude of c 1800 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Aromatic, much branched herbs up to 2 m high. Stem prominently quadrangular, glabrous, tender parts appressedly pubescent; branches horizontal, swollen above the nodes. Leaves ovate-lanceolate or ovate-elliptic, 6.5-11.5 x 2-4 cm, acute at apex, narrowed at base, crenate-serrate along margin, only sparingly puberulous, membranous; petioles slender, c 3 cm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Flowers pale-white, arranged in  globose clusters of racemes; bracts ovate-lanceolate; bracteoles linear-lanceolate. Calyx lobes 5, subequal, sessile; lobes narrowly triangular, acute, connivent, glandular pubescent. Corolla white. Bilipped (3+1), glabrous within. Stamens 4, didynamous with copious long hairs. Ovary 0.4 mm long; style 6 mm long, gynobasic. Nutlets small, ellipsoid, brownish, smooth, shining.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The herb is considered to be diuretic and carminative. Decoction of the leaves is taken for cough and asthma and that of the roots for dropsy. The powder of the leaves are used as a sternutatory. A lotion prepared from the roots is used in rheumatism.


Chemistry:
Isolation and chracterisation of two alkaloids, Patchoulipyridine and epiguaipyridine from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
solation and synthesis of dhelwangin from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Tricyclic sesquiterpene, seychellene from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
Total synthesis of seychellene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
Stereoselective total synthesis of seychellene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).
