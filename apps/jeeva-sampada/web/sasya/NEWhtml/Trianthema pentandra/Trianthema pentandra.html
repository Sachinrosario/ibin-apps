DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Diffuse, much-branched, 7-45 cm high. Stem and branches more or less papillose, angled and grooved. Leaves elliptic-oblong, sometimes slightly obovate, 1.2-3.0 x 0.5-1.3 cm, rounded at apex, narrowed at base, glabrous or more or less papillose; petiole distinct, 0.5-1.2 cm long, dilated at base, slightly sheathing. Flowers sessile  to subsessile, few or many-flowered axillary fascicles; bracteoles thinly membranaceous. Calyx deeply 5-lobed; lobes ovate-oblong, 2-3 mm long with a short apiculation at the back below the apex. Stamens 5. Capsules 4 mm long, exerted, the cap with broad deflexed horns, dividing into two 1-seeded portions, the lower half of the capsule containing 2 seeds. Seeds orbicular-reniform, 1-2 mm in diam., compressed, rugulose, dull black.

Uses:
 The plant is used as an astringent in abdominal diseases and is also stated to produces abortion.
Chemistry:

Pharmacology:

·        The methanolic extracts of Trianthema pentandra (whole plant) showed a highly significant decrease in the ulcer index in aspirin-induced gastric ulcers in rats. (Akhtar and Ahmad, 1995).

·        The co-occurrence of lethality to brine shrimp larvae and selective antimicrobial activity in the extract of Trianthema pentandra L. (Adoum et al., 1997)

 

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for pain in bladder (Part not mentioned, Sen et al, 1985); Rheumatoid (Root, Singh and Pandey, 1980)
