Chemistry:

·        Presence of flavonoids-quercetin and avicularin-in Egyptian plant by PC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Isolation of an arabinan, composed of galacturonic acid,+++++++ galactose, arabinose with traces of xylose and rhamnose, from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Leucocyanidin, luteic and ellagic acids and a new glycoside-amritoside, mp. 2480-from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Quercetin-3-a-arabinopyranoside from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Methyl arjunolate isolated from anthocyanidins; five flavonoids-quercetin, myricetin-3-glucoside, 3-glucosylglucoside and 3-glucosylglucosylglucoside of kaempferol and sinesin an uncommon pigment shown to be dihydrokaempferol-7-glucoside-identified in flowers and leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        A saponin containing oleanolic acid isolated from fruit. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Gibberellins A1, A3, A4, A5, A6 and A7 isolated from immature seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Leaf essential oil (0.5%) contained myrcene (0.99), dl-limonene (8.01), carophyllene (7.7), caryophyllene oxide (37.5), eugenol (13.0) and 1, 8-cineole (2.7%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        a-pinene, b-pinene, limonene, menthol, a-terpenyl acetate and isopropyl alcohol isolated by GLC from volatile oil of leaves; a mixture of glycosides isolated which on acidic hydrolysis yielded maslinic acid, 2a-hydroxyursolic acid and b-sitosterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Leaf essential oil of Philippinese plant contained a-pinene (24.0), limonene (35.0), longicyclene (7.0), caryophyllene (12.0), b-bisabolene (18.0) and caryophyllene oxide (4.0%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Detection of b-pinene (<0.5), limonene (0.5), b-copaene (1.0), b-caryophyllene (95.0), farnesene (<0.5), a-and b-humulene (0.5), b-bisabolene (1.0), a-and b-selinene (1.0), cadinene (<0.5) and curcumene (<0.5%) by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Chemical investigation of hemicelluloses of seeds, containing galactose (59.6), arabinose (35.9) and a uronic acid (45.0%), revealed presence of chain residues of (1®4)-linked galactose, (1®5)-linked arabinose and terminal arabinose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Synthesis of b-bisabolene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Eight ellagitannins-pedunculagin, casuarinin, stachyurin, tellimagrandin I, strictinin, casuariin, casuarictin and 2, 3-hexahydroxydiphenoyl glucose-isolated; isostrictinin also isolated and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        b-sitosterol, quercetin, leucocyanidin, gallic acid and new tannin-2, 3, 4-trigalloyl-6-(m-trigalloyl) glucose (I)-isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Oral administration of leaf extract inhibited hyperglycaemia in alloxan-induced diabetic rats. It also inhibited adrenaline-induced lipolysis in fat cells from rat epididymal adipose tissue. The activity may be due to the presence of isostrictinin, strictinin and pendunculagin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Isolation of new ellagitannin-guavins B- and its structure determination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Three new tannins-guavins A, C and D-isolated from leaves and their structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Ellagic acid, guaijaverin, quercetin and oleanolic acid isolated from fresh flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Flavonol fraction inhibited peristalsis of guinea ileum in vitro. Spasmolytic activity of leaf is mainly due to presence of quercetin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Gibberellins A1 and A5 synthesised. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        a-pinene (37.8) and 1, 8-cineole (18.9%) determined in leaf essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Lauric and myristic acids as major constituents (34.0%), b-caryophyllene and oxygen-containing sesquiterpenes (25.0%) as well as caryophylladienols identified in essential oil of fruit of Nigerian plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Capric, lauric, myristic, palmitic, stearic and oleic acids, glucose, fructose, sucrose, arabinose, galactose, myo-inositol and an acidic polysaccharide composed of rhamnose, arabinose, galactose and glucuronic acid isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Six new complex tannins-guajavins A and B, psidinins A, B and C and psiguavin-isolated from bark and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        (+) gallocatechin identified in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Isolation of guajavarin, isoquercetin, hyperin, quercitrin and quercetin-3-O-gentiobioside from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Volatile constituents obtained from four cultivars of Psidium guajava fruits by simultaneous steam distillation-solvent extraction, and analyzed by GC/MS. More than 40 compounds were identified in each cultivar, 17 of which have been identified for the first time as volatile constituents of guava. Each cultivar has a typical composition, characterized by a specific ratio between the main components and classes of substances. The major volatiles were  either b-caryophyllene, limonene, 3-phenylpropyl acetate, (Z)-3-hexenyl acetate or (E)-cinnamyl acetate. (Pino et. al., 1999).

·        Sixteen carotenoids were isolated from the flesh of Brazilian red Psidium guajava. Many functions are attributed to carotenoids. (Mercadante et. al., 1999).

·        Psidium guajava was found to be rich in Vitamin-A and a number of carotenoids namely lycopene, a-carotene, gamma-carotene, zeinoxanthium, 5-6, 5', 6'-diepoxy-b-carotene and 5-8 epoxy 3, 3', 4'-trihydroxy b-carotene. Lycopene is a principal pigment in this plant. (Sharma et. al., 1999).

·        The essential oil of the leaves of Psidium guajava L. was analysed by GLC, GC/MS and IR. The oil showed the presence of sesquiterpene hydrocarbons as the major make up of the oil; caryophyllene, aromadendrene, a- and b  -selinene and b-bisabolene being the most prominent. Among the oxygenated derivatives; caryophyllene epoxide, caryophylladienol and sesquiterpene alcohols of the viridiflorol and ledol types were identified. The microbiological screening of the essential oil, on the other hand, exhibited marked activity against Sarcina lutea, Mycobacterium phlei, Bacillus subtilis, Proteus vulgaris, Proteus mirabilis and Proteus morganii. (Karawya et al., 1999)

·        Volatile compounds from guava (Psidium guajava L.) were extracted using dichloromethane. Among these volatile compounds, methyl 2-methyl-thiazolidine-4   -(R)-carboxylate (cis and trans) and the ethyl 2-methyl-thiazolidine-4-(R)-carboxylate (cis and trans) could be identified for the first time in fruits.  The chemical synthesis of the reference compounds and other analogues allowed the further comparison with the guava extracted thiazolidines. The absolute configuration of the cis/trans isomers was determined by nuclear magnetic resonance (1H-NMR) and by nuclear overhauser enhancement (2D NMR NOESY). (Fernandez et al., 2001)

·        Four antibacterial compounds were isolated from leaves of guava   (Psidium guajava L.), and the structures of these compounds were established on the basis of chemical and spectroscopic evidence. Two new flavonoid glycosides, morin-3-O-a-L-lyxopyranoside and morin-3-O-a-L-arabopyranoside, and two known flavonoids, guaijavarin and quercetin, were identified. The minimum inhibition concentration of morin-3-O-a-L-lyxopyranoside and morin-3-O-a-L-arabopyranoside was 200 /ml for each against Salmonella enteritidis, and 250 mg/ml and 300 mg/ml against Bacillus cereus, respectively. (Arima  and Danno, 2002)

·        Two new triterpenoids, guajavolide (2a,3b,6b,23-tetrahydroxyurs-12  -en-28,20b -olide and guavenoic acid (2a,3b,6b,23   -tetrahydroxyurs-12,20(30)-dien-28-oic acid, long with one known triterpene  oleanolic acid  were isolated from the fresh leaves of Psidium guajava.  Their structure elucidation and stereochemistry were determined by spectroscopic experiments, including 2D-NMR techniques. (Begum et al., 2002)


Pharmacology:

·        (+)Gallocatechin exhibited antimutagenic activity against UV-induced mutation in Escherichia coli. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Psidium guajava was screened for in vitro antimicrobial activity and was found to be active. (Rabe and Van Staden, 1997).

·        Oil of Psidium guajava leaves obtained by steam distillation was given orally to study its effects on the exudative and proliferative phases of the inflammatory reaction, using the technique of carrageenan-induced paw edema and cotton pellet granuloma in male albino rats. The antiinflammatory activity was compared with ketorolac tromethamine. In carrageenan-induced paw edema, 0.8 ml/kg of the volatile oil showed antiinflammatory activity comparable to that of ketorolac tromethamine. The oil was also found to be effective in cotton pellet granuloma studies. (Kavimani et al., 1997)

·        Preventive effect on diabetes of the hot water extract (GvEx)  from leaves of Psidium guajava L., intake of guava tea by the humans prevents increases  in the blood glucose level presumably by inhibiting digestion of sugars in  gastrointestinal tract. Consequently, daily intake of guava tea might be useful to prevent the development of diabetes, which is frequently generated in human adults. (Deguchi et al., 1998)

·        Oral administration of 100, 200 and 400 /kg of Psidium guajava essential oil produced a significant antinociceptive effect in the formalin test and at 200 and 400 /kg in the acetic acid-induced writhing test in adult male albino mice. Of the major components only a-pinene, but not the b-caryophyllene, demonstrated significant antinociception in the formalin test. Neither the essential oil nor the major components could exert any significant effect in the hot-plate test. Pretreatment of mice with caffeine (20 /kg, i.p.), significantly inhibited the antinociceptive effect of essential oil in the formalin test. The antinociceptive effect of Psidium guajava oils has been suggested to be mediated by endogenously released adenosine. (Santos et. al., 1998).

·        In vitro antiamoebic activity of some Congolese plant extracts used as antidiarrhoeal in traditional medicine indicated that of the 45 plant extracts tested, 35 exhibited an antiamoebic activity and 10 were inactive. The highest activity was obtained with extracts from root bark of Psidium guajava was studied. (Tona et. al., 1998).

·        The methanol extract of the leaves of Psidium guajava was found to inhibit paw oedema induced by carrageenan in rats and pain induced by acetic acid in mice, and exhibited an antipyretic effect. Oral administration of the extract reduced intestinal transit time and prevented castor oil-induced diarrhoea in mice. A CNS depressant activity was exhibited by the extract by potentiating the phenobarbitone sleeping time in mice. (Olajide et al., 1999)

·        The medicinal properties of Psidium guajava for certain types of diseases covering cancer were attributed to the presence of carotenoids and other bioactive components and Vitamin-A in different parts of it. (Sharma et. al., 1999).

·        The anticough activity of Psidium guajava Linn. (Guava) leaf extract was evaluated in rats and guinea pigs. The results showed that water extract of the plant at doses of 2 and 5 g/kg, p.o. decreased the frequency of cough induced by capsaicin aerosol by 35 and 54%, respectively, as compared to the control, within 10 min after injection of the extract, (P < 0.01). However, the anticough activity is less potent than that of 3/kg dextromethorphan which decreased frequency of cough by 78% (P < 0.01). An experiment on isolated rat tracheal muscle showed that the extract directly stimulated muscle contraction and also synergized with the stimulatory effect of pilocarpine. This effect was antagonized by an atropine. Moreover, growth of Staphylococcus aureus and b-streptococcus group A, as determined by the disc diffusion method, was inhibited by water, methanol and chloroform extract of dry guava leaves (P < 0.001). The LD50 of guava leaf extract was more than 5 g/kg, p.o. These results suggest that guava leaf extract is recommended as a cough remedy. (Jaiarj et al., 1999)

·        The effects of Psidium guajava leaf extract on the bleeding time and three main mechanisms of haemostasis: vasoconstriction, platelet aggregation and blood coagulation, were investigated. The water extract of guava leaves did not shorten bleeding times in rats. Guava leaf extract potentiated the vascular muscle contraction induced in rabbits by phenylephrine, and when given alone it stimulated human platelet aggregation in vitro in a dose-dependent manner. On the other hand, it significantly prolonged blood coagulation; activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) (P less than 0.05) test. The higher the concentration of the extract the lower APTT was observed. Water extract of guava leaves showed ambiguous effects on the haemostatic system. (Jaiarj et. al., 2000).

·        The effects of hexane, ethyl acetate and methanol extracts of Psidium guajava leaves (20, 100, 500 and 1250 /kg) on the central nervous system in mice have been reported. These three extracts exhibited mostly dose-dependent antinociceptive effects in chemical and thermal tests of analgesia. The extracts also produced dose-dependent prolongation of pentobarbitone-induced sleeping time. However, they had variable and mostly non-significant effects on locomotor coordination, locomotor activity or exploration. The ethyl acetate extract was found to be the most active, followed by the hexane and the methanol extracts. (Shaheen et. al., 2000).

·        Alcoholic and aqueous extracts of Psidium guajava  parts  against human pathogen Klebsiella pneumoniae, it is found that the combination  of extract and antibiotic chloremphenicol is more effective than antibiotic  alone. The mixture of alcoholic extract and antibiotic was found more effective than the mixture of aqueous extract and antibiotic. (Tyagi and Bohra, 2001).

·        The methanolic extract of P. guajava (leaves) showing significant inhibitory activities against the growths of Salmonella spp. (two isolates), Shigella spp. (S. flexneri, S. virchow and S. ysenteriae) and enteropathogenic Escherechia coli (two isolates).  (Lin et al., 2002)

·        Evaluation the safety and efficacy of a phytodrug (QG-5(R)) developed from guava   leaves, standardized in its content of quercetin and orally administered to a   group of adult patients with acute diarrheic disease. Capsules containing 500   of the product were administered to 50 patients every 8 h during 3 days.  Results obtained showed that the used guava product decreased the duration of abdominal pain in these patients. (Lozoya et al., 2002)

·        Psidium guajava aqueous bark and methanolic extracts were found to possess   anti-bacterial activity. (Abdelrahim et al., 2002)

·        Effects of the aqueous extract from Psidium guajava L.  at concentrations having an equipotent radical-scavenging  activity on myocardial injury produced by global ischemia followed by  reperfusion were tested and were further compared with those of quercetin and  gallic acid, major antioxidative components of P. guajava L. and L. wrightii,  respectively. Extract significantly attenuated ischemic contracture  during ischemia and improved myocardial dysfunction after reperfusion.   Decreases in high-energy phosphates and increases in malondialdehyde in the reperfused hearts were significantly lessened with both plant extracts. Quercetin and gallic acid also exerted similar beneficial effects. These results indicate that P. guajava have cardioprotective effects against myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury in isolated rat hearts, primarily through their radical-scavenging actions. (Yamashiro et al., 2003).

·        The composition of the volatile oil obtained from the hydrodistillation of  the leaves of Psidium guajava L., growing in Nigeria, was studied by GC-MS.  Forty-two compounds, representing more than 90% of the volatile mixture, were identified. The significant compounds were limonene (42.1%) and b-caryophyllene (21.3%). (Ogunwande et al., 2003)

·        Inotropic effects of extracts of Psidium guajava L. (guava) leaves on the guinea pig atrium. (Garcia et al., 2003)

Miscellaneous Information

·        Aqueous extracts of Psidium guajava used as root dip treatment of tomato seedlings reduced the bacterial wilt incidence by 80.0, 75.99 and 72.01 percent respectively. Extracts of Psidium guajava is significantly effect in reducing the bacterial blight severity in rice when these were sprayed @ 20 percent over crop foliage 72 hours before inoculation with the pathogen. (Bora and Semul, 1998).
