DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial herbs up to 1.5 m high, erect, tufted, aromatic. Leaves linear-lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 8.5-14.5 x 0.5-1.0 cm, flat, cordate and amplexicaul at base, glabrous; sheaths terete, glabrous; ligules membranous. Inflorescence a compound 1.0-1.3 cm long, oblong panicles; spathes 3-5 cm long; spatheoles c 2 cm long, enclosing 3-6 spikelets. Sessile spikelets ovate or ovate-oblong, 3-5 mm long, awned. Lower glume ovate-oblong, obtuse, grooved on the back; upper glume lanceolate, acute; keel winged, serrulate. Lemma c 3.5 mm long, nerveless, ciliate, awned. Palea absent. Upper florets bisexual. Pedicellate spikelets male. Lower glume oblong-lanceolate, obtuse; upper glume 3-nerved. Lemma hyaline. Palae absent. Caryopsis oblong, 2-3 mm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Floral and seed  biology:
Inflorescence: Spathate panicle erect, compound, narrow; spatheole green to reddish green,20-40 mm long; peduncle hairy in the upper part. Raceme 15-20 mm long, lower raceme base and lower most pedicle swollen ,rachis internodes and pedicel  pillose along the margins, sparsely pilose on the back; Flower: Sessile spikelets 3.5 mm long ,glabrous ;lower glume charteceous,1mm wide, ovate with deep median groove  broadly winged,2-nerved; long, pedicelled awn 12 –18 mm spikelets 4 mm long glabrous; lower glume lanceolate,8-nerved. Androecium stamens 3 in whorls, odd stamen being anterior, filaments  free and delicate, anthers basifixed, dithecous, introrse and opening usually by longitudinal slits. Gynoecium monocarpellary with superior unilocular ovary having single anatropous ovule adnate to adaxial side of ovary, placentation basal, styles two, stigmas plumose; Fruit caryopsis; Seed one having thin ovary wall closely adheres to it;  It flowers between September - February.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal: 
Plants are highly cross pollinated by wind; Seeds are dispersed by wind.
Genetic variability:
C. martinii var. motia is diploid   (2n=20) and var. sofia is tetraploid   (2n=40). In nature considerable variations are found in seed progeny plant height, number of tillers, size and weight of, inflorescence herb yield, oil yield and geraniol contents.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds or vegetatively through slips.

Soil type : It grows well in well-drained medium loam and light sandy loam soil

Nutrient requirements :   In general, a mixture of 40 kg /ha of nitrogen, 50 kg /ha of phosphorous and 40 kg per/ ha. of potassium is applied at the time of planting in the   soil of moderate fertility. About 60-kg/ha nitrogen as a top dressing in 2-3 split doses are govern during growing season of the crop. A same dose is repeated during subsequent years. Beneficial effects of micronutrients consisting of copper, magnesium and zinc   have been reported.

Water management: It is hardy grass and tolerate long period of drought. It can grow as rain fed crop provided the rain is well distributed. Growing in north India and semi arid regions of south India, where frequent irrigation is required give good results. Irrigation is required during rain free period.

Harvesting period/ Method: Harvesting done 7-10 days after flowering is best time to obtain maximum oil yield. However, number of harvest feasible in the year is governed by temperature, humidity and soil fertility.

Detection of geraniol (65.0-84.9), geranyl acetate (6.4 - 12.10%), limonene, p-cymene, methylheptenone, 2-nonanol, linalool, citranellol, farnesene, b-terpineol, b-humulene, a-terpineol and farnesol in essential oil from var. motia by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.
Determination of geranyl formate (5.0-15.0), eight terpene esters (0.5-1.0% each) and unidentified new compounds inessential oil samples by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Essential oil contained limonene, a-terpinene, myrcene, b-caryophyllene, a-humulene, b- and g-selinenes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Geraniol (76.0-83.0), geranyl butyrate, (EZ)farnesyl acetate, (ZE), (EZ) and (EE)farnesols, estragole and neryl acetate identified in essential oil from Madagascaran plants. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Isolation of cymbodiacetal (fig. 2) from essential oil and its crystal structure elucidation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.
Geraniol (79.19) geranyl acetate(9.15) and linalool (3.64%) determined in essential oil from Indian plants by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
The essential oil of palmarosa (Cymbopogon martinii (Roxb.) Wats. var. motia Burk.) seeds collected from three different geog. locations in India was analyzed by capillary GC and GC-MS. The composition of the oil samples was compared with that of the oil of flowering palmarosa herb. Besides the main constituent, geraniol (74.5-81.8%), 55 other constituents, including those present in trace amounts, were identified in the seed essential oil. Although the composition of the seed oils is similar to that of the herb oil, quantative differences in the concentration of some constituents were observed. The seed oil was found to contain lower amounts of geranyl acetate and higher amounts of (E, Z)-farnesol than the herb oil. (Mallavarapu et al., 1998) CA. 1999,129:257678.

Pharmacology:

Essential oil showed high antimicrobial activity due to presence of geraniol as major component. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Oil of Cymbopogon martinii leaves obtained by distillation was given orally to study its effects on the exudative phase of the inflammatory reactions, using the technique of carrageenan-induced paw edema. Oil of Cymbopogon martinii showed dose-dependent anti-inflammatory activity comparable to that of diclofenac Na. (Krishnamoorthy et al., 1998) CA. 1999,129:76149; MAPA. 1998, 9804 : 2163.
