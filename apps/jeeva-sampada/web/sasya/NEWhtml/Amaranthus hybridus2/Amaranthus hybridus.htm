HABIT & HABITAT

A tall, robust annual grown as a leafy vegetable in Maharashtra, in the Nilgiri hills, and in Coorg. It is also grown as an ornament. Leaves purple or green; spikes slender, lax; bracteoles of female flowers 1.0-1.5 times as long as the perianth segments; fruit without distinct “neck”.

Availability : It has been grown as a staple crop only in the Himalays and in the hills of Southern Indian.

Miscellaneous Information

Birds eat the seed. In Central Africa the plant has been used in making red ink. The plant is used in piles to purity blood and in strantgury as a diuretic. It is valuable as an antiscorbutic. 

The leaves are used to relieve chest congestion and also for local application in scrofulous sores. 

The plant is detergent and astringent, recommended in menorrhagia, diarrhoea and dysentery. Externally it is used as an application in ulcerated condition of throat and mouth, as douche in leucorrhoea, and as a cooling wash for ulcers, sores, etc. it is reported to be diuretic
