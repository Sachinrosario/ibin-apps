DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary panicles composed with numerous spikes ;Flower: Bracteate, complete, regular, hermaphrodite; Calyx: hairy, teeth short, ciliate, the two upper connate except at the tip,  the lowest the longest, subacute; Corolla:  Pale yellow,  with  the  long claw, the limb-obovate-orbicular; Androceium: Stamens 9 in one bundle, the sheath of the filaments at the top each anthers minute, basifixed , with the cells back to back, dehiscing by apical slit; Gynoecium: Ovary pubescent, usually hairy at the base, ovules 2-4, stigma small, terminal ; Fruit: Pods  narrowed  at the base into a long stalk which is twice as long as calyx ; Seed: 1-4, flat-compressed ; 

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting time: March-June.           
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by   wind, birds or animals. 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=20

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Biochanin A, tectorigenin and a new isoflavone – 5,4’-dihydroxy-6,7-dimethylisoflavone (7-O-methyltectorigenin) (fig. 1a)– from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Synthesis of 7-O-methyltectorigenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Isolation of 7-O-methyltectorigenin from immature green pods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Biochanin A-7-glucoside (sissortin) and an isoflavone-O-glycoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

A new isoflavone from flowers identified as 5-hydroxy-6,7,4’-trimethoxyisoflavone (fig. 1b) m.p. 184°C. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

5,7,4’-trihydroxy-8-methoxyisoflavone (isotectorigenin) from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A new allylphenol of latifolin type – dalbergiphenol,(fig. 2) b.p. 154°/0.3 mm – isolated from heartwood along with dalbergenone, dalbergin and methyldalbergin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

A new isoflavone glucoside – caviunin-7-O-gentiobioside – isolated from pods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Isocaviunin, tectorigenin, dalbergin, biochanin A and 7-hydroxy-4-methylcoumarin from mature pods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Isocaviudin isolated along with tectorigenin-7-O-glucoside and caviunin-7-O-glucoside from mature pods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Isocaviunin-7-gentiobioside isolated from pods and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Seed oil (4.1%) contained fatty acids composed of linoleic (52.5%), palmitic (16.2%), oleic (14.6%), linoleic (9.80%) and stearic (7.0%) acids and lipids comprising of neutral lipids (88.5%), glycolipids (7.2%) and phospholipids (4.0%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

CIMAP:
  Annual av. phenol content was least in Bauhinia variegata (3.58 mg/g), while max. in Cassia siamia (16.61 mg/g). In majority of species, phenol was more during rainy season as compared to winter and summer. Higher phenolic conc. during rains is probably due to built in mechanism to protect the leaves from microbial infection which is common in this season1.
