HABIT & HABITAT
Annual, erect herbs, 0.4-2.0 m high. Stem and branches strongly ridged, glabrous. It occur almost in all part of India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves oblong-lanceolate to narrowly linear, 1.5-14.5 x 0.2-3.0 cm, acute or obtuse at apex, tapering at base into a slender petiole, glabrous, usually tinged with red. Flowers in terminal dense, many flowered, silvery to pink, conical at first but later cylindrical, 2.5-18.5 x 1.5-2.0 cm spikes; peduncles up to 20 cm long; bracts and bracteoles lanceolate or lower deltoid, 3-5 mm long, persistent. Tepals or perianth narrowly elliptic-oblong, 6-10 mm long, acute or obtuse at apex. Utricle obovoid, ovoid to globose, 3-4 mm long, rounded at apex. Seeds lenticular, 1.25-1.5 mm long, reticulate.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:  

Inflorescence: Cylindric blunt or acuminate spike; Flower: At first pinkish afterwards whitish , crowded and imbricate, bracteate, bracteolate; Perianth: Perianth of 5 segments slightly connate below, seplas linear lanceolate, acute, scarious with 3 close slender striae on the back ;Androecium: Stamens 5, short, filaments connate into a cup, anther 2-celled ; Gynoecium: Ovary 1-celled, ovoid, style filiform, elongate after flowering, sometimes exerted in fruits, stigma simple ; Fruit: Capsule circumscissile about in the middle ; Seed:  4-8, sub reniform, compressed, black, polished, shining.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Seeds are aphrodisiac and used for the treatment of doarrhoea, blood and eye diseases.
Chemistry:

·        Celosianin and isocelosianin found to be 10-coumaroyl-betanicin-5-O-glucuronosylglucoside and 10-feruloyl-isobetanidine-5-O-glucuronsylglucoside respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Hordenine, b-sitosterol and anicistrocladine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        24-ethyl-22-dehydrolathosterol (69.6), 24-ethyllathosterol (17.5), 24-methyllathosterol (3.9), 24-methyl-22-dehydrolathosterol (3.8), avenasta-7-en-ol (3.3) and 24-methylenelathosterol (1.9%) determined in seeds by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·        Hexadecadienoic, tricosanoic, lignoceric, cerotic and a-aminobutyric acid together with 1,1’-[3-(2-cyclopentyl-ethylidene)-1,5-pentadiyl]bis-cyclopentane identified in roots by HPLC and GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·        b-sitosterol, cholesteryl palmitate, 3,5-dihydroxybenzaldehyde, 4-hydroxybenzoic acid, 3,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid, n-butyl-b-D-fructoside and sucrose isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        The fatty acids of Celosia argentea were investigated using GC/MS techniques. The structure was elucidated using 2-substituted 4, 4-dimethyloxazoline derivative along with methyl ester before GC/MS analysis. (Azam and Sherwani, 1999). MAPA 9906-3855.

·        Citrusin C, indican, (3Z)-hexenyl-1-O-(6-O-a-rhamnopyranosyl-p-glucopymnoside), (3Z)-hexenyl-1-O-p-glucopyranoside, 7E-6,9-dihydromegastigma-7-ene-3-one-9-O-0-glucopyranoside and trans-ferulic acid were extracted from Celosia argentea leaves and their skin-lightening activities were tested. Citrusin C showed the highest skin-lightening activity and was used in manufacturing skin-lightening cosmetics.(Sawab, Akiyoshi; et. al.; 1999).

·        Cosmetics, bath preparations and detergents contain ≥ 1 extracts selected from those of Celosia argentea and other plants. The plant extracts show long-lasting skin-moisturizing effect and prevents dryness, rough skin, chap, dandruff, split hair, etc. Moisturizing effects of the extracts was examined by measuring conductance of skin treated with the extracts. Lotions, creams, shampoos, hair tonics, face cleansers, bath salts, mouthwashes, dishwashing detergents, etc. containing the extracts were also formulated. (Toki Masako et. al., 2000).

·        The betalains of yellow, orange and red inflorescences of common cockscomb (Celosia argentea var. cristata) were compared and proved to be qualitatively identical to those of feathered amaranth (Celosia argentea var. plumosa). In addition to the known compounds amaranthin and betalamic acid, the structures of three yellow pigments were elucidated to be immonium conjugates of betalamic acid with dopamine, 3-methoxytyramine and (S)-tryptophan by various spectroscopic techniques and comparison to synthesized reference compounds; the latter two are new to plants. (Schliemann et al , 2001)

·        Three new bicyclic peptides, celogentins A , B , and C , have been isolated together with a known-related peptide, moroidin , from the seeds of Celosia argentea, and their structures including absolute stereochemistry were determined by using extensive NMR methods and chemical means. (Kobayashi et al., 2001)

A unique bicyclic peptide, moroidin, from the seeds of Celosia argentea (Amaranthaceae) strongly inhibited the polymerization of tubulin. The stereostructure of moroidin was reinvestigated by spectroscopic data, chemical degradation, and molecular dynamics simulation. (Morita et al., 2001)

Pharmacology:

·        The water extract of Celosia argentia showed sigficant hepato-protective effect on CCL4 induced liver injuries in rats and D-glactosamine/lipopolysaccharide induced liver injury in mice. The hepatoprotective guided fractination of this extract led to the isolation of an active constituent, celosian (mp 255°C) and was found to be an acidic hetroglycan containing 4% of proteins. (Hase et al, 1996). MAPA 9702-0834.

·        Celosian (an acidic polysaccharide from Celosia argentia) significantly protected elevation of serum enzyme levels. It also suppressed the elevation of ALT level liver injury in mice in dose dependent manner. Celosian reduced DNA fragmentation in both D-Ga1N/LPS- and Con A-induced liver injuries. (Hase et. al., 1996/97). MAPA 9803-1506.

·        Celosian did not reduce the release of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha), it protected against recombinant human TNF-alpha (rhTNF-alpha)-induced liver injury in D-galactosamine sensitized mice (Hase et al., 1996)

·        Celosian, an acidic polysaccharide from the seeds of Celosia argenteo (Amaranthaceae) was found to be a potent antihepatotoxic agent for chemical and immunological liver injury models in animals. The immunomodulating action of celosian was studied to clarify the preventive mechanism of celosian on liver injuries. Celosian induced tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha) production in mice. Celosian also induced the production of interleukin-1 beta (IL-1 beta) and nitric oxide (NO) in macrophage cell line J774.1 in a concentration-dependent manner (1 to 1000 micrograms/ml). Moreover, celosian induced IL-1 beta secretion in human mononuclear cells. In addition, celosian enhanced gamma interferon (IFN-gamma) production activity of concanavalin A (Con A) in mice spleen cells, though celosian alone did not significantly influence IFN-gamma production. (Hase et al., 1997)

·        Celosia argentea seed  (CAE) extra ct administration resulted in increased production of IL-2, IFN-gamma and decreased production of a Th2 cytokine (IL-4) from splenocytes stimulated by PMA and A23187. Thus, the anti-metastatic effect by CAE is based on its immunomodulating properties including induction of cytokines such as IL-12, IL-2 and IFN-gamma leading to a Th1 dominant immune state and activating macrophages to the tumoricidal state. This may provide a basis for the inhibition of cancer metastasis. (Hayakawa et al., 1998)

·        Antibacterial activity of crude alcoholic extract of Datura alba and Celosia argentea leaves were studied against pathogens isolated from infected burn patients. The disc-diffusion method showed significant zone of lysis against all the pathogens studied and the results are comparable to the conventional antibiotic cream namely Silver Sulphadiazine (SSD). On comparing the efficiency of the two extracts, extract of D. alba exhibited more than 50% increase in antibacterial activity compared to C. argentea. (Gnanamani et al., 2003).

·         Effect of an alcoholic extract of Celosia argentea seeds (ACAS) on blood glucose and body weight in alloxan-induced diabetic rats. ACAS was found to reduce the increase of blood glucose in alloxan-induced diabetic rats (27.8% at 250 mg/kg and 38.8% at 500 mg/kg body weight). Chronic administration of ACAS significantly (p<0.01) reduced the blood glucose in alloxan-induced diabetic rats for two weeks. Also the extract prevented a decrease in body weight in alloxan-induced diabetic rats. These results suggest that the ACAS possesses anti-diabetic activity in alloxan-induced diabetic rats. (Vetrichelvan et al., 2002)
