DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tall herbs; rootstocks ovoid; tubers sessile, cylindric. Leaves in tuft; lamina oblong-lanceolate, c 50 x 8 cm, caudate-acuminate at apex, tapering at base. Herb with large ovoid root stock, sessile tubers thick, cylindric, bright yellow inside; petiole 60 cm long, leaves green, 30-45 cm by 10-20 cm; peduncle 15 cm or more long hidden by sheathing petiole, spike 10-15 cm, about 5 cm in diameter; bracts pale green, ovate, about 3.7 cm long, those of the coma pale pink; flowers yellow, as long as the bracts.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in 10-15 cm long spikes. Corolla white; flowering bracts pale green; bracts of coma tinged with pink; labellum with a central yellow band.
CIMAP:
   Inflorescence: Autumnal spikes; Flower: Bract greenish-white, complete, hermaphrodite,,; Calyx: Obtusely 3-toothed ; Corolla: White , lobes oblong, acute, lip semi-elliptic, yellow ; Androecium: only 1 perfect rest is reduced to staminodes,, anthers linear 2-celled ; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled ,inferior, ovules many, style slender, stigma entire; Fruit: Loculicidally 3-valved capsule ; Seed: Often arillate. Flowering & Fruiting : September-November. 
Floral and seed biology:
June - November.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through rhizome.

Soil type:  Sandy loam is most suitable for the crop.

Nutrient requirements: Farmyard manure, urea, super phosphate and potash is useful for the crop.

Water management:  Plant requires light irrigation before digging,                                       

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is ready for harvest after 8m months of planting when the leaves and stem is dried.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Part used:
Rhizome-stimulant, antirheumatic, anthelmintic, carminative, blood purifier, antiperiodic, in sprains, conjunctivitis, skin diseases.
Uses : In Ayurveda rhizome is pungent, bitter. It is used in blood diseases, leucoderma, scabies, urinary discharges, bad mouth taste, dyspepsis, elephantiasis, small pox, swellings, boils and snake-bite. In Unani rhizome is used for liver affection, jaundice, urinary discharges, scabies ( Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).


Chemistry:
CIMAP:
The av. curcuminoid content of the rhizomes of C. longa included 1.11, 0.86 and 1.62% of curcumin (I), demethoxy – I, and bis-de-methoxy-I, resp; no changes in these levels were obsd. during a 17 wk. Growth period1; Both cured and uncured dried rhizomes of four turmeric (Curcuma longa L.) cultivars were examd. For changes in curcumin, essential oils, and oleoresins during 12 mo storage at ambient temp. curcumin content continued to decline for ≤ 10 mo, but the change, thereafter was minor. However, essential oil and oleoresins decreased throughout the storage period. The max. losses in curcumin, essential oils and oleoresins were 23.4, 27.5 and 24.2%, resp., after 12 mo in cured rhizomes of cultivar EM-321 and uncured rhizomes of cultivar PCT-2 2; Twenty seven accessions of Curcuma longa were grown in the climatic conditions of North Indian plains in Lucknow. Rhizomes harvested from each accession were hydrodistd. For their essential oil. The oil content of rhizomes varied between 0.16% and 1.94% on a fresh wt. basis. The essential oil of each accession was evaluated for its major terpenes: β-pinene, p-cymene, α-curcumene, β-curcumene, ar-turmerone, α-turmerone and β-turmerone. The accessions could be classified into two types: (a) those in whose essential oils the sum of seven major terpenes was in the range 58-79% ; (b) those in whose oils the sum was 10-22%. The rhizomes of all the accessions were also evaluated for their curcumin content, which was found to vary from 0.61% to 1.45% on a dry wt. basis
NBPGR:Essential oil contains ar-tur-merone and ar-curcumene as major constituents.  Some of the other are a and b-pinene, sabinene, myrcene, a-terpinene, limonene P-cymene, perillyl alcohol, turmerone, eugenol, iso- eugenol, eugenol methylether and iso-eugenol methyl ether.  Curcumin and related compounds have been reported as major constituents of the rhizomes.  Recently, a number of sesquiterpenes have been reported from C. longa viz. the sesquiterpenoids of germacrane, bisabolane and guaiane skeletons.
