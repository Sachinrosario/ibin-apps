DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  Tall, evergreen tree 50 m high with spreading branches and pendulous branchlets, branchlets densely pubescent, bark thick, fissured at places; wood aromatic oily, leaves needle-shaped, 25-50 mm long as thick as broad, dark bluish green, cones reddish brown, 80 mm - 1.25 cm long and 50-80 mm across.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Essential oil from wood chips diaphoretic, insecticidal in skin diseases and ulcer, bark-astringent, febrifuge, antidiarrhoeal.

Pharmacology:

·        Stem bark extract showed significant anti-inflammatory activity in rat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)a.

·        Hexane soluble alcohol extract of Cedrus deodara wood was fractionated and himachalol, a sesquiterpene alcohol, was isolated and tested for anti-passive cutaneous anaphylaxis (PCA) and mast cell stabilizing activity in rats. Himachalol showed significant anti-PCA activity (30 to 88 percent) with 12.5 to 50mg/kg p.o doses. Mast cell stabilizing activity (68 to 76 percent) was observed against compound 48/80 and in the case of sensitized mast cells, against egg albumin induced degranulation. (Gupta et al., 1997).

·        C.deodara wood essential oil was found to possess significant anti-inflammatory activity in carageenin induced oedema in rats. it was devoid of analgesic, sedative and motor incoordinating activities in mice. However, it caused a significant increase in pentobarbitone-induced hypnosis which may be due to inhibition of drug metabolizing enzymes. (Tandan et al., 1998)

·        Volatile oil of Cedrus deodara, administered orally at the doses of 50, 100 and 200 mg/kg body weight, significantly inhibited the pedal edema induced by compound 48/80 in rats. The oil significantly inhibited compound 48/80 induced degranulation of isolated rat peritoneal mast cells at concentration ranging from 25-200 micro g/ml.  C. deodara wood oil also significantly inhibited the  enzyme lipoxygenase at a concentration of 200 micro g/ml. The anti inflammatory activity of wood oil has been attributed to its mast cell stabilizing activity and the inhibition of leukotriene synthesis. (Shinde  et al., 1999).

·        The volatile oil extracted by steam distillation of the wood of C. deodara was examined for its oral anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity at the doses of 50 and 100mg/kg weight. It produced significant inhibition of carrageen-an induced rat paw edema and of both exudative-proliferative and chronic phase of inflammation in adjuvant arthritic rats at doses of 50 and 100mg/kg body weight. The oil at both tested doses was found to possess analgesic activity against acetic acid-induced writhing and hot plate reaction in mice. (Shinde et al., 1999).

Miscellaneous Information

·        Herbal formulation prepared from Cedrus deodara and other herbs was found to be effective and non-toxic for the control of various arthropod pests in small animals and mange in dogs. (Sharma, 1996).

·        Oil of Cedrus deodara was tested for olfactory response of 10 days old larvae of rice moth, and found to be effective. (Behal,  1998).

·        The efficacy of herbal drug AV/EPP/14 containing Cedrus deodara and other herbs was evaluated in vitro against ectoparasite of dogs. The formulation of 1:4 concentration was effective in killing larva, nymph and adult ticks in 5, 30, 72 hours respectively. Against fleas, lice and mites cent percent mortality was observed in 8m in, 7 min, and 7hours respectively. (Bhilegaonkar and Maske, 1998).

·        The volatile oils from wood chips of Cedrus deodara exhibited absolute toxicity, inhibiting the mycelial growth of Aspergillus niger and Curvularia avoidea completely. The MIC of C. deodara is 1000 ppm. The oil showed a broad fungitoxic spectrum inhibiting the mycelial growth of 10 and 12 out of 15 fungi. The fungitoxicity of the oils was not destroyed by autoclaving and storage (up to 90 days). (Singh and Tripathi , 1999).

·        Essential oils of Cedrus deodara were found most effective resulting in 58.67 percent and mortality when potter’s Precision Laboratry Spray Tower (without food) was used against pube beetle. In the case of housefly, when food was not provided to adults and Potter’s Precision Laboratory Spray Tower was used oils of Cedrus deodara gave 66.67 percent mortality respectively. (Singh and Mehta , 1998).

·        Topical treatment of dogs infected with Demodex canis with a herbal formulation AV/EPP 14 containing Cedrus deodara and other herbs, at 1:4 concentration showed good miticidial action. All the dogs responded to treatment with no recurrence of infection in 20-25 days.  (Pathak and Shukla ,1998).

·        Oil of the Cedrus deodara was examined against different insect pests/vectors.  Cedrus oil also showed significant metamorphic inhibiting activity against D. koenigii. Significant oviposition deterrence activity was also exibithed against P.operculella by oil of Cedrus deodara.(Tare,  2000-2001).
