Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Moderate to large, deciduous tree occurs along river banks in moist flats in eastern and southern India. With spreading crown and stout branches. Light demander, drought and fire resistant. Susceptible to frost. Occurs throughout the India.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Moderate to large, deciduous tree, with spreading crown and stout branches.  Flowers purple, appearing when the tree bare of leaves, in cymose fascicles, the male racemes compound, the female simple; pedicels very short, fulvous-pubescent. Bracts ovate, acute, pubescent outside, ciliate. Calyx ovate-orbicular, ciliate. Petals 4, ovate-oblong, acute. Stamens in the male flowers equaling the petals. Ovary in the female ovoid-oblong, in the male rudimentary, 4-lobed. Drupes reniform, compressed, red.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Natural reproduction by seed is generally adequate and occurs only on deep soil. Seeds are dispersed by birds, which eat fruits. Artificial reproduction may be done by direct sowing or by transplanting nursery-raised seedlings or stumps, preferably a year old. The seeds, however, do not retain their viability for long and the germination capacity is only c. 43%. The tree may also be propagated by branch cuttings; 2-3 year old branches succeed better than older ones. Tree stumping in Mysore has been proved successful.

 

 

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diseases

Tree is subject to attack of rust (Phacospora odinae Mundkur), brown leaf rust (Ceratelium lanneae [V. Hoehn] Arth.) and leaf spot (Meliola geniculata Syd. & Butler).

Pests:

Among insect pests, botrychid beetle (Heterobostrychis aequalis Waterh.) and few other borers of felled timbers are known to attack green logs and converted timber. Plocaederus obesus Gaha., a cerambycid beetle, also attacks felled timber, but not the living tree; the larvae of this insect pupates inside the wood in wide gallaries, sometimes 15 cm. Deep from surface. Pachydissus sp., Xyleborus parvulus (Eichhoff.) and Glenea multiguttata Guer. Also attack the tree. The grubs of the last species feed on the bast.

Xeromphalina campanella (Fr.) Maire – root and butt rot

Corticium koleroga (Cke.) Hohnel – thread blight from Calcutta (West Bengal)

Marasmius campanella Holterm – from Calcutta (West Bengal) causing bark cracking and die-back of branches.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel: Wood used in admixture with other woods as fuel. But considered to be bad fuel. 

Wood used locally for house building, packing cases, furniture, wheel spokes, cattle yokes, oil presses, rice pounders, plough shafts, spear shafts, combs, brush backs, wooden jars, mine props, cutting blocks, boot lasts, troughs, well construction, dug-outs and boats.

Used for carving and turnery. Suitable for commercial plywood and tea chests, slack cooperage, bobbins, rollers in jute mills, inferior pencils, second quality slate frames and railway sleepers after treatment.

Wood used for preparation of chemical and semi-chemical pulp. Chemical pulp used for the manufacture of writing and printing papers and semi-chemical pulp used for brown wrapping paper. Both the pulps easy to bleach and possess satisfactory strength properties. Timber is suitable for external use. Used for furniture.


Harvesting Practices: Coppices and pollards well. Sawn stock should be open-stacked under cover. Ends of boards and scantlings should be treated with paraffin, wax, resin or tar and pitch to prevent end splitting.

Produce and Products:

Gums

Chemistry:
 Calorific value of the wood: 4,933 cal, 8,880 Btu.

Processing Techniques:

Difficult and slow to season. Should be converted soon after felling. Sapwood and heartwood should be treated separately. Sapwood liable to split and discolour, dries quickly than heartwood.

Wood can be kiln dried in 8-10 days, in addition to initial steaming, one intermediate steaming and one more towards the end for 2-3 hrs are necessary. Presence of gum makes kiln drying difficult.

 

Other uses:

Bark employed in native medicines.

A mucilaginous gum, Jhingan gum, exudes from wounds and cracks in the bark, soluble in water. Used in calico printing, paper and cloth sizing, inferior varnishes and inks, in plastering and white washing and for preserving fishing nets. Also used in confectionery.

A good fodder tree for cattle, sheep, goats and especially for elephants.

Bark yields a strong fiber.

Miscellaneous Information

Plant yields gum, which is used for sizing paper and cloth. It is also used for confectionery.
Planted as a roadside tree.

Wood: Sapwood white to yellowish white when first exposed, turns brownish grey, sometimes with purplish cast, with age. Heartwood light pinkish red to light red, turns red or brownish red with age, rather lustrous, straight or narrowly interlocked and close-grained, medium and even textured, fairly strong, moderately hard and light. Specific gravity 0.55, 36 lb./ c. ft. Considerable variations in the weight of wood recorded, probably due to variations in sapwood and heartwood. Grain often interlocked in broad bands.
