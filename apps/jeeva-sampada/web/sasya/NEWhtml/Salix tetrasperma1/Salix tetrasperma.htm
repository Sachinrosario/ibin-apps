Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A much-branched tree. Moist cool places in subtropical climate. Found growing along the banks of rivers and streams and in wet swampy places throughout the greater part of India, ascending to an altitude of 1,800 m. in the Himalayas and 2,100 m. in the Nilgiri hills.

Fast growing. Frost hardy. Light demander. Sensitive to drought. Can withstand inundation for some period.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A much-branched tree. Bark rough with deep vertical furrows. Young shoots silky, becoming glabrous. Leaves lanceolate, acute or acuminate, usually serrulate, sometimes obscurely so, green and glabrous above, white and more or less silky beneath when young. Stipules subulate, deciduous.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers appearing after the leaves; catkins hairy, peduncles silky-villous, leafy at the base.

Male flowers : sweet-scented, sessile. Bracts ovate, concave, subacute, silky-villous, veined. Disk of 2 yellow glands. Stamens 5-10, filaments free, of various lengths, the longest about twice as long as the bract, anthers yellow.

Female flowers: pedicellate, bracts smaller than those of the male, oblong or obovate-oblong, hairy. Disk small, ½-annular, yellow, clasping the pedicel at the side opposite the bract. Ovary fusiform, glabrous. Ovules 4-6 (usually 4). Stigma sessile, 2-branched, each branch 2-lobed. Capsule glabrous, on a long slender stalk. Seeds 4-6.

Floral and seed biology:

Tree grows well from cuttings planted in moist places. Two types of the plant are distinguished: one that grows in the high land is called the Naga-veh, and the other, the wet land type, is called Pani-veh.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Good firewood and charcoal. Calorific value low. Yields gunpowder charcoal.

Wood: Sapwood is white or nearly so, merging into the heartwood, which is uniformly light-red, turning light reddish brown to light brown with age. The wood is lustrous, especially sapwood, with silky sheen when first exposed, but becomes dull with exposure. It is straight or somewhat interlock-grained, medium fine- and mostly even-textured, very light to heavy (sp. gr. 0.37; wt. 385-849 kg./ cu.m.) and soft to moderately hard. It is fairly durable under cover and seasons well with a little care; the logs should be converted when green and the stacks of sawn material open-piled. It is, perhaps, not difficult to treat the wood. It is very easy to saw, though the dried wood is rather tough, works well by hand and turns easily, but does not finish to a smooth surface, requiring hand finish.

Wood used for posts and planks, house building, bent-wood furniture, ploughs and agricultural implements. Suitable for cabinet and fancy work and match-boxes. Also employed for pencils and cricket stumps and may be tried as patternwood for brush backs.


Harvesting Practices: It coppices and pollards well.

Other uses:

Leaves medicinally used. Also used as fodder.

Branches used for basket making.

Extensively used for soil conservation, stream bank protection and land reclamation. Also suitable for live check-dams.

Used for roadside avenue planting and recreational forestry.

Leaves - Leaves are medicinally important to cure rheumatism, epilepsy and venereal diseases.
