HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect shrubs, 1.2-2.4 m high; usually shaggy tomentose at tips, it is found along Western Himalaya from Kashmir eastwards up to 2000 m., upper ganga plains, Bengal, Assam, Bangladesh, Pakistan (Hazaria).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A stout shrub branches an erect under shrub or shrub, 1.2-2.4 m. in height. Bark: brown, thin, smooth. Leaves: ovate or ovate lanceolate, stellate-tomentose beneath. Flowers: rose-pink, in cymes, drupes white.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence:Axillary chyme ; Flower:  Bracteate, complete, reguler, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Lobes small, stellate hairy on flowering time; Corolla: Lobes small, tubular, sub-cylindric,;Androecium: Stamens 4, anthers exserted, small, ovate, 2-celled, glandular ; Gynoecium: Ovary imperfect, 2-celled, style linear, stigma dilated ; Fruit: Drupe, small, globular; Seed: Shortly oblong, exalbuminous. Flowering & Fruiting : August-November. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
The leaves applied hot, are applied hot are reported to give relief in rheumatic pains.  A paste of the seed is employed in treating oral ulcers.  The seeds are reported to be employed in leprosy, and as diuretic.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:
Leaves contain b-sitosterol, calliterpenone and its monoacetate, ursolic acid, luteolin, apigenin, luteolin –7-0- glucuronide apigenin –7-0- glucerronide, b-sitostecol-b-D-glucoside, 2 a – hydroxyursolic acid and crategolic acid.  Seeds contain calliterpenone and its acetate and oleanolic acid

NBRI:
A new tetracyclic – calliterpenone (fig. 2a)– and its acetate isolated and their stereo structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Structure of calliterpenone revised to 3-oxo-13b-kaurene-16a,17-diol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Luteolin, apigenin, luteolin-7-O-glucuronide and apigenin-7-O-glucuronide from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Isolation and identification of flavone CMF-1 (fig. 1) as 3,3’,7-trimethoxy-4’,5-dihydroxyflavone, from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

L(+)a-Amino-b-(p-methoxyphenyl)propionic acid identified  in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

A new diterpenoid – isopropylidenocalliterpenone (fig. 2b) was isolated from essential oil of leaves along with calliterpenone and its monoacetate and characterized as 16a,17-isopropylideno-3-oxo-phyllocladane. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for blisters, boils on tongues (Fruit, Maheshwari et al, 1980; Maheshwari et al, 1981; Nautiyal, 1981; Weed, Shah and Joshi, 1971); Cuts (Bark, Megoneitso and Rao, 1983); Headache (Leaf, Tiwari et al, 1979); Indigestion, abdomen trouble (Seed + Root, Balodi, 1988); Rheumatism (Leaf, Sharma et al, 1979; Nautiyal, 1981); ulcers (Seed, Uniyal, 1968); Wounds (Bark, Megoneitso and Rao, 1983; Leaf, Gaur et al, 1980); edible (Fruit, Malhotra et al, 1985)
