HABIT & HABITAT:
Shrubs, 3-6 m high, Fairly common in deciduous forests. Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Orissa, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. Bangadesh, Sri lanka, Nepal, Myanmar.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrubs, 3-6 m high. Leaves ovate, lanceolate, ovate-lanceolate or ovate-elliptic, 1.0-12 x 0.7-4.5 cm, acute to acuminate or sometimes subobtuse or rounded at apex, subcordate or subobtuse at base, margin serrate, pubescent above, densely tomentose beneath; petioles up to 7 mm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Umbellate cymes: ; Flower: Male and bisexual; Male Flower: Sepals linear-lanceolate, softly hairy outside, petals oblong, rounded at the apex, entire hairy on the back, glandular, stamens 40, intermixed with white hairs; Hermaphrodite Flower: Calyx: Sepals linear-lanceolate, softly hairy outside; Corolla: Petals oblong, rounded at the apex, entire hairy on the back, glandular; Androceium:  Stamens 40, intermixed with white hairs, anther very small; Gynoecium: Ovary densely villous, the hairs often equalling or sometimes exceeding the stamen, style glabrous, stigma 5-lobed ; Fruit: Drupe fleshy, wrinkled, slightly 4-lobed, sparingly stellate, hairy;

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers polygamous in axillary, umbellate cymes; peduncles 1-3 together, up to 1 cm long; pedicels 2-5 mm long. Sepals elliptic-lanceolate, ca 8 mm long, hirsute. Petals oblong, ca 3 mm long, rounded, white. Stamens more than 40. Drupes subglobose, ca 1.2 cm across, obscurely 4-lobed, fleshy, wrinkled, densely hirsute or hispid. It flowers almost throughout the year.

Flowering & Fruiting time: August -September. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Roots are used in blisters, boils, cuts, dysentery, testical inflammations, venereal diseases and wounds. Roots and barks are also useful in rheumatism.
