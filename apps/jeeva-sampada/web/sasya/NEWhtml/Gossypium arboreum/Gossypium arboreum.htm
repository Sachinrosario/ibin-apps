HABIT & HABITAT

Tree cotton is a perennial or annual shrub, a mature of Indo-China, and found throughout the rain-fed savannah areas from Africa through Arabia and India, to China, Japan and East Indies. It is cultivated in Burma, Malaysia, Korea, Japan and Taiwan.
Availability : Plenty, throughout India.


Conservation/ Threat Status : Cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tree cotton attains a height of 2-10 ft., has slender trailing branches which are often purplish. The twigs and young leaves are finely puberulent, caescent or hairy. Leaves are deeply cut into 3-7 lobes, lobes ovate-oblong or curvilinear, acte. Bracteoles closely nest the bud and flowers, nearly triangular, longer than broad, entire with 3 or 4 coarse teeth at the apex.

Flowers purple, red, yellow or white with or without red blotch at the base of petals. Bolls or fruits tapering, profusely pitted, usually 3 or 4 locules, opening wide when ripe. Seeds upto 1 ad have proximal or a locule small two layers of hair, tint and fuzz. Lint is white, grey or brown. Fuzz is green, grey or white and uniformly distributed over the seed or confined to a tuft at each end.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
About six or eight flower buds are produced on each fruit limb, one arising at each joint. Usually large plant may have many more flowers. The buds appear at first as small, pyramidal green structures called squares. Flowers open about 21 days after the squares are first recognizable. A flower bud tat is to open the following day may be recognized by its enlarged but well twisted corolla; flowers used in hybridizing work are emasculated at this stage .

Flowers are complete, perfect, protoandrous, solitary, axillary or in an elongated inflorescence, pale yellow with or without purploish blotches to purple red with a darker center. Flowers are surrounded by an involucre of large, leafy bracts (epicalyx) that are generally persistent. The corolla is 2-3 times as long as the involucre . Inside the corolla and attached to it at the base is a whorl of numerous stamens. The filament are united below to form a tube which surrounds the pistil. The staminal tube shows ten ridges. The pistil consists of a small conical ovary, a slender, thread-like style, and a stigma. The vary is superior. The stigma appears to be simply the outer end of the style slightly enlarged, grooved or lobed, and covered with a stigmatic surface, which is receptive.

There are four sets of nectarines, one inside the flower and tree extra floral. The floral nectarines form a ring about 1/16 inch wide on the inside of the calyx near its base. The secretary part of the nectary consists of numerous pluriseptate glandular hairs, or papillae closely aggregated.

The nectarines secret a sweetish liquid, which attracts bees, butter flies and certain other insects.

The boll (capsule) tapers to the apex and is usually 3-celled, rarely 4 or 5 celled. The seeds are small, covered, oval and dark brown or nearly black. They are covered with grayish green or rust coloured fuzz and yellowish white rusty white tint. Sometimes fuzz is about .

About 2000 free endosperm nuclei are produced 7 to 8 days after pollination, when centripetal wall formation is initiated at the micropylar pole. Young dicot embryo develops 15-16 days after pollination.

 

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Flowers are usually self-fertilized. Fertilization with pollen from other plants may range from less than 5% to as much as 50% depending on the activities of insects. There probably is little crossing, aside from that by insects. Insects such as bees and butterflies are attracted by nectarines, floral and extra floral both. Bees visit extra floral nectarines, avoid the floral ones. Self incompatible clones have also been observed

Phenology: Flowers during September-October. Fruiting occurs during October-December.


Pollinators:It is more or less dependent on insect pollination and hive bees are the preferred pollinators (Todd and Mc Gregor, 1960) (5). Pollination occurs immediately after anthesis; pollen grains stickyard germinate within half an hour.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Biology :

Seeds are small, oval, dark brown or nearly black and have two coats. Epigeal over coat hairy.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:

The Mexican boll weevil (Anthomus grandis Boh.) and the pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella Saund) are considered the most destructive pests. The adult female Mexican boll weevil bores into flower bud and lay her eggs; the resulting larvae attack and complete the resin of the boll. The adult female pink follwarm lays her eggs in flowers and on the green bolls; when the larvae emerge, they bore into the bolls and attack the seeds. Preventive measures against these pests include destruction of aged cotton plants and infested bolls, planting as early in the season as possible, and the use of early maturing varieties. The best direct protection against boll-weevil infestations is to spray the plants with calcium arsenate or one of the newer organic insecticides such as aldrin or dieldrin; disinfection of the seeds before planting is recommended for avoiding the pink bollworm.

Other pests include, the cotton leaf worm or cotton caterpillar (Alabama argilacea Huba) attacks leaves, flowers, young bolls, and other tender parts of plant; Southern grassworm (Laphygma frugiperda S. and A.) grow on stems and eat the growing tips of cotton plants; the cotton Colluworm (Chloridea obsoletea Hub) destroys cotton squares and bolls; cotton square-bores (Uranotes melinus Hubn); Cotton red spider (Tetianychus telarius L.); Cotton Aphis, or cotton louse (Aphis gossypii Glover) on leaves; Cotton stainer (Dyducus suturellus H. Schf) damages bolls and squares; cotton wire worms (Monocrepidius vesperinus Fabricius)feeds on roots; cotton Flea Hopper (Psallus seriatus Rent); Tarnished plant bug (Lygus pratensis L.) punctures young squares; Rapid plant bug (Adelphocoris rapidus Say); Thrips; cotton root Aphis (Aphis maidiradicis Forbes) found on the taproot of cotton.



Diseases:

Fusarium Wilt is caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. vasinfectum (Ath.) Snyder and Hansen (F. vasinfectum Atk.). It attacks the older plants and can be controlled by using resistant varieties and burning of diseased plants.

Verticillium Wilt caused by Vertcillium albo-atrum Reinke and Berth is not so prevalent or harmful. No control measures are known. Root knot of cotton is caused by a nematode Heterodera radcicola Greef and Muell which enters the roots. Various chemicals, such as sulphur, formaldeyde, carbon bisulphide, calcium carbide, potassium sulphocarbonate and ammonium sulphate, have been applied to the soil in the effort to eradicate nematodes. Chemical fertilizers that will stimulate plants to increased growth and vigor may be helpful as control measures. Cotton Anthrachose is another disease caused by Colletotrichum gossypii South also called pink boll rot is widespread in most of the cotton rowing regions of the world. The annual loss due to this disease amounts to million of dollars. Planting only seed from disease free fields, the use of crop rotations and fall plowing have proved to be effective in controlling this disease. Other methods are sterilizing seed by the use of conc. H2SO4, HgCl2, soaking seed in hot water for a few minutes, spraying Bordeaux mixture.

Other diseases are Texas Root Rot cased by Phymatotrichum omnivarum (Shear) Duggar; Diplodia boll Rot by Dipodia gossypina Cooke; Smut on leaves by Doassansia gossypii Lag; Red leaf blight due to poor soil; True rust caused by Kuehnuela gossypii (Lag), Crown gall by Pseudomonas tumeficiens; Crazy top by Acromania causes abnormal branching and fruiting of the plants; Cluster cup rust by Accidium gossypii; Areolate mildew on leaves by Septocylindrium areoles (Atk) (1).

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
fiber

Cotton fiber is used in making articles of clothings which include shirtings, outerwear, underwear, gloves, hosiery and handkerchiefs. Household articles include bed sheets and covers, pillow cases, towels, table cloths, mosquito netting, flannel, blankets, tapestry fabric etc. Among the industrial articles, we have a still large umber of items such as bags, belli, belting, industrial thread, awnings, tents, tarpaulins, industrial hoses, felts, insulation, cellulose etc. The linter is used for sliffing cushions, pillows, mattresses etc.

PULP

Stalks of cotton plant yields tough fiber used in making rough paper. The hill of cotton seed also used in making paper and fiber board.

FEED

Leaves are sued as fodder. Oil seed cake is used as stock feed and hull is used as low grade roughage.

MEDICINAL

The bark of the stem and of the roots possess medicinal properties as a substitute for Ergot.

OIL

The Kernel or meat yields oil which is used in making cooking oil, salad oil. It also yields low or inferior oil for soap making. The refined oil is also largely converted by deodorizing and hydrogenation into solid vegetable fats and finds use in this form in the preparation of margarine.

OTHERS

Oil cake is also used as fertilizer. Stalks yield pyroligneous acid .

Parts Used:

Root bark, stalks, leaves, lint, cotton seed - hull, kernel or meat


Harvesting Practices:
Harvesting begins about six months after sowing. In many parts of the world, cotton is hand picked as cheap labor is easily obtainable. Hand picking is continued over a period of two months or more because all the fruits do not ripen at the same time. Cotton is picked as soon as the boll opens. If left in the field for a longer period it may fall out or be damaged by rain. It is desirable to pick dry cotton free from trash. In general hand picking produces considerably cleaner cotton compared to mechanical harvesting.

In the Western sub humid parts of the cotton belt where labour is very scarce, mechanical harvesters, all working on the principle of suction, have been designed to pick cotton. For successful harvesting, it is necessary to defoliate the plants by spraying chemicals such as calcium cyanide. It also faces all the capsules to ripen simultaneously .



Produce and Products:


Chemistry:
A raw cotton fiber on average fives the following analysis, 94% of cellulose; 1.3% protein; 0.9% pectic substances; 0.3% sugar; and traces of pigments and mineral matter. A waxy coating impregnating the primary wall plays a vital role in the spinning and finishing processes.

The oil content of cotton seeds in India varies a great deal and 75% of them vary between 17.5 and 22.5 percent of oil. Seeds coming in a lower class may contain much lower percentage going down to 15 percent and below. The chemical and physical contents of cotton seed oil are as follows : Specific gravity 0.92; refractive index 1.47; saponification number 191 to 196; iodine number 105 to 115; Reichert Miessl value 0.7 to 0.9.

The Indian decorticated cake will contain about 38% of albumin oils, 24% of carbohydrates, and 13% of oil

 

Processing Techniques:

Processing of fiber - After harvest, before raw cotton is spun to yarn and voven into cloth it passes through processes such as ginning, boiling, grading, marketing, opening, picking, carding, combing and drawing.

After the removal of dirt, boll and leaf fragments, and other foreign matter, the raw cotton is conveyed to the hopper of a gin of either roller or raw type. The fiber as it comes from the gin is pressed hydraulically into bales of 500 pounds which are partially wrapped in jute or hessian covers (baggings), bound with iron bands called ties and are marketed in his form. Baled cotton is at first broken an then the fibers are passed through a 'scutcher' where they are beaten, shaken and rolled to remove all the foreign matter and the strands are separated and delivered in a uniform layer. At the last picking machine, the cotton is condensed into a sheet form called 'lap'. The remaining lumps of fibers are further separated into individual fibers on the carding machines which help to place the fibers parallel and also facilitates the removal of immature fibers and impurities such as trash and vegetable matter. Combining is a process where short fibers are removed, while drawing consists of straightening and aligning the fibers. The flucy lap is condensed through a funnel into a soft untwisted rope or 'sliver'. Slivers of the prepared fibers are then twisted (spun) into yarn and thread.

Mercerisation of cotton fiber - In mercerized cotton, named after John Mercer, the cotton yarn or cloth is treated under tension with sodium hydroxide. Mercerisation gives cotton a high, luster, makes it easier to dye, and products brighter and fuller shades.

Water Repellent Cotton - In water repellent cotton the individual fibers of cotton are rendered hydrophobic by treating with chemicals such as sodium ammonium stearate. After, drying, they are passed through a second solution of aluminium acetate. The metallic soap like deposition on the fabric gives excellent water repellency.

Absorbent cotton - Absorbent cotton is that from which the oily or waxy coating has been removed to enable it to absorb more water a property which is valuable in the manufacture of underclothing, towels, napkins and bandage materials.

By suitable chemical treatments, we can also manufacture flame-proofed cotton and resin impregnated fabric, the latter being crease-resistant.


Other uses:

The hulls of cotton seed is burnt as fuel. Water repellent cotton finds extensive use in awning, tents and other protective coverings.

Cotton acted upon by strong nitric acid turns into nitrocellulose or gin cotton and is used as an explosive. Cotton is also acted upon by salts of aluminium and of tin, and after such treatment is able to absorb and firmly retain many kinds of dyes, which is a property made use of in the employment of alum and chloride of tin as mordants. Considerable inferior cotton and the fuzy called 'linters' is turned to commercial use as raw material in the manufacture of artificial silk.

Refined cotton seed oil is used for packing of fish and cured meat. In USA, cotton seed oil is used in the manufacture of larel substitute. Cotton seed flour, derived from specially processed cake, is a valuable supplement for cereal flour. Hulls are also an important source of furural. Pharmaceutically, cotton seed oil is used as an emollient. The refined cotton seed oil is used as a solvent for the preparation of steroid hormone infections.

Miscellaneous Information

 A transgenic cotton called Bt. Cotton which contains the gene for resistance against insect has been permitted to Indian farmers for growing in fields. It contains a gene for insecticidal protein found in Bacterium Bacillus turngensis. It has resistance against the cotton boll worm and also shows higher yield.

A report of the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, USA describes the development of transgenic cotton, using bacterial polyester genes and fire specific promoters, producing novel cotton fibers with a normal texture but with thermal properties of a winter-weight fabric.

Cotton grown without the synthetic fertilizers, fungicides, growth regulators and defoliants and duly certified by a recognized certifying organisation is designated as organic cotton. Also referred to as green-cotton, eco-cotton, environment friendly cotton or natural cotton. Cotton is grown over 80 countries in about 35 million hectares of which India has the largest area of nine million ha. The present day conventional cotton production and processing technology require excessive use of agrochemicals particularly pesticides, besides chemical for bleaching and dyeing which cause serious environmental pollution and health hazards. Organic cotton, thus can minimize the use of hazardous pesticides in cotton production. There has been a preference for eco-friendly cotton in recent years in Europe, the US, Australia, New Zealand and other developed countries.
