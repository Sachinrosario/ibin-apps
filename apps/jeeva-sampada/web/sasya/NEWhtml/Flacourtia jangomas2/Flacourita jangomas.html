HABIT & HABITAT
A large deciduous shrubs or small spreading tree up to 9 m tall, It is found in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura, Orissa, Andra Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A large deciduous shrubs or small spreading tree up to 9 m tall, often armed low down with stout sharp decompound spines on the trunk, bark smooth, pale broen, blaze pale yellow freckled with yellowish brown, darkening on exposure.Leaves simple, ovate to ovate-lanceolate, oblong, acuminate at base, crenate-serrate at margins, glabrous, dark green above and shining surfaces. Flowers deciduous, greenish white, few in racemes or corymbose fascicles. Sepals 4-5 subequal, minutely puberulous out side, densely within. Male flowers: stamns many, filaments glabrous. Female flowers: ovary sub-globose, styles 4-6, connate into a column, stigma bilobed, recurved. Berries ovoid, subglobose, green, purple on ripening.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence: Glabrous racemes; Flower:  Small, iregular; Calyx: Sepals 4-5, small, imbricate; Corolla: Petals  ;Androecium: Stamens many, anthers versatile; Gynoecium:  Ovary on glandular disk, flask shaped, neck contracted, styles 2, stigma notched, ovules in pairs on placenta; Fruit: the size of plum, purple ; Seed: Obovoid.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Barks and leaves are astringent, acrid, sour, refrigerant, stomachic and diaphoretic. They are useful in vitiated conditions of “Pitta”, ulemorrhagia, odontalgia, diarrhoea, haemorrhoids, stomatitis and debility of the extremities (Unani). Fruits are sweet, sour, astringent, acrid, refrigerant, digestive, stomachic, alexipharmic, anti-inflammatory and depurative. It is also useful in hyperdipsia, rheumatism, nausea, dyspepsis, flatulence, colic, inflammations, skin diseases, diabetes, jaundice and tumours (Ayurveda).


Chemistry:  

A novel limonoid - jangomolide (fig. 1) isolated from stem and bark along with ostruthin and limonin and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).
The triterpenes, mortenone and katonic acid were isolated from the petroleum ether extracts of Flacourtia jangomas. (Mukherjee et al, 1999). C.A. 1998, 128, 99806p
