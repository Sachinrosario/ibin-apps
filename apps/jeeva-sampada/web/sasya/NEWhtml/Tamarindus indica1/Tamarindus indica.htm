Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A large tree. Occurs in Tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical), dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical), sub-tropical regions. Slow growing. Light demanding, frost sensitive, drought resistant, tolerably wind-firm. Can grow on variety of soils, thrives best on deep alluvial soils in warm climate. Tolerates drought but not the frost.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large tree may go as high as 15-18 m. Large tree of even 10 m circumference. Bark brownish grey and somewhat shaggy. Branches spreading, glabrous. Leaves bipinnate, 2-5 in. long; rachis slender, channelled; stipules linear, caducous. Leaflets subsessile, 10-20 pairs, tolerably closely set on the rachis, oblong, obtuse, glabrous, reticulately veined, slightly leathery.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in lax few-flowered racemes at the ends of the branchlets. Pedicels slender, articulated below the calyx, glabrous; bracts concave, enclosing the buds, caducous. Bracteoles small. Calyx tube narrowly turbinate, segments subequal, oblong, somewhat oblique, obtuse or subacute, 4 sepals, cream coloured, ovate. Petals 3 (an upper and 2 lateral), subequal, obovate-oblong, yellowish with pink stripes. Stamens 3 fertile, connate nearly half their length; filaments pubescent at the base, anthers oblong. Ovary stalked, ovules 8-12 or more; style pubescent, equaling the stamens. Pods 10-15 cm long, cinnamon brown, plump and slightly curved, subcompressed, scurfy. Seeds 3-12, obovate-oblong, truncate at the ends, compressed, with a hallow oblong pit on each flat faces, smooth, brown.

Phenology:Flowers during May - June. Fruiting during September - October.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Tree is easily propagated from seeds. It can be easily reproduced from cuttings and by budding. Propagation by means of gootee has been suggested to ensure replication of desirable parental characters.

Seed is sown in March-April. Seeds boiled in water for 5 minute give better germination. Seeds may be sown either in polybags or in situ.

Planting /transplanting is done in rainy season. Trees do better under alluvial soil and drought resistant. Fruits do not ripen in colder climate. Spacing is 8X8 m.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood very good fuel and for making charcoal. Preferred for making gunpowder charcoal. Calorific value : sapwood 4,909 cal., 8,837 Btu; heartwood 4,969 cal., 8,945 Btu.

Wood: Wood is hard, difficult to work and mostly used for agricultural implements, tool handles, wheels, mallets, ricepounders and oil-mills and for turnery. Suitable for printing blocks and tent-pegs. It yields decorative planks for paneling and furniture. In South India, used for constructional work and has been recommended as a substitute for teak and sal for beams, rafters, purlins and trusses. Yields useful timber.

Other uses:

Tender leaves, flowers and the young seedlings eaten as vegetable. Leaves make good fodder. Seeds are also roasted and eaten.

Leaves and seeds medicinally useful.

Leaves eaten by goats and cattle. They may also be tried in green manuring of soil.

Tree has been recorded as a host of the Lac insect and of a silkworm of genus Anaphe.

Flowers considered to be a good source of honey.

Powder of the kernel is used as sizing material in the textile and jute industry and as cattle feed.

Acidic pulp of fruit is ingredient of culinary preparations. Seeds used in jam, jelly and confectionery industries.

Ideal avenue tree, because of its shade, ornament and longevity, ornamental, shade, along roads in parks and gardens.

Seeds - Pulp around the seed is sharp tasting and used in drinks. It is an ingredient of sauce. Overripe fruits are used to clean brass and copper.
Root - Walking sticks are made from root.

Miscellaneous Information

Plant shows nyctitropic movements.
Fruit is dispersed by ruminants. It is rich in vitamin C, protien, tartaric acid, mallic acid and citric acid.
It is cultivated as an ornamental, also for shade.
The plants have been micropropagated.
Callus and embryogenesis has been obtained in vitro anther culture.
