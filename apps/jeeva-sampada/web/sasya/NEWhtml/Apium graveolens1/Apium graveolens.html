 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, biennial or perennial, erect, branched herb, 50-150 cm tall from a tap root. It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh.

Conservation/ Threat Status : Plant grows wild as well as commercially cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves oblong to obovate, 7-18 x 3.5-8 cm, simply pinnate leaflets trilobed, ovate to suborbicular 2-5 cm in diam.; petioles 3-25 cm long; cauline leaves mostly 3-parted or trilobed, sessile. Umbels opposite leaves, peduncles short to abortive. Involucre and involucel lacking. Rays 3-15, slender, 1-4 cm long, spreading-ascending. Umbellets 5-10 flowered, the matured pedicels 1-6 mm long. Flowers greenish-white; calyx teeth minute; stylopodium low conical, style short, ascending. Fruits ovoid to orbicular, 1-2 mm in diam., mericarps subpentagonal in transection, ribs prominently obtuse. Carpophore shortly 2-fid.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Compound umbel ; Flower: Flowers small, white ; Calyx: Calyx teeth obsolete;: ;Gynoecium: Carpels semiterete, subpentagonal, primary ridges, distinct, filiform; Fruit: Schizocarp with two mericarps, suborbiculate to ellipsoid, aromatic and slightly bitter; Flowering & Fruiting: September-January. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil Type: Plant flourishes well in sandy loam to medium loam type well drained fertile soil.

Irrigation: Plant is moisture loving , 4-5 irrigations are required when it is grown as winter crop and irrigation is less if it is grown as temperate crop if there are well distributed rainfall during summer and springs.

Nutrient requirements: It responds well to the application of nitrogenous fertilizers.

Harvesting: Harvesting after 185-190 days after transplanting has been found to give better results as up to this time seeds becomes completely mature.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
In  Ayurveda system of medicine the seeds are used as stomachic, aphrodisiac and tonic. It is also used for the improving appetite and astringent to bowels; cure “Kapha” and “Vata”, used for heart diseases.  It is also useful in ophthalamia, bronchitis, vomiting, hiccough, rectal troubles, abdominal pains, toothache and tumours. In Unani system of medicine the seeds are used as laxative, carminative, appetite and anthelmintic. It is also used in ophthalmia, scabies, asthma and scorpion strings. The seeds are also used against heart diseases, amenorrhea, urinary discharges, fever with cough, rheumatism, chest pain and inflammation.

b-carotene, vitamin A palmitate, vitamin K1 and a-tocopherol identified in seed extract. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

A new dihydrofurocoumarin – (-)2,3-dihydro-2-(1-hydroxy-1-hydroxy-methylethyl)7H-furo[3,2g][1]-benzopyran-7-one (fig. 5) – isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

b-selinene, limonene, butyl-4,5-dihydrophthalide, caryophyllene, tritylphthalide and 1-phenylpentane identified in seed essential oilo by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)h.

Isolation of 3-n-butylphthalide and 3-n-butyl-4,5-dihydrophthalide from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Choline, ascorbate from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Seselin, bergapten, rutaretin, celereoin, celeroside, apiumoside, vallein and nodakenin from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Octane-4,5-dione, 2-isopropyloxy-ethane, sabinyl acetate and 1,4-butanediol detected in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Mixture of essential oils of this plant and Cuminum cyminum in 1:1 ratio showed potent fungistatic and insect repellant activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Determination of triglycerides (72.3), free fatty acid (5.5), 1-monoglycerides (2.5), 2-monoglyserides (4.0), 1:3-diglycerides (3.5), 1:2-diglycerides (3.0),sterols ester (1.0), phosophatidylethanolamine (1.0), lysophosphatdulethanolamine (1.0), phosphatidylinositol (10%) and phosphatidylcholine (tr) in oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Limonene (72.16) and b-selinene (12.17%) determined in seed oil by GC-MS and GC/FTIR. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Fresh essential oil of ripe fruits found to contain more that 90% limonene, sedanolide, b-selinene, pentylcyclohexadiene and mycrene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Sterol composition in celery (Apium graveolens L.) was studied by gas liquid chromatography. Sterol content is 0.2-0.7% in glyceride oils, sitosterol – 33.0 – 58.4% and stigmasterol – 26.6 – 47.5% have been identified as major components in all of these glyceride oils. Minor components are cholesterol, brassicasterol, campesterol D7 – campesterol, D5-avenasterol, D7 – stigmasterol and D7 – avenasterol. (Zlatanov and Ivanov, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 174019g.

A new brassinosteroid was detected in seeds of Apium graveolens and it was shown to be 2-deoxy brassinolide by gas chromatography – mass spectral analysis. (Schmidt et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 310387j.

Using enantioselective multidimensional gas chromatography (enantio – MDGC) system, the genuine occurance of 3-butylphthalide enantiomers and 3-butylhexahydrophthalide stereoisomers in celery – celeriac – celery seeds and fennel extracts. (Bartschat et al, 1997). C.A. 1999, 131, 213422z.

Using enantioselective multidimensional gas chromatography with heptakis {2,3-di-O-acetyl-6-O-(tert-butyldimethylsilyl)}-b-cyclodextrin as the chiral stationary phase, the simultaneous analysis of the 3-butylphthalide anantiomers and all eight 3-butylhexahydrophthalide stereoisomers was achieved. Enantiomeric distributions ofcompounds in celery, celeriac, celery seed, and fennel extracts were elucidated. (Bartschat et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9804-2292.

Antioxidant property was determined in terms of the protective activity against rat liver microsome lipid peroxidation. Induced by Celery and malondialdehyde release. Celery juice showed activity (50%). (Gazzani et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 329940e.

Isolation and purification of hexane extract of Apium graveolens L. seeds led to the characterization of three compounds: b-serlinene (I), 3-n-butyl-4,5 dihydrophthalide (II) and 5-allyl-2-methoxyphenol (III). Compounds I – III demonstrated 100% mortality on fourth – instar Aedes aegyptii larvae at 50, 25 and 200 mg/ml respectively in 24 hrs. Also, II inhibited the growth of Candida albicans and Candida kruseii at 100 mg/ml. It inhibited both topioisomerase – I and II enzyme activities at 100 mg/ml. Compound – II displayed 100% mortality at 12.5 and 50 mg/ml respectively, when tested on nematodes. The triglyceride, 1,3-di[(cis)-9-Octadecanoyl]-2-(cis, cis)-9,12-octadecadienoyl glycerol (IV) and III were isolated for the first time from  Apium graveolens seed although IV was not biologically active. (Momin et al, 2000). C.A 2000, 133, 263852b.


Pharmacology:
Essential oil found to possess tranquillising as well as anticonvulsant activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Seeds had CNS depressant effect whereas alkaloidal fraction from seeds showed tranquillising activity in mice and rats. It also protected mice against maximal eletroshock seizures but was ineffective against metrazol and strychnine convulsions. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Both dialysed and non-dialysed solutions of aqueous extract on i.v. administration caused abrupt decrease in blood pressure of rabbits and significant increase in force of contraction of isolated heart of guinea pigs with no marked change in rate of contraction. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

3-n-Butylphthalide and 3-n-butyl-4,5-dihydrophthalide exhibited anticonvulsant activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

 The novel phthalide derivative viz., NG-072, isolated from the extracts of Apium graveolens has been reported to be useful in the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease. (Masashi et al, 1992). MAPA 1996, 9606, 3865.

Antihyperlipidemic property of aqueous celery extract was observed in wistar rats. The serum total cholesterol TC, low density lipoprotein, cholesterol and triglyceride concentrations showed a significant reduction. 3-n-butylphthalide (BuPh) a unique compound in the celery having lipid lowering activity was not present in the aqueous extract. The above activity was due to some other active principals present in aqueous celery extract. (Tsi et al, 1995). C.A., 1996, 124, 341754z.

Hepatic triacylglycerol lipase activity was found to be significantly lower in the celery – treated rats while the reverse was observed for the hepatic microsomal P450 content. (Tsi et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9601, 0222.

The antihepatotoxic effect of methanolic extracts of the seeds of Apium graveolens studied on rat liver damage induced by a single dose of paracetamol (3 g/kg p.o.) or thioacetamide (100 mg/kg, s.c.) by monitoring several liver functions tests, viz., serumtransminases (SGOT and SGPT), alkaline phosphates, sorbitol dehydrogenase, glutamate dehydrogenase and bilirubin in serum. Significant hepatoprotective activity of the methanolic extract of the seeds of Apium graveolens. (Singh and Handa, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9604, 2184.

The extracts from celery seeds are used for flavouring the fragrance compositions for perfumery and as additive for cosmetics and pharmaceutical products. The extracts obtained by supercritical CO2 extraction method contained considerable amounts of b-selinene and sedanolide and the aroma was decidedly more interesting than that obtained by traditional methods. (Kakol et al, 1995).

Aquoeous extract of celery (Apium graveleons) was administered to genetically hypercholesterolemic mice exhibited marked increase in cholesterol synthesis in the liver and intestine. Triton induced hypercholesterolemia in mice by the mobilization of hepatic cholesterol into the plasma compartment, as well as increasing the hepatic cholesterol synthesis. After intraperitoneal administration for one week in the RICO rats, celery extract significantly reduced the serum TC level while the serum TG level remained unaffected. (Tsi and Tan, 1996/97). MAPA 1998, 9803-1623.

Intraperitoneal infusion of 3 – n- butylphthalide (BuPh), a constituent unique in celery (Apium graveolens) at doses of 2.0 and 4.0 mg/day produced a transient hypotensive effect while a dose of 0.5 mg/day showed a significant hypotensive effect only on day 12. (Tsi et al, 1997) MAPA, 1998, 9802-0906.
