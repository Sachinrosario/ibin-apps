Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Cannabis sativa is a native of central Asia and is of very ancient cultivation in Asia and Europe. It is said have reached China more than 4,500 years ago. Its spread to the other parts of world in the past coloumbian times. It is now known in most tropical and temperate countries (1). It is practically naturalised in the Sub-Himalayan tract in India and is abundantly met in waste lands from punjab eastwards to Bengal and Bihar and extending southwards to Deccan. In India, the cultivation of the hemp plant is permitted in the Districts of Almora, Garhwal and Nanital in U.P. It is also cultivated to a small extent in Kashmir, Kerala and Nepal (2). It thrives on rich, fertile, neutral to slightly alkaline, well-drained silt or clay loams with moisture retentive subsoils and does not grow well on acid, sandy soils. Hemp for fiber requires a mild temperate climate with at least 67 cm annual rainfall, with abundant rain while seeds are germinating and until young plants become established. It can tolerate annual precipitation of 3 to 40 dm annual temperature of 6 to 27°C and pH of 4.5 to 8.2

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Annual herb, usually erect, stems variable, robust up to 5 m tall, with resinous pubescence, stems angular, sometimes hollow, degree of branching depending on the condition of cultivation, especially above the first pairs of true leaves, basal leaves opposite, the upper leaves spirally arranged, leaves alternate, stipulate, stipules small, pointed, persistent, long petiolate (4-6 cm long), palmate, with 3-11, rarely single, lanceolate, serrate, acuminate leaflets up to 10 cm long, 1.5 cm broad, paler green beneath (1). All parts of the plant emit a characterise odour and are covered by a hairy pubescence, vicid to the touch.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers monoecious or dioecious, male flowers in axillary and terminal panicles with few leaves, calyx with 5 deeply divided, widely spreading lobes, yellow, 5 mm long, petals absent, stamens 5 with slender filaments and long pendent anthers dehiscing by an apical pore. The female flowers germinate in the axils and terminally, with one 1-ovulate ovary (1). Male plants die soon after anthesis. Female inflorescences axillary and terminal, leafy, flowers in pairs, each flower enveloped by membraneous spathe-like, dark-green secondary bract with thick glandular hairs, calyx entire, thin, closely enveloping ovary, ovary sessile with solitary, pendulous ovule, styles 2-partite, 5 mm long, curving downwards and outwards. Female plants continue to live for 20-40 days after pollination until seeds are ripe.

Breeding System :
Hemp is cross-pollinated. The male plants will be taller and have about five green or yellow sepals, which will split open to fertilize the female plant with pollen. The female plant is shorter and has a small pistillate flower, which really doesn't look like a flower at all but rather a small bunch of leaves in a cluster.

Phenology :
Flowering takesplace 4-5 months after sowing.

Pollinators:

The flowers are wind pollinated.

Seed Biology :
Fruit a brown, shining achene, variously marked or plain, tightly embracing the seed with its fleshy endosperm and curved embryo. Seeds are roundish ovate in shape, 3-5 mm long and 3 mm in diameter, brown to grey in colour. Seeds weigh 1.5-2.5 gm/100 seeds (3).

Seedling Germination :
The seeds will germinate at low temperatures, but not below 10 C. Good seeds should give 90 per cent germination and if properly stored will remain viable for upto 2 years.

Cultivation:

Propagation mainly by seed. Before sowing, the land is well prepared and manured. Land is ploughed several times to a depth of about 20-23 cm and repeatedly harrowed. Seed sown by machine in rows from 12 cm upwards, placing the seed at depth of 3.5 cm at rate of 40-60 kg/ha. In many countries seed are sown by broadcast method. When grown for seed (oil), seed sown by drills; then such plants sometimes reach height of 5.3 m with thick stems up to 5 cm in diameter, much-branched. For fiber, stems up to 2 m tall and 0.5 cm in diameter are best, larger stems tend to get woody and have lower fiber content. Besides, they are more difficult to handle during harvesting, retting and scutching. Plants require little attention during cultivation, except for weeding during early stages of growth. Hemp grows rapidly and soon crowds out weeds. After plants are 20 cm tall, weeding is abandoned. Hemp tends to exhaust the soil of nutrients. On medium fertile soils a dressing of farm manure or a green-manure crop should be added and turned under. Chalk, potash, or gypsum may be applied to the soil to add the needed nutrition. Sodium nitrate and ammonium along with potassium sulfate have a beneficial effect on the fiber crop. Fiber-producing plants should always have plenty of proper nutrients, especially nitrogen, which is the most important element needed. Irrigation is seldom practiced. Experimentally, drug plants have been propagated from cuttings but such plants do not come true as to drug content of parent (1).

In India, plants remaining in the field after harvesting for fiber are allowed to set seed. They are cut after the fruits are ripened and dried and threshed for seed collection. Grown solely for seeds, an average crop yields 1.3 to 1.6 MT/ha seed. In India no systematic attempt has been made either to collect the seeds from wild plants or to raise the crop for seed purposes.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

1. Gram caterpillar: Heliocoverpa armigera Hubner
Symptoms: Polyphagous, moths stout, light brown, caterpillars yellowish, make holes in pods and feed on the seeds within.

Control measures: Dust 4% Carbaryl or spray 0.05% Endosulfan or 0.04% Manocrotophos

2. Lucerne caterpillar: Spodoptera exigua Hubner

Symptoms: Dark brown moths lay eggs on the lower portion of the young plants. Caterpillars feed on leaves.

Control measures: Collect and destroy eggs and larvae, dust 10% BHC or spray 0.05% Dichlorvos DDVP or Endosulfan.

3. Tobacco caterpillar: Spodoptera litura Fabricius

Symptoms: Polyphagous, breeds throught the year, ubiquitous, moths greyish brown with paterned forewings, tiny dark caterpillars feed gregariously, scraping the leaves, later the caterpillars segregate and assume cutworm habits, feeding voraciously on leaves in nurseries and fields.

Control measures: Dust 4% Carbaryl or 10% BHC (aviod spraying insecticide that are highly toxic)

4. Leaf caterpillar: Amyna octo Guenee

Symptoms: A minor pest, larvae are green and semilooper with white subdorsal stripes, they feed on leaves, the adults are brown moths.

Control measures: Dust 4% Carbaryl or spray 0.05% Endosulfan or 0.04% Manocrotophos

5. Black thrip: Heliothips indica Bagnall

Symptoms: The nymphs and adults feed on the leaves which become yellow and dry up

Control measures: Spray 0.05% Malathion or 40% Nicotine sulphate

6. White ants: Microtermes obesi Holmgren

Symptoms: White ants prefer sandy soils, feed on the roots, the plants dry up and dry.

Control measures: Mix 5% Aldrin or Chlodane dust with soil before planting.

7. Mite: Tetranychus telarius Linn

Symptoms: Mites live in webbed colonies on the under surface of the leaves, causing whitish and brownish patches, the affected leaves dry up (4).

Economic Potential and Uses

Hemp seed oil finds use in the paints, fuel for lighting, varnish industries as a substitute for linseed oil and in the manufacture of soft soaps and printing ink. It is also used for edible purposes in Asia. It is said to be an excellent medium for grinding colours, Hemp oil is a brand-new product for the beauty care mass-market. Its high content of essential fatty acids help the action of the hydrolipid coat. Hemp oil can be used for body, facial creams and lotions, sunscreen, soaps. The hemp oil will be more suitable for natural cosmetics. It can be used as it is for massage. The penetration is extraordinary fast and efficient. It is best consumed as a table oil, on salads or as a butter/margarine substitute for dipping bread, similar in use to olive oil.

Hemp seed cake contains about 30% protien and can be used as an animal feed. It is belived that hemp seed meal may contain nacrotic substances which may have injurious effect on livestock when fed in large quantities. The meal has good manurial properties (2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9).

Parts used

Uses

Fibre is used in the manufacture of fine cordage, twine, sail cloth, tarpaulins and carpet yarns. Loose fibre is used for caulking boats, pumps, engines and other machinery. Hemp stems can be used in the manufacture of coarse paper. Leaves are added to soups in southeast Asia.

Three types of narcotics are produced:

Hashish/Bhang, the dried leaves and flowers of male and female shoots

Ganja, dried unfertilized inflorescence of special female plants and

Charas, the crude resin, which is probably the strongest.

Modern medicine uses cannabis in glaucoma and alleviating the pains of cancer and chemotherapy. More resin is produced in tropical than in temperate climates. Lewis lung adenocarcinonoma growth has been retarded by oral administration of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol, delta-8-tetrahydrocannabinol and cannabinol. The delta-9 also inhibits the replication of Herpes simplex virus (2, 3).

Harvesting Practices
Hemp is ready for harvest four to five months after planting, rarely earlier for some varieties. Harvesting depends on the climatic conditions, the variety of hemp grown and whether the crop is being grown for hemp or seed. In temperate areas, hemp is usually harvested from mid July to mid August. Both male and female plants look alike until they flower; then the male plants turn yellow and die, whereas the female plants remain dark green for another month until the seed ripens. Male plants are ready to harvest for fiber when the leaves change from dark green to light brown. The best yield of fiber (and only male plants are used) is then obtained. Hemp is harvested when the staminate flowers are beginning to open and shed their pollen. Seed is harvested from the female plants when most of it falls off when the plant is shaken. Best time of day to harvest seed is in early morning when fruits are turgid and conditions damp. As fruits dry out by mid-day, seed loss increases due to shattering. Usually stems are cut and the seeds shaken out over canvas sheets or beaten with sticks to extract the seeds. For fiber, hemp plants are cut 2-3 cm above the ground by hand with a hemp knife, similar to a long-handled sickle and spread on the ground to dry. In some areas, the entire plants are pulled up and laid out to dry. Hand cutting, one man can cut about one-fifth hectare per day. Sometimes specially designed harvesters with a tractor are able to harvest four hectares a day (3).

Produce and Products

Three major products are obtained from the plant viz., Fibre, Oil, and nacrotics (Bhang, Ganja and Charas). Apart from these fibre, oil and nacrotics have various industrial and medicinal properties.

Chemistry

Oil from the seeds contains oleic acid 15%, linoleic acid 70%, and linolenic and isolinolenic acids 15%. The seed cake contains 10.8% water, 10.2% fat, 30.8% protein, 40.6% N-free extract, and 7.7% ash (20.3% K2O; 0.8% Na2O; 23.6% CaO, 5.7% MgO, 1.0% Fe2O3, 36.5% P2O5, and a trace of Mn2O3). Trigonelline occurs in the seed (2, 3, 9).

Processing Techniques

At present extraction of oil from hemp seed is not being carried out on a large scale. That being processed, is sometimes relatively unhomogenous, mature seeds mixed with green ones. This is due to the unavailabilty of requisit amount of seeds. The presence of unripe seeds not only increases seed crop moisture content, it also lowers oil yield and modifies its taste. After harvest, hemp seed are dried to reduce its moisture content to 10% or less, so as to prevent sprouting during storage. Batches of this material are then fed into a hydraulic screw press and a pressure of 500 bars is progressively applied, resulting in only a minor elevation in temperature. Best quality oil is obtained from the first fractions recovered. Approximately 35% of the available oil remains in the seed cake (Jones 1995). The pressing process is sometimes repeated with this crushed residue to obtain a small additional amount of oil, although quality is decreased.
This "cold pressing" does not allow an extraction yield equal to that of techniques employing solvents or high temperatures, but it has the advantage of minimizing degradative changes in the oil. A small amount of oil is also unrecovered during the subsequent filtration process. It may be refined by treatment with 0.25-5.0% H2S04 (on weight of oil), neutralization with a base, remaval of the excess soap and bleaching with an odsorbent. However further refining procedures should be avoided in order to preserve the native qualities of this product. Bottling must occur quickly and filling under nitrogen into opaque bottles, then refrigerating, offers significant protection against oil degradation due to oxidation and the action of light, although freezing is necessary for long-term storage. Addition of anti-oxidants extends shelf life of the oil at room temperature (McEvoy et al. 1996) (6, 7, 12).

Other Uses
Plants have medicinal importance and are regarded as tonic, intoxicant, stomachic, antispasmodic, analgesic, narcotic, sedative and anodyne. Seeds and leaves are used to treat old cancer and scirrhous tumors. The seed, either as a paste or as an unguent, is said to be a folk remedy for tumors and cancerous ulcers. The decoction of the root is said to cure hard tumors and knots in the joints. The leaf, prepared in various manners, is said to alleviate cancerous sores, scirrhous tumors, cold tumors, and white tumors. The plant is also used for mammary tumors and corns. Europeans are said to use the drugs from Cannabis pipes in cancer cures (3).

Plant parts have a greater array of folk medicine uses: alcohol withdrawal, anthrax, asthma, blood poisoning, bronchitis, burns, catarrh, childbirth, convulsions, coughs, cystitis, delirium, depression, diarrhea, dysentery, dysmenorrhea, epilepsy, fever, gonorrhea, gout, inflammation, insomnia, jaundice, lockjaw, malaria, mania, mennorhagia, migraine, morphine withdrawal, neuralgia, palsy, rheumatism, scalds, snakebite, swellings, tetany, toothache, uteral prolapse, and whooping cough. Seeds ground and mixed with porridge given to weaning children.
