 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT :
It is a shrub, Found in the open forests of Koni.A slender herb, arising from a stout rootstock, found throughout India. The herb is widely cultivated in tropical Asia for the rhizomes, which are use in medicine. 
Availability :  
It is available in Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Rootstocks perennial, light brown; leaves oblong-lanccolate; corolla-segments whitish, lip yellowish white irfth deeply bifid mid-lobe; fruits globose, small, 17 mm.long; seeds many, very small, purple. The rhizomes are fleshy with scaly epidermis, golden-yellow inside, and possess a camphoraceous odour with a warm spicy taste.

Chemistry
The rhizomes on steam-distillation yield a pale yellow essential oil (yield, 0.5% from a sample from Thailand) with the following constants: d, 0.895; [<.]D, - 33.2°; nD, 1.489; ester val. after acetylation, 36.5 % by wt of total alcohols (calculated as C10H18O) in the oil; soluble in 4.6 vol. of 80% alcohol. The major constituent of the oil is the tertiary alcohol, terpinen-4-ol (c. 35%); other identified constituents are œ- and ß-pinenes, sabinene, myrcene, terpinene, limonene, þ-cymene and terpinolene. The oil may be used as a source of terpinen-4-ol, which is widely employed in perfumery for the preparation of artificial geranium, pepper, rose and other oils, and also in soap perfumes and flavour compositions (Casey et al., loc. cit.).
The occurrence of an active diastase in the rhizome has been reported. The enzyme resembles in its activity œ-amylase of Aspergillus oryzae at opt. pH 5. 6-6.0 and the opt. temp., 55° .
