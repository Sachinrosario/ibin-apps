Produce and Products: Preparation of beverage from sulphited mango pulp

Mango pulp forms an important basic material for the preparation of squash, juice, nectar, ready-to-serve (RTS) beverage etc. ripe fruits were washed, stored, trimmed to remove the undesirable portion and pulped in a pulping machine. The pulp is analyzed immediately for total soluble solids (TSS), acidity, pH and ascorbic acid4 content and 8-carotene. Organoleptic evaluation of the chilled RTS beverage was carried out by a trained panel of 10 judges using numerical scale, 4=excellent, 3=good, 2=fair, 1=poor for colour flavour and taste.

RTS beverage prepared contains 15 percent pulp, 130 Brix and 0.3 percent acidity is filled hot at 800 C into 200 ml bottles, crown corked, processed for 15 min at 80-850 C, cooled and stored at room temperature. “Totapuri’ mango withstands long distance, when transported in bulk in truck loads to Ludhiana from south India, while other varieties from Uttar Pradesh are transported after packaging in wooden boxes. Large incidence of fruit fly insect damage in “Totapuri’ necessities slicing, manually to remove the insect damaged portion prior to pulping. TSS contents ranged in ‘Chousa’, ‘Langra’ ‘Dusehri’ and ‘Lucknow local’ varieties from 19.5 to 20.0 in ‘Totapuri’ and ‘Desi’ from 16.5 to 17.0 and in ‘Fazli’ 14.3 percent. ‘Desi’ mango pulp recorded highest acidity (0.32 percent). This variety has minimum Brix-acid ratio (53.2) and ‘Dusehri’ the highest (162.5). Ascorbic acid content was maximum in ‘Desi’ mango (34mg/100g) and in others it ranged from 11 to 34 mg percent (Table 1). B-carotene was maximum in ‘Dusehri’ and minimum in ‘Langra’ (Table 1). The yield of stones was maximum in ‘Desi’ and minimum in ‘Langra’. Similar trend was found for the content of peel, also. Loss in handling during pulping was maximum in ‘Desi’ being 4.3 percent. ‘Langra’ variety was rated best on the basis of yield followed by ‘Fazli’, ‘Totapuri’, ‘Chousa’, ‘Dusehri’, ‘Lucknow local’ and ‘Desi’ (Table 2)

Mango fruit production constitutes about 70% of total fruit production in world. (Annual Report, Department of Horticulture, Shimla 1991). A few popular products from mango like pulp, juice, concentrate, jam, chutney, pickles and dried products are prepared commercially. Mango juice is rich in carbohydrates, minerals, vitamin-C, starch, pectins, carotenoids, but lacks in proteins, fat and some essential amino acids.

At present, though a few fortified products for infants and weaned children are available in the market, refreshing protein-rich beverages particularly from soy protein are not many. The mango pulp can be fortified with protein from soybean for development of new protein-enriched product. A combination of protein isolate with mango pulp could result in the preparation of more nutritious mango beverages.

Supplementing the apple juice with egg yolk for beverage preparation increased the protein and fat contents (Vyas and Joshi 1982). The addition of increased proportion of protein isolate of mango pulp increased the acidity, reducing sugars, ash, fibre and calorific values, while pH decreased. Although residual trypsin inhibitor activity was detected in the protein isolates, no such activity was recorded in the product. Elimination of trypsin inhibitor is essential for the digestibility of product.

The product was rated very good with respect to taste, flavour, colour, overall quality upto 9 months of storage, implying that the blending of protein isolate with mango pulp has resulted in improvement of nutritional quality. The newly developed protein-rich mango beverage has good acceptability, and hence it can be developed commercially on a large scale.

Effect of cooking procedure and variety on acceptability of unripe mango beverage

Saltish unripe mango drinks are very popular in India during summer. Kaushik developed a beverage base from unripe ‘Dushehari’ mangoes, which could be diluted before use. 

Unripe fruits of ‘Deshi’, ‘Dushehari’, ‘Safeda’ and ‘Chousa’ varieties, dropped by wind with slight physical injuries but otherwise sound, were procured from the Horticultural Research Centre of this University. They were washed well in warm water of about 500 C, cooked and pulped. Cooking procedure was standardized using ‘Dushehari’ mangoes only because of their early availability. Cooking procedures tried were (A) whole mangoes boiled for 25 min in twice their amount of water, peeled and destined, (B) mangoes peeled, sliced, destined and slices boiled for 15 min in 1.5 times water, (C) whole mangoes autoclaved for 20 min at 15 p.s.i., peeled and destined, (D) slices obtained as in procedure (B) were cooked in an autoclave for 15 min at 15 p.s.i. and (E) whole mangoes oven-baked at 2150 C for 25 min, peeled and destined. Ratio of mango or its slices to boiling water and/or cooking times were standardized by trial and error to ensure softening of the pulp without scorching. Pulp was blended in a blender. Since, recipe was not standardized at this stage, beverage was prepared using 20.0 percent pulp, 1.0 percent NaCl, 0.1 percent citric acid and 5.0 percent sugar. Pulp obtained by the above methods had a tendency to settle down due to inadequate size reduction in blender. Therefore, three methods of pulping pressure-cooked whole mangoes were tried – (i) manual separation of pulp and blending in a blender, (ii) passing whole fruits through a pulper, and (iii) passing the pulp from the pulper through a colloid mill. Pulp (1 part) was mixed well with water (5 parts), 25 ml of this suspension was centrifuged for 10 min at 3500 r.p.m. and the volume of supernatant was measured. Larger supernatant volume was taken as an index of larger pulp particles. All the four varieties were pulped separately by procedure ‘C’ and beverages were prepared using 16.7 percent pulp, 1.5 percent NaCl, 0.2 percent citric acid, 5.0 percent sugar and 1.0 percent of a commercial spice-mix.

Beverages were prepared from 4 commercial mango varieties of North India-‘Deshi’, ‘Dashehari’, ‘Safeda’ and ‘Chousa’. Pulp yield was lowest (35.0 per cent) for ‘Deshi’ mangoes due to their thick peel and large stones (Table 1). Pressure cooking reduced its TSS and acidity slightly. Yield of pulp from other varieties ranged between 64.4 and 68.4 percent which is in the range of 45-75 per cent reported by Roy and Singh. Sensory qualities of beverages form these four varieties were compared (Table 2). ‘Dashehari’ beverage got the highest sensory (4.0) and was rated significantly superior (Table 2). It was followed by ‘Deshi’ and ‘Safeda’, while ‘Chousa’ beverage was liked least. This trend was observed in all the quality attributes except for consistency. The panelists gave almost equal marks for consistency of all the 4 beverages. This was also reflected by the narrow range of their viscosity (11-14 cp).
