REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
      Inflorescence: Terminal panicles; Flower: Complete, regular ; Calyx: Small, flat, 5-toothed, deciduous ; Corolla: Petals 5, spreading, imbricate;Androecium: Stamens 10, inserted on the short disk, filament dilated in side, front villous, anhthers linear-oblong ; Gynoecium: Ovary oblong, 5-celled, at length 1-celled, style 0, stigma oblong fusiform ; Fruit: Large, 1—celled, globose ; Seed: Burried in pulp, oblong, compressed . 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : August - November. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=18.

Chemistry:  

Two unidentified compounds isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
Feronolide, mp. 115°, from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Feronone, mp. 195°, from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
Ursolic acid and a new flavanone glycoside - 7-O-methylporiol-4'-b-D-xylopyranosyl-D-glucopyranoside (fig. 1) - isolated from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Aurapten, bergapten, isopimpinellin, 6-methoxy-7-geranyloxycoumarin and marmesin isolated from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Seed and fruit contained oil (34) and protein (28%); oil composed of palmitic (15.2), oleic (43.4), linoleic (20.4) and linolenic acids (20.6%) besides traces of palmitoleic and stearic acids; b-sitosterol, b-amyrin, lupeol and stigmasterol from unsaponifiable matter of seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
Psoralen, bergapten, orientin, vitexin and saponarin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.
Methylchavicol and 1,4-dimethoxy-2-allylbenzene identified in essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Roots yielded geranyl umbelliferone, bergapten, osthol, isopimpinellin, xanthotoxin, marmesin and marmin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Umbelliferone, dictamnine, xanthotoxin, scoparone, isopimpinellin, isoimperatorin and marmin detected in pericarp by HPLC-GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Anisic acid isolated from leaf essential oil and its mass fragmentation determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
A new furanocoumarin - fernolin (fig. 2) isolated from roots together with aurapten, marmesin, bergapten and xanthotoxin; new compound characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Estimation of methylchavicol (27.2), trans-anethole (10.94), thymol (24.4) and p-cymen-7-ol (7.3%) in leaf oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.
Oil exhibited antimicrobial activity against Candida albicans and Cryptococcus neoforamns. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Estragole (76.0), trans-anethole (13.5) and cis-anethole (2.1%) determined in leaf oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Phytochemical studies of leaf of Feronia elephantum Correa resulted in the isolation of b-amyrin, lupeol, psoralen, bergapten, xanthotoxin, umbelliferone and marmesin in addition to C-flavonoid glycosides, saponarin, isovitexin, vitexin and orientin. Gas chromatography (GC) analysis of the unsaponifiable matter of the leaves revealed the presence of series of C16-32, cholesterol,campesterol, stigmasterol and b-sitosterol. Twelve fatty acids were identified in the saponifiable fraction and quant. Determined by GC. Palmitic, linoleic, oleic, linoleic and stearic acids were the major components (84%). The total tannin content in the different organs of the plant was established gravimetrically. (El-Fishawy, 1994). C.A. 1995, 123, 237628g.
The stem bark of Feronia limonia (Fam. Rutaceae) yielded (-)-(2S)-5,3'-dihydroxy-4'-methoxy-6",6"-dimethylchromeno-(7,8,2",3")-flavanone along with several known compounds including an alkaloid, five coumarins, a flavanone, a lignan, three sterols and a triterpene. The structures of these compounds were determined by spectroscopic methods, mainly 1D and 2D NMR. (Rahman and Gray, 2002)
the acetone extract of Feronia limonia dried leaves afforded a potent mosquito larvicide, identified as n-hexadecanoic acid and found to be effective against fourth instar larvae of Culex quinquefasciatus, Anopheles stephensi and Aedes aegypti, with LC50 of 129.24, 79.58 and 57.23 ppm, respectively.( Rahuman et al, 2000)

Pharmacology:  

Feronolide was a potent depressor of blood pressure and stimulant of gut. It had potent parasympathomimetic properties. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.
Ferenone showed depressant action on frog heart. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.
An antitumor pectic polysaccharide from Feronia limonia. (Saima et al, 2000)
The antimicrobial screening of compounds by a microdilution technique resulted in MICs in the range 25-100 microg/ml. Other biological activities of the known compounds are also discussed. (Rahman and Gray, 2002)

Ethnobotany: 

Medicine for asthma, bronchitis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Body pain (Root, Chaudhuri et al, 1975); Diarrhoea, digestive complaints, dysentery (Fruit, Bhalla et al, 1982); Itch (Bark, Saxena and Vyas, 1983); Phthisis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Tonic (Fruit, Bhalla et al, 1982); Edible (Fruit, Maji and Sikdar, 1982; Arora, 1981; Singh and Singh, 1981; Shah et al, 1981; Singh and Maheshwari, 1985); In fermentation of Caryota liquor (Stem, Chaudhuri et al, 1975); In jellies (Fruit, Shah, 1984)

Miscellaneous Information
Any Other

Plant extracts obtained from Feronia limonia (leaves) and some other plants were tested for their antimicrobial properties against certain bacterial species. Feronia leaf extract was ineffective on Bacillus pumilus and X. campestris, Vibrio cholerae was found quite sensitive to this extract. (Mehta et al, 1983)
