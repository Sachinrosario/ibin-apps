 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
An evergreen or sub-evergreen tree, found throughout the temperate Himalayas from Kashmir eastwards up to Bhutan and also in Assam and Manipur at an attitude of 1800-3600 m. It often ascends to the upper limit of tree growth.


Availability :  
Kharshu oak forests occur in regions of heavy snowfall and moderate rainfall.  The tree does not extend into drier parts of inner Himalayas.  It is gregarious and forms pure forests along the tops and upper slopes within its well marked zone.  It is associated with spruce (Picea smithiana), silver fir (Abies pindrow), etc.  It is found both on deep rich moist soil in sheltered localities and on poor rocky ground on the crests of ridges; in the latter areas it is usually stunted and gnarled.

 


Status : 
Common

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES


Tree up to 30 m tall with a clean bowl of up to 10-12 m long and ca. 2 m or more in girth. Bark dark-gray, rough, exfoliating in irregular woody scales, leaves elliptical or oblong, spinous, toothed or entire; male spikes crowded, female spikes short, acorn sub-globose in clusters of 3 or more, about 2.0-2.5 cm in diameter.

Seed Biology :

Seedling Germination :
Under natural conditions the plants multiply from seeds, particularly if abundant overhead light is available. Seedlings are often suppressed by Strobilanthes wallichii Nees  a weed which produces a dense matted growth. Can be raised artificially by sowing in contour lines or by dibbling. Light demander.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Wood, leaves, bark

Uses
Leaves contain 8% tannin and bark 7-11%, there are reports of some bark samples yielding up to 23.7%. Bark tannin yields protocatechuic acid and oak red.
