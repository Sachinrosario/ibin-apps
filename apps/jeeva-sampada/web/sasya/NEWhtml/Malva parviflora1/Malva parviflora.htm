HABIT & HABITAT                                                                           
A small spreading pubescent herb found in the North-West Himalaya at altitude 1,000 to 2,000 ft, also in Upper Bengal,Sind, and the Punjab and distributed over the greater part of India.
Habitat:    Canal banks, Cultivated fields, Dumping grounds, Moist grounds, Near habitations, open forest, River banks, Road sides, slopes and Water-logged areas.  

Altitude: 0-2000 
Biotic Zone: Deccan, Himalaya, Temperate regions and Western Himalayas.
Soil:  It prefers  rich soil,

Availability : 
 Country:   Afghanistan, Arabia, Asia, Baluchistan, Europe, India, Levant, Malayasia, Mediterranean, Nubia, Pakistan, Persia and  Subtropical countries.
Zone:    Central, East, North, North East, South and Westzone.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Annual or short-lived perennial herbs; branches decumbent to ascending, 0.2-0.5 (-1) m long, glabrate to pubescent. Leaf blades reniform-orbicular, usually 2-8 cm wide, shallowly 3-lobed, 5-lobed, or 7-lobed, margins undulate, crenate.. Leaves sub-orbicular, cordate: Flowers purplish or white; fruits round, flattened ; seeds black, glabrous.  Flowers solitary or few in dense cymes, greatly exceeded by the leaves, pedicels filiform, up to 1 (-2.5) cm long in fruit; involucral bracts filiform to linear, 1-4 mm long; calyx 3-5 mm long, accrescent in fruit, spreading, up to 15 mm in diameter, scarious, veiny; petals lavender blue, scarcely exceeding the calyx. Mericarps 10-11, up to 2.3 mm high and 3 mm wide, with reticulate-rugose walls and acute, slightly winged margins, indehiscent. Seeds ca. 1.8 mm long, glabrous.”

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruits , Leaves, Seeds, Roots.

Uses
  1. The plant is used as a pot-herb ; green fruits are also eaten. 
  2. In Mexico, the plant is cooked like spinach, which it resembles in taste, though more fibrous.
  3. It is a rich source of calcium, iron and carotene.
  4. Seeds are used as a demulcent in coughs and ulcers in the bladder.
  5. In Kanawar the Root is used by women to cleanse their hair; woolen cloth is also washed with it.
  6. Root is also employed as Risha khatmi.

Other Uses
  1. The plant is considered emollient and pectoral. 
  2. An Infusion of the leaves is taken as a nerve tonic in South Africa. 
  3. A decoction of the leaves is used as a taenicide and as a lotion for bruises ; 
  4. leaves are also used for poulticing wounds and swellings. The seeds arc demulcent and used in cough and ulcers in the bladder ; they yield 18% of a fatty oil. 
  5. In Kumaon, the root is said to he used for cleansing hair and washing woollen clothes 
  6. The plant is considered poisonous to livestock ; animals develop spasms if they arc worked or driven soon after feeding on the plant : the symptoms are stiff gait, repeated falling and struggling to rise and trembling or shivering, particularly in shoulders and hind quarters. Many cases of 'staggers' in sheep have been recorded in Australia : cattle and horses are also susceptible. Lambs are liable to staggers from the milk of the ewe. Rest and change of diet ensure recovery but no immunity can lie built up. 
  7. The plant also causes 'pink-white' in hen's eggs ; an unsaturated fatty acid, malvalic acid (C,,H^O,)- present in the fatty oils from the leaves and seeds of the plant is responsible for the pink-white disease. Malvalic acid possesses strong surface active properties : th epotassium salt of the acid enhances the haemolysis of sheep erythrocytcs 
  8. As a medicine.
