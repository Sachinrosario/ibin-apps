DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Large, woody climber; young branches hairy; roots tuberous with several strings of tubers connected by thin roots. Leaves 3-foliate, subcoriaceous, glabrascent above, silky hairy beneath, terminal one broadly ovate. Flowers blue, fading to bluish purple, often white, in axillary or terminal, lax raceme. Pods ellipsoid-oblong, 5-7.5 cm with bristly hairs, 3-6 seeds redish brown.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine roots are used as aphrodisiac, refrigerant, emollient, laxative, galactagogue, diuretic, rejuvenating, emetic, alterant, expectorant, febrifuge and cardiotonic. It is useful in vitiated conditions of Pitta and Vata, arthritis, burning sensation, constipation, agalactia, strangury, ematiation, cardiac debility, intermittent fevers, pharyngitis, leprosy, dyspepsia, tuberculosis, cough and spermatorrhoea.

Chemistry : 

b-sitosterol, stigmasterol, daidzein, puerarin and a new isoflavone, C-glycoside - 4',6''-diacetylpuerarin (fig. 1) from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
A new pterocarpan - tuberosin (fig. 2) isolated from roots and tubers and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
A new pterocarpanone - hydroxytuberosone (fig. 3) isolated from tubers and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Isolation of two pterocarpenes - anhydrotuberosin (fig. 4a) and 3-O-methylanhydrotuberosin (fig. 4b) and a coumestan - tuberostan (fig. 5) from tubers and their characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
A new isoflavone - puerarone (fig. 6) and a coumestan - puerarostan (fig. 7) isolated from tubers and their structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Pharmacology:

Butanol extract of Puraria tuberosa has been found to exhibit contraceptive activity. In toxicity studies butanolic extract appears to be safe in rats. (Shukla and Mathur, 1996/97). MAPA 9803-1607.
Pureria tuberosa was undertaken with other important medicinal plant on patients suffering from sexual dysinfection. (Chavhan and Gaikwad, 1998). MAPA 9803-1402.
