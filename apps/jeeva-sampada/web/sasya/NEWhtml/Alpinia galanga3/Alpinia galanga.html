HABIT & HABITAT
The Greatest Galangal is native to Indonesia but has now become naturalized in many parts of India.  It is frequently met with in the sub-Himalayan region of Bihar, West Bengal and Assam; lt is extensively cultivated all over India mostly in shady situation.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A herb, 1.8 – 2.4 m. in height, with tuberous aromatic rootstock, occuring throughout India and cultivated for its rhizomes; Leaves: oblong lanceolate, acute, glabrous, 30-60 cm. long, ligule rounded; Flowers: greenish white, streaked with red in dense-flowered, 30 cm. long panicles; Capsules orange or red, globose.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
 Rhizome: well marked insecticidal properties, also bitter acrid, thermogenic, aromatic, repulsive, carminative, disinfectant, aphrodisiac expectorant, bronchodialator, febrifuge, diuretic; used in medicines for rheumatism, stomach disorders, bronchial catarrh, astonic; stimulant, improves taste and voice.


Chemistry:
Chemical constituents:
 1’-Acetoxychavicol acetate; Acetoxyeugenol acetate; Caryophyllenols I and II; n-pentadecane; 7 – neptadecane, fatty acid methyl esters;  Methyl cinnamate, Cineole and d-pinene; a-pinene; b-pinene,cineole,terpinene and othe monoterpenes.
NBRI: 
Fourteen flavonoids detected by chromatography, of which seven identified as quercetin, kaempferol, quercetin-3-methyl ether, isorhamnetin, kaempferide, galangin and its 3-methyl ether. (Bleir and Chirikdjian,1972).

1’-acetoxychavicol acetate (I) and 1’-acetoxyeugenol acetate (II) isolated from seeds along with caryophyllene oxide, caryphyllenol II, pentadecane, 7-heptadecene and fatty acid methyl esters. (Ogiso and Kobayashi, 1974, C.A. 1974, 81, 68544h; Mitsui et al, 1976).

Essential oil contained a- and b-pinenes, 1,8-cineole, linalool, cedrol and eugenol. (Rui et al, 1982). C.A. 1982, 97, 150558e.

Isolation of 1’-acetoxychavicol acetate, 1’-acetoxyeugenol acetate and 1’-hydroxy-chavicol acetate. (Janssen & Scheffer, 1985, Itokawa et al, 1987).

Two new diterpenes- galanal A (fig. 1) and galanal B (fig. 2) (15-epimer of galanal A)- isolated from seeds and their structure established. (Morita and Itokawa, 1986. C.A. 1986, 105, 130685z; Morita and Itokawa, 1988).

Galanolactone, (fig.3) (E)8(17),12-labdadien-15,16-dial and (E)8(17)-epoxylabd-12-en-15,16-dial isolated from seeds and characterized. (Morita and Itokawa, 1988).

Isolation of a new compound –di(p-hydroxy-cis-styryl)methane- along with p-hydroxycinnamaldehyde from the chloroform extract of rhizomes. (Barick et al, 1987).

Galanalactone synthesized. (Herlem et al, 1989).

The major compound Mycrene (94.51 and 52.34%) determined in rhizomes and leaf essential oil respectively. (Charles et al, 1992). C.A. 1993, 118, 154117k.

The potent odorants were determined by combining  the GC elute sniffing and dilution methods. Sixteen oxygenated compounds were identified and among them 1’-acetoxy chavicol acetate was identified as an aroma constituent of galanga 1st time. High proportion of 1,8-cineol and other phenolic compounds of galanga have a great influence on the aroma quality from ginger five compounds, 1,8-cineol, linalool, geranyl acetate eugenol, chavicol acetate were determined as the potent odorants of galanga. In addition, acetic acid, bornyl acetate, citronellyl acetate, 2-acetoxy-1,8-cineol and the isomer, methyl eugenol and 1’-acetoxy chavicol acetate were also selected as the following important components. (Mori et al, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 115734p.

An antimicrobial diterpene was isolated from Alpinia galanga & the structure was elucidated by spectral data and identified as (E)-8b, 17-epoxylabd-12-ene-15, 16 dial (I). (Haraguchi et al, 1996).

Monoterpenoids of linalool and geranyl acetate ,1,8-cineole and acetoxy-1,8-cineoles, and some C6-C3 compounds of derivatives of eugenol and chavicol were determined as important odor components. The optical purity and odor difference between the enantiomers were determined by GC and GC elute sniffing. (S)-enantiomer possessed the characteristic cool, woody and ginger-like odor, (R)-enantiomer which played an important role in the odor of the fresh rhizomes. Among the 8 isomers of acetoxy-1,8-cineoles, (6R)-1,3,3-trimethyl-2-oxabicyclo[2,2,2]oct-5-yl acetate (trans-2-acetoxy-1,8-cineole) which presented a woody and galanga-like odor was the most important odor constituent. (Kubota et al, 1997; Kubota et al, 1998; Kubota et al, 1999). C.A. 1997, 133, 104049d; C.A. 1999, 130, 80559e; C.A. 1999, 130, 181662p.

(S)-(+)-O-methylmedelate esters of trans- and cis-1,3,3-trimethyl-2-oxabicyclo[2.2.2]octan-5- and 6-ols(2- and 3-hydroxy-1,8-cineoles) were prepared, and eight diastereomers were separated. The absolute configuration of the asymmetric carbons of the cineole moiety of each diastereomer was determined by (1)HH NMR data according to the Mosher theory. Each mandelate was reduced with LiAlH(4) to obtain optically pure hydroxy-1,8-cineoles, this being followed by acetylation to afford optically pure acetoxy-1,8-cineoles. These acetates were subjected to chiral GC, using a cyclodextrin column, and the enantiomeric purity of trans- and cis-1,3,3-trimethyl-2-oxabicyclo[2.2.2]octan-5- and 6-yl acetates in the aroma concentrate from the rhizomes of Alpinia galanga was determined as 93.9 (5S), 19.4 (5R), 63.5 (6R), and 100(6R) %ee, respectively. The aroma character of each enantiomer was also evaluated by GC-sniffing. (Kubota et al, 1999).

Three hydroxy-1,8-cineole glucopyranosides, (1R,2R,4S0- and (1S,2S,4R)-trans-2-hydroxy-1,8-cineole beta-D-glucopyranosides, and (1R,3S,4S)-trans-3-hydroxy-1,8-cineole beta-D-glucopyranoside, which are possible precursors of acetoxy-1,8-cineoles as unique aroma components, were isolated from the rhizomes of greater galangal (Alpinia galanga W.). Their structure were analyzed by FAB-MS and NMR spectrometry, and the absolute configulation of each aglycone was determined by using GC-MS analysis with a capillary column coated with chiral stationary phase. The composition of the diastereomers of (1R,2R,4S0- and (1S,2S,4R)-trans-2-hydroxy-1,8-cineole beta-D-glucopyranosides in the rhizomes was determined as 3:7 by a GC-MS analysis after preparing the trifluroacetate derivatives of the glucosides. (Someya et al, 2001).  

Pharmacology:

Alcoholic extracts of the rhizomes of Alpinia galanga showed good anthelmintic activity against human Ascaris lumbricoides. (Raj, 1975).

1’-Acetoxychavicol acetate was active against seven fungi, its minimum inhibitory concentration ranging from 50.0 to 250.0mg/ml, 1’-acetoxyeugenol acetate and 1’-hydroxychavicol acetate also showed antifungal activity. (Janssen & Scheffer, 1985).

1’-acetoxychavicol acetate and 1’-acetoxyeugenol acetate exhibited anti-tumor activity against sarcoma 180 ascites in mice; presence of 1‘-acetoxy group inthese compounds essential for anti-tumour activity (Itokawa et al, 1987).

Galanal A(fig. 1) and B(fig. 2), galanolactone,(fig. 3) (E)8(17),12-labdadien-15,16-dial and (E)8(17)-epoxylabd-12-en-15,16-dial showed cytotoxic and antifungal activities. (Morita and Itokawa, 1988).

An antimicrobial diterpene was isolated from Alpinia galanga in the screening for potentiations of phytochem, antibiotic action -8b,17-epoxylabd-12-ene-15,16-dial (I) synergistically enhanced the antifungal activity of quercetin and chalcone against Candida albicans. (Haraguchi et al, 1996).

An antimicrobial diterpene was isolated from Alpinia galanga in the screening for potentiators of phytochemical antibiotic action. The structure was elucidated by spectral data and identified as (E0-8 beta,17-epoxylabd-12-ene-15,16-dial(1). Diterpee I synergistically enhanced the antifungal activity and chalcone against Candida albicans. Antifungal activity of I was reversed by unsaturated fatty acids. Protoplasts of C. albicans were lysed by I. These results suggest that antifungal activity of I is due to change of Membrane permeability arising from lipid alternation. (Haraguchi et al, 1996).

Extract from shogaols are used in medicine, cosmetic and diabetic products as antiseptics and deodorants. Alpinia galanga rhizomes were extracted with a mixture of ethanol : water (50:50) and dried. The antiseptic and deodrant activity of the above shogaols-containing extracts was studied. (Jean and Cariel, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 166185w.

The polyherbal capsule in an antihistaminic and mast cell stabilizes and has been found to be very effective in allergic rhinobronchial conditions in human subjects. It contains time tested herbal ingredients in optimum condition which includes Alpinia galanga and other herbal plants. (Chauhan and Agarwal, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 313507v.

The antioxidant and microbial stabilities of galangal (Alpinia galanga) extract in raw minced beef were examined at 4+/-1 degree C. Raw minced beef containing galangal extract (0 to 0.10%, wt/wt) were prepared. Lipid oxidation during refrigerated storage was assessed by monitoring malonaldehyde formation, using the thiobarbituric acid reactive substances method. In minced beef, added galangal extract Improved oxidative stability. Galangal extract at higher concentrations of 0.05% and 0.10% (wt/wt) were also found to extend the shelf-life of minced beef. Addition of alpha-tocopherol (0.02%, wt/wt) to galangal extract may prove useful in inhibiting lipid oxidation and increasing microbial stability of minced meat. (Cheah and Gan, 2000).

Alpinia galanga extract used for knee pain in patients with osteoarthritis. (Altman and Marcussen, 2001).

Miscellaneous Information
Other Information:    

Acaricides containing A. galanga seed extract (500 mg/m2) showed 100.0 or 98.0% mortality against Tyrophagus putrescentia and Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus. (Yamahar, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 305150a.

MeOH extracts of A. galanga rhizome has strong lethal effect on Callosobruchus chinensis adults at 200mg/insect. The extract from A. galanga rhizomes had also a strong lethal effect on Plutella xylostella larvae. The most active compound in the A. galanga rhizome was isolated and identified as 1’-acetoxychavicol acetate (I). LD50 of I to topically treated C. chinensis were 3.0 and 4.5 mg/insect, respectively. (Dadang et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 200546b.

Galangin with anti-oxidative and free radical scavenging activities is capable of modulating enzyme activities and suppressing the genotoxicity of chemicals. (Heo et al, 2001).
