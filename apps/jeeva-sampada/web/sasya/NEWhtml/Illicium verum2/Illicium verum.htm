Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Star anise is an evergreen small tree. Illicium is the only genus in the family illiceaceae, composed of evergreen aromatic shrubs or small trees distributed in South East Asia, and South East USA. Forty-two species are reported in the genus. Star anise is known to occur rarely in North Eastern region of India, mainly Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh. It is indigenous to subtropical East Asia and is extensively cultivated in certain areas in China and Indo-China.

Climate and soil
Not well known. Main cultivation areas lie in cooler tropics and subtropics at altitudes up to 2000 m, with an average annual temperature of 12-18°C, average annual rainfall of 1000-2000 mm and soil pH of 5.8 (1).

Availability :

Only known from cultivation. Most probably originated from south eastern China and northern Indo-China. It is cultivated in Taiwan, Hainan and Japan. Cultivation in other parts of the world seems difficult (1).  

Conservation/ Threat Status : Cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Evergreen small tree, reaching 8-15 m in height; trunk diameter at breast height up to 25 cm; bark white. Leaves alternate, simple coriaceous, glandular-punctate; petiole about 1 cm long, blade elliptical to ovate or lanceolate, 5-15 cm x 1.5-5 cm, margin entire, apex acute, lower side pubescent (1).  

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers axillary, solitary, bisexual regular1-1.5 cm in diameter, white-pink to red or greenish yellow; pedicel 0.5-1.0 cm long; perianth lobes 7-12, arranged spirally; stamens 11-20, arranged spirally with short, thick filaments; carpels usually 8, free arranged in a single whorl (1).

Fruit
Capsule-like follicetum, 2.5-4.5 cm in diameter, consisting of an aggregate of 5-8 follicles arranged around a central axis in the shape of a star - star-shaped. Follicles are boat shaped, hard, reddish brown, 1-2 cm long containing one seed, spiliting around the ventral edge when dry (1).  

Breeding system and varietal improvement: Propagated by seeds. 

Phenology:
Starts flowering when the plants are 6 years old. Normally it flowers twice a year. 

Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology: Seed sub-cylindrical to compressed ovoid, 8-9 mm x 6 mm, smooth, glossy, light brown, containing copious, oily endosperm (1).  

CULTIVATION ASPECT:
Chromosome number:
2n=28

Selection of planting material:
Seeds are collected from vigorous trees, 15-20 years old, and only fully matured, brown seeds are chosen. Seeds lose their viability very rapidly, so should be sown in nursery within 3 days of harvest. Soaking the seeding for 6 hours in warm water (35-37°C) stimulates germination. In nursery the seedlings are to be protected from direct sunlight (1).  

Planting:
The seedlings are transplanted at 4-5 leaf stage in to the secondary nursery and 3-years old seedlings are used for field planting, 5-7 m apart in well manured planting holes. The plant starts bearing in 9th or 10th year. The fruits are generally harvested before they are fully ripe. The harvested fruits are steam distilled for the essential oil. Fruit are also dried and exported (1).

Management after planting:
Regular weeding, to facilitate harvesting of fruits and preventing fire hazards. Mulching is done at the end of rainy season to retain much of the soil moisture for the coming dry season. Application of farm manure (about 7 kg/tree) and additional ammonium sulphate at the start of the rainy season is recommended (1).  


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:Does not suffer seriously from pests. 

Diseases:
Does not suffer seriously from diseases.

Nematode Induced Diseases
Radopholus similis has been reported to cause occasionally some damage (1).

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Star anise has a pleasant, aromatic flavour and taste; aroma resembles that of anise. The essential oil is extensively used as flavouring agent in confectionery, chewing gum, animal feeds, pickling, liquors, pharmaceutical products, perfumes, beverages, gelatin, puddings, meat and meat products etc. It is used to scent soaps, tobacco, dental cream and toiletry articles. Dried ripe fruits are often found in potpourris. Wood is fine-grained and suitable for pulping, but not recommended for forest plantations because of its slow growth (1). Seeds are chewed after a meal to aid digestion. In Japan, the back is ground to a powder and burnt as incense in the temples (3).  

Parts Used: Dried ripe fruit. 


Harvesting Practices: Fruits are harvestable 3-4 months after flowering, before they are fully ripe, the stage at which the essential oil content is highest, usually in August-September. Two harvests per year are possible. Trees can remain reproductive for several decades. Harvesting is carried out by climbing trees and picking the fruits by hand or by using a long pole with a little hook connected to the end to detach the fruits. In china young twigs and leaves are also harvested and either distilled separately or together with fresh fruits for essential oil production. Yield: on an average 5-10 kg fresh fruits/tree are obtained from trees 1-25 years old and 10-20 kg from trees more than 25 years old. After drying, 100 kg of fresh fruit yields 25-30 kg of dried fruits (1).  

Post Harvest Handling
For use as a spice harvested fruits are placed in flat baskets, exposed to the sun for about 10 days and then preserved in a cool dry place and traded. For essential oil, fresh fruits or occasionally dried ones are steam distilled for 48-60 hours. Oil is then stored in air-tight containers at temperatures not exceeding 25°C, protected from light. Filtration and rectification in modern distilleries is then necessary for getting pure oil (1).


Produce and Products: Star anise fruits and star anise essential oil are the produce. Fresh fruits contain 3-3.5% oil - anethole (70-93%), having a colourless or pale yellowish colour with the aroma of anise oil and sweet flavour (1).  

Chemistry: Contains catechins, pro-anthocyanidin, essential oil, fixed oil and mineral substances. Essential oil is obtained by steam distillation of fresh or partly dried, whole or comminuted fruits (yield of 3-3.5%) and is mostly present in the fruit wall, not in seed. Its chief constituent is trans-anethole (70-93%). It also contains small amounts of cis-anethole, which is 15-38 times more toxic toanimals than trans-anethole. Fresh leaves and twigs contain 0.3-0.4% essential oil which has a composition similar to oil from fruits and is often blended with fruit oil. Decorticated seeds contain 55% fatty oils of which the composition is approximately: oleic acid 60%, linoleic acid 20%, myristic acid 10% and stearic acid 8%. 

In US regulatory status ‘generally regarded as safe” (GRAS) has been accorded to star anise (GRAS 2095) and star anise oil/oleoresin (GRAS 2096). The maximum permitted level of star anise oil in food is 0.07%. Oil is used as a starting material for the production of synthetic oestrogens (eg. diethylstilbestrol, diethylstilbestrol dipropionate) and perfumes (eg. p-panisaldehyde) (1).  
Fresh fruits yield 2.5-3.5% essential oil and dry fruits yield 8-9% essential oil.

Other uses: Medicinal uses
Fruits are sweet, aromatic, carminative, digestive, stomachic, expectorant and deodorant. Useful in dyspepsia, flatulence, spasmodic colonalgia, dysentery, cough, asthma, rheumarthritis, hemiplegia, facial paralysis and halitosis (2). Fruit is estrogenic agent used to increase milk secretion, promote menstruation, facilitate childbirth, increase libido and alleviate symptoms of male climacteric. Oil is used in a number of pharmaceutical preparations. It is offered in pharmacopoeia of many countries. It relieves colic and is a common ingredient in coughs syrups and lozenges. In traditional medicine a powder of fruits is used to treat abdominal colic, lumbago arising from a deficiency in the kidney, vomiting and epigastric pain due to cold in stomach and diarrhoea. Essential oil has stimulant, antiseptic, stomachic, diuretic, carminative, and mildly expectorant properties. It is a part of antitussive formulation and is employed against rheumatism, body lice and bed bugs, but may cause dermatitis in some people (1). In homeopathic medicine, a tincture is made from the seeds (3).

Economic potential:
Star anise is known to occur rarely in India, occurs mainly Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh.

Export Potential:
China and Vietnam are the major producers and exporters.

Miscellaneous Information

 1. Star anise fruits are often adulterated with the fruits of I. cambodianum Hance or l. anisatum L. known as the Japanese sacred anise tree (also called poison leaf, false aniseed). 
2. The fruits of this tree are poisonous and used as fish poison. 
3. Branches of this plant are used to decorate Buddhist graves.
4. A monograph on the physiological properties of star anise oil has been published by the Research Institute for Fragrance Materials (RIFM) (1).
