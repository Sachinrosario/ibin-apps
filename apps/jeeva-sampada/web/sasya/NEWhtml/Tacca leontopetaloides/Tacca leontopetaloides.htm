DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A tender, tuberous, perennial herb, c. 60 cm. high it is also grown for ornamental foliage. Rootstock globose, up to 30 cm. in diam.; leaves long-petioled, succulent, 60-90 cm. across tripartite, segments 2-3-fid, the ultimate lobes ovate lanceolate; flowers drooping, green, tinged with purple forming a dense umbel, enclosed in a green, striped scape; berries yellow, globular, 6-ribbed'; seeds angular.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Tubers.

Uses

  1. The plants are cultivated for their tubers which form an important article of food. 
  2. They yield a starch, called TAHITI, FIJI OR EAST-INDIAN ARROWROOT, which is a substitute for and adulterant of true arrowroot. 
  3. The starch is used to prepare porridges, cakes and other sweetmeats, and is mixed with wheat-flour for making bread. It is recommended as a food for invalid .It can also be used as laundry starch.

Chemistry
The flour made out of tubers had the following composition: water, 18.0; total nitrogen 0.01; ether extr., 3.0; reducing sugars, nil fibre, 0.05; and starch, 76.0%. The presence of b sitosterol, ceryl alcohol and a bitter principle, named taccalin (probably C18H26O7; 0.003%), has been report in the tubers. The tubers gave positive tests for the presence of alkaloids. Leaves and seeds are reported to contain an alkaloid.

Back to Top

Processing Techniques : 
The fresh acrid, bitter and poisonous tubers, when macerated and repeatedly washed with water, yield a nutritive starch excellent culinary properties.

Back to Top

Other Uses
  1. The tubers are used for the treatment of piles. A bit! extract prepared by washing the grated tubers in running water is a rubefacient, and is also given in diarrohea and dysentery.
  2. The split stems, petioles and scapes make braiding material after curing and are used in hat-making.
