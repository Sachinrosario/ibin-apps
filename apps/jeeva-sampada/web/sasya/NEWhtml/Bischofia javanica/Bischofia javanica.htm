HABIT & HABITAT

Found in sub Himalayan forest from Kumaun eastwards throughout Eastern India, in the Deccan peninsula throughout the eastern and western ghats and in the Andaman islands. Grown as ornamental avenue tree, also planted in tea and coffee plantations for shade.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A monotypic genus of large, timber yielding trees.
An evergreen tree upto 20m in height with a cylindrical bore of 9-12m, a girth of 1.5-2.5 m and a spreading crown.
Bark dark brown, exfoliating in angular scales;
Leaves trifoliate, leaflets variable, ovate to oblong - lanceolate or elliptic oblong, crenate, acuminate, bronze green.
Flowers green a greenish yellow, in slender, axillary or lateral, paniculate racemes;
Berries reddish brown to blue-black, compressed globose, fleshy, 3-4 seeded;
Seeds dark brown, smooth, shining, embedded in a soft, colourless edible pulp.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling germination :Natural regeneration is by seeds which germinate in most soil under shade. The fresh seeds have a germination capacity of upto 50% which is lost within a year of storage.

The seeds are best sown in Sep-Oct. in porous soils in bones or in the nursery and watered copiously. The beds are covered with straw to retain the moisture in the soil till the seedlings appear above the soil. Germination begins in 2-3 weeks. The soil is periodically stirred to promote aeration and to curb the growth of weeds.

The seedlings are ready for transplanting when they are 8-10 months old, during the rainy season. In coffee plantations, they are planted at a distance of 7-10 m. Propagation through stump planting have been found to give successful result.

The growth of seedling is fast, the mean annual girth increment being 3.9 cm.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Timber :
The sapwood is light cream coloured to reddish brown, narrow, grading into the heartwood. Heartwood is red to dark reddish brown or chocolate brown often with a purplish tinge, dull, with a rough feel and strong scent of vinegar when freshly cut, the scent disappearing with age.

Wood is moderately durable.
The wood is extremely durable under water but is susceptible to infestation by Martesia and Feredo spp. Use on the page -

Pulp
The hardwood has larger fiber length (1.82 mm) and smaller fiber dia (0.019 nm) than the typical hardwood.

It yields 44-49% unbleached pulp and 39-43% bleached pulp. Unbleached pulp is suitable for making brown wrapping paper, bleached pulp is suitable for making writing and printing paper.

Produce and Products:
Wood - construction of bridges, house posts, rafters, pile foundation, boats, rug-outs, wells and wheels. As sleeper wood in Assam, manufacture of cheap pencils, penholders, tea chests, heavy packing cases, plank for furniture and ceiling, for carving (in Assam) and fuel wood.

Pulp - unbleached pulp - for making brown wrapping paper
- bleached pulp - for writing and printing paper.

Bark - tannins as red dye to dye rattan baskets. In Samoa, bark for throat ailments, high fever and burns.
Hexane extract of plant shows anti-ulcerative activity.
Leaves - astringent used for sores, toothache, some eye diseases.
Seed - oil used as raw material in the surface coating industry.

The fruits are often confused with those of Flacourtia jangomas Research because of the Common local name and are eaten by villagers around Gorakhpur (UP)


Chemistry:
The bark contains tannins. It contains epi-friedelanol acetate, friedelin, betulinic acid as its ester, and B-sitosterol.
Twig bark contains tannins, 16, non-tanins, 13%
The roots contain B-amyrin, ursolic acid, and B-sitoserol.
The hexane extract of the plant showed anti-ulcerative activity.
