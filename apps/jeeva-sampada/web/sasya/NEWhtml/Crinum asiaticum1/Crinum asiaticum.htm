Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Bulbous herb, growing along river beds and also in forests, frequent on steep rocky hill slopes.

Availability :

Throughout India
Conservation/ Threat Status : Rare in wild but commonly cultivated in gardens.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Bulbs globose, 5-10.5 cm in daim., narrowed into a neck 10-30 cm long, clothed with old leaf sheaths; roots fleshy. Leaves 20-30 per bulb, glabrous, thick, 90-150 x 10-18 cm, linear lanceolate, shortly acuminate, flat, narrowed with sheathing base, bright green with smooth margins. Scape 45-90 cm long, 2.5 cm in diam., flowers white, 15-50 in umbels; bracts 7-10 x 2-2.6 cm., Pedicel 0.6-2.5 cm long. Perianth tube greenish-white, 7-10 cm long, cylindric, slender, perianth lobes 7-10 x 1-1.2 cm, linear, recurved or revolute. Stamens reddish, filaments slender, 6-8 cm long, anthers 1.2- 1.9 cm, versatile. Ovary three celled, green 1-2 x 0.5-0.6 cm, ovules many in each cell, style pink-purple, 12-15 cm long. Fruit subglabrous, 2.5-5 cm in diam., usually one seeded, beaked by fleshy base of the perianth, dehiscing regularly.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
It reproduces by seeds. Similarly new bulbs are formed on old bulbs.

Phenology:

Peak season of flowering is from May-July, however, flowering is throughout year if cultivated. Spike to 1 m. Flowers white (May, November); filaments purple.

Pollinators:
Insects. Crinum species are mostly pollinated by hawkmoths.

Other uses:

The bulbs are bitter, acrid, thermogenic, expectorent, vulnerary, laxative, carminative, anthelmintic, aphrodisiac, diuretic, diphoretic, nauseant, emetic, anti-inflammatory, antipyretic and tonic.
