HABIT & HABITAT
A tuberous herb of cultivated, commonly found in cultivated fields and Plains, Midlands, along Rivers and Streams and Upper Ghats. 

Availability : 
This species enjoys an Indo-Malaysian distribution. This species enjoys an Indo-Malesian distribution. In India its wild occurrence is recorded in the states of Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Leaves obliquely oblong acuminate. Spathes broadly ovate or oblong, greenish pink with pale blotches, purple at the base within, appendage red purple. Flowers monoecious crowded in cylindrical masses. Berries sub globose or ovoid and red. Seeds exalbuminous. This species requires warm and humid weather for vegetative growth and cool and dry weather for development of corms. It can hence be grown as seasonal, though it is a perennial. It requires well-drained soil.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Spadix as long as the spathe; appendage globose, conoid or amorphous. male inflorescence aubturbinate. anthers crowded, pale yellow. female inflorescence with ovaries crowded, sessile; style purple; stigma 2-3 lobed. berries red, ovoid.

Seed Germination :
It is propagated usually by vegetative means.
Though corms : Corms for planting are collected in February-May Cut Corm pieces with atleast one bud from seed corm weighing 0.5 to 1 kg tipped in cowdung and are allowed to dry under shade before planning May-June. Alternately, fresh cut corm pieces with 1 or two buds, teaed with antifungal and as prophylaxis against mealy bugs, are used as planting ThTc'ots are planted at a spacing of 75 x 75 cm normally or at 90 x 90cm spacing in flat beds or beds with broad ridges. Rich sandy loam preferred. The corms sprout in a month's time. Inter cultivation, weeding and earthing up is taken after a month of planting.
