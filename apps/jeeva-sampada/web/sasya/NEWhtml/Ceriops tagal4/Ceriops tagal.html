HABIT & HABITAT
A small straight-stemmed tree, found along the coastal forests of peninsular
India, in Sundarbans of West Bengal, Andaman and Nicobar.

Often occurring as short, stunted trees (especially in very saline environments), they may grow to 5 m high in areas having some freshwater influence.  

Estimated to range from Tropical Moist to Rain through Subtropical Moist to Rain Forest Life Zones, tagal mangrove is estimated to tolerate annual precipitation of 10 to 80 dm, annual temperature of 20 to 26°C, and pH of 6 to 8.5. Usually on well drained soils, within the reach of occasional tides in the inner mangrove. Sometimes occurs under Rhizophora or Bruguiera forest, but may form dense monospecific stands.

It is found in  Mangroove forest, Tidal swamps along the coast, evergreen forest, Tidal forest.

Found in West Coast and Konkan regions in India.

Availability :  

 

In India, Ceriops is found in Karnataka, Kerala and Maharashtra states.

Andamans, Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, southern China, Taiwan, through Malaysia to Micronesia, northern Australia, and Melanesia to New Caledonia. Rare and local in South Africa. Not widely introduced.

Reported from the African, Australian, Hindustani, and Indonesia-Indochina Centers of Diversity, tagal mangrove, or cvs thereof, is reported to tolerate diseases, insects, pests, salt, and waterlogging

Distributed from East Africa to the islands of the West-Central Pacific. In Kenya, Ceriops tagal occurs as a distinct zone, just landward of the Rhizophora mucronata zone. The trees in this zone exibit a certain degree of dwarf-growth, since their heights are usually not exceeding 4 meters. However, taller individuals (up to 8 meters) occur within the Rhizophora mucronata zone.

 


Status : 

Most common in mangroves.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

Tree up to 9 m in height, rarely 25bm and 30-60 cm in girth, often degenerating into a bushy shrub under unfavourable conditions, sometimes producing  aerial roots. Bark white, fairly smooth; leaves pale green , 3-6 cm, obovate to obovate-oblong, rarely elliptic-oblong glabrous, thick; flowers small, white or pale green, in clusters of 2-10 on the apex of new shoots; fruit dark brown, 1.5-2.5 cm long, club-shaped, with a faintly ribbed,  exhibiting vivipary with 15-25 cm long radicle.  

Flowers are very small (<1 cm, usually 0.5 cm). Like all mangroves of the family Rhizophoraceae, Ceriops tagal is also viviparous. Propagules are slender and long, but can easily be recognized from the propagules of Rhizophora mucronata which are fatter.

Pollinators:
Pollinated by animal vectors  which include moths, butterflies and other insects, as well as birds and bats. 
Ceriops tagal have explosive pollen discharge from petal pouches, to ensure that nectar-feeding birds collect pollen.


Seed Biology :
vivipary-Seeds germinate and develop into seedlings while still attached to the plant - instead of after a period of dormancy and dispersal is by seedlings instead of seeds


Seedling Germination :

Seeds are collected from July to September and are sown in pits or trenches from mid-August to the end of September. The survival rate is 90-95%. The plantation requires protection  in the initial years.   

Seed germinating in fruit, hypocotyl narrowly club-shaped, sharply angular. Grows on well-drained soil, within the reach of occasional tides in the inner mangrove. In Singapore, this species does not grow very well from its rarity and small size (15 m tall elsewhere).

Viviparous development provides a source of mangrove seedlings for replantation without a nursery stage.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Leaves, bark, wood

Uses

Leaves contain –  15% tannin, Twig bark contains   25% tannin, Bole bark contains  over 40% tannin.  Tannin content varies with the age of bark.  Leaves give a strong, supple pinkish leather.  They can be used in combination with the bole bark. Bole bark is highly valued in tanning industry.

  • Bark is used for tanning as a source of black and red dye.  They are very important tans imparting  a good red colour to leather.
  • A red cutch, Mangrove-cutch is obtained from the bark and the cortex and is used along with Indigo for obtaining different shades of black, brown and purple.  
  • Treating nets and sails with the bark extract is said to preserve them from decay.
  • The spray-dried extract of a blend of ceriops, myrobalans and Acacia nilotica bark contain 65 percent tannin and the blend is suitable for the manufacturing of crust leather. 
    Tannin, dye, timber
  • Leaves are suitable for  direct application in the tanning industry and give a strong supple and pinkish brown leather  free from crack of grains.
  • A blend of the bark of  C. tagal and the leaves of Anogeissus latifolia  in equal proportions found to be a good substitute for wattle (Acacia spp.) and produced exceptionally good results.
  • Besides in leather industry, the tan-extracts are widely used for softening water in the boilers in locomotives to prevent or remove scaling, in drilling oil-well in combination with caustic soda to remove  the viscosity of the drill-mud and for miscellaneous uses such as manufacture of ink, preservatives, treatment  of fishing-nets, in plastic industry, and in the manufacture of ion-exchange resins, preparation of tannin-formaldehyde glue for use in plywood industry.

Other Uses

  1. Timber : In house-building, pit-prop and boat building, fuel wood, source of pulp, excellent charcoal.
  2. Plant possesses astringent properties. A decoction of bark is used to stop hemorrhage, and is applied to malignant ulcers, and also given after childbirth.
  3. Boiled fruits are eaten in the Andaman's.
