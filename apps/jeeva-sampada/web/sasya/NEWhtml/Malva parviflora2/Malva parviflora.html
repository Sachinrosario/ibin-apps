HABIT & HABITAT
It is a annual herb about 15-45 cm high, found in INDIA: N.W.Himalaya, alt. 334-667 m. Upper Bengal, Punjab & Rajasthan. Common in moist water-logged areas and cultivated fields. EUROPE, ARABIA.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herbs, 15-45 cm high, ascending or prostrate, spreading,. Stem branched at base, pubescent with scattered patent simple and stellate hairs, ultimately glabrescent. Leaves suborbicular, 1.5-7.0 cm across, often slightly 3-7 lobed, rounded to obtuse at apex, crenate-serrate at margin, cordate at base, 5-7 nerved with scattered stellate hairs on both surfaces; petioles 1.0-2.5 cm long with a line of stellate and simple hairs on upper side; stipules lanceolate to deltoid, 1-3 mm long, acuminate with ciliate margins.
NBPGR:
A spreading herb, 15-45 cm. High, pubescent and with some stellate hairs; leaves 2.5-6.3 cm. Diam; suborbicular, cordate obscurely 5-7 lobed, finely crenate; petioles long, upto 5-10 cm pedicels. Axillary 1 or few together short rarely exceedings 2 cm.  Involucral bracts short, linear caducous, hairy at tip.  Calyx in fruit accrescent, reticulately veined, glabrous; Lobed, lobes ovate; corolla 1.2-2 cm. Diam, purplish or white.  Fruit 6 mm diam, carpels 10 glabrous, strongly reticulated the two margins of the black keeled, sometimes slightly winged, often with a few minute teeth, seeds black glabrous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers 2-6 in axillary fascicles, rarely solitary, white; pedicels 3-8 mm long, persistent up to 10 mm, stellate-hairy or glabrous. Epicalyx segments linear, up to 5 mm long, simple hairy, falling prematurely. Calyx cup-shaped, c 5 x 3 mm, divided to the middle; lobes ovate, c 5 x 2 mm, acute, outside with minute stellate and simple hairs, hairless inside. Corolla bluish-white, petals terminal half-broader than base (obovate), 3-7 x 2-3 mm, glabrous or occasionally with minute stellate and simple hairs, glabrous inside. Staminal column about 3 mm long. Schizocarp discoid, 5-8 mm in diam., 2-3 mm long; mericarps 8-10, reniform, trogonous with sharp angles, dorsally with raused reticulate venation, margins keeled on the back, rarely slightly winged, glabrous, indehiscent. Seeds reniform, 1.8-2.0 mm in diam., glabrous, browinsh-black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plant-emollient, pectoral leaf-nervine in wounds, swellings, seed-demulcent, bechic, in ulcers of bladder.


Chemistry:
Pressure of sterols and /or terpenes in the plants indicated by preliminary tests. A neutral polysaccharide - MVS-1 from seeds composed of arabinose, galactose and glucose in molar ratio of 3:6:7 respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Pectic peptidoglycan - MVS-V - isolated from seeds composed of protein (57.0). and polysaccharide (43.0%); polysaccharide moiety consisted of galacturonic acid, arabinose, xylose, galactose and rhamnose in molar ratio of 24: 6:5:3:8. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Isolation of two novel polysaccharides - MVS -IIA and MVS-IIG - from seeds; MVS-IIA composed of arabinose, galactose, mannose in molar ratio of 14:28:1 and MVS-IIG composed of glucose, galactose and mannose in molar ratio of 10:1:1. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Another polysaccharide - MVS-IIIA - consisting of arabinose, xylose, galactose and galacturonic aicd in molar ratio of 16:1:8:3, isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Seeds afforded another new acidic polysaccharide - MVS-IVA - containing 3.3% proteins. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Another acidic polysaccharide - MVS-VI - from seeds found to be composed of arabinose, xylose, galactose, glucose, rhamnose and galacturonic acid in molar ratio of 30:15:20:3:2:10. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.
Previously isolated neutral polysaccharide MVS-I found to consist of arabinose, galactose and glucose in molar ratio of 3:6:7. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Pharmacology: 

Polysaccharides, MVS-I, MVS-IIIA, MVS-IVA, MVS-VI and peptidoglycan, MVS-V, exhibited significant reticuloendothelial system-potentiating activity in carbon clearance test. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.
Polysaccharides MVS-I, MVS-IIA, MVS-IIIA and MVS-IVA also showed anti-complementary activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.
Anti-complementary activity also shown by polysaccharide MVS-VI. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.
Polysaccharide MVS-I and polysaccharide-rich fraction (MVS-V-Chandigarh) obtained from peptidoglycan, MVS-V, also exhibited remarkable hypoglycaemic action. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.


Ethnobotany:  

Medicine for cough (Seed, Karnick et al, 1981); Emollient (Part not mentioned, Malhotra and Basu, 1984); Pectoral complaints (Part not mentioned, Malhotra and Basu, 1984); Piles (Leaf, Singh and Pandey, 1980); Ulcer (Seed, Malhotra and Basu, 1984); Urinary complaints (Root, Rawat and Pangtey, 1987); Edible as vegetable (Leaf + Shoot, Arora, 1981)
