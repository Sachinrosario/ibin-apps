Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Pleasantly scented, aromatic, stout and non-hairy herb, growing upto 2-4 feet tall, with erect stalk round and hollow at maturity. Fennel seed (which is botanically a fruit) has pleasing fragrance and pleasant aromatic taste, valued mainly for the aromatic, volatile oil.

Climate and soil
Fennel is a cold weather crop and comes up well in fairly mild climate, up to an altitude of 1800 m, 500-2000 mm rainfall, mean annual temperature of 6-24°C, and soil pH of 6.3-8.3, throughout the subtropical and temperate regions as a summer crop (kharif) in parts of Karnataka and Maharashtra, and as winter (rabi) crop in North India. In India, it thrives in the sunny, limey, well-drained loams of Gujarat, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh (2). It does not thrive well in South India except at higher elevations. Prolonged cloudy weather and high humidity at flowering time is conducive for diseases like powdery mildew and pests like aphid. Dry and moderately cool weather, particularly during the month from January-March, favours both good yield as well as quality of the produce. Crop is susceptible to frost particularly at flowering stage. All type of soil except sandy soil; thrives well in rich well drained loamy or black or sandy loam soils. Black cotton soil and loamy soils containing lime are suited for cultivation if drainage is proper. Saline and water logged soils are not suitable for cultivation (1).

Availability :

Most probably originated from southern Europe and the Mediterranean. It is cultivated throughout the world and India and has naturalized in many places. It is grown throughout south east Asia and is sub-spontaneous in several mountains in east Java. It occurs in Argentina, Bulgaria, Germany, Greece, India, Lebanon, Egypt, France, Japan, Italy, Morocco, China, Turkey, Indonesia, Pakistan, Russia, USA, Hungary, Sri Lanka, Netherlands, Russia, USA and Romania (1).

Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild and cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Robust, perennial, glabrous, glaucous, aromatic herb up to 2 m tall. Stem erect, terete, round, longitudinally striate, profusely branched at all heights, internodes hollow at maturity. Leaves alternate, decompound, sheathed, lower leaves largest; leaf sheath forming an open cylinder, at base embracing stem, 2-15 cm long, margins white, scarious, rest of the petiole subterete, 1-10 cm longer than the sheathing part, longitudinally striate, blade triangular in outline, up to 30 cm x 50 cm, 2-6 pinnately divided into filiform, acute blue-green lobes 1-14 cm long (1).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
It is a cross pollinated crop. Inflorescence is a terminal compound umbel, up to 20 cm in diameter, usually smaller; peduncle 5-16 cm long, unequal in length. Besides main stem the plant may produce 2-5 side tillers. Each tiller bears 4-8 branches in alternate pattern. In favourable environment secondary branches are also produced. Each branch bears a terminal compound umbel. Each umbellet bears 5 to 8 small flowers. Calyx vestigial at the top of the ovary petals 5, distinct, subovate in outline, up to 1.5 mm x 1 mm, with strongly inflexed, notched apex, yellow with a thin membranous outgrowth on the ventral side of the midrib; stamens 5, about 1.5 mm long; pistil with inferior, bilocular ovary, 2 styles, each with a stylopodium at base ad a stigma at top (1).

Fruit
Fruit an ovoid cylindrical, usually slightly curved schizocarp, 3-8.5 mm x 2-2.5 mm, up to 1 cm long, laterally compressed, light green to yellow-brown, splitting at maturity into 2 mericarps each with 5 prominent ridges and oil-vittae between ridges (1).

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Cross fertilization is predominant in fennel, most cultivars are highly heterozygous and heterogeneous (1).

Phenology: Flowering occurs 3-4 months after sowing.

Floral and seed biology:
Seed Biology
Seeds are greenish yellow or yellowish brown in colour, with testa adnate to the pericarp.


CULTIVATION ASPECT:
Seed germination:
Epigeal, germinates 2-3 after sowing, normally well between 15-25°C with better germination in dark than in light. Seeds retain their viability for 2-3 years (1).

Seedling development
Initial development is slow, with a period of 2-2.5 months from sowing to stem emergence.

Local cultivars :
Botanical varieties of Common fennel: Foeniculum vulgare Mill; var. more common, cultivated Indian fennel. Bitter fennel: F. vulgare var. vulgare, often grow as a weed and also cultivated in many Central European and Mediterranean countries. Silver Fennel (French fennel, Roman fennel, Florence fennel) F. vulgare var. dulce cultivated in west Europe (1).

Released varieties:
S-7-9, PF-35, CO-1, Guj. Fennel-1, Guj. Fennel-2, RF-101, Azad saunf-1, RF-125, Rajendra Sourabha and Pant Madhurika.

Chromosome number:
2n=22

Selection of planting material:
Seeds are the planting material. Vegetative multiplication by root or crown division or through in vitro culture is also possible (1).

Planting:
Field preparation/cultivation: Nursery is raised and then transplanted at the end of August in kharif season. Land should be ploughed 3-4 times in heavy soil and 2-3 times in light soil up to a depth of 15-20 cm to make soil friable. After planking, beds of convenient size are prepared, irrigation channels are laid, finally one more planking is given to get fine seed bed free from clods. If the soil moisture is low irrigation should be given before preparatory tillage. FYM/compost @ 10-15 t/ha has to be incorporated with soil during field preparation. Similarly endosulfan powder @ 25 kg/ha may be applied to soil for control of termites and insect pests.

Sowing time, seed rate and spacing:
Direct sowing by broadcasting or line sowing (line 45 cm apart). Rabi crop is sown directly in the field while kharif crop is transplanted. Optimum time for fennel sowing is from mid September to mid October, 45 to 60 days prior to the date of transplanting, delayed sowing to 15th November reduces yield. In rabi (rainfed crop) direct sowing is done in the first fortnight of October in rows at 30 x 40 cm apart and 45 x 10 cm spacing in Gujarat. A seed rate of 10-12 kg/ha is sufficient for direct sowing. Seedbed of 3 x 5 m are prepared by about 15th June, by mixing manures and fertilizers with the soil. Seeds are sown by broadcasting on beds. Soaking seed in water prior to sowing is suggested to enhance germination. Before sowing seeds are treated with a fungicide like mancozeb @ 3 gm/1 kg of seed. Deep sowing should be avoided. Azetobactor inoculation to seed/seedlings prior to sowing/planting is found advantageous at Hisar.

Transplanting method: For transplanting one ha 5-7 kg seed is used, and seedlings raised in 100 m2 area is enough for one ha. Generally 45 to 60 days old seedlings are transplanted by the end of August in kharif season. Transplanting of fennel during second half of October at 45 x 10 cm after harvesting bajara (Kharif crop) is also beneficial. Transplanting should be preferably done in the evening.
Seed rates needed are:
General - Broadcasting 9-12 kg/ha
Transplanting 3-5 kg/ha
Rajasthan - Broadcasting 10-12 kg/ha
Transplanting 3-4 kg/ha
Gujarat - Broadcasting 9-12 kg/ha
Transplanting 3-5 kg/ha

Management after planting:
Weeding: One or two weeding and hoeing have to be given. First weeding 30 DAS while plants are about 5 cm tall. Excess plants are to be removed at this stage keeping plant-to-plant distance of 20 cm within rows in line sown crop. Second weeding depends on weed growth in the field. Earthing up of plants at the time of weeding and hoeing is beneficial. Integrated weed management with pre-emergent weedicide Pendimethalin @ 1.0 kg/ha, supplemented with one hand weeding in 50 days after sowing (Rajasthan and Haryana).

Irrigation: Fennel is a long duration crop has high water requirements. One or two light irrigations immediately after sowing is essential to facilitate germination, subsequently irrigate at an interval of 15-20 days depending upon the crop stage and weather condition and water holding capacity of soil. Care should be taken that the crop should not suffer a moisture stress during grain formation stage. The crop must be irrigated immediately after transplanting.
Fertilizer and manure: In addition to this 90 kg N and 30 kg P2O5 is also given to fennel crop. 1/3 of total dose of N (30 kg/ha) and full dose of P2O5 (30 g) is added to soil at the time of field preparation. Remaining amount of N given in 2 equal splits at 45 DAS and at the time of flowering along with irrigation.

Fertilizer recommendation for fennel in major growing states
State Recommendations
Gujarat For rabi crop 90 kg N, 45 kg P2O5, 25Kg K2O/ha and 25T FYM. N is given in 3 splits i.e. 50% N & P as basal 25% N at 30 DAS and balance 25% at 60 DAS. FYM given as basal for kharif 80 kg N, 60 kg P2O5 + 25 T FYM.
Rajasthan 90 kg N, 40 kg P2O5/ha applied in three equal splits ie 30 kg N and 40 kg P2O5 as basal, 30 kg N after 30 days and 30 N after 60 days of sowing.
Haryana 50 kg N & 30 kg/P2O5. N is applied in 3 splits, 50% as basal and at 30 and 60 DAS with irrigation.
Bihar 60 kg N, 40 kg P2O5 & 20 kg K2O. 60: 50: 10 kg NPK/ha, N is two splits.
Uttar Pradesh 15 kg N, 50 kg P/ha as basal dose followed by 15-30 kg N/ha as top dressing.

Other practices: Tender seedlings are to be protected from hot sun. Intercropping fennel + radish is found remunerative in Bihar.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:
Aphids: Aphis foeniculoseora
Control: Spray Endosulphan (0.05%), Monocrotophos (0.03%). Repeat the spray at an interval of 10-15 days.

Heliothis caterpillar
Control: Spraying of Endosulfan @ 0.07% or Monocrotophos @ 0.05%.


Diseases:

Powdery mildew: Caused by Leveillula taurica.
Symptoms: white powdery patches on the plant and shrivelling and shedding of fruits.
Control: Spray Karathane (0.1%) or wettable sulphur (0.25%) once at flower initiation stage and another after 15 days (500-800 Lits.). Dusting with sulphur (300 mesh) 25 kg/ha also is recommended.

Sugary disease: Caused by (Sclerotinia sclerotiorum). Disease is spread through aphids, the vectors considered to be primary cause of infection.
Control: Vector control by spraying with Phosphomidon (0.2%) or Dimethoate (0.03%) or Quinalphos/DPVP @ 0.05% or Malathion 0.05% at 15 days interval (3 sprays) commencing from 50% emergence of flowers and also at seed developing stage to checks aphids that spread the disease. Judicious application of fertilizer and irrigation.

Stem & root rot: Caused by Rhizoctonia solani
Control: Treat seed with Bavistin @ 2g/kg seed and 0.2% Captan or Brassicol. Drenching soil with Bordeaux mixture or Copper oxychloride at 0.2% to 0.3%.

Leaf Blight: Caused by Ramularia foeniculi & Alternaria tenius
Control: Spray Indofil (0.2%), Bavistin (0.1%) or any Copper fungicide (500-800 Lit/ha), twice at 60 and 90 days after sowing.

Gall midge: Caused by Systole albipennis.
Control: Five sprays of the insecticide Methyldemoton 0.025% or Monocrotophos 0.036% or Thiometon 0.03% or DDVP 0.05%) at 15 days interval commencing from emergence of flower.

Leaf spot
Symptoms: Fungus causes brown to black lesions on leaves, stem peduncle and seeds. Severely infected plant give a blighted appearance.

Gummosis
Control: Use certified seed, follow crop rotation.
Storage control
Fennel should be stored after proper drying. At the time of storage, moisture content should not be more than 8-9% fennel seed should be kept in gunny bags and in humidity free stores.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Fennel plant is pleasantly aromatic, mainly used in culinary preparations as well as for masticatory purpose. Used for flavouring since antiquity. Whole or crushed seeds of fennel are used for flavouring soups, fish and meat dishes, sauces, pickles, chocolates and other food materials and an ingredient of curry powder. Fennel seeds roasted or fresh are chewed after meals. Fennel fruits have characteristic aromatic flavour. In western countries even the whole plants are distilled to extract the volatile oil. Fennel volatile oil is used in flavouring food products, including alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages, desserts, candied baked goods, condiments and relishes. Maximum permitted level in food products is about 3%. Fruit residue after essential oil distillation may be fed to cattle. Oil and seed has important use in the meat industry. Commonly is used to scent detergents, soaps and perfumes, flavour, beverages, baked foods, condiment, ice creams, liqueurs, seasoning meats and sausages. Bitter and sweet fennel oil are applied in perfumery, with maximum permitted levels of 0.4%. Bitter fennel oil is used to a limited extent and mainly in cosmetics. Leaves are used for garnishing, leaf stalk are used in salads and vegetable, eaten raw or cooked and used as potherb especially in fish sauces and garnishing. Stem pieces are chewed for their pleasant taste. Most of the produce is meant for domestic consumption (1). This is used medicinally, in toothpastes, soaps, perfumery and air fresheners (4). Fennel seeds are also dry roasted. Candied fennel seeds are normally taken after food. In severe cough and cold conditions, fennel decoction is consumed. Widely used in pickles in North India, it is added in side dishes too. For bread, fish preparations and pork varieties, fennel is extensively used. Candied fennel is used as a breath freshener (5).

Parts Used:

Dried fruit commonly called as fennel seed, young leaves and stem.


Harvesting Practices:
Crop is ready for harvest 170-180 days after sowing, when fruits turn yellow in the whole plant (for seeds). Since all the fruits do not mature at a time, umbels are harvested in 4-5 picking at 10-15 days interval. For good quality of fennel, umbels should be picked when seed development is completed and before seeds start turning yellowish. As the umbels maturity is not synchronous, the harvesting is done in 3-4 rounds at the 10-15 days interval. Over ripening of umbels should be avoided as it reduces the quality. After harvesting, umbels should be dried in sunlight for 1-2 days and then in shade for 8-10 days to retain the attractive green colour, else exposure to sun for a longer duration leads to the loss of colour, luster and quality of seed. For producing chewing type fennel umbels are harvested at 30-40 days after pollination when seed size is just half of fully developed seeds.

Tender shoots and leaves for culinary purposes should be gathered before flower bud formation (1). Yield: 10-19 q/ha may be obtained, but yields are low in the first year.

Post Harvest Handling
Threshing should be done on hard (Pucca) threshing floor and after complete drying, fruits are separated from the stems and cleaned. Seeds are stored in gunny bags. Grades: Special, Good and Fair (seed) Standard (powder) (1).

Storage
Fennel should be stored after proper drying. At the time of storage, moisture content should not be more than 8-9%. Seeds should be kept in polythene lined gunny bags in humidity free stores.

Produce and Products:
Fennel seed, ground fennel seed, sweet fennel oil and bitter fennel oil.

Medicinal uses
Fruits are sweet, acrid, bitter, emollient, refrigerant, alexipharmic, carminative, digestive, stomachic, antiemetic, intellect-promoting, ophthalmic, haematinic, expectorant, anthelmintic, cardiac stimulant, galactagogue, sudorific, febrifuge, aphrodisiac and tonic. Useful in vitiated conditions of pitta and vata, hyperdipsia, burning sensation, cough, bronchitis, anaemia, ophthalmopathy, dyspepsia, anorexia, flatulence, colic, dysentery, haemorrhoids, vomiting, cardiac diseases, agalactia, strangury, dysuria, fever, skin diseases, splenopathy, nephropathy, cephalagia, inflammations, consumption and general debility (3). Fennel is used in Ayurvedic or domestic medicines. Dioscorides and Hippocrates mentioned fennel as diuretic and emmenagogue. Roots are applied traditionally as diuretic and purgative. Fruit in combination with bark of Alyxia species, gives an agreeable odour to medicines and is useful in the treatment of spruce. In India, leaves are considered as diuretic, fruit juice is administered to improve eyesight, and hot infusions of the fruits are applied to increase milk secretion and to stimulate sweating. In Chinese herbal medicines fennel is used against gastro-enteritis, hernia, indigestion and abdominal pain, to resolve phlegm and to stimulate milk production. Ground spice and essential oil has antioxidant properties. Essential oil is antifungal and antibacterial properties. It also shows antiviral properties against potato virus X (PVX), tobacco mosaic virus (TMV) and tobacco ring spot virus (TRSV). Anethole is effective against Staphylococcus aureus, E. coli, Candida albicans and Corynebacterium sp. (1). An infusion is used in the treatment of indigestion, abdominal distension, stomach pains and urinary disorders. It helps in the treatment of kidney stones and, when combined with a urinary disinfectant, makes an effective treatment for cystitis. Fennel is a tonic for the digestive system and deals quickly with nausea. It also seems to strengthen the contractions of the internal muscles. Fennel helps the body cleanse itself and is particularly effective in expelling alcohol. It can also be used as a gargle for sore throats and as an eyewash for sore eyes and conjunctivitis. Recent research shows that this spice also lowers blood pressure. It helps regulate energy to the spleen, stomach, liver and kidneys and can be used topically for joint inflammation. This herb has been used for centuries to promote lactation and will also hasten a period. As an aphrodisiac, it increases libido (4).

Chemistry:
In US regulatory status 'generally regarded as safe' (GRAS) has been accorded to common or bitter fennel (GRAS 2481), sweet fennel (GRAS 2482) and sweet-fennel oil (GRAS 2483).
Fennel contains 1.0-3% of a volatile oil, composed of 50%-60% anethole and 20% d-fenchone. The main chemical compounds in essential oil are anethole, methyl chavicol, limonene and fenchone. Anethole is responsible for the sweet taste, is allergic and weakly insecticidal (1).
Composition: Fatty oil: 10.0%.

Quality is mainly attributed to seed size, lusture, colour, aroma, fiber content and grains with at least one percent volatile oil. Percentage of volatile oil varies considerably, low in Indian varieties (0.7-1.2%) and higher in Eastern European varieties (4-6%). Main constituent of fennel oil is anethole and d-a-fenchone. Good quality oil contains 50-60% anethole, which contributes to the sweet taste and flavour. Sweet European fennel has higher anethole content. Aromatic sweet flavour of fennel depends on the quantity of anethole and fenchone in its oil. Bitter taste is due to fenchone, the lower its quantity, the sweeter the taste. Harvesting the crop at proper stage and PHT are important to produce quality fennel. Anethole and other terpenoids may have mild estrogen-like activity and inhibit spasms in smooth muscles, such as those in the intestinal tract. Proportions of the two ingredients vary according to strain and region (4). Seeds contain 9.5% protein, 10% fat and 13.40% minerals. Important minerals are calcium, phosphorous, iron, sodium, potassium and are also rich in vitamins. Oil content varies with varieties/geographical types/climate. 1000 g seed weight is 4-8 g.

Miscellaneous Information

 · Commercial spice samples are adulterated with sand, stem tissue, fruit residues left after distillation, immature or mouldy fruits or with other umbelliferous fruits like anise fruits.
· Monographs on the physiological properties of sweet fennel oil and bitter fennel oil have been published by the Research Institute for Fragrance Materials (RIFM) (1).
· In Greece, it was a symbol of success.
· In Rome, the young fennel shoots were used as food.
· Pliny considered it good for improved vision.
· Culpeper recommends it as an antidote for poison (2).
· Ancient Greek athletes ate fennel seed so they would gain strength, but not weight. There is a belief that serpents eat it 'when they cast their old skins, and they sharpen their sight with the juice by rubbing against the plant.'
· In medieval times, Fennel was employed, together with St. John's Wort and other herbs, as a preventative of witchcraft and other evil influences, being hung over doors on Midsummer's Eve to warn off evil spirits (4).
