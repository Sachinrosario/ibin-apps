DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs, 1-2 m high; stems erect, puberulous, leafy. Leaves palmately 5-fid, 8-15 cm in diam; lobes ovate,wedge-shaped, acuminate.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers blue, 2-3 cm long, in many-flowered, simple or sparingly branched, 15-30 cm long racemes.  Upper lateral sepals ovate or broadly obovae. Petals heads somewhat 5-shaped; spur recurved. Follicels 5, finely hairy.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: Tubers used for poisoning arrows and as a medicine for its action as narcotic and sedative, regarded as heating and stimulent, useful infever, cephalagia, affections of the throat, dyspepsia and rheumatism.
Chemistry:
  •  Alkaloids bikhaconitine (Fig. 1.1), chasmanconitine (Fig. 1.2),, indaconitine (Fig. 1.3),   and pseudaconitine (Fig. 1.4),   from root of A. ferox were isolated by adsorption chromatography method. (Klasek at al., 1972).
  •  From the root of A. ferox four alkaloids has been isolated. Pseudaconitine is the major constituent and the other three minor alkaloids are identified as bikhaconitine, veratroyl pseudaconine and diacetyl pseudaconitine. (Purushothaman and Chandrasekharan, 1974).
  • New alkaloids-2-(1H)-quinolinone (Fig. 2), and 3, 4-dihydro-6-hydroxy-2-(1H)-quinolinone-isolated from root tubers and characterized. (Bhogi and Katz 1993). C.A. 1994, 121,175172b.
  •  A new diterpenoid alkaloid–14-O–acetylsenbusine A-isolated from root tubers and its structure established; in addition, vakoghavine, chasmaconitine, crassicauline A, falconericine, bikhaconine, pseudoconine, neoline, senbusine A, isotalatizidine were isolated and several earlier assignments of aconine, senbusine A have been revised (Fig. 3) , (Bhogi and Katz, 1994).
  •  Four new lipoalkaloids lipopseudaconitine, lipoyunaoonitine, lipoindaconitine and lipobikhaconitine-isolated from root tubers and their structures established by C13-NMR; They were shown to be mixed C-8 linoleoyl, palmitoyl and stearoyl esters of norditerpenoid alkaloids namely of veratroylpseudaconine, anisoylyunaconine, benzoylindaconine, and veratroylbikhaconine. (Fig. 4), (Bhogi and Katz, 1994).
  •  Aconitine songorine and virescenine were isolated from subterranean parts of A. ferox. (Bessonova et al., 1995), CA. 1996, 125, 81829b.
