Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
A large forest tree, up to 40 m tall; A Native of Amazon basin of Northern Brazil, Bolvia, Colombia, Peru, Venezuela, and Guianas, mainly along banks of the Amazon and upper Orioco Rivers and their tributaries. Introduced in to Srilanka in 1880 and in Singapore in 1881. Ranging from Subtropical Moist to Tropical Dry through West Forest life Zones, Brazil nut is reported to tolerate annual precipitation of 13.5-41.0 and annual temperature of 21.3-27.4 degree celcious. A tropical tree some times gregarious, preferring high land, beyond reach of periodical floods. Thrives best in rich alluvial soil, in a hot moist climate.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 A large forest tree, up to 40 m tall. Leaves alternate; short-period, leathery, oblong, with wavy margin,30-50 cm long, 7.5-15 cm broad; flowers in large errect spike-like racemes, white to cream, sepals united, but finally separating into 2 deciduous sepals; fruit large brown, woody, globose, 10-15 cm diameter, weighing up to 2 Kg., with an operture at one end which is closed by a woody plug and must be broken open to extract the' nut inside '; fruit may remain on trees several months after ripening; seeds 12-24 per fruits , triangular, with a brown horny testa.

Seed Germination :

Cultivation:
Plants will start from the seeds softened in moist sand; The embryo has to be removed by opening the seed coat at the edges. The fungicide- treated embryos germinate in ca 20 days in plastic bags or cups. when out planted in holes, ca 200 gm P has to be added to the hole. At 1.5- 2 years, the root is ready for top grafting. Leaves will be removed from the branches 8-10 days before the scion is removed, promoting more vigorous buds. Thirty days after grafting, the root stock will be ringed distal to the graft, allowing the scion to gradually outgrow branches of the rootstock. Trees are propagated from seed or gootee (by layering). From 10-25 years are required for fruiting to begin.

MAJOR PESTS AND DISEASES
Disease: The following fungi is known to this tree; Actinomyces brasiliensis, Aspergillus flavus, Cephalosporium bertholletianum, Cercospora bertholletiae(Gray spot), Cunninghamella bertholletiae, Fusarium spp. Myxosporium sp.Pellionella macrospora, Phytophthora heveae, Piptocephalus sphaerocephala, Phomopsis bertholletianum, and thamnidium elegans. The nematode Meloidogyne incognita is known to cause severe galling on the roots.
Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used :
Nuts.

Uses: 

  1. Nuts used as food. 
  2. The shells and the spoiled kernels supplement firewood in the power plants providing heat for dryers.
  3. The imperfect nuts are used for oil extraction, the press cake employed as feed for animals, whose manure could be used to extend fuel.

Chemistry :
The Brazilnuts are noted for their rich oil content(65-70%), protein(15-17% of fresh weight; 50% of defateed flour), and usually high levels of the sulfur containing aminoacids, 8.35 by weight. In this regard and in it's high selenium, Brazilian nnut may be unique among edible plants.With usually high levels of lecithin, and probably tocopherol, as well as selinium, the brazilnut canhave several positive and a few negative health consequences.
