Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
 An erect or geniculately ascending, simple or branching, glabrous annual, 15-60 cm in height, commonly found in Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan , Gujrat, Maharastra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu in India. It is commonly found on sandy or dry, arid and semi arid soils and also on the wastelands, fallow fields, lawns, canal banks and in shallow waters.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Culms 45 cm in height, arising from rhizomes; leaves lanceolate, rough, ciliate or glabrate, 2.5 to 12.0 cm x 0.5 cm ; inflorescence a raceme, with 2 to 1 flowered spikelets; caryopsis ovoid-oblong, pale, smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Grass,seeds.

Uses

·        The young grass is highly nutritious and is considered to be one of the best for grazing and hay making. It is fairly rich in nitrogen, ash, sodium and silica. It is suspected to have an intoxicating effect on milky animals and stimulates the production of milk.

·        In times of scarcity the seeds, mixed with bajra are consumed. The deglumed fruits are ground and baked in to thick unleavened bread, known as bhurat-bread. It is regarded as the most nutritious famine food. During normal times also, the flour is mixed with ghee and sugar and is given to children to improve health. In some parts of Rajas then, the grass is considered a natural food after rains.

Chemistry
Analysis of a sample of the grass from Rajasthan (India) give the following average values, on dry weight basis: crude protein-21.18; ash-13.3; calcium-1.72; phosphorous-0.30; magnesium-2.48; sodium-3.49; potassium-3.55; and soloca-2.06%.
