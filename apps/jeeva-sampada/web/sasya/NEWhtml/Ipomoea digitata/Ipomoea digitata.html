DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial herb with large, ovoid or elongated tubers. Stems long, twining, thick, tough, glabrous. Leaves ovate-lanceolate, 10-15 cm in length, often broader than long, deeply palmately, divided, acute at apex, entire at margin, 5-7-lobed, glabrous. Flowers in corymbose-paniculate raceme; bracts minute, deciduous. Corolla campanulate, purple, 3.8-6.3 cm long. Ovary 4-celled. Capsules, ovoid, 4-valved, surrounded by enlarged fleshy sepals. Seeds coated with detouchable brownish hairs.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine the root is sweet and used as tonic, aphrodisiac, diuretic, alterative. It is used in leprosy, vomiting, blood diseases, improve voice and complexation. In Unani system of system the root is sweetish and bitter. It is used in sythilis, gonorrhoea and inflammation. Leaves enrich the blood.


Chemistry:
Alcoholic extract of tubers showed stimulant as well as depressant actions on different organ systems along with convulsant effect on central nervous system. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

b-sitosterol and another sterol mp. 72°C, from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Fixed oil from tubers contained palmitic, stearic, oleic, linoleic and linolenic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Extract produced relaxant effect on blood vessels and smooth muscles of guinea pigs, rats and frogs. This action together with depressant effect on myocardium, may account for hypotensive action of drug. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Taraxerol and sitosterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Taxerol, taraxerol acetate, umbelliferon, scopoletin, scopolin, octadecyl(E)-p- coumarate and n-butyl- a- D- fructopyranoside were identified in the roots of Ipomoea digitata. (Dai et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133: 347156r.
