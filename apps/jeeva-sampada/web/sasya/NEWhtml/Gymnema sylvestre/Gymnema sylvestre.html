DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Large, woody, much-branched climbers; young stem and branches terete, pubescent. Leaves broadly elliptic-obovate or oblong, 3-6 x 1.2-3.0 cm, shortly acuminate at apex; rounded or cordate at base, sparsely to densely pubescent, often glabrous above; petioles 0.5-1.2 cm long, pubescent.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in umbelliform cymes; peduncles densely tomentose; pedicels slender, 4-8 mm long; bracts minute, ovate-oblong, hairy, ciliate. Calyx pubescent, divided to the base or nearly so; segments oblong, c 2 mm long, obtuse, ciliolate. Corolla pale yellow, 4-5 mm across; tube campanulate, c 1.5 mm long; lobes thick, ovate-deltoid, spreading, recurved, glabrous; Corona lobes prooduced beyond the sinuses of corolla-lobes. Follioles  terete, rigid, lanceolate, 5-7 x 0.8 cm, glabrous. Seeds nearly ovoid or oblong, flat, c 1.2 cm long, pale brown, broadly margined.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Leaves are referred for the treatment of diabetes, gastric disorders, glycosuria, cornea opacity and other eye diseases. Roots are also useful in stomachache and snake bite.
BSI:
In Ayurveda plant is bitter, acrid, cooling, tonic, alterative, anthelmintic, alexiteric; useful in eye complaints; cures opacities of the lens, cornea, vitreous body; good in diseases of heart, piles, leucoderma, inflammations; cures burning sensation, biliousness, bronchitis, asthma, ulcers. Leaves are referred for the treatment of diabetes and roots for stomachache. Fruits are bitter, pungent, heating, stomachic, anthelmintic, alexiteric, good in diseases of the heart, urinary discharges, bronchitis, ulcers  (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933). Leaf is used as medicine for eye diseases, cornea opacity in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh and also in diabetes, gastric disorders, glycosuria and urine complaints. Root is used in snake-bite. Root and leaves used in stomachache (Jain, 1991).

I and II had antisweet activity against sweetness induced by sucrose. (Yoshikawa et al, 1989). C.A. 111:74803
Five oleanane-type triterpenoid saponins, gymnemic acids VIII-XII as antisweet principles were isolated from the leaves of Gymnema sylvestre (Asclepiadaceae). Their structures were established on the basis of spectral and chemical evidence. They were characterized as glucosideuronic acid derivatives of gymnemagenin acylated with acetyl, tigloyl and/or 2-methylbutyroyl moieties. (Yoshikawa et al, 1992). C.A. 118:251412.
The structure of gymnemagenin (3b,16b,21b,22a,23,28-hexahydroxyolean-12-ene), the sapogenin of the antisweet principles of Gymnema sylvestre, was established by x-ray analysis of the 3b,23;21b,22a-di-O-isopropylidene derivatives on the basis of this result, the structure of deacylgymnemic acid was elucidated as the 3-O-b-glucuronide from carbon-13 NMR spectra. Five antisweet principles, gymnemic acid III, IV, V, VIII, and IX, were isolated in pure states from the hot water extract of leaves of Gymnema sylvestre. Of these, three (GA III, IV, and V) were known, while two (GA VIII and IX) were new compds. The structures of GA VIII and IX were elucidated as 3'-O-b-D-arabino-2-hexulopyranosyl gymnemic acid III and IV, respectively (Liu et al, 1992). C.A. 117:188249.
Besides six known gymnemic acids, four new tritepenoid saponins, gymnemasins A, B, C and D, isolated from the leaves of Gymnema sylvestre, were identified as 3-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-b-D-glucuronopyranosyl]-22-O-tigloyl-gymnemanol, 3-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-b-D-glucuronopyranosyl]gymnemanol, 3-O-b-D-glucuronopyranosyl-22-O-tigloylgymnemanol and 3-O-b-glucuronopyranosylgymnemanol, respectively The aglycon, gymnemanol, which is a new compd., was characterized as 3b,16b,22a,23,28-pentahydroxyolean-12-ene. (Sahu et al, 1996). C.A. 124:198177.
Investigation of hypoglycemic activity of major saponin constituents from "gymnemic acid", a crude saponin fraction of Gymnema sylvestre, exposed not only two new saponins, gymnemosides a (I) and b (II), but also gymnemoside b and gymnemic acid V as active principles. Furthermore, an acetyl group linked 16- or 22-hydroxy group in I and II was found to migrate easily to primary 28-hydroxy group, while acyl migration from 28-hydroxy group in gymnemic acid I was little observed. (Murakami et al, 1996). C.A. 124:198145
Following the characterization of gymnemosides -a and -b, new triterpene glycosides, gymnemosides-c, -d, -e, and -f, were isolated from the leaves of Gymnema (G.) sylvestre R. BR. Their chem. structures were elucidated on the basis of chem. and physicochemical evidence as follows: 21-O-benzoyl-28-O-acetylgymnemagenin 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosiduronic acid (gymnemoside-c), 23-O-[b-D-xylopyranosyl (1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl] gymnestrogenin (gymnemoside-d), 23-O-[b-D-xylopyranosyl (1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl]-28-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl (1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl] 23-hydroxylongispinogenin (gymnemoside-e), 23-O-[b-D-xylopyranosyl (1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl (1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl]-28-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl] 3b,16b,23,28-tetrahydroxyolean-18-ene (gymnemoside-f). The inhibitory effects of gymnemosides-c, -d, -e, and -f and principal triterpene glycosides from Gymnema sylvestre on glucose uptake in rat small intestinal fragments were examined, and gymnemic acids II, III, and IV, gymnemasaponin V, and gymnemoside-f were found to exhibit the inhibitory activity. (Yoshikawa et al, 1997). C.A. 128:125789.
The content and compound of free sugars were determined with 10 plant extract samples that have been reported to possess significant blood glucose-lowering activity. Glucose and fructose were the main free sugars. Quite a few plants also had substantial amounts of myo-inositol and sucrose. Nephrolepis tuberosa tuber contained the most free sugar (46.8%), followed by Costus speciosus rhizome (24.9%). (Nahar et al, 1998). C.A. 128:319349.
From the glycosidic fraction, six triterpene glycosides, gymnemosides a, b, c, d, e, and f, were isolated together with nine known triterpene glycosides. The structures of gymnemosides a and b were determined on the basis of chemical and physicochemical evidence as 21-O-tigloyl-22-O-acetylgymnemagenin 3-O-beta-D-glucopyranosiduronic acid and 16-O-acetyl-21-O-tigloylgymnemagenin 3-O-beta-D-glucopyranosiduronic acid, respectively. The inhibitory activity of each triterpene glycoside from gymnemic acid was examined to determine its impact on the increase of serum glucose level in oral glucose-loaded rats. Gymnemoside b and gymnemic acids III, V, and VII were found to exhibit a little inhibitory activity against glucose absorption, but the principal constituents, gymnemic acid I and gymnemasaponin V, lacked this activity. (Yoshikawa et al., 1997)a.
Six oleanane-type saponins (e.g. I), along with two known triterpene saponins, were isolated from the leaves of Gymnema sylvestre. The structures of the oleanane triterpene glycosides were characterized as longispinogenin 3-O-b-D-glucuronopyranoside, 21b-benzoylsitakisogenin 3-O-b-D-glucuronopyranoside, 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl oleanolic acid 28-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl ester, oleanolic acid 3-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D- glucopyranoside 3-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl, oleanolic acid 28-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl ester and 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl oleanolic acid 28-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl ester on the basis of hydrolysis and spectral evidence, including 1D- and 2D-NMR (TOCSY, ROESY, HMQC and HMBC) and FABMS analyses. (Ye et al, 2000). C.A. 133:161792.
The air-dried leaves of Gymnema sylvestre R. Br. (Asclepiadaceae) were extracted with 95% ethanol, and various triterpene compounds were isolated. The longispinogenin, sitakisogenin, and chichipegenin obtained were characterized by the lack of a 23-hydroxyl functional group when compared to the known constituents of the same species growing in India, i.e., 23-hydroxylongispinogenin, gymnestrogenin, and gymnemanol. Such a difference may serve as a characteristic feature to distinguish between Gymnema sylvestre plants growing in the two localities. (Ye et al, 2001)a. C.A. 136:99150.
Three new oleanane-type triterpene glycosides, along with the sodium salt of alternoside II (I), were isolated from an ethanol extract of the leaves of Gymnema sylvestre. The structures of these new saponins were identified as 21b-O-benzoylsitakisogenin 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-b-D-glucuronopyranoside (II), the potassium salt of longispinogenin 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-b-D-glucuronopyranoside, and the potassium salt of 29-hydroxylongispinogenin 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-b-D-glucuronopyranoside (III). The aglycon of III, gymnemagenol (IV), was characterized as 3b,16b,28,29-tetrahydroxyolean-12-ene. Structure elucidation was accomplished by interpretation of NMR (DQF-COSY, HMQC, and HMBC) results, FABMS, and hydrolysis. I and II exhibited antisweet activity. (Ye et al, 2001)b. C.A. 134:234349.

The effects of Gymnema sylvestre (Indian medicine) on the insulin resistance of rats with streptozotocin-induced diabetes were studied by the glucose clamp technique. Gymnema sylvestre extract (120 mg/kg/day) treatment did not improve insulin resistance. (Tominaga, 1995). C.A. 124:106171.
Gymnemic acids (GA1 and GA2) suppressed high K+ induced contraction in guinea pig ideal longitudinal-muscle, inhibitory effect of GA1 and GA2 on smooth muscle were assumed to be a result of inhibiting glucose uptake. (Shimiza et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 195212w.
Gymnemic acids (I) extracted from leaf of Gymnema sylvestre inhibit glucose absorption in the intestine associated with sodium dependent transport and also inhibit the intestinal absorption of oleic acid and histidine. (Yoshika et al, 1996). C.A. 1999, 126, 347186f.
Nine fractions containing gymnemic acids, extracted from the leaves of Gymnema sylvestre, were evaluated for their effects on various aspects of glucose transport in guinea pigs and rats. Some of the fractions had hypoglycemic activity, and experiments suggested that they did so by inhibiting glucose uptake in the intestine. (Shimizu et al, 1997). C.A. 126, 338669.
Gymnema sylvestre showed as high antioxidant effect as a-tocopherol. (Inoue et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 320863d.
Effect of water solution fraction of alcoholic extract of Gymnema sylvestre leaves on glycogen content by isolated rat hemidiaphragm was studied in normal and glucose fed hyperglycemic rats. The leaf extract by itself failed to alter the hepatic glycogen content in normal rats. In glucose fed rats, the leaf extract lowered the glycogen content of the tissue significantly (P<0.05) and this was further lowered when both exogenous insulin and leaf extract was administered. (Chattopadhyay, 1998). C.A. 129:225554
Chromium decreases the portion of systolic blood pressure elevated by high sucrose intake as shown previously, but high levels of sucrose ingestion can eventually overcome this bis(maltolato)oxovanadium overcame sucrose-induced elevation of SBP as well as some of the "genetic hypertension." Different from chromium, this decrease was not overcome by high levels of dietary sucrose. The invention provides nutritional supplements and methods for administering nutritional supplements that improve glucose metabolism, particularly for persons with diabetes. A first nutritional supplement, or "Phase I" supplement, comprises a source of vanadate and a source of chromium. A second nutritional supplement, or "Phase II" supplement, comprises Gymnema sylvestre and lipoic acid. The nutritional supplements are alternated to prevent accumulation of the nutrients in the body and also to overcome desensitization that can occur over long periods of continuous use. While the nutritional supplements may be alternated at almost any frequency and taken over almost any duration, it is preferred that each Phase be taken for between about 2 and about 6 mo, most preferably about 3 mo or about 90 days, before alternating back to the other Phase. (Womack, 1998). C.A. 128:261966
Reduction of preference for sucrose was probably caused by gurmarin contained in the gymnema diet and subsequent restoration of the preference may be due to suppression of the effect of gurmarin by salivary gurmarin-binding proteins induced by the gymnema diet. (Katsukawa et al., 1999)
Effects of an alcoholic extract of Gymnema sylvestre (GS4) on insulin secretion from rat islets of Langerhans and several pancreatic b-cell lines. GS4 stimulated insulin release from HIT-T15, MIN6 and RINm5F b-cells and from islets in the absence of any other stimulus, and GS4-stimulated insulin secretion was inhibited in the presence of 1 mM EGTA. Blockade of voltage-operated Ca2+ channels with 10 mM isradipine did not significantly affect GS4-induced secretion, and insulin release in response to GS4 was independent of incubation temp. Examination of islet and b-cell integrity after exposure to GS4, by trypan blue exclusion, indicated that concns. of GS4 that stimulated insulin secretion also caused increased uptake of dye. Two gymnemic acid-enriched fractions of GS4, obtained by size exclusion and silica gel chromatography also caused increases in insulin secretion concomitant with increased trypan blue uptake. These results confirm the stimulatory effects of Gymnema sylvestre on insulin release, but indicate that GS4 acts by increasing cell permeability, rather than by stimulating exocytosis by regulated pathways. Thus the suitability of GS4 as a potential novel treatment for NIDDM can not be assessed by direct measurements of b-cell function in vitro. (Persaud et al, 1999). C.A. 132:73477.
Qurs-e-Ziabetus 16 (QZ-16) is a polypharmaceutical herbomineral formulation used by Unani physicians in non-insulin dependent diabetes mellitus (NIDDM). It contains Gymnema sylvestre and streptozotocin treatment significantly increased the blood glucose in the control group relative to the group treated with QZ-16. (Nazmi et al, 1999). C.A. 132:8873.
Effects of gymnemic acids on fecal steroid excretion in rats. Three kinds of extracts from Gymnema sylvestre leaves, extract (GSE), acid ppt. (GSA) and column fractionate (GSF), of which the gymnemagenin (an aglycon of gymnemic acids) concentrations are 58.87, 161.6, and 363.3 mg/g respectively, were used for the experiments. These were administered to rats orally at the dose of 0.05-1.0 g/kg for 22 d. Rats were given free access to water and nonpurified diet without cholesterol, and the differences in fecal excretion of steroids and gymnemic acids were investigated. Although there were no significant effects of GSE, GSA and GSF decreased body wt. Gain and food intakes in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.01). GSF (1.0 g/kg) significantly increased fecal excretion of neutral steroids and bile acids in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.05), esp. those of cholesterol and cholic acid (CA)-derived bile acids. The increases in fecal steroid excretion of cholesterol, total neutral steroids, total bile acids and CA-related bile acids were acute and significantly correlated with fecal gymnemagenin levels (r2 = 0.2316-0.9861, P < 0.05). These results demonstrated for the first time that a high dose of gymnemic acids increases fecal cholesterol and CA-derived bile acid excretion. Further studies are needed to clarify the effect of gymnemic acids on cholesterol metabolism (Nakamura et al, 1999). C.A. 131:125263.
Compounds derived from Gymnema sylvestre leaf materials are disclosed. Methods for isolating such compounds are also disclosed. The compounds may be administered orally, i.v., s.c. or transdermally and are useful for treating patients having diabetes, impaired glucose tolerance, and various conditions associated with or symptoms of diabetes. Additional the compounds reduce polydipsia, polyuria and polyphagia, regenerate the pancreatic islets of Langerhans, including beta cells, increase endogenous insulin, lipase and amylase levels, increase production of proinsulin and c-peptide, and lower blood lipids and triglycerides and free fatty acids. An hydroalcoholic extract of G. Sylvestre was prepared and all water-sol. component were removed. Diabetes was induced in rats, followed by treatment with the above preparation for a three week treatment period. The treatment group demonstrated significantly reduced fasting blood glucose levels, increased pancreatic amylase and lipase levels, and dramatically increased the number of pancreatic islets and beta cells. (Shanmugasundaram et al, 1999). C.A. 131:327491.
Gurmarin (10 microg/ml), a protein extracted from Gymnema sylvestre, depressed significantly (40-50%) the phasic taste responses to sugars (sucrose, fructose, lactose, and maltose) and saccharin sodium recorded from the greater superficial petrosal nerve (GSP) innervating palatal taste buds in the rat.(Harada and Kasahara, 2000).
Antihyperglycemic action of a crude saponin fraction and five triterpene glycosides (gymnemic acids I-IV and gymnemasaponin V) derived from the methanol extract of leaves of Gymnema sylvestre R. BR. (Asclepiadaceae) in streptozotocin (STZ)-diabetic mice. Insulin-releasing action of gymnemic acid IV may contribute to the antihyperglycemic effect by the leaves of Gymnema sylvestre. Gymnemic acid IV may be an anti-obese and antihyperglycemic pro-drug. (Sugihara, 2000). C.A. 135:40766.
Extract of Gymnema sylvestre improved serum cholesterol and triglyceride levels through influence over a wide range of lipid metabolism in rats. (Shigematsu et al., 2001)
Effect of chitosan from Gymnema, on lowering plasma total cholesterol tended to be enhanced when chitosan was used in combination with the extract In addition, long-term administration of the extract had no effect on hematology and blood-chemical parameters. (Shigematsu et al, 2001). C.A. 135:221107.
