Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large tree attaining a height of 300 ft., or more, with a clean straight bole under forest conditions, but often tending to brnach freely when grown in the open. Bark deciduous in long thin strips or sheets.  It grows well in Nilgiris. 

Availability :

Plenty


Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild and cultivated

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves on juvenile shoots opposite, sessile, cordate-ovate, covered with a bluish white bloom; adult leaves alternate lanceolate, 6-12 in long and 1-2 in broad. Stems of seedlings and coppice shoots, quadrangular.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Anti insect, antiviral, insect repellent. Bean bruchids, potato tuber moth. To protect beans in store 10-20 eucalyptus leaves should be distributed per kg. Potatoes should be stored on a bed of leaves.

Parts Used: Leaves

Miscellaneous Information

Eucalyptus oil, according to the Indian Pharmacopoeial List, should be colourless or pale yellow, odour amomatic, camphoraceous; taste, pungent, camphoraceous feeling of cold.

Mixed with an equal amount of oliveoil, it is useful as a rubefacient for rheumatism. It is also used in ointments for burns. It is used as a stimulating expectorant in chronic bronchitis and asthma.
