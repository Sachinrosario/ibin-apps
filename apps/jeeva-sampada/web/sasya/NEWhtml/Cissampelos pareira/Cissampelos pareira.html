REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillay cymes; Flower: Minute, pedicellate, yellowish; Male flower: Bracteate, pedicellate; sepals 4, hairy outside concave; petals combined to form cyathiform corolla, hairy without half the length of sepals, filaments longer than corolla ; Female flower: Bracteate, pedicellate; sepals 1, ovate-oblong, vilous on outside; petals 1 subrotund, half the length of sepals; ovary1, style short 3-fid ; Fruit: Drupe sub-globose, compressed, hairy red; Seed: Horse shoe shaped. Flowering & Fruiting : July-September . Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind or  birds.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Hayatin (fig. 1), m.p. 298° dec., furnished hydrochloride, picrate, chloroplatinate, methiodide and methochloride; hayatinin (fig. 2) furnished HCl and picrate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Hayatin, L-curine and d-isochondrodendrine from roots and vines. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Structure of hayatinin, m.p. 231°C, elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Root bark showed presence of eleven quaternary alkaloids, three of which were termed menismine, cissamine and pareirine in addition to known 1-bebeerine, hayatinin, hayatin and d-isochondrodendrine, five more tertiary alkaloids present in root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

A new alkaloid (+ +)-4’’-O-methylcurine (fig. 3) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Structure of hayatin established as dl-bebeerine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.

Hayatidin (fig. 4), m.p. 179°C,identified as dl-4’’-O-methylbebeerine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)g.

Hayatinin shown to be racemic mixture. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)h.

Cissamine chloride (fig. 5), m.p. 215° C, was identical with cyclanoline chloride. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)i.

Cycleanine, (-)bebeerines, hayatidin, hayatinin, hayatin and (+)quercitol from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Crystal structure of cycleanine determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Isolation of laudanosine, nuciferine, bulbocapnine, corytuberine and magnoflorine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

A new antileukaemic tropoloisoquinoline alkaloid – pareirubrine – isolated and its structure established by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Isolation and structure elucidation of two azafluoranthene alkaloids – norimeluteine (fig. 6a) and norruffscine (fig. 6b). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Pareirubrines A (fig. 7a) (earlier designated as pareirubrine) and B (fig. 7b) isolated as antileukaemic compounds and their structures determined by X-ray analysis; grandirubrine and isomerubrine also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

A novel condensed tropone- isoquinoline alkaloid (yield 0.0004%, C18H13NO3, m.p. 97-99°C) named pareitropone (fig. 8), showing potent cytotoxicity (IC50 0.8 nanog/ml) against cultured p-388 cells, has been isolated from roots of Cissampeoles pareira. (Morita et al, 1995). MAPA 1995, 9504, 2567.


Pharmacology:
Hayatin methiodide had relaxtant activity similar to that of d-tubocurarine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)j.

Hayatin, L-curine and d-isochondrodendrine showed curare-like activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)k.

Leaves extract used for external application in itch, sores and sinuses. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Hayatinin methochloride possessed similar muscle-relaxant properties a d-tubocurarine chloride. During steady partial block by hayatinin, responses to brief tetanic stimulation, to neostigmine and to an increase in calcium concentration were similar to those observed during block by d-tubocurarine. It was concluded that hayatinin caused post-synaptic block. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Norimeluteine (3.6 mg/ml) and norruffscine (5.8 mg/ml) showed cytotoxic activity against P-388 cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.
