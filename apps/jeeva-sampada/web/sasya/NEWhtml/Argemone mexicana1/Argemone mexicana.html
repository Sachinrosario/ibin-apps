HABIT & HABITAT
Argemone mexicana is a prickly annual herb native to Mexico, Southern and Western United States and has been naturalized in Brazil, India, Africa, and other subtropical and tropical places. Satyanashi occur as wasteland weed in almost every part of India. It is reported as field weed in mustard crop in parts of India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Argemone mexicana is a prickly, glabrous, branching annual herb to bushy undershrub, divaricately branching from below with yellow juice and showy yellow flowers. Stem clasping, oblong and woody. The height of this plant varies between 0.3 to 0.12 meters. Leaves are thistlelike, simple, alternate, exstipulate, subsessile, sub-amplexicaul, sinuately pinnatifid. Lobes sharply and irregularly dentate, spiny on the margin and on veins beneath glaucous, white spotted, prickles, sharp. Fruits are capsules, 0.75-1.5 inches long, prickly and oblong or elliptic ovoid.

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers are solitary terminal with prickly peduncles, yellow or yellowish white in colour. Flowers are 2.5-5.0 cm in diameter, sepals valvate, caducous. Petals in 2 series, imbricate, parallel viened, obovate and crumbled, deciduous. Bracts foliaceous. Stamens many, carpels 4-6, syncarpous, unilocular, superior, ovules many, parietal placentation as many as the carpels. Ovary covered with soft spines, styles reduced with 4-6 lobed with reddish stigma.

Economic Potential and Uses

Seeds yield 22-36 per cent of a nauseous, bitter non edible toxic oil which causes lethal dropsy when used with mustard oil for cooking. Seed oil, popularly known as Satyanashi oil is used as an illuminant, lubricant, in soapmaking, used in medicine for external application in skin diseases and for protection from termites. Mixed with other oils such as linseed oil, it can be used in the paint industry. Oilcake cannot be used as cattle feed due to its toxic effect, however it could be used as manure.

Parts used

Roots, leaves, seeds and yellow juice.


Back to Top

Uses

The plant is found suitable for the reclamation of alkaline soils. Dried and powdered plants are recommended as green manure as it contains sufficient amount of Nitrogen, Phosphorus and Potassium. Seeds resemble mustard seeds and in India it is used to adulterate mustard seed.

Other Uses
According to Ayurveda the plant is diuretic. purgative and destroys worms. It cures lepsory, skin-diseases, inflammations and bilious fevers. Roots are anthelmintic. In India roots are used in the treatment of piles. Juice is used to cure scabies, ophthalmia and opacity of cornea. Seeds are purgative and sedative. In Homoeopathic system of medicine, the drug prepared from this herb is used to treat the problem caused by tape-worm. This valuable and neglected plant has been strongly recommended as an aperiant, anodyne and hypnotic.
