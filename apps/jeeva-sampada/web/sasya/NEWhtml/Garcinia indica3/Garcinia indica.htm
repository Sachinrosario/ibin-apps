Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Graceful slender tree found in tropical evergreen forests of W. Ghats. Introduced in France, Bouran and Mauritius.

Availability :

Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Kerala.
Conservation/ Threat Status : Endemic

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Graceful, slender trees, usually up to 10 m tall, sometimes up to 15 m, with conical crown, usually buttressed at base; trunk blcakish; wood greish-white, hard; bark light brown, very thin, smooth, rather shiny; branches often drooping, young ones suberect, slender, irregularly straite. Leaves 6.5-11 x 1.5-5 cm, lanceolate or ovate-oblong, contracted into petiole at base, acute or acuminate at apex, margin membranous, shiny, dark green; lateral veins 7-18, slender, prominent; petioles 5-12 mm long, slender; young leaves red, beautiful. Male flowers: Small, white; buds are large as pea, almost globose; pedicels stout, gradually thickened upwards, up to 4 mm long; bracts scale-like, caducous. Sepals 4, decussate, outer pair smaller, 3-4.5 mm long; inner pair 4.5-5 mm long, ovate-rotundate, base narrow, thick, fleshy, yellowish to pinkish orange. Petals 4, 5-6 mm long, thick. Stamens inserted on a hemispheric subqudrate torus; filaments short; anthers oblong, bilocular, truncate. Rudimentary pistil absent or if present as long as stamens. Female flowers: Terminal, shortly peduncled; pedicels short, ca 3 mm long, stout. Sepals and petals similar to male flowers. Staminodes 1-3 mm long; filaments short, very thick. Ovary subglobose, 4-8-loculed; stigmas convex, coronate, sessile, rays as many as locules, often 2-seriate. Berries almost spherical, not furrowed, 4-8-loculed, purple, orange-pink or wine brown, surrounded at base by persistent sepals; pulp red, acidic, fleshy. Seeds 5-8, compressed.
