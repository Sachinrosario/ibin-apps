Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Caesalpinia crista L. is a large scandent prickly shrub found throughout the hotter parts of the world such as Florida, Louisiana, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Hawaii, Guam, and American Samoa, particularly along the sea coast and up 2,500 ft. on the hills. In India, it grows wild in the scrub forests of the eastern Himalayas, in Assam and West Bengal. It is also found in the Eastern Ghats in Andhra Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh. Gray nicker bean is intolerant of shade and is usually open-grown or at least grows in broken sunlight. It tolerates salt spray, salty soils, and occasional flooding with seawater. The species grows in all textures of mildly acid to alkaline soil. Annual rainfall in the areas where gray nicker bean grows ranges from 750 mm to 1800 mm. It grows most frequently on the beach strand, on coastal dunes, and at the better-drained edge of mangroves. Gray nicker bean also grows inland in disturbed areas (4, 5, 6, 7).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Caesalpinia crista L. also known as Gray nicker bean or nicker nut is robust, spiny, reclining or scrambling, woody or herbaceous vine, puberulent and armed, 1.5 m in height (unsupported) and 6 m or more in extension and stems to 6 m long, 1 cm thick and abundantly armed. Plants usually have a single stem arising from the ground but often branch low on the stem. The bark is dark brown with numerous recurved prickles. The stems grow stiffly upright for 0.75 to 1 m before curving into a more horizontal direction. The stems, twigs, and leaf rachises are covered with straight or curved prickles. Leaves alternate, evenly pinnate; petioles to about 4 cm long, with recurved usually paired prickles; pinnae in 4 or 5 pairs, 4-12 cm long, usually with recurved prickles; leaflets in 4 to 8 pairs, shortly petiolulate, ovate to elliptic, usually 2-4 cm long (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers are yellow in colour, 1.8 cm across, dense at the top, long peduncled terminal and the pedicels are very short in the bud. Flowers perfect or staminate, in simple or compound racemes to 20 cm long. Calyx pubescent, 5-8 mm long; petals orange-yellow, broadly oblong, 7-10 mm long; stamens as long or shorter than the petals. Legume tardily dehiscent, suborbicular to oval, compressed, 3-9 cm in diameter and densely prickly (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10).

Seed Biology :
Fruits: Prickley, inflated legumes are flattened oval shaped and 5 to 10 cm long, reddish-brown when dry. Within are one to three (usually two) smooth, hard, 2-cm long seeds that are olive drab in the pods and remain so until exposure to the sun bleaches them to a light gray colour. The pods open partially upon drying and eventually release the seeds. The seeds are almost globular in shape with smooth shiny surface. On an average seeds weighs 409 seeds/kg (4, 5, 6).

Seedling Germination :
Germination is hypogeal. Seedlings reached 26 cm in 40 days after sowing. Dormancy of seeds can be overcome by acid scarification, light and temperature treatment or treatment with concentrated sulphuric acid for 30-90 minutes. The seeds acid treated for 90 minutes, if exposed to blue spectrum of light for 72 hrs at 300 C, exhibit 100 per cent germination (5, 10).


Cultivation:

Growth of gray nicker beans is fairly rapid at all stages. Older plants grow a meter or more per year. Individual stems live at least 4 years. Plantations undoubtedly could be established with potted seedlings or by direct seeding scarified seeds. Control could probably be obtained by lopping with a machete followed by herbicide treatment of the sprouts. The plant is wild and no attempt has been made to cultivate this plant on large or commercial scale (5).

Economic Potential and Uses

The seeds contain 20-24 per cent of pale yellow thick semidrying oil having a disagreable odour. Oil from seeds is an emollient and used as an embrocation to remove frekles from the face, as a cosmetic and to stop discharges from the ear. The semidrying oil extracted would be useful for manufacture of high-quality alkyd resins, polishes and paint. The oil is also used in the convulsions and paralysis (4, 5, 7, 10).

Parts used

Root bark, leaf and seed

Uses

Gray nicker bean helps to protect the soil by avoiding soil erosion. The seeds have been used for centuries and are still used as jewelery, prayer beads, good luck charms, and worry stones. They were anciently used as standards of weight in India. The ancient African game of mancala traditionally employs gray nicker beans as game pieces. The species is sometimes planted as a hedge to prevent undesired entry into property and could be planted for dune stabilization. Preparations of the seeds and other plant parts are used to treat a large range of ailments. Seed extracts have been shown to lower blood sugar in laboratory animals and effectively suppress or cure infections of several species of round worms (4, 5, 10, 11, 12).


Harvesting Practices
There is no organised way of seed production or collection. Fallen seeds are manually collected from shrubs.

Produce and Products

Oil of commercial importance is extracted from the seeds. Seeds are used as jewelery and as standard wieght. Apart from that, various plant parts are used in ayurvedic formulations.

Processing Techniques

On a commercial scale oil is obtained from the seed by expression or by extraction by solvents the former being a common practice in the villages. There are three traditional methods of expressing oil from seed. (1) Boiling method (2) Chappa method and (3) Bullock Ghani method. In boiling method the depulped seed is boiled in water for some time and then dried. Thereafter it is roasted on a hot pan until the shell become crisp and friable. The whole seed is crushed into powder and it is boiled in water for some time after which the oil floating at the top water is separated by decantation. In the Chappa method the powdered kernel is steamed over a vessel of boiling water and pressed between the two limbs of chappa. In bullock ghani method the powdered kernel is sprinkled with little hot water and pressed in a Bullock-Ghani (7).

Other Uses
The root bark is emmenagogue, febrifuge, expectorant, anthelmintic an stemachic. It is useful in amenorrhoea, flatulence and dyspepsia. Leaves ar anthelmintic, emmenagogue and febrifuge and are useful in elephantiasis, intestinal worms, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea, fevers and pharyngodynia. Seeds are bitter, astringent, acrid, thermogenic, anodyne, anthelmintic, antiinflammatory, antiperiodic, digestive, stomachic, liver tonic, depurative, expectorant, antipyretic, contraceptive, aphrodisiac and tonic. They are useful in arthralgia, inflammations, hydrocele, cough, asthma, lucoderma, leprosy, skin diseases, dyspepsia, dysentery, colic, haemorrhoids, intestinal worms, hepatopathy, splenopathy, diabetes and intermittent fevers. Tender leaves are used in disorders of liver. Leaves and seeds are used externally for dispersing inflammatory swellings. Kernels are used as a tonic in fever. Root is diuretic and useful in gravel and stone in bladder (4, 6, 10, 11, 12, 14).
