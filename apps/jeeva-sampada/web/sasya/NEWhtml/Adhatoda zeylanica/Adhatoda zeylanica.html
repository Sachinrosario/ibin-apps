DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrub, 1.2-2.4 m high with many long opposite ascending branches. Stem terete, glabrous, bark yellowish. Leaves elliptic-lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate, 6-18 x 2.0-6.5 cm, acute or acuminate at apex, subentire or crenulate-undulate at margin, cuneate or decurrent at base, minutely puberulous when young, glabrous when mature; main nerves 10-12 pairs, venation reticulate; petioles 1.3-2.5 cm long. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Flowers in short, dense, axillary pedunculate spikes, 2.5-7.5 cm long, towards the ends of branches; peduncles 3.5-9.5 cm long, stout; bracts foliaceous, elliptic-obovate to ovate, 1.0-2.5 x 0.3-1.2 cm, apiculate, ciliate; bracteoles elliptic-oblong, 1-2 x 0.5 cm, acute with ciliolate margins. Calyx 5 lobed; lobes equal, shortly connate, imbricate, lanceolate, 0.7-1.2 x 0.3 cm, acute at apex, thick, puberulous. Corolla white or creamy, 1.0-1.5 x 1.0 cm across; tube 1.5 cm long, throat villous; lobes 5, 2-lipped; upper lip erect, ovate-oblong, 2 x 1.3 cm, shortly 2-fid; lower lip as long as upper, 3-lobed, middle lobe broad. Stamens 2. Capsules clavate, 1.5-2.2 x 0.8 cm, subacute, dark brown, 4-seeded. 
Floral and seed biology:
Seeds orbicular-oblong, 0.3-0.6 x 0.4 cm, glabrous. Fl. & Fr.: November - March.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Roots are diuretic. It is useful in bronchitis, asthma, biliousness, vomiting, sores eyes, fevers, gonorrhoea. Leaves are emmenagogue and useful in gonorrhoea. Flowers improves circulation of blood, lessen strangury and jaundice. Fruits are useful in bronchitis.
