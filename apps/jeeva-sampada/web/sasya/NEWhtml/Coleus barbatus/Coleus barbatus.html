HABIT & HABITAT
Ascending, stout, aromatic, perennial herbs, 30-60 cm high. Found in subtropical Himalaya, Deccan Peninsula ascending to about 2500 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Ascending, stout, aromatic, perennial herbs, 30-60 cm high. Stem arising from thick and fleshy root-stocks, slightly branched, wooly at base, decumbent, sub-succulent, softly white hairs, dotted with reddish-orange oil globules in between hairs. Leaves petiolate, ovate-oblong or elliptic, 5-10 x 0.5-4.5 cm, obtuse at apex, narrowed at base, crenate along amrgin, pubescent on both surfaces, lower surface gland dotted; petioles slender, 0.5-2.5 cm long.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence: Long spike like racemes ; Flower:  Showy, pedicels short decurved, hermaphrodite ; Calyx: Bilipped, upper lip widest, lower lip short, narrowly triangular, pointed teeth ; Corolla: 2-lipped, upper lip short erect and with four shallow rounded lobes and lower lip stipitately narrowed into neck, curved upwards ;Androecium:  Stamens 4, didynamous, connate below sheath, anther cells equally confluent, filaments combined at the base; Gynoecium: Ovary 4-partite, style sub-equally 2-fid; Fruit:  Nutlets smooth nearly black; Seed: 4, shining . 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in verticels, 1.5-2.5 cm long; rachis stout, densely hairy; bracts c 2.5 cm long, caduceus. Calyx c 0.8 cm long, glandular hairy outside and hairy inside, 2-lipped (1+4), lower lip with 4 teeth. Corolla bluish, bilabiate; tube geniculate; upper lip 4-lobed, with red glands; lower lip boat-shaped. Stamens 4. Nutlets ellipsoid, smooth, brown.

Flowering & Fruiting :  September- January. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome numbers - 32, 28.

Pharmacology:

Essential oil showed antimicrobial activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)j.

Coleonol showed hypotensive and spasmolytic activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)k.

Barbatusin showed inhibitory activity at 200 and 400 mg/kg against Lewis lung carcinoma and lymphocytic leukaemia P388 respectively in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)l.

Diterpenoids (I, II and III) showed blood pressure lowering and cardioactive properties. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)m.

Naphthopyrone (0.5 rug/kg, i.v.) decreased blood pressure in cats from 110 mm to 30 mm Hg in 2 ruin lasting 45 min; it had LD50 150 mg in cat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)j.

An unidentified diterpene from roots exhibited hypotensive and spasmolytic activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)k.

Coleonol lowered blood pressure of anaesthetised cats and rats and of spontaneously hypertensive rat; in small doses it had positive inotropic effect on isolated rabbit heart and on cat heart in viva, whereas large doses had depressant action on central nervous system. Colconol also exhibited nonspecific spasmolytic activity on smooth muscle of gastrointestinal tract. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)l.

Forskolin stimulated renin release from isolated perfused rat kidney two-fold at 0.1mM or three-fold at 1.0 mM; stimulation was blocked by angiotensin but not by the b-adrenoceptor antagonist, atenolol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)m.

Topical ocular application of forskolin lowered intraocular pressure (IOP) in rabbits, monkeys and human volunteers. A topical suspension of forskolin (1.0%) significantly lowered lOP (70.0%) in I hr but remained significant for at least 5 hr. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)n.

Forskolin increased force of contraction and pacemaker activity of isolatcd guinea pig atrium. It potentiated Ca2+ -dependent contraction in atrium. Forskolin lowered Na+ -K+ -ATPase and increased adenylate cyclase activities in rat heart slices. Forskolin and cardiac glycosides had additive positive inotropic effccts on guinea pig atrium. It inhibited norepinephrine -induced contractions in isolated guinea pig artery and angiotensin 11-induced contractions in guinea pig colon and aorta. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)o.

Forskolin (1.0 mM) inhibited arachidonate – and ADP-induced serotonin release in human platelets by 100.0 and 60.0% respectively. It enhanced or inhibited phosphorylation of human platelet proteins, depending on the molecular weight of protein involved. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)p.

Barbatusol (3 mg/kg, i.v.) in rats, induced potent lowering of blood pressure associated with discrete bradycardia. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)q.

Forskolin showed positive inotropic and chronotropic effects on isolated spontaneously beating guinea pig atrium. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)l.

Forskolin (0.1 mM) reduced amplitude of spontaneous contractions in myometrial strips from pregnant rats by decreasing frequency of action potential discharge without change in resting potential or camp accumulation. At 1.0 mM it abolished contraction and action potentials, hyperpolarised the membrane and increased cAMP accumulation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)m.

Forskolin (0.26-1.82 mM) produced concentration-dependent increase in tension developed (Td) and, to a lesser extent, in the maximum rate of rise in tension (dT/dt max) on isolated hemidiaphragm of rat during direct electrical stimulation. In calcium-free medium, effects of forskolin on Td and dT/dt max were weaker than in calcium- containing medium. Thus, forskolin increases isometric contraction of isolated hemidiaphragm by activating cAMP-generating system in presence of calcium. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)n.

Sustained contraction in rabbit aorta induced by norepinephrine was inhibited 10.0 nM to 10.0 mM forskolin in concentration-dependent manner. It also inhibited contraction induced by norepinephrine in Ca2+ -free solution. Forskolin inhibited increase Ca2+ influx due to norepinephrine, but not that due to high K+, Caffeine-induced transient contraction was not inhibited by 10.0 mM forskolin. It inhibited Ca2+ release induced by norepinephrine, but not that by caffeine. Forskolin increased tissue cAMP content in resting, norepinephrine-treated aorta. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)o.

In anaesthetised rabbits forskolin (10.0 mg/kg per min) increased blood flow in cerebrum and increased flow to ocardium and kidney despite decrease in mean arterial pressure. Forskolin did not alter cerebral oxygen consumption. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)p.

Forskolin at minimum concentration (2.0 to 20.0 nM) increased heart rate, left ventricular pressure and tissue level of cAMP in concentration-dependent manner in isolated perfused rat and guinea pig heart. In both species, maximal effects observed with 2.0 mM forskolin. Increase in cAMP was greater in rat heart than in guinea pig heart at any concentration. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)q.

Forskolin (1.0-100.0 mM), a direct activator of adenylate cyclase, elevated intracellular cAMP levels in dose-dependent manner; at lower concentration it markedly stimulated spontaneous PGE2 production in isolated renal arteries of dog which was the result of a cAMP-independent action of forskolin, possibly related to activation of PGE2 isomerase. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)r.

Effect of forskolin, added either before or 5 min after onset of potassium-evoked release of vasopressin from isolated neurointermediate lobes of rat, determined. Forskolin (1.0 mM) added before stimulation enhanced potassium-evoked release of vasopressin throughout two successive 5 min periods of stimulation, but a higher concentra- 11 (10.0- 80.0 mM) produced no effect during first 5 min of stimulation but inhibited release during second 5 min period. When added 5 min after onset of potassium stimulation, it (1.0-80.0 mM) reduced amount of vasopressin released during the remaining 5 min of stimulation. Forskolin produced concentration-dependent increase in cAMP during both the control and potassium stimulation periods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)s.

Intra-arterial injection of forskolin (10.0 to 333.0 mg) into cat nictitating membrane (CNM) preparation produced dose-related inhibition of contractions elicited by electrical stimulation pre- and post-ganglionic sympathetic neurons. Forskolin (0.5 mg) lowered intraocular pressure in rabbits and suppressed contraction of electrically-stimulated CNM, partly by inhibiting sympathetic neuronal function. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)t.

Single instillation of forskolin (1.0% ) showed no effect on intraocular pressure but instillations at an interval of 5 min, lowered it in normal volunteers after 1 hr. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)u.

Forskolin eye drops lowered intraocular pressure in healthy volunteers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)v.

Forskolin stimulated stomach acid secretion in isolated guinea pig parietal cells, anaes- thetised rats and in dogs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)w.

Forskolin (0.1-1.0 mg/kg, i.v.) caused a dose-dependent stimulation of acid secretion in anaesthetised rats. Higher doses were not tolerated; stimulating effect of combined infusion with histamine or carbachol on acid secretion also studied. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)x.

Forskolin induced maximum immuno-stlmulant activity at 5.0 mg/kg dose in female mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)y.

Forskolin (0.5-10.0 mM) caused hyperpolarisation and relaxation of smooth muscle cells of guinea pig taenia caeci. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)z.

Inhibitory effects of forskolin on nerve conduction determined in de-sheathed sciatic nerve preparation of frog. It (2.5 mM) caused 50.0% decrease in amplitude of action potentials when nerve conduction was partially blocked by tetradotoxin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a1.

Forskolin at 25.78 mg increased percentage of motile spermatozoa in bull. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a2.

Forskolin induced partial de aggregation of ADP- or collagen-aggregated human platelets in vitro, which was assumed to be due to increase in platelet cAMP by forskolin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a3.

1,9- dideoxyforskolin showed anti-inflammatory activity in carrageenin-induced rat paw oedema (ED50 2.2 mg/kg, i.p.). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a4.

Internal infusion of forskolin (0.2mg/kg/min) in anaesthetized dog increased renal blood flow (RBF) and glomerular filteration rate (GFR) to same degree. It also increased urine flow and urinary sodium excertion. Increase in RBF and GFR may be due to preferential action of forskolin in dilating afferent arteriole. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.

Effect of forskolin on teratogenicity of ephedrine in embryonal chick heart studied; 4-day chick embryos administered forskolin (1.0 nmol) together with ephedrine (0.5 to 5.0 m mol) were examined for malforamtion on day of incubation. Frequency of malformed embryos increased three-fold. Forskolin (1.0 nmol) alone did not induce cardiac malformation, and increase in camp through stimulation of adenylate cyclase by forskolin may be associated with potentiation of ephedrine – induced cardiovascular malformation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)j.
