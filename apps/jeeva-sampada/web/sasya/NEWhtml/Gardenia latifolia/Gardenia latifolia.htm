Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Small deciduous ornamental tree. Found throughout the greater part of India, mostly in dry forests. It is sometimes met with as an epiphyte.

Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small deciduous tree, with divergent branches forming a rounded head; bark smooth, pale green, flaking off in small round pieces; young parts glabrous but coated with a resinous exudation. Leaves opposite or 3-nately whorled crowded towards the ends of the branches, subsessile, broadly elliptic or obovate, rounded or very shortly and obtusely acuminate at the apex, apple green above, paler beneath, glabrous or more or less pubescent; main nerves 10-20 pairs; stipules adnate to the base of the petiole, connate, forming a thin truncate tube at first enclosing the young leaves, but after the fall of the leaf, separating at the base and forming a loose ring round the stem.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers solitary or 2-nate, subsessile, white changing to yellow, fragrant. Calyx densely pubescent; limb broadly tubular, slightly dilated at the apex; teeth 5-9, lanceolate-subulate, unequal, recurved and with ciliate margins. Corolla tube striate, densely pubescent outside; lobes 5-9, obliquely obovate-oblong, obtuse.

Fruit nearly globose, without ribs, appressedly pubescent when young, pale-green and speckled, surmounted by the calyx-limb, epicarp dry, fleshy; endocarp bony, yellow, polished within, 3-valved. Seeds numerous, small, flattened, pale-brown, in purplish-gray pulp. Placentas 4-5.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:Wood used as fuel.

Wood: Wood creamy white to pale yellowish white turning to pale yellowish brown on exposure, sometimes with darker streaks, with no distinct heartwood, somewhat lustrous with smooth feel, moderately hard, strong, moderately heavy, average weight 755 kg/m3 (47 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content. Specific gravity 0.85, 54 lb./ c ft., fine and even textured with straight grain. Easy to saw and work, durable.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings distinct, delimited by dark latewood fibres. Pores small to very small, not visible to the eye, distinct under the lens, numerous to very numerous, mostly solitary or in short radial multiple of 2-3; vessel lines indistinct. Soft tissues diffuse, but usually indistinct, not visible to the eye, or difficult to make out even under the hand lens. Rays not visible to the eye, but seen under the lens as fine, numerous closely-placed lines.

Wood has been recommended as a substitute for boxwood for engravers’ use. Substitute for boxwood. Suitable for making combs and turnery articles, light furniture, camp beds, tobacco jars, shuttles, mallet heads.
