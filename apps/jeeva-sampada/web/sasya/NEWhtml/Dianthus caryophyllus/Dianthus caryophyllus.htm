DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 An erect perennial herb with jointed stems, Stems branched, hard, almost woody below. Leaves thick, linear and opposite. Flowers terminal, long-pedicelled, rose, purple or white, with a fragrance reminiscent of cloves. The rich spicy perfume of carnation flowers has been developed as a result of cultivation. Wild forms are practically scentless. It is noteworthy that some highly specialized horticultural forms with bright colored flowers are almost devoid of fragrance. About 2,000 garden varieties, grouped by florists according to size, colour and markings of flowers, are distinguished; some with dwarf and close habit are suited for rock gardening. The plant is well adapted for cultivation in sand or aggregate to which the necessary plant materials are supplied by sub-irrigation

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers are bracteate, peidcellate, complete hermaphrodite, pentamerous, hypogynous, cyclic. Calyx: sepals 5, gamosepalous, tubular, valvate, persistant and green. Corolla: petals 5, polypetalous, longer than sepals. Androecium: stamens 10, polyandrous, anthers oblong, filament slender and of equal length. Gynoecium: Bicarpellary, syncarpous, superior, free-central placentation, styles 2, long and entire, stigmas 2 and simple.

Phenology :

Pollinators:

Seed Biology :
Fruit a capsule and the seeds are endospermic, embryo straight.


Seed Germination:
Carnation plants may be raised from seeds sown thinly in well-drained pans during October. The pans are watered to keep the soil just moist. When 2 inches, high seedlings are transplanted 6-9 in. apart in prepared beds. If required, they may be transferred to pots containing equal parts of loam, vegetable mould and decayed cow dung with a little sand. When 6 in. high, the plants are chopped to stimulate side growth. The calcium requirement of the plant is high and it is usual to apply ground limestone or chalk in early spring.

The commonest method of propagating carnations, however, is by cuttings, which are best obtained from the central part of flowering stems. Cuttings (2-4 in. long) root easily when inserted into clean sand and are ready for potting in 4-5 weeks. Fresh sand must be used for successive batches of cuttings. Multiplication by layering may be carried out in pots, or shoots may be bent into the soil directly. Usually a joint half-way between the commencement of a shoot and its tip is chosen for making the cut. Layers may be separated after 2 months.

Cultivation:
Succeeds in a sunny position in most good soils tolerating a pH range from 6 to 8 but disliking acid soils. A very tolerant plant, succeeding in the salt laden air and strong winds of the coast as well as in the sulphur polluted air of cities. This species is not hardy in the colder areas of the country, it tolerates temperatures down to about -10°c. A very ornamental plant, the carnation is widely cultivated in the flower garden and is grown commercially in France for its essential oil. There are many named varieties. The flowers of the species have a rich clove-like perfume, though almost no scent is present in the orange and yellow-flowering cultivars. The flowers are very attractive to butterflies and moths. Plants are prone to mildew or leaf spot in humid climates.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:
The major insect pests of carnation include aphids, red spider, mite, thrips and tortrix moth. Insecticides such as HETP, Azobenzene, nicotine sulphate and DDT are helpful in controlling the pests.

Diseases:
Carnation plants are susceptible to a few fungal diseases. Spraying by Bordeaux mixture has been recommended as a control measure and thorough sterilization of soil checks infection. Diseases cause heavy damage usually in the third year, and it is usual to clear the crop after 2 years to obviate the onset of diseases.

Processing Techniques:
The fresh flowers have a rich clove-like perfume.
Carnations are cultivated on a large scale in France, Holland, Italy and Germany for their flowers. In certain parts of France and Holland, the flowers are also used for the extraction of perfume. Only light coloured flowers are useful for this purpose. The flowers are harvested after they remain exposed to bright sunshine for a few hours during the sunny months, when they contain the maximum amount of essential oil.

The perfume is extracted almost exclusively by solvent extraction. A solid extract (concrete) is obtained in 0.23-0.29% yield by extraction with petroleum ether. The concrete which has a high wax content is subsequently treated with alcohol to remove the in-odorous matter. The pure extract (absolute) thus obtained (yield, 9-12% of the concrete) is finally steam-distilled to obtain the volatile oil. Carnation absolute is used in sophisticated perfumes. Many of the carnation oils available in the market are compounded from synthetics rounded off with the natural perfume. The flowers are considered to be cardiotonic, diaphoretic and alexiteric. The whole plant is employed as vermifuge in China.

Other Uses :
  1. An essential oil is obtained from the flowers[46, 171]. It is used in perfumery. 500kg of flowers produce 100g of oil[61]. The flowers are harvested when they are fully open in the morning, preferably after 3 hours exposure to sunlight[238]. The flower heads are dried and used in pot-pourri, scented sachets and cosmetic products[238, 268]. The plant is quite rich in saponins. The leaves can be simmered in water and this water can then be used as a soap for cleaning the skin, clothes etc[201].
  2. Carnation flowers are an aromatic, stimulant herb that has been used in tonic cordials in the past to treat fevers, though this use is now obsolete. It is traditionally prescribed in European herbal medicine to treat coronary and nervous disorders. The flowers are considered to be alexiteric, antispasmodic, cardiotonic, diaphoretic and nervine.

 Miscellaneous Information
Cultivars:
There are some named forms for this species, but these have been developed for their ornamental value and not for their other uses. Unless you particularly require the special characteristics of any of these cultivars, we would generally recommend that you grow the natural species for its useful properties. We have, therefore, not listed the cultivars in this database[K].
