DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A strongly aromatic, perennial, fleshy herbs. Stem hairy, erect, fleshy, 30-90 cm long. Leaves opposite, shortly petiolate, broadly ovate or cordate, 2.5-8 x 4-6 cm, acute at apex, serrate at amrgin, fleshy, tomentose
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Long spike like racemes ; Flower:  Showy, pedicels short, hermaphrodite ; Calyx: Bilipped, upper lip widest, lower lip short, with pointed teeth ; Corolla: 2-lipped, upper lip short erect and with four shallowed rounded lobes and lower lip stipitately narrowed into neck, curved upwards ;Androecium:  Stamens 4, didynamous, connate below sheath, anther cells equally confluent, filaments combined at the base; Gynoecium: Ovary 4-partite, style sub-equally 2-fid; Fruit:  Nutlets black; Seed: 4, shining . 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowers purple in close whorls on terminal racemes. Calyx tube unequal, bilabiate, pubescent. Corolla pale-purple. 2-lipped; upper lip broad; lower lip long projecting. Nutlets 4, elliptic, smooth.

Flowering & Fruiting :  December-March. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=32

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Leaves have a specific action on the bladder and urinary diseases, vaginal discharge. Juice mixed with sugar is used in colic in children. Decoction of leaves is  given for asthma, chronic coughs, chronic bronchitis, epilepsy.


Chemistry:
Cirsimaritin and b-sitosterol-b-D-glucoside identified in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Oleanolic, 2a,3a-dihydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic, crategolic, pomolic, euscaphic, tormentic, ursolic and 2a,3a,19a,23-tetrhydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic acids isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Salvigenin, 6-methoxy-genkwanin, quercetin, chrysoeriol, luteolin, apigenin, eriodictyol and taxifolin from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Thymol (41.30%), carvacrol (13.25%), 1,8-cineole (5.45%), eugenol (4.40%) abd b-caryophyllene (4.20%) isolated from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Estimation of carvacrol (40.40%), limonene (10.58%), thymol (8.12%), eugenol (7.35%), ethyl salicylate (5.50%), g-terpinene (3.58%), b-selinene (1.38%), p-cymene (1.15%), a-pinene (0.46%), b-caryophyllene (0.43%), myrcene (0.17%), b-pinene (0.11%) and an unidentified phenolic fraction (4.25%) in essential oil (0.1%); p-cymene, terpinen-4-ol, thymol, caryophyllene, verbenone and tert-butylanisole detected in essential oil of Pakistani plant by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Essential oil was analyzed and found to consist mainly phenolic compounds with carvaerol (72%) being the major compound, along with (Z)-1,3-hexadiene (0.1%),(Z)-3-hexanol (0.6%), (E,Z)-a-farnesene (0.2%), (E,E)-a-farnesene (0.2%) and a-muurolene (0.2%). (Prudent et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 17563s.

Pharmacology:

Essential oil was found to have activity against vibrio cholera with a MIC of 0.125 mg/ml. (Prudent et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 17563s.

Antioxidant is prepared from Coleus extract. The Coleus extract is manufactured by extraction of Coleus with solvents such as MeOH. Manufacture of antioxidants from C. forskholii and C. blumei was shown. (Udagawa et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 15553x.
