Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT

A medium-sized tree attaining the height of 30 to 40ft, found in the sub-Himalayas forest up to 3000 ft altitude, also in Madhya Pradesh and South India.


Availability :  
Found in Sub-Himalayan forest from Jumna eastwards, also in Central and Southern India, Chittagong, and Burma. Distributed in Bengal, throughout greater parts of India, Andamans, Cocos islands, wet zone forests of Western Ghats and in Eastern and Northern India.

Status : 
Occasionally cultivated.  Rare in dry deciduous forests.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

Tree has a straight cylindrical bole, generally 20-25 ft. high and 6 ft in girth. Bark grayish-brown, exfoliating in large irregular flakes; leaves imparipinnate, often covered with red galls; flowers yellow or greenish-white, polygamous, drupes yellowish-green to black, globose (ca 0.3 in. diameter) fleshy containing 3-4 stones.

Seed Biology :

Seedling Germination :
The drupes fall to the ground in the rainy season and germination takes place in the next rainy season. Some seeds germinate after two years. Most of the seeds germinate early in the rains of the following year. Transplanting is done with one and a half to two months old seedlings.

People in Aravalli hills in India obtain large size cuttings of Garuga pinnata and plant it just before the on-set of monsoon. This is a very quick and reliable method of regenerating livelihood trees.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Wood, fruit, bark

Uses

  • The bark and especially the red leaves and their red galls are used for tanning in many parts of the India
  • Gum-Resin: This tree yields a greenish-yellow, translucent exudation in small mamilliform masses, having a mild terebinthinate odour and taste.  It has been generally regarded by Indian writers as a true gum amongst others, classifying it with Gum acacia.   In Bombay it is employed as a medicine.

Chemistry
Gum-resin: Only a small part of it is soluble in rectified spirits, causing a slight turbidity; in water it rapidly disintegrates, forming a tolerably thick mucilage, in which globules of ole-resin, may be seen with the microscope.  The insoluble proportion is amorphous, flaky, and white; after its removal the mucilage is precipitated milk-white, by rectified spirit.

Other Uses
  1.  Timber is also used for planking, canoes, boxes, drums, cabinet work and house buildings, and fuel wood, gives charcoal of excellent quality. Being liable to insect attacks it does not possess good reputation.It may also be tried for boards. wood is also used as a fuel. Furniture (heartwood); boarding (sapwood).
  2. Pulp suitable for making brown wrapping paper.
  3.  Drupes are eaten raw, cooked or pickled, possess cooling and digestive properties.
  4. Juice of stem is used as a remedy for the opacity of conjunctiva.
  5. Leaf juice along with honey is given for asthma.
  6. A decoction of the roots used in Philippines for pulmonary affections. 
  7. Leaves and shoots used as fodder, bark and leaf galls are used for tanning.
  8. Being fire resistant and easy to propagate, it is valuable for afforestation purposes
  9. The fruits are eaten raw or pickled , but are strongly acid; the fruit is considered a semi-medicinal article of diet.
