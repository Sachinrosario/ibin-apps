Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
An hardy perennial everreen shrub, native to the Pacific Islands and Polynasia. In the Pacific Islands kava does best in the cool, moist highlands up to 1000 feet above sea level where the daytime summer temperatures are between 80 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit. It may be grown in southern Florida, but elsewhere it requires greenhouse temperatures.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An evergreen shrub, it grows to a height of 2m with a spread of 2m. The stem is erect and branching; the leaves are heart shaped, green and acuminate; the flowers are whitish, male and female flowers occur on separate plants, occurring in short, axillary spikes; the fruit are small drupes. The stem is dictiotomous, that is, two-forked, with spots. The upper rhizome is the part of the plant that is used and is starchy with the faint pleasant odor with a pungent bitter taste.

Floral and seed biology:

Cultivation: It prefers rich soil with good drainage and moist, well-composted soils in a protected, partially shaded position. It does well on stony ground. The best crops are grown on virgin soil. If two consecutive crops are raised on the same soil the second crop will be poor. Plants should be spaced about 6 feet apart either way. This furnishes about 1200 plants per acre and it is drought and frost tender. The plant rarely produces seeds and is generally propagated by cuttings of the firm wood.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:
The medicinally used parts are the peeled, dried, cut rhizome, which has normally been freed from the roots and the fresh rhizome with the roots.


Harvesting Practices:
The main rootstock begins just below the surface of the ground and continues for 2 feet or more. It gets to be 3 to 5 inches thick after 2.5 to 4 years growth, and may weigh 12 to 16 lb. Rootstocks usually reach maximum growth at about 6 years, but the older the plant the more potent it will be. These may be dug and used fresh or dried in the sun. These may be dug and used fresh or dried in the sun. The lower stems are also active. Before drying, the rootstocks and lower stems should be scraped of their outer coating and cut into pieces weighing 0.5 to 1.5 oz. each. Drying reduces weight to about 1/5. The tough radiating roots may be dug and used fresh at any time but the plant should be given time to generate new roots.

Chemistry:

Other uses:
It acts as Diuretic,Urinary antiseptic, Circulatory stimulant, Antispasmodic, Analgesic, Anaesthetic, Anaesthetic effect in the gastric mucosa & bladder mucosa, Mental stimulant in small doses depressant in large, Reubefacient, Antifungal. It can be applied in Urinary prostatic and vaginal infections, (Leucorrhoea, gonorrhea, vaginitis, enuresis), Rheumatic conditions associated with urinary troubles, scanty urination, or water retention, Joint and muscle pain, Traditionally as a tonic in debility, Anxiety, insomnia, and nervous tension and Conditions associated with skeletal muscle spasm. Kava root has been found valuable in the treatment of Neuralgia, particularly of the trifacial nerve, toothache, earache, ocular pain, reflex neuralgia, anorexia, gonorrhoea both acute and chronic, vaginitis, leucorrhoea, nocturnal incontinence and other ailments of the genitourinary tract. Being a local anaesthetic it relieves pain and has an aphrodisiac effect; it has also an antiseptic effect on the urine.
