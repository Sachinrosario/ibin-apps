Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Annual herb of the mint family native to E. Asia, it is a traditional crop of China, India, Japan, Korea, Thailand, and other Asian countries. Found almost throughout the Himalayas up to an altitude of 3,500 m. and in the hills of Assam and Bihar. Found growing in sunny open fields, roadsides, waste and open woodlands. Perilla prefers light to medium moist well-drained and rich soil (1).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Perilla has deep purple stems and purple to red tinted leaves that last till summer and fall. It is a very aromatic plant, with a strong minty smell. Growing up to 150 cm in height, the stems are square, reddish-purple and branching. The leaves are large, up to 6 inches in diameter, petioled, opposite, ovate and serrate, edges ruffled or curly, dark green tinted red to purple (especially on the underside) and hairy. Sometimes the leaves are so large and red that they remind one of a slice of raw beef, hence they are named as beefsteak plant. The best diagnostic characteristics of perilla are the net-patterned testa of the nutlets. Perilla superficially resembles basil and coleus. Dry skeletons of the plants persist into the spring, their racemes retain dry papery calyces when the purple to white flowers have fallen away (2, 3, 4).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
The flower spikes are long, up to 10 inches and born in the leaf axils. Flowers are small about 1/4-inch long and tubular, white in axillary and terminal racemes or pink to lavender and numerous (2).

Breeding System :
Plant perpetuates through Self-pollination and yields well.

Back to Top

 

Phenology :
Flowering takesplace from July to October. Perilla has been used by plant physiologists to investigate flower induction. Long nights induce flowering, but different accessions have different critical night lengths. Plants become photosensitive at the fourth leaf pair stage. Flowering starts 18 to 20 days after the start of long nights. After 30 long nights, plants will bloom until they die, regardless of subsequent day length (3, 4).

Pollinators:
Perilla is a very attractive plant and attracts butterflies.

Back to Top

 

Seed Biology :
Nutlets are commonly called as seeds, seeds are small, rounded, pale brown with reticulate markings. 1000 seeds weighing 0.8-5.3 g depending on variety. Seeds have less viabilty. At room temperature, the seeds looses their viability within a year, but lowered temperature or humidity improves storage life dramatically (1, 3).

Seedling Germination :
Seeds germinates readily in 10-14 days, if sown in the following spring. The optimum germination temperature range is 20°C-22°C. Cool temperatures or dry conditions during germination are likely to have a detrimental effect on germination and seedling emergence.

Cultivation:

Perilla requires an equitable climate to grow well, it is unsuitable for areas that experience out-of-season frosts. Warm temperatures, long day length and adequate moisture are required for good vegetative growth, and short days for flower production. Most soils considered suitable for horticulture will support perilla. Sandy soils rich in organic matter are considered optimum. Perilla is an annual plant propagated by seed. Plants flower and seed in late summer-autumn.

The most common production method for green leaf perilla is under cover in glass or plastic houses. Leaf production occurs during long days with perilla quickly running to seed when short days commence in autumn. With artificial heating, lighting, irrigation and successive planting it is possible to produce perilla continuously. A less expensive production method is semi-protected planting. Seedlings are grown in heated beds in a plastic house in late winter, transplanted in spring and harvested during summer. Using this method, plants are protected from wind and rain when growing conditions are marginal. However, without artificial lighting, plants quickly bolt to flower and seed in autumn. The least expensive production method involves seedling production in unheated beds in early spring followed by transplanting in late spring. Fertiliser application depends on residual soil fertility levels and soil type. Annual dressings of an NPK fertiliser would, in most cases, be prudent (1, 5).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

Cutworm, mites, aphids and leaf-eating caterpillars are all pests of perilla. A range of chemicals can be used to control these pests.

Diseases:

Reports suggest that fungi including damping off, downy mildew and rust, may also cause problems (5).

Economic Potential and Uses

The crude oil is deep yellow or greenish, where as the refined oil is light yellow in colour. The oil has pleasant smell and resembles linseed oil in appearance as well as in composition. The seeds of perilla contain 31 to 51% of a drying oil. All drying oils leave a hard protective surface when dry. Perilla oil has been used as a drying oil in paints, varnishes, linoleum, printing ink, lacquers, and for protective waterproof coatings on cloth. It has also been largely used for edible purposes in the country of origin and it is also used as fuel. Perilla stand oils have a better gloss, exterior durability and are more water resistant than linseed stand oil. The refined oil is useful in the preparation of alkyd resins on account of its drying properties and its polymerization at a high temperature. Seed cake after oil extraction is high in protien and fibre and has proved to be fairly palatable to animals.

The volatile oil from dried leaves of perilla is used sparingly as a flavouring agent in table sauces, confectionary and dentifrices. It possesses a strong antiseptic action and is used as an antimildew agent. One of the aldehyde isomers is 2,000 times as sweet as sugar and four to eight times as sweet as saccharin, it is used as a tobacco sweetener. Perilla alcohol, prepared from perilla aldehyde, is used in fragrances, and has legal food status in the United States and Europe. Volatile oils of the plant are also used in aroma therapy and for perfume (2, 3, 4, 6, 7).

Uses

Perilla is edible and medicinal. The entire plant is very nutritious, packed with vitamins and minerals. The leaves have a very pleasant sweet taste and are used as a spice, cooked as potherbs or fried, and combined with fish, rice, vegetables and soups. It is also chopped and combined with gingerroot, then added to stir-fries and salads in many Asian countries. In Japan the foliage also provides a red (anthocyanin) food colouring and specialized red-leaved perilla varieties are used in the production of pickled plums. In addition to food colouring, perilla adds a antimicrobial agent to pickled foods. The seed heads can be collected and dried for use in arrangements, potpourris and wreaths. The crushed plant also makes an effective insecticide. The seeds are eaten by people and used as bird seed. The plants are grown as ornamentals (3, 4).

Harvesting Practices
The fruits do not ripen simultaneously, those at the top being generally the first to ripen. The ripe fruits may be easily shed and so harvesting requires much care and attention. The fruits are usually harvested at the end of September and beginning of October. After the fruits are fully mature, the plants are cut near the ground and either tied in small bundles or spread out loosely on the ground for drying. They are thoroughly dried and the bundles are threshed on ground. This breaks the capsules and loosens the seeds (7).

Produce and Products

Oil is obtained from the seeds, which is of industrial importance. Essential oil is extracted from the leaves. Seed cake after extraction is used as livestock feed. Seeds are edible and known to have medicinal properties.

Processing Techniques

The seeds are crushed using a stone roller and shelling is done manually followed by winnowing or the perilla oil is also recovered by expression or solvent extraction of the roasted seeds. The raw oil contains mucilaginous matter and is refined by coagulating the break by heating, allowing to settle and finally filtering. The oil may be refined also by alkali treatment (8, 9).

Other Uses
There are many scientifically proven medicinal uses of Perilla. It has been used for centuries in Oriental medicine as an antiasthmatic, antibacterial, antidote, antimicrobial, antipyretic, antiseptic, antispasmodic, antitussive, aromatic, carminative, diaphoretic, emollient, expectorant, pectoral, restorative, stomachic and tonic. The plant constituents confirm these uses in alternative medicine and ongoing studies have revealed that this plant is useful in curing many cancers as well as various other diseases and disorders. It is a pungent, aromatic, warming herb. An infusion of the plant is useful in the treatment of asthma, colds, cough and lung afflictions, influenza prevention, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, food poisoning and allergic reactions (especially from seafood), and to restore health and balance. The stems are a traditional Chinese remedy for morning sickness and restless fetus in pregnancy, though some say the herb should be avoided by pregnant women.

Medicinal tea: To ¼ cup dry herb add 1 pint of boiling water, allow to steep 10 to 15 min. Drink throughout the day for colds, flu, sore throat, and congestion. Also can be boiled and the steam inhaled to clear the sinuses (2, 3, 4, 10).
