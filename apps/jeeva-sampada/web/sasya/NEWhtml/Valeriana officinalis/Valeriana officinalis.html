DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  Root stocks short, suberect, and hardly thicker than the stem. Radical leaves absent in flowering time or pinnate, lateral leaves near the root are often ovate, entire or slightly toothed; cauline leaves several, all pinnate; lobe narrowly oblong. Basal leaves long-stalked, leaflets 6-10 pairs. Flowers white or flesh coloured, upper bracts 2.5 mm long, oblong-linear, shorter than the fruit. Petals united so as to form a tube ending above in 5 lobes. Fruit glabrous.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Roots are used as stimulant and antipasmodic. It is useful in hysteria, epilepsy, cholera and alied affections. It is also used in the advanced stages of fevers, low asthmic and inflammation.


Chemistry:

·        A HPLC method for valepotriares, Valerenic acid and its derivatives was developed. Retention times and UV max. for Ref valerian compounds separated by HPLC  are  reported. Results of selection   experiments with respect to essential oil valerenic acid and valepotriates of V. officinalis are given. (Bos and Woerdenbag, 1996). CA. 127:159017y

·        A HPLC method combined with diode array detection is described by which the valerian constituents valtrate, isovalterate, acevaltrate, didrovaltrate, isovaleroxyhydroxydidrovaltrate, valeric acid, hydroxyvalerenic acid and acetoxyvalerenic acid, as well as the valepotriate decomposition products baldrinal and homobaldrinal, can be separated and identified simultaneously. (Bos, Rein et al., 1996).

·        Valeracetate a new guaiane type sesquiterpene and, 3, previously known sesquiterpenes have been isolated from Valerian officinalis and their structures determined spectroscopically. (Tori et al., 1996). MAPA 9604-2453.

·        A new valerenane sesquiterpenoid, Me valerenate (I) was found in the essential oil from underground parts of Valeriana officinalis. Spectral data of ME valernate which was synthesized from valerenic acid are given. (Bos Rein et al., 1997). 128:17813400

·        The roots and Rhizomes of Valeriana officinalis samples yielded 0.22-1.55 percent (v/w) essential oil on a dry weight basis. Oil components present in all samples were bornylacetate (2-36 percent), Myrtenyl acetate (trace-9 percent), Valerenic acid (0.3 –3 percent) and one as yet unidentified tertiary sesquiterpene alcohol.  (Bos et al., 1997) MAPA 9805-2980.

·        Sesquiterpenes from Valeriana officinalis in pharmaceutical preparations have been determined. Several liquid and solid compound preparations containing extracts of this plant were investigated. The quantitative determination of flavonoids and sesquiterpenes was performed by two HPLC methods suitable for routine analysis. The determination of liquid formulations detected 0.03-0.86 mg sesquiterpenes per 1g of the sample while the solid dosage forms contained 0.16–1.85 mg sesquiterpenes per unit. (Bookstaller and Schmidt, 1997). CA: 127:19556910.

·        The essential oil components of Valeriana officinalis were analyzed. Gas chromatography / mass spectrometry and identified gas chromatograph retention index were used. 41 components were separated, 36 of them were identified, which accounted for 94.7% of the total peak area. The main constituents were bornyl acetate, camphene, 3-thujene b-pinene and sabinol. (Wu et al., 1999).

·        To investigate the variations of active compounds between species, varieties and individuals of Valeriana cultivated under the same environmental condition the contents of valerenic acid derivatives and valepotriates in rhizomes and roots of different plant material were analyzed by HPLC method. Different species or varieties of Valeriana yielded 11.65-0.15 mg/g of valerenic acid derivatives, and 1.81-0.03 mg/g of valepotriates. The variation of micropropagated plants was much less than the seed propagated plants.  The ratio of mean values between valerenic acid derivatives, and valepotriates was similar in all groups (3< ratio < 8) except one group of micropropagated plants (ratio > 20).  (Gao and Bjork, 2000).  C.A. 133:14597b.

·        Valerinic acid was isolated from dry underground parts of Valeriana officinal (Rhizoma cum radicibus Vaerianeae) to obtain a standard sample for evaluating the quality of valerian preparations. The technology employs smaller amounts of both initial material and solvents, the sample handling procedures are simpler and the product yield is higher. (Komarova et al., 2000). CA 135.185561f

·        Headspace sorptive Extraction. (HSSE), is here applied, to the headspace sampling of medicinal and aromatic plants. The analyte partion coefficient between HSSE- PDMS stir bar and sample headspace (K) the concentration factor (CF), the reproducibility and the minnium recoverable amount were determined by analyzing standard solution of high volatility C5-C7 compounds with different polarities and structures. Valeriana officinalis L. was analyzed by HSSE-GC with PDMS stirbars, and their capacity was compared with those of S-HS and HS-SPME with different fibers. HSSE showed very high concentration capability with both standard and sample components. (Bicchi, 2000). CA 134:2181u.

·        A process for preparing a pharmaceutical active extract of the root of a plant of the family valerianacae specification valeriana officinal L. is described. This process comprises the adding the roots to an alcoholic extract solvent to from a mixture where in the extraction solvent comprises between approximately 50% to 100% in remainder of water and heating the mixture to a temp of  approximately 80>C for a period of at least two hours. By this process valernic acids is obtained in the extract and has a content of valepotriates and valepotriate degradation products that is substantially reduced with respect to the content of valerenic acids in the roots. ( Michael  and Basu, 2001). CA 134:136667r.

Pharmacology:

PHARMACOLOGY

·        The study compares the effects of three different extracts of Valeriana officinalis L.  roots on the release of GABA in rat brain cortex synaptosomes. Both  the aqueous and the hydro alocoholic extracts of valerian stimulated the release of [H] GABA in synapotosomes, where the ethanolic extract did not show  a significant effect on the release thus suggesting that activity the activety  remained perferntially in the polar fraction  of the extract using HPLC the  authors have found that the amount of GABA present in the aqueous extract was comparable to that hydrocholic extract and that the  amino  acid  was  absent  in the ethanolic extract the GABA in the extract  is responsible for the  valerian- induced [3H]GABA release observed. In synaptosomes.(Ferreira et al.,1996). CA 125:185690r.

·         A novel method of inhibiting at least one symptomol of Restless Leg Syndrome (RLS) and its related disorders. Including disorder such as periodic limb movements in sleep (PLMS) and periodic limb movement disorder. The patient is given pharmaceutically effective amount of Valeriana, preferably an extract of Valeriana.  The method may also be used to treat a host in order to diminish undesired limb movements Valerenic Acid. (Hoffman and Loullis, 1999). CA 133:286474 u.

·        Two multiple crossover studies were performed on adult female subjects to screen for acute sedative of plant extracrs of Valeriana Officinalis. Extract administered in both studies displayed an increase of power in the d and q band a decrease in the b band. Sedating effects of plant extracts could be identified by quantitative EEG anal and by self-assessment instruments. (Schulz et al., 1998). CA 130:144041q.

·         The use of valerian extracts as well as isovaleramide, isovaleric acid and its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters and substituted amides and other valerian related compounds in combination with NSAIDS to exhibit clinically significant pharmacological properties which implicate a treatment for acute muscular aches, strains and sprains which occur from a localized, external insult to a particular muscle or muscle group outside of or peripheral to the CNS. (Artman and Balandrin, 1999). CA 131:2046265.

·        The possible effect of the aqueous ethanolicextract   Valeriana officinalis on melatonin receptors of cryosectioned human cerebelium was investigated.  One fraction of each HPLC separation predominantly inhibits binding of radio labelled melatonin to its receptor. The inhibitory capacities of the positive fractions correlate with the concentrations as suggested by the corresponding peak areas of the chromatogram. (Pietz et al., 1996). MAPA 9803-1433.

·        Sedative properties of V. officinalis have been known since 2000 years. In addition to this indication it is also used as an antispasmodic. Modern biochemical receptor assays have shown the interaction of total extracts and some purified fractions of V. officinalis with the inhibitory neurotransmitter gamma, aminobutyric acid (GAMA).  (Morazzoni, Bombardelli, 1995). MAPA 9505-3003.

·        An ethanol total extract of the roots of V. officinalis in dose equivalent to 0.5-800 mg valerian root/kg b.w.i.p. was tested in male mice  for possible neuropharmacological  efficacy and in this respect compared with diazepam and  haloperidol. The extract did not modify spontaneous motility, nociception or body temperature and did  not produce palperbral ptosis. However it was anticonvulsant against picrotoxin with an  ED50   between  4.5 and 6 mg/kg and it prolonged thipental anaesthesia. After fractionation of crude extract, the antipicrotoxin activity was present mainly in the methylene chloride fraction. (ED50=0.25 mg/kg). Pure valerenic acid (12.5 mg/kgb.w.i.p.) also exerted an antipicrotoxin effect. (Hiller and Zetler, 1996). MAPA 9605-2779.

·        Only 4 placebo-controlled studies in patients with sleeping problems are available on the best known “herbal sedative“throughout the world, Valeriana officinalis.(Schlz et al., 1996). MAPA 9803-1438.

·         Valeriana Officinal is a phytopharmacon with sedative effect. The result indicates that Valeriana offcinalis extract Li 156 is able to displace Mel completely from its own binding site. This effect was dose-dependent. Data indicate that Valeriana officinalis  acts on the  Mel-receptor and that  this effect is not due  to valerenic acid. Valeriana officinalis acts as a sedative interacting with Mel- receptor only. (Fauteck et al., 1996-97). MAPA 9803-1490.

·        Sedative effect of phytotherapeutics containing valerian root (valeriana officinalis) was tested in 50 male alcoholic abusers suffering from insomnia. The preparation showed a significant improvement in sleep quality over placebo. (Widy- Tysziewicz, and Schiminda, 1997). MAPA 9803-1450: CA 127:215038v.

·        Valeriana officinalis   showed significant sedative & anticonvulsive effect. (Xu, et al., 1997). CA 129: 325962r.

·        Jatamansi (Nardstachys jatamansi) when administered to 22 hypertensive patients with no lipid derangements shows a symptomatic improvement with fall in blood-pressure (both systolic and  diastolic) Various symptoms viz Anidra, Dourbalya, Shirahshoola, Kshubadhata, Shrama and  Bhrama were graded as per their severity and recorded. A shift in symptom graded as per their severity and recorded. A shift in symptom grade scores was observed after 3 months of treatement i.e. patients showed a generalized improvement. (Bharti ; et.al., 1999). MAPA 2000-06-3338.

·        Prasham contains  Nardos tychys, jatamansi, Vitis vinifera and other herbs. It gives freshness and removes fategue  along with  improving memory and power of concentration. (Suru, and Sardeshmukh, 1995). MAPA 9005-2731.

·        A Valerian lemon balm combination to treat minor sleep disorders was tested. The preparation proved to be  well tolerated by  most  subjects (93% of the participants in the  Valerian / lemon balm group  and 91% in the  placebo group). There was no serious adverse events. The valerian /lemon balm group revealed siginificant higher quality of sleep (33%) compared to the placebo group (9%), b-value =0.04. In conclusion these results indicate that this Valerian/lemon balm formulation is well tolerated. (Cerny and Schmid, 1999). CA 132117445j.

·         A pilot study with a fixed extract combination Ze 91019 of valerian and hop was conducted in 30 patients suffering from mild-moderate non organic insomnia. The diagnosis was confirmed by polysomnographic standard examinations. The patients were treated with two tablets in the evening. Each tablet contains 250 mg valerian extract and 60 mg hop extract. No adverse events were observed. (Fussel et al., 2000). CA 134275609m.

·        V. Officinalis showed pharmacodynamic sedative activity. (Zhang, and Yao, 2000). CA 134:32833a.
