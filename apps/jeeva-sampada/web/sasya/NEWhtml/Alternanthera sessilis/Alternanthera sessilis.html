HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennial or sometimes annual, erect or prostrate or ascending or sometimes creeping herbs, up to 80 cm long. It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem 4-angular, 2-5 mm thick, rooting at nodes; internodes c 10 cm long, decreasing upwards. Leaves variable, linear-lanceolate to oblong, ovate or obovate-spathulate, 0.8-9.0 x 0.2-2.0 cm, obtuse or shortly acuminate at apex, cuneate to attenuate at base, entire or obscurely denticulate at margin, glabrous or thinly pilose; petioles c 5 mm long. Flowers in axillary, solitary or 2-5 together, subglobose, c 5 mm across, sessile, heads; bracts deitoid-ovate, 0.75-1.0 mm long, persistent; bracteoles similar to bracts, 1.0-1.5 mm long, persistent. Tepals or perianth ovate-elliptic, equal, 1.5-2.5 mm long, shortly acuminate to blunt, white or often purple-tinged, 1-nerved. Stamens 5. Utricles obcordate, 2.0-2.5 mm long, strongly compressed, dark brown. Seeds discoid, 0.75-1.0 mm across, brown.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Inflorescence: Small axillary sessile heads; Flower: White, hermaphrodite, shining, regular, complete, bracteate, bracteolate; Perianth: perianth segments 5,unequal, the 2 lateral, innermost and concave; Androecium: Stamens 2-5, filaments short, connate in a cup with or without intervening staminodes, anthers 1-celled; Gynoecium: Ovary obcordate, broader than long, compressed, style very short, stigma captillate; Fruit: Utricle obcordate, compressed with thickened margins; Seed: inverse, sub – orbicular. 

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting: July– December. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are disperse by wind, birds and animals.

Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 34, 96.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
a and b spinasterols have been isolated from Alternanthera sessilis  . (Dogra et al, 1977). C.A. 148715a, 87, 1977.

Hexane extract of whole plant of Alternanthera sessilis   yielded 24-methylene cycloartanol, cycloeucalenol, stigmasterol, b-sitosterol, campesterol, a-spinasterol, 5-a-stigmasta-7-enol. (Houng-Jer et al, 1981). C.A. 117760e, 94, 198.

Nonacosane, 16-hen triacontone, b-sitosterol, stigmasterol and handianol have been isolated from Alternanthera sessilis  . (Sinha et al, 1984).

Saponin having oleanolic acid as aglycon and glucose and rhamnose as sugar moieties was isolated from the leaves of Alternanthera sessilis. (Mpuza et al, 1987).

Saponins from leaves of Alternanthera sessilis   contain a major component which has been isolated as a peracetylated and methylated derivatives and its structure has been established as 3b-O-(b-D-glucopyranosyl uronic acid)-28-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl oleanolic acid. (Andre et al, 1992). C.A. 23287x, 117, 1992.

Flavone glycoside, rohinetin-7-O-b-D-glycopyranoside (fig.1) was isolated fromleaves of Alternanthera sessilis . (Sahu et al, 1993). C.A. 265763d, 120, 1994.


Pharmacology:

Alkaloidal extract of Alternanthera sessilis   has no toxic effect at low dose level where as slight hepato and nephrotoxic effects were observed in moderate and high dose levels. (Gupta et al, 1995).

Imunological cross reactivity of Phosphoenolpyruvate Carboxylase in leaf extract of Alternanthera sessilis   was studied. (Gayathri et al, 2001).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for bone fracture (leaf, Rao and Neogi, 1998); Eye compliances (leaf, Shah et al, 1983); Rheum (Singh and Pandey, 1980); Bite of rabid dog, jackal, diarrohea, dysentry, malarial fever, night blindness, puerperal fever, Vetinary medicine (part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Edible Vegetable (leaf, Jain and De, 1966; Ball, 1967; Ramchandran and Nair, 1981; Ramchandran and Nair, 1981; Stem, Prasad and Abraham, 1984; Das et al, 1983; Gupta, 1962); Repels bees (spray of root juice, Krishna and Singh, 1987);
