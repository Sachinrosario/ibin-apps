HABIT & HABITAT
Annual, erect, aromatic, dioecious, suffruticose herb, up to 2 m tall; It is found in Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Rajasthan.
Availability :  Plenty, Throughout India 
Specimen examined:  Himachal Pradesh: Gonda, Lahul dist., 3434 m, 08.07.1938, N. L. Bor, 12436 (DD); Uttar Pradesh: Tehri, Garhwal, 24.05.1956, K. C. Sahni, 24776 (DD); Bridle path, Mussoorie, 1800 m, 31.07.1960, H. O. Saxena, 864 (DD); West Bengal: Sharswatipur, Jalpaiguri dist., 22.05.1944, S. K. Mukherjee, 949 (DD).
Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant grows wild as well as commercially cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem and branches angular, pubescent. Leaves alternate or lower opposite, palmate, 3-9 foliate, upper ones simple, lanceolate; leaflets lanceolate or oblong, 2–10 x 0.5–1.75 cm, acuminate, serrated, coarsely pubescent on both surfaces; stipules free. Flowers white or creamish; male flowers in short terminal panicles; female ones solitary or in axillary, leafy spikes. Fruits achene, enclosed by membranous calyx, compressed, brownish yellow. Seeds with fleshy cotyledons.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flower which are small are unisexual, male having 5 almost separate pale yellowish segments and female is single, hairy, glandular, five veined leaf enclosing the ovary in a sheath. Ovary is smooth one celled, with one hanging ovule and two long hairy thread like stigmas extending beyond the flower for more than its own length. Fruit is small, smooth, light brownish-grey in colour and completely filled by seeds. January – July.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
It cross pollinated, by insects and bees, and seeds are dispersed through wind and also by animals. 
 
Genetic variability:
Variation in C. sativa is considerable (2n=20) following mainly two types of pattern on the basis of which two widespread types which has been raised to the rank of sub-species, viz  sativa Linn. and indica (Lam.) Sub sp. sativa  has   relatively limited potential for the production of the intoxicant material but with more fiber and oil and the plant has relatively short period of vegetative growth whereas sub sp. indica has considerable potential for production of intoxicant material and has long period of vegetative growth.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

 Soil type: Plant requires rich friable loamy soil, which is retentive of moisture, must be repeatedly

  ploughed during summers.

Nutrient requirements: A dressing of farmyard manure or green manure is helpful sometimes chalk, gypsum and potash are applied. Trials have shown that nitrogen has beneficial effect.

Water management: The plant responds well to annual rainfall of 30 –80 cm, in areas with low rainfall some irrigation is    required at regular intervals.

Harvesting period / Method: The plants are harvested when the lower leaves fall off and the top of flowering stalks begins to turn yellow.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine leaves are bitter. It is used in leprosy; indigestion, cough, melancholy, dropsy, insanity. Inflorescence is used in abortification, convulsions, intoxication and dulls pain. In Unani system of medicine leaves causes thirst and biliousness. Water extract of leaves anthelmintic. The oil extract is good for earache. Bark is tonic and used in hydrocele, piles. Seeds check vomiting and causes headache. Resin is smoked to allay hiccough and bronchitis.
