Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Large evergreen tree suitable for shade trees or as avenue tree. Native to subtropical coastal areas of Australia but successfully cultivated for timber or shade in semi-arid and tropical climates from sea-level to 230 m. occurs throughout the country. Found in Subtropical regions like Himalayan foothills and Central Indian hills, East Indian Hills and parts of Western Ghats. Leaves fern like, deeply pinnatifid, dark green above and silvery below.

Grows between altitudes from sea-level to 2300 m, in temperate to subtropical climates. Annual rainfall in its natural habitat is 700 mm to over 1500 mm, most of which falls during summer. It has been introduced in many areas with an annual rainfall of 400-600 mm with 6-8 dry months. It also grows in areas having rainfall of as much as 2500 mm annually. It prefers warm to subtropical temperatures, generally with a mean annual temperature of about 200 C. Mature trees can withstand occasional light frost but young plants are frost sensitive. It grows well on sandy to loam soils of differing fertility levels and on acid soils. It tends to be a deep-rooted species and does not tolerate waterlogging. Sensitive to waterlogging. Drought resistant and fairly resistant to frost, but rather brittle and should not be grown in situations exposed to high winds.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An evergreen tree with long conical crown. Leaves alternate, 6-12" long, fern like, deeply pinnatified, dark green above and silvery below, covered with silky grey hairs. Flowers orange-coloured, solitary or several together, borne in racemes, on short leafless branches of old wood. Fruit an oblique, coriaceous follicle, containing 1 or 2 seeds.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

 

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowers during November-December. New leaves develop as old leaves fall. Flowers appear during March-May when golden yellow flowers cover branches in a flat horizon.


Pollinators:


Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by entire transplanting.It is easily propagated by seeds and regenerates naturally. Under normal storage conditions seeds remain viable for only a few months after collection, but dried and well-stored seeds kept in a refrigerator remained viable for two years. About 7000 seeds weigh a kg. Normally, plantations are made by transplanting nursery-grown seedlings (0.6 cm high) in half-filled 45 cu cm pits. Cuttings are also recorded successful. No seed treatment is required. The plant has good ability to compete with weeds. On good sites a growth of 2 m per year is recorded and in 15-20 years the normal height is 20 m.

The plant is grown in rows, single and multiple at 4m X 4 m and 4 m X 3 m distance. The tree is poor coppicer but pollards well. Under this system, the trunk is first cut at 3-4 m above the ground when the trunk attains a diameter of 10 cm. a large number of vigorous new shoots grow around the cut portion of the trunk which can be cut frequently at as short as 6 months interval depending on the purpose.

Pruning and thining are done to give shape. Appropriate care for fire protection and to avoid pests is adopted.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diseases:

Rosellinia bunodes (Berk. & Br.) Sacc. – root rot

Armillaria mellea (Vahl.) Quel. – root rot

Ganoderma australe (Fr.) Pat. – root rot

Corticium salmonicolor Berk. & Br. – pink disease attacks and girdles stems and branches in Industrial Plantation Circle, Kerala and other parts of South India.

Botryodiplodia theobromae Pat. – leaf spots in Allahabad (U.P.)

Mycosphaerella grevilleae Munjal, Chona & Kapoor – leaf spot

Cercospora agharkeri – leaf spot in Mahabaleshwar

Cytospora grevilleae Sahni – leaf spot

Seimatosporium grevilleae (Loos) Shoemaker – leaf spot and blotches resulting sometimes in premature defoliation.

Pestalotia banksiana Cav. – leaf spot

Cercospora – leaf spot

Dothiorella – leaf spot

Dendrophthoe falcata (L.f.) Ettingsh – mistletoe

Dendrophthoe neelgherensis (W. & A.) v. Tiegh – mistletoe

Loranthus sp. - mistletoe

On dry sites, dieback and growth reduction after 20 years of age are reported.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Whole plant, leaves and flowers.

Uses: 

Wood moderately good fuel wood. Wood can be used for cabinet work, staves of casks and cooperage. If cut to show silver grain to advantage, it can be employed for ornamental paneling, parquet floors, furniture, sleepers, air-freight cases, toys, veneering and plywood. Considered suitable for making bobbins. Timber is used for cabinet work. Suitable for paper pulp.

Melliferous plant - Attractive golden flowers make the plant an important honeybee plant. Because of affecting the growth of understorey. The stem is brittle, damaged by high winds.

Shade - it provides light shade to coffee and tea plantations.

Manure - Leaves valued as green manure in coffee and tea Plantations.

Miscellaneous Information

Tree with its fern-like foliage is ornamental when young. Cultivated as a shade tree in tea and coffee plantations and commonly planted in gardens and avenues.

Beautification - It has been widely used in the hill regions for street and roadside plantations. It fits well in agroforestry or bordering farms, community lands and roadsides.

It is cultivated as an ornamental tree, roadside tree.
Also planted in coffee plantations for shade.
Tree is a source of gum.

Wood: Wood hard, light, 36-45 lb. c ft., reddish brown, elastic, durable.
