DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES :
A deciduous shrub growing to 1.8m. The flowers are hermaphrodite (have both male and female organs) and are pollinated by Insects. The plant prefers light (sandy) and medium (loamy) soils and requires well-drained soil. The plant prefers acid soils and can grow in very acid soil. It can grow in semi-shade (light woodland) or no shade. It requires moist soil. leaves samll oblong acute at both ends serrulate glabrous, seems minutely pabescent, bracts leaf like sub persistent, clay, sparsely minutely pilose teeth ovate acute dentate, corolla glabrous with out. kharia mts: alt 3-5500ft.An erect shrub, 6 ft. height branches glabrous, pubescent towards the extrems leaves 1 1/4 by 1/4- 1/3 in., minutely acutely serrulate, petiole very short, often pubescent. pedicels 1/8-1/4 in., bracts 1/2-1/5 in., some of the upper often pubescent in fruit. calyx teeth 1/16 in., often glandular-denticulate. corolla 1/5 by 1/10 in., Filaments hairy: anthers dehiscing by short apical slib beaks sparred. Berry 1/5 in. dia. globose.The globose fruit is about 5mm in diameter.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
This plant variety is deciduous, that is it looses its leaves each year.

Breeding System :

Pollinators: 

Insects are responsible for pollinating this variety.

Seed Biology :
The globose fruit is about 5mm in diameter.

Seed Germination:
Propagation: Seed - sow late winter in a greenhouse in a lime-free potting mix and only just cover the seed. Stored seed might require a period of up to 3 months cold stratification. It is best to sow the seed in a greenhouse as soon as it is ripe. Once they are about 5cm tall, prick the seedlings out into individual pots and grow them on in a lightly shaded position in the greenhouse for at least their first winter.

Cultivation:
Requires a moist but freely-draining lime free soil, preferring one that is rich in peat or a light loamy soil with added leaf-mould. Prefers a very acid soil with a pH in the range of 4.5 to 6, plants soon become chloratic when lime is present. Succeeds in full sun or light shade though it fruits better in a sunny position. Requires shelter from strong winds. Dislikes root disturbance, plants are best grown in pots until being planted out in their permanent positions. Plants in this genus are notably resistant to honey fungus.Propagation: Seed - sow late winter in a greenhouse in a lime-free potting mix and only just cover the seed.  Miscellaneous Information
 Landscaping and Planting:
  1. This plant variety generally cannot be successfully grown in areas where the soil quality is of a poor standard, ie lacking in sufficient nutrients.
  2. This plant variety does not tolerate heavy clay soils.
  3. It is best suited grown in a light to a medium soil mixture.
  4. For optimal results it is preferable to plant in a well drained soil.
  5. This variety prefers a semi shade to full sun position.
  6. It is preferable to plant this variety in a moist position.
