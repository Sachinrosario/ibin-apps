DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  A much-branched sparingly pubescent herb with  pale purple or white flowers and brown black seeds. Perennial herbs, 25-50 cm tall, multi-branched, usually procumbent, scabrous. Stipule small, ovato-lanceolate; petiole 3-12 cm, stellato-veluinous; leaf blade reniform, rarely 5-7-lobed, 1-3 ´ 1-4 cm, papery, adaxial surface sparsely velutinous, abaxial surface sparsely stellato-puberulous; base cordate, margin minutely denticulate. Flowers usually 3-4-fascicled in axils of leaves, rarely solitary on stem. Pedicel 2-5 cm, sparsely stellato-puberulous; bracteoles 3, lanceolate, ca. 5 mm, stellato-puberulous. Calyx campanulate, 5-6 mm, stellato-puberulous, 5-lobed, lobes triangularly acuminate. Corolla white to pinkish, 10-12 mm in diam.; petals 5, subcordate. Staminal column puberulous. Style branches 13-15. Fruit flat globose, ca. 5-6 mm in diam., mericarps 13-15, non-reticulate, puberulent. Seeds reniform, ca. 1 mm in diam., reticulate or not.

Other Uses
  1. The dried leaves of Malva rotundifolia yield the pharmacopoeial drug Malvae Folia or Mallow Leaves, used as demulcent and emolient. M. rotundifolia is used in glycosnria and stomach disorders and as eminenagoguc. It is used also in fomentations and poultices for relieving sore throal and ophthalmia and for maturing abscesses.
  2. Seeds are demulcent and are prescribed in bronchitis, cough, inflammation and ulceration of of the bladder and in haemorrhoids : they contain 7-8% of a fatty oil  
  3. Flowers contain tannin.
