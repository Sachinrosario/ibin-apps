HABIT & HABITAT:
 Herbs, 0.5-1.5 m long. Stem erect and much branched; branches ascending; INDIA: Cultivated. EUROPE, MALAYSIA, AUSTRALIA, AFRICA.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  Undershrubs, scandent, much branched, armed, spinous; branches triquetrous, striate. Stem woody, terete, grey-white; spines decurved, more than 4 mm long. Cladodes in clusters of 2-6, oblong-linear, 1.0-2.5 cm long, falcate. Flowers small, white, in solitary or fascicled, simple or branched racemes; pedicels jointed in the middle; bracts ovate to triangular. Perianth segments linear-oblong, 0.2-0.3 cm long, spreading. Berries globose, ca 0.7 cm in diam., rugose, 1-seeded, red, with a wrinkled pericarp.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Simple or branched racemes; Flower: White, numerous, pedicel jointed, hermaphrodite; Perianth: Petaliod, 6, partite, campanulate or spreading  ; Androecium: Stamens 6, adnate to the base of the perianth segments, filaments free, anthers oblong perianth; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled, ovules 2 or more in each cell; Fruit: Berries subglobose, more or less 3-lobed, red when ripe; Seed: 1-3 shining, black, spherical.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting: October-April. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated by seeds.

Soil Type: Plant requires deep, loose, well drained soil rich in humus or well manured sandy loam is best for growth with pH 6.0-6.7.

Irrigation: Irrigation should be done frequently.

Nutrient requirements: 100 kg/ha potash, common salt and also the application of farmyard manure found to be beneficial.

Harvesting: Harvesting is done from 3rd year onwards by cutting them when thy are 15-20 cm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Plants is diuretic, laxative, cardiac and sedative. Infusion made from the root is used against jaundice and congestive torpor of the liver.
Roots are bitter, sweet; oleaginous, cooling, indigestible, appetiser; alterative, stomachic, tonic, aphrodisiac, galactagogue, astringent to the bowels. It is useful in dysentery, tumours, inflammations, biliousness, diseases of he blood and eye, throat complaints, tuberculosis, leprosy, epilepsy and night blindness (Ayurveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).

CIMAP:

The annual changes in carbohydrate content in the roots of two cultivars of asparagus (A. officinalis cultivar UC 157 and cultivar “Junon”) grown in the relatively mild winter climate of Israel were studied. During the autumn (August – Nov.) when the ferns senesce and the subsequent winter dormant season (Nov. –Feb.). The roots were characterized by a  const. And significant decrease in fructan content in both culktivars contributing to a loss of over 30% of the stored fructans. During the harvest (Feb. – Apr.) there was a further decline in root fructan content. During the post harvest period of fern growth, as the unharvested ferns develop and expand, the root fructan content declined to its lowest levels. Following this, there was a sharp and continous increase in root fructan levels, as well as a transient increase in sucrose levels for the following three summer months (May-Aug). The general pattern of root carbohydrate changes were similar in the two cultivars, although there were differences of possible physiol significance 1; Changes occurring in the content and compn. of dietary fiber of white asparagus during storage in different conditions were studied (room temp. ; 2˚ C in polyethylene bags; 2˚ C and 95% relative humidity). The neutral sugars and uronic acid compn. of dietry fiber was detd. by gas chromatog. and by a spectrophotometric method. In the insol. dietry fiber, glucose was found in a major amt. followed by galactose, xylose, araboinose, mannose, rhamnose and fucose. In the sol. dietry fiber, galactose was the predominant neutral sugar followed by arabinose, mannose, glucose and rhamnose. The content of uronic acids was significantly higher in the soln. dietry fiber than in the insol. one. The modifications observed in dietry fiber of the asparagus stored at room temp. are more rapid and pronounced than those in the refrigerated conditions, which prolong the conservation. Statistical analysis indicated that the changes were significantly affected by the type of storage and time 2.

An antifungal saponin was isolated from the bottom cut of white Asparagus officinalis. The structure of the saponin was identified as 3-O-{a-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1à2)}{a-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1à4)}-b-D-glucopyranosyl}(25S) spirost-5-ene-3b-ol from chemical and spectral data. This saponin was shown to be identical with collettinside III from Dioscorea collettii. (Shimoyamada et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9703-1620.

Two oligofurastanosides, namely methyl protodioscin and protodioscin were isolated from the seeds of asparagus officinalis. Their structural identification was performed using detailed analysis of 1H and C-NMR spectra including two-dimensional NMR spectroscopy (COSY, HMQC, NOESY and HMBC), and chemical conversions. (Shao et al 1997). MAPA 1998, 9801-0223.

Altheine in Asparagus officinalis was determined by HPLC using zorbarx-C18 column, 0.05mM sodiumacetate – methanol (80: 20) as mobile phase and m-benzaldehyde-2-mercaptoethanol as derivatizing agent. (Liu and Han, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 291365x.

Seven constituents were isolated from the dried stem bark of Asparagus oficinalis L. they were elucidated by chemical reactions and spectral data as palmitic acid, tetracosanoic acid b-sitosterol, daucosterol, yamogenin-3-O-[a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à4)-b-D-glucopyranoside], asparasaponin II, and asparasaponin I. (Zuo et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 133, 14582t.


Pharmacology:
Spirostanol glycosides asparagosides C and D, showed 20.0-44.0% inhibition in growth of mammary gland adenocarcinoma 755 and cancers of pancreas and cervix uteri in inoculated mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Root extract is phytotoxic. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Crude saponin exhibited antifungal activity against Candida, cryptococcus, trichophyton, Microsporum and epidermophyton. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Methylenedioxycinnamic acid at concentrations of about 25ppm strongly inhibited Lepidium sativum root and shoot growth. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

The 3-O-{a-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1à2)}{a-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1à4)}-b-D-glucopyranosyl}(25S) spirost-5-ene-3b-ol was used to inhibit the growth of some kind of fungi at mml-1 levels. (Shimoyamada et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9703, 1620.

GSH is a key antioxidant of cells with many essential metabolic functions. High concentration of GSH was found in asparagus. (Mills et al, 1997).

Methyl protodioscin and protodioscin have been shown to inhibit the growth of human leukemia HL – 60 cells in culture and macromolecular syntheses in a dose dependent manner. The inhibitory effect on DNA synthesis was found to be irreversible. (Shao et al, 1997) MAPA, 1998, 9801-0223.
