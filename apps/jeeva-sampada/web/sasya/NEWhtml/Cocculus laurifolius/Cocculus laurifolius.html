DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Shining axillary panicles; Flower: Regular, complete ; Calyx: Sepals 6, 2-seriate, acute, outer smaller ; Corolla:  Petals 6, smaller, usually auricled; Male Flower: Bracteate, minute, stamens embraced by petals, anthers subglobose, cells bursting transversaly ; Gynoecium: Ovary 3, styles usually cylindric ; Fruit: Drupe, minute, globose ; Seed: Curved, albumen fleshy. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : September- December. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The report chromosome number 2n= 26.

CIMAP:
 The content of crude alkaloids showed marked fluctuations in the leaves of C. laurifolius from Jan. to dec. the highest content (0.5%) of alkaloid was in Jan. and the lowest (0.2%) in July. The yield of coccuvinine was highest in Oct. and lowest in Sept. A high %age of this base in the crude alkaloid mixture was found in Apr. the yield of coccuvine was highest in Jan. and decreased in Apr. and May, the conc. of this base again decreased in August. Isococculidine, a major component of the leaves from Dehradun (India), was a minor component in the leaves from Lucknow (India) in Feb., May and June. Similar differences were observed for coccuvinine and coccuvine between the samples from Lucknow and Dehradun. The best time for harvesting the leaves of C. laurifolius for biol. active alkaloids in Jan1.


Pharmacology:
 Cocculine nitrate and cocculidine nitrate showed hypotensive action in dogs due to their ganglionic blocking action. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)j.

Cocsulinine showed anticancer activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)k.

Intra-arterial injection in isococculidine HCl (250 mg) produced progressive neuromuscular blockade which was more marked on indirectly elicited twitches on directly stimulated muscle contraction. On i.v. administration it produced neuromuscular blockade associated with more marked hypotension than d-tubocurarine. It directly inhibited denervated skeletal muscle. It also showed ganglion blocking action. LD50 of isococculidine i.p. in mice was 50 mg/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)l.

Isocorydine, O-methylisocorydine and boldine methochlorides, exhibited neuromuscular-blocking and hypotensive actions. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)i.

Gross behavioural effects following intracerebroventricular administration of d-coclaurine in mice and on dopamine uptake in mouse iris showed that d-coclaurine (25 mg) inhibited locomotor activity and caused ptosis and catalepsy. Pretreatment with coclaurine suppressed locomotor. Coclaurine (20 mg/kg, i.v.) suppressed dopamine uptake in mouse iris pretreated with a-methyltyrosine, suggesting neuroleptic-like property of coclaurine in blocking effects of dopaminergic stimulating agents. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)j.
