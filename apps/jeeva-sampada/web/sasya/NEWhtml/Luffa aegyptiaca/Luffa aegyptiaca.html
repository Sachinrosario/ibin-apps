HABIT & HABITAT
 Climbing, monoecious, glabrous herb. Stems pentangular, twisted, glabrous or slightly pubescent. It is found in Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Tripura, Meghalaya, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Laccadeep.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tendrils 3-fid, sparsely pubescent. Leaves orbicular-reniform, 10-25 cm in length, often broader than long, palmately 5-lobed, rarely 7-lobed; lobes acute or acuminate, triangular, distantly denticulate, both surfaces finely scabrous. Probracts fleshy, ovate. Flowers axillary. Male flowers in 4-20 flowered raceme, usually crowded near top; buds ovoid, pointed; calyx campanulate; lobes lanceolate, acute; petals spreading ovate-oblong, yellow with green veins; stamens 5. Female flowers: solitary; staminodes 5. Fruits fusiform, cylindrical or clavate, 15-45 x 8 cm, ends blunt, marked with longitudinal lines. Seeds black or grey, compressed, smooth or slightly tuberculate, margins narrowly winged.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine fruits are laxative and oleaginous. It removes biliousness. It is used in leprosy. Seeds are cathartic and emetic. In Unani system of medicine fruit cures biliousness, spleen diseases, leprosy, piles, fever, haematuria, syphilis and bronchitis.

Pharmacology:

·        Anti-allergic activity of bryonolic acid compared with that of glycyrrhetinic acid in rats. Brynolic acid (0.6 mg/kg, i.p) inhibited homologous passive cutaneous anaphylaxis more strogly than glycyrrhetinic acid at the same dose. It significicantly inhibited delayed hypersensitivity in mice unlike glycyrrhetinic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994) I  .

·        Oleanolic acid reduced  in vitro immunolaemolysis of antibody-coated sheep erythrocytes by guinea pig serum by inhibiting C3-convertase of classical complement pathway; threshold concentration of oleanolic acid required  acid  for inhibition of C3-convertase was 100.0µ/ml ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994)j.

·        Efect of oral administration of the ethanolic extracts of Luffa aegyptiaca (seeds) and Carissa edulis (leaves) on blood glucose levels both in normal and streptozotocin (STZ) diabetic rats. Treatment with both extracts significantly reduced the blood glucose level in STZ diabetic rats during the first three hours of treatment. L. aegyptiaca extract decreased blood glucose level with a potency similar to that of the biguanide, metformin. The total glycaemic areas were 589.61 +- 45.62 mg/dl/3 h and 660.38 +- 64.44 mg/dl/3 h for L. aegyptiaca and metformin, respectively, vs. 816.73 +- 43.21 mg/dl/3 h for the control (P lt 0.05). On the other hand, in normal rats, both treatments produced insignificant changes in blood glucose levels compared to glibenclamide treatment. (El Fiky et al., 1996)
