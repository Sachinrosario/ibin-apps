HABIT & HABITAT
 Annual, monoecious, climbing herb; stems slender, ribbed, slightly hairy. It is found almost throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tendrils elongated, slender, 2-3 fid. Leaves orbicular-reniform or ovate, 5-10 x 7-12 cm, cordate at base, margin distantly denticulate; lobes usually 5, sometimes 7, membranous, puberulous above, pubescent or scabrous beneath, acute at apex, petiolate. Flowers short, white. Male peduncles in pairs, first one 1-flowered, the later racemed, 6-14 flowerd; pedicels filiform, erect; bracts minute; peduncle straite; calyx tube 15-16 cm long; lobes lanceolate, 1.5 mm long, acute-triangular; corolla lobes fimbriate, white, ca 1 cm long; filaments slender, glabrous, c 1.5 mm long; anther oblong, ca 3 mm long. Female flowers axillary, solitary, peduncles 3-12 mm long; sepals and petals like those of male flowers; ovary oblong; style filiform. Fruit ovoid or spindle-shaped, 5-6 cm long, at first green with white stripes, ultimately red. Seeds 8-10, oblong, compressed, undulate at margin, truncate or emarginated at apex, attenuate at base, 11-12 mm long, corrugate.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine the whole plant is used in bronchitis. Root cures bronchitis, headache and boils. Leaves are used in biliousness. Fruits are bitter and pungent and is used as antipyretic and stomachic. It cures asthma, biliousness, bronchitis, itching, leucoderma, blood diseases, leprosy, ulcers, erysipelas and eye diseases. 

Chemistry:

·        b-sitosterol, its glucoside and luteolin-7-glucoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Isolation of cucurbitacin B from fruits and its 13C-NMR spectrum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Pharmacology:

·        Significant blood glucose lowering activities is observed in Trichosanthes cucumerina. (Kar et al, 2003).

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for bronchitis, heart disease (Root + Leaf, Fruit, Shah et al, 1983); Jaundice (Fruit, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Purgative (Fruit, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973); Stomach complaints (Root, Krishna and Singh, 1987)

Miscellaneous Information

·         The chloroform fraction of extracts of T. cucumerina roots showed antibacterial activity against Pseudomonas aeruginosa. At 1750 micro ram concentration the zone of inhibition against P. aeruginosa was comparable to that of gentamycin. (Kolte et al, 1994). MAPA 9505-3088.

Antibacterial activity of the nonsaponifiable parts of the fixed oils of Tichosanthes cucumerina along with other species of Trichosanthes was studies. Petroleum extracts of the seeds were found to possess appreciable antibacterial activity against Bacilis anthracis and Xanthomonaas malracearum. (Hariti and Rathee, 1995). MAPA 9603-1513.
