DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrubby, twining; stems long. Slender woody at the base, grooved, glabrous; Leaves: variable; in the broad from 10-12.5 by 7.5 cm.; in the narrow form 3.8-10 to 1.3-2.5cm., from linear – oblong, usually obtusely acuminate, glabrous, entire with somewhat undulate margins, base wneate, rounded;  Flowers: in few-flowered axillary racemes; bracts small, ovate, acuminate, opposite the pedicles; Pedicles long, thickened above.  Perianth greenish white, reaching 4.5 cm. long, with globose inflated base, then bent at the right angle and suddenly narrowed in to a cylindric tube with oblique trumpet shaped mouth gradually passing into a long narrow.  Anther 6, capsules 3.8-5 cm. long, oblong or globose-oblong,  opening from below upwards 6-valved, the pedicles splitting in to 6 filaments.  Seeds deltoid-ovate, acute, flat, winged.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary racemes; Flower: Hermaphrodite, complete ; Perianth: Glabrous, pale green, inflated bases narrowed into a cylindric tube terminating in horizontal funnel shaped purple mouth and lip clothed with purple tinged hairs;  Androecium: Stamens 6, adnate 1-seriately above the ovary, anther adnate, dehiscing extroresly ;Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 5-6 celled, placenta parietal, ovules many, 2-seriate, style thick; Fruit: Capsule oblong or globose oblong; Seed: Flat, Ovate, winged Flowering & Fruiting: April-August.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering- June to October  Fruiting- November to March
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.  

Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n=12.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Parts Used and Uses:
Plant; used as an abortifacient. EtOH (50%) Extract- diuretic and antiinflammatory. Dried stem and root- used as drug, which should be used in minimal doses; the drug pomotes digestion and controls menstruation; Root- considered as a stimulant, tonic and emmenagogue.

Root: a crystalline substance probably a glucoside a micro-crystalline principle glucoside in nature named isoaristolochic acid, allantoin, 0.05% carbonyl compounds and a small amount of an oil with the odour of isovanillin, ishwarone, ishwarane, aristolochene.


Chemistry:
Chemical constituents:
·         Aristolochic acid (fig. 1), m.p. 283° isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

·         Aristolochic acid D, its Me ether lactam  (fig. 2a) and aristolactam-b-D-glucoside (fig. 2b) was isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

·         A tetracyclic sesquiterpene – ishwarone (fig. 7b)– isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

·         Two new sesquiterpene hydrocarbons – ishwarane (fig. 7a) and aristolochene (fig. 3) from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·         Structure of a tetracyclic sesquiterpene – ishwarone was determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·         A new sesquiterpen hydrocarbon (I, fig. 5) isolated and characterized as 5b-H,7b,10a-selina-4(14),11-diene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

·         Five new phenanthrene derivatives (II-VI, fig. 6) isolated and characterized in addition to isolation of stigmast-4-en-3-one, sitosterol and two uncharacterized isomeric sesquiterpene alcohols m.p. 103° and 150°. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

·         Ishwarol was isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

·         Ishwarol (fig. 4) was isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

·         Synthesis of ishwarone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·         Two sesquiterpene alcohols – 12(S)-7,12-scoishwaran-12-ol (fig. 11) and (+)ledol-isolated from roots and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·         Aristolactam N-b-D-glucoside (fig. 12) along with 3b-hydroxystigmast-5-en-7-one and 6b-hydroxy-stigmast-4-en-3-one isolated and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

·         Isolation and characterization of 2-hydroxy-1-methoxy-4H-dibenzo[de,g]quinoline-4,5-(6H)-dione (fig. 8) from roots; cephaeradione A, aristolactam A and b-sitosterol-b-D-glucoside also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

·         Aristololide (fig. 9) along with 5a-stigmastan-3,6-dione, (-)cubebin and (-) hinokinin isolated from roots and its structure detrmined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

·         New napthoquinone – aristolindiquinone (fig. 10) isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

·         Synthesis of aristolindiquinone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·         Crystal structure detrmination of aristolindiuinone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·         Methyl nonacosen-12-oate isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

·         Aristolochic suspected as the causal factor of Chinese herbs nephropathy, is a well known carcinogen but its ability to induce fibrosis. (Cosyns et al, 1998).

The essential oil of the aerial parts of A. indica was analyzed by gas chromatog.-spectroscopic (GC-FID and GC-MS) and olfactoric methods. b-caryophylene, a-humulene, ishwarone, caryophyllene oxide I, ishwarol, ishwarane and aristolochene, as well as linalool and a-terpinolene were identified. (Jirovetz et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 135, 37004m.

Pharmacology:

·         Alcoholic extract of root active against adenocarcinoma 755 in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

·         Studies on interceptive properties of three fractions of chloroform extract in mice showed 40 to 60 mg/kg as MED. Histological study of uterus and ovary revealed no exchange except for presence of degenerated corpora lutea in case of one fraction. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

·         LD50, i.p., value of aristolochic acid inmice was 14.32 mg/kg. It neither prolonged survival time of tumor-bearing mice nor enhanced immune function of the mouse reticuloendothelial system and did not prevent the radiation and 2-dehydrodulcitol – induced damage to erythropoietic stem cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

·         Oral administration of aristolochic acid showed dose-dependent mutagenic activity in granuloma pouch assay. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

·         Aristolic acid, a potent antifertility agent, was non-mutagenic in Ames’ test whereas aristolochic acid and its analogs were mutagenic; presence of nitro group in phenanthrene nucleus essential for mutagenic action. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

·         P-coumaric acid from Aristolochia indica (15mg/100mg/ b.wt. /day) at the given dose and suration disrupts spermatogenesis by modifying the pituitary-testicular axis. (Mani Maran, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9602, 0842.
