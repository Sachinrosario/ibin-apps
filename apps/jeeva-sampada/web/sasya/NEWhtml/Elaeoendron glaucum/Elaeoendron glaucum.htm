Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Moderate sized deciduous tree. Often found on clay soil. Frost hardy. The tree is found throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 6,000 ft. in the Himalayas.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

 

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A moderate sized deciduous tree with grey or blackish bark, opposite, elliptic, coriaceous leaves. Yellowish brown flowers in axillary or extra-axillary dichotomous cymes.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, leaves and roots.

Fuel: A moderately good fuel. 

Wood takes fine polish and useful for cabinet work, combs and picture frames.


Harvesting Practices:

Back to Top

Produce and Products:

Tannins and Gums.

Chemistry:

The calorific value of the wood : Sapwood 4,867 cal., 8,762 B.t.u.; Heartwood 5,087 cal., 9,156 B.t.u.

Processing Techniques: Wood seasons well but requires careful treatment as thin planks liable to warp.

Other uses:

Tree planted as an ornamental tree in gardens.

Bark and the leaves contain 8-13.5 and 8 15% tannin respectively and may be used in tanning skins and hides. The leather obtained is yellow or yellow-buff. The bark dyes cotton fabrics ochre-red.

Bark reported to contain an alkaloid and two resins.

Powdered leaves have a sternutatory action and used as snuff to relieve headache and as a fumigatory in hysteria. The fresh root bark swellings. A cold water extract of the crushed roots used as an emetic; reported to be fatal in over-doses.

The plant yields gum (JAMRASI) which is colourless to reddish-brown, roundish, finely fissured tears, tasteless, when dissolved in water gives a clear slightly yellow adhesive. Ranked commercially as a gum of first grade.

Miscellaneous Information

Bark exudes a copious watery sap when cut. Wood: Wood is pinkish white to reddish brown with numerous wavy, concentric, lighter coloured bands. Moderately hard, heavy, 46 lb./ c ft., even and close grained. Sapwood and heartwood not distinct.
