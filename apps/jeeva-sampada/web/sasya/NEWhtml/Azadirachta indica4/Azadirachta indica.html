DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Large evergreen trees, 12-18 m tall and 1.8-2.4 m in girth, branchlets covered with a bloom. Leaves  imperipinnate, 10-45 cm long, crowded near ends of branches; leaflets 9-18, opposite or alternate, lanceolate, 4-6 x 1.0-1.5 cm, glabrous, oblique at base, crenate-serrate at margins.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers white, scented, in axillary panicles. Stamens monadelphous; staminal tube obconic, toothed at apex. Stigma with a light-coloured rim at base. Drupes c 1.5-2.0 cm long, smooth, yellow when ripe. Seeds surroundeded by a sweet pulp when  ripe, 1-seeded, ellipsoid, 7-8 mm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The bark is bitter, refrigerant, anthelmintic, astringent; prevents vomiting, burning sensation of heart, fever, thirst, bad  taste in the mouth, cough. It cures ulcers, leprosy, blood diseases,urinary disocharges. Leaves are anthelmintic, insecticidal and used in skin diseases,  Young leaves cure leprosy. Unripe fruits cure urinary discharges,skin diseases,  tumours, piles toothache. Seed oil is used to skin diseases. Leaf-juice is used to cure  snake-bite (Ayurveda). Bark is used in amenorrhoea. Leaves are used in lessening inflammation, rheumatism and also useful in syphilitic sores, boils. It is good for gum. The bark and leaves are used in leucoderma, lumbago, piles,syphilis, earache. Flowers are stomachic. Seeds are used to treat leprosy (Unani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
