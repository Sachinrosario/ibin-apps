Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Small tree. Common in arid plains. Found occuring throughout India particularly in Haryana, Gujrat and in dry rocky hills of Rajasthan (the Aravallies).

In India, average temperatures are between -40 and 480C. Tree is extremely hardy and resistant to drought and is one of the main cash crops of the desert regions. It can grow under subdesert conditions where annual rainfall is as low as 200 mm, with 8-11 dry months in the year, but prefers 300-450 mm. It will also survive with annual rainfall as high as 800 mm. Tree will grow naturally in sand; it also grows in clay, except where the rainfall is high enough to cause waterlogging (800 mm or more).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Small thorny tree. Prickles grow in threes at each node. Branches flexuose, glaucos-gray, young shoots pubescent. Leaves bipinnate, main rachis pubescent with a gland on the petiole below the lowest pair and one between the uppermost pair of the pinnae, stipular spines usually 3, lateral 2 nearly straight or slightly curved upwards and the middle one curved downwards, all dark brown, polished, pinnae 3-5 pairs with pubescent rachis. Leaflets 8-15 pairs, linear, subobtuse, glabrous, glaucos-green, petiolules very short.

Flowers fragrant, in spikes 2-4 in. long, peduncles axillary. Calyx companulate, divided 1/3rd of the way down, teeth distinct, deltoid. Corolla white twice as long as the calyx, divided about ½ way down, lobes lanceolate, acute. Filaments white. Anthers yellow. Pods shortly stalked, linear-oblong, thin, flat, flexible, attenuated at both ends, reticulately veined, pale brown and glabrous when ripe. Seeds 5-6.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Plants are raised from seeds. The seeds should be collected when pods begin to become bronze coloured, it obviates the necessity of seed scarification and ensures maximum seed germination percentage. Further, the moisture condition (water potential) of the bed should not be allowe to fall below 600 joules/kg during the germination period as beyond this point, the germinability is severely affected, irrespective of the extent of seed maturity.

In plantations, seeding is done on contour ridge-cum-trench in gravelly or rocky sites. The seeds germinate in clumps 4 days after sowing and the total germination period is 8 days. The seedlings attain an average height of 11 cm in first growing season in gravelly soils and 20 cm in sandy soils. The seedlings can stand transplanting in extremely arid regions. One or two weedings are necessary in first year. Seedlings are tender to fire and to some extent to frost. The plantations have to be protected from browsing by goats, damage by desert rats and also from white ants.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: wood used as fuel

Wood: The heartwood is black and hard and takes beautiful polish, but being small in size, it is used only for cart wheels, sugarcane crushers, Persian water wheels, fencing posts and agricultural implements.

The tough roots and stems of young trees are utilized for the manufacture of tool handles and weavers' shuttles.

Produce and Products:
Gum - Yields a true gum arabic of commerce, which is also known as Senegal gum. Gum exudes from the gum cysts which develop in the inner barks of some trees. It is believed that the gum is produced by living and physiologically active cells of the phloem under certain pathological conditions, apparently brought about, besides other causes, by rod shaped bacteria (Bacterium acaciae Greig-Smith) found conspicuously in all the tissues affected by gummosis.

Other uses:

liked for fodder

Gum - The principal use of gum arabic is in confectionery as an emulsifier, for preserving flavours of soft drinks and spray-dried instant foods and also in manufacture of chewing gums. It is used in pharmaceutical industry as a binding agent in the manufacture of cough pastilles and other medical preparations or as a coating for pills. Another major use of gum arabic is in the manufacture of adhesives for domestic and office use, for stamps and envelops. It is also used in lithographic printing and to a small extent in sizing of paper and cloth and in the textile industry for finishing silk and crepe. It has a variety of applications in the paint and ink industry and in cosmetics.

The bark of the long flexible strands of the surface roots furnishes a fibre. It is used for cordage, well ropes, fishing nets, horse girths and foot ropes.

The roots are said to be used for dysentry, gonorrhoea and nodular leprosy.

Food & Fodder - The seeds are sometimes eaten as a vegetable. The leaves and fallen flowers often collected for cattle fodder.

Seeds - Seeds are dried and preserved for human consumption as vegetables.

Erosion control and soil rehabilitation - This is a nitrogen-fixing species that probably increases soil fertility. It is highly suitable for agroforestry systems and is already widely grown in combination with watermelon, millets, forage grasses and other crops. It can be used in desertification control to reestablish a vegetation cover in degraded areas as well as for sand-dunes fixation and wind-erosion control.
