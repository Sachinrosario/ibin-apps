DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial herbs, deciduous. Culms robust, 5-14 m tall, 2-8 cm in diam.; internodes 30-40 cm long, hollow in wet and solid in dry climate, tufted; culm sheaths variable, 7-28 cm long, slightly auricled, ciliate on margins, back covered with golden brown stiff hairs; ligules narrow. Leaves linear-lanceolate, 2.5-23.5 x 0.5-3.0 cm, acuminate, pointed, twisted tip, rounded at base, scabrous on margins. Inflorescence a large branched panicled with dense, glabrous, spaced heads; rachis rounded, smooth. Spikelets 7-11 mm long, hairy, fertile and sterile mixed. Mpty glume 2 or more, ovate, spinescent, many-nerved. Lemma ovate. Palea ovate or obovate, emarginate, 6-8-nerved; keels ciliate. Caryopsis brown, ovoid, subglobose, 7-8 mm long, shining, beaked with persistent style.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Spikelets in dense globular heads ; Flower: Bracteate with distinct spiny tip; Perianth : represented by two rudimentary free tepals known as lodicules; Androecium: Stamens 6, anthers dithecous, versatile; Gynoecium: Ovary superior with two feathery styles; Fruit: Caryopsis; Seed:1 . Flowering & Fruiting : Sporadically.

Floral and seed biology:
November - April.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Silicious matter found near the joint used as a cooling tonic and astringent medicine. Leaves are given to animals during parturition from a supposition that they cause a more rapid expulsion of the placenta
