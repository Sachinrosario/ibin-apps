DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence: Racemose raceme; Flower: Large, bright yellow, pedicillate, complete, hermaphrodite, actinomorphic, tetramerous and hypogynous ; Calyx: Sepals 4, in two whorls of two each, polesepalous, petaloid ; Corolla: Petals 4, polypetalous, cruciform, each petals distinguished into claw and limb ;Androecium: Stamens 6, in two whorls, polyandrous, tetradynamous, 4 inner long amd two outer short, dithecous, basifixed, and introrse; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, syncarpous, ovary superior unilocular but become bilocular later on due development of false septa, ovles many in each locule, placentation parietal, style and stigma bilobed; Fruit: Pods glabrous, sub-erect, valves with mid-rib and flexuous veins; Seed: Small, erect, pale or dark .


   
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : January-April .
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  birds, cattles, wind or water.   
 
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 20.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Chemical Variability: Automatic gas chromatog. on silicone oil columns was used to Sep. Fatty acid Me esters from varieties of rape and white mustard. The % of plamitic, oleic, linoleic, linolenic and eruic acids for springs turnip rape grown at Svalov was 2.4, 24.5, 16.8, 10.1 and 30.0 resp; at Izmir sown in autumn it was 2.6, 28.7, 15.2, 7.8 and 27.8 resp. and at Izmir sown spring it was 3.0, 29.0, 17.0, 7.3 and 26.7 resp. Mineral nutrition and variation of harvesting times around the normal have little influence on fatty acid pattern. The variation between single seeds with in the population is illustrated for winter rapes 64-849 and var. Matador, for spring turnip rape, and for crambe abyssinica. Breeding expts show that eruic acid content is under embryonic control and the genes act in an additive way with no dominance. Crossing between Canadian strain and Swedish summer rape gave F2 seeds with no euric acid and considerable variation in the other unstad. acid contents. Strains with 60-70% oleic or 35-40% linoleic have been obtained with a pos. correlation between linoleic and linolenic contents. Generally yield and straw stiffness is lower in the euric acid free strains. By repeated selection inbred strains, euric acid in winter rape has been reduced from 55 to 40% and that in winter turnip rape from 46 to 30%. In white mustard , a range of strains from 15 to 60% eruic acid has been obtained, The redn. in this acid being balanced by an increase in oleic acid content. Rape seed strains with fatty acid compous resembling peanut oil have been obtained1; At the early stages of development toxic substances are presented by isothiocyanates, but beginning with the bussing phase, by glucosinolates. The total toxicity of some rape varieties is 2.5 to 3 times lower than that of winter bird rape. The best low-euric and low-glucosinolate variety affored 30.1 quintals seed/ ha; its oil content was 42.3%2; the brinded Korean cabbage (BKC) with various ingredients was stored at 5° and 15° for 13 days to examine the changes in pH, total acidity, ascorbic acid and chlorophylls. Decreased in pH, total acidity in the BKC stored at 5°C, indicating these changes are closely related to the storage temp. The effect of ingredients was various: garlic, red pepper powder and fermented anchovy juice accelerated the decrease in pH and increase in total acidity; mustard powder and leaf suppressed their changes and green onion and ginger had no effect. At both temps, ascorbic acid was remained at lowest level, in the BKC with fermented anchovy juice. Degrdn. of chlorophyll was slow in the BKC with the leaf mustard, which maintained the high level of ascorbic acid during storage. Meanwhile the degrdn. of chlorophylls was severly fast in the BKC with garlic, which maintained moderately high level of ascorbic acid, resulting in greatly increase in the prodn. of  pheophytin and pheophorbide. UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:
Rutin in seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

An acidic arabinogalactan isolated from rape-seed is comprised of L-arabinose, D-galactose and D-glucurosnic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

a-D-galactopyranosyl-(1®6)-a-D-galactopyranosyl-(1®1)-L-myo-inositol (fig. 1) isolated from rape seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structural studies on an arabinan isolated from rape-seed revealed a highly branched structure. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Steam volatile content of seed oil was 0.38%, 3-butenyl isothiocyanate, 2-phenylethyl isothiocyanate and phenyl acetonitrile isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Free and esterified sterols compositions of seed oil was found to be brassicasterol (12.7, 5.5), campesterol (28.2, 36.3), sitosterol (58.0, 52.6) and 5-hydro-avenasterol (1.1, 5.6% respectivey). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

a-Farnesene identified as major compound. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Isolation of isorhamnetin-3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside and trans-sinapolymalic acid from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Structures of new gibberellins GA85 and GA86 confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Brassicasterol synthesized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
