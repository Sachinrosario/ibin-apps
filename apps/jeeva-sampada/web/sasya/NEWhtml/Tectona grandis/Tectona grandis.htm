Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A large deciduous tree. Occurs in tropical regions of medium rainfall areas of the Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical). Teak is indigenous to India, Burma, Thailand, Indonesia and other islands in the Far East. The species grows naturally and is also planted in central and southern India, while in the North, it is widely planted but the quality is average. It needs subtropical or tropical climate, but it is grown almost in every state of the country. Best under hot and humid climate.

Deep well-drained soils are good for quality timber. Found on various geological formations. Prefers rich soils. (deficiency in nitrogen causes strong chlorosis and premature defoliation). Species is not naturally gregarious. The majority of teak forests occur naturally on hilly or undulating terrain and flat alluvial ground with good sub-soil drainage combined with fair rainfall. Teak is normally a component of mixed deciduous forests. The species is a strong light demander, is sensitive to frost and drought and possesses a great power of resisting the effects of fire than most of its associates though fire may cause hollowness in lower part of the bole.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

A large deciduous tree. Branchlets quadrangular, channelled, stellately tomentose. Leaves elliptic or obovate, acute or acuminate, the upper surface rough but usually glabrous, the lower clothed with dense stellate grey or tawny tomentum, entire, base usually cuneate. Main nerves 8-10 pairs with 2 or 3 large branches near the edge of the leaf, joined by numerous parallel transverse veins.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers shortly pedicellate, in large terminal branched tomentose cymose panicles, with lanceolate bracts at the forks. Bracts beneath the calyx linear-lanceolate. Calyx in flower stellately tomentose, semiglobose-companulate, the lobes 5 or 6, spreading, subequal, in fruit enlarged to 1 in. or even more long, bladder like, enclosing the fruit, ovoid, more or less crumpled or irregularly plaited, reticulately veined. Corolla white, glabrous, limbs with spreading subequal lobes. Fruits subglobose, somewhat 4-lobed, the pericarp soft with dense felted stellate hairs, endocarp bony.

Phenology:

Leaf fall occurs during November - January, extends up to March. New leaves appear during April - June. Flowering occurs during June - August. Fruits ripen during November - January.

Floral and seed biology:


Plants are raised both by seeds and vegetative methods. Seeds need treatment like putting in hot water and then soaking in cold water or putting them along with cowdung manure in pit for a fortnight. Sown in early spring. They sprout in 3 weeks. Seedlings with 2 leaves are picked and planted in beds. They are allowed to grow for a year before being transplanted out at spacing of 7-8 m.

Cutting from shoot or root (stumping) is best as it is easier and the plants establish soon in the pits. Coppicing is also practised.

Transplanting is done during July.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Good fuel. Calorific value is high. Calorific value of sapwood is 4989 kcal and of heartwood is 5535 kcal.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, white to pale yellowish or greyish-white. Heartwood golden brown often with darker streaks turning deeper brown on exposure. Wood with an oily feel and characteristic odour reminiscent of old leather; moderately hard, moderately heavy, average weight 650 kg/m3 (40 lb/m3) at 12 per cent moisture content; medium coarse and uneven-textured with straight-grain.

A ring-porous wood. Growth rings distinct, delimited by large early wood pores. Pores in the early wood large, clearly visible to the eye, partly filled with tyloses and occasionally with yellowish-white deposits, usually forming a conspicuous zone at the beginning of the growth ring; transition from early wood to late wood gradual; latewood pores moderately large to small, mostly solitary and in short radial multiples; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly vasicentric, forming a thin sheath round the pores and also delimiting growth rings, distinctly visible under the lens. Rays moderately broad, visible to the eye, distinct under the lens, rather few and somewhat widely-spaced.

Activated charcoal is prepared from saw dust.

Wood widely used for building construction, railway coach construction, furniture, poles, posts, for high-class furniture and for making various grades of plywood.

Oil is extracted from wood which is used in treatment of eczema and ringworm. Fresh wood sinks in water.
Wood is much used for ship building, bridges, flooring, furniture.

Harvesting Practices:

Produce and Products:

As a fuelwood high calorific value : sapwood – 4,989 cal and heartwood – 5,535 cal. But due to its prohibitive cost, not used as fuel. Activated charcoal prepared from sawdust. Tree is girdled so that it dies and it is left standing for 2 years before extraction of wood.

Medicine - Seeds, flowers, bark are medicinally useful.

Miscellaneous Information

 
Tree planted as an avenue tree. Grown along roads and field bunds, except in cold regions. Also used as soil stabilizer.

On the basis of origin teak is distinguished in two classes - Central Indian teak and South Indian teak. Central Indian teak has darker wood with weavy streaks while the other has light coloured and more straight grained wood.

Tree in Annamalais has found to be attained the age of 500 years. A tree in Kakanankote forests of Mysore showed 680 annual rings when cut down. The plants have been micropropagated.
