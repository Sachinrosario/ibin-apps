Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Tree of temperate and sub-tropical regions. In India, the pear is cultivated cheifly in Punjab, Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Madras. Uttar Pradesh, Assam and Bengal have smaller areas under cultivation.

Pear is less hardy and grows in general in a somewhat warmer climate than the apples. It requires 900-1000 hours below 70 to adequately break its rest period. In India it is grown at elevations of 1200-1800 m. in Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Kashmir, Uttar Pradesh and Assam and in the Nilgiris at elevations of 1600-2000 m. It is best grown on a deep and warm soil retentive of moisture. It is said to be comparatively less affected by waterlogging during the rainy season.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Trees are usually upright growing to the height of 8-10 m and some are spreading 6 m. Canopy is conical to oval and round. Trunk is mostly strong and straight. Bark is scaly and of dark brown colour which splits off readily. Branching may be strong but with weak crotch angles. Roots, tap root with spreading rootlets.

Leaves are deep green, ovate, glossy on upper surface leathery with prominent veins, particularly the mid rib, petiole round, green and 2 cm long. Leaf margin toothed, leaf size is 15X5 cm.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers usually axillary, white and many, borne in clusters, medium sized.

Fruit is round to pear shaped drupe, green to greenish yellow or pale yellow. Few varieties have pink or red blush over the skin. The endocarp is thin, smooth or hard, non-separable from the mesocarp, mesocarp is fleshy, juicy, sweet, green to yellowish and sweet scented, usually 2-3 cm thick, both are edible. Fruit stalk is 3-5 cm long, remains attached to fruit.

Seeds are 5-10, placed in core in centre of the fruit, light to dark brown, conical, 0.5X2.0cm,usually swollen and flat but shining.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Seedlings are raised from the seeds of pear or Kainth (Pyrus pashia). It is propagated by budding or grafting on some kind of rootstock grown first in the seedbed and then in the nursery.

Cutting and rootstocks can also be raised for getting seedlings. Transplanting in temperate region is done during early winter or late winter but in other regions, in the monsoon season.

Spacing is generally 6X6 m for budded plants while seedlings are planted 8X8 m apart.

Also grown by grafting.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Nectria sp. - Bark canker, serious in orchards in the Amritsar (Punjab) region.

Ganoderma sp. - White rot of root and root collar

Venturia pirina Aderh. - Scab

Coniothecium chomatosporum Corda - Stem black

Corticium salmonicolor Berk. & Br. - Pink disease

Sclerotinia fructigena Aderh. & Ruhl. - Brown rot

Quadraspidiotus perniciosus Comst. - The San Jose scale, major pest

Number of beetles which defoliate the plant damage the fruits are reported to infest the pear in Himachal Pradesh, Punjab and Uttar Pradesh.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel.

Wood: Wood is reddish yellow or pink, soft, close and even grained. Owing to its extraordinary smoothness and evenness of texture, it is said to be excellent for engraving and turning, and especially for mathematical and drawing instruments and rules. It is valued for furniture making.

Other uses:

Fruits - Fruits are eaten when and fresh. Used for making jam, juice, preserve, candy, etc. while half-ripe fruits are used as vegetable after cooking. Fruits are canned. Good source of minerals and roughages.

Fruit juice - A clear juice of excellent flavour can be prepared. It is recommended for use in jellies and sherbets, and after acidification as a beverage.

Dried pear - Preserved by sun-drying; cull pears from canneries are used more for this purpose than the whole pears.

Wax - A low melting light green wax can be separated from the peels.

Miscellaneous Information

 Trees are suited as avenue, shade, wind-break and for road-side plantation.

Suitable for homesteads and field edges.

This species includes a large number of varieties of which two are important, viz. var. communis [syn. P. communis var. pyraster Linn.; P. pyraster (Linn.) Borkh.] and var. sativa DC. (syn. P. domestica Medik.; P. sativa Lam. Et DC.). There are also ornamental forms such as cut-leaves, lobe-leaves and variegated.
