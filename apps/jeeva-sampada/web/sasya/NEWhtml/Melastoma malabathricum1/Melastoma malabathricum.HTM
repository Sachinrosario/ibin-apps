HABIT & HABITAT: 
Spreading shrub, rarely small trees, distributed chiefly from South and East Asia to Australia and Polynesia and about 4 species occur in India. A very variable shrub, sometimes a small tree, found near water courses and moist places in the humid parts of India up to an elevation of 1,800 m. and in the Andaman Islands; it is occasionally cultivated in gardens. Seen throughout valleys near streams

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Bark reddish brown, thin; leaves lanceolate to oblong. The fruit is ovoid, truncate with edible pulp.


Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers in terminal corymbose, panicles, mauve purple; fruit broadly ovoid, truncate, pulpy within. Flower colour bright mauve or purple. Fruits in December and January.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Leaves, Flowers, Fruits, Roots ,Bark.

Uses:  

  1. The leaves, flowers and fruits are eaten.
  2. The plant acts as feed for silkworm.
  3. Fruit yields a purple dye used for cotton cloths.

Harvesting Practices

Produce and Products:
Black or purple dye.

Back to Top

Chemistry
Leaves gave amino acids,glycine,valine,leucine,aspartic acid, glutamin acid,methionine,tyrosine,iso-leucine and hydroxyproline,roots gave beta-sitosterol and a triterpene melastomic acid.

Other Uses
  1. The fruit yields a black or purple dye. It is stated that an ink can be prepared from it. 
  2. A pink dye is obtained from the leaves and roots. 
  3. The ashes of the plant are used as a dye mordant. 
  4. Atlas silkworm is said to feed on the plant and yield a fine silk.
  5. The pulp of the fruit is sweet and slightly astringent.
  6. The bark and the roots are used for healing wounds and other skin diseases; they are also employed in the preparation of gargles. 
  7. The plant is used to treat diarrohea and dysentery and eucorrhoea.
