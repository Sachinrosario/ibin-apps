Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A shrub or occasionally small tree. Arid region, dry areas of Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana and Peninsular India (dry tropical).

Light demander, although seedlings prefer shade. Rainfall range 300 - 400 mm. Drought hardy, frost sensitive. Found on medium-heavy soils on alluvial plains of Rajasthan, on loamy sand and clay loam. On rocky habitation planted with Prosopis cineraria on alluvial soil.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A shrub or occasionally small tree with a short twisted or bent trunk. Bark grey or whitish grey. Branches numerous, drooping, stiff, divergent, whitish. Leaves whitish-green, coriaceous and somewhat fleshy when mature, linear-lanceolate or elliptic-lanceolate, acute or subobtuse, often mucronate, glabrous; main nerves indistinct. Dense growth of small plants seen around the parent stem surrounded by a ring of root suckers.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers greenish-white, sessile, in erect axillary panicled spikes, often clustered. Calyx cleft about ½-way down; lobes 4, rounded, obtuse. Corolla a little longer than the calyx, deeply cleft; lobes obovate-oblong, obtuse. Drupes subsessile, globose, yellow when ripe. Seeds are greenish yellow.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by entire transplanting or by root and shoot cuttings. Free regeneration with root suckers and natural layers. Also planted by direct sowing or nursery raised seedlings. Best time for plantation is July.

Plant coppices well. Regenerates freely by root suckers.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Moderately good fuelwood. When mixed with deodar and pine scrap it can be used for burning bricks.

Wood: Light red or light yellow, with a small, irregular, purple heartwood, moderately hard and moderately heavy to heavy. Weight from 609 to 865 kg/m3.

Wood can be used for building, agricultural implements, Persian wheels and knees of boats.

Other uses:

Fruit - Fruit sweet and eaten by natives, locally called Pilu. Fed to cattle for increased lactation.

When dried it forms an article of trade and tastes like currants.

Leaves - Favorite diet of camels during first half of the hot weather, but other animals will not eat it.

Oil - Oil extracted from seeds (40 - 50%) suitable for soaps after deodorizing. Oil also used for candle manufacture and indigenous medicines. It is a potential industrial substitute of coconut oil. Also used for making candles.

Seedcake - Seedcake suitable as cattle feed and manure. Seed cake is rich in proteins and carbohydrates.

Medicinal - Fruits, bark and leaves used in indigenous medicines.
