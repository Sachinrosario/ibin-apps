HABIT & HABITAT
Perennial, twining herb with terete stem and branches. It is found throughout India; also widely cultivated in gardens.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves imparipinnate; leaflets 5-7, subcoriaceous, elliptic-oblong, obtuse, sparsely hairy. Flowers solitary, axillary or in fascicles, bright blue or white; corolla papilionaceous, deep blue or white, vaxillum ovovate, 4.5 x 2.5 cm. Pods linear-oblong, 5.5-6.5 x 0.3-0.5 cm, flattened, apically beaked, sparsely appressed, glabrous, sharply beaked with 5-persistent sepals. Seeds 6-10, reniform, 6-7 x 5 mm, yellowish brown, smooth.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Axillary or solitary ; Flower: Flowers blue or white, pedicellate, bracteate, bracteolate, complete, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Tubular, teeth lanceolate, shorter than tube; Corolla: Much exserted, bright blue with sometime orange centre ;Androecium: Stamens monadelphous, anthers uniform; Gynoecium: Ovary stalked, ovules many, style elongated, incurved more or less flattened, bearded along inner side; Fruit: Pods long, flattened, nearly straight, sharply beaked; Seed: Seeds 6-10, yellowish-brown, smooth. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : June-January. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:The reported chromosome number 2n=16.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine roots are used as laxative, diuretic, alexiteric, anthelmintic, depurative, aphrodisiac and tonic. It is useful in ophthalmopathy, tubercular glands, hemicrania, burning sensation, strangury, helminthiasis, leprosy, leucoderma, elephantasis, inflammation, bronchitis and asthma. In Unani system of medicine roots are used as purgative and useful in ascites.


Chemistry:
A lactone – aparaajitin (fig. 1), mp 92°C – from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

g-sitosterol, mp 145°C, from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Taraxerol from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

A phenol glycoside, 3,5,7,4’-tetrahydroxyflavone-3-rhamnoglycoside, an alkaloid, mp 235°C, ethyl a-D-galactopyranoside and p-hydroxycinnamic acid polypeptide, mp 210°C from defatted seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

Hexacosanol, b-sitosterol and an anthoxanthin from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Kaempferol-3-O-rhamnosyl(1®6)-glucoside and kaempferol-3-O-rhammnosyl(1®6)galactoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Kaempferol-3-O-glucoside, kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside, kaempferol-3-O-neohesperidoside and clitorin (fig. 2), mp 198°C, which was characterized as kaempferol-3-O-rhamnosyl(1®2)-O-rhamnosyl(1®2)-O-[rhamnosyl-(1®6)]-glucoside isolated from leaves of Formosan plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Malvidin-3b-glucoside, delphinidin-3b-glucoside and 3’-methyl ether of delphinidin-3b-glucoside from blue flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Stigmast-4-ene-3,6-dione isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Isolation and identification of cyanin chloride and kaempferol from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Palmitic (18.5), stearic (9.5), oleic (51.4), linoleic (16.8) and linoleic(3.8%) acids from seed oil; seeds contained protein (38.4%), amino acid analysis of protein given. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Six acylated anthocyanins – ternatins A, B, B, C, D, E and F – isolated from blue flowers along with kaempferol and its 3-glucoside, robinin, quercetin and its 3-glucoside; ternatins A and B partially characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Six new anthocyanins – ternatins A1, A2, B1, B2, D1 and D2 isolated from flowers and their structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Structure of ternatin D1 established as 3-O)6-O-malonyl-b-D-glucopyranosyl)-3’,5’-di-O-(6-O-(E-4-O(6-O-E-p-coumaryl-b-D-glucosyl)-p-coumaryl)-b-D-glucopyranosyl)delphinidin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Three trypsin inhibitors isolated from seeds and partially characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Five new ternatins have been isolated from Clitoria ternatea flowers and characterized as delphinidin 3-malonyl G having 3’-GCG-5’-GCG(1), 3’-GCG-5’-GC, 3’-GCGCG-5’-GC (II), 3-GCGC-5’-GCG (III) and 3’-GCGC-5’-GC side chains; in which G is D-glucose and C is p-coumaric acid. (Terahara et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 124, 112352b.

Clitoria ternatea contained linoleic acid. (Hussain and Devi, 1998). C.A. 1999, 131, 71213f.

Eight new anthocyanins ternatins C1, C2, C3, C4, C5 and D3 and pretematins A3 and A4 were isolated from flowers.

Three flavonol glycosides, kaempferol 3-O-(2"-O-a-rhamnosyl-6"-O-malonyl)-b-glucoside, quercetin 3-O-(2"-O-a-rhamnosyl-6"-O-malonyl)-b-glucoside, and myricetin 3-O-(2",6"-di-O-a-rhamnosyl)-beta-glucoside were isolated from the petals of Clitoria ternatea. (Kazuma et al., 2003).

Pharmacology:

Neonatal rat pups (7 days old) were incubated with either 50 mg/kg body weight or 100 mg/kg body weight of aqueous root extract of Clitoria ternatea (CTR) for 30 days. These rats were then subjected to open field, two compartment passive avoidance and spatial learning (T-Maze) tests (i) immediately after the treatment and (ii) 30 days after the treatment, along with age matched normal and saline control rats. Results showed no change in open field behaviour, but showed improved retention and spatial learning performance at both time points of behavioural tests, indicating the memory enhancing property of CTR which implicates a permanent change in the brain of CTR treated rats. (Rai et al., 2001)

Treatment with 100 mg/kg of Clitoria ternatea aqueous root extract (CTR), for 30 days in neonatal and young adult age groups of rat, significantly increased acetylcholine (ACh) content in their hippocampi as compared to age matched controls. Increase in ACh content in their hippocampus may be the neurochemical basis for their improved learning and memory. (Rai et al., 2002)
