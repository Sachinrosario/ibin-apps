Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Citrullus lanatus is a trailing or climbing hispid considiered native of tropical and subtropical Africa. It is of ancient cultivation in the Mediterranean region and was grown in Egypt in remote times. It reached India in prehistoric times. It is cultivated widely throughout India for the fruits which are used as dessert. Major states growing watermelon include Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab and Haryana. The average temperature for growth would be around 30-350 C with maximum ranging around 400 C and minimum between 20-250 C for watermelon. Watermelon can be grown on a variety of soil types. Watermelon grows in hot and dry situations. It cannot withstand water logging and frost. The sandy or sandy loams are considered best. The soil should be well drained. Alluvial riverbeds are also good for production of watermelon. The crop can withstand acid conditions better than any other cucurbits. Optimum pH range is 6.5-7.0 (3, 4, 5, 17).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Watermelons are slender, hairy annual, often sprawling over the ground. Root system very extensive and superficial. Stems rather thin, herbaceous, angular, grooved, 1.5-5 m long, with long white hairs. Tendrils 2-3 fid. Leaves 5-20 x 2-12 cm, ovate or triangular ovate in outline, scabrous on both surfaces, densly so on nerves beneath. 3-4 pairs pinnate lobes, lobes again divided and toothed with broad apices; petiole 1-10 cm long, shorter than lamina. Fruits globose or oblong, varying greatly in size up to 60 cm or more long, rind mostly glabrous, green or cream, striped or mottled green, hard, but not durable. Flesh red, green, yellow or whitish, usually sweet and many-seeded (5, 6, 7, 8).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers unisexual, solitary, axillary, usually more male flowers than female. Male flowers in the axil of a spoon shaped, 8-10 x 3-4 mm bract; on an elongate, 1-5 cm long, villous peduncle. Calyx tube broadly campanulate, narrowly lanceolate and 5-lobed. Corolla deeply 5-partite, pale yellow, 2.5-3 cm diameter. Female flowers borne on peduncles of 2-6 cm long. Calyx and corolla as in male flowers. female flowers have 3 staminodes, ovary ovoid with woolly hairs, style 0.5 cm long with 3 lobes (5, 6, 7, 8, 9).

Breeding System :
Citrullus lanatus is a cross-pollinated crop.

Phenology :

Pollinators:
The watermelon is pollinated by Insects, particularly Honey bees. Hand pollnation also assists fruit setting.

Seed Biology :
The seeds are numerous, smaller size about 6-10 mm long, pyriform, compressed and black, brownish and white in colour. 15 seeds weigh one gram.

Seedling Germination :
Germination is epigeal. The seeds of watermelon should be soaked in water overnight and then should be kept in moist cloth or gunny bag near a warm place before planting. Germination would start within three to four days. Soaking of seeds in ethephon at 480 mg/litre for 24 hr improves the germination in watermelon at low temperature. Seed dressing with Thiram controlled fungi effectively and also enhanced seed germination (17).

Cultivation:

Planting Season: In North Indian plains it is sown in February-March whereas in North eastern and western India best time of sowing is November to January. Optimum sowing time of watermelon in West Bengal is 15th of November. In South and Central India sowing is done in December-Janaury. In Rajasthan rainy season crop of watermelon is also sown in the month of August- September. In the Northern Indian hills the crop is sown in April to May.

Planting Systems: The sowing may be done on raised beds, furrows, trenches or pits as the case may be. The commercial crop is generally sown directly. Two seeds are sown per hill on both sides of raised bed or furrow and 3-4 seeds per hill in case riverbed trenches. In case of pit method, pits of size 60 x 60 x 60 cm are dug at recommended row and plant spacing and filled with FYM and soil in equal

proportions. Fertilizer may also be applied per pit. Four seeds per pit are sown. The seed rate would be 3.5-5 kg/ha with the spacing of 2.5-3.5 m between rows and 60-120 cm between plants.


Nutritional requirement: The crop of watermelon requires 15-22.5 tonnes of FYM/ha, which may be mixed with the soil before sowing or applied to the pits.

Pruning and Training: In watermelon apical shoots were removed and side shoots allowed to grow. When 3-4 side shoots were allowed to grow it gave significantly higher fruit yield than unpruned plants.

Irrigation: Less irrigation is required for watermelon when compared with other melons. It should be irrigated at an interval of 25-30 days.

Interculture: To keep crop free of weeds start intercultural operations 20-25 days after sowing. Depending upon the season about 2-3 weeding operations would be required. Several workers have reported that pre-plant application of bensulide @ 4 kg/ha or trifluralin @ 1-2 kg/ha can be used for weed control in watermelon (3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 17).


Varieties:

Sugar baby: This variety is an American Introduction. Ita vines are medium long. The fruits are slightly smaller weighing 2 to 5 kg, round having bluish black rind and deep pink flesh (TSS 11 to 13%) and brown small seeds, early maturing and yield 150 q/ha in 85 days.

Asahi Yamato : It is a mid-season Japanese introduction, producing medium sized fruits averaging 6 to 8 kg. The fruit is round, non-stripped skin and rind colour is light green with deep pink flesh (TSS 11 to 13%). The fruits ripen in 95 days. It yields 225 q/ha. Released by IARI, New Delhi.

Arka Manik: Fruits round to oval with green rind and dark green stripes, average weight 6 kg. Flesh deep crimson with granular texture, pleasant aroma, high sugar content (12 to 15% TSS) and few small seeds arranged in order. Possesses compined resistance to powdery mildew, downy mildew and anthracnose diseases. Yields 600 q/ha. Released by IIHR, Hessarghatta.

Arka Jyoti: Fruits oval, dark green with deep blue angular stripes, average weight 5-6 kg. Flesh bright crimson with good granular texture, pleasant flavour, high sugar (11 to 13% TSS) and low seed content. An early variety with good keeping quality. Yields about 840 q/ha. Released by IIHR, Hessarghatta.

Shipper: This variety is an American introduction. Its vines are long vigorous. Fruits medium to large weighting 6-10 kg, round, with characteristic tripartite blossom end; rind is thick and dark green; flesh red with moderate sweetness (8 to 9% TSS); seeds are uniformly light brown and its average yield is 300 q/ha. Released by punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana.

Special No.1: Fruits are round and small with red flesh and red seeds. It is early in maturity. The average TSS is 7-8%. Released by punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana.

Durgapura Meetha: A late cultivar maturing in 125 days, fruits round with light green rind. TSS 11% with dark red colour; average fruit lJ\aight 7-8 kg; seed with black .tip and margin. Good keeping quality. Released by Agricultural Research Station, Durgapura, Rajasthan.

Durgapura Kesar: A late cultivar, fruit weight 4-5 kg; skin green with stripes, flesh yellow, sweet, seeds are large and yellow. Released by Agricultural Research Station, Durgapura, Rajasthan.

There are several other cultivars, locally grown which are named after the region in which they are grown. However private seed companies also market hybrid watermelon varieties like Madhu, Milan, Hybrid-4, Hybrid-5, Surabhi, Maya, Madhuri, Khushboo, Madhubala and Red Honey (10, 11, 17).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

Spotted Cucumber Beetle: It is yellow-green in colour with 12 black spots on its back. The adults feed on young plants and larvae feed on the roots. High populations stunt or kill plants. Use sprays of Malathion for control.

Striped Cucumber Beetle: It is yellow in colour with black stripes and is a carrier for bacterial wilt. The insects may dig for emerging sprouts. Use sprays of Malathion for control.

Aphids: Dark bluish green aphids suck juices from the leaves. The leaves curl downward and plants are stunted. Aphids transmit cucumber mosaic. Use sprays of Malathion for control.

Thrips: They are small, cream to brownish insects, which rasp the undersides of leaves. The feeding causes deterioration of vines stressed from maturing the crop or by other factors. The damage is worse in dry seasons. Thrips are controlled with sprays of Diazinon.

Mites: Mites are tiny insects causing yellow specks and fine webs on the leaves. Heavy infestations stunt plants. Spider mites are controlled with sprays of Malathion or Diazinon (13).

Diseases:

Alternaria Leafspot (Alternaria cucumerina) : Lesions are round to irregular target spots on older leaves. The symptoms are first observed at the crown of the plant. As the spots enlarge, concentric rings are formed in the lesion. The disease is favored by continuous wet conditions. The fungus is controlled with a 2 year cucurbit-free rotation, destruction of the previous crop residue and application of fungicides as needed.

Cercospora Leafspot (Cercospora citrullina) : Leafspots have a dark brown center and a yellow halo. Infected leaves are first observed at the crown of the plant. When the disease is severe, foliage loss will restrict fruit development and result in sunburn of fruit. The fungus is controlled with fungicides. Applications should begin soon after runner development.

Anthracnose (Colletotrichum orbiculare) : Symptoms appear first on crown leaves as small, brown-black spots usually after vines begin to run. These lesions are also visible on the underside of leaves. The disease can be seed borne and symptoms can sometimes be seen on young transplants. The fungus produces sunken spots on the rind of fruit. Small anthracnose spots can be confused with pimples disease. A combination of seed treatment, crop rotation and fungicide applications are necessary for controlling this disease on susceptible varieties. Protective spray applications should be made when vines start to run and should be continued at 7 to 10 day intervals during periods of humid or rainy weather.

Powdery Mildew (Sphaerotheca fuliginea, Erysiphe cichoracearum) : The first symptom is a white to gray dusty material on the upper leaf surface. The fungus quickly spreads to completely cover the leaf surface. Once the leaf is covered, it dies and has a crisp texture. This disease is favoured by high humidity. The fungus is best controlled with fungicide applications when symptoms are first observed.

Downy Mildew (Pseudoperonospora cubensis) : The disease is most severe during periods of cool temperatures and rain. Irregular yellowish to brown spots, often vague in outline, appear on upper leaf surface near the crown. Brown spots later become more distinct on both sides of the leaves. The underside of leaves exhibit a brown to gray fungal growth on wet mornings, which disappears during dry weather. Excellent control of this disease can be achieved with mefanoxam, which must always be used simultaneously with a protectant fungicide such as mancozeb or chlorothalonil, to prevent the emergence of resistant strains.

Leaf Mosaic (viruses -Watermelon Mosaic Virus, Papaya Ringspot Virus, Zucchini Yellow Mosaic Virus, Cucumber Mosaic Virus) : Leaves of infected plants have a mosaic pattern, i.e., areas of light and dark coloration. This mosaic may be in association with raised areas on leaves. Leaves may also be distorted in shape. Diagnosis of the mosaic viruses can not be made with certainty from symptoms alone. Sometimes plants may be infected by more than one type of virus. Losses from virus infection are more significant if young plants are infected. The viruses listed above are aphid-transmitted and can overwinter in weeds. Virus control by insect control is not usually feasible. Weed control in and around the field may help to reduce losses, except for virus introduction via winged aphids.

Fusarium Wilt (fungus - Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. niveum) : The fungus causes browning of the xylem (water-conducting tissue) of the crown and runners. The fungus persists many years in soil and so, watermelons should not be replanted into infested soils for at least five years.

Damping-Off (fungi- Rhizoctonia solani, Pythium spp.) Seedlings wilt and die. The lower stems have a watersoaked appearance. Damping-off is usually a problem of direct-seeded watermelons germinating in cool, moist soil, but Pythium aphanidermatum can rapidly kill new transplants in warm soils. A soil application of mefenoxam (Ridomil Gold) at the time of planting will prevent damping-off caused by Pythium species. Other diseases of watermelons are: Lasiodiplodia Fruit Rot/Diplodia Stem-End Rot (fungus - Lasiodiplodia theobromae), Gummy Stem Blight (fungus - Didymella bryoniae), Charcoal Rot (fungus - Macrophomina phaseolina), Bacterial Rind Necrosis (bacterium - Erwinia spp.), Bacterial Fruit Blotch (bacterium - Acidovorax avenae subsp. Citrulli) (13).

Economic Potential and Uses

The seed contains 21.3% of oil, which is yellowish or greenish in colour. The oil has pleasant odour and taste, and is used for cooking and as illuminant. Oil is made up of non-saturated fatty acids, thus has high nutritional values. The seed is free from alkaloids and cyanogenetic glycosides and can be used as cattle feed (3, 5, 7, 14).

Uses

It is a common man's fruit relished by both rich and poor alike. The sweet juicy pulp of ripe fruit is eaten fresh. The fruit juice is also consumed by many after adding a pinch of salt and black pepper. It can be used for preparing pickles and sweets. The fruit juice forms a cooling and refreshing beverage highly valued during summer. It is reported to have diuretic properties. The seeds can be extracted from the fruit, roasted over a fire and ground into a white meal which is evidently rich in proteins and oil, and is tasty. Seeds are parched and eaten with other grains. The meal can also be used as a cosmetic, it is smeared over the body, evidently resulting in a smoother, healthier, reddish coloured skin. The young fruit and leaves can be cooked and eaten as a vegetable. The fruits contains 95% water, 0.2% protein, 0.3% minerals and 3.3% carbohydrates per l00g fresh weight. It is also a rich source of iron (3, 5, 7, 17).

Harvesting Practices
Watermelons must be harvested at the right stage of maturity and should come from strong healthy vines. A dull sound while thumping the fruit with a finger indicates the fruit maturity, whereas sharp sound denotes an unripe watermelon fruit. Dryness of the tendrils at the point of attachment to the vine is another indication of maturity. Other sign of maturity is that it changes its belly colour from green to yellow touching the ground. Seed extraction of watermelons can be done by hand on small scale, but for large scale extraction, flesh should be allowed to ferment. Watermelon seed can also be extracted mechanically by using the axial flow vegetable seed extracting machine. Seed yield of 225-250 Kg per hectare can be obtained (3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 17).

Produce and Products

Fruits are valued for its sweet pulp. Oil is extracted from seeds. Seed cake is used as livestock feed. Apart from that seeds have some medicinal properties.

Processing Techniques

The processing of watermelon seeds is carried out in bullock-driven wooden ghanis (kohlus) and powder driven ghanis (rotary mills). The ghani (kohlu) is devised on a pestle and mortar rotating system driven by a pair of bullocks. In semi-urban areas, where electricity is available, most of the bullock-driven ghanis have been replaced by motorized rotaries for crushing seeds. These rotaries take less space than the ghanis, but watermelon oil is not being exploited on a commercial scale (3, 14, 16).

Other Uses
Considered to be antiseptic, demulcent, digestive, diuretic, laxative, pectoral, repellant and the watermelon is used in folk remedy for alcoholism, aphtha, calculus, cancer, catarrh, cystitis, diabetes, dropsy, hepatic congestion, hypertension malaria, nephritis, renitis, stomatitis. strangury and urogenital ailments. The juice of the root has been used to check bleeding following abortion. The leaf decoction is used for fever, the fruit juice an antiseptic in typhus fever. In many different cultures, the seeds are prescribed for urinary ailments, like cystitis and gonorrhea. Seeds, like those of many other cucurbits are used as vermifuge. The rind is powdered and ashed and applied to aphthous sore mouth. Crude seed extract lowers the blood pressure. Ayurvedics consider the green fruit astringent, aphrodisiac, and refrigerant, the leaves hematinic. Unani consider the fruits depurative, diuretic, expectorant, stomachic, and use them for itch, scabies, and sore eyes. They consider the seed a stimulant to the brain. Dried, parched, and ground seeds are steeped in water and drunk to stop urinary burning. Skin is sometimes dried and used in the same way. Decoction of bruised and boiled seeds drunk as a diuretic. Expressed liquid from fresh seeds used as a vermifuge. Thick rind, bound around the forehead, believed to relieve headache. Rind is mashed and applied as a poultice for liver trouble (3, 7, 8).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

Watermelon crop is grown in garden lands and river beds in India. In river beds, mix cropping system is followed hence the exact area and production is not known. The sweet, juicy pulp of the ripe fruit is eaten fresh throughout the tropics and subtropical regions. The seed is also eaten as a snack after shelling and roasting. The fruit has cooling effect and is used as an expectorant, diuretic and stomachiec and in allaying thirst. India has varied agro-climatic conditions making the cultivation of whole range of crops possible from temperate to tropical. There is no proper survey of the foreign markets taken up regarding the quality requirements. Further, no new market has been explored. There is a need to have research and development activities of export oriented types in this crop (17).

Miscellaneous Information

Breeding: In India main objectives of watermelon breeeding are to develop an early variety and high yield which is giving round fruits, more sweet fruits (high TSS), uniform pink flesh, high proportion of edible flesh, less seeds and resistance to abiotic and biotic stresses. Several cultivars and F1 hybrids have been developed in watermelon in India. These varieties are developed by following several breeding methods such as introduction, selection, hybridization, heterosis breeding, modified backcross method, polyploidy breeding and resistance breeding.


Introduction: Some varieties were introduced from USA and Japanese in our country and those found promising under Indian conditions were recommended for commercial cultivation. These include Asahi Yamato from Japan, Sugar Baby and Shipper from USA.

Selection: Varieties Special No. 1, Durgapura Meetha and Durgapura Kesar are developed through selection.

Hybridization: By employing hybridization followed by modified backcross method a variety Arka Manik (from a cross IIHR 21 x Crimson Sweet from USA) has been develop by IIHR, Bangalore.

Heterosis breeding: Being a dessert fruit, increased yield alone will not be the main breeding objective and fruit quality is equally important. In fruit quality, besides sweetness (TSS), edible fresh proportion is also important since fruits of many local collections of watermelon have large proportions of white and less sweet flesh. IHR 6 x Charleston Gray, IHR 20 x Crimson Sweet, and Sugar Baby x Crimson Sweet were found to be promising hybrids that recorded more than 50% heterosis for yield, excelled over all other hybrids and parents in fruit quality, and were very sweet under Bangalore conditions. The Arka Jyoti (F1) released by IIHR. Bangalore in 1973 is a cross between variety (IIHR 20) and American variety (Crimson Sweet).

Polyploidy breeding: Polyploids were produced by treating seedlings at the cotyledon stage with an emulsion of 0.2% colchicine, stearic acid, morpholine and lanoline. Seed fertility in tetraploid plants was greatly reduced and triploid seeds germinated only when the seed coat was partly removed. Fruits produced on triploid plants had seeds devoid of embryos. At Indian Agricultural Research Institute, New Delhi a seedless watermelon, namely Pusa Bedana (a F1 between Tetra 2 (tetraploid) x Pusa Rasal) has been produced. The fruits are medium green in colour, weighing 3-4kg. The fruits are seedless.

Resistance breeding: The cultivar Arka Manik carries high resistance to powdery mildew and moderate resistance to anthracnose. High degree of resistance to red pumpkin beecle has been reported in the lines Sl. 120, S. No. 98, and Afghan (17).
