Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Trees with stellate hairs found throughout the greater part of India chiefly in mixed deciduous forests, but not in arid areas, it is common in the sub Himalayan tract. Light demander but can tolerate moderate shade in infancy, fairly frost hardy and drought resistance.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

Availability: Large supplies available in the east zone, comprising Assam, Bihar and W. Bengal. Central, western and south zones also supply fair quantities while limited supply from north zone.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tree with stellate hairs. Bark grey, exfoliating in irregular flakes or thin long strips. Leaves palmately 7-nerved, cordate, usually 3 -7 lobed. Lobes often angular, the mid-lobe the longest, glabrous above, hoary tomentose beneath. Flowers in terminal panicles, polygamous. Panicles covered with tawny tomentum. Involucral bracts 4-6, exceeding the calyx, spreading in fruit, persistent, strongly nerved, accrescent, in fruit. Sepals 5, connate below the middle, ovate, incurved over the fruit. Corolla white or pink; petals 5, emarginate, exceeding the calyx, clawed, with a tuft of hairs at each side of the base of the claw, filaments united in a tube little more than the half their length, then divided into 5 spreading branches, each carrying 3 subsessile anthers;. Staminal tube hairy at the base. Filaments longer in the male than in the carpellate flowers. Ovary 2 3-celled, ovules 2 in each cell. Capsule globose, woody, loculicidally 3 valved, about the size of a very small pea, covered with mealy tomentum, globose, top slightly umbonate. Seeds reniform, striate, brown-black.

Floral and seed biology:

Cultivation:

Natural reproduction is freely by seeds which have low germinating power but are produced in great abundance. Seeds are often washed up in early rains into heaps mixed with dead leaves and loose soil and such conditions are favourable for germination.

Artificial reproduction usually is done by transplanting nursery raised seedlings (2-3" high) either entire or after pruning the roots and shoots. Pruning has been reported to be advantageous in some cases. Stumps 0.4-0.9" in diameter are also suitable.

Direct sowing in UP, tree stumping in Mysore and propagation by root pieces under garden conditions in Dehra Dun has proved successful.

Growth rate is rapid. Comparatively short lived but its rapid growth after first season enables it to push its way through gaps in forests, till it reaches the height of 30 ft. or more when it is overtaken and suppressed by large and long lived trees. In teak plantations, light demanding teak suffers seriously if Kydia is not removed.

Coppices well, pollards satisfactorily and also produces root suckers.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Whole plant, Bark and leaves.

Uses:

Fuel: It is used as fuel with  But it is considered as bad fuel as it burns with unpleasant odour. Charcoal is prepared from the wood. Seasoned wood is made into planks which are useful for interior work in building construction. Wood is useful to a limited extent for agricultural implements, oars, carriage, spoons and ladles, matchbox and match splints, packing cases. Fairly good for match manufacture, boxes as well as splints, also good for cheap grade pencils. It is suitable for picture and slate frames but raising of grain has been observed during grooving and chamfering, also liable to crack while nailing. It is also suitable for veneers, brush backs, turnery, toys and other small articles, bobbins and shuttles, carriage and wagon work, shoe heels and core work. May also be used for cheap grade of pencils.

In Gujrat, timber is used for well construction, canal work, water wheels. Sapling stems are strong and elastic and suitable for banghy sticks.


Harvesting Practices:

Produce and Products:

Gums.

Chemistry:

Calorific value of wood: 5,067 cal., 9,122 Btu.

Processing Techniques: Green conversion and prompt stacking in open piles recommended. Kiln seasoning may also give satisfactory results.

 

Other uses:

Fiber is made from inner bark which is used locally for coarse ropes. fiber is strong when green but becomes brittle on drying. Ropes from the bark fiber are used for tying rafts. They are sometimes used as elephant drag ropes.

Young bark - Young bark is mucilagenous. Cold infusion of it is used in gur making for clarifying sugarcane juice. It also coontains gum.

Leaves - Leaves are lopped for fodder. Leaf paste is applied in body pains. Leaves are also used in poultice for skin diseases. These are chewed when there is a deficiency of saliva.

Miscellaneous Information


Wood: sapwood creamy white, wide; heartwood greyish-brown with a purplish tinge, small but sharply defined; soft, occasionally somewhat harder, light, sometimes slightly heavier. Specific gravity 0.38 – 0.56 air dry. Lustrous when quarter sawn. Straight grained, coarse to medium textured.

Easy to season, but liable to sapstain and surface discolouration during drying. Not durable in exposed situations. Fairly durable under cover.

Tree with bark is used for coarse ropes.
