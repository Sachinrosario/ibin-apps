DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  A tree has a dense rounded crown. Leaf oblong-lanceolate, 8-14 in. long and 2-4 in. broad, with pointed apex and toothed margin; the upper part of the leaf as well as the veins beneath are covered with hairs. The wood is pale yellow to light reddish brown, moderately hard and heavy, somewhat twisted-grained and rather coarse-textured.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
 The flowers are solitary, terminal,fragrant 6-8 inch across; sepals yellowish green, petals white, 2-3 in. wide. fruits large,6-7 in. wide, yellowish green, hard and tough. are , 3-5 in. diam,consisting of 5 closely fitting imbricate sepals enclosing numerous seeds embedded in a glutinous pulp. Seed is small and compressed

Breeding System :
Cross- pollinated

Phenology :
The tree flowers during May-August and fruits ripen during Sept- February. Flower large, 5-8 in diameter, hard, consisting of 5 closely fitting imbricate sepals enclosing numerous seeds embedded in a glutinous pulp. There is also report of flowering in April-May in some places.

Pollinators:
Insect do the pollination but some times seed dispersal under natural conditions is brought about by wild elephants, which eat the fruits and eject the seeds or by forest streams, which carry down until stranded the buoyant fruits, which fall into them.


Seed Biology :
Seed is small, compressed reniform with hairy margins embedded in a glutinous pulp.

Seed Germination:

For artificial reproduction, the seeds are sown about May in boxes or nursery beds, or seedlings that develop from fruits falling to the ground are collected and planted in the nursery beds. Seedlings are transplanted at the commencement of the first rains when they are one year old or 3-4 in high. Growth is moderately fast.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruits, Wood, and Bark.

Uses
  • Ripe fruits are gathered and thickened sepals, which are sour, used as flavoring in curries or made into jams and jellies. The acid juice sweetened with sugar is used as a cooling drink.


Harvesting Practices
Ripe fruits are harvested or fallen fruits are gathered and further processed.

Produce and Products
Wood is use as timber, It is also used as planks and rafters for internal work. It is also used for making tool handles, gun stocks and bottoms of boats. It is also used for oar and telegraph.

Other Uses :

  1. Ripe fruits are gathered and thickened sepals, which are sour, used as flavoring in curries or made into jams and jellies. The acid juice sweetened with sugar is used as a cooling drink.
    The bark is used in tanning since the bark and leaves contain tannin. Green leaves are used as feed for tussar silk worms.
  2. The fruits are said to posses tonic and laxative properties, and is used for abdominal pains. The bark and leaves are astringent. Dried leaves are used as a substitute for sandpaper in polishing ivory and horn.
  3. Timber is used in the form of planks and rafters for internal work. Also used for making tool handles, gun-stocks and bottoms of boats. It may be used for oars and telegraph posts. Selected planks are also used for cabinet work. Wood is also used for railway sleepers.
    Used for making gum stocks and in general construction. It makes a good firewood and charcoal.
  4. The bark, leaves and fruits are of medicinal importance and its fruit is used in curries as it possesses an acidic taste.
