HABIT & HABITAT :
 A thorny, small bush or a shrub, with widely divari- cating flexuous branches, found in Punjab and Rajasthan and in the peninsular India from Deccan and Konkan, Southwards, ascending to an altitude of c. 900m. The shrub grows in abundance in the grazing lands of the arid and semi-arid areas of India. In Rajasthan, it forms c. 14 per cent of the total composition of the grassland flora. It also frequently occurs in the cultivated fields of the arid region, as the farmers encourage it to grow for fodder in the fields in association with the crops.

Cultivation :
Cultivation for Fodder-Z. nummularia may be planted in overgrazed land, depressions, and in rocky and semi-rocky sites. There are very few trees and shrubsthat equal it in hardiness and thriftiness with respect to soil fertility and moisture. Because of its deep root- system, it thrives on shallow soils and in the driest oflocalities. It propagates naturally through root suckers as well as by seeds. Direct sowing and transplanting are also effective. Fruits ripen in November and may be usedlor seed. Germination is low (30 %) and the seeds take about seven days to germinate. Transplanting of one- year old potted seedlings, raised in nursery, gives high survival and results in good stand in the field. For Planting in sandy soils, pits of 60 cm. x60 cm. x60 cm. have been found suitable. Young plants need protection "om browsing, though once established, they can withstand fairly heavy browsing, regenerating freely "rough root suckers. One year old seedlings, planted "ith the break of monsoon during normal rainfall years, result in cent per cent survival

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Leaves.

Uses:
Some wild seedlings may be used as rootstocks.The leaves are used in scabies and other skin diseases. Smoke from the dried leaves is inhaled for the treatment of colds and coughs. The fruit is cooling and astringent and said to be useful in bilious affections. (Gupta et a!., 1966).
The shrub is grazed by animals when young and lender: but when the plant becomes hard, woody and thorny, only sheep and goats are able to browse on it. When the red-coloured berries appear in winter, the leaves fall or are collected, dried and stored as pala for feeding cattle, camels, sheep and goats. Being rich in digestible crude proteins and minerals, the leaves play a very vital role in increasing the carrying capacity and nutritional status of otherwise depleted and apparently poor scrub grazing lands. Also, the leaf fodder is abundantly available even when the associated grasses have dried. These shrubs check wind erosion, help in eposition of soil in and around them and bring about a change in the micro-habitat, causing con-genial conditions for the appearance of better species of perennial grasses. Besides being a potent source of fodder,it is also used for fencing, and for fuel with high calorific value. It has, therefore, a definite place in the integrated economy of the arid lands. Sociological studies made at the Central Arid Zone Research Institute, Jodhpur, show that more than nine-tenths of the villagers indicated their preference to Z. numnwiaria in the grazing grounds in the villages. Though the plant is not cultivated for its fruits, they are edible and have a pleasant sub-acidic taste. Fruits are eaten by shepherds and also sold to a limited extent. In some areas of Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh, the fruit is dried and the pulp made into powder for subsequent use.

Chemistry
The dry matter content of the green leaves was 90.4 per cent. The digestive crude protein and total digestible nutrients were 5.7 and 58% of dry matter. The leaves contain tannins (9%) 
The twig bark contains 12 per cent of tannin. The root bark contains cyclopeptide alkaloids nummularine A, B and C; mucronine-D; and amphibine-H.

Other Uses
It has been recorded as a lac-host from Gujarat, and Taimara (Bihar). In Punjab this species has been observed bearing Rangeeni strain of lac, Laccifer lacca . A study on the palatability and intake of various feeds by three species of gerbils showed that Z. nummularia seeds with pulp were preferred by the gerbils; the seeds are now being used as a bait for their control by saturating them with a lethal dose of poison The shrub yields very hard heartwood, dark in colour, with high calorific value (7,900 B.t.u.). It thus affords a good quality fuel and high quality charcoal. The wild bushes have successfully been top worked with commercial types of Z. mauritiana
