DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs with perennial tuberous rootstocks. Stem elongate, leafy. Leaves oblong or oblong-lanceolate, up to 30 cm or more, variable in breadth, glabrous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers in terminal spikes white, ascending and closely imbricate; bracts large, oblong, 2.5-3.8 x 0.5-2.0 cm, obtuse at apex, green, 1-flowered. Calyx shorter than the bracts. Corolla tube 5-6.3 cm long; segments linear, 1.5-2.5 cm long; staminodes lancealate, 1.5- 2.5 cm long; lip cuneate, deeply bifid, 1.3-2.0 cm broad not clawed, rounded. Capsules globose, glabrous.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
In Ayurveda system of medicine the rootstocks used in inflammation, asthma, pains, bronchitis and vomiting. In Unani system of medicine it is useful in laxative, vomiting, diarrhoea and pain. The rootstocks are also used as a stomachic, carminative, tonic and stimulant (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).


Chemistry:
Structure determination of a furanoid diterpene – hedychenone (fig. 1a) isolated from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

7-hydroxyhedychenone (fig. 1b) isolated from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Benzyl cinnamate, 1,8-cineole, benzyl acetate and lindyl acetate isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Detection of cineole, limonene, g-terpinene, b-phellandrene, p-cymene, linalool, b-caryophyllene and b-terpineol in rhizome essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Anew diterpene – 6-oxo-labda-7,11,13-trien-16-oic acid lactone (fig. 2) isolated from rhizome. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

The petroleum ether extractive of Hedychium spicatum rhizomes contains tritriacontan, ceryl palmitate, hedychenone and following fatty acids, myristic acid (6.95%), palmitic acid (25.3%), stearic acid (10.9%), oleic acid (19.64%), linoleic acid (17.4%), linolenic acid (7.43%) and unidentified acid (12.32%). (Bhatia et al, 1995). C.A. 1996, 125, 793349b.


Pharmacology:

Essential oil of rhizomes showed tranquillising activity of short duration; it depressed conditioned avoidance response, rotarod performance and potentiated pentobarbitone hypnosis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Hexane extract of rhizomes showed marked anti-inflammatory activity, whereas benzene extract possessed significant analgesic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for asthma (Rhizome, Malhotra and Balodi, 1984; Sharma et al, 1979); Blood purification (Rhizome, Malhotra and Balodi, 1984); Bronchitis (Rhizome, Sharma et al, 1979); Eye disease (Rhizome, Megoneitso and Rao, 1983); Gastic disease, tonic, check vomiting (Rhizome, Malhotra and Balodi, 1984).
