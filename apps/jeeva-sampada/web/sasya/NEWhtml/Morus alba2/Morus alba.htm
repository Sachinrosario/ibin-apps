Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Fruits are used for preparing beverages. The juice is fermented to yield spirituous liquors. (Ambasta, 1986)

The fruits are eaten directly as fresh and can also be used to prepare pickles, jam, jelly, sherbut and wine. The fruits have high nutritive and medicinal value. However, the fruit yielding potentiality and its role in animal and human nutrition has not been fully understood and exploited.
The nutritive value and composition vary with varieties of mulberry. Usually the fruit consists of moisture (85%), protein (0.7%), fat (0.4%), carbohydrates (12.2%), fibre (0.8%) and mineral matter (0.4%). They also contain calcium (80 mg), phosphorus (20 mg), iron 2.6 mg), Thiamine (58gm), nicotinic acid (0.2 mg), riboflavin (92 mg) and ascorbic acid (10 mg) per 100 gm of fruits.

It is also reported that mulberry fruits contain a flavonoid (possibly eriodictyol), malic acid, citric acid, pectin, mucilage and colouring matter. Reducing sugar constitutes bulk of the carbohydrates.

Mulberry fruit is principally used in the preparation of syrup which possesses regrigerant and laxative properties. The juice which is refrigerant may be used as a drink in febrile diseases and will check thrist and cools blood. Fruit juice is used for sore throat, dyspepsia and melancholia. It is commonly used for preventing high fever as febrifuge and given to patients with symptoms of fever during endemic malaria. It has the property of preventing diarrhoea and cold. However, it should be prevented when the patients have confirmed amoeobiosis. As the mulberry fruit juice has the capacity of reducing high fever, it is used to provide excessive strength for withstanding the effect of high fever. The fruit has special taste and attracts women who are on their family way. This is due to Vitamin C, which gives a sour taste and makes them chew the fruit repeatedly.
The fruits are being used for preparation of jam, jelly and juice. In Greece, it is fermented to derive intoxicating beverage. Mulberry fruit is having high source of Vitamin C, which is commonly used for the preparation of special wine and beer in most of the cold countries. The fruit can also be used as a colouring and flavouring agent. Mulberry can be utilized in a big way to increase the income. (Doreswamy and Naika, 2000)
