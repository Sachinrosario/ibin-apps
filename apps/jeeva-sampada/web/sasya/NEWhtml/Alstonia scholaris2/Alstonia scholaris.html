HABIT & HABITAT
In India it is distributed in tropical regions of western Himalayas , through the sub Himalayan tract, in eastern India, on the westcoast and in the moisture parts and in the Andamans. In the Himalaya it ascends upto 1000 m; also found in Bangladesh and Pakistan. Planted in the gardens.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrub, up to 2.6 m; leaves whorled, simple, 10-20 cm by 1.2-3.8 cm, lanceolate, finely acuminate, petiole short, slender; cymes subumbellate; flowers often racemose on the branches, 2.5 cm long, slender, top barrel- shaped; follicles 10-12 cm by 0.8cm, sword shaped, coriaceous, long beaked.
BSI: A tall tree, 6-17 m high with bitter milky latex, glabrous except the inflorescence; bark grayish, rough. Leaves 4-7 in a whorl, oblong-lanceolate or obovate, 10-18 x 3-6 cm, acute at apex, cuneate at base, entire at margin, shining, coriaceous, glabrous; petioles 0.5-1.2 cm long. Flowers greenish white, terminal, umbelliform cymes; peduncles 2-5 cm long. Calyx 2-3 mm long, pubescent; lobes ovate-oblong, 1.5-2.0 mm long, obtuse. Corolla tube 6-8 mm long, villous within, a ring of hairs at mouth; lobes oblong, 3.0-4.5 mm long, rounded or subtruncate at apex, pubescent. Follicles 20-40 cm long, slender, pendulous. Seeds linear-oblong, 3-5 mm long, flattened, fringed hairs at both ends.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
 Bark: in colic pain; Plant: used in the treatment of leprosy; Twig: hung in the room of the newly confined woman to lessen the activities of evil sprit on the new born, preventive and curative of diseases caused by change of season. Good for headache, sores and some sores, ragging fever, discharge of sperm with urine, hiccup, insufficiency in breast milk, gout, cold congestion, dyspepsia; Latex : caries, pimple pyorrhoea; Flower : asthma, respiratory troubles. Homeopathy: Malarial fever, anaemia,indigestion, general debility and other stomach ailments. BSI(Uses): The bark is used as an astringent, tonic, anthelmintic, alterative and antiperiodic. It si a valuable remedy for chronic diarrhoea, dysentery and catarrhal fever. Milky latex is applied in ulcers and mixed with oil in earache.

Modern uses:
 Bark: Known in commerce as Dita bark and is used in medicine as bitter, febrifuge and astringent, in treatment of malarial fever, chronic dysentery, diarrhoea and in snake bite; Milky juice : applied to ulcers.
Chemistry:
Chemical constituents:
Root and Root: echitamine chloride, a-amyrin, Lupeol-Oac, stigmasterol, b-sitosterol, campesterol, alkamicine-its Nb-oxide and Nb-methiodide, Y-akummicine, Nb-di-Me-echitamine, tubotaiwine; stem-bark: hydrochloride of echitam-ine, echitamdine, a glyceride of venotarpine, sterols, two isomeric lactones; Latex: caoutchouc and resine; Leaf : picrinine, nareline, akuammidine, picralinal, akuammigine, betulin, ursolic acid, b-sitosterol, flavonoids, phenolic acids, scholarine; flower : picrinne, strictamine, tetrahydroalstonine, n-hexacosane, Lupeol, b-amyrin, palmitic acid, ursolic acid.

NBRI: 

Triterpenes, a-amyrin acetate (m.p. 224-25°) and lupeol acetate (m.p. 215-216°) was isolated from the bark of Alstonia scholaris. (Chakaravati et al, 1960).

Structure of the quaternary alkaloid echitamine, (fig. 1) C22H29O4N2+ has been determined by an X-ray study. (Hamilton et al, 1962).

Indole alkaloid- picrinine (desacetyl-desformyl-picraline, m.p. 223-25°) was isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Chatterjee et al, 1965).

Echitamine chloride has been isolated as the major alkaloid from Alstonia scholaris root & bark. (Talpatra and Talpatra, 1967).

Neutral constituents a-amyrin and its acetate, lupeol acetate, stigmasterol and a-sitosterol were isolated from the root bark. (Talpatra et al, 1968).

Picralinal (Fig. 2) (m.p. 179-180°) was isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Rastogi et al, 1970).

Picrinine, strictamine (fig. 20), tetrahydroalstonine and uncharacterized indole alkaloid have been isolated from the flower of Alstonia scholaris. (Dutta, 1976).

Indole alkaloids, akuammicine Nb-methiodide, akummicine-Nb-oxide, y-akuammigine, Nb-demethylechitamine and tubotaiwine were isolated from the roots of Alstonia scholaris. (Boonchuay & Court, 1976).

n-hexacosane, lupeol, b-amyrin, palmitic acid and ursolic acid have been isolated from the flowers of Alstonia scholaris. (Dhar, 1977).

Pseudoakuammigine (Fig. 3) in addition to betulin, cersolic acid and b-sitosterol have been isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Banerji & banerji, 1977).

Alkaloidal glycoside- venoterpine glucoside (Fig. 4) and echitamine were isolated from the stem bark. (Biswas & Saharia, 1978).

Indole alkaloid scholarine (fig. 5) was isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris and characterized as 12- methoxyechitamidine. (Banerji & Siddhanta, 1981).

Flavanone glycoside Isookanin-7-O-a-L-rhamnopyranoside (fig. 6) was isolated from the roots of Alstonia scholaris and characterized a 8,3’,4’-trihydroxyflavanone-7-O-a-L-rhamnopyranoside. (Chauhan et al, 1985).

Scholaricine (m.p. 180° c), alkaloid has been isolated from Alstonia scholaris (Atta-ur Rahman et al, 1985).

An alkaloid 20(s)-19,20-dihydroxy condylocarpine (fig. 19) was isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris and structure has been elucidated  by NMR studies. (Atta-ur-Rahman, 1986).

Alkaloid 19,20-Z-vallesamine (fig. 7a) has been isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris and the stereochemistry has been deduced from NOE measurements. (Atta-ur-Rahman et al, 1987).

Monoterpene, P-menthane-1,2,8-triol (fig. 8) was isolated from flowers. (Datta & Mathur, 1987).

Indole alkaloid, Alstonamine (fig. 9) and Sitsirikine type indole alkaloid, rhazimanine (fig. 10) have been isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Atta-ur-Rahman and Alvi, 1987).

Crystal structure of picrinine was determined. (Ghosh et al, 1988). C.A. 1989, 111:15419j.

Indole alkaloid, alschomine was isolated from the leaves along with its C-5 Isomer isoalschomine (fig. 12) and known alkaloids picrinine, picralinal & nareline (fig. 13). (Abe et al, 1989).

Isolation of alschomine and isoalschomine from Taiwanese and Thai Plants, Picrinine, picralinal nareline, 6,7, secoalstonamine, N-demethyl-6,7, secoals tonamine and 19-hydroxytubotaiwine from Phillipine plant and alkaloids 6,7-decoalstonamine and N-methyl-6,7,secovasallesamine and 6,7-seco-alstonamine were obtained as major alkaloids along with pseudo-akuammigine, picraline and Na-methyleburnamine from Indonesian Alstonia scholaris . (Abe et al, 1989). C.A. 195196e, 112, 1990.

Alstonia scholaris bark fat, ash, protein, carbohydrate, alkaloids, vitamins, Ca & Fe were determined. Protein comprised 12 amino acids including 5 essential ones. (Yashi & Patel, 1989). C.A. 150578u, 111, 1989.

Lagunamine (fig. 11) (19-hydroxytubotaiwine, m.p. 230-35°), angustilobine B (fig. 12) acid (m.p. 230-235°) and iosbanine (fig. 13) (6,7,-seco-6-nor-angustilobine B, m.p. 108-110°) were isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris and 17-O-acetylechitamine was isolated from the bark along with echitamine. (Yamachi et al, 1990).

Isolation & structural elucidation of new alkaloids-19-epischolaricine (fig. 14), Nb-methylscholaricine (fig. 15), Na-methyleburnamine (fig. 16) and vallesamine Nb-oxide (fig. 17) from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Yamauchi et al, 1990).

Lupeol acetate, b-sitosterol have been isolated by conventional acid base seperation. (Khaleque et al, 1991).

Three indole alkaloids nardine ethylether, sepi-nardine ethyl ether and scholarine-N(4)-oxide in addition to nareline methyl ether, picrinine & scholaricine (fig. 18) were isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Toh-seok et al, 1997).

Four flavanoidal glycopyranoside and kaempferol-3-O-(2glu-rhamnosylrutinoside) in addition to the flavonoidal aglycons isohamnetin and kempferol have been isolated from the leaves of Alstonia scholaris. (Desoky, 1999).

From the hexane fraction of the alcoholic extract of the leaves of Alstonia scholaris five compounds have been isolated and identified as 4a,14a,24-trimethyl, 9b-19-cyclo-5a-cholest-24(29)-en-3b-ol, stigmasterol, betulin, betulinic acid and aamyrinacetate. (Desoky et al, 2001). C.A. 27505r, 134, 2001.  

Pharmacology:

Strictamine from Alstonia scholaris shows monoamineoxidase inhibitor activity by both in-vivo and invitro tests, picrinine exhibits CNS depressant but strictamine exhibits anti-depressant activity. (Bhattacharya et al, 1979).

Alstonia scholaris showed anti-microbial activity against streptomycs aureus, S. poratyphi B and Aspergillus niger. (Yashi & Patel, 1989). C.A. 150578u, 111, 1989.

The petroleum ether and methanolic extract of the bark of Alstonia scholaris were found to be devoid of anti-malarial activity in mice infected with plasmodium berghci. (Gandhi & Vinayak, 1990).

Echitamine chloride from Alstonia scholaris showed significant anti-cancer effects on methyl cholanthrene induced fibrosarcoma in rats. (Kamrajan et al, 1991).

Hepatoprotective effect of Alstonia scholaris on CCl4 induced liver injuries in rat by lowering D-galactosamine, induced serum transaminases (Lin et al, 1996).

Alstonia scholaris is one of the ingredient in tropical skin care preparation. (Nakayama et al, 1997). C.A.P. 23548q, 127, 1997.

Echitamine chloride, showed highly promising anti-cancer effect on the microsomal drug detoxifying system was studied in Sarcoma-180-induced mice. (Saraswathi et al, 1999).

Aqueous extract at 50mg/kg also enhanced phagocytic activity of  immuno suppressed mice. Significantly, at 50 & 100 mg/kg the extract prevents the decrease of immune system induced by predinisone, at 50mg/kg. Induced the cellular immune response while at 100mg/kg inhibited the delayed type of hypersensitivity reaction. (Iwo et al, 2000).
