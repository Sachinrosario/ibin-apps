HABIT & HABITAT
Prostrate, glabrous or puberulous, more or less succulent herbs; branches forked, up to 55 cm long. It is found occur throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves opposite or sub-opposite in unequal pairs, both linear or broadly obovate, the upper one pair 2.0-3.6 x 1.5-3.0 cm and lower 1.0-1.3 x 0.5-0.8 cm, rounded and often apiculate at apex, much dialated and membranous at base. Flowers sessile, pinkish-white, in pouch like petiolar sheaths. Calyx tube scarious; lobes 5, cuspidate, slightly petaloid. Stamens 10 or more. Capsules small, almost concealed in the petiolar pouch, lid truncate, slightly concave with 2 spreading teeth, carrying away at least one seed, the lower part 3-5 seeded. Seeds reniform, muricate, dull-black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The plant is bitter, hot and used as analgesic, stomachic, laxatives and alterative. It cures “kapha”, bronchitis, heart diseases, anaemia, diseases of blood and inflammation. The roots applied to eye to cure corneal ulcers, itching, dimness of sight and night blindness.


Chemistry:

·        Ecdysterone isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        3,4-dimethoxycinnamic acid and b-cyanin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Trianthema portulacastrum seed oil (12.5%) has been examined for its physico-chemical characteristics and fatty acid compounds. TLC of the oil into lipid cluster resulted into polar lipids (4.8%) and neutral lipids (95.2%). Fractionation of the neutral lipids provided hydrocarbons (0.3%) was esters (0.5%) sterol esters (4.0%) triglycerides 84.5%, free fatty acids 1.8%, diglycerides (2.5%) and mono glycerides (1.6%). The fatty acids which ranged from C8 to C20 have also been repeated. (Ashrat and  Riaz, 1996).

·        Extraction of Trianthema portulacastrum with dichloromethane has led to the isolation of a new flavanoid, 5,2’-dihydroxy-7-methyoxy-6,8-dimethylflavone (C18H16O5, mp 257 degrees), along with 5,7-dihydroxy-6,8-dimethylchromone (leptorumol) which has been previously reported from a fern species. X-ray analysis of leptorumol is also reported. (Kokpol et al., 1997) MAPA 9705-3093

·        An antifungal tetrapenoid named trianthenol 1 has been isolated from the chloroform extract of Trianthema portulacastrum. Its structure was established as 15-hydroxymethyl-2,6,10,18,22,26,30-heptamethyl-14-methylene-17-hentri acontene on the basis of spectroscopic data including high resolution mass and two dimensional NMR techniques. A benzaldehyde derivative 2, a pentacyclic triterpenoid 3 and benzoic acid derivatives 4-5 are also reported for the first time from Trianthema portulacastrum.  (Nawaz et al., 2001). MAPA 2001-02-1040.

Pharmacology:

·        The aqueous extract of the leaves of [T. portulacastrum] (at 50,100 or 200 mg/kg) protected the mice from the hepatotoxic effect of carbon tetrachloride as evidenced by significant reduction of elevated serum enzyme levels of SGOT, SGPT, alkaline phosphatase, sorbitol dehydrogenase and glutamate dehydrogenase as well as serum bilirubin and urea contents in both acute and chronic liver damage. Significant reduction in carbon tetrachloride induced hepatic lipid peroxidation was also observed. Marked reduction in fat degeneration of liver of test animals was also observed. (Mandal et al., 1994).

·        Effect of an indigenous diuretic namely Trianthema portulacastrum (Vaishakha) on the pattern of urinary and serum electrolytes in an experimental model using albino rats was studied. The urinary volumes and urinary excretion also was significantly increased in rats treated with water extract or with fresh juice of the drug. The animals in the control group did not show any significant changes. The above changes suggest that his drug is having diuretic action. (Murthy et al., 1996). MAPA 9606-3458.

·        The antihepatotoxic potential of an ethanolic extract of the whole plant T. Portulacastrum (excluding the root) was evaluated against EtOH-CCl4 induced acute liver damage in mice. Concomitant with EtOH – CCl4 treatment of dose of 50, 100 or 150 mg/kg was administered per os once daily for 3days. The substantially elevated blood serum enzymic activities of GOT, GPT, LDH, alkaloid phosphatase, sorbitol and glutamate dehydrogenase due to EtOH-CCl4 treatment were dose-dependently restored. There was inhibition of blood serum bilirubin and urea levels. The extract prevented the elevation of hepatic malondialdehyde formation and depletion of reduced glutathione content in liver of mice intoxicated with EtOH-CCl4. the plant possessed a potent hepatoprotective action against EtOH-CCl4 induced hepatocellular injury. (Bishayee et al., 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 43890c.

·        CCL4 was administered per os (p.o.) 3 times a week for five weeks. Daily administration (p.o.) of the extract at 100 or 150 mg/kg was started two weeks prior to the commencement of CCl4 treatment and is continued during the total period of the treatment. The extract affords the liver a unique protection against the toxicant-mediated oxidative stress contributing to the abolishment of lipid peroxidation through modulation of hepatic glutathione status. (Mandal et al., 1996). MAPA 9803-1545.

·        The ethanol extract of Trianthema portulacastrum dose-dependently decreased the activities of serum glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase, glutamate pyruvate transminase, lactate dehydrogenase,alkaline phosphatase, glutamate dehydrogenase and sorbitol dehydrogenase as well as serum levels of bilirubin and urea which were otherwise significantly elevated with the chronic CCl4 treatment. The extract also restored CCl4-induced inhibition of hepatic microsomal enzyme glucose-6-phosphatase. The extract showed a marked liver protective action comparable to that of silymarin, a standard hepatoprotective drug. (Mandal et al., 1997). MAPA 9706-3523.

·        The effects of an ethanolic extract of T. portulacastrum were examined on he[patic lipid peroxidation, glutathione status, and activities of antioxidant enzyme following chronic treatment of mice with CCL4. Daily oral treatment with the extract started 2 week prior to the commencement of CCl4 administration and continued for 7 consecutive week. The elevated lipid peroxidation of the liver due to the chronic CCl4 treatment normalized dose-dependency following treatment with the extract. The CCl4 treated mice showed some decrease in their hepatic-reduced glutathione (GSH) level, meanwhile their GSH/GSSG ratio decreased. The extract increased dose-dependently the activity of the glutathione reductase and glutathione S-transferase in the liver. These were otherwise reversed by CCl4 treatment alone. (Mandal et al., 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 336522g.

·        The chemopreventive efficacy of the Indian medicinal plant Trianthema portulacastrum L. was tested in a chemical rat hepatocarinognesis model in male Sprague-Dawley rats. Hepatocarcinogenesis was induced by the potent carcinogen diethylnitrosoamine (DENA). Treatment of the rats to the basal medium with aqueous, ethanolic and chloroform fractions of the plant extract a t a dose of 100mg/kg body weight once daily reduced the incidence, numerical preponderence, multiplicity and size distribution of visible neoplastic nodules. A decrease in the percentage of liver parenchyma occupied by foci seems to suggest the anticarinogenic potential of the plant extract in DENA-induced hepatocarinogenesis. (Bhattacharya and Chatterjee, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 145796f.

·        The chloroform fraction of Trianthema portulacastrum L. has been found to be very effective in restoring glutathione levels and the levels of Phase I (cytochrome P-450 monooxygenase) and Phase II (UDPGT) enzymes, which undergo substantial changes during chemical rat hepatocarcinogenesis. Various fractions of T. portulacastrum (crude aqueous, [ethanol EtOH] fraction, chloroform-fractions) in the form of plant extracts were administered to the control as well as to the experimental animals at a dose of 100 mg/kg body weight to the basal medium. After 22 weeks on this regimen, animals were sacrificed, and the different biomarkers were assayed from the hepatic tissues. The results obtained after treatment with the chloroform extract of T. portulacastrum were remarkable. The ethanol and crude aqueous extracts were less effective. Ethanol control, chloroform controls and Trianthema controls displayed similar values as that of normal untreated animals with regard to the different parameters tested. Our findings reflect strong anticarcinogenic potential of the chloroform extract of T. portulacastrum as far as these important biomakers are concerned. (Bhattarcharyya and Chatterjee, 1998)a

·        The effect of an ethanolic extract of the plant Trianthema portulacastrum on the CCl4-induced chronic hepatocellular damage of Swiss albino mice has been investigated. The administration of plant extract (at 100 or 150 mg/kg) restored the CCl4-induced alterations of the haematological parameters to the normal level. The extract of T. portulacastrum elicited a marked protection against CCl4-induced hepatotoxicity as indicated by the several haematological parameters, related indices of formed elements, and different fractions of plasma protein. The hepatoprotective effect of T. portulacastrum could be caused by its critical involvement in modulating several factors associated with erythropoiesis, and the boosting of general immunity of the host. (Mandal et al., 1998). MAPA 9901-0285.

·        The chemopreventive efficacy of the Indian medicinal plant Trianthema portulacastrum L. was tested in a chemical rat hepatocarcinogenesis model in male Sprague-Dawley rats. Treatment of the potent carcinogen diethylnitrosoamine (DENA) induced hepatocarcinogenesis in rats to the basal medium with aqueous, ethanolic and chloroform fractions of the plant extract at a dose of 100 mg/kg body weight once daily reduced the incidence, numerical preponderance, multiplicity and size distribution of visible neoplastic nodules. Morphometric evaluation of focal lesions showed a reduction of altered liver cell foci/cm2 and a reduction of the average focal area. A decrease in the percentage of liver parenchyma occupied by foci suggests the anticarcinogenic potential of the plant extract in DENA-induced hepatocarcinogenesis. (Bhattarcharyya and Chatterjee, 1998)b.

·        The underlying molecular mechanisms of the antihepatotoxic activity of Trianthema portulacastrum by monitoring its effect on mouse liver DNA-chain break, sugar-base damage and chromosomal aberrations, during chronic or acute treatment with carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) have been studied. T. portulacastrum extract offer unique protection (P< 0.05-0. 001) against the induction of liver-specific structural-type chromosomal anomalies 15, 30 or 45 days after the last CCl4 insult, compared to control mice. This was further evidenced by extract-mediated protection (15 days prior feeding following a single necrogenic dose of CCl4 of the generation of DNA chain-break and Fe-sugar-base damage assays. The observed hepatoprotective mechanism could be due to its ability to counteract oxidative injury to DNA in the liver of mouse. (Sarkar et al., 1999).

·        The chloroform extract of Trianthema portulacastrum L. showed remarkable effect in the reduction of DENA-induced rat hepatocarcinogenesis. (Bhattacharyya and Chatterejee, 1999).

·        The underlying molecular mechanisms of the antihepatotoxic activity of Trianthema portulacastrum by monitoring its effect on mouse liver DNA-chain break, sugar-base damage and chromosomal aberrations, during chronic or acute treatment with carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) have been studied. ·        A study was conducted to confirm the diuretic effect of Trianthema portulacastrum. Patients were divided into three groups G1, G2 and G3 having 10, 10 and 5 cases each. Drug was administered in the form of soft gelatin capsule containing powder of whole plant at doses 5gm/day/70kg body wt. In 3 divided doses. G2 patients were give fresh juice of plant (40mlday70kg body wt.) in two divided doses and G3 was treated as control. Patients of G2 showed more potent diuretic activity in the form of increased urine volume than G1 patients. Powder form had only mild activity. (Murthy et al., 1999). MAPA 2000-03-1509.

·        Different extracts of aerial parts of Trianthema portulacastrum were tested for hepatoprotective activity against CCl4­­ in rats. The alcoholic extract showed significant activity lowering the serum enzymes like GOT and GPT in rats intoxicated with CCl4 and were also comparable to that of silymarin. (Mehta et. al., 1999).

·        An antifungal tetraterpenoid named trianthenol 1 has been isolated from the chloroform extract of Trianthema portulacastrum. Its structure was established as 15-hydroxymethyl-2,6,10,18,22,26,30-heptamethyl-14-methylene- 17-hentriacontene on the basis of spectroscopic data including high resolution mass and two-dimensional NMR techniques. PHARMACOGNOSY

·         Microscopic and macroscopic characters, physical and other constants such as stomatal ratio (1:95), stomatal index (lower – 14.80 upper – 13.50) and frequency (lower 16.2mm2 and upper 73.2mm2), palisade ratio (O), vein-islet numbers (3.21/mm2), vein termination number (13.39/mm2) of leaf were examined. Microscopic analysis of the leaf powder invariably showed spherical mineral crystals, compound sieve plates, vessels with scalariform thickening and bordered pits, remnants of water storage cells in addition to common histological inclusions in a disorganized state. (Banerjee and Mukherjee, 1995). MAPA 9703-1413.

·         Stems of Trianthema portulacastrum standing as constant include respective lengths and widths of epidermal cell, trichome, hypodermal cells, endodermal cells, cortex radius, respective diameters of cortical cells, pith cells, length: breadth of vascular bundles, pith, instrastelar tissue, extent of secondary growth and phallemed. Microscopic analysis of the stem powder invariably showed remnants of tracheid, vessel, sieve tube, sieve plate, parenchyma, starch grains, fibres and collenchyma cells. Abundants of remnants of parenchyma, tracheids and vessels in general and presence of sphaeraphides, compound sieve plates and starch grains in particular have been suggested. (Banerjee and Mukherjee, 1998). MAPA 2000-05-2738.
