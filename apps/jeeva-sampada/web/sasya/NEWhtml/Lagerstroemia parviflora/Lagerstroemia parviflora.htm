Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Medium sized deciduous tree. Occurs throughout India in the plains ascending upto 1000 m. altitude. Drought resistant. Light demander and frost hardy.

Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

Availability:

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large tree, youngest branches sometimes slightly pubescent. Leaves elliptic-oblong, acute or acuminate, green and glabrous above, pale, sometimes hoary-tomentose beneath, conspicuously reticulately veined, narrowed or cuneate at the base; main nerves 6-10 pairs, acruate, prominent beneath. Flowers in large usually compound panicles, pedicels short, pubescent. Calyx usually hoary-tomentose; tube hemispheric, lobes triangular, acute, patent or often reflexed. Petals oblong-obovate, with a long slender claw. Capsules ellipsoid, not ribbed. Seeds cultriform, with terminal papery wing and thick stiff curved ridge along one edge.

Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Natural reproduction takes place by seeds. Fertile seeds are not produced in abundance every year. Loose, porous, bare soil appears to favour natural reproduction. Immunity from damage by grazing and good power recovery from the effects of fire and mutilation are factors which favour natural reproduction.

 

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Tree is subject to attack of white stump rot (Fomes durissimus Lloyd), dark brown heart rot (Fomes fastuosus Lev.), white spongy rot (Fomes pectinatus Kl.) spongy yellow heart rot (Fomes rimosus Berk), white pocket rot (Polyporus gilvus [Schw.] Fr.), tar spot (Rhytisma lagerstroemiae Rabenh.) and grey heart rot (Tramates incerta [Currey] Cke.)

Fomes robiniae – heart rot

Cercospora lythracearum – leaf spot from Khandala (Maharashtra)

Cercospora paramignyae Thirum. & Chupp – leaf spot from Jabalpur (M.P.)

Sphaceloma lagerstroemiae Wani & Thirum – infects leaves, petioles and young stem in Pune (Maharashtra)

Pestaliopsis guepini (Desm.) Steyaert – leaf infection from Dharwar (Karnataka)

Gloeosporium sp. – leaf infection from Coorg (Karnataka)

Helixanthera ligustrina (Wall.) Dans. - mistletoe

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

An excellent fuelwood. Calorific power 8.35% of pure carbon.

Charcoal made from lendia wood suitable for producer gas . calorific value : sapwood 4,951 cal, 8,913 Btu; heartwood 4,885 cal, 8,794 Btu.

Wood: Strong, hard, heavy. Average unit weight at 12 per cent moisture content 735 kg/m3. Specific gravity 0.75, 46-48 lb./ c ft. Sometimes exhibit very short wavy grain.

Wood used for building construction, fencing, bridges, boats, oars and agricultural implements, also for carts, cooperage, wood pipes and water tanks. Suitable for boxes, tool handles, tent pegs, wedges for ship’s hatches, golf stick shafts, leather cutting blocks, bent wood furniture, picker arms and telegraph poles. Selected timber with figured grain should make decorative furniture. Recommended for railway sleepers after treatment.


Harvesting Practices: Tree coppices and pollards vigorously and is eminently adapted for working as coppice, a rotation of 30 years being generally sufficient for the production of poles for building purposes.

 

Produce and Products:

Tannins and Edible gums.

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:

Difficult to air season as prone to end splitting, surface cracking and warping. Should be green converted, preferably in damp or cool weather and piled under cover, well protected from hot winds and sun and allowed to dry slowly.

Other uses:

Bark and leaves yield tannin which produce good fawn colour on light leather. Bark used to dye thread. It yields an inferior fibber.

Tree lopped for fodder.

Host for tussar silk worm.

It yields sweet edible gum.
