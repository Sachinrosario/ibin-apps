DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  A genus of erect, sparingly branched shrubs, with large pinnate foliage and conspicuous fruits, occurring in Eastern Himalayas and Western China. The follicles are rather large and oblong, and contain numerous seeds embedded in a white pulp.
A decidious shrub growing to 3.5m. is frost tender. The flowers are hermaphrodite (have both male and female organs). We rate it 2 out of 5 for usefulness.
The plant prefers light (sandy), medium (loamy) and heavy (clay) soils. The plant prefers acid, neutral and basic (alkaline) soils. It cannot grow in the shade. It requires moist soil.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination:
Seed - best sown as soon as it is ripe in a cold frame, it then usually germinates freely in early spring[K]. Sow stored seed in February in a greenhouse. This usually germinates well, within 1 - 3 months at 18°c. Prick out the seedlings into individual pots.
 Cultivation;
Prefers a rich moist loamy soil and a sunny position sheltered from cold winds. Succeeds in partial shade.
This species is not very hardy in Britain, it probably needs greenhouse protection in most parts of the country. The flowers and young growth in spring are very frost-tender and can be damaged by late frosts. The flowers are produced at the tips of the new upright growths in the spring.
