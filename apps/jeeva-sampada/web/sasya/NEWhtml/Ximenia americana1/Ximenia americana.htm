DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  A large straggling, variable shrub or a small tree, up to 4.5 m. in height, with spiny branches, semiparasite on the roots of other trees or self parasitic, found in the dry firests of the Deccan Peninsula and rocky coastal regions of the Andamans up to an altitude of c. 800 m. The plant makes an attractive hedge. Bark reddish brown, rough with deep fissures; leaves ovate to broadly elliptic, oblong or roundish, glabrous, 2.5-5.0cm*1.3-3.8 cm; flowers white or yellowish white, fragrant, in short, few-flowered, axillary racemes; drupes ovoid or ellipsoid, deep orange-redor scarlet whenripe, up to 2.5cm. long, one seeded.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
The ripe fruits, leaves, Kernels, Roots. Root bark and wood.

Uses:
  • The ripe fruits (Wild Plum, Wild olive) are aromatic and acidic-sweet, and are used as substitute for lemons. 
  • The flesh is used for making jams and jellies.
  • The raw fruits have the odour of the oil of bitter almonds.
  • In South Africa, the fruits are used for beer -making. The fruits and seeds are credited with laxative properties.
  • The kernels are eaten raw or roasted but only a few time as they may prove deleterious to health.
  • They are cooked and mixed with sago for bread making.
    In Karnataka, the kernels are crushed and boiled in water to extract an oil. On cooling the oil solidifies and is used as a substitute for ghee in South India and elsewhere.

Other Uses
  1. The syrup made from the fruits is used for dropsy and rheumatism.
  2. The roots are said to possess cathartic properties and have been used for venereal diseases 
  3. The powdered roots are used for treating inflammation of gums and toothache. 
  4. An infusion of the Pulped roots is used as an anathematic, and a hot water extract of the roots is used against sanguineous diarrohea and jaundice. 
  5. The freshly crushed leaves roots and leaves are applied locally for relief from febrile headache.
  6. The root bark is used against post-measles diarrohea in some parts of Uganda.
  7. It is reported that its extract may release an antibacterial principle with in the body, which shows healing effect locally in the gut.
  8. The bark is astringent and is applied to to sores on domestic animals asan insect repellent.
  9. It contains tanins(17% ) and is used to impart a reddish tinge to leather. 
  10. The crushed leaves have the smell of bitter almonds, and are sometimes used as flavoring. The young leaves are said to be boiled and eaten. 
  11. But in Australia the leaves are reported to be strongly cyanogenetic. A
  12.  decoction of the leafy twigs is used for febrile cold and cough, and as as laxative, also as a mouth washing, toothache, and as as an eye lotion in eye troubles.
  13. The wood is pleasantly scented when fresh, and is sometimes powdered and substituted for sandal wood. It is suitable for turnery, toys, etc.
  14. It is also used as a fuel and for making charcoal.
