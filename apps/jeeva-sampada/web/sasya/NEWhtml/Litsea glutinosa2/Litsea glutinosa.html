DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An evergreen shrub or tree, upto 25 m. in height and 1.5 m in girth, with a clean bole 6.0 m. back brownish grey, somewhat corky, viscid inside, leaves very variable in size and shape, elliptic – ovate or oblong – lanceolate, pubescent, aromatic; flowers in umbellate heads, yellowish; fruit globose, black or purple. Leaves arranged spirally, coriaceous, oblong-elliptic or ovate, 10-30 x 3-13 cm, glabrous above, lateral veins prominent beneath
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers arranged in 6-13 flowered umbel-like racemes, involucre present. Berry globose, 0.8-1 cm in diam., dark brown.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Stem and bark are used in boils, bone fracture, dysentery, inflammation, pain in umbilicus region, skin diseases and sprains. Bark is used in treatment diarrhoea and dysentry. Leaves are used in cough and cold. Leaves and flowers are employed in poultice and wounds.
Fruits are antiparalytic, anticephalagic, antihysteric, carminative, in dizziness and loss of memory.
CIMAP: Fruits are antiparalytic, anticephalagic, antihysteric, carminative, in dizziness and loss of memory.
NBPGR: Essential oil from fruits (berries) is a source of citral.  Other compounds include methyl heptenone, d-limonene, l-sabinene and citronellal.  The essential oil from bark yielded α and β pinene, camphene, linone, citronellol, citronellal, linalool, citral - a and b and geraniol. The stem-bark contains two alkaloids-laurotetanine and its N-methyl derivatives.


Chemistry:
Absolute configuration and crystal structure of (9s)sebiferine iodomethylalte (fig. 1) by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

A water-soluble mucilage containing arabinose and xylose in molar ratio of 3:4:1.0 isolated from bark and partially characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

The essential oils isolated by hydrodistn. of the fresh fruits of Litsea glutinosa (Lour.) C.B. Rob. have been analyzed for the first time.  The oils of green (unripe) and black (ripe) fruits of this species contained more than 40 and 30 compds., of which 28 (90%) and 22 (98%) constituents could be identified resp. by GC/MS.  Both the oils were characterized by a high proportion of (E)-b-ocimene (70.8% and 84.1%, resp.).  Predominant minor constituents of these oils were caryophyllene oxide (5.0% and 0.9%), b-caryophyllene (3.4% and 4.3%) and (Z)-b-ocimene (1.8% and 2.1%).( Choudhury et al, 1996)


Pharmacology:
A water-soluble arabinoxylan (D-xylose and L-arabinose in the molar ratio 1.0:3.4) was isolated from the mucilaginous bark of Litsea glutinosa (Lauraceae). The results of methylation analysis, partial hydrolysis, and 1H- and 13C-NMR. spectroscopy indicated a backbone of (1®4)-linked b-D-xylopranosyl residues substituted at both positions 2 and 3 with side chains composed of either single or (1®3)-linked arabinofuranosyl residues. Both a-L- and b-L-arabinofuranosyl residues were present. It is possible that side chains composed of two b-L-arabinofuranosyl residues are attached mainly at O-2 of some xylosyl residues. (Hearth et al, 1990).

The methanol extract of the bark of Litsea glutinosa showed antibacterial activity, comparable to chloramphenicol, against all 16 tested microorganisms. (Mandal et al, 2000).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for bruises, sprain (Bark, Gaur et al, 1980) .
