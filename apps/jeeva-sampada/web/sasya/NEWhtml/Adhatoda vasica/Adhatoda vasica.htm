DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A dense shrub 4-8 ft. high with many long opposite ascending branches. Stem with yellowish bark, terete, glabrous. Leaves elliptic-lanceolate, acuminate, minutely puberulous when young, glabrous when mature, entire, dark-green above, paler beneath, base tapering. Main nerves 10-12 pairs with reticulate venation between.

Flowers in short, dense axillary pedunculate spikes, towards the end of the branchlets. Peduncles stout, shorter than the leaves. Bracts elliptic, subacute, glabrous or nearly so, 5-7 nerved, closely reticulately veined. Bracteoles oblong-lanceolate, acute, with ciliolate margins, 1-nerved, reticulately veined. Calyx glabrous or slightly pubescent, divided to within 1/12 in. of the base; segments imbricate, oblong-lanceolate, acute, 3-nerved, reticulately veined. Corolla white, with a few irregular rose-coloured bars in the throat, pubescent outside; tube ½ in. long, the lower half cylindric, the upper half much laterally inflated. Upper lip ovate-oblong, curved, obtuse, notched; lower lip as long as upper, the lobes ½ in. deep, oblong, rounded, the middle lobe the broadest. Filaments hairy at the very base, long, stout, curved; lower anther-cells minutely apiculate (not white- spurred) at the base. Ovary pubescent; lower part of the style pubescent. Capsules clavate, subacute, shortly and bluntly pointed, pubescent; solid stalk flattened. Seeds orbicular-oblong, tubercular-verrucose, glabrous.

Other uses:

Leaves - A yellow dye is obtained from the leaves.

The leaves and the root are considered as efficacious remedy in all sorts of cough and colds.

Root, flower and fruit used for treating cold, cough, whooping cough, chronic bronchitis and asthama, as sedative, expectorant and antispasmodic.
