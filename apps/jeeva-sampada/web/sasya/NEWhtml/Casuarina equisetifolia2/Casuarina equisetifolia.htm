Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Casuarina equisetifolia has been introduced into India, cultivated on the coastal regions from Gujarat to Orissa, some parts of West Bengal and in the Andamans.

Introduced as a timber tree, evergreen wind break and sand binder and now cultivated for ornament, topiary work hedges and for fuel.

It is a fast growing, draught-hardy, care-free species for sites and climates as varied as coastals and dunes, hot humid tropics and even semi-arid regions. The tree remains unaffected even by cyclones in coastal areas. It can be grown upto an alt. of 1,500 m. It is characteristically a sea-shore plant.

The tree thrives best on loose, sandy soils and also on latert, rich, loam soils and some marshy places in open situation.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A tall, straight-stemmed, evergreen tree, generally 15-25 cm. but can attain heights of upto 50m, 0.60-1.20 m or upto 1.8m in girth.

Branches bear clusters of long, slender, green, drooping, cylindrical, jointed needle-like brachlets, or cladodes, internodes 3-8mm long. Leaves whorled, reduced to minute scales or spine like as in Equisetum or Pinius; bark grey rusty brown or deep maroon, brittle with cream blaze, peeling off in vertical strips; female in ovoid or cone like heads; fruits echinate, hard, 1-2 cm in diam; seeds light, membranous.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology: The tree flowers during Feb-April and again during Sept-Oct. The fruits ripen in June and December.


Pollinators: Wind

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination

The tree is normally propagated through seeds. Vegetative propagation through air-layers and suckers is also know. Side or lateral shoots can be treated with hormones to induce root before transplanting. The viability of seeds is low, hence fresh seeds from 12-18 years old trees are better for sowing. They are collected, dried and stored in earthen jars.

Seed is sown usually in Nov. in well prepared and matured nursery beds 3.6m x 1.2 m. One hectare of plantations requires about 1/2 kg seed for nursery sowing. The nursery beds are made up of light 8 porous soil mixed with fine manure and ash, and levelled. The beds are watered with a fine 'rose' to prevent washing off. They are spread with BHC or Gammexane to prevent caterpillars.

Germination starts in 7-10 days and is complete in a 30 days. Early and complete germination is obtained if the beds are covered with straw or palm-leaf.

The first transplanting of seedlings is carried out 6-18 months after sowing with the onset of South-West monsoon on the West-Coast, and at the time of North-East monsoon on the East coast. The seedlings of 7-10 cm are pricked out and placed 7-8 cm apart in beds in the open and watered thrice a day. These are again transplanted in the field when 3-60 cm in height.

A spacing of 2.7m on all sides is recommended in South India and in Orissa, 3.6m in Maharashtra and Gujarat. In A.P. direct transplanting is done.

If the plantation is grown for fuel, moderate spacing with no thinning and with a short rotation is recommended. If cultivated for timber, moderately close planting with long rotation and thinning is practiced.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Few diseases, pests and angiospermic parasites have been recorded on the tree, most commonly on the seedlings in the nursery and on the logs and stumps.
Pests:
Among pests, the crickets, chiefly Brachytrypes achatimus Stell and B. portentosus (Licht), attack the tree Arbela tetraonis, a bark eating caterpillar, Coelosterna scabrator Fabr, a long corn and the grubs of Dryctes rhinoceros causes considerable damage to the plantations.

The mealy bugs, Icerya aegyptiaca Dougl, J. formicarium Newstead and Pericerya purchasi (Mask) and Labioproctus spp. also attack the tree. Ants, crabs and grasshoppers cause damage. Lorathus spp. and Cassytha filiformis Linn. attack plantation in Karnataka and Gujarath.

An interval of 2 years between felling and new plantations and planting other spp. such as Anacardium occidentale Linn., Pitecellobium dulce Benth., Dalberga sissoo Roxb., Azadiracta indica Juss, Pongamia pinnata Pierre, Sapindus trifoliatus Linn and Syzygium cuminii (Linn) Sked is recommended as preventive measures.


Diseases:

Trichosporium vesiculosum Butler - causing blister disease is favoured by excess water and congestion. Can be prevented by avoiding pruning and lopping of branches and introducing sanitary practices such as trenching.

Macrophomina phaseoli (Maubl) Asby - virulet wilt in seedlings.

Daedalea flaida LeV, Fomes durissinus Lloyd, F. fastuosus Liv, F. Senex Nees and Mont on the logs and stumps; Trametes floccose Bens. on bark and trunk, Ganoderma lucidum (Leys. ex. Fr.) Karst. at the base of the tree

Deplodia Fr. sp. on the needles, Phoma casuarinae Tass. A symbiotic fungus is found in all parts of the tree.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Timber - The wood is strong, heavy to very heavy (wt. 849-997 kg/m3) ard, clox and straight - grained and medium to fine texture. The sapwood is orange-yellow or pale brown; the heartwood is light to dark reddish brown. The timber is moderately durable and resistant to termites and wood rotting fungi in exposed situation.

The wood known as BEEF WOOD is extensively utilized as fuel, and for charcoal. The ashes retain heat for a long time.

The wood is employed for posts and poles, masts, mine-props, rafters, oass, and helms, yokes, shafts for sledge-hammers, mallets, clubs, walking sticks, shingles, gates and fencing and felloes of wheels.

The wood is suitable for paper-pulp, hardboard and chip board. It is a promising raw material for writing, printing and wrapping paper.
The average length of fiber of the pulp is 1.08 mm and diameter. 0.11 mm. The unbleached pulp has been found suitable for the production of wrapping paper. The bleached pulp, produced from a mixture of hardwoods with equal proportions of Casuarina, sal and salai (Boswellia serrata Roxb. ex. Colebr) possessed satisfactory properties for the production of writing and printing paper.

Soil Conservation and Reclamation

The plant is cultivated in coastal region for soil reclamation and for soil erosion control. This is suitable for grassy blanks, estuaries, river banks and water ways also. It is grown as evergreen hedges, shelter belts, windbreaks, avenue trees and as sad-binder. Also recommended for agro-social forestry.

BIOMASS PRODUCTION - The plant produces a greater biomass per unit area per unit time. An appreciable amount of nutrients are added to the soil through decomposition of litter.

NITROGEN FIXATION

The plant has the ability to form root nodules and fix atm. N2. The plant is nodulated by an actinomycetes, Frankia sp. No fixing property of the plant is useful for the cultivation of the plant in poor soils and also for subsequent spp. in the area.

Parts Used:

Stem, tree bark, needle, fruit, resin.


Harvesting Practices:
The plantation are worked under clear-felling system, with a rotation ranging from 7-35 years. 33 year rotation yield greater volume of wood, shorter rotation of 15 years is considered profitable, and silviculturally, 7 year rotation is good.

Produce and Products:
· Writing, printing and wrapping paper.
· Hardboard and chipboard.
· Tree bark - contains tannin.


Chemistry:
The bark contains catechol-type tannins (6-18%). The tannin is reported to be better than that obtained from some mangroves. The barks contains (+) - gallacatechin, (+) - catechin, (-) - epicatechin, (-) epigallocatechin, gallic acd, protocatechnic acid, methyl gallate and several amino acids, polyols, and alicyclic acids.

The needles contain quercetin, isoquercetin, aempferol, (+) - catechin, ellagic acid and amino acids, polyols and alicyclic acid. They contain phytosterols and exhibit antibacterial activity.

The fruit contains B-sitosterol, gallic acid, prolocatechnic acid, hydroquinone, quercetin, kaempferol and its 3-B-D-galactoside (trifolin), ellagic acid, juglanin, afzalin, (+) - catechin, (+) - gallocatechin, (-) - epicatechin-3-gallate and (-) - epigallocatechin-3-gallate.

Other uses:

Bark - the bark is reddish maroon and blue black in colour. It is a tonic, astringent. It is useful in diarrhoea, dysentery, treatment of acne (powdered bark). A lotion is efficacious as gargle for sore throat, in beri-beri, in coughs, ulcers, constipation, stomach ache. Tannin from bark is used for dying cool and silk fabrics yellow, for tanning hide.

The tannin is reported to be better than that obtained from some mangroves. A blend of its bark tan with that of dhawa (Anogeissus latifolia (Roxb.) Wall. ex. Bedd.) can replace konnam (Cassia fistula Linn.).

Fresh bark is rubbed on fishing linens to toughen and preserve the fishing nets.
Needles are made into compost and ash is used as manure. Used as a remedy in ear disease, possess anticancer activity.
