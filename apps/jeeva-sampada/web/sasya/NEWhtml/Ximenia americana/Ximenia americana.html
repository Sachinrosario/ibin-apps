Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Ximenia is a small genus of shrubs and spiny trees, distributed in the tropics. Ximenia americana is a large, straggling, variable shrub or a small deciduous tree, 4.5-8 m. in height, with spiny branches, semi-parasitic on the roots of other trees or self-parasitic found in the dry forests of the Deccan Peninsula and rocky coastal regions of the Andamans, mostly solitary tree dispersed in open country, savannahs, understorey of dry forests, in dry woodlands, or on riverbanks. up to an altitude of 800 m. It is drought tolerent and grows in the areas with 300-1250 mm rainfall with 14-300 C temperature. It thrives on many kinds of soils, often poor and dry, including clays, clay loams, loamy sands, sandy clay loams and sands (5, 6).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Ximenia americana is a small to medium sized, much branched spiny tree with long vine like thorny branches. Bark reddish brown, rough with deep fissures. Twigs bear small scales, spines, 1 cm thin and straight. Leaves alternate, ovate to broadly elliptic, simple or tufts, oblong or roundish, glabrous, up to 7 x 3cm, blue-gray-green, folding upwards along midrip, tip round or notched (2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11).

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers white or yellowish white in colour, small, bisexual, or sometimes polygamous, 10 mm long in short racemes which are axillary or on the ends of lateral twigs, buds oblong, subacute, pedicels short, bracts minute, subulate. Calyx small, glabrous, deeply divided, 5 lobed, ovate, acute, ultimately reflexed. Petals much longer than the calyx, 11 mm long, equalling the stamens in the length, linear oblong, acute, shaggy inside. Ovary glabrous, ovoid conical, longitudinally sulcate and rugose (2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11).

Phenology :
Ximenia americana flowers and fruits throughout the year, independent of climatic regimes. Seeds are collected in July and August (6, 7).

Pollinators:

Seed Biology :
Fruit is a drupe, ovoid or ellipsoid, deep orange-red or scarlet when ripe, up to 2.5 cm. long, one-seeded. The kernel are soft and cream-coloured, with a filbert-like taste. It amounts to 68-75% of the tallownut (seed) or 45-59 % of the whole ripe fruit (5).

Back to Top

Seedling Germination :
Fruits are thin skinned, plum-like and must be picked from the tree as they perish quickly. Pretreatment of the seed is not necessary. Fresh seed should be sown for satisfactory germination. The seed can be stored for long periods (6).

Cultivation:

Ximenia americana is propagated from seed and cuttings. Vegetative propagation is probably possible. The tree regenerates naturally from seed and coppices. Regeneration in natural forests is very sparse, so partial protection of its natural habitat could promote natural regeneration. No attempt has been made to cultivate this crop on large scale. Hence very little information is available on the cultivation of this species (6).

Economic Potential and Uses

Seeds yield 49 per cent of pale yellow non-drying oil without any unpleasant odour or taste. On cooling, the oil solidifies and is used as a substitute for ghee in South India and other parts of the world. The oil is reported to be used for lubrication and soap and candle-making. It is stated to be suitable as a pomade for promoting hair growth. Black or red vegetable dyes are mixed with the oil for hair dyeing. The oilcake is reported to be unsuitable for livestock feed, but can be used as manure. The roots also contain fat which is rich in acetylenic acids, including xymenynic acid (5, 6, 7, 10).

Parts used

Fruits, seeds and leaves

Uses

The ripe fruits are aromatic and acidic-sweet and are used as a substitute for lemons. The flesh is used for making jams and jellies. In South Africa, the fruits are used for beer-making. The kernels are eaten raw or roasted, but only a few at a time, as they may prove deleterious to health. They are cooked and mixed with sago for bread- making. The oily kernels are used for softening leather. The leaves are used for flavouring and the young leaves are said to be boiled and eaten. Bark contains 17 % tannin and is used to impart a reddish tinge to leather. The wood is slightly lustrous, highly durable, twisted-grained, fine-textured, tough, very hard and heavy. It is easy to work and takes a high natural polish. The wood is pleasantly scented when fresh and sometimes substituted for sandalwood. It is suitable for turnery, toys, etc. It is also used as a fuel and for making charcoal (5, 6, 7, 10).


Harvesting Practices
There is no organised way of seed production or seed collection in India. In rural areas, people manually collect the seeds from the forest floor.

Back to Top


Produce and Products

Frutts are used in the preparation of jams and jellies. Oil is extracted from the seeds, which is used for soap and candle making. Wood is used for turnery works. Oil cake can be used as a fertilizer.

Processing Techniques

In Karnataka, the kernels are crushed and boiled in water to extract an oil. The oil can also be expresssed from direct direct expression of seeds in a hydraulic press or in an expeller. The expressed oil (at 50° in a hydraulic press) was very viscous and mucilaginous. A sample of the oil extracted with petroleum ether was pale yellow without any unpleasant odour or taste (5).

Other Uses
Leaves: A decoction of the leafy twigs is used for febrile cold and cough and as a laxative, also as a mouthwash in toothache, and as an eye-lotion in eye troubles. An ethereal extract of the leaves gave positive test for the presence of free triterpenoids or steroids.

Fruits: The fruits and seeds are credited with laxative properties. A syrup made from the fruits is used for dropsy and rheumatism. An infusion of the bruised fruits is given to calves for the treatment of sanguineous diarrhoea.

Bark: The bark is astringent and is applied to sores on domestic animals as an insect-repellent.

Roots: The roots are said to possess cathartic properties and have been used for venereal diseases. The powdered roots are used for treating inflammation of gums and toothache. An infusion of the pulped roots is used as an anthelmintic and a hot-water extract of the roots is used against sanguineous diarrhoea in livestock. A decoction of the roots and leaves is used as a febrifuge and also for diarrhoea and jaundice. The freshly crushed roots and leaves are applied locally for relief from febrile headache. The root bark is used against post-measles diarrhoea in some parts of Uganda (5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 13).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

The species is multipurpose and useful for arid and semi-arid areas as it is drought resistant. The wood is heavy, hard and very durable. The seed contains a non-drying oil suitable for soap and lubrication. It has also been used as body and hair oil and for softening leather. Furthermore the roots, bark and leaves of this species are used for medicinal purposes (10).

Miscellaneous Information

The edible qualities of the kernels may vary due to the presence of a cyanogenetic principle which depends upon the climatic and edaphic factors. Considerable fluctuation in the content of hydrocyanic acid may account for the contradictory findings with regard to the toxic nature of the plant or its products. The chief cyanogenetic principle present is sambunigrin, which on enzymatic hydrolysis yields d-mandelonitrile and glucose. Mandelonitrile on further hydrolysis yields hydrocyanic acid. The kernels are rich in proteins and fat but are regarded inferior to the other oilseeds due to the presence of a rubber like substance (5).
