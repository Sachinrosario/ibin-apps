DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An erect branched aromatic herbs, c 15-60 cm high. Stem 4-angled, minutely hairy. Leaves long petiolate, lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate, 1.5-8.0 cm long, acuminate at apex, narrowed at base, serrate or double serrate along amrgin, puberulous on both surfaces, prominently gland dotted beneath; petioles 0.2-2.5 cm in length. Flowers in terminal dense spike, bearing flowers only one side; bracts orbicular, pointed. Corolla purplish or pinkish, tube exserted. Nutlets elliptical, tapering at base, ridged, brown.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Pubescent, broad spikes; Flower:  Minute, bracteate, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Calyx 5-toothed, throat naked, teeth triangular ; Corolla: Pink or purpulish, base exserted ;Androecium: Stamens 4, divergent, filaments naked, anthers divaricate ; Gynoecium: Ovary superior, syncarpous, bilocular style bifid ; Fruit:  Nutlets oblong, smooth; Seed: Smooth . Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The plant is carminative and astringent.


Chemistry:
Apigenin and its 7-O-glucoside, luteolin-7-O-glucoside and linarin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

5-Hydroxy-6-methylflavanone- 7-0-galactopyranoside, 5-hydroxy-6,7-dimethoxy. flavone, 5-hydroxy- 7 ,8-dimethoxyflavone, 5-hydroxy- 7 ,4'-dimethoxyflavanonol and 5,7-dihydroxy-4’-methoxyflavone isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of 6-methyltritriacontane, 13-cyclohexylhexacosane, b-sitosterol, its glucoside and palmitic, linoleic, linolenic and ursolic acids, 5,7 -dihydroxy-4'-methoxyflavone, 5-hydroxy- 7,4'- dimethoxyflavone and acacetin-7-0-glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Linalool, ethyl benzoate, a-carvone, limonene, a-terpinene, dihydroactinidiolide, 2-methoxy-l,3,5-trimethylbenzene, l-octen-3-ol and aromadendrene identified in essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

b-sitosterol, its glucoside, ursolic acid, 2a-hydroxyursolic acid, tormentic acid, negletin, apigenin and luteolin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Naginataketone as main component of essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

A compound which exhibited long UV- mediated antiviral activity, was isolated from the leaves and twigs using bioassay-guided fractionation and identified as the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon fluoranthene. (Yip et al, 1995) C.A. 1995, 122, 273867x.

Chemical constituents of the oil were found to posses different compounds. One oil contained rosefuran (84.8%)as major constituent, whereas other oil was rich in dehydroeslsholtzia ketone (65.2%). (Thappa et al, 1999) C.A, 1999, 130, 309053k.

Essential oil from Elsholtzia ciliata where analysed by capillary GC and GCMS. Main constituents were neral (15.2 – 20.5%), geranial (19.5 – 26.5%), limonene (10.9 – 14.2%) and (Z)-b-farnesens (10.8 – 11.7%). The oxygenated compounds found such as linalool, neral, geranial, geraniol, neryl and geranyl formates and acetates, (Z)- methylgeranate and (Z)-nerolidol, contribute to the characteristic odor of these oil. (Dung et al, 1996.) MAPA, 1996, 9605-2974.

CIMAP:
Capillary gas chromatog. (GC), GC-MS, and 1H, and 13C-NMR spectroscopy revealed that the essential oil of E. cristata growing in the kumaun region contains 88% dehydroelsholtzia ketone along with some minor acylfuran derivs. The species with respect to its oil compn. is unlike that which grows in Japan and Kashmir and contains elsholtzia ketone as the main constituent1.
