REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Long narrow spike ; Flower: Minute, yellowish, polygamous; Calyx: Minute, campanulate, equal, 5-lobed ; Corolla: Oblong in bud, long, equal, narrow ;Androecium: Stamens 10, free, shortly exerted, filaments filiform, anthers crowned with glands ; Gynoecium:  Ovary sub-sesile, many ovules, styles filiform; Fruit: pods woody, very large, disoid ; Seed: Compressed, orbicular .

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Structure of entagenic acid (fig. 1) isolated from kernels elucidated as 3b, 21a,22a-trihydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

O-(b-D-Glucopyranosyl)- L-tyrosine (fig. 2) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A volatile gas liberated during acid hydrolysis of crude saponin identified as methylmercaptan; echinocystic acid isolated from acid sapogenin fraction of the hydrolysate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

b-sitosterol, a-amyrin, quercetin, gallic acid and cyanidin chloride isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Dopamine-3-O-glucoside (fig. 3) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Prosapogenin A, b-sitosterol and lupeol isolated from seeds; hydrolysis of prosapogenin A yielded entagenic acid and D-glucose, L-arabinose and D-xylose (1:1:1). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Structure of entagenic acid confirmed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Myristic, palmitic, stearic, arachidic, behenic, oleic, linoleic and linolenic acids obtained from seed oil hydrolysate; triglycerides comprised of monosaturated dioleinand dilinolein, saturated oleolinolein, diolinolein, triolein, oleodilinolein and trilinolcin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Fatty acid mixture from oil contained saturated acids (18.0),oleic acid (45.0) and linoleic acid (37.0%).(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A process is developed for preparation of entanin from seed kernels. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Revised structure of entagenic acid (fig. 4). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

A new sulphur-containing amide -entadamide A (fig. 6) isolated from seeds and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

In addition, entadamide B (fig. 7) also isolated from seeds and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Two new Entada saponins -ES II (fig. 5a) and ES IV (fig. 5c) isolated from bark and characterised as 3 – O - [b - D – xylopyranosyl (1à2) a - L -arabinopyranosyl(1à6)][b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)]-2-acetamido-2-deoxy-{3-b - D – glucopyranosyl – 28 – O - [b - D – apiofuranosyl (1à3) - b - D –xylopyranosyl (1à2)] [2 – O – acetyl - b - D – glucopyranosyl (1à4)] – 6 – O -[6(R)6- hydroxy - 2, 6-dimethyl- 2(E)2, 7-octadienoyl] - b - D – glucopyranosyl - oleanolic acid and -entagenic acid respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Entada saponin ES III (fig. 5b) isolated from bark and characterised as echinocystic acid bearing same carbohydrate moieties as in case of ES II and ES IV. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Isolation and structure determination of a homogentisic acid glucoside -phaseoloidin (fig. 8) from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

A new amide - entadamide C (fig. 8) isolated from leaves along with entadamide A; its structure and absolute configuration determined and confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Isolation and structure determination of 2-hydroxy-5-butoxy-phenylacetic acid, 2b-D-glucopyranosyloxy-5-butoxyphenylacetic acid, entamide A-b-D-glucopyranoside and 2,5-dihydrophenylacetic acid methyl ester from Indonesian plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for anasarca, cancer (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Cuts, wounds (Leaf, Joseph and Kharkongor, 1981); Dropsy, epilepsy (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Eye disease (Seed, Mudgal and Pal, 1980); Liver complaints (Seed, Joseph and Kharkongor, 1981); Snake bite (Seed, Murthy et al, 1986); Veterinary diarrhoea, Swell throat (Seed, Pal, 1981); Edible (Seed, Arora, 1981; Borthakur, 1981; Bhargava, 1981); Symbolic charm (Seed, Mehra et al, 1975); for washing hair (Rao, 1994, MAPA 1995, 9503-1937).


Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:
2-hydroxy-5-butoxyphenylacetic acid and 2,5-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid methyl ester were cytotoxic to cultured P-388 cells with ED50 values of 1.0 and 1.7 mg/ml respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
