DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A large glabrous evergreen tree 12-15 m. high with a compact leafy head and short erect trunk bark smooth scaly.  Leaves are 6.3-10 by 3.2 - 5 cm. elliptic, shortly acuminate, glabrous, base a cute or rounded; petioles 1.3 - 2.5 cm. long.  Flowers white, fragrant, nearly 2.5 cm. across, solitary or in fascicles of 2.6; buds ovoid, acute, pedicels 6-20 mm. long, appressedly pubescent, often deflexed calyx 1 cm. long, fulvous-pubescent, segments – 8, the four outer ovate lanceolate, acute, the 4 inner narrower than the outer.  Corolla longer than the calyx, tube 1.5 mm. Long, lobes 8 mm. long, about 24 in 2 series the inner series of 8 the outer of 16 lobes, linear-oblong, acute, stamen and opposite the inner circle of lobes; filaments short, glabrous; anther glabrous slightly twisted, acuminate; staminodes 8, alternate with the stamens, lanceolate, acuminate, densely clothed on the back and margins with white hair, ovary appressedly silky-pubescent, style groove slightly longer than the corolla.  Berry about 2.5cm long ovoid, yellow when ripe.  Seed solitary, ovoid, compressed brown, shining.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Bark-tonic, febrifuge, leaf-antidote for snake bite, pulp of ripe fruit - antidysenteric; seed-purgative; bark and pulp of ripe fruit-astringent.


Chemistry:
NBPGR: β – Sitosterol and its glucoside, α – Spinasterol, quercitol, taraxerol and lupeol and its acetate are present in the aerial parts as well as the roots or seeds. The aerial parts in addition gave quercetin, dihydroquercetin, myricetin glycosides, hederagenin, ursolic acid hentriacontane and β-carotene. The bark contained an alkaloid consisting largely of a tiglate ester of a base with a mass spectrum identical to those of laburinine and iso retronecanol and a saponin also which one hydrolysis gave β- amyrin and bassic acid. Seed oil was comprised of capric, lauric, myristic, palmitic, stearic, arachidic, olic and linoleic acids.

NBRI :  Quercitol, ursolic acid, glucose, a triterpene alcohol,mp. 236°C, quercetin, dihydroquercetin and b-sitosterol glucoside from fruits and seeds; taraxerone, taraxerol, betulinic acid, a-spinasterol from bark; hentriacontane, b-carotene and lupeol from leaves, heartwood and roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Mixture of saponins contained in bark yielded on hydrolysis b-amyrin and basic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Myricetin-3-O-L-rhamnoside and myricetin-3-O-b-D-galactoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

D-mannitol, b-sitosterol and its glucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A new steroidal saponin isolated from roots and characterized as 5a-stigmast-9(11)ene-3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®5)-O-b-D-xylopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Oil (22.4%) from seed kernels yielded oleic (64.1%), linoleic (14.5%),palmitic (10.7%), stearic (10.2%) and behenic (0.5%) acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Two new pentacyclic triterpene acids – mimusopsic (fig. 1) and mimusopic (fig. 2) acids – from seeds and their characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

A Lupene-type triterpene  was isolated from Mimusops elengi. ( Jahan and Ahmed, 1995) C.A. 1996, 124:226462

Two novel triterpenoid saponins, mimusopin (3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-2b, 3b, 6b, 23-tetrahydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic acid 28-O-a-l-rhamnopyranosyl-(1 ® 3)-b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1 ®4)[a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1 ® 3)]-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1 ®2)-a-L-rabinopyranoside)(I) and mimusopsin {3-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1 ® 3)-b-D-gluco-pyranosyl]2b, 3b, 6b, 23-tetrahydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic acid 28- a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1 ® 3)- b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1 ® 4)-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1 ® 2)-a-L arabinopyranoside} were isolated from the seeds of Mimusops elengi.(Sahu et al ,1995) C.A.1996, 124:25638.

A new sterol, a C-3 epimer of chondrillasterol, was isolated from M. elengi and its structure detd. by spectral methods.(Jahan et al, 1996) C.A1996,125:41573.

Six saponins were isolated from the seed kernel of Mimusops elengi, Three of them are new compds.: 3-O-(b-D-glucuronopyranosyl) 28-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1 ® 3) b-D-xylopyranosyl(1 ® 4) [a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1 ® 3)] a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1 ® 2) a-L-arabinopyranosyl) protobassic acid,3-O-(b-D-glucuronopyranosyl)28-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1 ® 3) b-D-xylopyranosyl(1 ® 4) a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1 ® 2) a-L-arabinopyranosyl) 16-a-hydroxyprotobassic acid and 3-O-(b-D-glucopyranosyl(1 ® 3) b-D-glucopyranosyl) 28-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1 ® 3) b-D-xylopyranosyl(1 ® 4) a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1 ® 2) a-L-arabinopyranosyl) protobassic acid. (Lavaud et al, 1996) C.A. 1996,124:170582.

In addition to characterization of taxifolin, alpha-spinasterol glucoside, Mi-glycoside 1, two new triterpenoid saponins mimusopside A and B were isolated from the seeds of Mimusops elengi and were, respectively, defined as 3-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl protobassic acid 28-O-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1-->2)-alpha-L-arabinopyranoside and 3-O-beta-D-glucopyranosyl 16 alpha-hydroxy protobassic acid 28-O-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1-->2)-alpha-L-arabinopyranoside by a combination of LSIMS, FABMS, 1H and 13C NMR and some strategic chemical transformations.(Sahu, 1996)

A novel minor triterpenoid saponin, mimusin, {3-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1® 6)-b-D-glucopyranosyl]-2b,3b,6b,23-tetrahydroxyolean-12-en-28-oic acid 28-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1®3)-b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1®4)-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl- (1®2)-a-L-arabinopyranoside} was isolated from the seeds of Mimusops elengi, in addn. to two known triterpenoid saponins, Mi-saponin A and 16a-hydroxy Mi-saponin A.  (Sahu et al , 1997). C.A. 1997,126:274739

The seeds of Mimusops elengi yielded a new triterpene, mimusic acid, whose structure was elucidated as 2b,3b,16a,23-tetrahydroxyoleana-5,13(18)-dien-28-oic acid (I) by NMR spectral studies. (Sahu et al, 1999) C.A.1999, 130:264811.

 Two Pentacyclic triterpenes have been isolated from Mimusops elengi and assigned structures 3b,6b,19a,23-tetrahydroxy-urs-12-ene and 1b-hydroxy-3b-hexanoyllup-20 (29)-ene-23, 28-dioic acid, respectively, on the basis of spectroscopic studies including 2D-NMR. The compound 1 showed moderate inhibiting activity against beta-glucuronidase enzyme (Jahan et al, 2001)


Pharmacology:
The methanolic extract of Mimusops elengi caused hypotensive activity in anaesthetized rats. On intravenous administration (i.v.) at a dose range of 2-16 mg/kg, it produced about a 7-38% fall in mean arterial blood pressure, in a dose-dependent manner. The effect was independent of adrenergic, muscarinic and histaminergic receptors. The hypotension was also unchanged after autonomic ganglion or angiotensin-converting-enzyme blockade. Administration of calcium channel blockers, however, including nifedipine (0.9 mg/kg) and verapamil (3.9 mg/kg), caused corresponding reductions of 81 and 64% in extract-induced hypotension. (Dar et al ,1999)


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for fever (Bark, Bhalla et al, 1982); Headache (Flower, Bhalla et al, 1982); Anasarca, bronchitis, carbuncle, dropsy, small-pox, menorrhoea, sores, syphilis, ulcer tongue (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Edible (Fruit, Maji and Sikdar, 1982; Vartak, 1959; Ball, 1967); Oil for cooking (Vishnu Mittre, 1981); Ornamental garlands (Flower, Mehra et al, 1975); Symbolically sacred (Agarwal, 1981).
