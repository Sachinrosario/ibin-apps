 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, erect herbs, 1.5-2 m high; stellate-hairy, ultimately glabrescent. Leaves  orbicular-cordate, 3-13 x 3.5-12  cm, deeply 3-7 lobed, lobes sub-rotundate or  triangular, acute at apex, crenate to dentate. Petioles 2-18 cm long.


Availability :  
INDIA: Cultivated throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Inflorescence: Terminal racemes; Flower: complete, iregular, hermaphrodite, bracteate ; Calyx: Sepals 5, more or less united; Corolla: Petals5,valvate;Androecium: Stamens indefinite, staminal-tube divided to the apex into numerous antheriferous linear filaments; Gynoecium: Ovary multicarpellary, ovule one in each cell, styles as many as carpels, longitudinally stigmatose on the inside ; Fruit: Rounded depressed fruit formed from carpels and seperating from eacvh other and from short central axis; Seed: reniform, cotyledons leafy. Flowering & Fruiting : January-March .



REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers  soliatry axillary or  interminal racemes by reduction of  upper leaves; pedicels 3-10 mm long; accrescent  up to 15 mm in fruit. Epicalyx segments 6-7, ovate-lanceolate, 1.0-1.5 x 0.3-0.5 cm. Calyx lobes ovate to ovate-lanceolate, 15-20 x 5-10 mm. Petals 4-7 cm long, variously coloured, usually red. Staminal column, 10-15 mm long. Schizocarps c 2 cm across, depressed globose, pubescent, enclosed by calyx; mericarps 20-40, c 4 mm across, longitudinally sulcate. It flowers in March - September.


Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.

Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 26, 42, 56.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Flowers and seeds are medicinal. The flowers yield a red dye (anthocyanin) and is used as indicator in acidimetry and alkalimetry.


Chemistry:
Deep yellow hollyhock (A.Rosea) yielde a new glycoside which was named aerbacin. It yields hydrolysis herbacetin (fig. 1) and glucose by spectroscopic and other studies, the q-position of aglycon is shown to be glucosylated. This was confirmed by complete methylation an hydrolysis. (Parthosarthy and Seshadri, 1965) C.A. 1967, 68, 46558d.

Kaempferol, its 3-glucoside, quercetin, its 3-glucoside, cyaniding-3-glucoside & 3- rutinoside isolated. (Nair et al, 1964).

Dioxanelignin yield was 6.2 % of plant material and 46.6% of lignin content. P-coumaric, guaiacolic & syringic acid residues identified in dioxanelignin molecule. (Geronikaki and Abduzinov, 1976) C.A. 1977, 86, 46558q.

Nudiflorin (fig. 2) identified as apigenin-7-b-(6-b-L-rhamnofuranoside)-D-glucofuranoside, isolated from flowers. (Pakuclina, 1977) C.A. 1979, 91, 189764v.

Seeds, leaves & flowers of Egyptian plant contained palmitic, myristic, stearic, oleic, linoleic acids, b-sitosterol & stigmasterol. (Karawya et al, 1979) C.A. 1983, 98, 2737t.

Isolation of rutin, chrysin, kaempferol, robinetin, acacetin & phlorectin from flowers. (Srivastava and Jain, 194).

Cyanidin-3-, 7-di-b-D-glucoside, cyaniding-3b-D-glucoxyloside, delphinidin-3b-D galactoside, petunidin-3b-D-rhamnoside & pelargonidin-7b-D-glucoside isolated from petals. (Afifi and Karwaya, 1988) C.A. 1990, 112, 95509r.

A rich source of ricinoleic acid (61.7%) has been found in Althea rosea seed oil. Identification was based on chemical and spectroscopic methods. The other fatty acids are myristic (1.1%), palmitic (25.3%), stearic (2.6%) and oleic (9.3%). (Daultabad and jamkhandi, 2000) C.A. 2000, 133, 360862n.

Leaves and flowers of Malva sylvestris and Alcea rosea were compared by determination of the swelling index as well as the content and viscosity of their mucilages. Mucilage of Alcea flower was superior to mucilage of leaves or flowers of Malva. High Mol. Wt acidic polysaccharide (HMWAPs) were isolated from the mucilages of leaves and flowers of both species. Mol. Wt. of HMWAPs was in range of 1.3 to 1.6 x 106 D HMWAP- content in mucilage from flower of Alcea was highest compared to content in mucilages from leaves or flowers from Malva. HMWAPs were found to be composed mainly of glucoronic acid, galacturonic acid, rhamnose and galactose. Methylation analysis of the carboxyl-reduced and deuterium labeled sugar derivatives of HMWAPs from leaves of Malva sylvestris elucidation of the principal structural features of both polysaccharides. (Classen and Blaschek, 1998) C.A. 1998, 129, 287840q.


Pharmacology:

Cellulase Cx and C1 amylase and invertase increased markedly in Althea rosea, Cordyline terminals and Jasminum flexile with leaf spot diseases caused by Coletotrichum dematium, Alternaria alternata and A. longissima, respectively. (Amena & Umabala, 1997) C.A. 1997, 127, 173947h.

Effect of hollyhock flower extract on doxorubicin induced renal disease, esp. nephritic syndrome (N.S) in rats were studied. The drug significantly increased the plasma protein level and decreased the lipid levels in N.S. and creatinine clearance rates in N.S. Rats. Thus, the drug significantly ameliorated the symptoms of N.S. rats. (Yin, 2000) C.A. 2000, 133, 109724e.

The effect of aqueous hollyhock flower (Althea rosea) extract for 30 days orally in group A1 and for 180 days in group A2. Leydig cells of group A1 manifested marked increased in the 3beta-HSD, G6PD and NADPD activities and in the Khanolkar reaction intensity. These findings were accompanied by the increase in the volume of Leydig cells and their nuclei. In group A2 leydig cells statistically insignificant changes in the G6PD and NADPD activities were observed, however, the significant increase in the 3beta-HSD activity and the Khanolkar reaction intensity indicated compensatory changes. The statistically significant elevation of the androgen level accompanied by a decrease in estrogen content in Romogenates of group A2 tests pointed weak antiestrogenic effect of the extract. The obtained results indicate an influence of the hollyhock extract on steroid metabolism. (Papiez, 2001).
