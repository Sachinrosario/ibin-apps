HABIT & HABITAT
Scandent or sarmentose or sraggling shrubs, spreading upto 3-15 m high.  It is common in India except in the arid zones. In dry, open, scrub jungles or evergreen forests or sholas from sea level to 2500 m. Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Srilanka, China, Malaysia, Thailand, Sumatra, Philippines, Malagasy, Mascarene Islands, S. & S. Africa.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Scandent or sarmentose or sraggling shrubs, 3-15 m high. Leaves digitately trifiliate,; leaflets seesile, oblong-elliptic to elliptic-obovate, 1,5-9.5 x 0.5-4.0 cm, obtuse or obtusely acute or acuminate at apex, entire to subentire or subserrate along margins, narrow to cuneate at base, membranous, glabrous; petioles slender, 1-3.5 cm long. Flowers in 1.5-10.5 cm long panicle, white, sweet scented. Male flowers: calyx lobes deltoid, acute, glandular. Petals narrow to broadly oblong, 1.5-6.0 x 0.8-1.5 mm, hooded at apex, glabrous. Female flowers: Sepals broadly triangular. Petals narrowly oblong. Drupes 4-5 grooved or lobed, orange or reddish when ripe, rind smooth. Seeds one in each locule, reniform, 2-3 mm long, ca 1 mm across, brownish, spiny. It flowers and fruits between Jan- July.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Root and root bark are aromatic, antiperiodic, antidiarrhoeic, astringent, stomachic and vulnerary. Infusion of fresh root bark and leaves are carminative. Fresh leaves are eaten for stomach pain. Ripe berries are prickled.
