 Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT :
This species occurs in the hilly tracts of Central Provinces, Bengal, Bombay, Madras and some of the lower Himalayan ranges. The tubers or rhizomes contain starch, which is used as a substitute for the true arrowroot powder from Maranta arundinacea Linn. The plant grows wild in many places. It is found in moist and cool situations at altitudes of about 1,500 ft, and is cultivated to a small extent.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers or Rhizome

Uses:

  • It is used in the preparation of milk puddings and is recommended for children and invalids. The commercial product is subject to adulteration with other starches, e.g., tapioca, sweet potato and rice flour.
  • It is one of the indispensable ingredients in curries and is used for colouring confections.
  • The powdered root is used as a fumigation in commencing catarrhs.

Harvesting Practices:
A yield of 3,900 lb. per acre has been reported from experimental farms in Madras

Chemistry
The chemical formula is C10H10O3 or C16H16O4, that melts at 172 degrees, forms red brown salts with alkalies, is converted by boric or sulphuric acid into rosocyanine by reduction with zinc dust into an oily body, by oxidation into oxalic or terephthalic acid, and by fusion with potash into protocatechuic acid.

Back to Top



Processing Techniques:
The tubers are washed and pulped, and the starch separated from fibre and other impurities by repeated washing and straining through cloth. It is sun-dried and ground into flour (yield, c. 12-5%). The product, which resembles arrowroot starch to some extent, consists of large, elongated, pear-shaped or club- shaped starch granules, and is easily digestible.
