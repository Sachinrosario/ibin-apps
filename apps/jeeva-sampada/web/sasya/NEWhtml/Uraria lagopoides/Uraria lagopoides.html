HABIT & HABITAT
Creeping or ascending, woody shrubs, It is found in Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Manipur, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Creeping or ascending, woody shrubs, 30-90 cm long. Leaves 2-5 cm long with pubescent rachis; leaflets solitary or one pair, terminal one larger, cordate or truncate at base, membranous, upper surface glabrous, lower surface hairy, reticulate; stipules setaceous, lanceolate. Flowers in racemes, white, 2.5-6.3 cm long; bracts silky, lanceolate. Calyx teeth, setaceous plumose. Pods 3.8 x 2 cm, glabrous, joints reticulate.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine plant is used as laxative, aphrodisiac and useful in fever, burning sensation, asthma, dysentery, vomiting, ulcer, tumour, eye diseases and bone fractures.

Chemistry:

·        3-Hydroxy-7, 4’-dimehtoxyflavone and five other flavanoids isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·        Dasamularishta, Dasamulakvatha and Dasamula taila contain in addition to other herbs, Uraria lagopoides. Thin layer chromatography patterns of these formulations were found to be quite different in the tested solvent systems. (Alam, et. al,1993). MAPA 9603-1298

·        GLC analysis of fatty acids isolated from the ethanolic extract of the roots of U. lagopoides indicating the presence of C16-28 fatty acids. (Grampurohit, et al, 1993). C.A. 122, 76586d (1995). BA 99(5), 686 86 (1995).

Pharmacology:

·        Aqueous extract of leaves showed antiimplantation activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Flavonoids exhibited activity against snake venom. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·        Uraria lagopoides is present in the Dasamoola used in Srilanka. (Tissera, M.H.A.; et. al., 1997).

 

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for abortifacient (Plant juice, Tarafder, 1983); Body ache (Root, Jain and De, 1966); Prevent miscarriage (Root, Tarafder, 1984); Snakebite (Root, Murthy et al, 1986; Saxena, 1986)
