DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs or undershrubs upto 3 m high, hirsute all over, rarely glabrous. Tap root tuber-like. Leaves extremely variable in shape and size, 4-18 x 3-20 cm, angular or 3-7 palmilobed to palmiparted, upper leaves narrower, often hastate or sagitate; lobes linear, lanceolate, ovate-obovate, obtuse, acute or acuminate at apex. Coarsely serrate to dentate, rarely entire, 5-9-nerved at base. Petioles 2-20 cm long. Stipules 3-10 mm long, linear to filiform, hairy.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers axillary, solitary; pedicels 1.5-6 cm, accrescent up to 15 cm. Epicalyx segments 8-10, free, 10-15 x 1-2 mm, linear, persistent. Calyx 1.5-3 cm long, stellate-tomentose outside, sericeous inside. Corolla yellow with dark purple centre, ca 10 cm across. Petals obovate, rounded at apex, fleshy and ciliate at base.  Capsules 4-8x 2.5 cm, ovoid to globose, acuminate with a rostrum of ca 3 mm. Seeds 3-4 mm, centrically ribbed, glabrous or minutely stellate-hairy, often musk scented.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 “Ambrette seed oil “ is extracted by distillation of crushed seeds which is used in high-grade perfumery. The seeds are reported to be used as a flavouring agent for coffee. In India they are mostly employed as an adulterant for animal musk and in perfume.

Chemistry:
Myricetin-3’-glucoside and a glycoside of cyaniding identified in flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

b-sitosterol and its b-D-glucoside, myricetin and its glucoside obtained from leaves and petals; dry fruit husk yielded only b-sitosterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Seeds contained oil (19.5), unsaponifiables (0.8), protein (36.0) and moisture (8.4%); seed oil contained 12,13-epoxyleic (6.4), malvalic (5.2) and sterculic (9.2%) acids in addition to even-numbered C10-C18 acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Trans-2-trans-b-farnesyl acetate (64.22) and ambrettolide (14.96%) determined in seed essential oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Stereoselective synthesis of farnesol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
