Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennial, erect herb, 30-90 cm high, India: Jammu & Kashmir at 1500-2500 m.
N.Asia, Afghanistan, W.Atlantic.


Availability :  

Conservation/ Threat Status : Plant grows wild.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem few or many from the rootstock, angled, roughly sulcate, slender, hoary pubescent. Radical leaves c 10 cm long, 2-3 pinnatisect, hoary pubescent to dull ferruginous, petioled; segments linear or oblong-linear, obtuse at apex; petioles narrowly winged.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Heads small, solitary, terminal drooping racemes; Flower: Homogamous or heterogamous, bracteate, outer flower hermaphrodite, 1 seriate, fertile, very slender, 2-3 toothed, inner disk flower sterile limb 5-fid ;: ; Fruit: Achenes elliptic-oblong or somewhat, obovoid; Seed: Erect, radicle short, testa membranous. Flowering & Fruiting: August- September. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind .

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: The whole plant is an aromatic tonic. Can be used in debility of the digestive organs.


Chemistry:
Chemial Variability: The compn. of wormwood oils produced from the leaves and flowering tops by simultaneous distn. – extn. were compared. Seasonal changes in wormwoos oil were also studied. Analysis of oils produced from plants collected at 3-mo intervals showed similar patterns in compn. of both leaves and flowering tops as well as small quant. differences in the oil compn. throughout the year. In comparison with differences found between worm wood chemotypes, sesonal variations did not significantly affect the oil compn., and neither did the presence of flowers. 1;

Caffeic, chlorogenic, ferulic, p-hydroxy benzoic and vanillic acids were identified in A. absinthium shoots. (Salnikova et al, 1993). C.A. 1995, 123, 251299g.

The thujone content of extract of absinthii herba was determined. Thujone was not enriched in the extracts. Its content was highest in extracts from distillation and lowest in extracts from percolation with purified H2O. The thujone content from industrial drug percolation with EtOH (30 Vol %) was < 37% of the content in native drugs. (Tegtmeier and Harnischfeger, 1994). C.A. 1995, 123, 179190v.

The essential oils obtained by steam distillation of leaves and flowers of A. absinthium were analyzed by G.C. a- and b-thujone, terpinen-4-ol, linalool, nerol, geraniol, a-pinene, 1,8-cineole were identified. (Nin et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 79563a.

Two sesquiterpene lactones have been isolated from the epigeal part of Artemisa absinthium. It has been established that one of them is identical with artabin (fig. 1) while the other has the structure of 3b,8a-dihydroxy-5bH,6bH,7aH,8bH-guai-4(15)-en-6,12-olide (fig. 2). (Safarova and Serkerov, 1997). C.A. 1998, 129, 242496r.

The essential oil of A. absinthium of Cuban origin has been analyzed by GC and GC/MS. More than 40 compounds were identified, of which bornyl acetate (23.02%) was the major constituent. (Pino et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 126, 2293790p.


Pharmacology:
Senescence – promoting effect of methyljasmonate was much stronger than that of ABA; even at 1.0-2.5 mg/ml, it completely eliminated antiscence action of 2.0 mg/ml kinetin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)m.

24-x-ethylcholesta-7,22-dien-3b-ol exhibited antipyretic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.

Artemisetin showed marked antitumor activity against melanoma B16 in mice and rats but only weak activity against Pliss lymphosarcoma and hardly any against sarcoma 180. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)h.

Mixture of diastereomeric homoditerpene peroxides exhibited antimalarial activity in vitro with EC50 of 1.0mg/ml. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
