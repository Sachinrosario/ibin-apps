REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence of several flowers; Flower solitary rarely in pairs white to bluish white very rarely voilet, actinomorphic, hermaphrodite, pentamerous and hypogynous; Calyx five-lobed, persistent and much enlarged than fruits, valvate; Corolla gamopetalous, funnel shaped, five lobed, valvate; Androecium stamens 5, epipetalous on the corolla tube and alternate with the lobes, anthers ovate, dithecous, introrse and dehiscing by longitudinal slits; Gynoecium multicarpellary, syncarpous, ovary become unilocular in the upper region by receding of placenta and the placentation a parietal, style linear and stigma capitate; Fruit berries green to yellow,orange to red grading into brown or purple, pendent and erect; Seeds white or cream to yellow, almost circular and having long placental connections; 

Floral and seed biology:

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
 Bees , ants and thrips are agents of pollination and cultivars are partially self or cross pollinated. Seeds are dispersed usually by birds and animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The haploid chromosome count for the plant is 12. Considerable variation occurs in size, shape, colour, pungency of the fruits, as no karyotype is character of single species. Besides tetraploid variants with triploid chromosome number and diploid showing meiotic irregularities have also been reported.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type:  Plant is cultivated on almost all well-drained and aerated soils, except the salty lands. The black and retentive soils are best for rainfed crop and rich, friable ,well-drained soil, loamy soil are preferred for an irrigated crop.

Nutrient requirements: The chilies respond to heavy manuring. A balanced use of fertilizers is prerequisite for higher yield. The manure can be supplemented by oil cakes of groundnut, pongram, margosa, mustard or castor.

Water management: Regular and continuous irrigations are required especially in the areas where rainfall is less. Higher-level sites need more irrigation.

Harvesting period / Method:
The stages of maturity at which chilies are collected depend on the cultivar and purpose of collection. For vegetable, the fully-grown chilies are gathered green, for canning they are allowed to develop red colour before harvesting and fruits are collected when they ripened and change colour.

The solvents used for HPLC separation and quantification of capsaicinoids were methanol and water at 1 ml/min flow rate. Instruement sensitivity was enhanced by altering the fluorescence detector excitation and emission wavelengths. Two analytical methods were developed. ·        The separation of aromatic and colouring components of paprika (Capsicum annuum) with supercritical CO2 as solvent was examined. The selectivity of CO2 is higher at lower temperature, i.e. at 40 degree C. The optimal operating pressure for the extraction of aromatic components from paprika is 15MPa and for the separation of colouring components the pressure is 40 MPa. Under these conditions the obtained extract was of high colour units (CU) value (180,000), the yield was 1.9 percent. The mass of CO2 needed for extraction of aromatic components per kg of raw material was approximately 51.4 Kg CO2 per Kg of paprika. In case of continuous countercurrent concentration of commercially available paprika oleoresins, the yield of extraction was 40 percent at 40 degree C and 15 MPa and the Cu value of obtained oleoresin which was free of organic solvent was 130,000. (Skerget, M. et. al., 1998). MAPA 9901-0698.

·        Paprika oleoresin was fractionated by extraction with supercritical carbon dioxide. Within the 2000-7000 psirange, total oleoresin yield always approached 100 percent.  Pigments isolated at lower pressures consisted almost exclusively of beta-carotene while pigments obtained at higher pressures contained a greater proportion of red carotenoids (capsorubin, capsanthin,zeaxanthin,beta-cryptoxanthin) and  small amounts of beta-carotene. The varying solubility of oil and pigments in SCF-CO2 was optimized to obtain enriched and concentrated oleoresins through a two-stage extraction at 2000 and 6000 psi. This technique removes the paprika oil and beta- carotene during the first extraction step. (Jaren-Galan, M. et. al., 1999). MAPA 2000-02-1256.

·        For the analysis of capsaicinoids(CAPS) in the placentas of  Capsicum annuum  fruits rapidly and safely, directly connected  system of supercritical fluid extraction and supercritical  fluid chromatography (SFE/SFC) was used. Pharmacology:

·        Capsaicin (10-300 mg/kg, i.v.) caused transient rise in blood pressure in anaesthetized dogs followed by sustained fall, whereas in anaesthetized rabbits it caused hypotension. It also caused a sustained increase in tension of spiral strips of proximal and distal mesenteric arteries and proximal and distal renal arteries of dog. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Intragastric infusion of capsaicin (10 mg/hr) caused significant increase of DNA content of gastric aspirate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Capsaicin severely impaired heat-reinforced behaviour in rats on single or multiple s.c. dose. It rendered hypothalamic neuron incapable of regulating against overheating in hot environment. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Capsaicin (1mM) produced depolarization in dorsal and ventral roots of isolated hemisected spinal cord of immature rat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Capsaicin (50 mg/kg, s.c.) treatment of 2-day old female rats resulted 3 months later in 81.0% decrease in mean pain response to tail-vein injection of hydrochloric acid and 63.0 and 72.0% increase in nociceptive threshold in rats with normal and inflamed paws respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Analgesic effect of morphine was markedly attenuated in adult rats pretreated with capaicin (50 mg/kg) as a result of impairment of certain hypothalamic preoptic neurons, while neonatal capsaicin treatment did not affect morphine analgesia. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Effect of capsaicin treatment on the nerve elements of small intestine in newborn cats resulted in degeneration of numerous nerve processes and some cell stomata also severely damaged. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Carotenoid fraction at 2.0-5.0 g/kg dose, p.o., showed anti-inflammatory activity and enhanced wound healing in mice and rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Capsaicin (3.3 mM) released acetylcholine from isolated myenteric plexus from longitudinal muscle trips of guinea pig ileum, pre-incubated with choline. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Capsaicin pretreatment reduced gastric acid secretion elicited by s.c. injection of histamine (0.1, 0.5 or 5.0 mg/kg) but did not effect responses to carbachol (4.0, 40.0 or 160.0 mg/kg) and pentagastrin (25.0 or 250.0 mg/kg) in conscious rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Capsacin (0.1 mg/kg, i.v.) caused rapid fall in blood pressure followed by marked pressor response, bradycardia and apnea in rats. It also induced pressor response in pithed rats. In isolated guinea pig trachea, capsaicin (100.0 mM) induced contractile response. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Capsaicin in concentration of 0.1 to 1.0 mM induced relaxation in precontracted longitudinal muscle strips of rat gastric fundus, which was potentiated by hexanethonium. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Capsaicin at concentrations previously shown to activate sensory afferent neurons, markedly inhibited responsiveness of platelets to aggregation by agonists. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Effects of intrathecal pretreatment with capsaicin on spinal antinociception by morphine examined. Pretreatment with capsaicin (50.0 mg) for 7 to 11 days reduced action of morphine in hot plate test but not in rat tail flick test. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        In mouse right main bronchus, both exogenous tachykinins and capsaicin did not show contractile effect while they produced indomethacin-sensitive relaxations which are mediated by stimulation of NK1 receptors, thought to be located on epithelial cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Central vagal and local axon reflex components of bronchoconstrictor responses evoked by inhalation of capsaicin aerosol in 5, 10 and 15 breaths, determined in anaesthetized guinea pigs. Results suggest that capsaicin-induced bronchoconstrictor responses are mediated by at least two mechanisms, a vagal and/or cholinergic reflex pathway and a noncholinergic pathway. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Inhibitory effect of capsaicin on cellular responses evoked by acetylcholine and ATP investigated in rat phaeochromocytoma Pc12 cells. Results suggest that low concentrations of capsaicin selectively antagonize responses mediated by nicotinic receptor channels without affecting those mediated by purinoceptor-coupled channels. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Sensitivity of auditory neurons in spiral ganglion to capsaicin was investigated in preparations from postnatal hamsters of age groups 6-22 days. Capsaicin altered shape, size and staining characteristic of spiral ganglion cells from all age groups. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Effect of feeding   red chilli (Capsicum annuum) on the levels of lipids, fecal bile acids and fecal sterols was studied in animals given 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (DMH) When the three experimental groups were compared with control the levels of cholesterol increased while phospholipids and free fatty acids decreased or showed no alternation in most of the tissues studied. Cholesterol-phospholipid ratio was high in both DMH as well as in the chilli +DMH treated groups. Weekly assessment of bile acids and neutral sterols showed a significant increase in the three experimental groups, when compared to the control rats. Morphological and histopathological studies show that there were number of visible malignant tumors in the colon and intestine of all the three experimental animals. (Chitra S.  et. al., 1994). MAPA 9501-0175.

·        In order to elucidate the vasomotor actions of capsaicin administration in vitro, on resistance vessels of perfused rat mesenteric bed has been reported. (Potenza et al, 1994).

·        Capsaicin, the pungent principle of hot chilli (Capsicum annum) can penetrate the membrane lipid matrix associated with calcium ion influx and cause release of different neuropeptides including substance P, a peptide related with pain from the sensory neurons. On repeated treatment with capsaicin, there is depletion of the neuropetides from the neurons giving a desensitized state that protects it from chemical irritant induced damages, indicating the therapeutic prospects of capsaicin as an analgesic and anti-inflammatory agent. Capsaicin has a profound effect on the bronchorespiratory system. Chilli vapour inhalation shows protective effect on any type of gaseous pollutant induced pulmonary hyperreactivity and chronic lung diseases. It also shows inhibition against cyclophosphamide, a known mutagenic agent, induced chromosomal aberrations, and DNA damages. Recently, it has shown some indications for its protective role on skin cancer caused from exposure to sunlight. (De, A. K., 1995). MAPA 9603-1837.

·        Neonatal capsaicin, the active principle of Capsicum annum treatment did not affect either basal glycaemia or blood glucose levels following subcutaneous glucose load (0.5g/kg), but significantly lowered blood glucose levels after oral glucose load (1g/kg). Capsaicin treatment in adult rats did not modify blood glucose levels after either oral or subcutaneous glucose load. (Perfumi, et. al., 1996). MAPA 9703-1548.

·        107 patients with sore throat were treated with the tablet (n=63) and the liquid form (n=44) of a combination from Phytolacca(D1), Guaiacum(D4) and Capsicum annum(D4). In the both groups a significant reduction of symptom scores for throat complaints, ear complaints and headache in connection with an improvement of clinical findings was found after 21/2 days of treatment. (Wiesenauer, et. al., 1996).

·        The results suggest that functional desensitization to capsaicin obtained from Capsicum annum induced contractile response in guinea pig bronchi does not involve NK-1 and NK-2 receptor, while excitatory effect of capsaicin is mediated via both NK-1 and NK-2 receptor. Capsaicin-induced excitation and desensitization on peripheral sensory nerve ending in guinea pig bronehi. (Jung Y. S., et. al., 1997). MAPA 9704-2132.

·        In the presence of a known colon carcinogen, 1,2-dimethyl hydrazine (DMH), the activity of beta-glucuronidase was found to be significantly increased in the distal colon, distal intestine, liver and colon contents and the activity of mucinase was increased in both the colon and fecal contents when compared to control rats. Chilli (Capsicum annum) administration also showed an increase. The increase in beta-glucuronidase activity has been found to increase the hydrolysis of glucuronide conjugates, liberating the toxins, while the increase in mucinase activity may enhance the hydrolysis of the protective mucins in the colon. (Nalini, et al, 1998). MAPA 9901-0299.

·        Capsicum annum antidote for poisonous bite. (Thangadurai, 1998). MAPA 9804-2496.

·        Rats were fed with diet containing 5 percent hot red pepper (Capsicum annum) spices (EG) and the standard diet (CG) for weeks. Total cholesterol level of plasma was determined. TLC of EG higher than in CG. However, atherogenic index did not change. Level of plasma triglyceride was also lower. Significant increase in total protein, albumin and urea concentration was observed. The results suggest that red pepper can stimulate the synthesis and catabolism of protein and cholesterol. Other components like uric acid; creatinine glucose and enzyme activities were not affected. (Arslan S. O., et. al., 1999). MAPA 2000-02-0844.

·        Capsaicin, the active compound present in ‘hot’ chilies peppers (Capsicum annuum), exerts a stabilizing effect on the erythrocytes making them more resistant to lysis under hypotonic stress. The protective action of capsaicin on osmotic fragility (OF) was not receptor mediated since no direct responsive effect was observed. The result suggest that the protective effect of capsaicin on OF is due to a direct interaction of capsaicin with the erythocyte membrane rather than due to any alteration in the intercellular metabolism of erythrocytes. (Rizivi, S. and Srivastava, P. P., 1999). MAPA 9905-3125.

·        Extracts of Arjuna (Terminalia arjuna), Vacha (Acorus calamus), Brahmi (Centella asiatica) and Jatamansi (Nardostachys jatamansi) when combined in equal doses were administered to 22 hypertensive patients with no lipid derangements. A symptomatic improvement with fall in blood-pressure (both systolic and diastolic) was noted. Various symptoms viz., Anidra, Dourbalya, Shirahshoola, Kshubdhata, Shrama and Bhrama were graded as per their severity and recorded. (Bharti et al, 1999).

·        The study was conducted in vitro to investigate the changes of indomethacin transdermal permeation pretreated by capsaicin isolated from Capsicum annuum and nonivamide, two compounds chemically similar to azone. The combined effect of low frequency ultrasound (20 kHz) and enhances on the indomethacin permeation was also evaluated. Capsaicin and nonivamide significantly enhanced the flux of indomethacin across nude mouse skin. Histological examination coupled with visual scores indicated the safety of capsaicin and nonivamide on skin structure. Simultaneous application of ultrasound and enhancers significantly increased skin permeation of indomethacin compared with either ultrasound or enhancers alone. Better effect was obtained by the combination with capsaicin than nonivamide. (Fang et al, 2001).
