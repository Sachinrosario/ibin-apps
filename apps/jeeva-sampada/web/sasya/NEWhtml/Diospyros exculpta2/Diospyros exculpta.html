DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Axillary cymes ; Calyx:  Lobed, often deeply truncate in the female often longer than male; Corolla:  Tubular, salver haped, lobed ; Male Flower: Stamens 4-64, anthers linear, rarely short, ovary rudimentary ; Female Flower: Staminodes 0-16, ovary 4-5 celled, ovules many, style 1-4; Fruit: Globose and woody; Seed: Oblong and compressed.

Floral and seed biology:

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Lupeol, betulin, betulinic acid, and b-sitosterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for cuts, wounds as hemostat (Bark, Singh and Maheshwari, 1983; Maheshwari and Singh, 1987; Part not mentioned, Singh and Maheshwari, 1985); Diarrhoea, dysentery (Fruit, Singh and Maheshwari, 1983; Maheshwari et al, 1986; Part not mentioned, Singh and Maheshwari, 1985); Scorpion bite (Root, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Edible (Fruit, Jain, 1984; Goel et al, 1984; Singh and Maheshwari, 1985; Maheshwari et al, 1986; Datar and Vartak, 1975)
