Annual, erect or slightly decumbent, branched, up to 1 m high herbs, it is found through out India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem terete or obtuse angular, glabrous or slightly pubescent. Leaves long petioled, ovate-lanceolate to rhomboid-elliptic, 2-12 x 0.8-6.0 cm, mucronate at apex, cuneate or attenuate at base, entire or repand at margin, glabrous or slightly pubescent on nerves when young; petioles up to 9 cm long, sometimes longer than the lamina; each leaf axils bearing a pair of c 1.5-2.5 cm long spines. Flowers in axillary clusters or in terminal, up to 15 x 1 cm spikes, green; lower clusters and lower part of the spikes bearing only female flowers; upper part of spikes with male flowers; bracts and bracteoles deltoid-ovate, awned, bracteoles shorter than the bracts. Tepals or perianth segments 5, ovate-oblong, 2.0-2.5 mm long, convex, shortly mucronate with transparent margins and green and purple median bands. Stamens 5. Capsules or utricles ovoid-urceolate, c 1.5 mm long with a 3-lobed  apex. Seeds compressed, 0.75-1.0 mm across, black.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Dense axillary clusters and in terminal and axillary dense or interuppted spikes; Flower: Numerous, sessile, bracteolate; Male Flower: Perianth of 5  membranous, subequal segments, sepals 5, ovate, bristle pointed ; Androecium: Stamens 5, staminode 0, anthers 2-celled, filaments free ; Female flower:  Perianth-segments oblong, erect in fruit, sepals 5, oblong, obtuse, apiculate; Gynoecium: Ovary ovoid, compressed, 1-celled, ovule solitary, subsessile, erect, styles 2; Fruit:  Capsule ovoid, thickened, at the top, membranous rugose; Seed: erect, orbicular, compressed. Flowering & Fruiting:  September- November. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated by seeds.

Soil Type:  Thrives best in loams.

Irrigation: First irrigation is given just after the broadcasting the seeds. The next irrigation is given after 15-20 days and then at the interval of 10-15 days till the crop becomes 2-3 months old.

Nutrient requirements: 20-25 carloads of well rotten farmyard manure and the mixture of 25 kg N, 25 kg P2O5 and 50 k2O is applied to crop.

Harvesting: Grains are harvested they are fully mature, foliage are cut periodically when leaves are crisp and green.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Plant extract is used for eruptive fever and also as lactagogue. Infusion of young shoots is used in eczema.
Chemistry:
Hentriacontane and a-spinasterol from leaves and stems; a new saponin mixture composed of Oleanolic acid, D-glucose and D-glucosonic acid isolated from roots along with a-spinosterol. ( Banerji and Chakravarti, 1973).

b-sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol, cholesterol and stearic, oleic and linoleic acids isolated. ( Behari and Andhiwal, 1976).

Quercetin and rutin (1.9%) obtained. ( Bech et al, 1977). C.A. 1977, 87, 197309h.

a-spinasterol octacosanoate and a new saponin -b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)- b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)-b-D-glucoronopyranosyl(1à3)-oleanolic acid (fig. 1) isolated from roots. (Banerji, 1979).

Isolation and structure of two new saponins elucidated as b-D-glucopyranosyl (1à2)- b-Glucopyranosyl (1à2)- b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à3)-a-spinasterol (fig. 2) and b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)- b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à3)-a-spinasterol (fig. 3). ( Banerji, 1980).


Pharmacology:
Chinese herbs used for sunscreen or/and whitening agents in cosmetics. Among them Amaranthus spinosus showed the minor tyrosinase inhibition activities. Amaranthus spinosus showed the UV-A absorption activity and UV-B absorption activity, on investigation, Kaempferol 3-O-rutinoside act as major ingredient responsible for the sunscreen activity. (Lai et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 195219z. Amaranthus spinosus mineral composition was studied, very high levels of iron 38.4mg/100g dw. ( Barminas et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 107146y.

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for abortificant (root, Tarafder, 1983); Anti emetic (whole plant, Jain & Tarafder, 1970); Blood purifier (whole plant, Sharma et al, 1979); Cold, cough (seed, Joshi et al, 1980); Colic, eczema, gonorrhea (root, Shah et al, 1983); Heat in body (seed, Vir et al, 1984); Laxative (root, Das et al, 1983); Piles (whole plant); Purgative (whole plant, Sebastian & Bhandari, 1984); Scorpion sting, Snake bite (root, Goel et al, 1984); Edible Vegetable (leaf, Maheshwari & Singh, 1984; Jain & De, 1966; Vartak, 1981; Singh & Singh, 1981; Jain, 1964; Singh & Maheshwari, 1985; Abraham, 1981; Vartak, 1959; Ball, 1967; Maheshwari et al, 1986; Chaudhuri et al, 1985; Maheshwari et al, 1981; Gupta, 1962; Kanodia & Gupta, 1968; whole plant, Ramchandaran & Nair, 1981).
