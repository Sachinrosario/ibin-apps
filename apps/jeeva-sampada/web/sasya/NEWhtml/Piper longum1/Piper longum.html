HABIT & HABITAT
Herbaceous climber, found in Warmer parts of India from central Himalaya to Assam, Khasi and Mikir Hills lower hills of West Bengal, Nepal, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Western-Ghats from Konkan southwards to Trivandrum often cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial, much branched, creeping or climbing, aromatic herbs. Stems glabrous or tomentose. Leaves alternate, ovate-orbicular, broadly ovate, oblong, glabrous, cordate, entire, acute or acuminate, dark green above, pale beneath, lower long-petioled uppershort petioled or sessile.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers dioecious, in male and female spikes in the axils of peltate bracts. Spikes leaf-opposed, cylindrical. Male spikes slender, yellow. Male flower: Stamens 2-4. Female spikes with bracts similar to ones but more distant and less stipitate. Female flowers: style short, stigmas 2-5. Fruit small ovoid, sunk in the fleshy rachis.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Root pungent-heating; stomachic, laxative; anthelmintic; Carminative; improves apetitie; Useful in bronchitis, abdominal pains; diseases of spleen, tumours, axiles, causes biliousness. Unripe fruit sweetish, pungent, heating, stomachic, aphordiseae, alternative, laxative, autidiarrhoetic, antidysenteric; useful in Vata & Kapha, asthma, bronchitis, abdominal complaints, fevers leucoderma, urinary discharges, tumours, piles, diseases of spleen, culous glands; increases biliosness (Aurveda).  Root and fruit used in palay, gout, and lumbago. Fruit- bitter; Carminative; tonic to liver; stomachic, emmenagogue, abortificient, aphrodisiae, haematinic, diuretic, digestive; general tonic, useful in inflamation of the liver, pains in joints, lumbago, might blindness. (Yunani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
Parts used:  Unripe fruit-alterative, tonic; fruit-carminative, as liniment for rheumatic pains and paralysis; roots-diuretic, stimulant, sudosific; ripe fruit and root-stomachic; fruit and root-analgesic, antiepileptic, anti-inflammatory, selective, antidysenteric, antileprotic, antidote for snake-bite, haematic, cholagogue, emmenagogue, abortifacient, anthelmintic, in diseases of the respiratory tract.

Chemistry:
NBPGR:  Roots gave alkaloids-piperine, piperlongumine (piplartine) and piperlonguminine, sesamin, methyl-3, 4, 5-trimethoxycinnamate, stem gave triacontane, 22, 23-dihydrostigmasterol, fruit gave N-isobutyldeca –trans-2-trans-4-dienamide, essential oil from fruit contains n-hexadecane, n-heptadecane, n-octadecane, n-nonadecane, n-eicosane, n-heneicosane, α-thujene, terpinolene, zingiberene, p-cymene, p-methoxyacetophenone, dihydrocarveol, phenylethyl alcohol and carophyllene, fruit also gave L-tyrosine, L-cysteine hydro chloride, DL-serine and L-aspartic acid; leaves yielded hertriacontane, hetriacontane –16- one, triacontanol and β-sitosterol.  Seeds gave sylvatin, sesamin and dieudesmin.
