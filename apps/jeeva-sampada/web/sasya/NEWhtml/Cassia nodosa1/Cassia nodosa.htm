Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
 A small to medium sized tree, up to 15 m in height, found in Delhi, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh and in the Andaman Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Leaves paripinnate, 20-30 cm long, leaflets 10-20 pairs, 5-10 cm x 2.5-4 cm, oblong, flowers pink, fading to dull white, in corymbose racemes; pods cylindrical, transversely ribbed, 20-35 cm x 1-2 cm.

Chemistry
The leaves contain rhein, chrysophanol, physcion and kaempferol, tetramethyl ether of kaempferol and kaempferol-3-rhamnoside. Analysis of the seeds gave: moisture-10.1; protein-13.8; pentosan-10.0; and mucilage-36.0%. They also contain a fatty oil with fatty acid composition of palmitic acid-6.79; stearic-13.21; archidic-0.89; oleic-52.21; linoleic-18.38; and linolenic-8.5%. The flowers contain nodososide, azralidoside, nodolidate, cyanidin-3-glucoside, beta-sitosterol, campesterol and their alpha-glucosides, ceryl alcohol, saturated hydrocarbons and fatty acids.

Other Uses
 

1.The wood is dark, moderately haed; sap woodlight brown; heartwood red. It is used for beams, posts, handles of axes and knives. The roots are used as laundry soap.

2.The tree is grown in gardens for ornament and for shade in tea and other plantations. It is also grown on farmlands for aesthetic effects.

3.Medicinal Uses: The leaves are purgative, though sometimes eaten with rice in South-East Asia. They are used for poulticing boils. The boiled leaves are used as a refrigerant. The ethanolic extract of the seeds exhibited haemoagglutinatng reaction in mules. The bark is rubbed on the skin for itch.
