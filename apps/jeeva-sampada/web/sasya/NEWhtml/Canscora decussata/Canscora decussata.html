HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, erect, somewhat flaccid simple or sparingly, decussate branched, glabrous herbs; usually slender in lower portion found throughout India (ascending to 2334 m)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stems 4-angled, angles sharply winged, 5.5-18.5 cm tall, 2-3 mm in diam. at base; branches decussate or crossed-armed. Leaves lanceolate, ovate or ovate-lanceolate, 0.7-3.5 x 0.4-0.9 cm, acute with minute mucro, subsessile-sessile, attenuate at abse, 3-nerved. Inflorescence terminal trichotomous cymes; peduncles winged, obsolete, 0.7-4.2 cm long; bracts narrowly ovate to ovate-lanceolate, acute, mucronate, 3-6 x 2-3 mm. Flowers tubular, white-pinkish, 0.6-1.6 cm long, pedicellate; pedicels quadrialate, 0.6-2.0 cm long. Calyx tubular, inflated, 4-winged; tube 0.6-1.2 cm long, contracted at mouth; lobes 4, ovate-lanceolate, pointed, 2-3 x 1-2 mm. Corolla tubular, funnel shaped, nearly bilabiate; tube nearly equal to calyx, 0.6-1.0 cm long, cylindrical; lobes 4, 4-6 x 1-2 mm, obovate, obtuse, upper 2 equal, separate, lower 2 combined nearly up to tip. Stamens 4,  only one fertile, inserted higher up; filaments filiform, 3-4 mm long, one fertile, longer; fertile anther oblong-ovoid, obtuse, c 1 mm long. Ovary oblong, obtuse, sessile, 6-7 x 2-3 mm; style slender, linear, c 5 mm long; stigma bilamellate, rotundate, revolute, minutely papillose. Capsules oblong-ellipsoid, as long as the calyx, 1.2-7.0 x 0.3-0.4 cm. Seeds numerous, minute, blackish-brown, compressed; testa nearly reticulate.

 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Terminal chyme ; Flower: Complete, hermaphrodite, regular ; Calyx: Tubular, 4-toothed, terrete ; Corolla: Tube cylindric, about as long as calyx, lobes 4, imbricate. ;Androecium: Stamen 1 perfect, 3 barren variously reduced, present at the mouth of crolla tube, anthers oblong; Gynoecium:  Ovary 1-celled, placenta not far intruded, style linear, stigma 2; Fruit: Capsule, 2-valved; Seed: Very small, polyhedral, dark brown . Flowering & Fruiting : April-November.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Drug is bitter, pungent, oleaginous, heating, laxative, alexiteric, anthelmintic and nervine tonic. It improves taste and appetite. It also cures tridosha, leucoderma, leprosy, tuberculosis and fevers. It is also used in inflammation, abdominal troubles, diseases of children. The fresh juice of the herb is given in all cases of insanity, in doses of about one ounce. The plant is pounded together with Cyperus rotundus and Withania sominifera and used to cure delirium.


Chemistry:
Compounds I and II isolated from whole plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Gluanone (fig. 1), canscoradione (fig. 2), friedelin, friedelin-3b-ol,b-amyrin,sitosterol,stigmasterol and campesterol were isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Newxanthones – 1,3,6,7-tetrahydroxyxanthone (fig. 3), 1,3,5,6– tetrahydroxyxanthone-2C-glucoside (fig. 4a) and 5,6-trihydroxy-3-methoxyxanthone (fig. 4b) – isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

New xanthones – xanthone A (fig. 5a), xanthone B (fig. 5b) and Xanthone C (fig. 5c) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Structure of three xanthones revised to 1-hydroxy-3,5,6,7-tetramethoxy-, 1,7-dihydroxy-3,5,6-trimethoxy- and 1,6,7-3,5-dimethoxy-xanthones; another new xanthone – 1,3,7-trihydroxy-5,6-dimethoxyxanthone – isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

(-)loliolide isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

1-methoxy-3,5-dihydroxyxanthone (fig. 4c) and its 3-O-rutinosyl derivative (fig. 4d) isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Two new xanthone glucosides and two free xanthones isolated and identified as 1-glucosyloxy-3-hydroxy-5-methoxy- (fig. 7), 7-glucosyloxy-1,6-dihydroxy-3,5-dimethoxy-(fig. 6a), 1,5,6-trihydroxy-3,7-dimethoxy-(fig. 6b) and 1,5,7-tyrihydroxy-3,6-dimethoxy-xanthones (fig. 6c); structures of the two previously reported xanthone-4 and xanthone-12 reassigned as 1,3,5-trihydroxy-6,7-dimethoxy- (fig. 6d) and 1,5-dihydroxy-3,6,7-trimethoxyxanthone (fig. 6e) respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Isolation of 3,6-dihydroxy-1,5,7-trimethoxyxanthone (fig. 8a), 5-glucosyl-1,6-dihydroxy-3,7-dimethoxyxanthone (fig. 8b) and 1,3,5-trihydroxy-6,7-dimethoxyxanthone from flowers and their characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).


Pharmacology:
Compound I reduced blood pressure and the rate and force of heart contraction. It increased the effect of acetylcholine on skeletal muscle and stimulated smooth muscles of intestines, uterus and bronchus. Compound II did not show these effects but prolonged the narcotic effect of pentobarbital. Its acute toxicity in rats was much less than that of compound I. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Mangiferin stimulated CNS,potentiated subanalgesic doses of morphine and exerted cardiostimulant and hydroelectric action on i.p. administration to mice, rats and dogs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)h.

CNS stimulant, reported above, was ascribed to sodium carbonate solution (2%) which was used as solvent; however, mangiferin showed significant anti-inflammatory activity in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)i.

An aqueous extract prepared from Canscora decussata (CdAqE), promotes the adhesion of peripheral neutrophils to human umbilical vein endothelial cells. The increased adhesion is as a result of induction of the expression of ICAM-1 and E-selectin on endothelial cells. CdAqE increases the expression of steady state transcript levels of these molecules as determined by RT-PCR. (Madan, et al, 2002).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for blood purification, stomachache (Stem, Srivastava and Varma, 1981)
