DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  Leaves large, oblong, attenuate and slightly obtuse upwards, short cuneate at the base, passing into a very short, amplexicaul, exauriculate petiole. Flowers 3-5 cm long, forming very long terminal and axillary, lax spikes, which after flowering reach 30-60 cm.; rachis quite glabrous. Bracteoles ovate-cuspidate sub equal, 4 times shorter than the calyx, almost pellucid. Calyx red, short cylindrical, shortly and acutely 5-dentate, along the ribs covered with stipitate, bifarous and subsessile glands. Corolla tube slender, 4 times as long as the calyx, limb wide, segments ovate, rotund, and cuspidate.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
The root is used as a powerful sialogogue. In South India, the dried root is highly valued as a remedy for secondary syphilis and leprosy. The milky juice is useful in ophthalmia and an external application for scabies. Its vesicating properties have been successfully utilized in curing certain cases of leucoderma. The root in combination with other drugs is prescribed for snakebite and scorpion-sting.


Chemistry:
The root bark contains an orange yellow pigment named plumbagin (2-methyl-5-hydroxy-1-4-naphthoquinone C11H8O3, m.p. 259-600) a sitosterol, a fatty alcohol, probably arachidyl alcohol, tannin and an amorphous brown pigment. Plumbagin is the active principle of the drug and it is present upto the extent of about 0.9 percent. The fresh root gives much higher yield of plumbagin than the roots stored for a long period.  
A new napthoquinone - 5-hydroxyplumbagin - along with plumbagin, sitosterol, stigmasterol and campesterol from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Isolation of a new dihydroflavanol - plumbaginol (fig. 1) from aerial parts and its characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Isolation of binapthaquinone roseamone (fig. 2) along with known nepthaquinone. The structure was elucidated by spectroscopic and chemical studies. (Dinda et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 5649m.
