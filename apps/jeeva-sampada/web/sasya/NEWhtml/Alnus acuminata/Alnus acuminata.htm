Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Quick growing tree, native to Central and South America. It grows well on steep mountains slopes. This species has given its best results on the humid lower mountain slopes in tropical latitudes. It occurs where mean annual temperatures range from 40-270C. it can withstand temperatures that dip briefly below 00C. The species is found on the moist flanks of mountains at elevations of 1,200-3,200 m. In the valleys, it has to be protected from cold dry winds or it will develop poorly. Usually found on deep, well-drained loams or on loamy sands of alluvial origin.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A quick growing tree. Broad, spreading root system close to soil surface. Light-grey, sometimes silvery bark. 

3 flowers in the axil of each bract of male catkin each with 4 stamens and 4 perianth leaves. The bracteoles present. All these leaves united with one another. 

In the female catkin only 2 lateral flowers and bracts. Flower chalazogamic.

After fertilization, one seeded nut is formed under which is a 5 lobed scale, the product of the subsequent growth of the 5 leaves. Seeds are winged.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: It is easily grown from seed. It is usually raised in nurseries and planted to the field after 1 or 2 years. When sown in soils where Alnus does not exist, the soil must be inoculated with appropriate nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel. Even-burning. Pruned branches and top-wood at the time of harvest are used as fuel.

Wood: Specific gravity of 0.5-0.6. Straight grained, fine-textured wood, which ranges from light to reddish-brown and has a lustrous surface and no odour.

The wood has strong, impregnable, fibres, a desirable attribute for making good pulp.

Its main uses are for timber, boxes, construction and furniture. It is often employed in bridges and pilings, furniture making, coffins, crates and plywood manufacture.

Other uses:

Watershed protection - Because it grows so well on slopes and because its root system tends to be lateral and extended rather than deep and confined, it is very useful for controlling erosion in steep and unstable soils.

Soil improvement - Although not legumes, Alnus species have nodules and are nitrogen fixing and they enhance soil fertility and benefit crops grown in conjunction with them. Clusters of light-yellow nodules occur on the roots of plants as young as 2 month old and are found from the base of the root to the ends of the rootlets.
