HABIT & HABITAT
Large, deciduous tree, c 25-50 m tall, buttressed at base. It is found in Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Tripura, Nagaland, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Goa Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Island.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  Bark bluish grey with vertical cracks. Leaves alternate, crowded at the end of the branchelets, broadly elliptic or elliptic-ovate, 10-20 x 7-16 cm, rounded or rarely acuminate at apex, narrowed at base, entire along margins, both surfaces puberulous in young and glabrous in old, coriacious. Flowers sessile, in axillary spikes, green to yellow, with an offensive odour; upper part of spike contains male flowers and lower one harmaphrodite; bracts linear, caducous. Calyx pubescent outside, wooly inside with long brown hairs; teeth rhoimboid, acute at apex. Fruits drupe, sub globose, gray, velvety, narrowed into a short stalk, 5-angled or ridged when dried.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine bark is used in anaemia and leucoderma. Fruits are bitter, pungent and used in bronchitis, sore throat, biliousness, inflammation, asthma, eye, nose, heart and gall bladder diseases. Seeds are used in thirst, vomiting, bronchitis, and corneal ulcer. In Unani system of medicine fruits are bitter and used in dyspepsia, bilios headache, diarrhoea piles and applied in eye. It is also used as brain tonic.
