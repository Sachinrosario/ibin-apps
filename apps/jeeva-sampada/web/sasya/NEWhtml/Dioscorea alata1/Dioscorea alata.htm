HABIT & HABITAT                                                                       
A large herbaceous climber, up to 50 ft. high, with quadrangular winged stems twining to the right. Dioscorea alata is a native of South East Asia and is considered to be closely allied to the wild species, Dioscorea pesimilis Pram & Burkill and Dioscorea hamiltonii. It is the most important species among cultivated yams and is grown throughout the tropics and in countries bordering the tropics.
It is also seen in cultivated fields and Plains, forest edges, plains and hilly forests,

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES                                                              
 The stem is four angled, with wide-spreading wings along the margins and rarely prickling. Quadrangular winged stems twining to the right. Leaves opposite or rarely alternate, usually 5- nerved. Bulbils globose, ovoid or obpyriform, sometimes much elongated or flattened, produced in large numbers in some races and sparingly in others. Tubers brown to black in colour, non- poisonous and edible; they may be single or several, polymorphous, cylindrical or clavate, and deeply descending into the soil, globose, stout and short, pyriform, lobed in various ways, fingered, fingered and fasciated, or losing its positive geotropism with subsequent change of direction in the soil.
Seventy-two races showing variations in size, shape and colour of tubers and in consistency and quality of flesh have been recognised. Individual tubers may attain 6-8 ft. in length and single tubers weighing 136 lb. have been recorded. In a few species the skin enveloping the tuber is thin, cracking and flaking. The flesh is tender, white or creamy white in some, and purple or magenta red throughout or just beneath the skin in others. Some races yield tubers with distinct flavours, e.g. Kamodio variety of Gujarat has the flavour of boiled rice; a variety from Philippines has a raspberry-like flavour. Tubers of some races are smooth and of others, covered with rootlets.

Seed Germination:
It is generally propagated by tuber tops bearing 2 or 3 buds each; in some races, aerial tubers are common and are used for propagation.

Cultivation:
The land is well ploughed, then holes are dug and manured, and cuttings of the yam are put into each, and watered till they sprout about the eighth day. The plant spreads over the ground if there is no frame at hand for it to climb.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers.

Uses

  • Tubers are starchy, and can be dried and ground into meal. They are used as vegetable in the same manner as potato. Their usage is gradually declining owing to the increasing popularity of potatoes. The hill tribes use the tuber as a
  • . substitute for rice.


Harvesting Practices
The yield of tubers under intensive cultivation ranges from 3 to 14 tons per acre.

Chemistry
Analysis of tubers of different races gave the following values (on dry matter basis) : albuminoids, 7.96-15.68; fat, 0.59-1.26; ash, 4.23-7.28; fibre, 2.19- 6.12; carbohydrates, 71.67-85.02; and P205, 0.44- 0.88%. Yams with a purple tint are sometimes used for colouring and flavouring ice creams. Dioscorea alata tubers contain on an average 21 %starch. The starch grains are transparent, Oval or rounded triangular in shape and do not separate easily during extraction with water. The tuber is considered anthelmmtic and useful in leprosy, piles and gonorrhoea.
