Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Medium sized deciduous tree suitable for marginal lands and field edges. Sub-Himalayan tract and outer valleys up to 1500 m from the Indus eastwards. Also in dry forests over eastern, central and southern India in the western peninsula. Found throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 1,300 m in the Himalayas.

Light demanding, requires good drainage, frost hardy. Seedlings frost tender, come up better under canopy or in cleared lines. Severe frost kills leaves, but saplings and poles recover and start growing again in summer. Fairly drought resistant, susceptible to fire damage, wind firm.

Coppices well.

Tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Middle sized tree with leaves divided less than ½ way down into 2 usually subacute lobes. Bark gray with longitudinal cracks, pale pink inside. Leaves rather broader than deep, rigidly subcoriaceous, deeply cordate.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers beautifully variegated with red and yellow, or sometimes pure white (var. candida), in few flowered, lateral, sessile or short-peduncled corymbs.

Pods long, hard, flat frequently variegated with reddish-brown streaks, glabrous, dehiscent, 10-15 seeded. Seeds brown, flat nearly circular with coriaceous testa.There are pink-white and purple flowering varieties.

Floral and seed biology:

Grown by entire transplanting and direct sowing. It is propagated from seeds sown in line; the soil is well loosened and is followed by regular watering and weeding. Stump planting also gives good results.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Wood is a moderately good fuel. Calorific value of the sapwood (ash 3.03%) 4791 kcal (moisture-free wood)

Wood: Wood is greyish brown and moderately hard (air dry wt 704 kg/m3). It is used for agricultural implements.

Other uses:

Leaves - Tree yields good fodder. Leaves are rich in reducing sugars and have good nutritive value for the healthy development of tasar silk worms, Antheraea mylitta Drury. Leaves are used for the preparation of bidis.

Gum - Tree yields a gum which is similar to cherry gum.

Seeds yield fatty oil on extraction with petroleum ether and only 6.1% in a hydraulic press.

Bark - Bark yields fiber. Also used for dying and tanning. Decoction medicinally useful.

Roots - carminative and used in dyspepsia and flatulence. Decoction is reported to prevent obesity.

Leaves, flowers, flower buds used as vegetables.

Flower - Flower buds are pickled. Flower buds and bark yields dye and medicine. Flowers are laxative.
