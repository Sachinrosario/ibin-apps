HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, perennial, ascending herbs, up to 1-1.5 m tall; branches terete, slightly steriate, glabrous. It is found in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Tripura, Manipur, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Island, Daman & Diu, Laccadives.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, perennial, ascending herbs, up to 1-1.5 m tall; branches terete, slightly steriate, glabrous. Leaves 1-foliate, oblong or lanceolate, 2-7.5 x 0.5- 2.0 cm, appressedly pubescent on nerves beneath; petioles 3-10 mm long; stipulate. Flowers erect, appressed to a rachis, redish purple, geminate in spicate raceme; bracts conspicuous, 1.2 cm long, ovate, acuminate, silky outside. Calyx 6-15 mm long, striate. Pods 9-15 x 2-3 mm, reticulately veined, shortly stalked, glabrous; joints 4-6, 1-seeded.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Inflorescence: Dense spicate racemes; Flower: bracteate, pedicillate, hermaphrodite, complete ; Calyx: Pubescent, ciliate, striate, as long as or longer than the two joints of the pods tbe funnel shaped, plicate; Corolla: not hardly exserted, orbicular, narrowed into claws, wings obliquely oblong, adhering to the keel, slightly curved, obtuse, usually appendiculate ; Androecium: Stamens 10, diadelphous, anthers uniform, filaments free, ;Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, ovules numerous, style incurved, stigma capitate; Fruit: pods usually exerted, very shortly stalked, glabrous, terete-compressed, apiculate, slightly moniliform, joints 4-6, reticulately veined; Seed: 1. 

Floral and seed biology:
September-November. Flowering & Fruiting:  September. 


Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 16.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  Root is used as substitute for liuorice.
Chemistry:

Myricyl alcohol, b-sitosterol and its acetate, rutin, pinitol and an unidentified flavonol isolated from leaves. (Jain and Gupta, 1981).

Three flavonoid glycosides have been isolated from the leaves of Alysicarpus longifolius and characterized as quercetin-7-O rhamnoside, chrysoeriol-7-Oxyloside and Kaempferol-3-O-xyloside-7-O-rhamnoside on the basis of spectral and chemical studies. (Jain and Gupta, 1986).
