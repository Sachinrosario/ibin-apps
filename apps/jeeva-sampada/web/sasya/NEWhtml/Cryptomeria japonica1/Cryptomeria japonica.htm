Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Monotypic tree genus, native of China and Japan. Seeds were first brought to India in 1844, and cultivation was started in Darjeeling, which now has extensive forests. In moist climate of eastern Himalayas, it grows very rapidly. It has also naturalised in the Western Himalayas and grows up to an altitude of 1800-2400 m. outside these altitude limits growth is stunted.

It thrives well on rich soils in damp cool climtes with heavy well-distributed rainfall.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Plant is tall, conical, much-branched evergreen tree, reaching a height of 50 m or more and a girth of 7-8 m. the bark is fibrous, reddish-brown and peels off in long strips. The leaves are linear, awl-shaped and spirally arranged. They are keeled on both the surfaces and have a somewhat spinous tip. The leaf bases are fused with the stem and form its outer covering. The branches are in whorls. The branchlets with the awl-shaped leaves are deciduous.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

The plant is monoecious. Male and female cones are usually borne on different branchlets, occasionally on the same branchlet. The male cones arise in clusters near the tip of the young shoots, while the female cones are terminal on the branchlets.

Male cone - A male cone cluster consists of 10-18 cones, each borne in the axil of a bract. Each cone bears 18-25 spirally arranged microsporophylls, the two lowermost microsporophylls are usually sterile. The stalk of each sporophyll is almost at right angles to the axis of the cone. It is a peltate structure which extends upward as a membranous flap and bears three or four microsporangia on its lower (abaxial) surface.

Female cone - The apex of the branchlets is generally used up during the differentiation of the terminal female cones. Rarely, the axis of the cone may grow into a vegetative shoot which bears a cluster of male cones. Young female cone has a curved stalk and emerges from the rosette of leaves at the time of pollination. Mature cone is yellowish-brown, and comprises 26-30 spirally arranged scales. The upper9-10 small scales are sterile. A mature fertile scale bears on the upper side 3-5 spinous processes at the distal end. The bract and ovuliferous scales are fused except at the tip. Each scale bears 3-4 adaxial ovules with micropyle pointing away from the cone axis.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

The male cones are initiated in June. The pollen is shed in February and male gametes are formed by May. The female cones appear in July - 1 month after the male cones. Undergoes a rest period from September to January.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Seeds do not have a period of dormancy. The germination (%) is low and is epigeal. Seeds are sown broadcast mixed with wood ash, fine earth or leaf mould, in shaded beds which are watered daily. Seeds germinate in 3-4 weeks and pricking out at a spacing of 3-4 in. each way is effected in June. The shade is removed after 3 months, and seedlings when 3 years old, are transplanted in the field at a spacing of 6 ft. each way in June or December - January.

Branch-cuttings has not proved successful in India..

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood used as fuel.

Wood: The wood is reddish brown and fragrant. It shows clearly defined growth rings, variable in width and slightly wavy. Early and late wood are well differentiated. Wood used for boats. Wood is used as a source of pulp for making paper.
