Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A large spreading, glabrous, deciduous tree, occurs in sub-Himalayan tract ascending up to 600 m, central India, Chhota Nagpur and the Indian Peninsula.

Thrives in deep porous soil with good drainage but becomes stunted and crooked on poor shallow soil. It is a moderate light demander and is not frost-hardy. Plants are present in forests of Dehra Dun, Saharanpur, Orissa, Chota Nagpur, Bihar, West Bengal, hills of Deccan, eastern slopes of western ghats.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Large spreading glabrous deciduous tree. Bark grey, pustular, exfoliating in somewhat corky scales. Leaves elliptic, acuminate, glabrous, entire (those of the seedlings and shoots often serrate), base founded or cordate; main nerves 5-7 pairs. Flowers usually male and hermaphrodite mixed, in short racemes and fascicles at the scars of fallen leaves. Sepals often 4, pubescent. Stamens 4 8 (often 6 or 7); filaments glabrous, anthers pubescent. Ovary compressed, pubescent, 1-celled, stalked, the stalk lengthening as the seed ripens, styles long, stigmatose on the inside throughout their whole length. Samara nearly orbicular, with reticulately veined wings.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Tree sheds its seeds in hot season and they germinate at the commencement of the rains. Protection from sun in early stages is beneficial. Rate of growth is fast. It coppices well.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Cercospora holopteleae Chiddarwar – leaf infection in Khandala (Maharashtra)

Colletotrichum holopteleae Hasija – leaf infection in Jabalpur (M.P.)

Mycosphaerella holopteleae Naphade – leaf infection in Pune (Maharashtra)

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used: Bark, whole plant, seeds and leaves.

Uses:

Wood is used as a fuelwood. It is also used to make charcoal. Wood is used for agricultural implements and brush backs. It is also used for indoor building purposes, cheap furniture, cabinet work, carving, ploughs, yokes, carts and carriages, combs, shoe heels, mathematical instruments, warper bobbins for jute mills, cotton rells, dugout boats. The wood is suitable for plywood, packing cases and boxes, slate frames, multi-ply jute bobbins, match boxes and splints and paper pulp.

Other uses:

Bark - Bark may be pulped and made into hard boardds and insulation boards.

Leaves and young fruits are eaten, though not relished, by livestock.

Bark and leaves are bitter, astringent and acrid. These are medicinally useful. Seeds also are useful in medicine and as deodorant for foul smell of body. Bark paste is used to cure ring worm infection.

Miscellaneous Information

Wood: Wood light yellow or yellowish grey, darkening on exposure, moderately hard, moderately heavy, average weight 595 kg/m3 (37 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; somewhat lustrous, medium and even-textured with somewhat interlocked grain. Unpleasent odour when freshly cut.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings distinct to indistinct. Pores medium-sized to small, just visible to the eye, distinct under the lens, moderately numerous, solitary and in short radial multiples of 2-3 rarely more, often filled with white chalky deposits appearing as white dots; vessel lines fine, distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues aliform to confluent, just visible to the eye, prominent under the hand lens as light coloured sheaths round the vessels often with narrow wing-like extensions, connecting adjacent pores. Rays moderately fine to moderately broad, distinct under the hand lens, rather numerous, somewhat closely-spaced. Ripple marks present, just visible to the eye, prominent under the lens, about 40 per cm.

The tree sheds its seeds during hot season.
