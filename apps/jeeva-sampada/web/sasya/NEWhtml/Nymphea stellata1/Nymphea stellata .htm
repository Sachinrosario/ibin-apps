 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A large, perennial, aquatic herb with a short, ovoid, acute rootstock, found in ponds and ditches throughout the warmer parts of India.
This species includes several varieties differing colour and size of flowers. It is often confused wit Nymphea  caerulea Sav. (Egyptian Blue Lotus) and capensis Thunb. (Cape Blue Water-Lily), but can be distinguished from them by the dentate nature leaves, smaller flowers and absence of fragraiw .

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Leaves peltate, 12-20 cm. in diameter, orbicular, or obtusely sinuate-dentate, glabrous both surfaces, often blotched with purple beneath.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers solitary, blue, white, purple or pink ; fan a spongy berry; seeds minute, longitudinal striate.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Egg-sized rhizomes, tender leaves and flow peduncles are used as vegetable.

Uses
  1. Various parts of the plant are edible. The rhizome is consumed boiled or after roasting. 
  2. The seeds are eaten in times of scarcity. They a made into flour and made into bread along with wheat or barley flour: it, however, imparts an obtainable odour to the bread.

Other Uses
  1. The powdered rhizome is given in dyspepsia diarrhoea and piles. An infusion of rhizomes and stems is considered emollient and diuretic ; it is us for blennorrhagia and diseases of the urinary tract.  
  2. In Malagasy (Madagascar), leaves are applied topically in erysipelas. Macerated leaves are used a lotion in eruptive fevers. A decoction of flowers considered narcotic. Seeds are stomachic and restoitive.

Economical Potential to the country
In Ceylon, efforts have been made to cultivate this species as an economic crop in paddy fields, which are usually uncultivated during the monsoon period. By planting the rhizomes with suitable spacing and prop manuring, a harvest of c. 2,500 kg. of rhizomes per hectare can be obtained.
