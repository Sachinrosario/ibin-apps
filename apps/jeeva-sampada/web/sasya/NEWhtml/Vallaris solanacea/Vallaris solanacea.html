DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves elliptic or oblong-lanceolate, 5.0-11.5 x 2.5-3.0 cm, acuminate at apex, acute at base, entire along margin, shining above, glabrous or pubescent; petioles 1-2 cm long, with glands in the axils. Flowers usually in subcorymbose cymes, fragrant; peduncles slender, glabrous or pubescent; pedicels usually decurved, pubescent; bracts linear or lanceolate, acute, pubescent. Calyx lobes ovate or lanceolate, 2.0-2.5 mm long, acute, pubescent; tube short. Corolla white, minutely pubescent outside; tube 2.5 cm long; lobes obovate, obtuse. Follicles oblong, 10-15 cm long. Seeds ovoid, c 1.25 cm long, beaked; coma c 2 cm long, silvery white.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:
  • Acoschimperoside P, vallaroside, mp. 122°, solanoside, mp. 219°, their monoacetates and vallarosolanoside, mp. 230o, isolated from seeds; vallaroside characterised as digitoxigenin-a-L-vallaropyranoside, solanoside as digitoxigenin-a-L-acofriopyranoside, and vallarosolanoside as oleandrigenin-a-L-vallaropyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969)c .
  • β-sitosterol, β-amyrin and ursolic acid isolated from leaves ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984)a ‑.

Pharmacology:

PHARMACOLOGY
  • Glucoside mixture from leaves possessed powerful digitalis-like activity in addition to general musculotropic activity on smooth muscle. It also caused emesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969)b .

Ethnobotany:

Medicine for abortifacient (stem, Tarafder, 1983); wounds (latex, Sharma et al., 1979). Cordage (fibre,  Goel et al., 1984).
