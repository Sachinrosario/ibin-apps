Chemistry:  

A lignan - cubebin, mp. 133°C - from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
Two new sesquiterpene hydrocarbons - bicyclosesquiterphellandrene (fig. 1a) and 1-epibicyclosesquiphellandrene (fig. 1b) - isolated from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
(-)Clusin, (-)cubebin, (-)dihydrocubebin, (-)hinokinin and (2R,3R)2-(3'',4'',5''-trimethoxybenzyl)-3-(3',4'-methylenedioxybenzyl)-1,4-butanediol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
a-ethoxycubebin, b-ethoxycubebin, dihydrocybebin monoacetone, heterotropan,magnosalin, 5''-methoxyhinokinin and 2,4,5-trimethoxybenzaldehyde isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Isolation of a oxygenated cyclohexane - piperenol B (fig. 2) - together with piperenol A, (+)crotepoxide and (+)zeylenol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Piperine, b sitosterol and alkaloid piplartine isolated from stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)

·        b-sitosterol isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Eugenol, methyleugenol, p-cymene, a-terpineol, teripinyl acetate and caryophyllene detected in essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·        Five propenylphenols – chavicol, chavibetol, allylpyrocatechol, chavibetol acetate and allyl-pyrocatechol diacetate isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·        Chavibetol (53.1), chavibetol acetate (15.5), caryophyllene (3.79), allylpyrocatechol diacetate (0.71), camphene (0.48), chavibetol methyl ether (0.48), eugenol (0.32), a-pinene (0.21), b-pinene (0.21), a-limonene (0.14), safrole (0.11), 1,8-cineole (0.04), p-cymene (0.11) and allylpyrocatechol monoacetate determined in leaf oil of plant grown in Phillippines. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Ursolic acid, its 3b-acetyl derivative, ursolic acid and b-sitosterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·        Stem bark of Piper chaba contained piperamine, pallitorine, 2,4-decadienoic acid piperidide and kusunokinin. (Connolly et al.,1995).

·        A new lignan, epimers of {8R, 8’R}-9-hydroxy, 3,4-dimethoxy, 3’,4’-methylenedioxy-9,9’ epoxylignan, was isolated from the chloroform-soluble fraction of a crude alcoholic extrac6t of P. chaba. Its proposd structure was established from NMR and mass spectral studies. (Bhandari et al., 1998).

·        Essential oil composition of the fruit oils of Piper chaba were investigated by GC and GC/MS. Piper chaba oils is found to contain few monoterpene hydrocarbons, a moderate content of sesquiterpenes and high content of aliphatic hydrocarbons. The content of caryophyllene oxide (7.4%) was the highest in Piper chaba and b-caryophyllene was 39.7, 10.2 and 5.2% in Piper chaba, P. nigrum and P. longum respectively. (Tewtrakul et al., 2000).

Pharmacology:

·        Chavicol, chavibetol, allylpyrocatechol, chavibetol acetate and allylpyrocatechol diacetate exhibited fungicidal and nematocidal activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

·        Oral administration of crude alcoholic extract of leaf stalk (1.25 and 1.5 g/kg daily for 30 consecutive days) manifested antifertility effect in male rats, leading to decrease in relative organ weight with 0 to 33.0% fertility rate, reduction in sperm count and sluggish motility. Significant increase (P<0.01) in cholesterol (adrenal and testis) was also observed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

·        Ursolic acid and b-sitosterol in decreasing order of activity, inhibited arachidonic acid-inhibited platelet aggregation, whereas aggregation induced by platelet-activating factor was inhibited only by ursonic acid and b-sitosterol. Ursonic acid (25.0 to 100.0 mg/kg) exhibited concentration dependent anti-inflammatory activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Drug prepared from P. chaba shows beneficial effects with respect to special behaviour in animals who have been treated with alcohol. LH-FSH producing basophils in the pituitary inc. and there was an increase in the sperm count. (Mitra et al., 1996).


Ethnobotany: Medicine for asthma, bronchitis, cholera, fever, phthisis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Gonorrhoea, whooping cough (Fruit, Gupta, 1981)

Medicine for abortifacient (Root, Tarafder, 1983a); Antifertility (Root, Tarafder, 1983b; Majumdar et al, 1978; Tuber, Tiwari et al, 1979); Antiseptic (Leaf, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Asthma, bronchitis, colic, dysentery (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); eye disease (Leaf, Kharkongor and Joseph, 1981); Haemostat on cuts, injuries (Leaf, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Madness, phthisis, sores, syphilis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Stomach disease (Leaf, Nagendra Prasad and Abraham, 1984); Wounds (Leaf, Tarafder, 1984); Edible masticatory (Leaf, Nagendra Prasad and Abraham, 1984); Symbolically offered to Shiva in religious ceremony (Leaf, Agarwal, 1981)
