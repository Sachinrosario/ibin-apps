UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Mucilage of seed of contains galactose and mannose in ration 1:1. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

A new chemonastic compound – M-LMF 5 – isolated and characterized as 4-O-(3,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid)-b-D-glucuronide; its structure also confirmed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Isolation of 2’’-O-rhamosyl derivatives of orientin and isoorientin from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

From the leaves of Mimosa pudica Linn, a new phenolic ketone, 4-(24'-methoxy-24'-methyl-1'-oxo-5'-n-propyltetracosanyl)-phenol has been isolated and characterized by spectral data. (Josewin et al, 1999) C.A, 1999,131:56459

The quantity of rutin from methanol extract of Mimosa pudica L. (leaves and flowers), was determined as 2.70 %. (Toker et al, 1997) C.A.1998, 128:86031

 


Pharmacology:
Tactile-stimulated movement by injection of mimosine into plant studied; it produced more rapid leaf movement and increased vacuole size from 4.6 mm to 6.3 mm. thus leaf movement may be controlled by mimosine working through vacuoles. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Ethanolic extract of Mimosa pudica leaves given by oral route to mice at a dose of 250 mg/kg showed a significant hyperglycemic effect. (Amalraj and Ignacimuthu, 2002)

Aqueous extracts from dried leaves of Mimosa pudica are employed to alleviate depression at various concentrations. Diazepam increased the open-arms exploration time in the elevated plus-maze test, but M. pudica did not show any comparable action at any tested dose. M. pudica therefore produced an antide-pressant-like profile similar to two tricyclic antidepressants.(Molina et al, 1999)

Inhalational and local anesthetics inhibited motor mechanisms of the mimosa plant. This may be a model to elucidate the mechanisms of action of anesthetics. (Milne and Beamish, 1999)

Aqueous and alcoholic extracts of dried roots of Mimosa pudica were tested for their inhibitory activity on lethality, myotoxicity and toxic enzymes of Naja kaouthia venom. The aqueous extract, particularly the normal water extract, displayed a significant inhibitory effect on the lethality, myotoxicity and tested enzyme activities of venom compared with alcoholic extracts. (Mahanta and Mukherjee, 2001)

Mimosa pudica root powder (150 mg/kg body weight) when administered intragastrically, altered the oestrous cycle pattern in female Rattus norvegicus. Nucleated and cornified cells were absent in all rats. The smear was characterized by leucocytes only, as in dioestrus, which persisted for 2 weeks. There was a significant reduction in the number of normal ova in rats treated with the root powder compared with the control rats, and a significant increase in the number of degenerated ova. (Valsala and Karpagaganapathy, 2002)
