Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large, deciduous, robust tree suitable for open lands and along pathways. It is one of the best known trees of India. There are extensive plantations in Central and South India. in the sub-Himalayan tracts it ascends 1500 m; also Chota Nagpur region, Indian peninsula and the Andamans.

After first year the trees can tolerate light frost and drought. Occurs from sea level to 1,600 m in India. In its native tropical and subtropical grasslands, annual rainfall varies from 500 to 2,000 mm and the summers are wet. Tree thrives on a variety of soils, though it prefers moist conditions and grows best on well-drained loam. The trees tolerate salt spray and grow well near the seashore.

Strong light demander.It is a fast growing tree.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A handsome, unarmed, deciduous tree with spreading umbrella of feathery foliage. Bark pale; young shoots glabrous. It may reach 30 m tall and a diameter of 1 m. Leaves abruptly bipinnate; main rachis pubescent or glabrous, furnished with a large gland on the petiole above the base and one below the uppermost pair of pinnae; petioles swollen and hairy at the base; pinnae 2-3 (rarely 4) pairs, 4-5 in long, their rachises glabrous or pubescent, swollen and hairy at the bases. Leaflets 5-9 pairs, with glands between their bases, the lateral leaflets elliptic-oblong, the 2 terminal obovate-oblong, all pale green, unequal sided, very obtuse, glabrous above, pubescent and reticulately veined beneath, base obliquely rounded or truncate; petiolules very short, hairy.

It produces white flower heads with striking green stamens, fragrant. Peduncles more or less pubescent, solitary or 2-4 together from the axils of the upper leaves; pedicels pubescent. Calyx pubescent; teeth short, deltoid. Corolla tube glabrous; lobes triangular, acute, pubescent outside. Stamens much longer than the corolla; filaments conate at the base into a short tube. Pods linear, oblong, bluntly pointed, thin, pale-yellow, reticulately veined above the seeds, smooth, shining. Seeds 4-12, ellipsoid-oblong, compressed, foveolate on the faces, pale brown.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Leaf shedding takes place during October - November in northern Indian conditions except in wet situations where it continues until January. Flowering takes place during March - April. Fruits are formed and develop by August and start turning yellow in November. These ripen from December - February and hang on trees until March - April when blown down by storms and rains.

Seed Germination & Propagation: It produces seed prolifically and easily propagated by seed - even by direct sowing. (Seeds remain viable for 4 or 5 years at ambient temperatures).

Seeds are immersed in boiling water and allowed to cool and soak for 24 hours. Germination is 50-90 per cent.

Seedlings need to be regularly weeded during their first 2 years of growth.

Artificial propagation is done by either direct sowing of seeds or planting nursery-raised seedlings, the latter being preferred, though stump planting is reported to give better success and is probably the easiest for raising this species.

Tree can also be propagated by stem cuttings or root-shoot cuttings. It sprouts root suckers vigorously on root injury. For stumps 8 cm tall, seedlings are pricked out in transplanted beds and maintained for about 15 months until they attain the required size for stump transpiration. Seeds are sown in March - April and watered regularly. The seedlings are transplanted during rainy season.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Good fuel. Calorific value of the sapwood and the heartwood is 5,163 and 5,166 calories. The calorific value of moisture free heartwood is 5,200 kcal per kg.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, white or yellowish-white, heartwood brown to dark brown or chocolate coloured with darker streaks and fairly lustrous. Wood moderately hard to hard, moderately heavy to heavy, average weight 640 kg/m3 (40 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; coarse and even-textured with straight to somewhat wavy or slightly interlocked grain.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings distinct to indistinct. Pores large to very large, clearly visible to the eye, scanty, solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3, occasionally filled with dark, gummy deposits; vessel lines prominent on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly aliform forming eyelets round the pores, rarely connecting adjacent pores. Rays fine to very fine, indistinct to just visible to the eye, distinct under the lens as pinkish or light-coloured lines of varying thickness, usually somewhat widely-spaced.

Wood is similar to kokko (Albizia odoratissima Benth.) and safed siris (Albizia procera Benth.) from which it is not possible to distinguish it under hand lens.

Timber. Heartwood is finding increasing use in furniture and as construction timber for houses. While not easy to work by hand, it carves and polishes well and resembles rosewood.

The tree is good substitute for teak and sal after suitable seasoning and preservative treatment. The wood gives 50 per cent yield of bleached pulp by the sulphate process, suitable for writing and printing paper. Since the wood is short fibred (av fibre length, 1.09 mm), admixture of its pulp with c 30 per cent of bamboo pulp may be necessary for the production of paper on a commercial paper machine.

Wood is used for internal panelling.

Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:

Gum - A reddish brown gum exudes from the bark and dries into stalactiform masses. It is used as an adulterant of gum arabic and sometimes as its substitute.

Other uses:

Fodder - The young foliage contains about 20 per cent protein and is fed to livestock. One tree, it is alleged, may provide 20 per cent of a water buffalo's annual feed, or 27 percent of a cow's. the leaves also make useful green manure.

Nitrogen fixer.

Medicinal - Plant is reported to have antiseptic, anti-dysenteric and anti-tubercular properties.

Root - Rootbark and root-gum are used as dental powder for strengthening the gums.

Beautification - In India, this attractive tree is often planted for shade in gardens and along roadsides and in tea, cardamom and coffee plantations. It is highly regarded by beekeepers for the light-coloured honey that is collected as nectar from the flowers of A. lebbeck.

Erosion control - The tree is a good soil binder and therefore often planted along embankments.

Seeds - Seeds are medicinally used.

Miscellaneous Information

 

It can also be propagated by stem cuttings or root - shoot cuttings. It sprouts root suckers vigorously when its roots are injured. Extra-floral nectaries on leaves attract ants.
Seedlings show mycorrhizal association. This helps the seedlings in successful transplantation and establishment on barren lands or under stressed conditions.
Tree is a nitrogen fixer.
The plants are micropropagated. Haploid plantlets have been produced through anther culture.
The tree is planted along roadsides and in gardens.
