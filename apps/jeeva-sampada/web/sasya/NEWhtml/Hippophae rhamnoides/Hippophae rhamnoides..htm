Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
A dioecious, usually spinescent shrub or a small tree up to 40 ft. high, with rough brown bark, occuring in the river beds of the drier ranges of the north-western Himalayas at altitudes of 7,000-12,000 ft.

Availability :  
It is widely distributed throughout the temperate zones of Asia and Europe and also throughout the subtropical zones of Asia at high altitudes but grows at an altitude upto 5,200 m. In India it is distributed in Himalayan region including Arunachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Lahul and Spiti valley of Himachal Pradesh.

It is being China, Russia, Britain, Germany, Finland, romania, France, Nepal, Bhutan and Pakistan.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Bark smooth, ashy or silvery gray, or finally rough & dark brown. Leaves small, linear-lanceolate, covered on both sides with silvery scales; flowers very small, greenish or yellowish, appearing with new leaves: male in axillary clusters, female solitary ; fruits enclosed by succulent receptacle, ovoid, 0.25 in. long, orange- yellow or scarlet; seeds oblong, testa crustaceous, shiny. It has special berry like fruit, which is different from common fruits or berries. Embyologically it develops from an ovary and calyx tube, which is connected with the ovary. Therefore, Seabuckthorn fruit is a combination of an upsplit, fleshy, expanded calyx tube and an ovary. The fruit is small, orange red in colour.

Cultivation
It can resist low temperature of upto -43 degree C and can withstand heat of 40 degree C. Some species grow well in regions that only have a precipitation of about 300 mm & some species also grow in soils with a ph 9.5 while others grow even soils that contain 1.1 % salts.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruits, Plants.

Uses

1.The fruit is acidic and is made into a jelly with sugar.

2.The berry contains enormous amounts of vitamin c which is 5 times higher than any 3.other fruit or vegetable, vitamin A

4.A decoction of berries is used in France for cutaneous eruptions.

5.The plant is given in lung complaints by Tibetans.

 

Harvesting Practices
Harvesting of small sized fruit is very difficult because of the thorny nature of the plant and also fruit adheres to the plant strongly. For this reason the branches are beaten with sticks to harvest the fruit  or the fruit is removed from the branches after breaking them off from the mother plant. 

Yield:
On an average 600 g ripe fruits are harvested from a well developed plant. The fruit harvested by one person in a day is 8-10 kg in beating method and 16-18 kg in branch separation method. 
Natural seabuck forest can yield 750-1,500 kg of berries per hectare.

Produce and Products 
Juice, ready to serve beverages, squash, syrup, beer, jam, oil, cheese and jelly.

Chemistry
The fruit is a rich source of vitamin C (ascorbic acid, 135-608; dehydro ascorbic acid, 30 mg./100 g.).
The mature fruit is rich in carotene (6"-8 mg./ 100 g. almost half that of carrots); it also contains vitamin B,. The carotenoid pigments present in the fruit are: /3-carotene, cryptoxanthin, zeaxanthin and its dipalmitic ester physalien, lycopene and y-caro- tene. An anthocyanin identical with peonin (from Paeonia sp.) and a flavonol, iso-rhamnetin, are also present. The fruit contains malic acid, citric acid, tartaric acid, traces of succinic acid, reducing sugars. pectins, tannins and mannitol .The fruit contains a dark red fatty oil (c. 2%) of pleasant odour and taste with the following characteristics: d, 0.9243 ; n, 1.4642 ; acid val., 7.35 ; sap. val., 190; iod. val., 76.5 ; solid, pt, 6.4° ; R.M. val., 0.79; acetyl val., 16.4; and unsapon. matter, 3.66%. The component fatty acids are: palmitic and stearic, 10.4 ; oleic, 63.4 ; and linoleic, 10.5%. The centrifuged oil is a concentrated source of carotene (164 mg./loo g.) The seeds contain (12.13%) a yellow, slow-drying oil with the following constants: d, 0.9278; n, 1.4739; s^d val., 4.4; sap. val., 192.5; acetyl val., 10.4; iod. val., 138.2; and unsapon. matter, 1.78%.
It contains aliphatic esters such as ethyl, 3-methylbutyl and cis-3-hexen-1-yl esters, ehtyl hexonate, -methylbutyl, 3-mrthylbutanoate, 3-methylbutanoic acid, 3-methylbutyl benzoate and 3-methylbutyl octanoate.

Processing Techniques 
The clarification juice can be prepared from seabuckthorn berries by pectinase enzyme treatment of the pulp at room temperature for 30 min. The clear juice can be obtained by filtration.

Other Uses

  1. The wood (wt., 38-54 Ib./cu. ft.) is yellowish brown, mottled, hard and close-grained. It is used as fuel and for making charcoal. alicifolia D. Don is a willow-like shrub occurring almost throughout the temperate Himalayas. It is allied to H. rhamnoides and has the same uses.
  2. A syrup prepared from it is used in lung complaints.
  3. Decoction is used for cutaneous eruptions.
  4. The pulp and the seed contain high quality oil, which is considered to be carrier of many concentrated bioactive substances.
  5. Seabuckthorn products based on fruit pulp or juices have therapeutic and chemopreventive activities.
  6. The juice is a good appetizer, prevents tumour production, sarcoma, lymphocytic leukemia and human gastric cancer cells.

Economical Potential to the country

 

Seabuchthorn has enormous potential for satisfying the soil erosion problem. Because of this China, Mangolia and former USSR are having this crop on approximately 810,000 hectares (natural growth) and 300,000 hectares (plantation).
