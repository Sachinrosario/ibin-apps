 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, woody, annual or perennial aromatic herbs or undershrubs, 50-80 cm. tall, softly hairy. it is found throughout India ascending to 2000 m in Himalayas. 


Availability :  
Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant is wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem tetrangular, sparingly hairy or densely pubescent, sometimes almost wooly. Leaves ovate to ovate-lanceolate, 2.0-10.5 x 1.2-7.0 cm, acute at apex, truncate or cordate at base, crenate-serrate at margin, densely pubescent on both surfaces; petioles 1-7 cm long, densely hairy.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Long peduncled spikes; Flower: Pale purple ; Calyx: Ovoid, straight, 5-toothed ; Corolla: Corolla tube short, annulate within, upper lip erect  ;Androecium: Stamens exserted, anther 2-celled, conviving; Gynoecium: Ovary superior, bilocular, style bifid; Fruit: Nutlets brown, compressed; Seed:  3-4. Flowering & Fruiting : Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.September-January.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers bluish-purple, in whorls in axillary cymes or spikes; bracts linear, 4-5 mm long, pubescent. Calyx bell-shaped; lobes 5, equal, lanceolate, 1.2 cm long, as long as the tube, acute at paex, ciliate, growing with the enlargement of the fruits. Corolla bluish-purple, bilabiate, c 2 cm long; upper lip ciliate, puberulous without. Stamens 4; filaments bearded, c 1.2 cm long. Ovary ovoid, 0.5 mm long.  Nutlets 4, ovoid, 0.1-0.25 cm long, angled, black, smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The plants are used in antipyretic, astringent, carminative, fever, muscles pain, nervous complaints. Roots are also used as antidote to poisonous bites.


Chemistry:
A macrocyclic diterpene – ovatodiolide (fig. 1.), m.p. 148° - isolated from leaves and its structure revised. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Cerotic, pentacosanic, lignoceric, tricosanoic, behenic, heneicosanic, arachic, stearic and palmitic acids isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Stereochemistry of macrocyclic diterpenoids – ovatodiolide (fig. 2a) and isoovatodiolide (fig. 2b) established by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Ovatodiolide, anisomelic acid, anisomelin, tetracosane, tetracosanol, b-amyrin, friedelin and betulonic acid isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

New acetylated glucoside – anisofolin A – isolated from aerial parts and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Anisofolins B and C isolated from aerial parts and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

5-hydroxy-3’,4’,6,7-tetramethylflavone, anisomelin and apigenin from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Isolation of 5,6-dimethoxy-7,3’,4’-trihydroxyflavone from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

n-hentriacontane, glutinone, friedlin, ovatodiolide,anisomelic acid, betulin, methyl p-hydroxycinnamate, b-sitosterol and its glucosidefrom aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Ovatodiolide and four related macrocyclic diterpenoids (I, fig. 3, Ii, fig. 4, III, fig. 4, IV fig. 5) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Isolation of cosmosiin hydrate, cosmosiin, terniflorin, pruning, pruning-6’’-p-coumarate and pruning-3’’,6’’-di-p-coumarate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

n-hexacosane, n-hexacosanol, b-amyrin, ovatodiolide, anisomelic acid isolated from unsaponifiable fraction of stem whereas saponifiable fraction contained stearic, palmitic and lignoceric acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Determination of b-pinene (28.0), eugenol (24.5), caryophyllene (15.2), 1,8-cineole (11.9), citral (9.5), methylchavicol (9.2), a-pinene (7.0), azulene (6.0), (+)limonene (3.0), nerol (2.2), borneol (2.13) and a-terpineol (2.1%) in leaf essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

The 1H and 13C-NMR spectral data of ovatodioline a diterpenoid from Anisomeles indicia was reported. (Shahidul et al., 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 157215g.


Pharmacology:
Ovatodiolide and diterpenoids I-IV were strongly cytotoxic to culturued kB cells, ovatodiolide being the most active with IC 50, 0.5mg/ml. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

 Ovatodiolide concentration dependently inhibited contractility and ca2+ current of isolated from heart and produced transient hypnotensive effect in dogs but did not inhibit angiotensin- converting enzyme. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

Ovatodioliide and diterpenoids I-IV were strongly cytotoxic to cultured KB cells,ovatodiolide being the most active with IC50, 0.5mg/ml. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

Ovatodiolide concentration-dependently inhibited contractility and Ca2+ current of isolated frog heart and produced transient hypotensive effect in dogs but did not inhibit angiotensin – converting enzyme. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Ovatodiolide inhibited the cytopathic effects of HIV-1 infection (IC50 12mg/ml) but it was cytotoxic to the host cells at 5.0 - 6.0mg/ml. (Shahidul et al., 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 157215g.

Hair growth stimulants involve extracts of Sphenomeris chinensis and those of Anisomeles indica. (Iwabuchi et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 63218w.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine antidote to poisonous bites (Root, Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984); Antipyretic (Whole plant, Bhatt and Sabins, 1987); Astringent, carminative (Whole plant, Sharma et al, 1979); Fever (Whole plant, Jain and De, 1966); Muscle pain (Whole plant, Pal, 1984); Nervous complaints (Whole plant); Refrigerant (Whole plant); Uter infection (Plant oil, Puri, 1983); Veterinary medicine dysentery (Whole plant); Swelling in neck (Leaf, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1981); Wounds (Leaf).
