Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Soybean is a herbaceous annual believed to be originated from Eastern Asia and were used as food long before the existence of written record. The wild form, a slender twining vine, Glicine ussrienssi, considered to be the progenitor of Glicine max occurs in China, Manchuria and Korea. There is no available record as to when Soybean was introduced in India.

Soybean is one of the important crop of the world. U.S.A., U.S.S.R., China, Brazil and Mexico are the important soybean growing countries. The united States of America (U.S.A) have become the world leading producer. Soybean production in India at present is restricted primarily to Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Uttar pradesh and Karnataka. Small acerage of test planting have however, also been grown in other states. It is distributed almost throughout India (3).

Soyabean grows well in warm and moist climate. Adequate rainfall, temperature and high humidity is suitable for soyabean cultivation. It does not tolerate drought and frost. Soyabean grows well at a temperature of 24° -27°C. Soil temperature of 15.5°C or above favour rapid germination of seed and vigorous seedling growth. The crop does not grow well if the temperature falls below 10°C. The annual rainfall of 62.5 -75 cm is ideal for its cultivation. Soyabean is a short day plant and highly photosensitive.

Soyabean can grow in well drained loam, sandyloam and clayloam soils. The soil pH should be 6.5 -7.0. Acidic and alkaline soils inhibit the germinations of seed. The acidic and alkalin soil should be reclaimed by applying lime and gypsum, respectively.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Soybean grows to an height of 0.5 to 2.0 m. Stem is sub errect, climbing and much branched. Number of axillary buds on the well-developed main stem which grow out to form lateral branches depends on cultivators and density of planting, 1-3 laterals usually develop. Buds in axils of cotyledons and primary leaves do not normally develop unless tip damaged. Leaves are trifoliate, long petioled, leaflets ovate, acute 5-10 cm long. Leaves alternate, rarely, petiole long, narrow, cylindrical, stipules small, free, lanceolate, stipels minute, leaflets ovate to lanceolate, usually pale green in colour, variably pubescent, 3-10 x 2-6 cm, base rounded, apex acute or obtuse; lateral leaflets often slightly oblique. Flowers are small, numerous found on short racemes in the leaf axils. Pods borne in clusters on short stalks in groups of 3-15, hairy, pale yellow, grey, tawny or black, slightly curved and usually somewhat compressed, 3-7 X l cm, dehiscent, 1-5-seeded but 2-3-seeded in most cultivars (7).

Flowers small, bracteoles 2, ovate, acute, calyx hairy, persistent, united for half length and with 2 upper and 3 lower lobes, anterior tooth longest, corolla white or lilac, standard about 5 mm long, hardly reflexed, ovate, emarginate, wings narrow, obovate, keel shorter than wings, not fused along upper surface, stamens monadelphous, or vexillary stamen free at base and occasionally entirely free, anthers uniform, globose, ovary hairy, sessile, few-ovuled; style curved, glabrous, with capitate stigma (7).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers are typical papillionaceous in structure with a standard petal, two wing petals and two keel petals combined together covering both gynoceium and androceium. Flowers are either pink or white in colour depending on varieties. Flower buds are formed at the internodal regions of the main axis and side branches (1). Flowers borne on short, clustered, axillary racemes of 3-15 flowers, up to 30 flowers recorded in terminal raceme of determinate types, 20-80 per cent of flowers drop off without forming pods.(7)

Breeding System :
Androceium consists of 10 stamens and Gynoceium with a ovary consisting of 2-4 ovules developing into seeds. Anthesis takes place during early hours of the day between 6 to10 AM. It is highly self-pollinated crop because of the cleistogamous nature of the flower i.e. pollination takes place before the opening of the flowers (1).

Phenology :
Soybean can be grown round the year. July to Sept are the best months for soybean sowing. Flowering in the early varieties starts around 35-40 days after sowing.

Pollinators:
Soya beans are completely self-fertile and are normally self-pollinated. The flowers open early in the morning and the pollen is shed just before or at the time of opening and is shed directly onto the stigma. The flowers are visited by bees and other insects, so that cross-pollination can take place, but this is usually considerably less than 1 per cent.

Seed Biology :
It is propagated through seeds. Seeds are small, hard, glabose, smooth structures with a small hilum, ranging in colour from very light straw through various shades of grey and brown to nearly black, contained 2-3 in the legume or pod. The pods are narrow hairy 2-10 cm long, slightly constricted between the seeds and carried on short stalks. Its diameter varies between 5-10 mm and its weight between 50-400 mg depending on varieties. 45-62.5 kg of seeds are required per hectare depending on the seed size (2).

Seedling Germination :
The seedlings usually appear above ground in 5-7 days. Germination is epigeal, the bent epicotyl first emerging and by straightening and elongating pulls the cotyledons from the soil. Radicle develops into a tap-root, which may extend to 150 cm, but most roots are in top 30-60 cm of soil, nodules when present are small, spherical, sometimes lobed (7).

Cultivation:

Soybean grows well in warm and moist climate. Adequate rainfall, temperature and high humidity is suitable for cultivation. It does not tolerate drought or frost. It is a short day plant and highly photosensitive. A little variation in day length is found to affect flowering significantly. The land should be well pulverised, free from weeds and clods and well levelled. The land should be prepared by ploughing 4-5 times followed by planking after each ploughing. There should be optimum moisture in the field at the time of sowing. Soybean is mainly cultivated as Kharif crop in June-July and is also sown as Rabi in Oct-Nov and Summer crop is sown in Feb-Mar, before sowing seed should be treated with fungicides to protect the crop from seed borne and soil borne diseases. The soybean is cultivated as pure crop, inter crop and mixed crop. The pure crop may be sown by broadcasting the seeds and by seed drill or behind the plough. In case of line sowing, the seeds should be sown in rows. Soyabean has a good scope as intercrop in arhar, cotton and upland rice in Northern India and in sorghum, cotton, sugarcane, arhar and groundnut in southern India. The soyabean is planted with the companion crop in the alternative rows or two rows of soyabean with one row of companion crop. The seed should be sown in lines by seed drill or behind the plough. Mixed cropping is also done with companion crops like maize, jowar, bajra, arhar and millets. The seed is sown after mixing with the seeds of companion crop or without mixing by broad casting method. Planking should be done after sowing to cover the seed and provide better contact of seed with the soil. Soyabean needs adequate soil nutrients. The recommended manurial schedule is 25 kg. N, 80 kg. P205 and 50 kg. K20 per hectare. If the seed is not inoculated with Rhizobium culture, nitrogen at the rate of 50 kg. per hectare should be applied in two split doses, half during the land preparation, and the balance in 30-40 days after sowing. Drought at flowering or before flowering results in premature falling of flowers and pods. So irrigation has to be provided at the critical stages of crop growth (3, 7, 8).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

1.Leaf miner- Aproaerema modicella

Syn: Stomopteryx nerteria Meyrick

 

Symptom: A small dark headed greenish caterpillar mines the leaf and eats the green matter with the result that the leaves become yellow and soon dry up. The grown up caterpillar folds the leaves and feed in them. The attacked plants do not grow properly and give a burnt up appearance.

Control measures: Spraying the crop with anyone of the following insecticides when they just make their appearance can control this pest

Quinalphos-25 Ec (Ekalux, Suquin etc) 1.0 ml per litre of water

Dimethoate-30 Ec (Rogor etc) 1.0 ml per litre of water

Carbaryl-50 per cent WP 2.0 ml per litre of water


2. Stem borer Beetle- Oberea brevis Gahan.

Syn: Melanagromyza Sojae Zehnter

Symptom: A small pale brown longicon beetle whose grub bores into the stem of growing plants. As a result, drying of leaves and withering of plants occur. Adults feed on leaves by making multiple punctures which appears as white spots on leaves.

Control measures: The pest can be controlled by spraying the crop with anyone of the following insecticides

Monocrotophos 36 SL (Nuvacron) 1.5 ml per litre of water

Phosphamidon 85 SL (Dimecron, Sumidon) 0.5 ml per litre of water

Endosulfan 35 EC (Thiodan, Hildan) 2.0 ml per litre of water

Methyl Parathion 50 EC (Metacid, Paratox) 1.0 ml per litre of water


3.Grey weevil- Mylocerus spp

Symptom: A small grey weevil that feeds on leaves and nibbles the leaf margin in the initial stage.

Control measures: this pest can be controlled by dusting the plant with Carbaryl-5 per cent @ 10- 15 kg/ha.


4. Bihar Hairy caterpillar- Spilosoma obliqua Walker

Symptom: Caterpillar feeds gregarously on the foliage and defoliates the crop leading to mortality.

Control measures:

Monocrotophos 36 SL (Nuvacron) 1.5 ml per litre of water

Dimethoate-30 EC (Rogor etc) 1.0 ml per litre of water

Endosulfan 35 EC (Thiodan, Hildan) 2.0 ml per litre of water

Methyl demithion 25 EC (Matassystox) 1.0 ml per litre of water


5. Tobacco caterpillar- Spodoptera litura Fab.

Symptom: The larvae of this insect is voracious feeder and causes severe defoliation. They feed on the surface of leaves and ultimately skeletonise them.

Control measures: Same as that of Bihar Hairy caterpillar.


6. Gram pod borer- Heliothis armigera

Symptom: The caterpillar makes holes in the pod and feed on the ripening seeds. While feeding on the developing seeds, the anterior body portion of the caterpillar remains inside the pod and

posterior half remains exposed.

Control measures: The pest can be effectively controlled by dusting the crop with Carbaryl 10 per cent @ 20 kg/ha or spraying Endosulfan 35 EC (Thiodan, Hildan) 2.0 ml/lit of water or Methyl Parathion 50 EC (Metacid, Paratox) 1.0 ml/lit of water or Manocrotophos 36 SL @ 1.0 ml/ lit of water.


Diseases:

1. Bacterial blight- Pseudomonas syrinage

Symptom: Small reddish brown spots surrounded by water soaked margin with yellow hallo appears on pods. Cool and moist conditions are favourable for this disease.

Control measures: The seed should be treated with Cerasan or Captan @ 3.0 gram/kg before sowing or the crop should be sprayed with Copper Oxychloride 50 per cent WP or Streptocycline (500 ppm) at an interval of 10 days depending on the severity of the disease.


2.Downy mildew- Peronospora spp

Symptom: Small chlorotic spots appear on the upper surface of the leaves which later turn greyish to dark brown with downy growth on the lower surface of leaves.

Control measures: The seed should be treated with Cerasan or Captan @ 3.0 gram/kg before sowing or the crop should be sprayed with Copper Oxychloride 50 per cent WP @ 5.0 gram/lit of water.


3. Dry root rot- Macrophomina phaseoli

Symptom: Small round or irregular black sclerotia appears below the epidermis on the affected stem and root. The plant wilt within a week. Dark sunken lesions are seen on the stem and dry rot symptoms appear on basal stem and main root. In advanced stages, dark sclerotial bodies appear on affected parts.

Control measures: Seed dressing with seed treatment chemicals (Thiram, Captan, Benlate @ 3.0gram/kg of seed), crop rotation and field sanitation including cutting down the diseased plants and burning them and drenching the infested soil with fungicide will help in reducing the inoculum.


4. Leaf spot- Cercospora sojina

Symptom: Numerous typical zonate grey spots with purplish brown margins appear on leaves and other parts of the plant except roots.

Control measures: The seed should be treated with Thiram or Carbendiazem @ 3.0 gram and 2.0 gram/ kg of seed respectively.


5. Yellow mosaic- Virus

Symptom: The affected leaves become yellow accompanied with a slight crinkling and reduction in size. Plants become stunted and set few pods only.

Control measures: Regular spraying of with a mixture of Endosulfan 35 EC @ 2.0 ml and Methyl demithion 25 EC @ 1.0 ml/ lit of water at an interval of 10-15 days keep the white fly population under control and crop become free from the incidence of yellow mosaic virus (3).

Economic Potential and Uses

Seeds furnish one of the worlds most important sources of oil and protein. Unripe seeds are eaten as vegetable and dried seeds eaten whole, split or sprouted. The Soybean contains 18 -25 per cent oil. The oil is used as cooking medium and for manufacturing vanaspati ghee and several other industrial products, such as paints, linoleum, oil cloth, printing inks, soaps, insecticides, disinfectants etc. Lacithin Phospholipids, a by- product of oil industry, are used as a weting and stabilising agent in foods, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, lathers, paints, enamel, plastics, soaps, detergent industries and is valued on account of its outstanding colour retention, both on exposure and on stoving, its ability to stand up for years without cracking or peeling, ease of application and good coverage. It takes a longer time to dry than linseed oil. Soybean oil can be used also in blends with tung, linseed or other drying oils. Soybean Lecithin is the term applied to the total phosphatides of soybean. It is obtained as a by-product of the soybean oil industry. It is a yellow, wax-like material consisting of lecithin, cephalin and inositol phosphatides. It is used as a wetting and stabilizing agent in food, cosmetic, pharmaceutical, leather goods, paint and plastics industries. It is also used in soaps and detergent, special emulsifiers and rubber products (3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12).


Oil cakes:

The soybean oil cake is used for preparation of biscuits, protein rich bread and other confectionary and bakery, high protein live stock feed. Meal of soybean protein is used for manufacturing synthetic fibre, adhesives, textile sizing, water proofing and fire lighting foam and many other uses (3).

Uses

Soybean protein and soybean meal are employed in the manufacture of adhesives, water paints, leather finishes, textile sizes, insulating and wall- board coatings, insecticidal sprays and fire fighting compounds. Soybean meal is used in the manufacture of plywood glue. A synthetic fibre, comparable to commercial casein fibre and suitable for blends with rayon or cotton, has been produced from the protein. The flour is used in bakery and other food products, and as additives and extenders to cereal flour and meat products, and in health foods. The residue left after the extraction of protein is used in the manufacture of phenolic type plastics (11). Processed to give soy milk, a valuable protein supplement in infant feeding which also provides curds and cheese. Soy sauce made from the mature fermented beans, and soy is an ingredient in other sauces. Roasted seeds are used as a coffee substitute. The highly nutritious sprouts are readily consumed in Asia (14).

The fruit, seed, oil and oil cakes are economically useful in one way or other. The immature fruits are used as vegetables. The dried seeds are eaten whole, split or sprouted milk prepared from soybean seeds is used as cow milk and also used for preparation of various products. Soybean is used for making high protein food for the children. Soybean contains 43.2 percent high quality protein. The soybean plant is also used as fodder and for making hay, silage etc. Soybean sauce is made from the mature fermented beans. The straw can be used to make paper, stiffer than that made from wheat straw.

Harvesting Practices
Soybean matures within 90-145 days depending on varieties. The plant is harvested when the leaves turn yellow and finally drops and only the pods remain on the stalk. Delayed harvest results in pre-harvest losses. Harvesting is either done by cutting the plants close to the ground with sickles or pulling the plants. Threshing can be done by beating the dry plants with bamboo sticks or wooden mallets. A good crop may produce 3000-4000 kg seeds per ha both during Kharif and spring. Yield of fodder crop is 15,000-20,000 kg per ha. Average yield of seed is 2000-2500 kg per ha (1).

Processing Techniques

Pretreatment

  • Soybeans are cleaned using combinations of stoners and separators or separators by themselves.

  • Premilling- this operation is designed to break the soybeans into pieces with the minimum yield of 'fines'.

  • Conditioning of the cracked beans can involve indirect heating to raise the temperature of the stock and to effect some drying; alternatively there can be heating with moisture adjustment by the addition of steam or water.

  • The conditioning process also includes the final tempering stage where in the heated moisture adjusted cracked beans are held for a sufficient period to bring about heat moisture equilibrium.

  • For direct extraction, the feed to: the flaking mill is not brought to the temperature normally reached for mechanical expelling, but to 60-70oC. The moisture need not be reduced as for expelling but is acceptable around the 9-11% level.

  • The final process prior to extraction is flaking the cracked, conditioned soybeans. Each extraction process will require a different thickness of flake, the continuous extraction plant operator tolerating a thinner flake than is the case with the batch unit.


Extraction

  • Extraction is by solvent using a batch or continuous process, by pressing using expellers or Screw presses, or by mechanical pressing using hydraulic equipment. Continuous solvent extraction using hexane is the method most commonly employed

  • Extraction with the solvent gives miscella and defatted flakes.

  • The miscella is filtered and desolventized to produce crude soybean oil

  • Crude oil is stored in tanks, washed with water and centrifuged to produce degummed soybean oil and a water emulsion, which after dehydration produces soybean lecithin.

  • Soybean oil is then alkali refined, washed with water, dried under a vacuum, bleached and filtered to produce refined soybean oil or for industrial purposes.

  • The refined soybean oil must be deodorized i.e. treated with steam at elevated temperatures and reduced pressures; before being used for edible purposes

  • The defatted flakes after further treatment yield protein flakes and meal for use as animal feed.

  • If the soybeans are dehulled before conditioning, the defatted treatment yield 50% protein extracted meal or high efficiency feeds and 50% protein flour for edible and industrial uses (2).

Other Uses
The seeds are consumed green, dry or sprouted, whole or split. Green seeds are used as vegetable, roasted and salted seeds are used in cakes and candies. Old Chinese herbals suggest that the soybean was a specific remedy for the proper functioning of the bowels, heart, kidney, liver and stomach. A decoction of the root is said to be astringent. The meal and flour are used to prepare diabetic foods due to the small amount of starch contained therein. Soybean diets are valued for acidosis. Since soybean oil has a high proportion of unsaturated fatty acid, It is recommended like safflower, poppyseed. etc. to combat hypercholesteremia. Natural lecithin derived from soybean, is indicated as a lipotropic agent and has been shown to prevent cirrhosis in baboons (11, 14). Soybean is also used in the antibiotics industry providing a cheap source of nitrogen and oil for the culture medium.
