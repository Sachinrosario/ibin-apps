DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Inflorescence: Dense panicles; Flower: Greenish-white, pedicellate, numerous, bract small, ovate, complete, hermaphrodite, regular ; Calyx: funnel shaped, tubular, irregularly 3-toothed; Corolla: tube cylindric,  lobes oblong, obtuse, sub-equal,lip broadly elliptic, with a pair of subulate glands at the base of claw, sessile, yellow, streaked with purple veins, emarginate Androecium: Stamen 1, perfect, filament free, anther cell diverging at the top, lateral staminode minute; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled,densely pubescent, ovules many in each cell, style filiform, stigma sub-globose; Fruit: Capsules,  orange-red, ; Seed: Globose.

Floral and seed biology:

Flowering & Fruiting: May-August . 

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 48.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:

Galangin and Kaempferide, along with small amounts of Kaempferol and quercetin, and a b-glucoside mixture of b-sitosterol 82.1, stigmasterol 13.1 and caampesterol 4.8% were obtained from the roots of Alpinia officinarum. (Tunmann and Hans, 1972). C.A. 1972, 77, 31555d.

Two new diarylheptanoids – 1,7-diphenylhept-4-en-3-one (I) and 7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxyphenyl)-1-phenylhept-4-en-3-one (II) – isolated along with 1,7-diphenyl-5-hydroxy-3-heptanone and 5-hydroxy-7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone from rhizomes; their structure elucidated. (Itokawa et al, 1981).

7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3,5-heptadione (fig. 1), 5-methoxy-7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone (fig. 2), and  5-hydroxy-7-(4’’-hydroxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone (fig. 3), by the interpretation of spectral data were isolated from the methanol extract of Alpinia officinarum . (Kiuchi et al, 1982).

Isolation of three new diarylheptanoids –7-(4’’-hydroxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-4-hepten-3-one (fig. 4), 5-methoxy-7-(4’’-hydroxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone (fig. 5) and 5-methoxy-1,7-diphenyl-3-heptanone (fig. 6)– from rhizomes. (Itokawa et al, 1985).

New diarylheptanoid-(3R,5R)1-(4’’-hydroxyphenyl)-7-phenylheptane-3,5-diol (I) – isolation from rhizomes. (Uehara et al, 1987).

Roots of Alpinia officinarum Hance were heated and extracted in acetone and solvent is removed by a decompression concentration. The extract is further processed to isolate galangin. Galangin was isolated from the acetone extracts of the roots of Alpinia officinarum . (Park et al, 1995). C.A. 2000, 133, 28543t.

5-cox-2 inhibitors were isolated and characterized as pinocembrin, galangin 3-Metyl ether, galangin, Kaempferid and 5-hydroxy-7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxy phenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone. (Kang et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 1904547m

Two diarylheptanoids i.e. 1,7-diphenyl-5-ol-3-heptanone and 1-phenyl-7-(3’-methoxyl-4’-hydroxyl)phenyl-5-ol-3-heptanone were isolated from rhizomes of Alpinia officinarum  Hance. They were modified by chemical reductions and acetylations and thus six diarylheptanoid derivatives were obtained. (Bu et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 120188d.


Pharmacology:

A flavonoid from rhizomes showed strong antifungal activity against Trichophyton rubrum, T. mentagrophytes, Epidermophyton floccosum responsible for skin diseases. It also showed activity against a number of gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria and pathogenic and nonpathogenic yeasts. (Ray and Majumdar, 1976).

Three new diarylheptanoids, 7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3,5-heptadione, 5-methoxy-7-(4’’-hydroxy-3’’-methoxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone, and  5-hydroxy-7-(4’’-hydroxyphenyl)-1-phenyl-3-heptanone, were isolated as inhibitor of prostaglandin biosynthesis from the methanol extract of Alpinia officinarum  Hance. (Kiruchi et al, 1982).

The rhizomes of Alpinia officinarum contain potent inhibitors against prostaglandin biosynthesizing enzyme (PG synthetase). (Kiuchi et al, 1992).

10 herbal extracts including extrcat of Alpinia officinarum  were used in combination with acyclovir against herpes simplex virus type 1 infection. (Kurokawa et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 334v.

A cosmetic compound having UV-absorbing activity is prepared by mixing one or more of herb extracts in which one of them is Alpinia officinarum . (Lee et al, 1995). C.A 2000, 133, 155142m.

1,7-diphenyl-4-hepten-3-one from Alpinae rhizome exhibited high minimum lethal concentration for the larvae of 0.12mM. (Suzuki et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 127, 85906p.

Methanol aquous extracts of 100 plants were screened for anti-oxidative activity using Fenton’s reagent/ ethyl linoleate system and for free radical scavenging activity using the 1,1-diphenyl-2-picryl hydrazyl free radical generating system. The result suggested that 14 plants out of which one was Alpinia officinarum  may act as the potential source of antioxidant. (Kim et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 26742e.

The content of trace elements in the rhizome, stalks and leaves of Alpinia officinarum  Hance and in plant soils was determined by inductively coupled Plasma spectrometry. The distribution of the most trace element was shown as rhizome < stalks < leaves. (Luo et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 124, 165004d.

A pharmaceutical preparation containing extracts Alpinia officinarum  in combination with other plants was used. (Hozumi et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 13447s.

Alpinia officinarum  have inhibitory effect on the crystallization such as nucleation.Crystal size and Rabit modification of cholesterol. (Saraswathi and Gnanam, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 199882h.

New formulations useful in the prevention and therapy of hair loss and seborrhea are provided which are based on essential oils obtainable through alcoholic distillation of some plants including Alpinia officinarum  which contain the essential oils, combined with salicylic acid with fatty acids having 10 to 14 carbon atoms. An ethanolic distillate containing was prepared by distillation of various plants including Alpinia officinarum . A hair lotion contained above distillate 21, salicylic acid 0.3. lauric acid 0.2, rosemary essential oil 6.9. pentasol PO .79, methanol 1.18. Rhum aromatized alcohol .34 & water q.s 100g. (Varona, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 316427p.

Cosmetics, bath prepreparations and cleansing compounds contain ³½ selected from extract of Alpinia officinarum . The plant extracts are useful for prevention and treatment of dryness of skin, rough skin, chapped skin, dandruff, scalp itching, split hair etc. A lotion containing sorbitol 2,1,3-polyoxyethylene glycol 2, polyethylene glycol, 1, polyoxyethylene oleyl ether 2, EtOH 10,E grandiflorum extract 2, Alpinia officinarum  extract 2, antiseptic and d H2O to 100% was prepared. (Doi et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 22165z.

Any other

Contents of total flavonoids in Alpinia officinarum  in areas of Zharyiang and shantou were higher than in the area of Meizhou, & the contents of total flavanoids in Alpinia officinarum  increases along with the growth period. But the contents of total flavonoids were almost constant in harvesting period. (Luo et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 217868q.

The extract of Alpinia officinarum  were applied to packaging paper for pears. The freshness and nutrient status of pear applied with the Chinese herbs were maintained better than the control group without the herbs. (Mao et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 227930a.

The content, hence the stability of volatile oils and their main components in thee inclusion compounds of A. officinarum volatile oils with beta-cyclodextrin have been examined with different storage times. (Li and Guan, 1990).
