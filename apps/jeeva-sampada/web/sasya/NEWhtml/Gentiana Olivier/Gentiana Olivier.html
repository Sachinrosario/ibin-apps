DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial, glabrous, branched herbs; rootstock stout, thick, completely covered with bases of radical leaves. Branches decumbent or ascending, arising from the caudex or root stock, quadrangular, purplish, 6.8-32.5 cm long, 2-3 mm in diam. at base. Leaves: radical rosulate, narrowly oblong-lanceolate or elliptic-lanceolate, spathulate, 3.0-13.5 x 0.3-1.5 cm, subacute-acute at apex, margins crenulate; coriaceous, purplish on dorsal surface; nerves 1-3, midrib prominent on dorsal surface; cauline linear-oblong, spathulate, 1.0-3.8 x 0.2-0.3 cm, acute at apex, base connate into 5-9  mm long tubes, margins revolute, crenulate; nerves solitary. Flowers blue with white dots, terminal, solitary- subsolitary, often 3-clustered, pedicellate, 5-merous, 2.4-5.5 cm long, 1.0-3.2 cm wide at throat; pedicels 4-lineolate, 0.8-3.0 cm long. Calyx tubular, campanulate; tube 0.7-1.7 cm long; intracalycular membrane distinct; lobes linear-subulate, 0.7-2.0 x 0.2-0.3 cm, acute, margins thin, crenulate; sinuses truncate. Corolla campanulate; tube 1.6-4.7 cm long; lobes ovate, 0.6-1.3 x 0.6-1.0 cm, acute; folds triangular, acute often bifid. Stamens 5; filaments subulate, winged towards base, 1.0-1.5 cm long; anthers oblong, obtuse, yellow. Ovary fusiform, subsessile-stalked, 1.5-2.5 x 0.4-0.5 cm; style attenuate, 2-3 mm long; stigma 2-lobed, papillose. Capsule elliptic-obovoid, stalked, 1.5-3.0 x 0.5-0.7 cm. Seeds numerous, oblong, trigonous; testa close, reticulate.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Axillary clusters; Flower: Small, fragrant, pedicellate; Calyx: Tubular, keeled, lobes 5, unequal; Corolla: Tubular ,funnel shaped corolla with short lobes ;Androecium: Stamens 5, borne in the middle of the tube, filaments linear ; Gynoecium: Ovary superior, 1-celled, with many ovules, style short ; Fruit: Capsule  compressed ; Flowering & Fruiting : April –June.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Oleanolic acid and ursolic acid from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Alkaloids – gentiananine, mp. 375°C decomp., gentianin  and gentianadine, mp. 77°C – isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Gentianine, gentianamine (fig. 2), gentianadine (fig. 1a), gentioflavine (fig. 3), oliverine, mp. 206°C and oliveridine (fig. 1c), mp. 260°C, isolated from aerial parts; oliveridine identified as methoxy derivative of oliverine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Gentiatibetine (fig. 1b) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Gentioflavine, gentianamine and oliveramine (fig. 4) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Isolation of isoorientin and its 4’-O-glucoside and 7-O-glucoside and orientin-2’’-O-glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Ethanolic and aqueous extract of Gentian olivieri and its crude alkaloids were tested for cardiovascular activity in normotensive, pentothal-anaesthetized rats. All three fraction produced decrease in heart rate systolic, diastolic and mean arterial blood pressure, with gentianine being the most effective. (Mansoor et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 90171r.

From the whole plant three new bitter glycosides, olivierosides A (fig. 5), B and C were isolated with known compounds, gentiopicroside, sweroside, 6’-O-b-D-glycogentiopicroside swertiapurimarin, eustomoside, eustomorusside. (Takeda, et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 320244q.

From the chloroform extract of Gentiana olivieri two alkaloids gentianine and gentianadine were isolated which was found to be non-toxic. (Mansoor et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 234340y.


Pharmacology:
G. olivieri significantly prevented the elevation of plasma and hepatic malondialdehyde formation (evidence of lipid peroxidation) as well as enzyme levels (AST and ALT) in acute liver injury, which might be ascribed to their potent hepatoprotective activity. (Aktay et al, 2000).
