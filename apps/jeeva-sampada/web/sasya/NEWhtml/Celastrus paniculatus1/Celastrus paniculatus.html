Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

It is an well-endowed, large decidous woody climbing twiner or shrub, Shoots are rough and speckled with pale lenticels. It grows almost all over India, especially in the hilly areas up to an altitude of 1,800 m.

Distribution: : Widely distributed in India, Burma, Siam, Indo- China, S.China, throughout Malaysia to Australia, New Caledonia (Ding Hou, l.c.). In India it is found along the base of the Himalayas from thr Jhelum eastwards, in the Middle and South Andamans, forest borders of the Western Ghats and in the South it is distributed in the hilly parts of the Konkan region, hills near Alandi (Poona district), the Deccan region, Gujrath and Belgaum district of Karnataka.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

This plant, a large deciduous climber with a stem upto 23 cm diameter, is a rambler by nature. Bark pale brown, rough and cracked, exfoliating in small scales. Leaves alternate, 2.5-4 by 1.5-3 inches, broadly elliptic ovate or obovate, crenate-serrate, rather firm, glabrous, lateral nerves arching, apex acuminate. Petiole 7.5-15 mm long, stipules minute. (1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 14, 15)

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY

Flowers 3.8 mm,green, unisexual, in terminal drooping panicles 5-20 cm long, pedicels pubescent, bracts small and lanceolate. Calyx pubescent outside and 5-fid, lobes semi-orbicular, ciliate. Petals 5, 1/8 inches long, oblong, rounded at the apex. Male flowers: Stamens 5, inserted on the edge of the disk, filaments short, anthers oblong about 1/12 inch long. Rudimentary ovary small, subconical, slightly 3-toothed at the apex. Female flowers: Ovary globose, narrowed into a short stout style, glabrous, stigma large, 3-lobed. Stamens inserted on the edge of the disk which is larger thn the disk in the male flowers, anthers small, without pollen, ovate, acute or subtriangular, about 1/32 inch long, stigma large, 3-lobed, ovules 2 in each cell. Capsules 1-1.3 cm diameter depressed, globose,tri-lobed, bright yellow 3-6 seeded and transversely wrinkled, enclosed in an orange-red aril. (1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 14, 15)

Seed Biology :

The seeds 1-6, often solitary, are ellipsoid or ovoid, yellowish- or reddish-brown in color, striate, enclosed in scarlet aril, which stains yellowish orange. (1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 14, 15)

Seedling Germination :

The best season for germination of Celastrus paniculatus is spring. (10)

Cultivation:

Celastrus paniculatus can be propagated by seeds. But, due it's dormancy direct sowing is not desired without prior treatment. Treatment of the seed with petroleum ether or alcohol increased the germination percentage. GA3 @ 350 mg/l solution also further augmented the germination. (10)

Economic Potential and Uses

From seeds an oil is obtained it is said to be excellent for beri-beri (note: vitamin B1 deficiency disease) and 10-15 drops daily acts as a powerful stimulant, oil is used to increase intelligence. seed oil enriches the blood, cures abdominal complaints, stomachic, tonic, treats cough, asthma,  leprosy, headaches and leucoderma. It is also used in adulterating sesamum oil and as a illuminant. (1, 10, 11)

Parts used :

Leaves, seeds, bark, roots. (10, 14)

Back to Top

 

Uses:

Roots: Root paste heated over an earthen pot and applied for sprains till cure by Paliyars (a tribe in Tamilnadu) The crushed roots are also used for pnemonia.


Seeds: Has the same medicinal properties as the seed oil. The seeds are hot, bitter, dry, acrid, appetizer, emetic expectorant, sodorific, liver tonic, aphrodisiac, stimulant, powerful brain tonic, stimulate intellect and sharpen memory, Cure joint pain, paralysis, rheumatism, weakness; Seeds used externally on foul, indolent ulcers and scabies.

Bark: The bark is abortifacient, depurative and brain tonic.

Leaves: Leaves are emmenagogue, and leaf juice is used in dysentery. It is also reported to be a good antidote for opium poisoning.(1, 10)

Other Uses

Ayurveda: Seeds are also used to increase memory and are used in loss of appetite, fever and rheumatism. Ayurvedic practitioners have used Jyotishmati (Celastrus Paniculatus) widely in psychosomatic disorders. It activates functioning of brain and also strengthens the person to endure the stress, may be emotional or physical. It has been reported to show a depressing effect on the central nervous system and a significant reduction in anxiety. (13, 18)
