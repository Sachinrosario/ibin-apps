Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Corypha has about eight species and they are the largest palms of all. Corypha palms are distributed widely in the tropics, from southern India and Sri Lanka, across Southeast Asia and south to New Guinea and northern Australia. Usually found at low altitudes on open plains or in forest areas subject to monsoonal flooding, they can occur as scattered individuals or as dense stands. In Asia, they are often associated with human settlement and play an important role in providing raw materials for making thatch, baskets, umbrellas, hats, string and many other items. They also provide food in the form of starch from the stem pith, an alcoholic beverage from the fermented sugary sap, edible seed and edible leaf 'cabbage' from pre-emergent leaf.

Natural population of this species exists in the moist forests of Kumpta and Manvar Taluk of North Kanara covering an extensive area near Garsappa Yena river, also on Yallapur Ghat (Blatter 1926). Its present population in the wild is unknown. Semi-wild populations also exists near Kottayam in Kerala in association with Coconut trees. This species is rarely cultivated ornamental palm.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Stem solitary, robust, dark grey in colour, with distinct half rounded leaf scar marks, 10-15 m long, to 90 cm in diam. near base; leaf base persistent from middle to upper part of the stem. Leaves costapalmate, massive; leafbase split into two halves; patiole about 3 m long, 15 cm broad at base; leafblade suborbicular, segments not deeply divided, to 1.9 m from hastula to the of the middle segment; free portion of segments, to 1 m long, to 10 cm broad at base; tip of the free segments obtusely lobed. Inflorescence terminal, about 6 m long, decompound; primary flower branches alternate, horizontal from main axis; ultimate flower branches (rachillae) 12-40 cm long, satin white, smooth. Flowers bisexual, inclusters of 3-6 flowers, each 4 mm long, pedicellate. Calyx obscurely 3-lobed, fleshy. Petals 3.5 mm long, oblong, obtuse, connate at base. Stamens 6, slightly longer than petals; opposite filaments stouter. Ovary contracted into a small pointed style; stigma minute. Fruits pedicellate; two aborative carpels conspicuous at the base. Seed globose, to 2.5 cm in diameter.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Flowers are bisexual, fruits are globose to oblong and the juice contains calcium oxalate crystals. This palm propagates through seeds.

Phenology: Flowwering April to September and Fruiting November to March. The drupe is shining.


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:
Seed Biology :The thin pericarp of drupe on drying becomes wrinkled. Large globose seeds are hardy and polished, basally attached to the drupe. Endosperm is horny with a small central cavity; embryo is apical but somewhat lateral. The seed are erect, albuminous, ruminate, sub-globose or sometimes oblong. The embryo is obconical and not spiral.

Seedling Germination :Corypha is a costal palm of low hills. Seeds germinate readily in about 2 months. Germination is remotive. Sandy soil and plenty of leaf molds are better for the germination of Corypha species. They need good drainage, plenty of water and humid climate.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Ornamental Palm.

Parts Used:

Leaves, Stem, Inflorescence


Harvesting Practices:
About 100 kg of edible starch can be extracted from the core of Talipot palm.
A mature tree also yields about 100 liters of sweet sap per day for several days from the cut end of the inflorescence.
Leaves are used for covering the roof of grain storage, mud huts etc.
The tender leaves are used for making mats, hats, and umbrellas, leaf blades as writing material.
Stem fibers are strong, can be used for making ropes.
Basal section of the stem is made into hollow cylinder for making "Drum" a musical instrument used by the tribal people in some parts of Sri Lanka.
The hard solid endosperm of the seed is Ivory-like and used for making buttons and beads.
Some ancient Hindu and Buddhist scriptures were written on Talipot leaves. The Talipot palm is also the national floral emblem if Sri Lanka.
