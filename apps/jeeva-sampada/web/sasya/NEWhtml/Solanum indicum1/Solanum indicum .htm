HABIT & HABITAT:
 A very common spiny herb or an under shrub up to 1 -8 m. high, found throughout the warmer parts of India, up to an elevation of 1.500 m. Generally it is found along roadsides, deciduous forest, evergreen forest, Forest, forest grounds, Hill slopes, Hills, jute fields, Maize fields, Moist deciduous forest, Mustard fields, Open areas, Open, dry rocky situations, Plains, Potato fields, River bunds, Road sides, Rubbish heaps, Sugar cane fields, Sweet Potato fields, Tea gardens, Vegetable fields and waste fields.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Stem much-branched, spiny, compressed, stout, often decurved; leaves large, sub-entire or pinnatified, ovate, sinuate or lobed; flowers blue in racemes of cyniosc clusters; berries globose, reddish when young or dark-yellow. 8 mm. diam.; seeds smooth or minute pitted, 4 mm. diameter. .

Other Uses
  1. Fruits are considered toxic, laxative and digestive. 
  2. The smoke from burning seeds is said to relieve toothache. In Africa, the plant is used against itch and ringworm. An alcoholic extract of fruits is active against Staphylococcus aiireus and E. coli : the extract of leaves also is active against E. coli 
  3. Root is carminative and expectorant, useful in coughs and catarrhal affections, dvsuria and colic. A decoction of root is prescribed as a ionic and is also used in difficult parturition. Root is pounded and used for the treatment of nasal ulcers. It is one of the ingredients of Dasaniula 
  4. Extracts of the whole plant have been tested for a wide range of biological activities. They influence blood pressure; they also have an effect on human-epidermal carcinoma of the naso-pharynx in tissue culture and on Friend-virus leukaemia (solid) in the mice. The herb is active against Hclininthosporinm sativnm 
  5. Fruits and leaves contain the glycoalkaloid solasonine; the presence of solanine in roots, leaves and fruits has also been reported. Air dried leaves contain 0-32 per cent of total alkaloids. The total alkaloid content of fruit varies from 0-2 to 1-8 per cent (dry wt. basis); plants growing in Jammu & Kashmir bear fruits with high alka loid content (crude glycoalkaloids, 4.8% and total alka- loids, 1-8%). Diosgenin is also present in leaves, stems and fruits of the plant. The utilization of S. indicum fruits as an alternative source of steroidal material for the preparation of cortisone and steroid sex-hormones has been suggested. Fruits contain the carbohydrates, maltase, saccharase and melibiase: a proteolytic enzyme, similar to pancreatic trypsin is also present.
