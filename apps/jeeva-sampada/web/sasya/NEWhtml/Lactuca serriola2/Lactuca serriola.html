HABIT & HABITAT
 Annual or biennial herb. It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh and Kerala near cultivated fields, amidst grasses on exposed dry slopes at 1500-4500 m. Afghanistan, Pakistan, Asia, Tropical Africa, Iran, Australia, Iraq.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem erect, 0.2-1.8 m tall, glabrous, shining and sparsely bristly, leafy, corymbosely or paniculately branched above. Leaves variable, runcinate-pinnatifid, 5.5-13.5 x 0.7-3.5 cm, finely spinulose-toothed, midrib prickly white beneath; lower leaves oblong-obovate, sharply lobed; upper linear-lanceolate, sagittate at base. Heads cylindric, panicled with ascending, corymbose branches, 1.0-1.2 x 0.2-0.3 cm, erect, glabrous; peduncles white, erect, with many appressed green, cordate bracts. Outer involucral bracts ovate to somewhat triangular, 8-12 x 1-2 mm. Ligules yellow. Achenes pale brown, oblanceolate, 6-8 mm long, including beak, flattened, slightly thick towards margin, 6-8 ribbed, smooth. Pappus silvery, white, 4-5 mm, soft, simple.

Uses:
Chemistry:

A new sesquiterpene lactone - 8-deoxylactucin (fig. 1) along with lactucipicrin, lactucin and jacquilenin isolated from rhizomes and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
Linoleic (57.8), oleic (31.9%), palmitic (6.6), stearic (2.0), linlenic (1.3) and myristic (0.3%) acids determined in seed oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
A new guaiane ester isolated from aerial parts and characterized as methyl 2-oxo-6a,15-dihydroxy-8a-(p-hydroxyphenylacetoxy)-guaia-1 (10),3,11-trien-5aH-12(oate) (fig. 2); 11b,13-dihydrolactucin, deacetoxymatricarin and loliolide also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
From the root of Lactuca serriola 7 compounds were isolated. Their structure were elucidated by IR, PMR, CMR, MS, NOE, H-H COSY and C-H COSY. (Makino et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 328035t.

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:  

Medicine for expectorant (Whole plant, Baruah and Sarma, 1987); Jaundice, malaria (Leaf, Baruah and Sarma, 1987); Edible as vegetable (Leaf, Kaul et al, 1985; Koul et al, 1982)

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

Out of 7 compounds one of the compound (11b, 13-dihydroxylactucin acetate) showed a remarkable cyttoxic activity in vitro (ED50=26.4 mol L-1). (Makino et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 328035t.
