HABIT & HABITAT
 A large deciduous tree with a straight cylindrical bole, 30-70 ft. in height and 8-10 ft. in girth. D. pentagyna is found almost throughout India. It occurs along the foot hills of the Himalayas from Oudh eastwards in Nepal, Bihar, Bengal, and Assam and extends southwards to Chota Nagpur, Madhya Pradesh, southern and western India and Andaman Islands. It is found in regions with min. temp., 32°-60°F., max. shade temp., 96°-115°F., and annual rainfall, 30-180 in. Where the annual rainfall is less than 45 in. the growth is stunted. It is common in the sal forests of Bengal and Assam. It is confined mostly to low ground in Chbta Nagpur but occasionally ascends up to 2,000 ft. It is common in the moist-deciduous forest of south India. The tree is a light demander and reproduces freely in burnt savannahs often form pure patches. Saplings stand jungle fires to a remarkable degree. It is sensitive to frost. Its coppicing power varies but as a rule it is good.
It is commonly found in deciduous forest, dense forest, dense tropical humid forests, Dry areas of coastal zone, Dry Forest, Dry Hills, Forests, grass land, Hills, Kannoth Reserve Forest, low hilly regions, moist deciduous forest, open forest, river sides, Sal forests, semi evergreen forest and Tropical Forests,
BioticZone: Chota Nagpur, Konkan plain, Peninsular India, Sub-Himalayan Tracts, West Coast, Western Ghats, Western peninsular India,
Soil: It prefers sandy loam.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Bearing a rounded crown of large leaves, 2-3 ft.long and 0.5-1 ft. broad. Flowers yellow, numer-ous fragrant, c. 1 inch in diam., borne in umbels along thick leafless branches. Fruits orange yellow, 0,5-0.75 inch in diam., succulent and edible.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers 2-2.5 cm across, sepals elliptic, petals ablong, lanceolate, stamens numerous, fruits size of nutmegs with 5 soft capsules enclosed by fleshy sepals, seeds reniform.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used

Uses

  • The buds and fruits are eaten raw or cooked. The fruits are reedily eaten by animals, particularly by deer.
  • The leaves are used as green manure. They contain (air-dry basis) : nitrogen,1.34; potash, 3.20: and phosphoric acid, 0.5.They are also used for thatching and as platters.

Processing Techniques
A method evolved for the preparation of beverage from the fruit
is given below.
For extraction of juice, take 1 kg small, washed segments and 1 litre water, and cook under pressure (10-5 g /mm2 for 25 min). Throw away the excess water. Blend in a waring blender the cooked fruit pieces with 5 litres water and strain through a domestic plastic strainer. The waste material is about 540 g/kg of fruit.
For setting, add half a teaspoonful of gelatin to the filtered juice and keep it overnight.
Then decant or siphon off the clear juice (about 3.75 litre). Now mix 15 g citric acid, 0.05 g ascorbic acid, 0.5 g sodium, 225 g thick jaggery syrup, 350 g sugar, 2 drops pineapple flavour, 8 drops menthol flavour and 1 g cardamom powder. Adjust the total volume to 5 litres with water and bottle it.
At the time of serving, dilute it.The juice is slightly astringent but tasty.

Other Uses

  1. Dry leaves are used for polishing ivory and horn. The bark contains 6% tannin and is said to yield a fibre suitable for cordage (Sahasrabudhe, Bull. Dep. Aqric., Bombay, No. 74, 1933, 16; Badhwar et al., loc. cit.).
  2. The timber seasons more rapidly than most other Timbers. The wood is used for house construction; planking. When quarter-sawn, it is used for almirahs: cupboards and panelling. It is most effective when used in inlays against darker wood. It is easy to peel after boiling treatment but veneers should be dried mechani- cally, not in the open air. It yields a second grade plywood for interior work. The wood is suitable 'for match making and is reported to resemble beech. The wood has a high calorific value (sapwood,5,176 cal., 9,317 B.t.u.; heartwood, 5,176 cal.. 9,315B.t.u.). It is used as firewood in N. Kanara.
