Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A moderate or large branching, commonly found in the dry forests in the foothills of the Western Himalayas, and from Rajasthan to Bihar Southwards through central Indian to Andhra Pardesh. It is very common in the dry rocky hills of central and western Indian in association with Sterculia urens, Odina Wodier and Cochlospermum gossypium. The tree is commonly gregarious and in some forests the tree forms almost pure stands.
A medium to large-sized, deciduous, balsamiferous tree, up to 8 m in height and 2.4 m in girth (normally .5 m )commonly found in the dry forests from Punjab to West Bengal, and in peninsular India. Bark greenish grey, yellow or reddish grey, fairly thick, smooth,exfoliating in thin, papery, resinous inside; leaves imparipinnate, 30-45 cm long; leaflets 2.5-6.3cm X.2-3.0 cm, ovate or ovate-lanceolate, variable; flowers small, white, in axillary racemes or panicles; drupes 2 mm long, trigonous, splsitting along 3 valves; pyrenes compressed, hard, winged.
 

Availability : The tree is common at the foot of the western Himalayas, in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh and further south in the peninsula.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: 

A medium-size deciduous tree with a bole 2-5’ in height, 3-5’in girth. Tree with ash coloured papery bark peeling off in thin flakes, young shoots pubescent, Leaves alternate, imparipinnate, crowded at the ends of branching. receme inflorescence, each branch produces 7.2+-2.3 inflorescence , each inflorescence produces 20-90 flowers. Flowers bracteate, pedicellate, bisexual , actinomorphic, 0 stamens, dithecous, adnate. Ovary superior, trilocular, axile placentation, red annular nectary disc. Fruits trigonous drupes, three-valved; seeds compressed, pendulous. Pyrenes compressed, hard, winged. Two varieties are usually distinguished;-
a)Var.serrata having pubescent leaves with crenate-serrata having pubescent leaves with crenate-serrate margins and 
b)var glabra which has entire, glabrous leaves with entire margins.
The commercially used Olibanum is the solidified oliogum-resin exuded from the tree.A good quality is of golden colour and is transparent. The colour varies from golden yellow to dark brown or to dark greenish brown.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system:
)Obligate outbreeder
2)Emasculation and bagging of the flowers did not result in fruit set thus, ruling out apomixis.
3)Autogamous self and geitonogamous. Self –pollination did not result in fruit–set.
4)Xenogamously cross –pollinated pistils showed normal tube growth through the stylar canal and entered the ovule.
5)Cross-pollinated flowers produced fruits. It is self-compatible spp.

Phenology:
Flowering extents December-March with the peak in last week of February.
Fruiting from March-May. Fruit dehisce and disperse with winged seeds by the third or fourth week of May.

Pollinators:
Oxycetonia versicolor and Mylabris sp. Occasionally visit the flowers, they carried no pollen grains. Apis dorsata and A. indica active foragers most frequent visitors and effective pollinators. 

Floral and seed biology:
Pollen grains are spheroidal or oval in equatorial view, trizonocolporate, rugulose exine.
Pollen grains in male flowers are 26.67+-0.59 micrometer in diameter. Pollen grains are 2-celled at the time of shedding.
Split fruit open along the septa and release winged seeds. Seeds are heart-shaped, dark brown and have a single wing surrounding the seed. Non-endospermous at maturity and each cotyledon is deeply tri-lobed.

Seed germination: Epigeal

Seedling establishment:
Involves development of perennating tubes in the initial years and die back of shoots during winter months. Seedling establishment is very poor.
Seed germination readily in 0-4 days. PGR-treatment with gibberellic acid increased seed germination up to 75%. Seeds viable only for 5 months. The epicotyl and the cotyledons of freshly emerged seedling are pinkish-red. After 2-3 days of exposure to bright Sunlight , their colour changes to dark green.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Anti fungal activity, Anti-bacterial activity.

Produce and products: 

volatile oil, resin and gums, salai guggal, timber (sapwood)
1)The bark is sweet, acrid, cooling and tonic. It is good for vitiated conditions of pitta asthma, dysentery, ulcers, haemorrhoids and skin diseases. The gum-resin is sweet, bitter, astringent, antipyretic, antidysenteric, expectorant, diaphoretic, diuretic, stomachic and emmenagogue. It is useful in fever, diaphoresis, convulsions, dysentery and stomatitis.
2)Oleo-gum-resin is used as incense, fumigant and in Pharmaceutical industry for relieving pain associated with arthritis.
3)Anti-inflammatory and anti-arthritic agent, astringent, stimulant, expectorant, diuretic, diaphoretic and antiseptic. It is useful in ulcers, goiter, cystic fibrosis of the breast, diarrohea,dysentary, piles and skin diseases.
4)Turpentine from B.serrata is found suitable for paints and varnish making.
5)Timber wood is used for second grade plywood.Used in agarbattis (Joss sticks) and for lighting coal stoves.
6)Used in the preparation of an ointment for sores and with butter for treatment of syphilis.
7)The non-phenolic fraction of the Oleo-gum-resin possesses antitumour, sedative and analgesic activity.
8)Among the Gonds of Uttar Pardesh, the stem bark is chewed to relieve toothache and to check pus formation in the mouth. A lukewarm paste of the bark is applied externally to relieve eye-inflammations.
9)In folk medicine, the powered flowers are used to treat colds and fevers.

Miscellaneous Information

The gum is credited with astringent, stimulant, expectorant, diuretic, diaphoretic, emmenagogue, ecbolic and antiseptic properties. It is reported to be useful in ulcers, tumours, goiter, cystic breast, diarrhoea, and dysentery, piles and skin diseases. It is used in the preparation of an ointment for sores, and with oleo-gum-resin possesses anti-tumour, sedative and analgesic activity, and is reported to produce a marked hypotensive effecting anaesthetized dogs. The essential oil from the olibanum showed anti-bacterial activity, whereas the essential oil from the leaves showed anti-fungal activity. 
The defatted extract of the gum exudates (oleo-gum-resin)was found to possess marked anti-inflammatory and anti-arthritic activity against adjuvant arthritis in experimental animals and was free from toxicity or any other side effects. It was also shown to possess marked cholesterol and triglyceride lowering activity. Clinical trials on rheumatic patients have shown promising results. As such, the possible use of Salai guggul as anti-hyperlipidemic and anti-atherosclerotic agent is being investigated at the Regional Research Laboratory, Jammu.

Religious aspects: Tribals particularly from Madhya pradesh plant near the temple and worship. Believed that evil spirit will eliminate if grow near the homes.
