 Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Mild hairy, much branched, annual herb, tall, found in wet places from Kashmir to Assam, and in Madras, ascending up to an altitude of  2,000 m.

Availability : 
Distribution: Himalaya, from Kashmir eastwards, Bengal, Assam, Burma.
Turkestan, Siam, China, Japan, Java & Borneo.
Status :

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Stems robust, grooved, branches hallow. Leaves large, broad ovate : flowers white, bright pink or rose, in  dense spikes :flattened, black, shining, orbicular; stamens 7-8 with 2 styles united for half their length. The ends of the branches on moistening become thickly glutinous. Nutlets 3 mm long round, flattened with rounded margins & rather concave faces, black & shining when ripe.

Other Uses
  1. The herb is used as a tonic and vulnerary. A concentrated infusion of it is reported to be poisonous to fish. The nuts are prescribed for the treatment of tubercular swellings and flatulence.
  2. The leaves yield two flavone glucosides, onentoside MS (mp 259°) and orientin (m.p. 255-57°), which on hydrolysis gave the glycones apigenin and luteohn. The presence of alkaloidal material has also been reported. The rootstocks contain oxymethyl-§ anthraquinone.
  3. The stem of the plant contains tannic acid. phenolic compounds, and saponins. Extracts of the dried stem stimulated respiration and produced a. temporary rise in blood pressure in anaesthetized dogs. They also exert an oxytocic activity on rabbits.
