Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A very large deciduous tree occurs in eastern Himalayas and Assam and Andhra Pradesh up to an altitude of 600 m and also in the low-level forests of the western ghats.


Availability :

Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A very large tree, trunk straight, not branched below, buttressed at the base. Bark smooth, grey, lenticellate. Leaves appearing after the flowers, broadly ovate, acuminate, irregularly serrate or dentate, bright green and (when mature) glabrous above, paler and with softly hairy reticulate venation beneath base cordate. Petioles terete, softly pubescent. Large buttresses.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers numerous, small, yellow.

Male flowers : subsessile, in pubescent panicles. Calyx lobe linear-oblong, subacute, glabrous, exceeding the tube. Stamens much exserted. Filaments very long; anthers small, subglobose.

Female flowers : sessile or nearly so, in pendulous spicate pubescent racemes. Calyx urceolate, pubescent outside; teeth 4, short, triangular, acute. Ovary faintly 8-ribbed, slightly pubescent. Styles much exserted; stigmas club-shaped. Fruit urceolate, truncate, 8-ribbed, glandular. Seeds minute, ellipsoid. Winged seeds.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Natural reproduction is through seeds, which are small and easily carried away by wind. Seeds seem to germinate rather freely and the rate of growth is quite rapid.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Wood reported to be a good fuel. Calorific value: 5,042 cal., 9,063 Btu.

Wood: Wood white or cream-coloured turning pale yellowish-buff or grey on exposure, soft to very soft, light to very light, average weight 320 kg/m3 (20 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; coarse-textured, with usually interlocked grain.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings usually indistinct or wanting. Pores large to very large, clearly visible to the eye, scanty, solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3, open; vessel lines conspicuous on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues vasicentric, visible only under the hand lens as thin sheaths round the pores, rarely with inconspicuous lateral extensions. Rays moderately broad, clearly visible to the eye, rather few and widely-spaced, giving a distinct radial fleck.

Wood used for the purpose not requiring much strength, such as light packing cases, tea boxes, match sticks, temporary structures, ceiling planks and canoes. Also found suitable for second-grade cheap plywood, used for cigar cases. The wood can be tried for production of writing and printing papers and for hard boards. Wood is used for making matches, canoes, etc.
