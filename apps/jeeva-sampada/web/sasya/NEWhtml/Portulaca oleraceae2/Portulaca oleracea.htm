Chemistry:  

Detection of noradrenaline, 4-(2-aminoethyl)pyrocatechol, 3-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl) alanine and catechol by PC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
Oleracins I (fig. 1) and II (5-O-b-cellobiosides of betanidin and isobetanidin respectively) and acylated betacyanins isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Mucilage (0.42%) from leaves; its acidic fraction consisted of galacturonic acid residues joined by a-(1®4)-linkages and neutral fraction composed of arabinose (4®1) and galactose (43%), with traces of rhamnose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
The volatile oil was analysed by GC-MS. Nineteen compounds were separated among which fifteen compounds were identified. The main compounds of the oil are linalool (18.96%), 2-hexadecen (9.01), 3,7,11,15-tetramethyl (13.55%). (Liu et al, 1994). C.A. 1995, 122, 310713s.
The fatty acid profile and beta-carotene content of purslane (Portulaca oleracea) were determined by GC and HPLC. The total fatty acid content ranged from 1.5 to 2.5 mg/g of fresh mass in leaves, 0.6 to 0.9 mg/g in stems and 80 to 170 mg/g in seeds. a-Linolenic acid (C18:3omega3) accounted for around 60% and 40% of the total fatty acid content in leaves and seeds, respectively. Longer-chain omega-3 fatty acids were not detected. The beta-carotene content ranged from 22 to 30 mg/g fresh mass in leaves. These results indicate that Australian purslane varieties are a rich source of alpha-linolenic acid and beta-carotene. (Liu et al, 2000).
The extraction and separation of alkaloids from Portulaca oleracea by continuous extraction are described. The analysis was carried out by UV method. (Wang et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 134105c.

Pharmacology:  

Aqueous extract of Portulaca oleracea leaves extract showed anti-HIV activity (Antoun et al, 1999). MAPA 2000-02-0981.
The 10% ethanolic extract of the aerial parts showed significant anti-inflammatory and analgesic after intraperitoneal and topical but not oral administration when compared with the synthetic drug, diclofenac sodium as the active control. (Chan et al, 2000).
10% ethanolic extract of Portulaca oleracea plant produced restriction of movement in animals during the routine screening studies. Therefore the effects of the extract on the locomotor activity, threshold to noxious stimulus, anti-convulsant activity and relaxant effects on the skeletal muscle were studied. The extract, on intraperitoneal administration, showed a significant reduction in the locomotor activity in mice, anti-nociceptive activity in rats using Tail Flick Method, an increase in the onset time of pentylenetetrazole-induced convulsions in mice and muscle relaxant activity in in vitro and in vivo experiments. The anti-nociceptive activity of the extract in rats was attenuated by naloxone pre-treatment indicating the involvement of opioid receptors in its anti-nociceptive effects. It is indicated from the results of the present study that P. oleracea possesses varied effects on both the central and peripheral nervous system. (Radhakrishnan et al, 2001).

PHARMACOGNOSY:

Pharmacognostic studies of leaves and stems indicated presence of carbohydrates, lipids, glycosides, alkaloids, sterols, triterpenes and flavanoids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Miscellaneous Information
Any Other

Portulaca oleracea showed strong antibacterial effects on Pseudomonas in vitro. (Yu et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 285728j.
The antifungal activity of each fraction of Portulaca olaracea was evaluated based on the dynamic hyphal growth response curves of test fungi Aspergillus and Trichophyton and the yeast Candida, the antifungal activity of each fraction of Poleracea was evaluated based on the dynamic hyphal growth. A crude sample obtained by EtOAc extract showed a specific and marked activity against dermatophytes of genera Trichophyton. (Oh et al, 2000). MAPA 2000-06-3553.
