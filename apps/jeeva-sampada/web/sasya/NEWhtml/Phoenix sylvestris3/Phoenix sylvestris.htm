DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Stem solitary, erect, usually robust, to 18 m long, covered with compact leafbases. Leaves about 5 m long; lower leaves arching; petiole short, flattended, strongly spinous at margins; petiolar spines, to 8 cm long, stiff, sharply pointed, triangular in cross section; leaflets stiff, induplicate, sharply pointed at tips; each, to 80 cm long, each projecting in different planes from the rachis. Inflorescence interfoliar about 40 cm long; prophyll semiwody, spathuliform, scurfy outside, opens by longitudinal split from the middle; peduncle flat, stout; flower branches simple fasciculate, bright orange in colour, to 30 cm long at anthesis. Male flowers white about 6 mm long; unopened male flowers angular, oblique; calyx with 3 prominent points; petals 3, much longer than calyx, deeply furrowed inside. Stamens 6, adnate to petals at base; anthers linear. Female flowers alternately disposed; calyx cupular with three prominent points; petals 3, imbricate. Fruits about 3 cm long, 1.5 cm broad at middle; olive shaped, deep brown when ripe.

Floral and seed biology:
Seed Biology :The seed is oblong 2 cm long with a vertical groove length-wise on one side. The small embryo is lateral and median in the albumen. The groove is incomplete and lies in the centre of the fruit on one side of it. There is a dorsal round depression, the hilum, through which the embryo comes out during germination, which is remotive.
Seedling Germination :Phoenix sylvestris grows in any habitat except alpine, in about 2 months. Moist ground or alluvium, on river banks, banks of streams where water table is about 6' below. They are good for germinating successfully. Leaf mold and rich loamy soil are necessary.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Leaves are used as thatch.


Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:
One of the most useful palms in India. Freshly obtained sap of the wild date palm is clear as water having 12-15% sugar and is a delicious drink rich in vitamin. Application of lime in the receiving pot delays the fermentation of the juice. When the sweet juice is boiled thick syrup is obtained which is consumed in various ways. After sufficient boiling sweet juice condenses into palm jaggery (Gur) which is molded in suitable blocks. It is often more expensive than the crystal sugar made from sugar cane. On an average date palm in one season yields about 40 kg jaggery.

Other uses:
Leaves are crushed and the beaten leaflets are made into brooms, baskets or woven into mats locally known as chattai. Old unproductive palms are cut and pieces of stem are used as fuel for burning tiles etc.
