Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A fast-growing, profusely branched, tall, evergreen shrub, generally 1.2-3.0 m in height, sometimes reaching a height of 6.0m, found in the dry zones of southern, western and central India extending up to Rajasthan in the north; also cultivated in some parts of Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal, and often planted in gardens for ornament and as hedges. 


Availability :

The shrub occurs on roadsides, wastelands, and railway embankments. It is plentiful in the drier districts of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild/ Cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Bark reddish brown, smooth; leaves 7-10 cm, with large, auricled or rotundo-reniform, foliaceous stipules; leaflets 7-9 pairs, with an orange erect, glandbetween each pair of leaflets, slightly aromatic, oblong-obovate, obtuse or emarginated, 1.5-2.5x0.9-1.2 cm; flowers yellow, in compound, terminal, corymbose racemes; pods pale brown, oblong, 5-15cm x 1.2-1.8 cm, flat, papery, flexible seeds, compressed, tapering towards the base 6-12.

Miscellaneous Information

 The bark is astringent and useful for gargles in sore throat, in enemas, rheumatism, and eye diseases. It is recommended in diabetes, forms an ingredient in a compound preparation. Its decoction is given in stomachache, and the juice off fresh bark in dysentery. In Indonesia, a kind of liquor is produced with the bruised bark and molasses. 

The leaves are anthelmintic, and good for ulcers, skin diseases and leprosy; Their infusion possesses slight purgative property. 

The pods are anthelmintic, emetic and useful in urinary discharges. Nonacosane, nonacosane-6-one, chrysophanol, emodin, and rubiadin have been isolated from the shell of the pod. The seeds are considered alexipharmic and refrigerant. They are used in chronic purulent ophthalmic and conjunctivitis, gout, gonorrhea, dysentery, cough and asthma. They are widely used in diabetes; the aqueous extract of seeds exhibited hypoglycemic activity in experimental animals.

The roots are considered astringent, alexeteric and useful in thirst, urinary discharges, skin-diseases and asthma, used as a tonic, show interferon-like activity against Ranikhet Diseases Virus. 

The flowers are astringent and used in throat troubles and urinary disorders. An aqueous extract of the flowers and leaves possesses hypoglycaemic activity.
