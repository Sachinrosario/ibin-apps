Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A small shrub. Hot desert climate. Prefers old alluvial plain. A shrub of desert areas of northwest India and Pakistan. High regenerating and coppicing capacity. It is found in northeast and peninsular India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A thorny shrub, branched near the root. Branches divaricate, slender, zigzag; bark light coloured. Leaves orbicular or ovate, spinous-dentate, clothed beneath with a whitish or buff tomentum, less densely tomentose above; petioles tomentose. Stipular thorns usually in pairs, one straight, sharp and slender, nearly as long as the leaf, the other short, hooked.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in axillary sessile pubescent cymes; buds globose; pedicels short. Calyx pubescent outside, cleft about half way down; lobes triangular-ovate, keeled on the inner face for about half their length. Petals cuneate, rounded or truncate at the apex, longer than the stamens. Filaments deflexed together with the enclosing petals. Disk 10-lobed, with a pit opposite to each lobe. Ovary 2-celled; styles 2, united to above the middle. Drupes globose, glabrous, red when ripe, edible.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Shrub yields very hard heartwood, dark in colour, with high calorific value (7,900 cal.). It thus affords a very good quality fuel and high quality charcoal.

Wood: Wood used for handles of agricultural implements, walking sticks.

Other uses:

Tree has been recorded as a Lac-host from Gujrat and Taimara (Bihar).

Leaves medicinally useful.

Shrub grazed by animals when young and tender; but when the plant is hard, woody and thorny, only sheep and goats are able to browse on it. When berries appear, leaves fall or are collected, dried and stored as pala for feeding cattle, camels, sheep and goats.

These shrubs check wind erosion, help in deposition of soil. Also used for fencing.

Fruits eaten by shepherds and also sold to some extent. In some areas of Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh, fruit is dried and pulp is powdered and stored for subsequent use. Tannin from bark locally used to prepare wine from fruits.

Twigs put along the wind direction in strips and covered with mud or sand – kanabandhana, for soil conservation. Bark - Bark used as tanning material.
