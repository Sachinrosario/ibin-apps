Taxonomic Details

Name : Usnea longissima Ach.
Family : Usneaceae
Synonyms : Usnea longissima var. himalayensis Räsänen
Common names: Eng:Old man beard, Bhotia, Garhwali: Syara, Baiga tribes of M.P.: ‘Jhavila’

Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Usnea longissima is widely known as ‘Old man beard’, a thread like fruticose lichen, very common in the temperate and alpine regions of Himalaya. It grows well in moist places on coniferous trees, where it forms spider web like appearance on the twigs. The long pendulous filaments prefer to grow on dry and decaying woods of tree.

Availability :

Plenty, throughout Himalaya.
Conservation/ Threat Status : very common in alpine – temperate regions of India as well as in the world.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: The filamentous thallus is greyish-green to brownish in colour and sometimes internally black and upto 60 cm long. Basal disc is absent. Branching is filamentous and branches are 0.25-1.0mm in diam. Cortex is mostly evanescent in filamentous branches and surface is represented by pulverulent to whitish powdery mass.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
As spices: The whole thallus is used as spices and a flavouring agent to increase the taste and fragrance of meat, pulse and other important vegetables used by Baiga tribes in Madhya Pradesh.
As stuffing material: The Bhotia tribe and other inhabitants of remote areas of Uttarkashi district, Garhwal Himalaya, use this species as a stuffing material for pillows and cushions.
As fodder: In the east Sayan coniferous mountain forests of Central Asia, Usnea species form the main food of Musk deer (Ustinov, 1969). In Kanchulakharak Musk Deer Breeding Centre, near Kedarnath Musk Deer Sanctuary in Uttranchal, every year during October-November a large amount of Usnea longissima is collected from the coniferous brich forests of Garhwal Himalaya and fed to the Musk deer in the Breeding Centre. Similarly in Arunachal Pradesh, yaks also eat this species as a winter staple food when most of the upper ridges are covered by snow (personal communication)
(Negi H.R. – Tiger Paper XXIII (1): 1996.
Medicinal
Bone Fracture: The species thalli along with some other ingredients are also used to cure bone fracture by the Baiga, a primitive tribe of Chattisgarh in Central India (Brij Lal 1988).
Inhibition of Tyrosinase activity: Natural Usnea longissima (200mg) was powdered in a mortar and then submerged overnight in a solvent (10ml) at 4oC or at 25oC, or extracted for 2 hours at the boiling point of each solvent. As the extraction solvent, water, methanol 50%, ethanol 50%, chloroform, ethyl acetate, benzene or hexane are used. After filtration, the solvent was removed in vacuo and the residue dried to give the extract. The extract of Usnea longissima showed 27.2% tyrosinase inhibition rate.
Inhibition of Gram-positive bacteria: Cultured extract of Usnea longissima exhibit good inhibition of Propionibacterium acnes, Staphylococcus aureus and Bacillus subtilis bacteria while the growth of Botrytis cinerea fungi is slightly inhibited by the extract.

Produce and Products:


Chemistry: Two main chemicals of Usnea longissima are barbatic acid and usnic acid. However a number of chemical strains are also known in the species. In Indian material seven chemical strains are reported
(1) usnic acid only, (2) usnic and barbatic acid (3) usnic, barbatic, barbatolic, and squamatic acid (4 usnic and diffractic acid (5) unsic, evernic acid (6) usnic and fumarprotocetraric acid (7) usnic & squamatic acid
Other chemicals are d-arabitol, isolichenin, lichenin, enzymes, ascorbic acid, riboflavin, calcium, iron, nitrogen and phosphorus.

Miscellaneous Information

Usnea longissima is one of the commonly growing lichen in most of temperate-alpine regions, particularly on coniferous trees, thus can be best utilized in biomonitoring studies of the forest condition of the area.
