DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Rootstock creeping. Flowering stem 15-45 cm high, leafy on the upper portion. Leaves 1-3 usually 2, alternate, long stalked, often purple-spotted, round 15-25 cm diam., deeply divided to the middle or base in to 3-5 lobes, which are sharply toothed and often with deep incisions. Flowers 3.8-5 cm diam., solitary, rarely 2, cup-shaped, white sometimes pink, appearing at the some times as the leaves. Sepals 3, petal like, soon falling off. Petals 6, stamens 6. fruit a large scarlet pulpy berry, 2-5-6-3 cm in length, containing money seeds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Rhizomes and root purgative hepatic stimulant, bile expellant, cholagogue, antidiarrhoeal, bitter tonic, alternative, anti-tumour, emetic, in skin diseases in veterinary medicine, catharlic for dogs and cats.


Chemistry:
Roots gave arytletralin lignans-podophyllotoxin, 4’- demethyl-podophyllotoxin, a and b-pettatin, deroxypodophyllotoxin, podophyllotoxone, iso-pieropodophyllone, 4’-demethyldes-oxypodophyllotoxin, 4’-demethylpodophyllotoxone and 4’-demthyliso picropodophyllone.

Astragalin (kaempferol-3-glucoside) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
Isolation of podophyllotoxin (fig. 1a), deoxypodophyllotoxin (fig. 1b), dehydropodophyllotoxin and picropodophyllin from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Four cytotoxic lignans - (1R,2R,3R)deoxypodophyllotoxin, podophyllotoxin, podophyllotoxin and 4'-demethylpodophyllotoxin - isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Two new arylteralin lignans - 4'-demethylpodophyllotoxone (fig. 2a) and 4'-demethylisopicropodophyllone (fig. 2b) isolated along with a-peltatin, b-peltatin, deoxypodophyllotoxin, isopicropodophyllone and 4'-demethyldesoxypodophyllotoxin; structures of new compounds determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Podophyllotoxin, 4'-demethylpodophyllotoxin, dehydropodophyllotoxin, picropodophyllin, podophyllotoxone and diphyllin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Total synthesis of (-)podophyllotoxin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
A water-soluble glucan, EPS, from the roots of Podophyllum emodi was obtained homogenously as determined by polycrylamide gel electrophoresis and gel filteration. EPS was composed only of D-glucose, and its mean molecular weight was established to be 1.6 x 104. Methylation analysis,periodate oxidation, Smith degradation, KI-I2 reaction,IR, 1H-NMR and 13C-NMR showed that it is n a(1®4)-linked a-D-glucan to which are attached glucosyl side chains at 6-O of the glucosyl residues of the main chain. (Li et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 101738x.

Pharmacology: A dichloromethane extract from Podophyllum hexandrum was found to give an insecticidal activity against larvae of Drosophila melanogaster. From the extract an insecticidal activity compound was isolated by bioassay-guided fractionation. The compound was identified as podophyllotoxin (I) by comparison of its spectroscopic characteristics with literaturedata. In bioassays for insecticidal activity, podophyllotoxin, showed a LC50 value of 0.24 mmol/mL diet against larva of D. melanogastes and LD50 value of 22 mg/ml against ulcer. Acetylpodophyllotoxin, however showed slight insecticidal activity in both assays, indicating that the 4-hydroxyl group was an important function for enhanced activity of podophyllotoxin. (Miyazawa et al, 1999). CA 2000, 132, 20066y.
Podophyllum hexandrum protect mouse jejunum against lethal irradiation. (Salin et al, 2001).
Effects of Podophyllum hexandrum was studied on radiation induced delay of postnatal appearance of reflexes and physiological markers in rats irradiated in utero. (Goel et al, 2002).
Aqueous extract of rhizome of Podophyllum hexandrum (RP-1) has been found to render protection against lethal whole body irradiation (10 Gy), damage to haemopoietic and gastrointestinal tissue etc. in mice. (Samanta and Goel, 2002).
