Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Sunflower is a annual plant with single stem and large inflorescence, which is a native of Southren united states and Mexico, from where it was introduced to Spain before the middle of sixteenth century. Sunflower as an oilseed crop was brought to India in 1969, it is a short duration crop and can be grown throughout the year. Sunflower is distributed in many countries of the world viz., Soviet Union, Argentina, Bulgaria, Pumania, Turkey and South America. Sunflower is distributed in throughout India. Major sunflower growing states in India are karnataka, Maharashtra, Andra pradesh and Punjab. Sunflower can be grown in wide range of conditions. Requires moderately warm climate. Thrives in the tropics at medium and high elevations and under suitable conditions in temperate climates (1).

Sunflower can thrive on a wide range of soils such as sandy loam, black soil and alluvial soil, but it performs better on sandy loam soil than on clay soil under similar management. However, good drainage is more important than soil type. Thus, fertile, well drained, medium textured soils having heavy water holding capacity are best suited for the cultivation of sunflower. Sunflower is highly susceptible to water logging. Avoid cultivation of sunflower on soils affected by salts and with poor drainage. the optimun pH requirementis 6.5 to 8.5. The crop is grown from 450S to 550N but greater production is between 20-500S. Sunflower cannot tolerate frost and requires a frost free period of 120 days. It is resistant to drought but requires continuous availability of soil moisture for the best performance.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Sunflower is characterized by single stem terminating in a capitulum. A very variable, erect, hirsute, annual herb,2.5-7.5 cm in diameter and 0.7-3,5 m tall. Tap-root strong to a depth of 3 m and with a large lateral spread of surface roots. Stems often unbranched, at first round, later thick, angular and woody, abundant xylem, pith often becoming hollow. Leaves are opposite at the base and alternate above the stout of the petioles. Leaf margins are entire, dentate, serrate or lobed, leaves are heart shaped to ovate with cordate bases (4). Lower leaves cordate and opposite, soon becoming ovate and alternate in spiral with two-fifths phyllotaxis, petiole long, decussate, lamina with 3 main veins, sinuate-toothed, hispid with stiff appressed hairs on both sides, 10-30 x 5-20 cm, tip acute or acuminate. Capitulum terminal, 10-40 cm in diameter, sometimes drooping, receptacle flat or dilated and convex, involucral bracts ovate or ovate, lanceolate, acuminate, ciliate, arranged in 3 rows (12).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Sunflower is a cross-pollinated crop with self-incompatability. Inflorescence is called as capitulum or head with bisexual flowers. The head or capitulum, which is the characteristic of the family compositae, consists of two types of florets from the outer whorl of the head. They are sterile without anthers. Outer ray florets neuter, with ligulate elliptic corolla, showy, yellow, strongly 2-nerved, deciduous, about 6 x 2 cm. Disc florets numerous, spirally arranged, hermaphrodite with an inferior ovary, about 2 cm long, subtended by bract, corolla tubular, dilated near base, somewhat hairy, 5-lobed, stamens 5, filaments flattened, free, anthers long, connate, ending in triangular appendage, often dark brown in colour, pubescent, with single basal ovule, style slender, stigma 2-lobed (12). The sepals are modified into two puppus scales. The five petals are united except at the tip to form a corolla tube. The five anthers are fused to form another tube. The style is inside the anther tube and the stigma is bilobed. In the head flower opening starts from outer whorl and proceeds towords the center. Depending upon the head and weather it takes around 5-10 days for completion of anthesis (4).

Breeding System :
Flowers are protandrous, in which the male and female organs mature at different time. So it is essentially cross-pollinated crop, besides showing varying degrees of self-incomatability (4). Honeybees are attracted by fragrance of the flowers and they serve as good source of nectar for bees. Flower opening is between 5-8 am. The pollen remains for 12 hours. The stigma becomes receptive on the following day and continues to be receptive for 2-3 days.

Phenology :
Sunflower unlike most other crops is not season bound. Barring the period of extreme freezing temperatures, the sowing can be adjusted as per the availability of land for planting. However June-July for Kharif, Sep-Oct for Rabi and Jan for Summer are the best months for sunflower growing. Flowering starts around 45 days after sowing (4).

Pollinators:
Sunflower is a cross-pollinated crop. Bees are main agents of pollination. Sowing of this crop should be adjusted in such a way that peak flowering and maturity of the doesn’t coincide with continuous rains, since rains at flowering disturbs the bee activity, causes pollen wash there by affecting the pollination, per cent seed set and ultimately the seed yield. In addition to natural cross-pollination and pollination by honeybees. Hand pollination is an important aspect sunflower seed production to get good seed filling. It is done gently passing the palm either bare or covered with muslin cloth over the pollen heads rubbing the pollen shedding heads with each other (4). The disc florets are protandrous. The pollen is shed in the anther tube, which is partly exserted from corolla. The developing stigma pushes up the pollen mass. Later the stigmas open outwards. The flowers are visited by bees and long-tongued flies for pollen and nectar and they bring about cross- pollination by crawling over the surface of the capitulum (12).

Seed Biology :
Sunflower reproduces by seed, seedlings can emerge from depths of atleast 10 cm. Achenes obovoid, compressed, slightly 4-angled, variable in size and colour, seldom less than 1 cm long, white, cream, brown, black, or white or grey with black stripes. The achene of sunflower, incorrectly called seed, is formed by kernel and a pericarp. Different types of achene can be observed on a flower head.

* Sterile achenes originating from a malformation at the time of flower initiation

* Empty achenes resulting from improper seed set

* Full achenes, containing the kernel of variable size filling more or less the hull.

There are about 1500 achenes per sunflower seed. The size of these seeds varies depending on their position, bigger the periphery, they are also richer in oil. Depending on the varieties, the seed become 7-25 mm long and 4-13 mm wide. The weight of 1000 seeds varies between 30-80 gm (3).

Eight to Ten kg of well filled plump seeds are required to cover one hectare. Seed treatment with brassical or captan at the rate of 3g per kg of seed can be effectively used for controlling seed borne fungal pathogens. The seeds are drilled at a depth of 5 cm by adapting a spacing of 45 cm between rows and 30 cm between plants in the row (1).

Seedling Germination :
For uniform establishment and better germination, seeds are pre-soaked for 12-24 hrs. Seedlings initially taprooted, develops a fibrous root system with maturity. Cotyledons oblong, 15-40 mm long, joined at bases, smooth, first few leaves are opposite, dull green, covered with short bristly hairs that are rough to touch. (5)

Cultivation:

Sunflower is a day neutral plant can be grown successfully in different seasons under varying conditions of day length, provided the temperature is favourable. Sunflower, basically a temperature plant, can also be grown commercially in tropical or sub-tropical conditions. It grows best with clear sky and occasionally rain shower during early stages. This crop requires a cool climate during germination and seedling growth and warm non-cloudy weather and high temperature during flowering to maturity. The crop can be cultivated upto an altitude of 2500 m but highest yield of oil is obtained below 1500m (1). Sunflower requires a fine seedbed, free of weeds and clods, for proper germination and good crop stand. to bury the residues or previous crop, ploughing with disc harrow followed by 2-3 cultivations and plankings may be required. fields vacated by potato may not require much seedbed preparation. However, adequate soil moisture is essential forgetting good germination (14).

At the time of sowing, the soil should be friable and free from weeds. Soil should be moist atleast at a depth of about 10 cm before sowing and this condition necessitates good irrigation before sowing. Use 5kg seed per hectare for hybrids to achieve the required plant stand. Low seed rate recommendation for hybrids is mainly because of smaller seed size and wider spacing for hybrids. Seed should be treated with Brassicol or Thiram @ 2 g per kg of seed to control seed and soil borne fungal disease. Generally two hoeing, 15 days and 30 days after sowing are sufficient for successful cultivation of the crop. A slight earthingup after first interculture is desirable. The crop is most sensitive to miosture stress. The critical stages for irrigation are germination, flowering, capitulum initiation and grain filling. Fertilizers 60:90:60 NPK kg/ha are required for raising a good seed crop. Apply 50 per cent nitrogen and the entire dose of P and K. The remaining quantity of nitrogen fertilizer shall be applied as top dressing when crop is around 30-35 days old (1).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

1. Earhead caterpillars/ capitulum borers (Heliothis armigera)

Symptoms: Head borer or American boll worm has become the noxious polyphagous pest in India and also causes heavy losses in sunflower. Newly hatched larva is green yellow which changes its colour with every instar. The full grown caterpillar is brownish green having grey longitudinal streaks on it. The adults lay eggs on the flower buds. After hatching the larvae feed on developing seeds and make tunnles through them. Bores the head causing severe losses and feeds on foliage.

Control measures: Head borer can be controlled by spraying 2.5 Kg of Sevin 50 WP (Carbaryl) or 2.0 litres of Asataf 75 SP (Acephate) or 2.125 litre Folothion 50 EC (Fenitrothion) or 2.5 litres of Dursban 20 EC (Chlorpyriphos) or Thiodan 35 EC (Endosulfan) or 1.25 litre of Nuvacron 30 SI (Monocrotophous) in 375 litres of water per hectare. Repeat after two weeks if necessary. Endosulfan is comparatively safe for honey bees. Spray when honey bee activity is minimum i.e. early morning or late evening and during the development of flower buds and again after15 days (14).


2.Grasshoppers (Atractomorpha crenulata)

Symptoms: Causes severe defoliation by feeding on leaves thereby affecting the economic yield of the crop

Control measures: Dust BHC 10% @25 kg/ha.

 

3.Parrots: Feed on seeds and make the heads empty and causes extensive damage, particularly when sunflower crop is in grain filling stage.


4. Cut worm (Agrotis spp.)

It is polyphagous pest and cuts off the tender stalks, near ground, of young crop plants during night. During day time the caterpillars remain hidden in the soil. It is more serious during March -April when sunflower follows potato. When flat sowing is practised, apply 5 liters of Dursban/Radar 20 EC (Chlorpyriphos) per hectare before sowing. These insecticides should be mixed in 25 kg fine soil and broadcast uniformly after last ploughing but before planking.Sow crop on ridges to avoid cut worm damage in the early stages (14).


5. Tobacco caterpillar (Spodoptera litura)

The polyphagus pest remain active throughout the year feeding on one or the other crop. The newly hatched caterpillar is pale-greenish with a black head and feeds on the outer epidermis of leaves. It poses a serous threat during April-May and defoliates the plants.


6. Bihar hairy caterpillar (Spilosoma obliqua)

It is also called Bhabbu Kuta. This is highly polyphagous and voraciousfeeder. The nefarious pest causes severe damage and plants looke denuded. Newly hatched larva is bright brown and its body is covered with hair and legs are brown. At the older stage it is much darker and with dark brown hair of almost uniform length. these insects feed gregariously and should be collected along with the plant and buried deep in soil.

For the control of above mentioned pests, spray 1.125 L. Folithion/Sumithion 50 EC (fenotrothin) or 1.225 L. Thiodan 35 EC (endosulfan) or 500 ml Nuvan 100 EC (dichlorvos) in 250 litres of water per hectare. Repeat after two weeks, if necessary (14).


7. Bird watching and scaring the birds in such cases is imperative starting from grain filling till harvest (4).

 

Diseases:

1. Head rot- Rhizopus spp

High humidity and injury to plants favour infection at lower surface of head where it is attached to the stem. Irregular water soaked spots appear, which gradually enlarge and become soft. Shedding back of heads takes place. Cracking of front side is also common. Seed filling is affected severely and seeds remain small and shrivelled. The yield losses range from 20 to 60 percent and oil reduction to the tune of 25-53 percent and increase in free fatty acids, which is undesirable.

To avoid this disease plough the soil with furrow turning plough to bury the debris of previous crop and residues of sunflower; treat the seed with Thiram @ 2g/kg seed; avoid excessive stress of irrigation; grow recommended hybrid and don't grow sunflower repeatedly in the same field; Spray with Indofil M-45 @ 625 g per hectare in 250 litre of water at 15 days interval starting at about 60 days stage.


2. Root rot or charcoal rot - Sclerotium Root rot, Rhizoctonia bataticola, Macrophomina phaseoli

It is soil borne disease that infects the roots. Roots turn brown and rot. Infection at early stages kills the seedlings rapidly. This also causes neck rot i.e. stem rotting immediately behind the head where infected stem turns brownish and darker towards the base. Roots turn grey and stunted. At later stages, it causes premature ripening, smaller and poor filled heads and a lower yield.

For its control, treat the seed with Thiram @ 2 g / Kg seeds or Drenching the soil with either brassical or wet ceresan and avoid moisture stress.


3. Leaf Rust-Puccinia helianthi

Reddish brown pastures appear on the lower surface of the leaves.

It can be controlled by spraying 0.2% Dithane Z-78 two or three times.

 

4. Leaf spot (Alternaria helianthi)

Dark spots with serrated margin and a halo.

Spray Bavistin (o.2%) to check the spread of the disease.


5. Powdery mildew (Erysiphe cichoracearum)

White powdery growth appears on the upper surface of leaves.

Spray 0.2% wettable sulphur or Morestan. (1, 11)

Economic Potential and Uses

Sunflower is mainly grown for extraction of oil. The oil is used for cooking, preparation of vanaspati and in manufacture of soaps and cosmetics. Its oil is used in the manufacturing margarine, shortening. It is especially recommended for heart patients. Edible oil of sunflower is of superior quality because of anticholestrol and non-cholesterol contents and is thus preferred over other oils for cooking food (1,15).

The oil is used as a cooking and salad oil and other edible products, in bakery goods, for packing sardines, and as a base in certain pharmaceuticals. It is one of the few oils that contain little or no linolenic acid and it can be used with advantage in the manufacture of shortenings of any desired con- sistency. It is considered equal to olive oil in nutritive value and is sometimes used as an adulterant. Sunflower seed oil is classed as a semi-drying oil and is employed particularly in blends with linseed and other drying oils in paints and varnishes. It is valued for the non-yellowing and the heat-resisting properties of films produced. A blend containing 30-40% sunflower seed oil and 60-70% linseed oil possesses satisfactory drying properties and gives films which are softer, glossier and more elastic than those of linseed oil alone. Sunflower seed oil is used as lubricant, for lighting purposes and treatment of shoddy in woollen manufacture. It has potential value as fuel in diesel motors. It is a fairly efficient foam destroyer and possesses bactericidal action against many bacteria. Sulphonated sunflower seed oil is suitable for the manufacture of high co-efficient liquid disinfectants (16, 17, 18).

- Sunflower meal in the form of cake is used for animal feed or manuring field.

- Hulls and stalks are used for the preparation of furfural.

- Dry stalks are used as fuel.

- Sunflower husk finds use in the preparation of ethyl alcohol and yeast.
- Seed is included in many commercial bird feeds.

Harvesting Practices
The crop matures in 90-100 days. The crop is ready to harvest when the top leaves are yellowing, drying and withering. However, the best time for harvest is when the back of earhead turns to lemon yellow colour and some of bract dryup. The mature heads are cut with sickle and are dried under sun. The dried heads are threshed by hand, by rubbing seed heads on a metal sheet or beating with sticks. The threshed produce shall be winnowed to obtain clean seed and are further dried under sun on a tarpaulin preferably during morning hours to bring down the initial moisture content to around 9 per cent (4).

Processing Techniques

Pretreatment

  • Threshed seeds are dried naturally or artificially.

  • Seeds dehulled usually in a machine combining a decorticator and separator. 2 types of

decorticator (i) Percussion- splitting by high speed impact,

(ii) Friction- rubbing seed between two furrowed discs.


Extraction of oil from sunflower seeds or kernels can be done using general equipments and operating conditions used for other oilseeds. The extraction of oil from sunflower is done by mechanical extraction, prepress solvent extraction and direct solvent extraction methods. Cleaning, drying and dehulling prior to extraction are the common steps in all the methods (17, 18).

Mechanical Extraction: In mechanical screw press or expeller method, the kernels or seeds are crushed or rolled and cooked. Cooking facilitates the disruption of oil-bearing tissues and also inactivates phospholipases and lipases, thereby reducing the concentration of non-hydratable phosphatides and minimizing the acid value.

Prepress Solvent Extraction: This is the most common method for sunflower oil extraction. In this method the kernels are screw-pressed to obtain cake, with about 16% oil content, as in the mechanical extraction method. The cake obtained is granulated or flaked and prepressed in a screw press or an expeller in single or two stages. In the two-stage press, the first stage has a shallow pitch. The second stage has a sharper pitch and operates at higher temperatures and pressures than the first stage. The pressed cake is granulated prior to solvent extraction. The solvent extractor may be of horizontal or vertical type. The oil cake may be moving or remain stationary. The solvent and the flaked oil cake move in counter flow. Hexane is widely used because of its low cost and low toxicity than the other solvents.

Direct Solvent Extraction: In this method, kernels are conditioned, flaked and oil is extracted directly instead of expelling or screw pressing and then flaking prior to oil extraction. The resultant meal contains about 2.5 to 3.5% oil.

Miscellaneous Information

Researches show that sunflower depresses the growth and productivity of succeeding crops. The reason postulated are the allelochemicals being secreted by sunflower crop. Decomposing residues of sunflower could also bring about allelopathic effects. Phytotoxic compounds released into the soil rhizosphere have been observed to cause varying degree of allelopathy/autotoxicity in crop. Field studies conducted in the department of Agronomy, Punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana on the allelopathic effect of spring sunflower on succeeding Kharif crops revealed that maize, sorghum, cowpea, pearlmillet, pigeonpea, mungbean and sesamum suffered 41.7, 36.7, 27.6, 28.8, 23.0, 10.8 and 9 percent reduction in yield, respectively, as compared to these when grown after wheat. Studies in allelopathic effects of different plant parts of sunflower indicated that sunflower roots reduced pigeonpea, cotton and mungbean yields the most followed by roots plus shoots treatment. Sunflower shoots did not have any harmful effect on growth of these crops.

Bioassay studies involving use of aqueous sunflower extract, decomposing plant residue and soil from area in proximity of sunflower crop revealed inhibition of gemination and growth of cotton, pigeonpea, sunflower and mungbean. These studies further showed that effect of leaf extract/residue was more than roots. The allelopathic effect of sunflower plant residue persisted upto 24 days after incorporation and beyond this period the effect was rather meagre.

Chromatograhic and physico-chemical analysis of allelochemicals in sunflower parts showed the presence of chlorogenic and isochlorogenic acids in both stems and leaves besides these some unidentified phytotoxins were also observed. The concentration of these was more in leaves than in stems. Further studies revealed that total phenolics was more in leaves followed by in roots and shoots. Sunflower raised under moisture stress conditions had higher phenolics as compared to the raised without any moisture stress.


Sunflower contains polyphenols, chlorogenic acid and caffeic acid. These cause development of colour in the flour at alkaline pH and also lower the nutritive value of the protein. Many solvent systems have been fused to remove the polyphenols from sunflower seeds. Solvents such as water (adjusted to various pH values), solutions of NaCl, MgCl2 and CaCl2, methanol, ethanol, acetone, isopropanol and butanol either alone for as aqueous mixtures have been used. However, none of the methods completely removes the polyphenols in a single extraction and repeated extractions are necessary. Such solvent extractions lead to changes in the protein composition and functional properties of the flour.

Since the hull fraction leads to high crude fibre content of the flour, it is essential to dehull sunflower seeds before defatting. Hulls form nearly 30 per cent of the seed material. However, dehulling and removal of polyphenols do not appear to have been tried on a large scale (20).
