DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Perennial, climbing shrub with milky latex; branches 3-5-angular; roots fleshy; stems brown when old. Leaves ovate or oblong, 41-5 x 1-14 cm, slightly lobulate, subacute or mucronate at apex, cordate or truncate at base, pubescent on both surfaces when young. Flowers white or pale green, in racemose cyme; bracts large, caducous, pinkish; outer 2 sepals enlarged in fruits, 3 inner sepals smaller. Capsules globose, 8-15 mm in diam., enclosed by enlarged sepals, glabrous. Seeds black, glabrous with pitted sides.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: In Ayurveda system of medicine the root is bitter. It has heating, anthelmintic and antipyretic properties. It is used in ascites, leucoderma, Itch, ulcers, constipation, abdominal troubles, anaemia, fevers, piles, tumours, jaundice, opthalmia and insect bite. It is also good in liver, heart and eye diseases. The black variety is used in loss of consciousness, burning sensation. White variety is used in bilious fever, inflammation and abdominal diseases. Red variety is used in “Kapha”. In Unani system of medicine the root is used in biliousness, body tomours, brain diseases, paralysis, muscle pain, bronchitis, pain in the joint and chest. It also removes bad humours.

Chemistry:  

Scopoletin isolated; glucose, rhamnose and fructose obtained by acid and alkaline hydrolysis of resin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Turpethinic acids A (fig. 1a), B (fig. 1b), C (fig. 2a), D (fig. 2b) and E (fig. 3) isolated from resin; all of them contained same sugar moiety identified as O-b-D-gluco- pyranosyl(l®3)-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®3)-O-b- D-glucopyranosyl(l®3)-O-b-D-glucopyranoside; aglycone of turpethinic acid A identified as 3,12-dihydroxypentadecanoic acid, B 4,12-dihydroxypentadecanoic acid, C as 3,12-dihydroxyhexadecanoic acid, D as 4,12- dihydroxyhexadecanoic acid and E as 11-hydroxyhexadecanoic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Betulin, lupeol and b-sitosterol isolated from stem. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).
