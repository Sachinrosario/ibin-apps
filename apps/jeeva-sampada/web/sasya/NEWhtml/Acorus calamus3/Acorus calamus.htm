Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT


An aromatic, erect, marsh herb; root-stock thick, creeping. Leaves in 2 rows, tufted, 1.5-3 ft. Long. Spathe leaf-like, 6-30in long, not enclosing the spadix. Spadis sesile, 2-4 long, cylindric, dense-flowered. Flowers bisexual, yellow-green; perianth of 6 segments. Berries yellow-green, angular, 1-3 seeded.

Sweet flag is a perennial plant native to India, which has achieved Worldwide distribution because of its high medical value. Swamps and still waters are its typical habitats where it can develop considerable stands. It will grow in situations up to 2000 meters. At these elevations it develops its greatest essential oil content. 

A semi-aquatic, perennial, aromatic herb with creeping rhizomes, growing wild and also cultivated throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 2,200 m in the Himalayas. Rhizome horizontal jointed. Some what vertically compressed, spongy within 1.25-2.5 cm in thickness, pale to dark brown or occasionally orange-brown in colour; leaves grass like or sword shaped, long and slender; Flowers small, yellow-green in a spadix berries green angular, 1-3 seeded; seeds oblong.

Availability: Plenty Kashmir, Sirmaues, Manipur, Nagahills, Koratagere Taluk in Karanataka. It is thrives best in marshy and moist place. Grows wild throughout India up to an altitude of 2,200 m in the Himalayas; also cultivated throughout India and Pakistan in marshes and on river Banks; Wild or cultivated up to 8,000 ft. On the Himalayas.

Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
They are source of calamus oil, which is an essential oil containing assarone and its isomer. 

Insecticidal, Repellent, Antifeedant, Antifertile,

Callasobruchus chinensis. Spodoptera litura, Trogoderma ramarium, Pieris brassicae, Rhizopertha dominica, Ceratitis capitata,Dacuscucurbitae, Cacus dorsalis, Sitophilus oryzae

Powdered rhizomes are used; effective in killing pests of stored Grains (Yadava et al., 1971:Pandey et al., 1977: Teotia and Pandey 1979); shows no residual effect ( Mukherjee and Govind, 1960). The growth rate of Peridroma saucia is reduced by topical application (Koul, 1995).

The rhizomes yield calamus oil (1.5- 3.5% dry wt) and the important constituent is asarone (up to 82%) and its B-isomer. Asarone (2,4,5-trimethoxypropenylbenzene) is a mild sedative and a potent tranquiliser. It is a central nervous system depressant(WOI, 1985).

Parts Used:  
Root stock used as an insecticide. Sweet flag as an insecticide can be used both in field and store. Most of the available information concerns the latter. Practitioners interested in experimenting could contribute a great deal to enhance its practical use.

Harvesting Practices: Rhizomes are harvested from matured plants. Sweet flag is easy to cultivate. Due to its site requirements it does not compete with other crops. In India over 4000 kg of dried rhizomes per hectare can be harvested annually. Even though its cultivation is easy and the possibilities as insecticidal plant in field and store are promising its practical importance is still minimal. 

Produce and Products:

Chemistry: β-asarone, pentobarbitone, hexobarbitone.

 Processing Techniques: Rhizome powder and essential oil are used.

Other uses: 
Published results about the use of sweet flag to protect crops in store are mostly based on scientific work.. 

Rhizomes of sweet flag are used in two ways:
1. Powder
2. Essential oil

Before grain is stored it should be well mixed with dried, powdered sweet flag rhizome at the rate of 50 kg rice: 1 kg sweet flag. Another source recommends a rate of 100 kg rice:1 kg sweet flag rhizome.

The effect of the essential oils of sweet flag rhizome on the dzukibean 
beetle (Callosobruchus chinensis) has been investigated by YADAVA with the following results:

5 different formulations were tested with reference to knock-
down effect (50 % and 100 %) and mortality (50 % and 100 %). The essential oils were obtained from the rhizomes by steam distillation.

Chickpeas were thoroughly coated with each of the above 
formulations. About 250 gm of so treated seeds were then put into jars. Into each were then introduced 50 adult weevils.

All formulations were found to be effective. None of the weevils 
survived and the seed was not affected. Increased concentrations brought about an earlier onset of the knockdown effect and a quicker death, particularly in the case of kerosene preparations. The protection appears to last long when the treated seed is not exposed to sunlight.

Miscellaneous Information

Asarone is a mild sedative apotent tranquillizer, and like ‘chlorpromazine’ a mild hypotensive and hypothermic substance. When administered intraperitoneally it behaves like a CNS depressant, producing no side effects. A dose of 0.0275 ml/100 g of asarone is lethalto guineapigs. It also ingibits the growth of Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

The plant has many medicinal uses.Anthelmintic, aphrodisiac, aromatic, astringent, carminative, diuretic, emetic, nervine tonic, purgative, stimulant stomachic, and tranquilliser.

Useful in abdominol distension, colic arising in stricture, dysentery, epilepsy, hysteria and insanity.

Traditionally used for microbial toxaemia. Asarone isolated from the drug has shown tranquillising activity similar to reserpine.
