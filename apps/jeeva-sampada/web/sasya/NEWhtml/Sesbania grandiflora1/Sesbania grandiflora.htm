Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A small tree, native of Malaysia. It is a plant of high rainfall areas of western ghats, West Bengal and Assam (wet tropical) and medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical) areas. Grown in many parts of India such as Punjab, Delhi, Bengal, Assam and the Andamans.

Plant adapted to altitudes up to 800 m with no frost. Grows on a wide range of soils, including poor, sandy, black clay, acidic and alkaline, using polluted water. The plant grows best on black-cotton soil. It is drought-resistant and quick-growing. It is an extremely fast-growing, light-demanding and frost sensitive species. It grows best where annual rainfall is more than 1,000 mm and there are only a few months of dry season.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A soft-wooded, small, evergreen tree reaching 20-30 ft. high. It has a straight, cylindrical and deeply furrowed bole and a light, spreading crown of feathery leaves.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers large, white or red. Flowers are 2-3 inches long and very showy. Pods often reach a foot long, each containing about 40 seeds.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowers at various times. It flowers profusely from September to December and fruits during hot season. Seeds ripen in April - May.

Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Plant is propagated by direct sowing or entire transplanting. It can be propagated by cuttings also. No seed treatment is needed. Rhizobium inoculation is beneficial. Clean seeds germinate readily without pretreatment. Seeds are sown in June-July. Polybag planting gives the best results.

 

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Colletotrichum capsici (syd.) Butler & Bisby - seedling blight

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:Wood used as fuel. Charcoal from the wood is used for gunpowder. Wood can be used as fuel within a year of sowing, but is not preferred because of its very light quality.

Wood: Agati wood is white, soft and not durable, weighing about 512 kg./ cu. m.

It is valued for making toys. In Bengal, the tree is grown for posts to support the roofs of houses and in Deccan as a substitute for bamboo.

Provides pulp. It makes a good semi-chemical process pulp, that can be blended up to 20 per cent with long-fibre pulp to make cheap papers.

Other uses:

The tender leaves, flowers and pods are eaten. Seeds taste like soybean and are rich in protein.

Tender shoots and leaves are relished by live stock and poultry.

Inner bark yields a good fibre. Average length of the fibre is 2.7 mm. The fibre is used for cords.

Tree yields gum.

Bark - Juice of bark is used by fishermen for toughening their nets. It is also used for tanning purposes and for colouring matting. Bark contains gum. The red gum is said to resemble Bengal kino.

Flowers - Flowers are sacred and are valued as offering in the month of Kartik. Flowers are edible. Juice of flowers is employed in the problems of vision.

Roots, bark, gum, leaves and flowers are used in native medicine.
Bark & leaves are medicinally useful.
Fruits are medicinally important.
Leaves have diuretic and laxative properties.

Miscellaneous Information

Grown as a support for pepper and betel vines, as shade plant for coconut seedlings and as wind-break in banana fields.

It can be used for afforestation of eroded lands and overgrazed grassy wastelands in the tropics. Plantations along the roadsides and fence lines and field boundaries beautify the landscape, and also serve as a shelterbelt because of feathery leaves and spreading crowns.

Cultivated ornamental plant with large red or white flowers.
Tree is a nitrogen fixer.
The plants have been micropropagated. In vitro plantlets have been obtained.
