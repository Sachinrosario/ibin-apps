Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT


A shrub, near salt-marshes, but not common. Trees are suited to coastal and estuarine zones. Occurs in salt marshes of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Orissa, West Bengal and in the Andamans.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A shrub; young branches with scars of fallen leaves and stipules, slender. Bark smooth, grey, with few, obscure lenticels. Leaves 3-4 in number by 1-1.5 inchs in size., oblong-lanceolate or elliptic-lanceolate, acute, shining above, dull beneath, glabrous, base cuneate; petioles ½-3/4 in long; stipules 1-2 in long.

Flowers : 2-5-flowered erect cymes, longer than the petioles; pedicels slender. Calyx tube cylindric or subfusiform, ribbed; lobes 8, about one-fourth the length of the tube, lanceolate, acute, erect. Petals as many as the calyx-lobes but shorter than them broadly oblong, 2-lobed, the lobes rounded and bearing short white hairs at the apex. Stamens twice as many as the petals, 2 opposite to each petal and embraced by it; filaments of each pair unequal. Ovary 3-celled. Fruit concealed by the enlarged calyx; protruding radicle cylindric, striate, truncate, reaching 4-5 in. before falling.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as firewood by coastal people of tropical and sub-tropical countries. Wood burns leaving little ash. Charcoal burns steadily, giving off intense heat without sparking.

Wood: The wood is grey brown and easily perishable under exposed conditions.

It is used for poles, for fishing-stakes and fish-traps.

Wood tar is sometimes prepared from the timber.

Other uses:

Leaves - Leaves contain 12 per cent tannin and are suitable for light leathers. They are also rich in carbohydrates, lipids and proteins and can be used as food for commercially important fishes and prawns. Also used as vegetable.
