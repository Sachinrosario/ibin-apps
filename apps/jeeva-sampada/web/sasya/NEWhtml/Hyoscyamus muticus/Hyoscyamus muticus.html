Chemistry:
Four new compounds – 15-hydroxyheneicosanoic acid, 3-hydroxytetratriacontan-30one, tritriacontan-3-one and pentatriacontan-3-one – isolated from leaves and stems along with tritriacontane and sitosterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A new compound – hentriacontanyl dotriacontanoate – along with tritriacontanoic acid isolated from leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

A capillary zone electrophoretic method (CZE) was developed using an uncoated fused silica capillary for the preparation and determination of the main tropane alkaloids. The applicability of the developed method for analysis of plant samples was examined by analyzing samples of transgenic Egyptian henbaneHyoscyamus muticus (L.) plants. A simple 40mM phosphate buffer at pH 7.8 using a voltageof 20 kV was found best for this purpose. The main tropane alkaloids,atropine and scopolamine as well as nor-(-)-scopolamine, and tropic aid, the precursor of tropane alkaloids, could be separated in less than 13 min. The linear concentration range for atropine was 5.00 – 140 mg ml (-1), for scopolamine 7.50 – 210 mg ml(-1) and for tropic acid 2.50 – 70.0 mg ml (-1). (Eeva et al, 1998).


Pharmacology:
Atropine showed tranquillising properties in low doses and thymoleptic activity in high doses, whereas scopolamine showed thymoleptic effects especially in higher doses. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Scopolamine hydrobromide (0.5 mg/kg, i.p.) increased the activity of mice, but did not interfere with either learning or retention of the passive avoidance response. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Atropine sulphate inhibited insulin release induced by oral but not by i.v. glucose in human subjects. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
