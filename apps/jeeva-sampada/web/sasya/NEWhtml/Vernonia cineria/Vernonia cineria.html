Chemistry:

·        b-amyrin acetate, b-amyrin benzoate, lupeol and its b-sitosterol, stigmasterol and a-spinasterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Luteolin, its 7-O-glucoside, isoorientin and chrysoeriol isolated from fresh flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·        a- and b- amyrins, their acetate, d-amyrin acetate and 3b-acetoxyurs-13(18)-ene isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·        Isolation of a new triterpene – 24-hydroxytaraxer-14-ene (fig. 1) – from roots together with campesterol and a-spinasterol; new compound characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Isolation and structure determination of a new triterpenoid – 3b-acetoxyurs-19-ene – from root; lupeol acetate also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Lupeol palmitate, a-amyrin palmitate, ditriacontanoic acid and stigmasterol-beta-D-lucopyranoside were isolated and identified from aerial parts of Vernonia cinerea. (Tandon and Shukla, 1995). MAPA 9505-3320.

Pharmacology:

·        The chloroform extract of Vernonia cinerea leaf induced significant diuresis, while the methanol and aqueous extracts induced significant antidiuresis in rats. The trend was a significant increase (in the case of diuresis) in natriuretic and kaliuretic activity and a significant decrease (in the case of antidiuresis) in natriursis and kaliuresis. (Adeboye et al., 1997). MAPA 9802-0770.

·        Administration of Vernonia cineria flower extract to rats was found to modulate most of the altered parameters in arthritic rats. Significant changes in the levels of lipid peroxide in plasma and spleen (P less than 0.001) was observed. The levels of antioxidant enzymes were significantly altered in haemolysate. The reduced glutathione (GSH) in blood increased to normal level. (Latha et al., 1998). MAPA 9904-2415.

·        The water soluble fraction of the methanol extract of the defatted dried ground whole plant of Vernonia cinerea shows significant diuretic activity in rats comparable to Lasix (Furosemide, U.S.P.), a known diuretic. (Herrera et al., 1998). MAPA 9905-3062.

·        The anti-inflammatory effect of an alcoholic extract from the flower of Vernonia cinerea was tested in adjuvant arthritic rats. Changes in paw volume, body and tissue weights and serum and tissue enzyme activities of ALT, AST, ACP, and cathepsin-D in adjuvant rats were reversed by oral administration of 100mg/kg body weight of the flower extract. The extract also reversed the major histopathological changes in the hind paws of the arthritic rats. Phytochemical studies revealed the presence of alkaloids, saponins, steroids and flavonoids. (Latha et al., 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 270266j.

·        Luteolin, luteolin 7-O-glucoside, luteolin 4’-O-glucoside chlorogenic acid and 3,5-dicaffeoylquinic acid which were effective inhibitors of the oxidative burst of activated polymorphonuclear leucocytes have been suggested to be important in the anti-inflammatory activity traditionally claimed for Vernonia cinera. (Abeysekera et al., 1999). MAPA 9906-3713.

 TOXICOLOGY

 ·         Four compounds, stigmasterol, stigmasterol-beta-D-glucopyranoside,luteolin 7-O-glucopyranoside and dotriacontanoic acid isolated from Vernonia cinerea shows significant antifeedant activity against two lepidopterous insects, Spodoptera litura and Spilosoma obliqua. (Tandon et al., 1998). MAPA 9903-1931, CA. 1998,129, 105488a.

·         Four compounds, stigmasterol, stigmasterol-beta-D-glucopyranoside, luteolin-7-O-glucopyranoside and dotriacontanoic acid isolated from Vernonia cinerea have shown significant antifeedant activity based on percent feeding-deterrence and effective doses (ED50) against two lepidopterous insects, Spodoptera litura and Spilosoma oblique. (Tandon et. al., 1998).

 

Ethnobotany:
 Medicine for cholera, dysentery (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Constipation (Leaf, Singh et al, 1987); Fever (Whole plant, Bedi, 1978); Impotency, lactation (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); leucorrhoea (Root, Saxena and Vyas, 1983); Malaria (Leaf, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1981); Night blind (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Piles (Whole plant, Rajwar, 1983); Skin disease, thread worms (Seed, Bhall et al, 1982); Spleen complaints (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Wounds (Whole plant, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973; Sharma et al, 1979)
