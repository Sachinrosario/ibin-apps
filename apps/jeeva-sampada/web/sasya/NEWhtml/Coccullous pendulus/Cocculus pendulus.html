REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Axillary panicles; Flower: Regular, complete ; Calyx: Sepals 6, 2-seriate, acute ; Corolla:  Petals 6, smaller, auricled; Male Flower: Bracteate, minute, stamens embraced by petals, anthers subglobose, cells bursting transversaly ; Gynoecium: Ovary 3, styles usually cylindric ; Fruit:Drupe, black- purple ; Seed: Curved .

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting: September- November. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.  
Genetic variability:

Chemistry:
Coclaurine, menisarine, sinactine, a trihydroxy compound, b- and g-sitosterols and a mixture of fatty acids were isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

A new bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloid – cocsulin, m.p. 272°C – isolated and structure assigned. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A new biscoclaurine alkaloid – pendulin,(fig. 2) m.p. 192°C – isolated and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structure and stereochemistry of cocsolin(fig. 1a) isolated from leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Cocsulinin and pendulinin together with cocsulin(fig. 1b) and cocsolin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Choline, hentiacontanol and b-sitosterol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Structure, configuration and synthesis of (+)ophiocarpinone, (fig. 3)isolated from stem and leaves, described. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Eight new bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloids – (+)kohatamine(fig. 4a), (+)1,2-dehydrokohatine,(fig. 5a) (+)1,2-dehydrokohatamine,(fig. 5b) (+)5’-hydroxyapateline( fig. 4b), (+)5’-hydroxytelobine,(fig. 4c) (+)1,2-dehydr-2’-nortelobine,(fig. 5c) (+)siddiquine (fig. 6a)and (+)siddiquamine(fig. 6b) isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Six new alkaloids –Cocsiline,(fig. 7a) N-norcocsulinin,(fig. 7c) cocsilinine,(fig. 7b) O-methylcocsulinin,(fig. 7d) nortrilobine (fig. 9) and pendulinine (fig. 8) isolated from leaves and stems and their structures  were elucidated; trilobine, O,O-dimethylcocsulinin, tetrandrine and isotrilobine also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of hernandezine, tetrandrine, penduline, cocsuline and punjabine from leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Two new sterols, coccupendulusterols A and B, and an unknown aliphatic hydrocarbon were isolated along with two known alkaloids, pendulinin and cocsulinine and hexacosane from the stems of Cocculus pendulus. (Ali et al, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9803-1719.
