DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence:  Compound umbel; Flower: Small, actinomorphic, hermaphrodite, pentamerous and epigynous; Calyx: represented by 5 teeth; Corolla: Petals 5 alternating with sepals, inflexed in bud and they fall off soon ; Androecium: Stamens 5, alternating with petals, filaments free, anthers dithecous, introrse and opening lengthwise.; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, syncarpous with an inferior ovary, placentation axile, ; Fruit: Cremocarp ; Seed: convex 
   
Floral and seed biology:

Flowering & Fruiting: December-March. 

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by birds and animals.

 
Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 22.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Loam soil that are well drained are suitable for cultivation of this crop.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to application of fertilizers.Farmyard manures are applicable during planting.

Water management: It is moisture loving plant and requires at least 2-3 irrigations .

Harvesting period / Method: When the plant is cultivated for herb oil, it is harvested after 3-31/2 months of planting when the plant are in bloom and the plant are in milky stage. For seeds, the crop is harvested when seeds are fully mature.

Chemical Variability: Four varieties of Indian dill (Anethum sowa) were studied to det. any characteristics which distinguish this species from European dill (A. graveolens). whereas A. graveolens  contained only 1 flavone, kaempferol, all A. sowa varieties contained of the 4 Indian dill varieties, only 1 was free of the toxic substances dill apiole. These 4 varieties contained 2-6% of volatile oil contg. 23.0-4.62% carvone, 5-35% dilhydrocarvone, and 24-45% limonene1;

4-vinyl-2-methoxyphenol, 4-hydroxy-3-methyl-6-(1-methyl ethyl)-cyclohex-2-en-1-one and nonacosa-10-one also identified in dill flower. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Determination of limonene (30.3), dillapiole (26.8), carvone (22.0) piperitone (8.2%), 8-dehydro-p-cymene and linalyl acetate in fruit essential oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Dill seed oil, essential oil seeds of Anethum graveolens were isolated and analysed by GC-MS. The main components a-carvone accounts for > 40%. (Jin et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 183820e.

According to the odor assessment of each volatile compound of Dill by the sniffing method of gas chromatography, a-phellandrene, 3,9-epoxy-1-p-menthene, myristicin, b-myrcene, and (z)-dihydro-carvone were the preferred components, while (E)-dihydrocarvone and elemicin were the least favored. (Pino et al, 1995). MAPA 1995, 9505-3285.

The steam distilled oil from fresh aerial parts including stalks, leaves and fruits (commonly known as dill herb oil) grown in Cuba was analyzed by GC and GC/MS. Among the 22 compounds identified, a-phellandrene (24.9%), p-cymene (14.4%), 3,9-epoxy-p-menth-1-ene (14.9%) and carvone (14.5%) were the major compounds. (Pino et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9606-0413.

The composition of the essential oil of dill (Anethum graveleons) was found to be different for the various plant parts. While a-phallandrene, b-phellandrene, dill ether and carvone occurred enatiomerically pure in all investigated plant parts and during all stages of maturity, both enantiomers of limonene were detected. (Faber et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9805-2663.

Pharmacology:

Isodillapiole, tribromide showed fungicidal activity against Helminthosporium oryzae and Aspergillus niger. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Mutagenesis inhibitors containing ³ 1 compound selected from dill apiole, apiole and myristicin as active ingredients. The mutagenesis inhibitors are used as pharmaceuticals and food as prophylactic agents against cancers. Dill apiole at 10-4 M showed 43.4% inhibition on mutation detected by modified Ames test. (Nakasugi, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, P25668v

Hexane fractions from methanolic extracts of Anethum graveolons revealed potent inhibitory activities against the resistance of multi-drug resistant Staphylococcus aureus SA2 when combined with ampicillin or chloramphenicol (Carvone) showed resistance inhibition at the level of 20-50 mg/ml when combined with 100/150 mg/ml of Am or Cm respectively. (Kim et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 191467t.

Indian dill oil, which was obtained from the seeds of Anethum sowa is called Anethi oil. The oil is used in perfumery as a replacement for flavouring purposes as it contains dillapiol, which is abortifacient and toxic to both liver and kidneys. (Ranade, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9804-2405.
