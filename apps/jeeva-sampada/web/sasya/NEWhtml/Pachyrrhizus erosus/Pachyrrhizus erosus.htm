Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Native to Mexico; also found is other areas of Central America; widely naturalised in various parts of the tropics in both hemispheres. 

Availability :

Commonly cultivated in parts of Bengal, Assam, Bihar and Orissa.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A coarse, hairy twiner, with large, fleahy tuverous roots, occasionally fond cultivated in parts of Bengal, Assam, Bhiar and orissa. Tubers simple or lobed, turnip-like orelogated, variable in size, up to 30 cm in diameter; leaves trifoliolate; leaflets large distantly toothed; flowers in lax racemes, blue or violet; pods hairy, 8-15 cm long with 8-10 seeds seeds yellow, brown or red almost square, more or less flattened, 5-11mm long and 5-10mm. broad.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Insecticidal, Anti-feedant

Parts Used: Ripe seeds

Aphids, Army worms, cabbage worms, diamond back moth, flea beetles, Mexican bean beetle, shrink bug, caterpillars. 

To make a spray, 2 kg pulverized Yam bean are stirred into 400 litres of water. Powdered seeds act as contact and stomach poisons and are highly effective against the flea beetle, Phyllotreta vittata; Mexican bean beetle, Epilachna varivestis; imported cabbage worm, Pieries rapae and aphids Myzus persicae, Orenga lanigera, Aphis rumicus and Macrosiphum pisi ( Crosby, 1971). Also toxic to melonworms, beetles, leafhoppers, etc. It is now used in commercial formulation for use in insecticidal sprays and dust.


Harvesting Practices: Matured fruits are harvested 


Produce and Products:


Chemistry: Pachyrrhizine, Pachyrrhizone, Erosone, Pachyrrhizid.

Processing Techniques:  

The seeds on extraction yield a toxic resin containing rotenone and several other compounds viz., pachyrrhizin, pachyrrhizin, erosone, pachysshizid most of which are related to rot enoids and pachyrrhizine. The seeds also contain two toxic water soluble, amorphous saponins, pachysaponins A$B which on hydrolysis, yield pachysapagenin A and B respectively (WOI, 1966).

Miscellaneous Information

 The leaves are poisonous to cattle other than horses; they contain pachyrrhizid. Leaves and stems contain fre steroids 

The stems are said to yield a tough fivre used in Fiji in making fishing nets. The plant can be grown as green manure with advantage, as grazing animals are said to eat it most unwillingly.
