Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea L.) is believed to be the native of Brazil to Peru, Argentina and Ghana, from where it was introduced into Jamaica, Cuba and other West Indies islands. The plant was introduced by Portuguese into Africa from where it was introduced into North America. It was introduced into India during the first half of the sixteenth century from one of the pacific islands of China, where it was introduced earlier from either Central America or South America. (1). Largely distributed throughout India. India occupies the first position, both in regard to the area and the production, in the world. About 7.5 million hectares is under cultivation annually and the production is about 6 million tonnes. 70% of the area and 75% of the production are concentrated in the four states of Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. Groundnut is widely distributed in India, China, Nigeria, Senegal, Sudan, Burma and the USA.


Groundnut is grown throughout the tropics and its cultivation is extended to the subtropical countries lying between 45 degrees N and 35 degrees S and upto an altitude of 1000 metres. The crop can be grown successfully in places receiving a minimum rainfall of 1,250 mm. The rainfall should be well distributed well during the flowering and pegging of the crop. Groundnut is grown on wide variety of soil types. However, the crop does best on sandy loam and loamy soils and in the black soils with good drainage. (1)


It is a low growing annual plant 1-2 ft (30-60 cm) high. The varieties under cultivation fall into three groups in respect of the habit of growth, namely bunched types, semi-spreading and spreading types. In the bunch group, the plants grow erect, possess light-green foliage, produce pods in clusters at the base of the plant and have round, plump non-dormant seeds, with light-rose testa. In the case of the semi-spreading and spreading varieties, the branches trail either partially or completely on the surface of the soil, produce pods all along them, possess dark-green foliage and are usually heavier yielding and later maturing than the bunch varieties (2).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Groundnut, in general, has a short-statured plant, with the main axis being upright (15 to 40 cm long) but the major part of the plant consists of the primary branches. Secondary and tertiary branches are found in the semi-spreading and spreading types, giving them a prostrate stature. The leaves are alternate, stipulate and quadri-foliate. Spirally arranged with phyllotaxy of 2/5, pinnate with 2 opposite pairs of obovate leaflets, mucronate, 3-7 x 2-3 cm, entire, stipules prominent, linear-pointed, about 2.5-3.5 cm long, adnate to petiole for less than half their length, petiole 3-7 cm long, pulvini at point where stipules become free and at base of each leaflet cause characteristic sleep movements in which the petiole folds downwards and the leaflets fold upwards until they touch (13). The flowers are orange yellow, typically papilionaceous, with a long calyx tube within, which is held the style borne on a superior ovary. The calyx and corolla lobes are borne on the top of the hyphanthium. The flowers are borne in the axils of the leaves on the fruiting branches. They are bisexual, Zygomorphic, complete and sessile. Petals are five, with one large standard, two wings and two fused keel petals. There are two sterile and eight fertile anthers, four of which are globose and four oblonge. (4)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers are borne on compressed spikes in the axils of foliage leaves and never at the same node as vegetative branches, one to several flowers. Flowers are most abundant at lowest nodes. Flowers sessile, 5-7 cm long, calyx tube elongates as bud develops, 4-6 cm long, very slender and often mistaken for pedicel, calyx lobes 5 with 4 fused into a superior lip above standard and 5th linear tooth below keel, 5 mm long, petals and staminal column adnate at base and inserted on top of calyx tube, petals 5, yellow, standard about 1.5 x 1.5 cm. Stamens 10, monadelphous, filaments fused for more than half their length, 2 stamens (opposite standard) sterile, 4 stamens with oblong, bilocular, introrse anthers alternating with 4 stamens with smaller globose anthers, pistil with ovary of single sessile carpel with 2-6 ovules, surmounted by long filiform style which elongates with calyx tube and ends in a club-shaped stigma among anthers (13).

Groundnut is predominantly a self-pollinated crop, the percentage of outcross in practically negligable (4). Flowers occur either singly or in clusters of three or four from leaf axils. In bunchy types large number of flowers arise towards the basal branches of the plant and only a few arise on upper part of branches, where as in spreading types flowers are found on the procumbent branches throughout. Flowering starts around 35-40 days after sowing. Species in the genus Arachis have an interesting reproductive biology because the seed containing pods mature underground instead of aerially as in most legumes (5). This is due to flowers don’t usually open and are self-pollinated, pollination takes place early in the morning. As soon as the fertilization is complete, the flowers fade, an intercalary meristem becomes active at the base of the ovary above the point of attachment of the hypanthium, producing new tissue below itself and resulting in an elongated stalk in the peg. The pegs are positively geotropic, enter the soil and bend in a horizontal plane. Generally two, and occassionally one, three or four, fertilized ovules are borne at the tip of the peg which later swells to become the pod (4).

The testa is generally pink, but varieties with red, white, purple and blotched testa, with various gradations of colours are available.

Breeding System :
The flowers are sessile, bisexual, and yellow in colour with fairly long calyx tube, which gives false appearance of pedicel. The calyx is tubular, slender ending in five lobes of which three are united into a big structure and other two are linear lanceolate (3). Corolla papilionaceous arising on the rim of the calyx tube and the kneels are united. Stamens are ten, eight fertile and two sterile staminodes. Of the eight fertile stamens four are with long anther lobes and four with rounded anther lobes. The gynoceium has a superior ovary with one to three ovules, and rarely more. The style is very slender passes through the length of the calyx tube and the manoadelphous staminal bundle. Stigma is terminal and hairy (4).

Phenology :
Groundnut is an annual plant grows to about 60 cm tall, it is grown mostly as a rainfed kharif crop, being sown from May to June depending on the mansoon rains, however it can be grown throughout the year as an irrigated crop (8). Flowering starts after one month of plant growth, flowers bloom for only about half a day and are self-pollinating.

Pollinators:
Flowers usually don’t open and are self-pollinated. Anthesis and pollination takes place at sunrise the following day at the time of petal expansion, self-pollination taking place within the closed keel. The flowers wither 5-6 hours after opening, after which the calyx tube is shed, leaving only the ovary and base of the style. There is a marked tendency to cleistogamy.

Seed Biology :

Seeds or kernels are enclosed in the pods. In bunch type- small or medium sized pods confined to the base of plant, containing 1-2 small round seeds or kernels within the shell. In runner type- medium sized pods borne along the length of the branches and containing 1-3 oval seeds in comparatively thicker shell. Seeds have a thin testa and without endosperm. They have two massive cotyledons, an epicotyl with three bud and has 4 foliage leaves and the two cotyledonary laterals have 1-2 leaves, the hypocotyl and a large radicle.

Seedling Germination :
Seeds of most of bunch types are non-dormant and germinate 10-15 days after harvesting, those of semi-spreading and spreading types are dormant and require rest peroid of 2-3 months before sowing but germination can be accelerated by moistening the seeds with the macerated kernels of non-dormant cultivators. On planting, the radicle emerges and grows rapidly and may reach a length of 10-14 cm in 4-5 days. Germination is neither epigeal nor hypogeal, as the hypocotyl carries the cotyledons to the surface of the soil where they remain. The length of the hypocotyl is dependent on the depth of planting and, if the seeds are buried deeply, may reach a length of 10-12 cm. The terminal bud develops quickly followed by the two lateral cotyledonary buds. The main axis exhibits little inhibitory growth on the lateral branches (13).

Cultivation:

Healthy and well-developed pods should be hand shelled or shelled with a suitable groundnut Sheller about fortnight before sowing. Well-filled kernels should be selected and treated with 5 g of thiram or 3 g of Captan per kg of kernels. The kharif crop is sown with a seed-drill or with a suitable planter at a depth of 8 to 10 cm. Spacing adopted differs from place to place. For the semi-spreading and spreading varieties, the spacings between the adjacent rows varies from 30 and 60 and within the row spacings between 10 and 15 cm. For the rainfed bunch groundnut the inter-raw spacings vary between the adjacent rows varies from 20 and 30 and intra-raw spacings between 10 and 20 cm. The quantity of well-developed seeds required per hectare with the above spacings will be about 110 kg for semi-spreading and spreading varieties and 120 kg for the bunch varieties. (1)

Before sowing, the field is given two ploughings and soil is pulverized well to obtain a good tilth. The third ploughing may be given just before sowing. Groundnut is a deep-rooted plant, it uses up both moisture and nutrients from the deeper layers of soil. Generally the following fertilizers schedules are recommended, depending on soil fertility, variety grown and quantum and distribution of rainfall. (1)

Usually, the application of only N and P is resorted to, since most of the Indian soils are rich in K. The application of N in two equal split doses, one before sowing and the other 30 days after sowing, is found to increase the yield. In virgin lands, when groundnut is newly introduced the application of a culture of Rhizobium as seed treatment is beneficial in increasing nodulation and nitrogen fixation. The application of gypsum at 500 kg per ha at the pegging stage will enhance pod formation.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

1.Aphids (Aphis craccivora)

Symptoms of damage: Black bodied tiny insects suck the sap making the plants stunted and yellow colour.

Control measures: Spray 0.025% of Methyl demetion or Metasystox or 0.05% of Endosulfan


2. Leaf -miner (Stomopteryx subsecivella)

Symptoms of damage: Dark green to brown hairless Larvae mine the leaves by folding and webbing them. Plants are retarded in growth and give a burnt-up appearance

Control measures: Spray 0.05% Dichrotophos or Fenitrothion


3. Pod-border (Euborillia stali)

Symptoms of damage: The earwigs feed on the both newly formed and mature pods and render them unfit for consumption

Control measures: Drill into the soil BHC 10% dust @ 25 kg/ha or Temik 10 g @ one kg/ha before planting


4. Red hairy caterpillar (Amsacta albistriga)

Symptoms of damage: The caterpillars occur in masses and defoliate the crop, reducing the yield

Control measures: Dust BHC 10% dust @25 kg/ha on young larvae or spray Metasystox 0.05 @ 350-400 litres per hectare


5.Termites (Odontotermae obsesus)

Symptoms of damage: Damage the roots causing the plants to die. Also, they scrape the shell and eat away the soft material. Apply Heptachlor 5% dust @25 kg per hectare.

Control measures: Apply heptachlor 5% dust @25 kg per hectare.


6. White grub (Holotrichia consanguinea)

Symptoms of damage: Grubs feed on young rootlets. Leaves of damaged plants turn pale, wilt and finally dry up.

Control measures: Apply Thimmet 10 G at 10 kg or Dasonit @ 20 kg/ha at the time of sowing. (1)


Diseases:

1.Bud-rot causal organism Virus

Symptoms of damage: Necrosis of the buds occurs and further growth of the plants is arrested. Axillary shoots are produced, owing to which the whole plant gives a whorled appearance. Such plants seldom produce any pods: resulting in a heavy reduction in yield

No control measure is available, but some cultivators are resistant to this disease.


2.Collar-rot (Aspergillus niger)

Symptoms of damage: Rooting of the hypocotyl region, wilting and death of the seedlings

Control measures: Treat the seed with Thiram or Captan 3 g/kg of seed.


3. Mosaic causal organism Virus

Symptoms of damage: Mottling of the leaves, accompanied with their malformation, smalling and plucking

Control aphids to arrest the spread of the disease.


4.Root rot causal organism Sclerotinia sp.

Symptoms of damage: The rot begins below the soil level, and spreads upwards and downwards to the root-system.

Control measures: Drench the soil with 0.02% wet ceresan


5. Rosette causal organism Virus

Symptoms of damage: Affected plants show a dense clump of tufty shoots with yellow leaves and erect habit.

Control aphids and jassids with an insecticidal spray to arrest an insecticidal spray to arrest the spread of the disease.


6.Rust (Puccinia arachidis)

Symptoms of damage: Orange-red pustules appear on both surfaces of the leaves. Eventually, the leaflets curl and drop off

Control measures: Spray 0.02% Hinosan to check the spread of the disease.


7.Tikka disease (Cercospora arachidicola)

Symptoms of damage: Necrotic circular spot surrounded by a light-yellow ring on the upper side of the leaves.

Control measures: Spray Benlate or Bavistin @0.05% at 2-3 week intervals

3-4 times, starting from 4-6 weeks after sowing (1, 21).

Economic Potential and Uses

The oil content of the seed varies from 44 to 50 per cent, depending on the varieties and agronomic conditions. Groundnut oil is edible oil. It finds extensive use as a cooking medium, both as refined oil and Vanaspati Ghee. Seeds yield a non-drying, edible oil, used in cooking, margarines, salads, canning, for deep-frying, for shortening in pastry and bread, and for pharmaceuticals, soaps, cold creams, pomades, emulsions for insect control, and fuel for diesel engines. Other products include dyes, ice cream, massage oil, paints, and peanut milk (20, 24).

It is also used in cosmetics, cold cream and lubricants, olein, stearin and other salts. The low graded oil is also used for candling. Groundnut oil now used in pharmaceuticals as a substitute to oilve oil (12, 13).

It has a therapautic value and is used for massaging polio patients, as a carrier for adrenaline in the treatment of ashthma and other ailments and to prepare a fatty drink for patients who need building up of the health before or after an operation (14).

The residual oilcake contains 7 to 8 per cent of N, 1.5 per cent of P 2O5 and 1.2 per cent of K2O and is used as a fertilizer. It is an important protein supplement in cattle and poultry rations. The ground nutcake powder is used as laundry starch and in the manufacture of paper, gummed taps and plastics.

Uses

Seeds are eaten raw, whole roasted and salted, or chopped in confectioneries, or ground into peanut butter. Young pods may be consumed as a vegetable. Young leaves and tips are suitable as a cooked green vegetable. Scorched seeds may serve as a coffee substitute. Peanut hulls are used for furfural, fuel, as filler for fertilizers, and for livestock feed, or sweeping compounds. Foliage provides silage and forage (20, 24). They are rich in protein and vitamins A, B and some members of B2 group. Their calorific value is 349 per 100 grammes. It is also consumed as confectionery product. The cake can be used for manufacturing artificial fibre/thread called Ardil which is mixed with cotton woolen and rayon. Nowadays, milk, curd, and butter are prepared from groundnut.


Harvesting Practices
Groundnut should be harvested when the leaves become yellow, rosy colour appearance of seed coat or dark brown colour of the inner shell of the pod. At this stage the pods become reticulated and within the pod, the seed is separated from the pod.

The plants could be either pulled or dugout through manual or mechanical ploughs and dried in the threshing yard after removing the soil for about 8-10 days. Long hour drying is not recommended, as it results in splitting of seed cotyledons. The pods should be removed from stalk either through machine or by hand and must be further dried in shade or during early hours of the day for 5-6 days to reduce the moisture content around nine per cent (4). Under rainfed conditions, the average yield of semi-spreading and spreading varieties is 1200-1400 kg of unshelled pods per hectare and that of bunch types is 800-1000 kg. The crops grown with supplemental irrigation produce 3,000 kg or more of pods per hectare. The pods Yield 70 to 75 per cent of kernels by weight. The yield of haulms is usually two to two- and-half times that of pods (1).

Processing Techniques

Pretreatment

  • Drying by natural and artificial methods.

  • Shelling by hand or mechanical decorticator viz., Beater type or Grater type.


Extraction

  • Oil extraction by screw expellers or hot or cold pressing of kernels in hydraulic presses.

  • Cake produced may be used as such or sent to a flaking mill and then to a solvent extraction plant for removal of further oil

  • Crude oil is refined (2).


Expeller pressing:

This method is most widely used to recover the oil, particularly in the developing world. The equipment employed range from the traditional bullock drawn ghani to the highly mechanized electrically operated screw press. Peanuts containing about 6% moisture are crushed and fed into the large conical end of the expeller chamber. Pressure is exerted as the screw turns, forcing the material towards small end. The oil is expelled through screen due to friction heat and pressure. The oil is collected beneath the expeller chamber, while the residue is extruded from the small end. More than one expeller units are often arranged side by side on commercial oil mills, where the oil is collected through a common channel at one place, filtered and packed in tin cans or barrels of suitable size as crude peanut oil.


Hydraulic Pressing:

The shelled groundnuts with about 6% moisture are crushed, heated with live steam and spread on press cloth with edges folded to prevent running. These are formed by a modified rack-and-cloth operation. The racks are placed in to tiers and about 14,000 psi pressure is applied. The crude oil is collected beneath the tiers. Similar to expeller pressing, a small amount of unshelled peanuts or peanut meal is mixed with the ground a groundnut as a press aid. The efficiency of oil extraction is lower than expeller perssing and the press cake is often not suitable for human consumption. The cake is subjected to solvent extration to remove the residual oil.


Solvent extraction:

The residual oil in the cake produced in hydraulic or expeller press is usually extracted by employing a suitable solvent. The system is rarely used for full fat peanut grits or meal. It is a closed system in which the partially defatted cake grits or meal is washed with hexane to remove oil. Other solvents include absolute ethanol and 95% ethanol. However, n-hexane is most commonly used. The solvent is removed by evaporation and recycled while the oil is further refined for edible purposes. Almost completely defatted cake meal obtained is used as cattle feed and manure. The meal can, however, be used for human consumption if both expeller pressing and subsequent solvent extraction operations are performed under hygienic conditions and if good quality peanuts are used (14).


Refining of oil:

The fresh crude oil obtained in hydraulic or expeller units is always subjected to refining before consumption. The steps involved in oil refining are mechanical removal of impurities such as meal and moisture by sedimentation, filtration or centrifugation of phosphatides by degumming, free fatty acids by alkali treatment, colours (bleaching) by treatment with activated charcoal and deodorization by steam heating under vacuum. The process also removes natural flavour, antioxidants and fat soluble vitamins.

Other Uses

Peanuts play a small role in various folk pharmacopoeias. In China the nuts are considered demulcent, pectoral, and peptic, the oil aperient and emollient, taken internally in milk for gonorrhea, externally for rheumatism. In Zimbabwe the peanut is used in folk remedies for plantar warts. The alcoholic extract is said to affect isolated smooth muscles and frog hearts like acetylcholine. The alcoholic lipoid fraction of the seed is said to prevent hemophiliac tendencies and for the treatment of some blood disorders (mucorrhagia and arthritic hemorrhages) in hemophilia (20, 24).

The plant stalks are fed (green, dried or silaged) to livestock. Groundnut shell is used as fuel for manufacturing coarse boards, cork substitutes etc. Groundnut is also of value as rotation crop. Being a legume with root nodules, it can synthesize atmospheric nitrogen and therefore improve soil fertility.

Miscellaneous Information

The more serious problem in the use of Groundnut flour for human food is the presence of anti-nutritional and toxic factors. Aflatoxin is not an intrinsic constituent present in groundnut. Aspergillus flavus, produces aflotoxin on groundnut which is not properly dried or stored, which is toxic to human beings and ruminate animals. Methods have been developed to remove the deleterious constituents or to render them harmless. Since the testa contains some anti-nutritional factors and also imparts unattractive colour to the flour it is essential to remove it for the preparation of edible groundnut flour. The operation for its removal consists in roasting the kernels at 80-90°C for 10 min, cooling and subjecting, them to abrasive action in a blanching machine whereby 90-95 per cent the testa is removed. The mould growth on kernels which produces aflatoxin does not occur when the moisture content of the kernels is below 9 per cent. Hence by open drying, the contamination can be prevented. However, if groundnut is harvested during rainy season, as it is done in some parts in India, Sun drying is not possible and drier have to be used. Several chemical methods have been suggested for detoxifying, the flour. These include wet treatment, ammonisation, solvent extraction and treatment with ozone, hydrogen peroxide etc. None of the methods appears to have been tried on commercial scale (19).

Groundnut is the premier oilseed crop of India, it holds a 35% share of the total oilseed area (24 million ha) and contributes nearly 40% of the total oilseeds production (20 million t). The low level of productivity in India is largely because the crop is rainfed, exposed to various biotic and abiotic stresses, and to the socioeconomic factors associated with small and marginal farmers in dryland areas. Concerted research efforts have been made to alleviate the constraints by: emphasizing the development of high-yielding cultivars with multiple resistances to foliar diseases and pests, resistance to drought, breeding for earliness, introducing efficient Rhizobium strains and developing economically viable production and protection technologies with an emphasis on integrated pest management. The use of these technologies can result in yield advantages of 50-63%. The encouragement of groundnut growers cooperatives in new niches and non-traditional areas will ensure adequate price support and marketing that will contribute to achieving high yields and crop sustainability (25).
