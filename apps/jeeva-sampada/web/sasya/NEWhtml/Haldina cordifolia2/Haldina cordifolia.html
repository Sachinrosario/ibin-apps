DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Large deciduous trees, stems often irregularly fluted and buttressed at base, commonly reaching 6 m girth and 33 m high; bark partly dry grey or blackish and partly pale ashy or silvery white, rather rough, exfoliating in irregular woody scales. Leaves opposite, orbicular, 10-25 cm in diam., abruptly acuminate at apex, base cordate, margin entire, glabrescent above, pubescent beneath, lateral nerves 5-8 pairs; petioles stout, 3.8-8.5 cm long, pubescent; stipules orbicular or oblong, 1.2-1.7 cm long, enclosing terminal bud at middle, caducous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers yellow, in globose axillary pedunculate heads, 1.5-3.0 cm in diam.; peduncles 1-4 together, 2-7 cm long. Sepals 5, linear. Corolla downy, 3.5-5.5 mm long, pubescent. Capsules cuneate, downy, splitting into 2 dehiscent cocci. Seeds many, minute, winged.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plants are used in cholera. Bark is used in antiseptic, fever, scars on skin and yellowing of the body, urine complaints and worms in sores. Stem is used in eye complaints. Leaves are also useful in cold, cough, headache and wounds.
