Chemistry:

·        Steroidal alkaloid glycoside – jurubine [(25-S)-3b-amino-5a-furostane-22a,26-diol-O(26)-b-D-glucopyranoside-isolated from roots; leaves contained N-free steroid saponins which on acid hydrolysis yielded neochlorogenin and paniculogenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Steroidal sapogenins – sisalagenone and torvogenin – obtained after acid hydrolysis of fruit extract. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        2,3,4-Trimethyltriacontane, octacosanyl triacontanoate, 5-hexatriacontanone along with triacontanol, 3-tritriacontanone, tetratriacontanoic acid, sitosterol, stigmasterol and campesterol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new steroidal saponin – torvonin A – isolated from leaves and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        A new steroidal saponin – torvonin B – isolated from leaves and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Determination of solasodine obtained by hydrolysis of glycoalkaloids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Chlorogenone (25( R)5a-spirostane-3,6-dione) and its 25(S)-epimer along with neochlorogenone isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Seven new steroidal glycosides, named torvosides A-G, were isolated from the aerial parts and from the roots of Solanum torvum. Their structures were characterized as (25S)-26-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl)-5-a-furostan-3-b,6-a,22-zeta,26-tetraol 6-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à3)-b-D-quinovopyuranoside), (25S)-26-O-(b-D-glucopyranosyl)-22-a-methoxy-5-a-furostan-3-b,6-a,26-triol 6-O-(b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à3)-b-D-quinovopyranoside), neosolaspigenin 6-O-(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à3)-b-D-quinovopyranoside), neosolaspigenin 6-O-(b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à3)-b-D-quinovopyranoside), (25S)-26-O-(b-D-glucopyranosyl)-6-a,26-dihydroxy-22-a-methoxy-5-a-furostan-3-one 6-O-(b-D-quinovopyranoside), (25S)-26-O-(b-D-glucopyranosyl)-6-a,26-dihydroxy-22-a-methoxy-5-a-furostan-3-one 6-O-(b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à3)-b-D-quinovopyranoside), and (25S)-26-hydroxy- 22-a-methoxy-5-a-furostan-3-one 26-O-(b-D-glucopyranoside), respectively. (Yahara et al., 1996) CA.126:44901

·        The methanolic extract of Solanum torvum fruit showed a wide spectrum of antimicrobial activities against human and animal clinical isolates. (Chah et al., 2000)

·        The C-4 sulfated isoflavonoid, torvanol A (1), and the steroidal glycoside, torvoside H (3), together with the known glycoside, torvoside A (2), were isolated from a MeOH extract of Solanum torvum fruits. Upon enzymatic hydrolysis with b-glucosidase, torvoside A (2) and torvoside H (3) yielded the corresponding acetal derivatives 4 and 5, respectively. Torvanol A (1), torvoside H (3) and compound 5 exhibited antiviral activity (herpes simplex virus type 1) with IC50 values of 9.6, 23.2 and 17.4 mug/ml, respectively. Compounds 1-5 showed no cytotoxicity (at 50 mug/ml) against BC, KB and Vero cell lines. (Arthan et al., 2002) CA. 137:44213


Pharmacology:

·        Solanum torvum Sw. was extracted with hot 50% ethanol or distilled water to study their platelet aggregating effects, comparing the effect of the platelet aggregation induced by ADP or collagen. The plant extracted with 50% ethanol exhibited a more potent platelet aggregating effect than the aqueous extracts, examining the extent of formation of platelet aggregates according to size distribution with the use of a platelet aggregometer employing a laser-scattering method, which is more sensitive to the detection of small-sized platelet aggregate formations than the conventional OD or impedance methods. The results suggested that platelet aggregation activating constituents in both plants may be more ethanol soluble than water soluble. (Moriyama et. al., 2003)
