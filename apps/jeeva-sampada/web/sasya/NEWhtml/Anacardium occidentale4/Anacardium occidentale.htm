HABIT & HABITAT


A small tropical tree, often found near the coast.
Availability :

Cultivated in west coast, Ratanagiri and north Kanara, Bombay and Goa.
Conservation/ Threat Status :

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Trees; leaves spirally arranged, obovate, obtuse or slightly emarginate at apex, cuneate or rounded at base; inflorescence panicles; flowers polygamous, pentamerous, yellow, streaked with red, sepals deciduous, petals recurved, densely short hairy on both surfaces, disc minute, stamens 7-10, , ovary one celled; fruits drupes, orange or crimson with a kidney shaped nut seated on it.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
fruits are used in preparation of juice and syrups.

The Cashew-apple has a fibry flesh and is full of juice and possesses an exotic flavour with fine and pleasing aroma. The juice is rich in sugars (Brix about 130 – mostly invert sugars) and as about 0.33 percent (as malic acid) and contains some tannins, fruit, because it contains astringent and acrid principles which produce a rough, unpleasant and biting sensation on the tongue and throat when eaten.

1. Juice: For the purpose of making juice, fruit of any colour may be used, but it must be fully ripe. The process consists in minutes at 5-10 lb. steam pressure, followed by washing and cooling in water. This helps to remove most of the astringent and acrid principles. The juice is then expressed with the help of a work type juice extractor, or some suitable type of basket press. The yield of juice varies from 50-60 percent, depending on the quality of fruit and the method of extraction employed. The worm type extractor is regarded as more satisfactory than the basket press; however, best results are obtained by employing the former for a prliminary extraction, followed by pressing the resultant residue in a basket press. The expressed juice is then treated with 0.045 percent gelatin (added as 5% solution in water). The precipitate formed is removed and the sugar and acid regulated to 0.35 – 0.4% with added cirtric acid. Contact with iron at any stage has to be avoided as it causes discolouration of the product. All equipment used should, therefore, be made of monel metal, stainless fruit may be held in perforated crates made of stainless steel, monel metal or aluminium.

The juice of the fresh (unsteamed) fruit gives an equally good product when treated similarly, employing higher proportion of gelatin, (viz. 0.25-0.35 percent), but the same proportions of sugar and acid.

The juice finally obtained by any of the above methods can be staisfactorily preserved by boiling it for a minute, followed by ‘lover-flow’pasteurization at 850 – 900 C. It yields a carbonated beverage of good quality by itself (after appropriate dilution and addition of sugar) as well as after blending with other fruit juices. It is, therefore, regarded as a suitable base for the preparation of true fruit and nutritious carbonated beverages, in preference to the synthetic ones, which have no food value.

Syrup: Juice from the steamed fruit is mixed with 6 – 7 per cent lime juice, stirred thoroughly and allowed to stand for abour 15 minutes, when a precipitate settles down. It is then strained through a thick cloth (long cloth is quite suitable) or about 120 mesh stainless steel or monel metal sieve, to remove the precipitate. In case of the fresh fruit, 0.2 – 0.3 per cent gelatin (as 5% solution in water) is also added along with 6- 7 per cent lime juice, the mixture is allowed to stand and the precipitate formed in this case is also removed by straining through cloth or sieve. The sugar concentration of the strained juice obtained in either case is raised to 350 – 360 Brix by adding the necessary amount of crystalline sugar. The resulting syrup, which can be satisfactorily preserved like juice, yields a refreshing beverage when diluted with cold water or plain soda, to three times its volume.

The following steps are involved in the preparation of wine in Goan method.
Methods of wine preparation:

Chop fine and remove seeds. No need to remove the skin. Pour boiling water and add 2gms Potassium Metabisulphite. Keep for 24 hrs. Pulp the fruit and strain it into the fermentation jar. Add 1/2 kg sugar, yeast and wheat. Keep for 8 days to ferment on the 9th day add the remaining 1/2 sugar. All this time keep on stirring every alternate day till the 21st day. Let it settle for 48 hours. Rack and add 2gms. Potassium Metabisulphite and siphon into bottles. Mature at least for 3 months (Saldanha , 2001).
