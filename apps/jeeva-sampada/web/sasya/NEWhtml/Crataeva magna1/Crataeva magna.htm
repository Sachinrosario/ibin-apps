Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A small tree, found almost all over in deciduous or semievergreen forests, often along streams, but also in dry deep boulder formations in the sub-Himalayan tract, up to 1000 m in India.

Distributed in India Bangladesh, Burma, Myanmar, China, and Ceylon, extending to Malaysia and Indonesia.



Availability : Peninsular India, Western India, Gangetic Plains and Eastern India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small tree with a much-branched head. Leaves deciduous, 3-foliate; petioles 3.8-7.6 cm long; leaflets 5-15 by 3.8-6.3 cm ovate, lanceolate or obovate, acute or acuminate, attenuate at the base, entire, glabrous on both surfaces, pale beneath and reticulately veined, the lateral leaflets oblique at the base; petiolules 6-9 mm long. Flowers many in dense terminal corymbs, greenish white; pedicels 2.5-4.4 cm long stout, glabrous. Sepals petaloid, small, distant, ovate, acute. Petals (including the claw) nearly 2.5 by 0.9 cm; claw up to 6 mm long, very narrow. Stamens longer than the petals, spreading. Gynophore nearly 5 cm long, terete, smooth or scurfy berry, on the thickened gynophore. Seeds imbedded in pulp, nearly smooth brown. The filaments of the stamens are purple or white when young, yellow or pale pink when old; the petals whitish when young, yellowish when old.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
ORNAMETAL TREE
The drug commonly known as 'Varunachhal' is derived from the bark of Crataeva magna (Lour.) DC

Parts Used:

The bark is hot, bitter at first, and the sweet sharp taste; easy to digest; laxative, vesicant, anthelmintic, detergent, bechic, expectorant; removes "vata"; good in strangury, diseases of the chest and the blood, tuberculous glands; causes biliousness.
The fruit is sweet and oily; laxative; removes "vata" , "pitta" and "kapha" (Ayurveda).
The bark promotes appetite, decreases the secretion of the bile and phlegm, and removes disorders of the urinary organs (Yunani).
