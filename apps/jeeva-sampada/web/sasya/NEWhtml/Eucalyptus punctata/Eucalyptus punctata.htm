Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Small to moderate sized tree. Indigenous to Australia. One of the best species tried in Nilgiris. It has been successfully grown at Mercara in Coorg. It succeeds moderately well on Simla hills at elevations below 4,000 ft. and in the plains of northern India, but not suitable for hot localities. It grows well in Dehra Dun.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Grown by entire transplanting. For raising plantations of Eucalyptus, seedling stock is raised in the nursery. Seeds are sown broadcast in boxes or beds during September/ October or February/March. Germination takes place in one to two weeks. Seedlings are pricked out in polythene containers when they are 5-10 cm high. The plants are watered regularly. Polythene bags are shifted from time to time to prevent plants taking roots in the soil. Nursery seedlings are outplanted with the onset of the summer monsoon. Transplanting is completed early to enalbe seedlings to establish during the rains. Seedlings in the polythene bags are generally to establish during rains. Seedlings in polythene bags are generally used for planting though naked-root planting is practised in some states. Before planting in pits, an insecticide is generally added to soil to protect seedlings against termite damage to which the tree is susceptible.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Cylindrocladium scoparium Morg. – seedling blight in Goa and Dehra Dum (U.P.)

Cylindrocladium quinqueseptatum Boedijn & Reitsma – seedling blight in Dehra Dun and Kerala.

Cercospora eucalypti Cke. & Mass. – leaf rust

Phytophthora cinnamomi Rands – root rot

Ganoderma lucidum – root rot

Corticium salmonicolor B & Br. – pink disease

Alternaria alternata (Fr.) Keissler – leaf spot

Mosaic virus – bushy growth

Little leaf – stunting and reduction in leaf size

Leaf crinckle – upward curling of leaves
