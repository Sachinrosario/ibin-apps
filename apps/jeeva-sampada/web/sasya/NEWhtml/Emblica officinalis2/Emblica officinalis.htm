Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Deciduous small or middle-sized tree with crooked trunk. Found in Subtropical regions like Himalayan foothills and Central Indian hills and in tropical high rainfall areas of Western Ghats, West Bengal and Assam (wet tropical), tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical), dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical).

Adapted to humid, tropical and subtropical climate, with annual rainfall above 800 mm, with long dry season, on altitudes from sea level to 1800 m. grows on a variety of soils including laterite, sandy, gravelly, deep clay, alluvial, alkaline, acidic and poor dry soils. Plant is sensitive to frost and drought. Prefers moist soil and can tolerate poor alkaline conditions.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Deciduous tree with crooked trunk and spreading branches; bark greenish-grey, peeling off in conchoidal flakes; branchlets glabrous or finely pubescent, 4-8" long, often deciduous. Leaves subsessile, closely set along the branches, distichous, fan like, light green, glabrous, narrowly linear, obtuse, imbricate when young having the appearance of pinnate leaves; stipules ovate, finely acute.

Flowers greenish yellow in axillary fascicles on the leaf bearing branchlets, often on the naked portion below the leaves, with fimbriate bracts at the base. Male flowers numerous on short slender pedicels. Sepals 6, oblong, obtuse. Disk 0. Anthers 3 on a short central column. Female flowers few, subsessile. Sepals as in the male. Disk a lacerate cup. Ovary 3-celled, styles connate at the base. Irregularly twice, 2-fid with acute lobes. Fruits pale green, fleshy, globose with 6 obscure vertical furrows, pale yellow, of 3 two-seeded crustaceous cocci. Seeds 6, trigonous, enclosed in a hard cover, which is light brown in colour.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Fruiting starts after 3rd year in case of vegetatively propagated trees otherwise after 7 years.

Leaves are shed during November-December. Usually leafless during February or March.

Flowers usually appear in the hot season and fruits ripen during the following winter.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: This is usually propagated by seeds. It may also be propagated vegetatively by budding, cutting and inarching. Propagated through grafted plants for commercial production. However, establishment through nursery raised seedlings is the most common method.

Fresh extracted seeds are sown in January-February. To hasten the germination, seeds are treated with 500 ppm G.A. for 24 hours and then sown about 2-3 cm deep in polybags or field. Spacing 5X5 m. Seeds can be soaked in water overnight before sowing. Coppices well and produces root suckers. Grafted plants of a superior variety known as 'Banarasi amla' are used in North India for establishing commercial plantation.

Tree has coppicing ability.



MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Ravenelia emblica Syd. – leaf spot from Dehra Dun (U.P.), Nagpur (Maharashtra), Tanjore (Tamil Nadu) and Bihar.

Phakospora phyllanthi Diet. – leaf spot from Pune (Maharashtra)

Pestalotia cruenta Syd. – fruit spot

Other uses:

Fruit - The richest known source of vitamin C. The fruit known as Emblic Myrobolon used as a medicine and for tanning and dying. Also made into sweetmeat with sugar and sometimes used as a pickle. Used in indigenous medicine.

Fruits are used in the preparation of writing inks and hair dyes. The dried fruit is detergent and is used as shampoo for the head.

Fodder - Leaves amd fruits are used as fodder for cattle.

Tannin - Fruits, bark and leaves are rich in tannin. Immature fruits are employed for tanning in combination with other tanstuffs such as myrobalans. The twig bark is of considerable value as a tanning material. The tannin is almost exclusively catechuic. It gives a brownish red extract and produces a leather lacking somewhat in flexibility, but with a soft grain. In combination with the leaves of Carissa spinarum (30%) and of Anogeissus latifolia (20%), the twig bark gives a good leather with a reddish brown colour. For the production and collection of tan bark on a commercial scale, coppicing is the system considered most suitable.

Dye - Leaves contain a brownish yellow colouring matter used in dyeing tusser and mulberry silks and wool, when used with iron mordant, a black colour is produced.

Green manure - Leaves are used as manure in areca and cardamom plantations in Mysore. They may be employed also for ameliorating alkali soils.
