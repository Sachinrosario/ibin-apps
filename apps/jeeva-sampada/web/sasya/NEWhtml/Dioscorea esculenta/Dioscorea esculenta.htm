HABIT & HABITAT
A prickly climber with puberulous or pubescent, sometimes villous, stems twining to the left. Dioscorea esculenta is probably native in Siam and Indo-China. It is cultivated in the moist tropical regions of Asia from Bombay coast in the west to the Pacific Islands in the east. In India, it is found in Malabar and Coromandal coast and in Deccan, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Orissa, West Bengal and Assam, ascending up to 3,000 ft. in Eastern Himalayas. It occurs also in Khasi-Naga hills, Garo hills and in Andaman Islands.
Dioscorea esculenta was once extensively cultivated in India. Since the introduction of potatoes, however, its importance has suffered and its cultivation is now confined to certain backward areas of Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Madras.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Leaves alternate, Bulbils absent. Tubers 4 to many, stalked, produced in bunches close to the surface of the ground; they may be sausage-shaped, globose or flattened and lobed, usually not longer than 12 cm., but sometimes reaching up to 50 cm. and weighing 3 kg.; rootlets present in some races; rarely thorny; skin thin, fawn-coloured, tawny or reddish tawny; flesh soft, white, edible, with varying sweetness.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers.

Uses :
  1. The tubers are starchy and free from dioscorne.
  2. They have a sweetish taste.

Harvesting Practices
Tubers are harvested 8-9 months after planting and yields under favorable conditions are heavy, up to 16 tons per acre. Individual plants have been reported to yield 10 Ib. of tubers and the yield per acre in Madras varies from 2.7 to 3.2 tons.

Chemistry :
The tubers are starchy and free from dioscorine. It is good in taste and in flavour and mealiness, they closely resemble potatoes. Analysis of tubers of var. fasciculata gave the following values (on dry matter basis) : albuminoids, 10.82; ash9.65; i.e.., 1.72; carbohydrates, 71.29; fibre, 6.52; and P,0,, 0.949r (Sampson, loc. cit.; Hooper, loc. cit.). Grated tubers are reported to be used as an application for swellings.
