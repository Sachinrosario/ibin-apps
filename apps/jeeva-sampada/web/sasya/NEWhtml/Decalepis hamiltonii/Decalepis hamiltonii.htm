HABIT & HABITAT
It is a climbing shrub, A monotypic genus distributed in the Deccan Peninsula and common in the forest areas of Western Ghats.
 Generally it is seen in the forests, Hill forests, hills, Near bushes & rocks, Peninsular.
BioticZone: Carnatic, Deccan peninsula, Peninsular India, Subtropical Himalayas, Tropical Eastern Himalayas and Western Ghats.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 It is a climbing shrub with jointed stem and orbicular or elliptic-obovate leaves. Its root has a strong aromatic odour and is markedly fleshy and cylindrical (1-6 cm. diam.). It has a sweet sarsaparilla-like taste accompanied by a tingling sensation on the tongue and is considered to be an appetiser and blood purifier. The root is pickled with limes.

Chemistry
The root contains 92% fleshy matter and 8%woody core. The volatile principle responsible for the aroma and taste of Decalepis root is 4-0-methyl-resorcylaldehyde (m.p., 42°C), which is present in a concentration of 0.8% in the air-dried material. It can be isolated by steam distillation; and better yield is obtained by extracting the powdered root with alcohol and, after distilling off the solvent, subjecting the residue to steam distillation. The root can be stored for long periods and is unaffected by microorganisms and insects; this is apparently due to the presence of the volatile principle, which possesses bacterio-static (growth of B. coli arrested by the aldehyde in 0.041% concentration) and toxic (fish die within 4 min. in 0.02% solution) properties. 4-0-methyl-resorcylaldehyde may probably find application as a preservative for canned and stored foods. The root contains, besides the aldehyde, inositol(0.40%), saponins, tannins, a crystalline resin acid (m.p., 245°), an amorphous acid (m.p., 180°), a ketonic substance (m.p., 83-84°), and a substance showing characteristics partly of sterols and partly of resinols. The sterol consists mainly of stigma and brassica sterols.
