HABIT & HABITAT:
A spiny herb or shrub, 3-4 m. tall, found throughout the tropical parts of India and in the Andaman's.

Parts used:
Plant and Fruits.

Uses
Fruits of the plant are cooked and eaten as a vegetablewb.- they are also dried and preserved. 

They are considered to be useful in cases of liver and spleen enlargement and a decoction of them is given for cough. Chemical composition: Analysis of dried fruits gave the following values : moisture, 12-3; protein, 8-3; fat, 1-7; minerals, 5-1; crude fibre, 17-6: and other carbohydrates, 55-0 g./lOO g. The mineral constituents (ing./100 g. material) are : Ca. 390; P, 180;and Fe, 22-2 (ionisable Fe, 1-5); vitamin A, 750 I.U.(l);il/-icl, 435: 

The fatty oil extracted from fruits (1.7%) is yellowand viscous with an aromatic odour. It lias the followingconstants : acid val., 10-4: sap. val.. 183-5: Hehner val.,90-8; iod. val., 113-5; and unsapon. matter, 2-3%. Sito-sterol-n-glucoside (n-i.p. 248-249°) separates out from thechloroform extracts of the fruits on standing/ Unripefruits and leaves contain the glycoalkaloid, solasoninc0.37% total alkaloids in air-dried fruits of the plantfrom Khasi and Jaintia hills). Hydrolysis of the neutralglycosidal fraction yields a steroidal sapogcnin, chloro-genin, which is rare in Solanitiii spp. 

The plant is used in the treatment of couglis: It is a sedative, diuretic and digestive. 

Leaves are used as haemostatic in the Cameroons: they contain no vitamin K or derivatives of naphthoquinone and the haemostaticaction may be due to the oil or pectins or both. Extracts of the plant affect the rate, and amplitude of respiration and also blood pressure; they also contract isolatedilcum of guinea-pig. Root is useful in poulticing cracks in the feet. The fumes of burning seeds are inhaled for toothache
