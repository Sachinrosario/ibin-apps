DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence: Decompound spatheate panicles, raceme with rachis internode and pedicel pillose, sessile spikelet with lower glume slightly depressed, flat on the back, Pedicelled spikelet little shorter, lower glume  with 5 or more distinct nerves. Fruit: Caryopsis. Seeds: one.
Floral and seed biology:

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are highly cross pollinated .Seeds are dispersed by wind.  
 
Genetic variability:
The  reported chromosome numbers=40, 60.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant requires well drained sandy loam for cultivation.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of manure and fertilizers.

Water management: Plant responds well to approximate annual rainfall.

Harvesting period / Method: Leaves are harvested when the oil constituents are high.

(Torres and Ragadio, 1998) CA. 1998,126:334188.
The essential oil of Cymbopogon citratus (DC.) Stapf. of Cuban origin has been analyzed by GC/MS. Twenty-seven compound were identified of which neral (38.2%) and geranial (49.5%) were the major constituents. (Pino and Rosado, 2000) CA. 2000,133:278687.
The composition of the essential oil of Cymbopogon citratus, obtained in semiindustrial scale, has been analyzed by gas chromatography. Retention index, relative retention time, and co-chromatography with pure compounds were applied. Components were also characterized by mass spectrometry. (Viturro and Bucu, 1998) CA. 1999, 129:152978.
The near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy method developed was rapid, simple and non-destructive and may prove beneficial for the accurate determination of the citral content of lemongrass oils and for the approximate citral content of lemon oils. (Wilson et al., 2002).

Pharmacology: Myrcene showed significant inhibition of nociception in mice at doses of 10. 0 and 20. 0 mg/kg (i.p.) or 20.0 and 40.0 mg/kg (s.c.). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.
Antimicrobial activities of the oils of Cymbopogon citrates was studied against Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Proteus mirabilis, Klebsiellapneumoniae, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Candida albicans. (Chalchat et al., 1997) CA.1998, 126:190819.
Lemon grass treatment significantly inhibited DNA adduct formation in both the colonic mucosa and the muscular layer but not in the liver. In addition, lemon grass extract treatment significantly inhibited aberrant crypt foci formation in both the initiation stage and the promotion stage. Especially in the promotion stage, lemon grass treatment inhibited the formation of larger ACF (with four or more crypts per focus), which was predictive of tumor incidence. Furthermore, lemon grass extract inhibited fecal beta-glucuronidase competitively and had antioxidant activity. These results suggest that the lemon grass extract inhibits the release of activated aglycon, methylazoxymethanol, from a glucuronide conjugate in the colon, and decreases the DNA adducts and ACF formation in the rat colon. (Suaeyun et al., 1997)
The essential oil (EO) from leaves of Cymbopogon citratus increased the reaction time to thermal stimuli both after oral (25 mg/kg) and intraperitoneal (25-100 mg/kg) administration. EO (50-200 mg/kg, p.o. or i.p.) strongly inhibited the acetic acid-induced writhings in mice. In the formalin test, EO (50 and 200 mg/kg, i.p.) inhibited preferentially the second phase of the response, causing inhibitions of 100 and 48% at 200 mg/kg, i.p. and 100 mg/kg, p.o., respectively. On the other hand, the opioid antagonist naloxone blocked the central antinociceptive effect of EO, suggesting that EO acts both at peripheral and central levels. (Viana et al., 2000)
Inhibitory effects of lemon grass extract on the early phase hepatocarcinogenesis in rats after initiation with diethylnitrosamine. (Puatanachokchai et al., 2002).
