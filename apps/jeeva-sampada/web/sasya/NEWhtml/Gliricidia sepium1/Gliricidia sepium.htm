Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A small or medium sized tree with short bole. Grown fairly widely in parts of Madras, Mysore, Bombay and Travancore-Cochin up to an elevation of 3,000 ft. Quick growing.

Temperature – 220 –300 C. It is found growing on the plains and foothills of Central America extending up to about 1,600 m elevation, mainly below 500 m. requires 1,500-2,300 mm and more rainfall per year. It does well in moist or dry soil, even with heavy concentrations of limestone.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small, thornless tree having an open crown and contorted trunk, grows up to 10 m high. Leaves large, imparipinnate, with 7-15 leaflets, bright green above and pale below.

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers purplish pink or white, borne in great profusion when leaves shed. Pods linear, 4-8" long, compressed, containing 10 or more seeds.


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowers in January-February. Leaves are shed in dry weather.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: The plant is easily propagated by seed; however, the natural regeneration of large cuttings (often nearly 2 m long) is a simple method of getting large specimens quickly. Seeds are soaked in hot water, cool off during the night and sown the next morning.

It may be propagated by seeds or cuttings. Propagation by cuttings is preferred since seeds are liable to insect attack and are not easy to obtain. Branches or cuttings, 3-6 ft. long, take root readily and can be planted in situ with an interspacing of c. 12 ft. or according to requirements.



MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Tree is fairly free from pests and diseases. It is sometimes attacked by mealy bug, Pseudococcus virgatus Cockerell, resulting int eh wholesale shedding of leaves.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:

Wood used as fuel.although not tall, tree produces much branch wood and coppice easily. Calorific value of dry wood: 4,900 kcal/kg.

Wood: Wood with sp. gr. 0.75, durable, highly restant to termites and decay and used for house posts, fences, stakes furniture, small articles, agricultural implements, tool handles, railway cross-ties, etc.

Wood is termite proof.

Other uses:

Loppings rich in nitrogen; tender twigs and leaves richer in manurial constituents than older cuttings.

Poison - Tree considered poisonous to rats and other rodents. Powdered seeds, leaves and bark mixed with rice and used as bait for the destruction of pests. Recent investigations show, however, that extracts of leaves, seeds, fruits and roots are not toxic to rats.

Leaves, petioles and bark are reported to possess slight insectcidal activity.

Shade and green manure – The tree’s long, leafy branches make it ideal as a shade tree. It is widely used to shade cocoa, coffee, vanilla and tea. The foliage is rich in nitrogen and the falling leaves enrich the soil beneath the trees. The foliage can also be cut and used to fertilize nearby crops.

Fodder – The leaves contain over 20 per cent crude protein and are nutritious for cattle. (They are, however, toxic to most other animals, including horses.)

Honey – The flowers are a good source of forage for bees. Honey obtained is red yellow in colour with an agreeable taste and aroma.

Seeds - Seeds yield a fatty oil.

Seeds & Bark - Seeds and powdered bark serve as rat/ mice poison.

Miscellaneous Information

Roots, bark and seeds are poisonous. The leaves may also be toxic to humans, although they are eaten in some parts of the tropics. Perhaps cooking inactivates the toxin.

Live fence - Plant easily propagated by cuttings, provided there is ample soil moisture. Even large branches will sprout roots and grow when they are stuck in the ground. So, suitable for live fences and wind-breaks. Used as shade and an ornamental plant.

Ornamental - The tree produces dense masses of attractive white or pink flowers.

Fruit dehiscence is explosive. Seeds are ejected to 40 m.
Widely grown for shade in cocoa plantations.
It serves as living fence.
It is green manure.
Seedlings show mycorrhizal association. This helps the seedlings in successful transplantation and establishment on barren lands or under stressed conditions.
Tree is a nitrogen fixer.
