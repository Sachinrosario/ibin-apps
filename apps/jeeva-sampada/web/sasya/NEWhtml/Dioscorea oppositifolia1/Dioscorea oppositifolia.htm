HABIT & HABITAT                                                                          
A climber with glabrous or finely pubescent stem twining to the right, This species is often confused with D. belophylla. D. oppositifolia is a native of S. India and occur; throughout the hills of Deccan at elevations 2,000-4,000 ft ; it extends to Ceylon, where it occurs in coastal areas also. About 4 varieties are recognised. The tubers descend deep into the soil and much labour is involved in digging them out.

 Generally it is seen along margins of evergreen forest, along slopes of ghats, coastal areas, foot hills, forest edges, forests, Hill forests, Hill slopes, Low levels, moist deciduous forest, Moist forests, open forest, Plains, Reserve forests, Sal forests and Scrub Jungle.
BioticZone: Deccan Plateau, Kanara, Konkan, Konkan plain, West Coast and Western Ghats.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES                                                               
  A climber with glabrous or finely pubescent stem twining to the right,  Leaves alternate, some times opposite. Bulbils absent. Tuber usually single, descending deep into the soil, cylindrical with a few rootlets; skin reddish; flesh white, soft and edible.The tubers descend deep into the soil and much labour is involved in digging them out.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers, roots.

Uses

  1. The roots and the aerial tubers are eaten.
  2. The root, ground and heated, is applied to reduce swellings.
  3. Also used in snake-bite and scorpion sting.
