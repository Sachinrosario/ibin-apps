DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves obtuse at apex, cuneate at base, emarginate, glabrous, short petiolate; stipules peltate, lanceolate, acute. Flowers in fascicles, unisexual, small, greenish -white, apetalous, on the underside of branchlets. Male flowers: perianth lobes 5-6, rotund or obcuneate; stamens 3, filaments in a column. Female flowers: perianth lobes obovate or oblong-obovate, styles 3, free, 2- lobed. Disc glandular. Capsules globose, depressed, smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Leaves are expectorant, diaphoretic. It is useful in strangury and sweats. Seeds have bad taste. It is used as carminative, laxative, astringent to the bowels, tonic to liver, diuretic, diaphoretic. It is also useful in bronchities, earache, griping, opthalmia, ascites. In Southern India an infusion of the leaves is given for headache.


Chemistry: Seed contained 16.3% oil and 14.6% protein; linoleic acid (64.4%) was predominant fatty acid in oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany: Medicine for headache (Leaf, Mishra and Sahu, 1984)
