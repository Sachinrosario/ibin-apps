DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Annual herb  ; Stem: Dark green, more or less hispid, usually glabrous above, branched ; Leaves:  Lowest leaves lyrate, slightly hispid with a large hispid segment, intermediate one, irregularly pinnatifid, upper one lanceolate, all stalked, dark green, margin faintly serrate. Corymbose racemes; Flower:  stalked, small, ebracteate, pedicles shrter than the calyx; Calyx: Sepals 4, narrowly oblong, pale green, spreading almost horizontally. ; Corolla:  Petals 4, alternating with sepals, with anarrow claw and  roundish blade, bright yellow, spreading ;Androecium:  Stamens 6, hypogynous, tetradynamous, the two with shorter filament lateral inserted lower than the others, the four longer filaments placed in pairs antero-posteriorly, anthers versatile; Gynoecium: Pistil oblong-ovoid, tapering into a short style, stigma capitate, bilobed, ovary one-celled, with two parietal placentae, ovules several  ; Fruit:  Siliqua oblong-linear,glabrus, tipped with short tapering beak ; Seed: 3-7 seeds in a single row, brownish red, minutely pitted . Flowering & Fruiting :.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or cattles.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
  
Chemistry:
Sinigrin, sinapine base and a volatile isothiocyanate (0.78%) from Egyptian mustard seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Phenylacetonitrile and 3-phenylpropionitrile isolated from volatile oil (0.26%) from Japanese seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Free and esterified sterols composition of seed oil was found as brassicasterol (19.3, 9.5), campesterol (22.8, 33.6). sitosterol (57.9, 56.2) and 5-dehydro-avenasterol (tr., 0.7% respectively). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Quercetin-3-a-L-(2-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosido-arabinopyranoside) (I) (fig. 1) – isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Verbenone as major component and allylisothiocyanate found in essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

The effect of methanol extracts of Mustard exhibited strong OH-scavenging activity. The OH scavenger of Brassica nigra (brown mustard) was isolated and purified by XAD-2 column chromatography and preparative HPLC, and was identified as a 3,5-dimethoxy-5-hydroxycinnamic acid and methyl ester by MS, 1H-NMR and 13C-NMR. (Chung et al, 1997). MAPA 1997, 9703-1354.
