Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Medium tree introduced into India from Israel. Occurs in medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical), dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical). Arid and semi-arid zones.

Because Acacia tortilis (subspecies raddiana) has grown so well when introduced to Jodhpur in India from Israel, fuelwood plantations are now being established elsewhere in Rajasthan, as well as in other parts on India (for instance, Haryana, Gujrath, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh).

The species grows well in hot, arid climates with maximum temperatures as high as 500 C. the subspecies raddiana grows where minimum temperatures are close to 00C. best adapted to low lands. Thrives where rainfall is up to 1,000 mm. However, depending on the subspecies, it is also extremely drought resistant and can survive in climates with less than 100 mm annual rainfall and long, erratic dry seasons. Tree favours alkaline soils. It grows fairly well in shallow soil less than 0.25 m deep, though it develops long lateral roots that can become nuisance in nearby fields, paths and roadways. In shallow soil, the plants remain shrubby and must be widely spaced to allow for their lateral root growth. It Grows faster, strong light demander, drought resistant, tolerates mild frost. Young seedlings frost tender.

Under extreme arid conditions, it is reduced to a 1 m tall shrub.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tree sometimes with several trunks spreading upwards and outwards like a fountain, supporting a flat-topped umbrella of feathery foliage. Thorns, a distinguishing feature. Leaves pinnate, 1.25-3.75 cm ling with 4-10 pairs of very small leaflets. Fragrant white flowers in clusters or borne singly. Pods yellow-brown, contorted or spiralled like a spring.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Fuel. Acacia tortilis spp. raddiana from Israel has established itself as a fuelwood Plantation in number of Indian states. Calorific value of red heartwood 4,400 kcal/kg. Makes superior quality firewood.

Can be a source of Charcoal in arid regions. Makes superior charcoal.

Wood: The wood is useful for making fencing posts and agricultural implements.

The bark is a rich source of tannin.

Other uses:

When pods fall on ground, prolific pods are used by herbivores as well as sheep and goats. The pods and foliage are eaten by livestock.

Wood also used for fence posts and for small implements used by the rural farmers.

Sand stabilization - Excellent species for sand-dune stabilization. In Rajasthan, it has stabilized over 800 ha of shifting sand dunes.

Miscellaneous Information

 Four distinct subspecies are known in different ecological zones:

  • Subspecies tortilis - Sahel, Middle East
  • Subspecies raddiana - Sudan, Middle East, Sahel
  • Subspecies spirocarpa - Eastern Africa, Sudan
  • Subspecies heteracantha - Southern Africa

Tree is a nitrogen fixer.


The plants are micropropagated.
