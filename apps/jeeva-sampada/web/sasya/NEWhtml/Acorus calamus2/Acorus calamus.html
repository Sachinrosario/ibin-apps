HABIT & HABITAT
Growing wild on the edges of swamps on the banks of rivers and ponds on North America, Europe and Asia .The countries growing this crop commercially are the USSR, central Europe, Rumania, India and Japan. In India growing wild and also cultivated throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 2,200 m. in the Himalayas.
Availability :  

Conservation/ Threat Status : It is commercially cultivated plant.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A semi-aquatic, perennial, aromatic herb with creeping rhizomes growing wild and also cultivated throughout India, Rhizome horizontal, jointed, somewhat vertically compressed, spongy within, 1.25 – 2.5 cm in thickness, pale to dark brown or occasionally orange brown in colour; Leaves: grass like or sword shaped, long and slender; Flowers: small, yellow-green in spadix; Berries: green, angular, 1-3 seeded; Seeds: oblong.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated readily by division fo clumps and of the rhizomes in early spring or at the  commencement of autumn.

Soil type: Clayey loams and alluvial soils are suitable for cultivation

Nutrient requirements:  Farmyard manure and compost are applied and mixed   into the soil before planting.   Urea and NP fertilizers are good for the crop.

Water management: Plant requires annual rainfall between 70-250 cm and during drier season plant must be  irrigated at every 2-3 days interval to keep the soil saturated with water.

Harvesting period / Method: Crop is harvested when the plant becomes 8-9 months old and the leaves start  turning yellow.

Chemical Variability: The variation of the essential oils in A. calamus and their major components with season and producing areas were investigated by steam distn. and internal standard method. The result has shown that June is the best season for A. calamus cropping and that the major components of the volatile oils in the same part of the plant from different producing areas have the same chemical structure type1.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used: Rhizome: aromatic, bitter, carminative, emetic, stimulant, stomachic, useful in dyspepsia, colic, remittent fevers, nerve tonic, in bronchitis, dysentery, epilepsy and other mental ailments, glandular and abdominal tumours and in snake bite; Essential oil: anticonvulsant.
Chemistry:
  • Structure of calacone from oil confirmed by synthesis (Fig. 1). (Vrkoc et al., 1961).
  • Structure of telekin (Fig. 2a) and isotelekin (Fig. 2b) established by chemical degradations. (Vrkoc et al., 1961; Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969).
  • Calarene (Fig. 3) was shown by GLC to contain 9-aristolene (25%) and b-gurjunene (10-aristolene) (75%) (Buchi et al., 1962; Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969).
  • Monocyclic ketones shyobunone (Fig. 4a), epishyobunone (Fig. 4b) and isoshyobunone (Fig. 4c) from essential oil of rhizome. (Iguchi et al., 1968).
  • A new sesquiterpene diol isocalamendiol isolated from essential oil from Japanes plant rhizomes and its structure elucidated; three monocyclic sesquiterpenes-shyobunone, epishyobunone and isoshyobunone and also calamendiol (fig. 5) isolated. (Iguchi et al., 1969).
  • A plausible precursor of isocalamendiol (Fig. 6a), preisocalamendiol (Fig. 6b) was isolated.  (Iguchi et al., 1970).
  • Structure of calamendiol revised and structure of shyobunone, epishyobunone, isoshybunone, isocalamendiol and calamendiol established. (Yamamura et al.,1971).
  • Preisocalamendiol (Fig. 7a), shyobunone (Fig. 7b), and related sesquiterpenes have been synthesized from (-) santonin. (Kato and Hirata, 1970).
  • Two new selinane type sesquiterpenes- acolamone and isoacolamone isolated from rhizomes. (Niwa et al., 1972).                                   
  • A flavone diglycoside isolated and identified as luteolin-6, 8-Cdiglucoside. (Elyashevich et al., 1974); C.A. 1974, 80, 121271z.
  • Structures of three new sesquiterpenes, acoragermacrone (Fig. 8a), acolamone (Fig. 8b), and isoacolamone (Fig. 8c) have been established. In particular acoragermacone is an important precursor of other sesquiterpenes in plants (Niwa et al., 1975).
  • Absolute configuration of epi-and isoshyobunone established (Niwa et al., 1977); C.A. 1978, 88, 105587s.
  • Two new sesquiterpenic ketones calamusenone (Fig. 9a) and isomer of calamusenone (Fig. 9b) of the guaiane type have been isolated from Eastern European sweet flag oil (Acorus calamus) together with a new tropone (Fig. 9c), the structure of which reflects its sesquiterpenic origin. (Rohr et al., 1979).
  • Structure of new sesquiterpenic hydrocarbon-(-) cadala-1, 4, 9-triene (Fig. 10). (Rohr and Naegeli, 1979).
  • Acoradin (Fig. 11a) together with 2,4, 5-trimethoxy benzaldehyde (Fig. 11b), 2, 5-dimethoxybenzoquinone (Fig. 11c), galangin (Fig. 11d) and sitosterol isolated from rhizomes and characterized. (Patra and Mitra, 1979).
  • Essential oil from root stocks of Turkish plant contained a- and b-pinenes, myrcene, camphene, p-cymene, car-3-ene, sapinene, a-terpinene, b-phellandrene, g-terpinene, cis-b-ocimene, limonene, terpinolene and trans-b-ocimene. (Tanker and Sarer, 1979); C. A. 1981, 94, 20225 w.
  • The crystal and molecular structure of epoxyisoacoragermacrone (Fig. 12b), which is and important derivative of acoragermacrone (Fig. 12a) isolated from Acorus calamus L. for the study of the structural organic chemistry and biogenetic reactions, has been determined by the X-ray method. (Veda et al., 1980).
  • Essential oil from rhizomes of Indian plant contained b- asarone (5.5%) whereas commercial European calamus contained 0-8.3%. (Stahl and Keller, 1981), C.A. 1981, 94, 197463y.
  • Acoramone (Fig. 13e) along with isoeugenol methyl ether (Fig. 13a), g-asarone (Fig. 13b), cis-asarone (Fig. 13c), trans-asarone (Fig. 13d) and assarlaldehyde (Fig. 13f) isolated; 13C – NMR of cis and trans – asarones studied. (Patra and Mitra, 1981).
  • (Z, Z) 4, 7-Decadienal isolated from oil and synthesized. (Vanlier et al., 1986), C. A. 1987, 106, 72677f.
  • Two new compounds- (Z) 3-(2, 4, 5-trimethoxyphenyl)-2-propenal (Fig. 14a) and 2, 3-dihydro-4, 5, 7- trimethoxy –1-ethyl-2-methyl-3-(2, 4, 5-trimethomyphenyl) indene (Fig. 14b) isolated from rhizome and their structures elucidated and confirmed by synthesis. (Saxena et al., 1986).
  • g-Asarone (Fig. 15) is synthesized by anodic methoxylation of methyl eugenol. (Vargas et al., 1989).
  • b-asarone (92.68%) determined in rhizome oil (4.5%) by GC-MS. (Nigam et al., 1990), C.A. 1991,144,214153y; (Huang et al., 1993).
  • Structure of and arylindane derivative (I) confirmed by synthesis. (Al, Farhan et al., 1992); C. A. 1982, 116, 194016s.

  • A new phenylpropane4 derivative isolated from rhizomes and characterized as 1-(p-hydroxyphenyl)-1-(O-acetyl)- prop-2-ene. (Chowdhury et al., 1993); C. A. 1994, 120, 27515 e.

Pharmacology:

  • Asarone and b-asarone prolonged hypnotic activity of anaesthetic agents, caused fall in rectal temperature and stoppage of heart at diastole (frog) and showed anticholinergic activity. (Sharma et al., 1961).
  • Both prolonged hypnosis due to pentobarbital and ethanol in mice; LD25 and iproniazid failed to influence this hypnotic potentiating property. They cause significant reduction in rectal temperature of mice and showed no analgesic activity. (Dandiya and Sharma 1962).
  • Both asarone and b-asarone showed cardiac depressant activity moderate hypotensive action in anesthetized dogs and antiacetylcholine activity acute toxicity doses of asarone caused ataxia, hypnosis and loss of righting reflex where as b-asarone failed to induce any of these responses. (Sharma and Dandiya 1962).
  • Essential oil showed spasmolytic activity in animals, which was approximately 10% of that of papaverine. (Jerzy et al., 1966); C. A. 1967, 66, 93653r.
  • Asarone did not produce any change in noradrenaline content of whole brain in rats its sedative effect is due to depression of the ergotropic division of the hypothalamus. (Menon and Dandiya, 1967).
  • Root extract exhibited antimicrobial activity against Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli and Aspergillus niger.  (Vashi and Patel, 1987); C. A. 1987, 107, 151207b.
  • Water-soluble dried powder of alcoholic extract of roots and rhizome of A. calamus L. was used. Plant extract at 10, 20-mg/kg ip did not afford protection to strychnine (1,5,2.5 mg/kg) induced convulsions and same effect was found on acetylcholine induced contractions of rectus muscle except that it inhibited caffeine citrate contractions in frog at 1, 10, 100 microgram/ml doses, it caused negative iono that and chronotropic effects in frogs. Dosages of 10, 25, 50mg/kg ip of herbal extract antagonize spontaneous motor activity and also amphetamine induced hyperactivity in mice.   (Panchal et al., 1989).
  • Activity of  ethanolic extract of A. calamus on central nervous system is similar to a-asarone. (Vohora et al., 1990).
  • Cis-asarone showed antifungal activity against Helminthosporium oryzae. (Saxena et al., 1990); C. A. 1991, 114, 214150v.
  • A decoction of the A. calamus root in combination with 0.25% soln. of anesthesin (1:1) is suggested as alveolitis prophylaxis after surgery. (Kikovatskaya et al., 1993), C. A.  122: 123128t.
  • Sweet flag has been valued for its rhizome and fragrent oils which have been used medicinally in alcoholic leverages, as a fragrant essence in perfumes and oils, and for insecticidal properties. Current research investigates sweet flags value as an insecticidal antibacterial and antifungal agent. (Motley, 1994).
  • A hair growth stimulating preparation comprising hair growth stimulus containing plant extract and other agents for a safe long term synergistic effect has been prepared. (Nishizawa et al., 1996)

·     Bioassays using the insect Tribolium freemani with extract from 43 topical plants showed that the ext. from sweet flag, Acorus calamus promoted pupation of the larvae under crowded condition. The chem. Formula of an active substance of this extract was detected as β - asarone. (Nakakita et al., 1994).

·     A formulation for cleansing of facial skin has been prepared containing A.calamus rhizome oil with other substance. (Horste  et al., 1997)

·     Bubble bath composition containing surfactant and plant extract has been reported which shows good dispersibility in water and prevent skin itching. (Tanaka et al., 1997).

·      Gastroprotective acetylsalicyclic acid tablets comprising 0.01-0.1 parts Rhizome calami and other substances have been prepared. (Perlitz et al., 1997).

·     Compositions containing asarones extracted from A. calamus is used for the prevention and improvement of vision disorder. (Mahira, 1998).

·      Sedative and hypothermic effect was studied in B-asarone, and it was concluded that it is allosteric modulatory agent. (Zanoli et al., 1998).

·      A. calamus has antibacterial activity against.  Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and klebsiella sp. (Rajendhran et al., 1998), B.A. 1999, 106: 63904.

·    Acorus calamus demonstrated strong antifungal but moderate antibacterial activities. (Ahmad et al., 1999).

·      Water and methanolic extract from crude drugs, having A. calamus one of them selected on the basis of traditional Chinese medicine theory, were screened for flavobacterium prolyl endopeptidase (PEP) inhibition. (Tezuka et al., 1999).

·    Ethanolic distillate of various plants (including A. calamus) containing essential acids having 10-14 C atoms is useful in prevention and therapy of hair loss and seborrhea. (Verono, 1999).

·    Male sexual activity enhancing soap containing Acorus calamus has been reported. (Sun, 1999).

  • The aqueous and methanol plant extract shows anti diarrhoeal activity against castor oil induced diarrhoea in mice and significant reduce the time and total weight of the faeces. (Shobha and Thomas, 2001).
  • The CCl4 and ethanolic extract contain flavonoids, which show antibacterial activity  (Chit et al., 2001).
  • Ethyl acetate extract of A. calamus showed high antioxidant activity in 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazy (DPPH) free radical assay. (Acuna, 2002).

Other information:

  • Vapours of A. calamus in different concentrations affected the hatching of eggs of all age groups and moulting of nymph of Dysdercus Koenigii and finally caused death (Saxena and Srivastava, 1972).

 Vapours of A. calamus change the morphology in thermobia domestice. (Saxena and Rohdendorf, 1974).

Vapours of A. calamus induces sterility in male home flies. (Mathur and Saxena, 1975).

·        Chalcone synthase is present in leaves of A. calamus, which is highly expressed in tannin containing idiobalst, as well as in oil cells. (Karwatzki et al., 1993).

·        Acorus calamus is a monocotyledonous wetland plant has developed a complex an aerobic response and can withstand extremely long periods of anoxia. (Bucher and Kuhlemeier, 1993).

  • In vitro effect of asarone of the rhizome of A. calamus showed mobility inhibition and nematocidal activity on second stage larvae of Toxocara canis. (Sugimoto et al., 1995).
  • Bath preparation and or skin cosmetic containing A. calamus extract fermeneted with 0.05-5.0 wt.%kojii or 0.1-1.0 wt. % enzyme at pH 6-7 comprising protein, vitamins, aminoacids, sugars, l ipids, trace elements and other substance has been prepared which are beneficial for skin. (Sakai, 1995)

·        The herb distillaties having (Concn, 1:45-5.5) 68-75, ethanol 18-22, a surfactant 0.3-0.8 and H2O 3.10%. The herb mixtures contain A. calamus 5-10% with other plants is used as refrigerator disinfecting and cleaning agents.

·        (Song et al., 1996). The most active insecticidal ingredient of Indian essential Acorus calamus oil was identified as cis-asarone. (Schmidt et al., 1997), B. A; 105: 174438.

·        Biological activity of essential oils of Acorus calamus adsorbated by natural zeolite has been studied. The volatile substances of A. calamus have been shown to completely inhibit the growth processes of the biotests used. (Petrov, 1998).

·        Essential oils of Acorus calamus (L.) rhizomes show toxic effects on both adult and eggs of Callosobruchus phaseoil (Gyllenhal). (Rahman and Schmidt, 1999).

·        The manufacture of antibacterial silk wool fibre by using hydrolyzate and antibacterial natural substances including A. calamus. (Kitano et al., 1999).

·        Fuel composition containing A. calamus along with other constituents is suitable for domestic cooling with air purifier properties. (Chu and Du, 1994). C.A. 1995, 122, 192202z.

·        A pesticide containg Acorus calamus and other plants for control of oncomelania has been prepared. (Wu and Wu, 1995).

·        A non-tobacco cigarette has been made mainly from Abutilon theophrasti, which was steeped in medicinal herb extract comprising sixteen kinds of Chinese medicinal herbs including Acorus calamus. (Wang, 1995).

·        A. calamus contains b-asarone which is reported to be carcinogenic. A rapid and accurate method using HPLC has been developed, which can be used for detection, monitoring and quantification of b-asarone in A. calamus and herbal preparation containing A. calamus. (Narayana et al., 1995).

·        Sesquiterpenoid isolated from A. calamus rhizome act as germination inhibitor of lettuce seed. These sesquiterpenoids have cadinane, acorane and eudesmane skeletons and their structures were elucidated by spectral evidence. (Nawamaki and Kuroyanagi, 1996).

·        Acorus calamus was described in the Indian “Ayurvedic” literature analyzed for minor and trace elements. (Samudralwar and Garg, 1996).

·        Powders prepared from sweet flag Acorus calamus rhizomes collected at both high (1700 m) and low (900 m) altitude in Eastern Nepal were admixed with wheat at, concentrations in the range 0.05-2% w/w and the wheat infested with Sitophilus oryzae or S granaries adults. Following a 7 day incubation, the mortality of both species was slightly less toxic than that from low altitude. (PaneruI et al., 1997).

·        Volatile substances of essential oil of A. calamus completely inhibited plant growth process in biotests. (Petrov, 1998).

·        Rhizome powder of A. calamus controls the powdery mildew, caused by fungus Erysiphe pisi. (Singh, 1999).

·        A. calamus extract is used in the preparation of health cigarette. (Song, 1999).

·        The root of Acrous calamus   with Vallisneria and cates SP. Were used or the main food for swan goose. (Zhang and Lu, 1999).

·        The inhibitory effects of 46 vegetables and herb extracts on hyaluronidase which is known to be related to inflammation and involved in migration of tumor cells, were examined. (Ippoushi et al., 2000).

·        Forty medicinal plants including A. calamus were studied for their effect on pancreatic lipase activity. (Gowadia and Vasudevan, 2000). 

·        The rhizome of A. calamus is also useful in Agriculture to maintain a functional alpha-amylase under anoxia, (Arpagaus and Sraendle, 2000).
