Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Leaves used in preparation of Local drinks. (Saklani & Jain 1994).
Tender leaves are also added in preparation of choarak. (Huidrom, 1996).
Methods of preparation is given under Allophylus species



Local (V1) and Vellipala (V2) varieties of jackfruit are obtained from local market in Madurai city. Mature sound and ripe jackfruit bulbs are selected. Seeds are removed along with seed coat from bulb. The bulbs (carpet) are cut into pieces, pulped (in a mixie) and passed through hand pulper. Jackfruit juice (500 g) is taken in a stainless steel vessel and sugar (25 g) is added to adjust the TSS to 220 Bx from 180 Bx (Local variety) and 200 Bx (Vellipala). The pasteurization of fruit juice is done by keeping inside the water bath for 30 minutes at 600 C followed by immediate cooling. It is transferred to one litre flat bottom flask (glass) and the starter inoculam (Saccharomyces cerevisiae var. ellipsoids) is added to the sterilized jack fruit pulp to initiate the fermentation (10 percent v/v) which is carried out at 22+ 10 and allowed to ferment for 72 h at 28 + 10 C.

The fermented jackfruit juice (wine) is filtered through filter paper (4 folded thick paper) and poured into a sterilized bottle (cap. 500 ml) by leaving 1” head space and sealed. The bottles can be pasturized in the water bath for 30 minutes at 600 C followed by immediate cooling and then the wine is stored at 5-70 C for ageing (6 months). The steps involved in the preparation of wine is given in Fig.1.

Sound ripe jackfruit bulb

Removing seed with seed coat from bulb

Cutting the bulb into pieces (2 cm)

Pulping in mixie for 10 min.

Adding sugar to adjust 220 Bx.

Pasteurizing for 15 min. (600 C)

Adding starter inoculam (10% v/v)

Fermenting the content (72 hrs)

Filtering wine (4 folded thick filter paper)

Filling in bottles and sealing

Pasteurizing for 30 min. (Water bath)

Cooling (immediately)

Ageing for sex months (6-70 C)

 

Fig. 1: Flow chart for the preparation of wine


The jackfruit wines are organoleptically evaluated before and after ageing (180 days) for various quality attributes such as colour, clarity, aroma, taste and overall acceptability. The fresh wine had an attractive colour (light yellow to deep yellow), cloudy body, yeasty odour and harsh in taste. During ageing the colour changes from highly acceptable to moderately acceptable condition. After clear body, pleasant aroma, and slightly bitter taste. The overall acceptability score values changes from 65.0 to 96.0 percent. Among the wines, the wine prepared from Vellipala is found the have high acceptability scores. Joshi et al., (1990) reported that the wine prepared from custard apple was compared to that prepared from grape in terms of body and taste and found to have high acceptability scores (Kotecha, et al., 1995). The jackfruit is found to be suitable for the preparation of wine and it is comparable with grape wine.
