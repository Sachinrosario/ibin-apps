DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Trees, up to 3 m high, with a long bole and spreading crown. Leaves pari or imparipinnate, usually confined at the tips of branchlets; leaflets ovate-oblong or lanceolate, 7-19 x 3-6.5 cm, acuminate at apex, entire or crenate at margin, base cuneate or oblique, membranous, glabrous. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in terminal panicles. Male flowers: 1.5-2.5 mm long; Sepals 4, ovate-triangular, green. Petals 4, elliptic-oblong, obtuse, white or creamy yellow. Female flowers: 1.5-2.5 mm long; sepals ans petals as in male flower. Fruiting pedicels 1-4 mm long. Follicles single, globose, 5-7 mm across, apiculate. Seeds globose, 4-6 mm across, bluish-black. It flowers and fruits between March to November.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Root bark is diuretic and used in Ayurvedic medicines. Stem bark is bitter, aromatic. Pericarp of unripe fruits tastes like orange rind and is prickled. Dried fruits are used as a condiment especially to add to taste fish curries in Western and Southern India. Seeds are pungent and taste like pepper. In Nagaland, powdered seeds are using for catching fish. An oil known as ‘Mullilam oil’ is extracted from dried mature fruits and used as an antiseptic and disinfectant; it also applied for treatment of cholera and inflammatory dermatosis. Wood is durable and is used as an substitute for sal and teak. It is also used in furniture. In Assam, it is occasionally used for making house posts.
