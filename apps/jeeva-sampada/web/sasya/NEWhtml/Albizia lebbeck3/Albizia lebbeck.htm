HABIT & HABITAT

Siris tree is a large, erect, unarmed, deciduous, spreading tree common all over India, from the plains upto 900m in the Himalayas, and also in the Andamans.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Siris tree attains a clear bole of 6-9m and a girth of 1.8m. The bark is dark brown to greenish black, rough with longitudinal and transverse fissures on outer surface; inner surface whitish with fine longitudinal striations; leaves bipinnate with 8-18 leaflets; inflorescence in glbose heads of greenish yellow flowers; pods yellowish brown, strap-shaped with 6-10 seeds (1).

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
A pollinium is formed by all the microspores. In A. lebbeck degeneration occurs in anthers only after the formation of microspores. The sterility is partly due to the failure in the mechanism of pollination.

Embryo lacks suspensore. Antipodals are persistent and the endosperm shows nuclear type of development.(2)

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
It can be raised by direct sowing and planting out seedlings from nursery. Stump-planting also has proved successful (1).

Floral and seed biology:
Seedling germination : The seeds are sown in the nursery during March-April and matured regularly but moderately and the beds kept well weeded. The young plants are ready for transplanting early in the rainy season (1).

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Several diseases and pests are reported on A. lebbeck. Ravenelia sessilis Berk. attacks leaves and pods. Colletotrichum compactum Ramakr. is recorded on it. Ascohyta saccardiana F. Tassi, Irpex flavus Klotzsch and Sphaerophragmium acaciae (Cooke) P. magnus cause brown spot, white spongy sap rot and brown rust respectively. Several insects of the orders Coleoptera, Lepidoptera and Hemiptera attack A. lebbeck (1).

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
WOOD :

The wood is light, coarse textured and has broadly and shallowly interlocked grain. The sapwood is white or yellowish white, and the heartwood is dark brown, streaked with dark and white shades. The tree is a good substitute for teak after suitable seasoning and preservative treatment. The wood gives writing and printing paper (1).

MEDICINAL :

The plant is reported to have antiseptic, antidysenteric and anti-tubercular properties. The bark has acrid taste. It is recommended for bronchitis, leprosy, paralysis and helminth infections. The bark and seeds are astringent, useful in piles and diarrhoea, and act as tonic and restorative. The root bark and root gum are used as dental powder for strengthening the gums. Extract of the bark possesses anthelmintic activity and expectorant action (1). A decoction of the bark and flowers protects the guinea pig against histamine as well as acetylcholine induced bronchospasm. Prolonged treatment with bark decoction protects the sensitized guinea pigs against antigen challenge. The seeds contain saponins having anti-ovulatory properties (1).

Parts Used:

Wood, bark, leaves, seeds, flowers, petioles, pods.

Chemistry:
Analysis of the heartwood gave : ether extr., 0.96; hot water extr, 14.4; ash 1.5; pentosans, 20.0; lignin, 22.0; holocelluose, 76.9; and L-cellulose, 45.6%. The heartwood contains melanoxetin, okanin, leucopelargonidin and (+) pinitol, lebbecacidin and a stereoisomer, melacacidin. Analysis of the leaves gave the following range of values (dry wt. Basis) : dry matte, 36.8-44.2; crude protein, 20.1-21.1; ether extr, 8.5-16.0; crude fiber, 17.6-19.0; - N free extr, 33.5-43.7; total carbohydrates, 52.5-61.3; total ash, 10.1-10.4; calcium 3.6-4.3; and phosphorus, 0.03-0.04%. The leaves are reported to contain caffeic acid, alkaloids, kaempferol and quercetin.

Analysis of the seeds gave (dry wt. Basis) : crude protein 39.5; ether extr, 6.8; N-free extr, 45.3; crude fiber, 4.2; total ash,4.2%, calcium, 172.0; phosphorus, 620.0; iron 11.5; niacin, 5.2; and ascorbic acid, 1.75 mg/100g. The seeds are rich in proteins and can possibly be included in animal diets.

The flowers on steam distillation gave a colour less sweet - smelling oil. On fractionation, the oil yielded p-nitrobenzoate, benzyl alcohol and benzoic acid. The residue gave lupeol (1).

Processing Techniques:
The wood gives 50% yield of bleached pulp by the sulphate process, suitable for writing and printing paper. Since the wood is short-fiberd, admixture of its pulp with 30 percent of bamboo pulp may be necessary for the production of paper on a commercial paper machine (1).
Other uses:

The tree is planted to chack soil erosion, as shade in coffee and cardamom plantations and as green manure. It is planted along roadsides and in gardens.

A reddish brown gum exudes from the bark and dries into stalactiform masses. It has a smooth polished surface. It gelatinises with a large amount of water and is not completely soluble. It is used as an adulterant of gum arabic and sometimes as its substitute.

The seeds, leaflets, petioles and pods are toxic to cotton stainer adults, whereas bark, root and seeds are toxic to melonworm larvae.

The leaves are nutritious and palatable, and can be sued as fodder (1). Kalidasa noted women wearing its flowers (400.A.1) (3).
