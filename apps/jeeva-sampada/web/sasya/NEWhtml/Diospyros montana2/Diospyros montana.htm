Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

small or middle deciduous sized tree, sometimes armed distributed throughout the greater part of india, but nowhere very common.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Small or middle sized tree, sometimes armed, young branches softly pubescent. Leaves ovate-oblong or elliptic, subacute or obtusely acuminate, more or less softly puberulous or tomentose when young, at length glabrous; base rounded, acute, or sometimes truncate.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers dioecious, in cymes which are axillary or along the old branches, or sometimes (the female often) reduced to a single flower.

Male flower: in short, few flowered panicles, pedicels bracteate at the base, bracts ovate, acute, strongly ciliate, buds ovoid, acute. Calyx 4-lobed, glabrous outside and inside; lobes broadly ovate, acute or 4 times as long as the calyx, lobes the little shorter than the tube. Stamens 16, in 2 rows of 8, in opposite pairs united at the base, the outer the longest, anthers lanceolate, usually with the long slender awn, glabrous. Rudimentary ovary fleshy, 4 acute teeth at the apex.

Female flower: axillary, solitary; pedicels nodding, stout, with two small ovate bracts a little above the middle. Calyx glabrous, lobes ¼ in. long, glabrous inside, ovate-oblong, obtuse. Corolla as in the male. Staminodes 4. Ovary globular, glabrous, 8-celled, ovule globose, supported by the persistent calyx whose lobes are slightly enlarged and reflexed, reddish-brown.

Seeds 2 - 8.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology: Flowers January – April.


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Under natural conditions seed germination occurs in the rainy season. Seedlings stand a fairly dense shade and under favourable conditions they persist until they establish themselves. The plant can be propagated from seeds; seedlings when about 1 ft. high are transplanted on fairly good soil; rocky land with deep sub-soil appears to be suitable. The plant coppices well and may be planted 10-15 ft. apart in fuel plantations.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

 

Argaronia laticostalis Guen., Grammodes goemetrica F., Hypocala biarcuata Wlk., H. moorei Butler, H. rostrata,F., H. subsatura Guen., Prodenia litura F. (defoliators) and Stromatium barbatum F. (borer) are the insect pests associated with the species. Meliola diospyri Syd., an ascomycete fungus, is reported on the leaves of the plant.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:Wood classified as good fuel wood. Calorific value : Sapwood 5,125 cal., 9,225 B.t.u.

Wood: Wood is greyish in colour, often tinged with yellow or brown, streaked with narrow darker bands, particularly towards the center and fine grained. It is soft to moderately hard, strong and heavy (wt. 44-50 lb./ cu.ft.). It takes good polish and used for making small articles of furniture. Also used for carts, agricultural implements and indoor work. Suitable for rafters, matches and carvings. Useful timber.
