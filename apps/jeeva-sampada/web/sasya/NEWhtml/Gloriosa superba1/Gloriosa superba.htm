Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

The plant is a handsome herbaceous annual climber with underground cylindrical white tuberous rhizome, growing upto 1,800 m in low forests.
Availability :

Throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

The root-stock is solid, fleshy yellow, cylindric tubers which are 15-30 cm long and pointed at each end. The stem is about 3-6 m long bearing parallel veined, stalkless leaves which are placed opposite to each other. The tips of the leaves behave like tendrils, clining on to neighboring palnts or other object.
Scandent. Leaves alternate, oblong-lanceolate, to 12 x 3.5 cm, base cordate, ± amplexicaul, apex acuminate, subsessile, tendrilled. Flower(s) bisexual, to 9 cm wide, either 1 and axillary, or subcorymbose at the ends of branchlets; pedicel to 12 cm, apex reflexed. Perianth lobes 6, oblong-lanceolate, 6 x 1 cm, reflexed or spreading, subequal, base narrow, margin crisply wavy, apex acuminate. Stamens 6, spreading; filaments to 4.5 cm; anthers 1 cm, versatile, slightly curved. Ovary oblong, 3-celled; ovules infinite per cell, on axile placentae; style to 4.5 cm, deflexed, apex 3-fid; stigmas 3. Capsule ellipsoid-oblong, to 5 x 2.5 cm, torulose; seeds many, globose, 4 mm, warty. The climbing Lily, the best known species, is a tall, weak-stemmed, slender deciduous tuberous rooted indigenous creeping plant, supporting itself by means of tendril-like prolongations of the leaves. Gloriosa can be grown pots or in the ground. They are planted just before the rains, one rhizome being put into a 20 cms. Pot. Good drainage and light rich soil and plenty of water during growth are necessary, the pot should be furnished with a globe over which the plant creeps and blooms. The root is poisonous. Flowers remain fresh for about a week on the plant.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
It is propagated by seeds or division of the Turmeric-like rhizomes.

Phenology:

Flowers red, showy, in peak during Nov.-Jan. Flowers at the end of stems during the rainy season in great profusion; they are peculiar, with long twisted or wavy crisped petals, reflexed after the manner of Cyclmens, which are light yellow in one half and crimson as they become old. The foliage dies down with the approach of the cold season and the plants remain dormant till the next rainy season.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
The Glory Lily is truly glorious as it produces eye-catching, multicolored and uniquely structured flowers which remain on the plant for at least 7 days. These flowers which are large, axillary or solitary have long extrose filaments which are nearly 13 mm. The petals or perianth of the flowers have wavy margins and the colours from the tips progress to base, from green onto yellow passing through orange and scarlet to crimson. Gloriosa superba is medicinally important plant.
