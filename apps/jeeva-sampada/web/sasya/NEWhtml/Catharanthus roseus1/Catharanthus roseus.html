HABIT & HABITAT
Perennial herbs or under shrubs, erect, much branched, woody at base, 10-75 cm high, rarely up to 1 m high. It is found to occur throughout India. Also cultivated in the garden.
Availability :  Plenty, Throughout India

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem and branches pale green or reddish, tetrangular, glabrous. Leaves shortly petiolate, elliptic-obovate or oblanceolate, 2-10 x 1-4 cm, acute or acuminate or obtusely apiculate at apex, attenuate or cuneate at base, minutely scabrid along margin, glossy above, pale beneath, glabrous, lateral nerves 10-12 pairs; petiole c 1.0-1.5 cm long, base 2-glandular. Flowers solitary or paired, axillary, white or pink or rose-purple, 2.5-4.0 cm in diam., pubescent outside, pilose within except anther level. Calyx c 5 mm long; lobes 5, subequal, filiform, 6 mm in length, acuminate at apex. Corolla salver-shaped, swollen near the base, 4 cm across; tube 2-3 cm long; lobes 5, ovate-triangular or obovate, c 2 cm long, obtusely apiculate or mucronate at apex; throat pilose. Stamens 5; anther 3 mm long, sagittate. Ovary 5 mm long; style 3 cm long. Follicles a pair, slender, 2.5-5.0 x 3 mm, striate, straight, glabrous, compressed. Seeds oblong, 3 x 1 mm, both ends rounded.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed  biology:
Inflorescence   axillary dichasial cyme or solitary axillary; Flowers are Ebracteate, pedicellate, complete   actinomorphic, hermaphrodite, pentamerous, hypogynous and cyclic; Calyx sepals 5, polysepalous, valvate, persitent;    Corolla   petals 5, gamopetalous, twisted, corolla hypocrateriform, purple; Androecium stamens 5, polyandrous, inserted at the mouth of corolla tube, epipetalous, dithecous, dorsifixed, introrse; Gynoecium bicarpellary, syncarpous, ovaries are free and superior, placentation marginal, style long, stigma drum-shaped and sticky , two ligulate hypogynous nectories are present on anterior and posterior side of the ovary; Fruit   etaerio of follicles; Seeds small and black in colour. 

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Pollinated by insects and seeds are dispersed by breaking of follicles.
Genetic variability:
There are three variants of C. roseus(2n =16) I) Rose purple flowered, II) White flowered and III) White flowered with rose purpled spot in the center.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant can grow in any type of soil except those   which are highly saline,alkaline or water logged. Light soil which are rich in humus are preferable for large scale cultivation.

Nutrient requirements: Farmyard manure, horse gram, sun hemp are applicable before sowing. A basal dose of 250 kg super phosphate, 65 kg muriate and 110 kg of urea are beneficial.

Water management:   A well distributed rainfall of 100 cm or more is ideal, however 4-5 irrigations once in 15 days during February, March and April are needed to get optimum yield.

Harvesting period / Method:  The crop is harvested after about 12 months of planting. Leaves are first harvested after 6 month then 9 months and third time leaves are obtained when whole plant is harvested.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Uses:
The whole plant particularly the root bark contains alkaloids which have hypotensive, sedative and tranquilising properties. It is used as a folk remedy for diabetes. The root is toxic, bitter, acrid, stomachic and tonic. The juice of the leaves is good for wasp-stings and menorrhagia. The ‘vincristine’ alkaloid obtained from this plant is useful in some kinds of leucaemia.
