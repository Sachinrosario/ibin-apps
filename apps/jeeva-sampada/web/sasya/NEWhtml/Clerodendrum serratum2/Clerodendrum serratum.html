HABIT & HABITAT
Shrub, 1-3 m tall. Stems 4 angled, scarcely woody; young parts usually glabrous. It is found in Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves opposite or ternate, ovate or elliptic, 11-22 x 6-13 cm, subsessile, acute at apex, acute at base, coarsely and sharply serrated, glabrous; petioles very stout, c 6 mm long. Flowers numerous in terminal, pyramidal panicle, c 30 cm long, pedicels often twisted; bracts ovate to lanceolate, 1.3-3.8 cm long, subpersistent, often coloured. Calyx cup-shaped, truncate, 5 mm long, puberulous; lobes small, triangular, acute, ciliolate, companulate. Corolla pale blue or bluish-purple, 7-9 cm long; limbs bilabiate, unequally 5-lobed, hairy within at insertion of the stamens, oblique at mouth, 2 upper and 2 lateral lobes elliptic, obtuse, flat, spreading. Filaments much curved, densely hairy at base. Ovary and style glabrous. Drupe 6 mm long, 2-4 lobed, dark purple or black at maturity; fruiting calyx cupuliform, somewhat accrescent.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Numerous, showy in lax pubescent dichotomous cymes; Flower: Bracteate, pedicellate, complete, hermaphrodite, regular ; Calyx: Puberulous, cup shaped, truncate not enlarged in fruit, lobes very small, triangular, acute; Corolla: Glabrous outside, pale blue, the larger lower lobe dark bluish/ purple, tube cylindric hairy;Androecium: Stamens 4, didyanamous, much exserted, anthers ovate, cells parallel, filaments much curved, densely hairy ; Gynoecium: Ovary imperfectly 4-celled, ovule solitary in each cell, style filiform, stigma shortly bifid; Fruit: Drupe somewhat succulent, broadly obovoid, 4 lobed with one pyrene in each lobe; Seed: Oblong. Flowering & Fruiting : August – sepetember .

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Roots have a pungent, bitter, acrid taste. In Ayurveda system of medicine roots are  used as stomachic, anthelmintic. It is also useful in bronchitis, asthma, ozoena, tumerous, burning sensation, hiccough, epilepsy, tuberculous glands, wounds inflammation and fever. In Unani system of medicine roots are used in appetite and lessens expectoration. It is also useful in inflammation, bronchitis, asthma and fever. Seeds are used as laxative.


Chemistry:
Alcoholic extract and saponin isolated from root bark caused release of histamine from lung tissue. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Glucose and D-(-)mannitol from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Hydolysis of crude saponin from bark yielded oleanolic acid, queretaroic acid and new serratagenic acid (fig. 1). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Two new diterpenoid glucosides, cleroserroside A and Cleroserroside B, were isolated from the aerial parts of Clerodendrum serratum Var. amplexifolium moldenke. (Yang et al, 1999). C.A 2000, 132, 149036t.

From the stem of Clerodendrum serratum b-sitosterol, 24(S)-Et cholesta-5,22,25-trien-3b-ol, 5-hydroxy-7,4’-dimethoxy flavone, luteolin, apigenin, scutellarien and ursolic acid were isolated. (Jaya et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 47670z.

Two new compounds, 7-O-m-coumaroyl-oxyugandoside and 19b-D-glucopyranosyloxyl-labl13(E)-en-8a,15-diol, were isolated from the Clerodendrum serratum. (Chen et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 28687q.

Two new iridoid glucosides, 7b-coumaroyloxyuganoloside (fig. 2a) and 7b-cinnamoyloxyuganoside (fig. 2b) were isolated from leaves. (Wei et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 117494j.

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for anasarca (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); appetite (Root, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973); Body ache (Root, Tarafder, 1986); Cholera, cuts, dropsy (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Cough (Root, Jain et al, 1973); eye disease (Leaf, Das et al, 1983; Baruah and Sarma, 1987); Fever (Root, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Tarafder, 1986; Singh and Singh, 1985; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Malaria (Root, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985); Rheumatism (Root, Baruah and Sarma, 1987; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Ulcers, wounds (Leaf, Goel et al, 1984); Edible as vegetable (Flower, Vartak, 1959; Leaf, Vartak, 1981; Rao et al, 1981); Local drinks (Root, Goel et al, 1984; Chaudhuri et al, 1977)
Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:
 Saponins caused disruption of mast cells of rat mesentery which was proportional to dose upto 40mg and maximum disruption effect was exerted in 30 min. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
