HABIT & HABITAT
Biennial herb, slender, 30-100 cm tall. It is Kashmir, Uttar Pradesh between 3000- 4000 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves oblong to oval, 8-15 x 3-8 cm, pinnately decompound, ultimate division linear, entire or divided; petiole slender, 5-13 cm long, scarious-sheathing; cauline leaves pinnately dissected, reduced upward, the upper sessile with broadly dilated, scarious-margined sheath. Peduncles slender, 4-12 cm long. Involucre and involucel of a few filiform bracts or bracteoles or commonly lacking. Rays 3-13, spreading ascending, unequal, 1-5 cm long. Umbellets 2-15 flowered, mature peduncles unequal, 2-13 mm long. Flowers white or pinkish; stylopodium conical. Fruits oblong, 3-5 x 2-2.5 mm, ribs prominent, filiform.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence: Terminal few flowered racemes ; Flower: Bracteate, pedicillate, complete, viscous hairy ; Calyx: hairy, segment oblong, subequal, obtuse ; Corolla: Petals, obovate, cuneate, reddish-yellow, tender, veined ;Androecium: Stamens 5, equal, all perfect. Gynoecium: ovary densely bristy with long hairs, at the bottom of the calyx, ovules many, style short stigma many ; Fruit: Pods ligulate, nearly straight, oblique, compressed, clothed with bristle hairs.  ; Seed: 4-6, trapezoid-ovoid, black shining . 
   
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : September.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda the seeds are used as antiperiodic, antidysenteric, carminative disease of head, lecucoderma, dysentery, abdominal disorder and tumours. In Unani system of medicine the seeds are used in carminative, stomachic, diuretic, expectorant, pectoral, tonic and anthelmintic. It is also useful in cooling brain, astringent to bowel. The burn powder is used in piles. A caraway bath is recommended for painful swelling of the womb and poultice for painful protruding piles and anthelmintic. It is used in inflammation “Khapha”.

 

Chemistry:
·        Trans- and cis- carveol separated from caraway oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·        Lipids from roots contained glyceryl esters of saturated and unsaturated fatty acids, phytofluene, b-sitosterol, umbelliferone and scopoletin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·        (+)carvone and (+)limonene comprised 87.5% of essential oil from seed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

·        Detection of a-pinene, camphene, b-pinene, myrcene, 3-carene, limonene, g-terpinene, p-cymene, cadinene, myristicin, carveol acetate, 4-terpineol and perillyl alcohol in essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

·        p-Cymene and cuminaldehyde identified in seed essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Lipids (10.5%) isolated from seeds contained hydrocarbons (0.2), triacylglycerols (66.0), waxes (0.1), free fatty acids (5.1), free alcohols (tr.), sterols (0.4) and chlorophyll (0.1%); petroselinic acid in both free and esterified forms also present in seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·        Optically pure (S)(+)carvone isolated from fruit essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·        Germacrene –D and b-elemene as major constituents in seed oil of Israeli plant; oil content of seeds increased rapidly with maturity of plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·        Isolation of three monoterpenes, anethofuran, carvone, and limonene from the Bioassay-directed fractionation of caraway oil. Their structures were determined on the basis of spectral analysis. These compounds induced the detoxifying enzyme glutathione S-transferase in several mouse target tissues. The alpha,beta-unsaturated ketone system in carvone appeared to be critical for the high enzyme-inducing activity. (Zheng . et al, 1992)

·        Dried fruits of caraway C. carvi were analyzed with on-line supercritical fluid extraction- gas chromatography using carbon dioxide at 50°C and 9.7 MPa of pressure. The solutes were cryogenically collected onto DB-1-capillary GC column and the effluent was vented out. The compounds were subsequently eluted from the collection column with helium onto the head of the analytical DB-5 capillary column and cryofocused with liquid nitrogen. The volatiles were detected by flame ionization detection and identified by mass spectrometry. (Kallio et al., 1994). MAPA 9503-1987.

·        The biosynthesis of the monoterpenes limonene and carvone in the fruit of caraway (Carum carvi L.) proceeds from geranyl diphosphate via a three-step pathway. First, geranyl diphosphate is cyclized to (+)-limonene by a monoterpene synthase. Second, this intermediate is stored in the essential oil ducts without further metabolism or is converted by limonene-6-hydroxylase to (+)-trans-carveol. Third, (+)-trans-carveol is oxidized by a dehydrogenase to (+)-carvone. To investigate the regulation of monoterpene formation in caraway, we measured the time course of limonene and carvone accumulation during fruit development and compared it with monoterpene biosynthesis from [U-14C]Suc and the changes in the activities of the three enzymes. The activities of the enzymes explain the profiles of monoterpene accumulation quite well, with limonene-6-hydroxylase playing a pivotal role in controlling the nature of the end product. In the youngest stages, when limonene-6-hydroxylase is undetectable, only limonene was accumulating in appreciable levels. The appearance of limonene-6-hydroxylase correlates closely with the onset of carvone accumulation. At later stages of fruit development, the activities of all three enzymes declined to low levels. Although this correlates closely with a decrease in monoterpene accumulation, the latter may also be the result of competition with other pathways for substrate. (Boumeester et al., 1998).

·        From the water-soluble portion of the methanolic extract of caraway (fruit of Carum carvi L.), an aromatic compound, an aromatic compound glucoside and a glucide were isolated together with 16 known compounds. Their structures were clarified as 2-methoxy-2-(4'-hydroxyphenyl)ethanol, junipediol A 2-O-b-D-glucopyranoside and L-fucitol, respectively. (Matasmura et al., 2002)a.

Nine monoterpenoids related to carvone and seven glucosides were isolated from the water-soluble portion of the methanolic extract of the caraway (fruit of Carum carvi L.), and their structures were clarified by spectral investigation. Among them, eight monoterpenoids and six glucosides were new. (Matasmura et al., 2002)b.

Pharmacology:

·        Thirty two patients with duodenal ulcer and gastroduodenitis, were treated with concomitant obstipation syndrome. The obstipation syndrome was treated with a combination of Carum carvi along with some other herbs. The laxative herb combination was effective in 100 per cent of the patients--daily defecation in 90.6 per cent and every second day in 9.4 per cent of the patients was attained. No effect on gastric mucosa was established as well as on the clinical effect of the main disease and on the percentage of the ulcer niche healing. (Matev et al., 1981)

·        Carveol showed antiasthmatic and antianaphylactic effects in guinea pigs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

·        Commercial oleoresins from Carum carvi were evaluated as antioxidants for limonene oxidation. Oleoresins from C. carvi exhibited weak antioxidant properties. (Lee and Widmer, 1994).MAPA 9686-3853.

·        Zeera-e-siyah (Carum carvi) was tested in 30 cases of Hurqat-e-Meda, in the dose of 6mg in two equal divided dosage for 6 weeks. At the end of the study the relief in pain in epigastrium, nausea and vomiting, distention of abdomen, water brash, burning in epigastrium, insomnia due to pain and tenderness in epigastrium was found in and 70.38% of cases respectively. (Siddiqui et al., 1994). MAPA 9505-2920.

·        The efficacy and safety of a standardized combination of peppermint oil (Mentha piperita 90mg) and caraway oil (Carum carvi 50mg) were investigated in a randomized double blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial in patients with non-ulcer dyspepsia. The primary variables of the patients who received peppermint oil/ caraway oil were significantly improved after 4 weeks of treatment compared to the placebo group (p= 0.015, p = 0.008; U-test). The same improvement could also be demonstrated for the other gastro-intestinal symptoms such as abdominal distention and eructation. The tolerance of the drug proved to be excellent. (May et al., 1996/97) MAPA 9803-1425.

·        Methanol extracts of Carum carvi (fruit and root) was screened along with other plants for polyacetylenic compounds using the ELISA for panaxytriol, and their antiproliferative activity was checked by MTT assay using the tumor cell lines MK-1, HeLa and B16F10. The presence of antiproliferative polyacetylenes was suggested. (Nakano et al., 1998).

·        The effect of enteric-coated (Enteroplant) and non-enteric-coated preparations containing a peppermint-caraway oil combination with 90mg peppermint (Mentha piperita) oil and 50 mg caraway (Craum carvi) oil was studied on gastro-duodenal motility with stationary manometry in six healthy volunteers. Both enteric-coated and non-enteric-coated preparations showed effects on the migrating motor complex (MMC); a decreases in the number of contractions and contraction amplitudes during the various phases of the MMC; non-enteric-coated preparations showed their effects mainly during the first MMC after administration; and enteric coated preparations showed their effects temporally delayed during the second MMC after administration. Enteric-coated and non-enteric-coated peppermint-caraway oil combinations have been reported to be safe preparations acting locally to cause smooth muscle relaxation. (Micklefield et al., 2000). MAPA 2000-03-1504.

Extracts from the plants Carum carvi singly or in combination with other herbs, in the form of a commercial preparation, STW 5 (Iberogast) and a modified formulation, STW 5-II, is tested for their potential anti-ulcerogenic activity against indometacin induced gastric ulcers of the rat as well as for their antisecretory and cytoprotective activities. It produced a dose dependent anti-ulcerogenic activity associated with a reduced acid output and an increased mucin secretion, an increase in prostaglandin E2 release and a decrease in leukotrienes. The effect on pepsin content was rather variable and did not seem to bear a relationship with the anti-ulcerogenic activity. The most beneficial effects were observed with the combined formulations STW 5 and STW 5-II in a dose of 10 ml/kg b.w., comparable with cimetidine in a dose of 100 mg/kg b.w. The anti-ulcerogenic activity of the extracts was also confirmed histologically. The cytoprotective effect of the extracts could be partly due to their flavonoid content and to their free radical scavenging properties. (Khayyal et al., 2001).
