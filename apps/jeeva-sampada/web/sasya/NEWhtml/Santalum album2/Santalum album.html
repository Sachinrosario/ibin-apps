 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A small to medium sized evergreen semiparasitic tree. Found in dry regions of India from the Vindhya ranges southwards; especially common in Karnataka and Tamilnadu at 1200m.
Availability :  

Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Due to over exploitation plants occurs only in the restricted areas. It is also grown in gardens.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A small to medium sized evergreen semiparasitic tree, with slender branches, sometimes reaching upto 18m, in height and 2.4m in girth.  Bark reddish or dark gray or nearly black, rough, with deep vertical cracks on old trees; leaves glabrous, thin elliptic-ovate or ovate-lanceolate, 1.5-8 cm X 1.6-3.2 cm, sometime longer; flowers, straw-coloured, brownish purple, reddish purple, or violet, unscented, in terminal and axillary paniculate cymes; drupe globose, 1.3 cm. diam. purple-black, with hard, ribbed endocarp; seeds globose or oboroid.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence axillary or terminal panicle cymes; Flower straw coloured, brownish purple to violet, unscented, bracts  minute; Perianth tubular, tube oval in shape, connate at the base of ovary 4-lobed,lobes triangular acute. Disks faintly 5-lobed, inferior, alternate with stamens. Ovary 1-celled, inferior;  Androecium stamens 4,opposite,filaments short ,inserted  in the mouth of  perianth alternating with the erect lobes, anthers too short, two-celled, introrse; Gynoecium  ovary  half, inferior, tapering one-celled, an erect central placenta, rising from the base and not reaching to the top  to the summit of  which are attached 3 or 4 pendulous ovules without the usual coverings ,style filiform, stigma small 3or 4 lobed on a level with the anthers ;  Fruit  globose drupe with hard ribbed endocarp; Seed  globose or ovoid; 
Floral and seed biology:

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Plants are pollinated by birds  or insects and seeds are dispersed by mechanical means.
 
Genetic variability:
 Plant has chromosome number 2n=20.Tetraploids has also been  produced with delayed flowering. Variation is seen in  number of  leaves, types of leaves, resistance to spike diseases and seed morphology

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is mainly propagated through seeds . However, it can also be propagated through root cuttings and  grafting.

Soil type: Plant is adapted to wide variety of soil and grows on clay to sandy loam soil and in Karnataka plant is found   growing in laterite soil.

Nutrient requirements: Plants responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers.

Water management:  Plant responds well in the areas with well distributed rainfall .

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is harvested for production of oil when the tree reaches a minimum age of 30 yrs.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used:  Stem-bark-antimalarial; wood-febrifuge, diaphoretic, anti-inflammatory, in head ache; wood oil-stimulant, antigonorrhoic, expectorant, as urinary antiseptic in dysuria, in bronchitis, gleet, tuberculosis of gall bladder; part of wood, oil from seed and stem-bark- in skin diseases.


Chemistry:
NBPGR: Leaves contain a peptide-γ-L-glutamyl-5- (trans-1-propenyl)-L-cysteine sulfoxide; a polyamine-sym-homospermidine, aminoacids-cis-and trans-4-hydroxy-L-proline; glycoprotein-hydroxyproline-containing protein.  Bark contains a triterpene-cis-12-en-3-β-yl-palmitate.  Chief constituents of essential oil from heartwood are α and β- santalol.  Other constituents include sesquiterpene hydrocarbons –α-β, epi-β santalene, α and β-aurcumene and β-farnesene.  Dihydroagarofuran is also present in the essential oil.
