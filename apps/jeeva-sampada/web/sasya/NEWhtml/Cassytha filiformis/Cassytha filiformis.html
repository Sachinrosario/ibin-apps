DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Parasitic, leafless, herbs. Leaf long, cord-like, matted together, glabrous or hairy, branched, green in colour. Flowers small, sessile, in spikes, 1-35 cm long. Perianth twice as long as  bracteoles. Fruit globose, c 6 mm in diam., smooth, white, enclosed in fleshy perianth tube.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Lateral divericate spikes; Flower: Sessile, hermaphrodite, small, bracteolate,  ; Perianth:  Twice as long as bracteoles, tube short, segments 6 in 2 rows, the outer segments short, rounded , ciliate, the inner one oblong, acute, concave, valvate ; Androecium:  Perfect stamens 9 or 6, filaments of first row eglandular with introrse 2-celled anthers, those of second row similar or sometimes reduced to staminodes, those of third row with 2-glandular filaments and extrorse anthers, fourth row is replaced by sessile staminodes; Gynoecium:  Ovary globose, free, closely covered by enlarged perianth in fruit, stigma sub-sessile, small; Fruit: Drupe, white, globose, smooth, enclosed in fleshy perianth tube and crowned with erect perianth segments ; Seed:  With thin testa and cotyledons ultimately confluent.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
The plant is substitute of  Cuscuta  reflexa (Ayurveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933). Plant powder with Sesamum  oil is used to strengthen  hair; mixture of butter and ginger with it used to clean inveterate ulcers. Juice mixed with sugar is used in inflamed eyes. The plant is used in dysentry, rickets, gonorrhoea and leucorrhoea.


Chemistry:
13 alkaloids were isolated; of these, structures of two new oxoporphine bases estanlished – cassamedine (fig. 1a) as 1,2,9,10-bis(methylenedioxy)-3-methoxydibenzo[de,g]quinolin-7-one and cassameridine (fig. 1b) as 1,2,9,10-bis(methylenedioxy)-3-hydroxydibenzo-[de,g]quinolin-7-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

struicture of two new base – cassyfiline – from Formosan plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Isolation and structures of new aporphine bases – cassythine (cassyfiline), m.p. 217°C and cassythidine (fig. 2), m.p. 206°C. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Isolation of an alkaloid – nantenine – and its synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Using a bioassay – direct fractionation method, three new compounds including an Aporphine alkaloid Cassyformine, an oxamporphine alkaloid Filiformine and a lignan (+)-diasyringaresinol were isolated. (Chang et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 38760t.

Bioassay directed fractionation from the MeOH extract of the fresh herb of Cassytha filiformis led to the isolation and characterization of six aporphinoid alkaloids, actinodaphnin, N-methylactinodaphnin, cathafiline, cathaformine, predicentrine and ocoteine. (Wu et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 63638.

Cathafiline, Cathafarmine, actinodaphnine, N-methylactinodaphnine, predicentrine and ocotrine exhibited significant antiplatelet aggregation activity. (Chang et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 38760t.

They showed potent inhibitory activity of rabbit platelet aggregation induced by ADP, arachidomi acid, collogen and platelet-activating factory (PAF). Alkaloid also exhibited strong inhibition of aortic contraction induced by K+ (80 mM) and norepinephrine (3 mM). (Wu et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 63638.
