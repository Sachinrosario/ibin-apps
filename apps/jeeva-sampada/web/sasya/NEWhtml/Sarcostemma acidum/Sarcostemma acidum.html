HABIT & HABITAT
Trailing leafless herbs. Stem stout, 3.0-5.5 mm in diam., green, glabrous. It is found in Deccan Peninsula, Bihar, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Flowers in sessile, many flowered, terminal, umbelliform cymes, waxy, fragrant; bracts minute, lanceolate. Calyx divided to the base or nearly so; segments ovate-oblong, 1.2 mm long, subacute, with membranous margins. Corolla wreenish-white, campanulate; lobes ovate-oblong, 1.0-1.4 cm long, revolute; outer corona 5-lobed; inner corona-scales with strongly inflexed apices. Follicles lanceolate, 10-12 x 0.7 cm, patering at both ends. Seeds ovate, 3-5 x 1.0-2.5 mm, flattened; coma 2 cm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry: a-amyrin acetate, lupeol, b-amyrin, acetate and b-sitosterol from twigs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
A new nonreducing disaccharide - sarcobiose (fig. 1) isolated from dried twigs and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Isolation and structure determination of new disaccharide - brevobiose (fig. 2) from dried twigs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Tigmobiose (fig. 3) isolated from dried twigs and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.
New pregnane ester diglycoside - brevinine (fig. 4b)- isolated from twigs and its structure elucidated as 11-O-benzoylsarcogenin-3-O-a-L-diginopyranosyl(1®4)-a-L-diginopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Another pregnane ester glycoside - brevine (fig. 4a) also isolated from twigs and characterized as 11-O-benzoylsarcogenin-3-O-a-L-diginopyranosyl(1®4)-O-a-L-diginopyranosyl(1®4)-O-a-L-diginopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
New pregnane genin - sarcogenin (fig. 4c)- isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Another pregnane derivative - bergenin (fig. 5)- isolated from twigs and characterized as 3b,14b,16a,17b-tetrahydroxypregn-5-en-20-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Pharmacology: S. acidum stem extract arrests spermatogenesis in male rats without noticable side effects. (Venma et al, 2002)
