Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A small much branched tree or large shrub, plant native to South Asia and grows wild, particularly in sub-Himalayan hill country. It is now found in many parts of Asia, Australia, the West Indies, tropical America and Africa. Dry tropical medium rainfall areas of the Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical) and subtropical climates, dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical).

Although it is an extremely robust plant, able to withstand severe heat as well as frost and drought, it does require tropical or near-tropical climatic conditions for best growth. It is a low-altitude species for cultivation below about 600 m in elevation. It is widespread in areas with annual rainfall 300-500 mm. Light demander. The species has no particular soil requirements and thrieves in a wide variety of soils, even in oolitic limestone. One of the most salt tolerant plants. Planted in dry areas and on sites unfit for other crops.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small much-branched tree with oval to spreading irregular canopy. Main trunk is strong and branching is profuse which may be crooked. Bark is light brown to dark brown and scaly. Height of the tree may go up to 15 m but mostly they are 12 m on seedling and budded plants are 6 m tall and of the same spread. Root system is deep and extensive. Young branchlets softly pubescent.

Leaves bifarious, ovate-elliptic, rounded at both ends, slightly inequilateral at the base, faintly and irregularly denticulate, glabrous above, covered beneath with a dense whitish or buff tomentum, strongly 3-nerved from the base. Prickles solitary or in pairs, when twin 1 often straight, the other bent.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Inflorescence is axillary cymose on current season’s growth. Flowers greenish-yellow, in small axillary clusters or short-peduncled axillary cymes. Calyx pubescent or woolly outside, glabrous within, cleft about half way down; lobes triangular, prominently keeled on the inner face for about half their length. Petals cucullate, rounded at the apex, deflexed with the stamens between the calyx lobes. Pedicels short, hairy. Stamens enclosed in the petals, but not exserted beyond them, filaments slightly dilated at the base. Disk with 10 grooved lobes. Ovary about half sunk in the disk, ovoid; styles 2, connate for about half their length.

Fruit is a drupe, round to elliptical or oblong, edible, globose, fleshy, smooth, yellow or orange when ripe. Epicarp is smooth, shining and does not separate from the mesocarp and endocarp. Mesocarp is thick, juicy, sweet and varies in thickness. Both are edible. Seed one in each fruit, round or oblong to oval. Seed coat is hard and of light brown to yellow in colour, smooth or rough, non-endospermic, pitted.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Grown by entire transplanting, direct sowing and by grafting. Seeds of wild species of Zizyphus spp. are used for raising seedlings. Seeds are sometimes soaked to quicken the germination or may be cracked to induce early germination. They are sown in February or in early spring. They complete germination in 8-10 weeks. Seedlings are budded or grafted.

Transplanting of 1 to 1.5 year old trees is done during early winter or on the onset of monsoon season. Spacing is usually 4-8 m.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Wood excellent for fuel and first grade charcoal. Calorific value of sapwood : 4,878 cal., 8,782 Btu.

Wood: The timber is hard, strong, fine grained and reddish in colour. It works well and takes good polish.

Wood used for house construction, agricultural implements, oil crushers, golf clubs, wells, axe and hoe-handles, gunstocks, sandals, yokes, harrows, toys, turnery and parts of wheels, cheap grade pencils. Suitable for the production of active carbon.

Other uses:

Fruits - The tree bears heavily, sometimes producing thousands of nutritious fruits per plant year after year. Fruits eaten fresh, dried, candied, stewed or smoked. Can be made into a floury meal, a butter or a cheese-like paste, which is used as a condiment. The juice can be made into a refreshing drink. Sometimes used medicinally, also provide fodder.

Trees also grown as wind breaks.

Leaves - Leaves considered to be good fodder for goats and cattle. Also used to feed tasar silk worm. Resinous encrustation from these insects used to produce shellac. Leaves are used for tanning.

Root bark as tanning material.

Live fence - Tree useful as live fence; its spiny stems and branches deter live-stock.

Lac insects - This is one of the few trees that can be used to host lac insects. The resinous encrustation from these insects is used to produce shellac.
