Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Rugged tree. Arid and barren slopes and wastelands. Native to southwestern corner of western Australia. Occurs from near sea-level to about 300 m, with isolated occurrences at higher elevations.

Drought hardy and grows with rainfall as low as 250 mm per annum but prefers regions with rainfall of 350-600 mm and as high as 1000 mm. The plant does not withstand frost and grows best where winter and summer means are between 130 and 300C respectively.

It grows rapidly. It adapts itself to a wide variety of soil conditions such as sandy wastelands and swamps, riverbanks and small rocky hills. It can grow under adverse dry conditions on moderate heavy clays and tolerate salt spray, soil salinity and alkalinity.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Seeds germinate readily. Young plants can often be found under mature trees in the hundreds. Seedlings are easily raised in a nursery and established in the field. This species develops root suckers and coppices freely.

Seeds are normally treated with boiling water, but nicking the seed coat, soaking in sulphuric acid and exposing the seeds to dry heat are also effective.

Produce and Products:

Gum - The damaged bark exudes copious amounts of a very acidic gum that seems to show exceptional promise for use in pickles and other acidic foodstuffs.

Other uses:

Sand-dune fixation - This is one of the best woody species for binding moving sand.

Windbreaks - It is useful for windbreaks, amenity plantings, beautification projects and roadside stabilization in semiarid regions.

Fodder - The leaves (actually phyllodes) are palatable to livestock when fresh or dried into hay. They are especially used as a supplementary feed for sheep and goats. Crushed seeds have been fed to sheep without ill effects. Regrowth of established bushes is so good that, trees can be completely grazed off without harming the plants.
