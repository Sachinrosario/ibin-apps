 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
Annual, climbing, hispid herb. It is found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, Assam Tripura, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
Availability :  

Specimen examined:
Uttar Pradesh: Mussoorie below Dhobighat, 1767 m, 17.07.1961, H.O. Saxena, 1998 (DD); Rispana, Dehra Dun, 08.08.1964, C.R. Babu, 33442 (BSD); Assam: Rotung, 334 m, 28.12.1911, I.H. Burkill, 37544 (CAL); Tripura: Naogaon near Rani Bazar, 200-300 m, June 1916, P.M. Debbarman, 1101 (CAL); Madhya Pradesh: Indore, 20.10.1918, P. Mukherjee, 182439 (CAL); Andaman & Nicobar Islands: Manipur, S. Andaman, 13.12.1892, s. legit., 182440 (CAL).
Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant is commercially cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves reniform-orbicular, 16 x 24 cm, 5-6 lobed, apex of lobes obtuse or acute, soft, dentate with whitish blotches on upper surface; petioles punctate hairy, 8-20 cm long. Tendrils multifid. Calyx lobes foliaceous. Fruits oblong, various; pulp yellowish; fruiting peduncle pentagonal. Seeds marginate.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence:  Solitary, monoecious ;  Male Flower:  Calyx tube campanulate, lobes 5,; corolla 5-lobed hairy; stamens 3, inserted low in the calyx tube, anthers connate; Female Flower: Calyx tube campanulate, lobes 5, linear,; corolla 5-lobed hairy; ovary oblong, style short, stigma 3, bifid, ovules many; Fruit: Fleshy indehicent, large ; Seed: Ovoid  compressed. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : Most of the part of year.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome numbers=24, 40.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant requires well drained loamy soil.

Nutrient requirements: Requires tonnes of farmyard manure at the time of planting and 80 kg/ha nitrogenous fertilizers and 70 kg/ha potassium and phosphorous at growing period give good yield.

Water management: Plant requires good irrigation facilities.                                       

Harvesting period / Method: Fruits are harvested when they are fully mature.

Chemistry:

·     Antigibberellin -cucurbitacin B -isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra1970-1979)a.

·     Detection aric, succinic, malic, citric, ascorbic and galacturonic acids by TLC; malic acid was ominant acid (362 mg/100 9 fresh fruit).(Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979 )b.

·     Total organic acids 9.47%, nonvolatile acids in i organic acids 98.6% and vitamin C content 13.8%. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)c.

·     Isorhamnetin-3-0-rutinoside, mpferol-3-0-rutinoside and kaempferol-3-0-robinobioside isolated from pollen; stigma tained only kaempferol-3-0-rutinoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)d.

·     From the sterol fraction of seed oil from commercial Cucurbita moschata Dutch ("calabacita") delta 5 and delta 7 sterols having saturated and unsaturated side chain were isolated by chromatographic procedures and characterized by spectroscopic (1H and 13C-nuclear magnetic resonance, mass spectrometry) methods. The main components were identified as 24S-ethyl 5 alpha-cholesta-7,22E-dien-3 beta-ol (alpha-spinasterol); 24S-ethyl 5 alpha-cholesta-7,22E,25-trien-3 beta-ol (25-dehydrochondrillasterol); 24S-ethyl 5 alpha-cholesta-7,25-dien-3 beta-ol; 24R-ethyl-cholesta-7-en-3 beta-ol (delta 7-stigmastenol) and 24-ethyl-cholesta-7, 24(28)-dien-3 beta-ol (delta 7,24(28)-stigmastadienol).( Rodriguez et al., 1996). CA. 126 : 6797.

·     The fatty acids linolenic acid, palmitic acid, stearic acid, arachidic acid, and doeicosanoic acid were isolated and identified as their Me esters from Cucurbita moschata.  The sterol glycosides 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl 24R-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7-en-3b-ol and 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl 24S-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7,22E-dien-3b-ol were also isolated and identified by spectral studies of their acetyl derivatives. (Han et al., 1999) CA. 132: 205432

·     HPLC separation of different carotenoids was carried out on a 6 gm reverse phase package with a ternary gradient elution methods using a diodearray detector.  The quantity of carotenoids in intact cotyledon of pumpkin is only 1-2 mg/, but 30-40 mg/g in the etiolated an imbibed cotyledon, 60-90 mg/g in excised and incubated one in darkness; maximum carotenoids occur in the incubated one in light: 350-500 mg/g.  Themain components were: lutein, b-carotene, cis-lutein, violaxanthin.  The rate of lutein and b-carotene increases during a short time senescenceinduced in darkness or light.  Mainly the quantity of oxidized carotenoids increases during senescence.  The most important carotenoids component is lutein which has been oxidized to lutein-epoxide and later luteoxanthin as a result of senescence and/or light stress. (Szabo et al., 1999)

·     Crystallization and preliminary X-ray analysis of the glycerol-3-phosphate 1-acyltransferase from squash (Cucurbita moschata). (Turnbull et al., 2001)

·     A new unsaturated hydroxy fatty acid was isolated from the leaves of Cucurbita moschata through repeated silica gel column chromatography and chemical methods. The structure of the new fatty acid was determined as 13-hydroxy-9,11,15-octadecatrienoic acid on the basis of several spectral data including 2D-NMR. The stererostructures of double bonds were determined to be 9Z, 11E and 15E by coupling patterns of related proton signals in the 1H-NMR and NOESY experiments. (Bang et al., 2002)

·     Chloroform and Ethyl acetate extracts of the edible part of Cucurbita moschata Duch, showed significant anticomplementary activities on the classical pathway of the complement system.  Bioassay-guided chromatography separation using silica gel, Sephadex LH-20, octadecyl silica gel, thin layer chromatography, and high performance liquid chromatography was performed to isolate the anticomplementary substances.  Among the isolated substances, apigenin, phytosterols, and mixt. of fatty acids, which were identified by NMR and gas chromatog.-mass  spectrometry, presented the strongest activities.  Especially phytosterols and mixture of saturated fatty acids isolated from the Et acetate extracts had 0.74 and 0.67 mg/mL as IC50, respectively, indicating that the Et acetate extraction is the best way to isolate the anticomplementary substances from Cucurbita moschata Duch. (Yang et al., 2002) CA. 137:182359


Pharmacology:

·     In rats with experimental carbon tetrachloride-in- duced fatty liver, serum glutamate-pyruvate transaminase and hepatic collagen levels were significantly decreased, whereas serum-lipoprotein level was increased by administration of cucurbitacin B (0.20 fig/kg). Hepatic damages, including fibrosis and cirrhosis, were also markedly reduced by cucurbitacin B therapy (0.20 mg/kg). Serum cAMP-cGMP ratio was increased from 1.95 to 2.56 following i.v. injection of cucurbitacin B or cucurbitacin E (0.20 mg/kg), suggesting that changes in cyclic nucleotide balance may be related to therapeutic mechanism of action of cucurbitacins in hepatitis.( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984)a

·     Angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor acts on the inhibition of ACE and causes a decrease in blood pressure. To isolate ACE inhibitors, crude water exts. of the edible portion of ripe Cucurbita moschata Duch were obtained after heating in water at 95°C for 2 h.  Crude exts. were then filtered using PM-10 and YM-1 membranes.  The membrane-filtered soln. was loaded onto Sephadex G-15 column equilibrated with a phosphate buffer.  Among the four major fractions of gel permeation chromatog., the second fraction had the highest inhibitory activity of 65%.  Further purifn. of the fraction using reversed-phase HPLC with a C18 column produced ACE inhibitors, which were identified as a mixture having mol. mass of 222 and 273 by Tandem mass spectrometry.(Jung and Song, 2001) CA. 137:37469
