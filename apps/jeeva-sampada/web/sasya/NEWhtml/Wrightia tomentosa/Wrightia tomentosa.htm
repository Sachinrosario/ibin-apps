Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT: 
A small, deciduous tree, up to 12 m. high, found throughout the (Tropical) warmer parts of India, ascending to an altitude of c. 2000 ft. in the Himalayas and to 4,000 ft. in the Nilgiris.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES 
The tree is planted for ornament. Bark grayish yellow to rust-coloured, corky, with light coloured specks; leaves elliptic, often tomentose, 7.5-15.0 cm. Long; flowers pale yellow with orange coronal scales, in corymbose cymes; follicles 15-30 cm. Long, connate into a cylinder, with a deep groove on each side; seeds with white silky coma. Bark is corky.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests: 

  1. The tree is infected by Hemileia wrihtiae in many parts of Tamil Nadu and Kerala. 
  2. In Calcutta, immature stages of the pest, Phylyctaenia tyres Gamer have been observed on the plant. 
  3. It also serves as a host to the angiospermic parasites- Dendrophthoe falcata and Viscum monoicum

Other Uses: 
  1. The wood is used for building purposes, furniture, combs, toys, packing-cases, pen holders, picture frames, bobbins and cheap-grade pencils, and sometimes for firewood. 
  2. In Rajasthan, it is used for lacquer work on turnery articles. On the West Coast, it is used to peg the binding which holds together the upper planks of sea-going dugouts. 
  3. The leaves are administered with salt for relief from toothache. 
  4. The Oraons use the leaves and roots in fever, they are pounded in water before use. In Nepal, the milky juice is used to stop hemorrhage.
  5. The yellow milky juice obtained from plant yield a fairly yellow dye on dilution with water, which is found to impart fast colouration to cotton fabrics.  The fruit contains a floss which is used for stuffing.
