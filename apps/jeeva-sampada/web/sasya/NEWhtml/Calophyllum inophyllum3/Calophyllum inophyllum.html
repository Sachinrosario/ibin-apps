 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Tree, upto 15 m tall, glabrous; It is found in West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Goa, Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Laccadives.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Bark is grey, smooth, mature bark deeply fissured. Leaves broadly elliptic or obovate-elliptic, 8-15 x 4-10 cm, coriaceous, rounded or retuse at apex, midvein prominent on lower surface. Flowers in lax few-flowered raceme or panicle, 5-15 cm long white, fragrant, 2.5-3.0 cm in diam. Sepals 4, concave. Petals 4, oblong, obtuse. Stamens numerous, connate at base. Stigma mushroom-shaped. Fruits drupe, globose, 2.5-4.0 cm in diam., greenish yellow, pulp scanty.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Few flowered - racemes ; Flower: polygamous, bracteate, regular,  dioeciouss; Calyx:  Sepals, ovate, orbicular, concave, reflexed, ciliolate, the two outer much the smaller; Corolla: Petals 4, oblong, obtuse, spreading, twisted;Androecium: Stamens numerous, filaments united, into 4-6 bundles, scarecly connate at the base, anthers erect, ovate, 2-celled, dehiscing longitudinally ; Gynoecium:  Ovary one-celled, ovule erect, style, ,long, twisted and stigma large, mushroom shaped; Fruit: globose, yellowish, , pulp scanty ; Seed: Erect, ovoid . Flowering & Fruiting : December-January. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: In Ayurveda system of medicine bark is used in heal ulcers and inflammation of eye. It improves complexion. Seeds yield an oil which is used externally in rheumatism and skin diseases.


Chemistry:
4-Phenylcoumarins – calophyllolide (fig. 1), inophyllolide (fig. 2) and calophyllic acid from ripe seeds; cinnamic acid, inophyllic and calophyllic acids and a new 4-phenylcoumarin – ponnalide from unripe seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

A new myricetin glucoside, myricetin and quercetin from androecium, leucocyanidin from petals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Friedelin, 3 new triterpenes – canophyllal (3b), canophyllol (fig. 3c) and canophyllic acid (fig. 3a). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

(+)Inophyllolide (fig. 4a), m.p. 188°C, its cis isomer, m.p. 149°C and 12-hydroxy derivative of cis isomer (fig. 4b), m.p. 200°C, from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

Jacareubin, 6-deoxyjacareubin and 2-(3, 3-dimethylallyl)-1,3,5,6-tetrahydroxyxanthone from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Myricetin, its 7-glucoside and quercetin isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Five piocicidal 2H,6H,10H-benzo[1,2-b:3,4-b:5,6-b’’]-tripyran derivatives – inophyllum A,B,C (fig. 15),D and E (fig. 16)– isolated from leaves; inophyllum C identified as (+)inophyllolide and E as its cis isomer. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Isolation and structure of a new 4-alkylcoumarin (fig. 8). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Five new coumarins (II to V, fig. 9a-9d and VI, fig. 11) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Structure of calophynic acid (fig. 10) established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Revised structure of ponnalide (fig. 17). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Structure of myricetin-7-glucoside confirmed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Calophyllolide isolated from nuts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A new xanthone isolated from heartwood and its structure determined as 2-(3-hydroxy-3-methylbutyl)-1,3,5,6-tetrahydroxyxanthone; in addition, jacareubin, its 6-deoxy derivative, 2-(3-methylbutyl-2-enyl)-1,3,5,6-tetrahydroxyxanthone and 2-(3-methylbuty-2-enyl-)-1,3,5-trihydroxyxanthone was isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

New compounds – inophyllums P (fig. 18a), G-1 (fig. 16) and G-2 (fig. 17) and an enantiomer of soulattrolide (fig. 18b)– isolated from Malaysian tree and characterized; inophyllums A, B, C and E also isolated; absolute stereochemistry of inophyllum A confirmed by X-ray analysis and erlative stereochemistry of other inophyllums thus determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Two new xanthones – caloxanthones A (fig. 13) and B (fig. 14)– isolasted from root bark along with maclauraxanthone, 1,5-dihydroxyxanthone and (-) epicatechin and structures of new compounds elucidated by 2D NMR. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Root bark afforded a new xanthone – caloxanthone C (fig. 15)– along with 4-hydroxyxanthone whereas heartwood of root afforded a new xanthone – 1-hydroxy-2-methoxyxanthone – in addition to 1,2-dimethoxyxanthone, 2-hydroxy-1-methoxyxanthone and 6-deoxyjacareubin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

The new compound (2S,3R)-2,3-dihydro-5-hydroxy-2,3,8,8-tetramethyl-6-(1-phenylethenyl)-4H,8H-benzo{1,2-b:3,4-b’}dipyran-4-one along with (2R,3R)-2,3-dihydro-5-hydroxy-2,3,8,8-tetramethyl-6-(1-phenylethenyl)-4H,8H-benzo{1,2-b:3,4-b’}dipyran-4-one were isolated from the leaves of Calophyllum inophyllum and identified from their spectral and chemical data. (Nizam et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 9703-1797.

The new compound (2S,3R)-2,3-dihydro-5-hydroxy-2,3,8,8-tetramethyl-6-(1-phenylethenyl)-4H,8H-benzo[1,2-b:3,4-b’]dipyran-4-one, along with (2R,3R) 2, 3 – dihydro – 5 – hydroxy - 2, 3, 8, 8 – tetramethyl – 6 - (1-phenylethenyl) -4H,8H-benzo(1,2-b:3,4-b’]dipyran-4-one were isolated from the leaves of Calophyllum inophyllum and identified from their spectral and chemical data. (Khan et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 81873m.

HIV reverse transcriptase isolated from C. inophyllum, a rapid and  accurate analytical method for determining the quantity of the inhibitors inophyllum A, B, C, D, E, and P was desired. A quantitative analytical method involving HPLC and LC-MS was established. (Kawazu et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 326600n.

The seeds of Calophyllum cerasiferum Vesque (Family-Clusiaceae), and Calophyllum inophyllum (Family-Clusiaceae) contain several known coumarins, among which were the potent HIV reverse transcriptase inhibitors costatolide and inophyllum P. Calophyllum cerasiferum contained (-)-calanolide B as its major coumarin constituent in significant amount and thus constitute a renewable source of this compound. (Spino et al, 1998).

The lipid composition and fatty acids from the seed oil of Calophyllum inophyllum from South Vietnam have been investigated. The contents of hydrocarbons, triglycerides, free fatty acids and polar lipid fractions have been determined by column chromatography. (Pham et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 131, 29853q.

The ethanolic extract of the fresh leaves of Calophyllum inophyllum afforded a pair of new epimers named as inophynone (R = a-Me) (fig. 12a) and isophynone (R = b-Me) (fig. 12b). Their structures were elucidated with aid of spectroscopic techniques. Some known constituents, cholesterol, friedelin, canophyllol and canophyllic acid were also isolated. (Ali et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 127592y.

New couamrin, 12-methoxy inophyllum – D (fig. 6a), in addition to six known coumarins, inophyllum – A, inophyllum – B, calophyllolide, inophyllum – P, inophyllum - C and inophyllum – D, have been isolated. (Joshi et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 53902n.

Oil from Calophyllum inophyllum seeds makes up about half of the fresh. It is similar in value to the oils from groundnut and palm kernel. The greenish yellow oil is liquid at room temperature. The iodine value of the oil of Calophyllum inophyllum shows it to be non-drying and with preponderance of unsaturated fatty acids. (Oderinde and Ajayi, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 249552t.

Three compounds were isolated from leaves of Calophyllum inophullum Linn. Two of them were identified as D:A – friedooleanan-3-one and the 28-hydroxy derivative. (Yao and Zeng, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 263491d.


Pharmacology:
Anticoagulant activity of chlophyllolide was compared with that of dicoumarol, ethylbiscoumacetate and nicoumalone in rabbits. Single oral dose of 50 mg/kg reduced coagulation valency to 18 to 22% after 48hrs. Oral administration upto 1.75 gm/kg was not fatal in rats. Its LD50 in rats i.p. was 560 mg/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.

Calophyllolide showed anticonvulsant and anti-inflammatory activities in rodents. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Calophyllolide showed potent anti-inflammatory activity in carrageenin-induced odema in rats;p ED50 was 140 mg/kg orally. It showed anti-inflammotry and antarthritic activity in formaldehyde-induced arthritis and adjuvant arthritis in rats. Its LD50 was 2.5 g/kg, p.o. It was devoid of ulcerogenc activity upto twice the ED50 dose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Dehydrocycloguanandin, calophyllin-B, jacareubin and 6-deoxyjacareubin, produced varying degrees of CNS depression characterized by ptosis, sedation, decreased spontaneous motor activity. Loss of musclr tone, potentiation of pantobarbitone sleeping time and ether anaesthesia in mice and rats. All the xanthones exhibited anti-inflammatory activity by both i.p. and oral routes in rats. Jacareubin and 6-deoxyjacareubin also showed antiulcer activity in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Inophyllums B and P inhibited HIV reverse transcriptase (IC 50 38 and 130 nM respectively) and were active against HIV-1 in cell culture (IC 50 1.4 and 1.6 muM respectively). Inophyllums A, C, D and E were less active. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Various crude extracts (ethanol, chloroform, water and butanolic) and pure compounds (friedelin, canophyllol, canophyllic acid and inophynone) from C.inophyllum were tested against various fungi and bacteria. The chloroform etract inhibited the growth of human and animal pathogens. Among the pure samples friedelin can be used as a growth inhibiting agent against human pathogens. (Ali et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 177958p.
