Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large, deciduous tree, distributed in the Himalayas from Kashmir to West Nepal, at altitudes of 900-3,600m. Tree is found growing gregarious in rich, moist, shady ravines of Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh up to Nepal.

It is moderate light-demander, requiring moist and shady place for its early development. In dry and hot places, its growth is poor and stunted. In its natural habitat, the absolute maximum shade temperature ordinarily varies from 260 to 390 and the absolute minimum from -40 to -120 or perhaps lower, and the normal rainfall from 100 to 250 cm or more.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large, deciduous tree, with a short, straight, cylindrical bole and spreading crown, 30 m in height and 7.5 m in girth in favourable localities. Bark grey when old, exfoliating upwards, in long and thin bands. Leaves digitate: leaflets 5-9, oblanceolate or oblong. Flowers white, tinged with red and yellow in large thyrsoid panicles. Capsules ovoid, rough outside, 5 cm long, leathery. Seeds 2-3, dark brown, smooth, shining.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology: Tree sheds leaves from October to early December and the new leaves appear in April, and flowers during April-June when it is extremely attractive.


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: The capsules open and the seeds (35-44/kg) fall to the ground during October - November. The seeds roll into depressions in the ground as they are smooth and round, and they are generally covered with earth and leaves and debris germinate successfully in the spring and establish themselves quickly. The seedlings are also found in large numbers in loose, moist soil among stones and boulders which hold up the seeds. Early growth of the seedlings is rapid, and by the end of the first season a height of 60 cm or more may be attained.

The ripe seeds are collected in October or November, and either sown immediately or stored in dry earth. They are drilled in the seedbeds with good moist soil at 15cm X 30 cm and covered with 2.5 cm layer of earth. The beds should be properly weeded and the soil hoed. They can be planted out in the field, or if larger plants are required, they should be planted in nursery beds and finally planted out in the following winter, while transplanting it may not be necessary to retain earth around the roots.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuelwood.

Wood: Wood is creamy white or pale pinkish white when freshly cut, but becomes pinkish brown on exposure, with light brown lines. The heartwood is not distinct. The timber is dull, with smooth feel, and without any characteristic odour. It is light to moderately heavy (sp gr 0.43 - 0.57; wt 479 - 560 kg/ cu m), soft, straight or narrowly and shallowly interlocked grained and sometimes waxy-grained in the radial plane and very fine- and even-textured.

Timber is largely used for buildings, packing-cases, water-troughs, planking, cooperage, platters, tea-boxes, boat-fittings, cabinet-making and turnery articles. It is also suitable for mathematical instruments, shoe-heels and match-splints. The wood is also used for bobbins. The hardwood, after suitable dyeing and waxing, has been found to be suitable for high quality pencils. Selected portions are useful for panelling in railway carriages, and for sport-goods. The pulp of this wood and other hardwoods, mixed with some long-fibred pulp, is suitable for making wrapping-paper. The bleached pulp is reported to be suitable for writing and printing paper.

Other uses:

Food & Fodder - The shoots and leaves are used as fodder. The fruits are eaten by cattle. In times of scarcity they are also eaten by the hill tribes, after steeping in water to remove the bitter components, or sometimes the seeds are powdered and mixed with wheat flour.

Fruit and Seeds - The crushed fruit is used for washing clothes. Crushed seeds, if fed to cattle, are reported to improve the quality and quantity of milk. Seeds are also given to horses for colic. The seeds contain oil, which is used in rheumatism; it is also applied to wounds.

Bark - It is made into a paste for application on painful, dislocated joints. Extract of the stembark is reported to posses fungicidal properties.

Roots - These are used as a cure for leucorrhoea.
