DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  A short low-growing shrub, with whitish stem leaves stipulate, paripinnate with pale yellowish leaflets frequently bordered and usually more abruptly pointed; large yellow flowers borne in axillary raceme pods narrowly elliptical or reniform, 8 seeded.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:  

Infloresence terminal racemes; Flower pedicillate, complete, zygomorphic, hermaphrodite brilliantly yellow colour, pentamerous and hypogynous ; Calyx sepals 5 , polysepalous, old sepal anterior ; Corolla petals 5,alternate with sepals,the adaxial petal always inside ,ascending imbricate ; Androecium 10 in whorls of 5 each, polyandros, dithecous,dorsifixed and introrse ; Gynoecium monocarpellary,ovary superior, unilocular, placentation marginal, style simple, stigma capitate ; Fruit is pod which is light green when young to dark brown to black when matures, flat ,thin, oblong,pubscent ; Seeds dark brown obovate-oblong.

 
   
Floral and seed biology:

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Insect and bird pollinated plant. Seeds are dispersed by wind.
 
Genetic variability:
Plant chromosome numbers are varible and reported to be 2n=26 and 2n=28.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant thrives on wide range of well drained soils from loams to heavy clays but is largely grown on dry red loam, sandy loams, including even coarse, gravelly soil with pH ranges between 6-8.5.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers especially under irrigated conditions. Where the soil fertility is low to medium the crop generally needs 50 –100 kg nitrogen,20-50 kg phosphate and 20 kg of potassium  oxide during  the period of growth of 135-150 days.

Water management: It is rainfed kharif crop requires little irrigation particularly if rains are irregular during the period of growth

Harvesting period / Method: Leaves are mature for harvesting in about 60 days depending on the locality and tender leaves start loosing their green colour and appear bluish and thick. Pods are harvested when still green.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Leaf and pods - laxative, especially useful in habitual constipation.


.Chemistry: 

Leaf: Glucoside, sennosides C, anthraquinone, pod: glycosides of rhein, sennosides A and B, anthraquinone glycerides.

·         New glycoside – aloeemodin dianthrone diglucoside – from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·         Anthracene glycosides of rhein and chrysophanic acid – sennosides A and B – from pods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·         Chrysophanol, physcion, aloe-emodin, rhein and rheum-emodin isolated from callus culture of cotyledons. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Chrysophanol, physcion, aloe-emodin, rhein and rheum-emodin isolated from callus culture of cotyledons. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Sennosides A, B, C and D, chrysophanol, emodin and physcion isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Chrysophanol, physcion, rhein, emodin and aloe present in glycosidic from tissue culture of plant; all these compounds except physcion also present in glycosidic from. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Method for separation and pur8ification of sennosides A, B and C as their calcium salts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Sennoside A1 isolated from pods characterized as (-) sennidin -8,8’-diglucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Levels of Ca, Mg, K, Na, Fe, Mn, Zn, Cd, Co, Cu, V, Ti, Mo and Ce in leaves tabulated; K and Ca present in largest amount. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·         Twenty amino acids identified in seeds by PC and TLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·         Tinnevellin glucoside along with 6-hydroxymusizin isolated; structure of former determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·         Sennosides A, B, C and D, chrysophanol, emodin and physicon isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·         Two isomeric aloe-emodin dianthrone diglucosides (I) isolated from leaves and their stereostructures at 10-10’ positions elucidated as trans and meso respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·         Water-soluble galactomannan composed of galactose and mannose in 3:2 ratio, isolated from seeds and partially characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·         1-Hydroxy-6, 8-dimethoxy-2-methylanthraquinone-3-O-rutinoside isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         A new bianthraquinone -4,4’-bis(1,3-dihydroxy-6,8-dimethoxy-2-methylanthraquinone) (I)-isolated from heartwood and its structure determined; 1, 1’-bis (4,5-dihydroxy-2-methylanthraquinone), chrysophanol and emodin also isolatede. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         6a, 7a: 22, 26:24, 25-triepoxy-5, 26-dihydroxy-17(13-18) abeo 5a-ergosta-2, 13, 15, 17-tetraen-1-one (Nic-1) and 11-hydroxyhexacosan-2-one isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         Tetracontanol (0. 78) and lup-20(29)-en-1b, 2b-diol (10.2%) determined in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         Root afforded 6, 8-dimethyoxy-2-methylanthraquinone-3-O-b-D-galactopyranoside and 1,5,8-trihydroxy-2-methylanthraquinone-3-O-b-D-glactopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         Isolation of rhamnetin-3-O-(6”-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl)-b-D-glactopyranoside from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         A new triterpenoid glycosed -19a, 24-dihydroxyurs-12-en-28-oic acid -3-O-b-D-xylopyranoside isoalated along with chrysophanol and physcion. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         A rapid and simple reverse-phase high performance liquid chromatographic method has been developed for the quantitative estimation of the medicinally useful laxative principles sennoside A and sennoside B in senna. Using a Bondapak C-18 column eluted with methanol:water:acetic acid:tetrahydrofuran (60:38:2:2) both compounds were well resolved with recoveries of 97 and 96% respectively. (Verma et al., 1996).

·         The official drug Sennae folium (obtained from Cassia angustifolia and/or C. acutifolia) was found to contain small amounts of volatiles (0.047% based on dry weight) which were analyzed in detail by GC and GC/MS. More than 200 compounds could be detected, 122 of them were identified, representing about 90.7% of the peak area of the total mixture. The volatile constituents can be classified into monoterpenoids (8.8% for sample A and 34.6 % for B), sesquiterpenoids (4.2% and 4.0%, respectively), phenylpropanoids (4.2%/15.2 fatty acids and esters (54.3%/14.2%), and miscellaneous compounds (19.3%/22.7%). Apart from hexadecanoic acid which was strong in both samples (36.8%/9.7%), the occurrence of menthol, geranylacetone, and (E)-anethole is of interest. (Schultze et al., 1996).

·         A rapid high performance liquid chromatographic method was developed for determination of sennosides A and B in senna leaves, pods and diet tea. The determination was carried out by using a gradient elution with acetonitrile and 1% acetic acid on an Inertsil ODS-2 column. The linear calibration ranges of sennosides A and B were 7.52apprx37.60 mug/ml (r = 0.9998) and 8.32-49.92 mug/ml (r = 0.9999), respectively. The recoveries of sennosides A and B were almost quantitative from the senna leaves and pods, respectively. The relative standard deviations of sennosides A and B for intraday and interday analyses were 0.24apprx1.72% and 0.23apprx1.71%, respectively. The contents of sennosides A and B in leaves and pods of Cassia angustifolia Vahl have been determined and the results were as follows: sennoside A 0.64, 1.46% and sennoside B 1.57, 2.30%. The contents of sennosides A and B in seven diet tea samples have been determined and the results were as follows: sennoside A 0.10apprx0.19% and sennoside B 0.16apprx0.28%. (Lin et al., 1998).

·         The cell suspension cultures of Cassia angustifolia (Leguminosae), called senna, were found to produce chrysophanol, physcion and rhein as anthraquinones, and chrysophanol-10, 10' -bianthrone, chrysophanol-physcion-10, 10'-bianthrone and chrysophanol-isophyscion-10, 10'-bianthrone as bianthrones, although no sennoside (glucosides of rhein-10, 10'-bianthrone), strong purgative agents in senna, was detected in the cell cultures. The chemical structures of the compounds isolated from the cultured cells indicate that they have the capability of forming the carbon skeleton of bianthrone, an aglycone of sennosides, and rhein, an important structure block of the aglycone, while the cultured cells lack the ability to produce easily detectable amounts of the bianthrone glucosides. (Sato et al., 1999).

·         The purified seed galactomannan contains mannose:galactose in a ratio of 2.90. The average molecular weight (M(w)) is 9.66x10(4) and the intrinsic viscosity (eta) is 209 mL/g. Methylation analysis, periodate oxidation, Smith degradation and 13C NMR studies confirm that the gum has the basic structure of legume galactomannans with a main chain of (1-->4)-linked beta-D-mannopyranosyl units to which single alpha-(1-->6)-D-linked galactopyranosyl units are attached through block pattern. (Chaubey et al., 2001).  

·         A reversed-phase column liquid chromatographic method for the analysis of sennosides A and B present in leaf and pod extracts of Cassia angustifolia has been developed using a Symmetry C18 column and a linear binary gradient profile. The method can be utilised for the quantitative determination of other sennosides as a baseline resolution for most of the constituents was achieved. The method is economical in terms of the time taken and the amount of solvent used (25 mL) for each analysis. The validity of the method with respect to analysis was confirmed by comparing the UV spectra of each peak with those of reference compounds using a photodiode array detector. (Bala et al., 2001).

Pharmacology:

·        Calcium sennoside A  and calcium sennoside B mixture in the dose range 50-800 µg/ml, produced dose-dependent contraction of rat colon which was blocked by piperazine citrate but not by atropine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Sennoside C showed as much purgative activity as sennoside A in mice. It exerted a potentiating effect of about 1.6 on the activity of sennoside A when administered in a proportion of 20% of drug combination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Pod extract showed laxative effect in normal and essential fatty acid-deficient rats. Oral administration of pod extract produced dose-dependent increase in number of soft faeces excreted by normal rats. At 30.0 mg/kg dose it reversed net absorption of water and incre4ased prostaglandin production in colonic lumen of normal rats by about four times. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        NIC-1 showed antifeedant and insecticidal activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
