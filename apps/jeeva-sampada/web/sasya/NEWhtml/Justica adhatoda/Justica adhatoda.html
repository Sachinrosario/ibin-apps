Chemistry:

·        An alkaloid-vasicinone-from leaves charecterised as 2,3-(ahydroxytrimethylene)-4-quinazolone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Vasicine, b-sitosterol, tritriacontane and vasicinine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Vasicinol, m.p. 2600, identical with 6-hydroxypeganine.  (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        6-hydroxypeganine isolated from leaves and structure confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Mass spectra of vasicine and vasicinol and its methyl ether studied. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        A new alkaloid-vasicinine, mp. 2080- from young inflourescences. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Four new quinazoline alkaloids-vasicoline, adhatodine, vasicolinone and anisotine-were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        vasicinone from inflorescence. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Vasicinone, mp. 2090, isolated from inflorescence. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new alkaloid -9-acetamido-3, 4-dihydropyridol[3,4-b]indole (I) and a new glucoside-O-ethyl-a-Dgalactoside-along with sitosterol-b-D-glucoside, galactose and deoxyvasicinone, isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Vasicol isolated from roots and structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Ca, Mg, K, Na, Fe, Mn, Zn, Cd, Co, Cu, V, Ti, Mo and Ce contents estimated in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        2'-hydroxy-4glucosyloxychalcone identified in flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Method for extraction of vasicinone and vasicine from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        New alkaloid- vasicinolone, mp. 2790 -isolated from roots and charecterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984). 

·        Adhavasinone isolated and characterised.  (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984). 

·        (+)vasicinone isolated from leaves.  (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984). 

·        Synthesis of vasicolinone.  (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984). 

·        A new quinazoline alkaloid isolated from leaves and charecterised as 1,2,3,9-tetrahydro-5-methoxypyrrolo[2, 1-b] quinazolin-3-ol (I). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·         Lignins composed of guaiacyl-, syringyl-and p-hydroxyphenyl-propane building units yielded vanillin, syringaldehyde and p-hydroxybenzaldehyde. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Vasicinol, vasicinone, deoxyvasicinone, deoxyvasicine (minor alkaloids) and vasicine isolated from leaves; percentage of minor alkaloids and total alkaloids showed seasonal variation; samples collected in Mar.-Apr. showed a higher percentage of minor alkaloids, whereas those collected in June-Sept. had higher content of vasicine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Two new aliphatic hydroxyketones isolated from aerial parts and characterised as 37-hydroxyhexatetracont-1-en-15-one and 37-hydroxyhentetracontan-19-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        29-methyltriacontan-1-ol along with b-sitosterol isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        From the leaves of A. vasica, the pyrrolo[2,1-b]quinazoline alkaloids 1-vasicine, 1-vasicinone, 1-vasicol, anisotine, 3-hydroxyanisotine and a new alkaloid, vasnetine (1 mg) were isolated and their structures established by 1H- and 13C-NMR spectral studies. The chemical shift assignments for these alkaloids were confirmed by 1H homonuclear COSY, DEPT, HETCOR, selective INEPT and HMBC NMR experiments. (Joshi et al, 1994). C.A. 1995, 122, 5435t.

·        Leaves root and barks of Adhatoda vasica contain large amounts of free sugars. (Sarraj et al, 1994). C.A. 1995, 122, 51378z.

·        A rapid method using HPTLC, which can be used for detection, monitoring and quantification of vasicine in A. vasica herbal preparation. The proposed method is precise av. (recovery 99%), (v<1%) sensitive (limit of quantification = 20 mg), specific reproducible (CV<2%). (Narayana et al, 1995). C.A. 1996, 125, 41930w.

·        Seasonal variation of total alkaloids from the leaves of A. vasica Nees (BLV) was studied. Older plants showed uniformly higher concentrations of alkaloids with much less variation. (Rajani, M. and Pundarikakshudu, 1996). CA-126, L69094q.

·        Two new pyrroloquinazoline alkaloids viz. 1,2,3,9-tetrahydropyrrolo-(2,1-)b-quinazolin-9-one-3R-hydroxy-3(2’-dimethylaminophenyl) (desmethoxyaniflorine) and 7-methoxy-3R-hydroxy-1,2,3,9-tetrahydropyrrol-O. [(2,)-b]-quinazolin-9-one (l-methoxyvasicinone) together with several known compounds were isolated from the leaves of A. vasica. Their structures were established by spectroscopic and X-ray analysis. (Thappa et. al., 1996). CA-125, 81925e.

·        A new triterpenoid 3a-hydroxy-D-friedetolean-5-ene, along with the known compounds, epitaraxerol and peganidine, were isolated from the aerial parts of Adhatoda vasica. The structures of these compounds were elucidated by spectral studies, X-ray diffraction studies or a bronchodilating and hypotensive principle of A. vasica, vasicinone were also carried out. (Rahman et al, 1997). C.A.  128, 32398p.

·        The methanolic extract, the alkaloids and non-alkaloids fractions of the leaves from Adhatoda vasica were investigated for their antibacterial activities using the paper disk and the dilution method. (Brantner and Chakraborty, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 306366h.

·        From the leaves of A. vasica grown in Egypt, 4 quinazoline alkaloids, anisotine, 3-hydroxyanisotine, vasicolinone, vasicoline, and the new isoquinoline 1-phenyl-2-methyl-6, 7-dimethoxy-1, 2, 3, 4-tetrahydroisoquinoline were isolated and identified by spectral data. The unsaponifiable fraction of the petrol ether extract of A. vasica leaves revealed high percentage of b-sitosterol and tricontane. The saponifiable part exhibited high percentages of linolenic, arachidonic, linoleic, palmatic, and oleic acids. (Abd El-Megeed F.; and Ahmed S., 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 349747v.

·        The ethyl acetate extract of Adhatoda vasica leaves and flowers was rich in flavanoids, out of which 5 glycosides and an aglycon were isolated and identified. These are vitexin, isovitexin, 2’’-O-xylosylvitexin, violanthin and apigenin. Volatiles of leaves and flowers were also investigated. Flower volatiles are composed of 36 compounds; the major 27 compounds were identified amounting to 95% of the total volatiles. The major compound was a ketone identified as 4-heptanone, 3-methyl-. Leaf volatiles were a complex mixture with more than 50 compounds, among these 27 compounds were identified comprising 82% of the total leaf volatiles. The major component was the hydrocarbon decane. Both leaf and flower volatiles showed higher antimicrobial activity against bacteria and yeast more than fungi. (Ahmed et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 332075u.

·        A reversed-phase HPLC method to det vasicine and vasicinone in A. varica leaves was developed and described. The vasicine content in aude A. varica was 0.0623 and varicinone eas 0.060%. (Chauhan, S.K.; et. al., 1999). 


Pharmacology:

·        Relaxation producing activity of dl-vasicinone on isolated guinea pig tracheal muscle was about 1/2000 that of adrenaline. 1-vasicine caused slight relaxation at low concentration but contraction at higher concentration. Compared to adrenaline, dl-vasicinone was 1/3800 as active against histamine- induced contraction. (Rastogi and ehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Uterotonic activity of vasicine in different species of animals in vivo was similar to that of oxytocin and methylergometrine. The effect was influenced by the degree of priming of uterus by estrogens and was markedly decreased after pretreatment of uterus with aspirin and endomethacin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Vasicine showed bronchodilatory activity both in vivo and in vitro comparable with that of theophylline. Vasicinone showed bronchodilation in vitro and bronchoconstriction in vivo. both in combination had more bronchodilatory activity in vitro and in vivo. Vasicine also exhibited respiratory and uterine stimulant activity and moderate hypotensive activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Vasicine showed abortifacient effect in guinea pigs depending on the stage of pregnancy and prior priming of animals with estradiol. in rats, it did not show any abortifacient effect. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Intraamniotic injection of vasicine hydrochloride was effective in including mid-trimester abortions at dose of 60 mg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Bronchodilatory activity of vasicinone was compared with that of isoprenaline and aminophylline. Acute toxicity in mice and absorption pattern in dogs of vasicinone administered by different routes confirmed activity and safety of vasicinone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Adhatoda vasica leaf extract showed modulatory influence on the enzymes of xenobiotic metabolism, antioxidant status and lipid peroxidation in mice. (Singh et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 173004t.

Miscellaneous Information

·         Skin lightening cosmetics contain chlesterice liquid crystals and extracts of A. vasica. A cosmetic composition contained cholesteryl 12-hydroxycstearatae, 3-0- cholesteryl  neptan oate, 3.0 cholesteryl oleate 2-0, cholesteryl butyrate 1-0, A. vasica extract 1.0, neutralized 1% carboxyvinyl polymer, 1.0; 1,3-butylene glycol 7-0 and preservatives 0.1% wt. parts. The compounds showed moisturizing, antioxidant and hyaluronidase inhibitory activities. (Ueda and Shimomura, 1995). C.A. 123, 152599y.

·         The invention relates to a skin composition having skin-lightening and rough skin-improving effects, wherein the composition contains conchilin hydrolyzate or reaction product of conchiolin hydrolyzate with succinic acid anhydride and Adhatoda vasica. (Shimomura, K. and Hattori, F.; 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 34875n.
