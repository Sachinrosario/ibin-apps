DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Large shrubs. Leaves oblong, 8-13 x 2-4.5 cm, attenuate at both ends, entire at margin, minutely pubescent; petiole 0.8-1.2 cm long. Flowers in spikes; peduncles axillary, opposite, stout, 1-2 cm long; head 1.0-2.5 cm in diam.; bracts ovate, 0.9-1.2 x 0.5-0.8 cm, subacute, narrowed at base, puberulous or glabrate; bracteoles narrowly oblong, 0.5-0.8 cm long. Calyx 0.4-0.6 cm long, sub-5-partite; segments narrowly oblong, acute, minutely pubescent, epically within. Corolla 1.5-2.5 cm long, creamy white, hairy; tube broadly cylindric, 0.4-0.6 cm long; lips oblong, upper emarginate, lower 3-lobed nearly to the base. Filaments stout, glabrous, except at base. Capsule not known.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds and cuttings.

Soil type: Plant grows in waste lands and wide variety of soils.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers.

Water management: Irrigation is necessary at the time of transplanting and regular irrigation is required during dry seasons.

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is harvested when it is in full bloom.

Chemistry:
Whole plant was dried, extracted with ethanol and the extract fractionated with ethyl acetate b -sitosterol and its glucoside were identified from ethyl acetate fraction. The defatted alcoholic extract was made alkaline and alkaloids were extracted with chloroform. The crude alkaloids (0.5% dark brown powder) were column chromatographed and deoxy vasicinone, m.p. 100 ° , vasicinone, m.p. 210 ° , and vasicine, m.p. 200 ° , were isolated and identified by spectral analysis. (Jain and Srivastava, 1986).
