HABIT & HABITAT:
A genus of laticiferous, dioecious or rarely monoecious shrubs or trees, distributed in Asia and Australia, and probably in Africa. Four species occur in India, but generally it is found all along river banks, Banks of River and streams, banks of rivers and streams, Deciduous forest, Dry Open areas, Dry regions, evergreen forest, Hill forests, mixed forests, Moist deciduous forest, Plains, Road sides, Scrub jungles, Thickets, Tropical Forests and Villages.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Tall evergreen tree, up to 10 cm high. Leaves alternate, petiolate, simple, obovate, crenate-serrate, acute, scabrous, 5 x 2.5 cm;stipules fanceolate, oblique. Inflorescence a globose head. flowers unisexual, white. In male flower perinath connate at base, lobes 4, oblong-ovate, obtuse,pubescent outside, free, in female peinath lobes ovate, obtuse; ovary straight, globose, 1-celled; ovule solitary; style arms 2; stigma simple. Fruit pisciform,yellow,edible, enclosed by the enlarged perianth.

Chemistry
Analysis of the root bark gave: Water extr., 19.3 alcoholic extr., 6.6; petrolium-ether extr.,2.8; total ash,14.0; and acid-insol,ash,5.1%.Free sugars,tannin,potassium chloride are present.A ticture of the rootbark showed a definite action on the myocardium. The root bark is rich in cardenolides; the total glycoside content in an Indian sample of the  air-dried root bark was 0.14 per cent. Ten cardenolide glycosides have been isolated from the ether and chloroform extracts of the rootbark, of which six have been obtained in a crystalline form.They are: Kamaloside,periplogenin on hydrolysis; C31H48O9;asperoside (3-digitoxigenin 2,3-di-O-methyl-Beta-D-glycoside),(3-strophanthidin 2,3-di-O-methyl-Beta-D-fucopyranoside C21H46O10);, indroside (C31H44O10),lucknoside (C31H46O10), and an unnamed substance (C31H48O10); the four other glycosides were obtained as amorphous powder.

Other Uses
  1. The twigs are chewed to make brushes for cleaning teeth and to cure pyorrhoea.
  2. The leaves are used as a galactagogue.
  3. A paste of the leaves is applied to swelling and buboes, and is given to stop excessive perspiration.
  4. The powdered root is prescribed in dysentery and diarrhoea.
  5. It is reported to be applied to the temples as a sedative in the treatment of neuralga.
