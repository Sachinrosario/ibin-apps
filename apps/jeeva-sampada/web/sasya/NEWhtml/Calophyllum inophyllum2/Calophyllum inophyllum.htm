Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A tree, common on sandy coast and laterite soils just above the high tide line, sometimes up to 200m often leaning out over sea. Native of East Africa. Distributed in tropical E. Africa to Taiwan, the Rynkyu & Live Islands and New Caledonia.

Availability :

Orissa, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Lakshadeep and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
Conservation/ Threat Status : Common on sandy coast and laterite soils.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Trees, up to 20 m tall with spreading crown; trunk 1.5 m in girth, wood reddish-white to brown, heavy; bark brown to pale grey (blackish) and fawn, smooth, often mottled with wide boat-shaped fissures; exudates milky or yellow, clear, very sticky; branchlets compressed or slightly flattened, glabrous; internodes (0.4-) 1-3 (-5) cm long; terminal buds 4-10 mm long, nearly triangular, plump, finely rusty tomentose. Leaves variable in size, 15-20 x 5-9 cm, broadly elliptic-oblong or obovate, often broadest a little above middle, cuneate or round and finally decurrent at base, rounded or shallowly emarginated, retuse or subacute at apex, thinly coriaceous, dark green, glabrous, shiny; midrib prominent below, venation distinct, close, raised on both surfaces giving the blade a striate appearance; lateral veins 4-20, ca 5 mm apart; petioles 1-1.5 cm long, sometimes longer, glabrous, stout, flat. Racemes 5-13 cm long, axillary, 5-15 flowered, glabrous. Flowers polygamous, bisexual, ca 2 cm in diam., marble white, fragrant; bracts 3-4 mm long, ovate, soon deciduous; pedicels (1-) 1.5-4.5 (-6.3) cm long, glabrous. Sepals 4, reflexed, 2 outer ones 5.5-10 x 4.5-8 mm, ovate or suborbicular, concave, inner petaloid, 9-15 x 7.5-10 mm, subelliptic. Petals usually 4 (or 3-5), reflexed, 9-16 x 5-10 mm, obovate or elliptic or oblong, rarely glabrous. Stamens numerous, (175-) 210-360 (-440); filaments creamy, 5-7 mm long, connate up to 2 mm into 4-6 bundles; anthers 0.7-2 mm long, rounded or retuse at apex, yellow when young, brownish at maturity. Ovary 1.5-3.5 mm long, depressed globose, stipitate, pink or light purple after pollination; styles much longer than ovary, 4.5-9 mm long, twisted; stigmas peltate, 3-5-radiate, 0.7-2 mm across. Drupes ca 2.5-5 x 2.5-4 cm, globose or spherical to obovoid, shortly apiculate, smooth, yellowish, brownish-green or pale brownish ochraceous, pulpy; pericarp thick; stone ca 2 cm in diam., subspherical, rounded at apex, smooth, basal plug 4-10 mm long.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
ORNAMENTAL FOLIAGE AND FLOWERING TREE
The name Calophyllum is expensive of the beautiful leaf; kalos, beautiful, and phyllon, a leaf. A beautiful large but very slow growing evergreen tree, with noble foliage of dark green polished leaves resembling those of Magnolia. Large racemes of delightfully fragrant white flowers are borne in May-June. These are succeeded by round fruits of the size of a lemon. Raised from seed. Called by some "The Alexandrian Lurel". Called also the "Dilo oil" Tree, after the oil from fruits.
