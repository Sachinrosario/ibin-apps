DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tree cactus, up to 12 feet tall (3.6 m), trunks up to 8 inches in diameter (20 cm) with Pink flowers, 2 inches long (5 cm), followed by red fruits, also 2 inches long (5 cm). Cactus leaves are typically oblong to spatulate-oblong shaped. When they are tender, they have vestigial true leaves, subtended by spines or spine-hairs; Good quality cactus leaves are thin, turgid, have a fresh appearance, and a brilliant green color. Some undesirable quality characteristics are abscission of the small leaves, thickening of the cuticle and an increase in the thickness of the pad.

Processing Techniques:
Cactus stems of Nopalea cochenillifera maintain quality for 12 days at 20C, although a weight loss of about 7% was observed after one week of storage in non covered cactus pads. Symptoms of curving inward were not reported on this specie. Storage at low temperatures (5 to 10C or 41 to 50F) extended cactus leaves post harvest life. Packing in vented polyethylene bags (that retard water loss) maintains their visual quality for about two weeks at 10C and for 4 weeks at 5C. Cactus stems packed in wooden crates and stored at 10C (80-85% R.H.) show a 21% of decay at the cut stem end after 10 days. If carefully harvested, the shelf life can be extended up to 21 days at 10C (85-90% R.H) without decay development. However, cactus pads begin to show signs of wilting due to moisture loss. Factors such as harvest, storage temperature, relative humidity, and packing technique can affect the shelf life of cactus pads.

Miscellaneous Information

 Acidity of cactus stems changes depending on storage temperature. Low temperature storage of cactus stems (Opuntia spp.) maintains or increases acid content, whereas storage at 20C decreases acid content It is observed that the changes in the acid content of cactus leaves of Nopalea cochenillifera stored at 4, 12 and 20C. Besides an increase in shelf life, storage at low temperature maintains vitamin content of cactus pads. Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) content of cactus stems decreases faster when stored at 20C than at 5C. The acid content is an important characteristic of cactus leaves, since it changes during the day in 20 cm long stems. The highest values are present in the morning (0.5% at 8:00 hrs) and they decreases to values of 0.1% in the afternoon (16:00 hrs) (Figure 1), the pH varies from 5.2 at evening to 4.4 in the early morning. Acidity content increases during development of the cactus pad. High acid content (100 mmol H+ per kg fresh weight) imparts an undesirable sour taste affecting the cactus pad flavor and decreasing consumer acceptability. Tender cactus stems (Opuntia spp.) are harvested commercially when they are 15 to 20 cm long and weigh about 90-100 g.
