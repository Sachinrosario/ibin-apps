HABIT & HABITAT
 Large climber; young stems densely pubescent or glabrous. It is found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Goa, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Large climber; young stems densely pubescent or glabrous. Leaves ovate, 7.5-30.0 x 6.3-25.0 cm, acute at apex, cordate at base, glabrous above, white tomentose beneath. Flowers in cyme; peduncles white tomentose; bracts large, ovate-lanceolate, thin, veined, deciduous. Calyx white tomentose. Sepals oblong-elliptic. Corolla infuldibuliform, rose-purple with darker throat, silky pubescent outside. Fruits berries, globose. Seeds brown, glabrous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence: Subcapitate chymes on white tomentose peduncles; Flower: Bracteate, bracteolate, pedicellate, hermaphrodite ; Calyx: white-tomentose outside, sepals 5, herbaceous, long-elliptic, obtuse; Corolla: Infundibuliform, the bands silky, pubescent outside, tube inflated, white, pubescent outside, rose-purple and glabrous inside; Androecium: Stamens 5, included, anthers oblong, straight ; Gynoecium: Ovary 4-celled, glabrous, ovules 4, style filiform, stigma 2-globose ; Fruit: Dry, hard, globose berry; Seed: Seeds 4, embedded in mealy pulp. Flowering & Fruiting: August-September. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Unani system of medicine the roots are used in gleet, gonorrhoea, strengury and chronic ulcer. Leaves are used as poultice for wounds and skin diseases.


Chemistry:

·         Eragine, isoeragine and penniclavine from seeds identified by TLC and PC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·         Seed extract showed hypotensive activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·         1-Triacontanol, b-sitosterol, epifriedelinol and its acetate were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·         Ergometrine, caffeic acid and ethyl caffeate were isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

·         The oil exhibited moderate antiseptic activity against several Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria and phytopathogenic fungi. Oleic acid identified as major component in oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·         Quercetin, kempferol and its 3-O-rhamnoside isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·         Two new glycoside isolated from leaves and characterized as 5-O-a-rhamnoside and 5-O-b-glucoside of 7,8,3’,4’,5’-pentahydroxyflavone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:

Medicine for antidote (Root, Srivastava and Verma, 1981); Boils (Leaf, Pal, 1980); Diabetes (Root, Tribedi et al, 1982); Diarrhoea, Dysentry (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Gonorrhoea, ringworm (root, Tribedi et al, 1982); Headache (Leaf, Saxena and Dutta, 1975); Small-pox, scores, stomach complaints, syphilis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Veterinary (Leaf, Pal, 1980; Root, Pal, 1981; Part not mentioned, Chaudhuri et al, 1975); Edible vegitable (Leaf, Shah, 1984).
