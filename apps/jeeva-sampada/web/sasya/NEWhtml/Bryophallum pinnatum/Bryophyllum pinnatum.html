Chemistry:
Structure and absolute configuration of isocitric acid (fig. 1). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Quercetin-3-L-rhamnoside-L-arabinofuranoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Quercetin-3-diarabinoside, m.p. 190°C, and kaempferol-3-glucoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Alkanes C25-C35 (n-hentriacontane, n-triacontane predominating), alkanols C26-C34, a-amyrin, b-amyrin and sitosterol isolated from nonsaponifiable fraction. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

P-coumaric, ferulic, syringic, caffeic and p-hydroxybenzoic acids, quercetin and kaempferol detected in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Wax hydrocarbons (C25-35), wax alcohols (C26-36) and fatty acids obtained from wax of leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

A flavone glycoside – quercetin-3-O-a-L-arabinopyranosyl(1à2)-a-L-rhamnopyranoside – isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

A new cytotoxic bufadienolide-1,3,5-orthoacetate – bryophyllin A – isolated together with bersaldegenin-3-acetate and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

A potent cytotoxic bufadienolide – bryophyllin B (fig. 2) – isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation and structure elucidaton of bryophyllol (fig. 5), bryphollone (fig. 3), bryphollenone (fig. 8), bryophynol (fig. 4) and two homologous phenthrene derivatives – 2-(9)-decenyl)phenthrene (fig. 6a) and 2(undecenyl)phenthrene (fig. 6b) from leaves; 18a-oleanane, y-taraxsterol, a- and b-amyrins and their actetates also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Isolation and structure elucidation of 24-epiclerosterol [24(R)tigmasta-5,25-dien-3b-ol], 24(R)5a-stigmasta-7-25-dien-3b-ol, 5a-stigmast-24-en-3b-ol and 25-methyl-5a-ergost-24(28)-en-3b-ol from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

A potent cytotoxic compound – bersaldegenin-1,3,5-orthoacetate (fig. 7)– isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Glutamic acid was major amino acid of the 16 amino acids detected in leaf protein hydrolysate; methionine, tyrosine and phenylalanine identified as three limiting acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e


Pharmacology:
Quercetin-3-O-a-L-arabinopyranosyl(1à)2-a-L-rhamnopyranoside showed anti-allergic activity in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Bersaldegenin-1,3,5-orthoacetate inhibited growth of several cncer cell lines. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Two adult cattle died within 48 h of being fed a large amount of Bryophyllum pinnatum plants collected from a house garden. Clinical signs became apparent the day after feeding and included hypersalivation, ataxia, severe cardiac arrhythmia and laboured respiration. The main necropsy findings were acute rumenitis, reduction of bronchiolar lumens and emphysema (Reppas et al,1995).

Neuropharmacological studies were conducted in experimental animals (rats and mice) with the methanolic fraction of Bryophyllum pinnatum leaf extract. The fraction produced alteration of behaviour pattern, caused dose-dependent potentiation of pentobarbitone sleeping time and had significant analgesic activity. Significant reduction of exploratory behaviour and loss of residual curiosity were among the effects observed with the fraction. The observations suggest that the methanolic fraction of Bryophyllum pinnatum possesses a potent CNS depressant action.( Pal.,1999)

Bryophyllum pinnatum leaf 60% methanolic extract was found to inhibit the growth of five out of eight bacteria used, at a concentration of 25 mg/ml. (Akinpelu., 2000)

A methanolic fraction from an extract of Bryophyllum pinnatum leaves was found to possess significant anti-ulcer activity in nine different experimental animals models. Premedication tests in rats revealed that the extract possessed significant protective action against the gastric lesions induced by aspirin, indomethacin, serotonin, reserpine, stress and ethanol. Significant protection with extract treatment was observed to occur for aspirin-induced ulcer in pylorus-ligated rats and for histamine-induced duodenal lesions in guinea pigs. Significant enhancement of the healing process was also found to occur in acetic acid-induced chronic gastric lesions in rats.( Pal and Chaudhuri., 1991) Extracts of Boswellia serrata gum resins and its constituents, the boswellic acids (BAs), activate the mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPK) p42(MAPK) and p38 in isolated human polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNL). MAPK activation was rapid and transient with maximal activation after 1-2.5 min of exposure and occurred in a dose-dependent manner. The keto-BAs (11-keto-beta-BA and 3-O-acetyl-11-beta-keto-BA) gave substantial kinase activation at 30 microM, whereas other BAs lacking the 11-keto group were less effective. Moreover, 11-keto-BAs induced rapid and prominent mobilization of free Ca(2+) in PMNL. Inhibitor studies revealed that phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI 3-K) is involved in BA-induced MAPK activation, whereas a minor role was apparent for protein kinase C. MAPK activation by 3-O-acetyl-11-beta-keto-BA was partially inhibited when Ca(2+) was removed by chelation. Our results suggest that 11-keto-BAs might function as potent activators of PMNL by stimulation of MAPK and mobilization of intracellular Ca(2+). (Altmann et al 2002).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for amoebic dysentery (Leaf, Bhattacharjee et al, 1980); Body heat (Leaf, Saxena et al, 1981); boils, sores, wounds (Leaf, Baruah and Sarma, 1984; Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984; Karnick et al, 1981); Gastric complaints (Leaf, Baruah and Sarma, 1984); Headache (Leaf, Rao, 1981; Whole plant, Saxena and Dutta, 1975); Influenza (Leaf, Sharma et al, 1979); Insect bite (Leaf, Baruah and Sarma, 1984); Kidney stone and other disease (Leaf, Lal and Yadav, 1983; Rao, 1981)
