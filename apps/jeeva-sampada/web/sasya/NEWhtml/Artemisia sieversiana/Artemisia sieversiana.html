HABIT & HABITAT
Annual or biennial erect herb, sparsely hoary pubescent, 25-90 cm high. It is found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh at 2500-3500 m.


Availability :  

Specimen examined:

Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant grows wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem pale green, often particularly branched upwards, more or less angled, sulcate. Leaves generally 2-pinnatisect, sometimes pinnatifid or simply lobed, gradually becoming entire upwards; segments linear to oblong, obtuse. Heads bracteate, lax, long pedicelled in panicled racemes, secund, sometimes clustered, 5-6 mm in diam. Outer involucral bracts oblong, herbaceous with obscure, scarious margins, 4 mm long; inner one oblong to ovate with broad scarious margins, 2.5 x 2.5 mm, hoary pubescent, innermost broadly oblong, with broad scarious margins, 3 x 1.5 mm, glabrous. Receptacles hairy. Outer florets female, fertile, 1 x 0.5 mm. Corolla dull-white, slightly dialated at base, glandular. Disc floret fertile, hermaphrodite, yellow, tubular, 1.5-2.0 x 0.5 mm, glabrous or sparsely glandular, limb 5-fid, ovate, acute. Achenes brown, obovate, 1.5 x 0.8 mm, obscurely trigonous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence: Terminal branched spikes; Flower:  Pedicellate, drooping, yellowish, bracteate ,outer flower hermaphrodite, fertile;  inner flower pistillate, sterile;  involucre bract, glabrous ; Pappus 0; Corolla: Corolla of outer flower slender, tubular,; corolla of pistillate flower regular, tubular,; Androecium:  Stamens 5, inserted on the corolla and alternating with the segments, filaments usually free above, anther bases obtuse, entire ;Gynoecium: Ovary 1-celled, ovules solitary erect, style arms of pistillate flower truncate, usually penicillate ; tips often connate, in the sterile flower; Fruit: Minute, ellipsoid, smooth, brown; Seed: Erect. Flowering & Fruiting:  July-September.  Plant is pollinated by insect. Seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plant is a good tonic, febrifuge and anthelmintic. It is applied externally as discutient and antiseptic.

 


Chemistry:

NBPGR:
 A. siversiana is a valuable source for obtaining chamazulene. The population of this species, growing in the kirghiz SSR, is hetrogeneous according to its essential oil compn. forms are observed contg. Chamazulene (1st group) or chamazulenogen (2nd group) or lacking these compounds (3rd group).
NBRI:
 Presence of artabsin,m.p. 135°C, absinthin, m.p. 179°C and another compound, m.p. 156°C. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Artemolin was isolated along with absinthin from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

New guaianolides- 11a,13-dihydro-4(Z) hanphyllin (fig. 1), ab-hydroxy-8-desoxy-11a,13-dihydrorupicolin B (fig. 2), 11a, 13-dihydroestafiatin (fig. 4), 2a,3a-epoxy, 11a,13-dihydro dehydrocostuslactone (fig. 3), 11-epiabsinthin (fig. 5a), 10’,11’-epiabsinthin (fig. 5b), 11,10’,11’ epiabsinthin (fig. 5c), artesieversin,8-desoxycumambrin B (fig. 6) and 8-isovaleryloxy-nerylisovalerate (fig. 7)- isolated from aerial parts of Mongolian plant along with sesamin,e,a-ashantin,e,e-sesartemin, e,a-e;e- and a,a-yan-gambins, absinthin, ludartin and anabsinthin; structure of new compound determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
