Chemistry:

·         Artocarpetin (fig. 1a), m.p.310°C (5,2’,4’-trihydroxy-7-methoxy flavone), artocarpanone(fig. 2), m.p.210°C (5,2’,4’-trihydroxy-7-methoxyflavanone), artocarpin (fig. 3a), dihydromorin, artocarpesin (fig. 4), mixture of morion, cyanomaclurin (fig. 6) and isoartocarpin (fig. 7a) from heart wood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

·         Cyanomaclurin and norartocarpetin (fig. 1b) from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

·         Proteins from latex by electrophoretic analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

·         Artocarpin dimethyl ether (fig. 3b), isoartocarpin dimethylether (fig. 7b) and dihydrocycloartocapin from wood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

·         Revised structure of cyanomaclurin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

·         Cycloartenyl acetate, cycloartenol and cycloartenone were isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·         A new compound- artoflavanone was isolated from roots besides b-sitosterol, betulinic acid and ursolic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·         Aurantiamide acetate isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·         Morin from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·         A a-D-galactose- specific lectin containing 3.0% carbohydrate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

·         Linoleic (40.2), Palmitic (30.2) and ricinoleic (7.2%) acids determined in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·         Two new prenylflavones- artonins A (fig. 8a) and B (fig. 8b)- isolated from root bark and their structures elucidated, heterophyllin and cycloheterophyllin also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

·         Root bark afforded two new Diels-Alder-type adducts- artonins C (fig. 9a) and D (fig. 9b) whose structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

·         Glucose, galactose, mannose, mannitol, glucuronic and succinic acid isolated from aqueous extracts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

·         Root and bark yielded two flavone derivatives (I, fig. 15 and II, fig. 16) having antihypertensive and antibacterial activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

·         Anti-plaque compounds- (+) catechin, procyanidin B3, afzelechin (4aà8)-catechin and procyanidin C1- isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

·         Three new isoprenylated flavones- artonins J (fig. 10a), K (fig. 10b) and L (fig. 10c) from root bark and their structure determination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

·         Two new flavanones- hetero- flavanones A (fig. 14a) and B (fig. 14b) isolated from root bark of Formosan plant and their structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.

·         New flavonoids – cycloartocarpin A (fig. 11) and heteroflavanone C (fig. 14c) - and a new fatty ester-9-hydroxytridecyl docosanoate (fig. 13) isolated from root bark and characterized; in addition, b-sitosterol, betulin, betulinic and ursolic acid isolated . (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.

·         Two new tetracyclic triterpenoid- enantiomers (III (fig. 17a and IV fig. 17b) isolated from dried latex and characterized as 24 (R)- and 24(S) 9,19-cyclolanost-3-one-24,25diols; cycloartenone and cycloartenol also isolated. 9,19-cyclolansot-23-en-3b,25-diol and 9,19-cyclolanost-25-en-3b-24-diol isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)j.

·         Isolation of a phenolic compound – heterophylol (fig. 12) from root bark and its structure determination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)k.

·         Six prenylflavonoids including two new compounds have in been isolated from the root bark of A. heterophyllus. The new prenylflavones have been named artocarpetin B [8-(g,g-dimethylallyl)-5,4’-dihydroxy-7,2’-dimethoxy-flavone] and heteroartonin A [3,3’-di-(g,g-dimethulallyl)-5,7-2’,5’-tetrahydroxy-4’-methoxyflavone. The known compounds were Kuwanon T, artonin A, artonin B and heterophyllin. (Chung et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 334983b.

·         A new flavanone, a new prenylflavone, a novel phenolic compound, heterophylol reported previously and nine known flavanoids were isolated from the root of A. heterophyllus. The two new flavanoids were characterized as 5,2’-di-hydroxy-7,4’-dimethoxyflavanone and 8-(g,g-dimethylallyl)-5,2’,4’-trihydroxy-7-methoxyflavone respectively. (Lin et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 165112j.

·         Seven tetracyclic tritepenoids of cycloartane series were isolated. The compounds have been characterized as cycloartenol, cycloartenone, 9,19-cyclolanost-25-ene-3b,24-diol (characterized as its diacetate) and (24R)- and (24S)-9,19-cyclolanost-3-one-24,25-diol. (Barik et al, 1995). MAPA, 1995, 9504, 2447.

·         A new natural Diels Alder type adduct, artonin X along with two known Diels Alder type, adducts, was isolated from the bark of A. heterophyllus. (Shinomiya et al, 1995). MAPA, 1996,9602, 0900.

·         Hexane ether extract of Artocarpus heterophyllus yielded three triterpenoids, viz., (24R)- and (24S) – 9,19-cyclolanost-25-ene-3b,24-diol  and 9,19-cyclolanost-25-ene-3,25-diol. (Barik et al, 1997). MAPA 1997, 9703-1660; C.A. 1997, 126, 303644z.

Fructose, glucose and sucrose were found to be the major sugars in all parts of jackfruit, exception the bark, which is devoid of glucose. Capric, myristic, lauric, palmitic, oleic, stearic, linoleic and arachidic acids were found as major fatty acids. (Chowdhury et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 80473k.



Pharmacology:

·         Procyanidin C1 strongly inhibited glucosyl trnsferase from Streptococcus mutans. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)l.

·         Benign and malignant effusions were studied using horseradish – peroxidase – conjugated jackfruit lectin. The reactivity proliferated mesothelial cell of benign effusions showed a predominance of mild staining, whereas moderate and intense staining was predominant in malignant effusions. Intense and irregular lectin binding was observed in macrophages irrespectively of the cause of effusion. (Sujuthan et al, 1996) C.A. 1996, 125, 218493j.



Ethnobotany:

Medicine antihelminth (Leaf, Baruah and Sarma, 1984); Carbuncle (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Post natal (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Small–pox, sores, sterility, stomach complaints, toothache (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Skin disease (latex, Bhattacharjee et al, 1980); Stomach complaints (Root, Karnick et al, 1981); Edible (Fruit, Prasad and Abraham, 1984; Shah, 1984; Leaf, Hajra and Baishya, 1981; Seed); Yellow dye (wood, Mittre, 1981); Furniture (Wood, Hajra and Baishya, 1981); Gum to seal holes in earthen pots (Latex, Rao, 1990); Music instrument (Wood, Jain and Borthakur, 1980; Joseph and Kharkongor, 1981).
