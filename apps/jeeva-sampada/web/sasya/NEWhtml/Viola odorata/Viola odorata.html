HABIT & HABITAT
Annual or perennial, prostrate herbs found in Jammu & Kashmir. Often cultivated elsewhere particularly in West Bengal, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. N. W. Asia, Europe, N. Africa.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual or perennial, prostrate herbs; rootstock stout; stems short; stolon 15-20 cm long. Leaves orbicular-reniform to broadly ovate, 1.5-3.5 x 2-4 cm, rounded to obtuse at apex, cordate at base, crenate-serrate at margin, glabrous to strigose; petioles 2-8 cm long; stipules ovate-lanceolate to subulate-lanceolate, 8-12 x 3-5 mm, glandular toothed, glabrous. Peduncles slender, 4-12 cm long, bibracteolate at middle.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers 1.0-1.5 cm across, violet or white, tinged with purple. Sepals ovate, c 7 mm long and c 2 mm wide, obtuse. Petals obovate, orbicular; lateral ones bearded or not; spur cylindric-obtuse, c 5 mm long. Stamens 5. Capsules globose, c 5 mm in diam., hirsute.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Uses: Roots are emetic. Flowers are used in headache, purgative, throat ache and lung troubles.
 Chemistry:

·        A compound, mp. 188°, and odoratine, mp. 62°, from roots and rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Isolation of dodecan-1-ol, pentadeca-5, 10-dien-1-ol, pentadec-5-en-1-ol, octadec-1-ene, eicos-1-ene and octadeca-9, 12-dienoic acid from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        The wax of V. odorata mainly contains hydrocarbons and fatty and cyclic alcohols and their esters with fatty acids. (Asibekowa et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 205495f.

·        The banafsha is important herbal drug is considered to be constituted mainly of flowers of Viola odrata. (Mehrotra, 1998).

·        The head space volatiles and n-hexane extract from Viola odorata L. flowers were investigated by GC-MS. A total of 93 compounds were identified of which 85 compounds were newly identified from Viola odorata L. flowers. The compounds newly identified in this study, 3-oxo-a-ionol, a-ionol,cis-3-hexenol, trans-2-hexenol, cis, cis-3,6-nonadienol, trans-2-hexenal, trnas-2-nonenal, cis-3-hexenyl acetate, cis-jasmone and 1,4-dimethoxybenzene were to make a contribution to the characteristic scent of Viola odorata L. flowers. (Tsuji and Yomogida, 1997). C.A. 126, 297457x.

Pharmacology:

·        Significant oral antipyretic activity in rabbits was exhibited by hexane-, chloroform- and water-soluble extracts of Viola odorata. (Khattak et al, 1985).

·        “Banfasha” an important herbal drug is considered to be constituted mainly of flowers of Viola odorata. (Mehrotra et al, 1998). C.A. 129, 72029w.

·        Cytotoxic activities of three naturally occurring macrocyclic peptides (cyclotides) isolated from the two violets Viola odorata L. were investigated. Activity profiles of the cyclotides differed significantly from those of antitumor drugs in clinical use, which may indicate a new mode of action. This together with the exceptional chemical and biological stability of cyclotides, makes them interesting in particular for their potential as pharmacological tools and possibly as leads to antitumor agents. (Lindholm et al, 2002).
