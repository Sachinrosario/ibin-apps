Chemistry:
Alkaloids – fangchinoline, d-tetrandrine, dl-tetrandrine, d-isochondrodendrine – isolated from roots, showed pharmacological activity similar to that of d-tubocurarine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

 Five new bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloids – cycleapeltine (fig. 4), cycleadrine (fig. 2a), cycleacurine (fig. 1), cycleanorine (fig. 2b) and cycleahomine (fig. 3) chloride – isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Tetrandrine mono-N2’-oxide isolated and characterized; isochondodendrine and chondocurine also obtained. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

(-)Curine isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

(+)Tetrandrine, homoaromaline, (-)curine, (+)isochondrodendrine and an unidentified alkaloid isolated from roots ofplant grown in Hai-nan. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Magnoflorine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

In addition to above – mentioned alkaloids, isotetrandrine, berbamine, fangchinoline, cycleanine and cycleanorine identified in roots and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Synthesis of fangchinoline. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

A water soluble rhamnoglacturonan, composed of uronic acid, galactose and rhamnose, isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Alkaloid extract of roots showed cytotoxic and antimalarial activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Phaeanthine and its (1S, 1'S)-enantiomer (tetrandrine) isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Dicentrine isolated as a major alkaloid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

(+)tetrandrine, (-)Iirnacine, (+)thalrugosine, (+)homoaromoline and (-) cycleapeltine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Isolation and structure elucidation of two bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloids -(-)2'-norlimacine (fig. 5b) and (+)cycleabarbatine (fig. 5a) from roots; in addition, (+) tetrandrine-2'-b-N -oxide, (+)berbamine, (-)repandine, (+)cycleanorine, (+) daphnandrine, (-)curine, (+) coclaurine atld (-)N-methylcoclaurine also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.


Pharmacology:

Tetrandrine decreased aseptic inflammation in rats, decreased vascular permeability and serum hyaluronidase activity, when given to adrenalectomised rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

(+)Tetrandrine (1.0 µM) caused 50% inhibition of thymidine and uridine uptake in HeLa cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

Tetrandrine (7 mg/kg, i.v.) significantly decreased toxic effect of cardiac glycosides, strophanthine and oubain, in guinea pigs. The dosage of strophanthin causing cardiac abnormality and death was increased by 89.4 – 113.1 and 67.0% respectively and corresponding values for ouabain were increased by 32.4 – 54.3 and 51.2% respectively. However, the cardiac glycoside toxicity-decreasing effect of tetrandrine was completely eliminated by Ca2+ administration. Tetrandrine was useful as supplement in clinical application of cardiac glycosides. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

Tetrandrine (32.0 µM) decreased contractibility and automaticity and prolonged refractory period, bur exerted no influence on excitability of guinea pig heart due to its antiarrhythmic action. The negative inotropic and automaticity effects of tetrandrine suggested that it might be a Ca2+ antagonist. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

Tetrandrine (9 mg/kg, i.v.) prevented arrhythmia induced by barium chloride (4 mg/kg, i.v.) in anaesthetized rats and protected rats from ventricular fibrillation and death induced by calcium chloride (120 mg/kg, i.v.). It 96 mg/kg,i.v.) shortened duration of ventricular tachycardia from 18 to 4.4 min in anaesthetized guinea pigs with arrhythmias induced by ouabain (100 µg/kg/2 min, i.v.) (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)h.

Tetrandrine given i.v. (10 mg.kg and 15 mg/kg lowered systemic blood pressure, decreased left ventricular pressure and dp/dt max in anaesthetized open-chest dogs. It also decreased the heart rate and prolonged PR internal. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)i.

Tetrandrine inhibited Ca2+ - induced contractions of isolated rat uterus. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)j.

O,O’-Dimethyl-(-)curine dimethiodide and dimethochloride were compared with tubocurarine for neuromuscular blocking activity. Studies showed that dimethyl derivatives were 1.5-3.0 times as potent as the parent compounds and 0.5-4.0 times as potent as tubocurarine in rats. Neuromuscular-blosking produced by (-)curine dimethiodide and its derivatives was readily reversed by neostigmine in sciatic-tibialic preparations. Those quarternary salts were nondepolarising blocking drugs like tubourarine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)k.

Dimethyl-(-)curine was more powerful neuromuscular blocker than other alkaloids; dimethyl-(-)curine dimethylchloride was four times as potent as dimethyl-(-)curine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)l.

Tetrandrine inhbtied contraction of guinea pig tracheal and ling dtrps and human bronchial and ling strips induced by slow reacting substances of anaphylaxis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

At 25.0 mg/kg, i.v., given prior to SRS-A, it antagonized the augmentation of respiratory air flow by SRS-A. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Tetrandrine antagonized noradrenaline-induced contraction of isolted rabbit vascular strips and inhibited K+ (60.0 mM)-induced contraction of thoracic aorta strips. It also antagonized calcium-related vascular effects. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

Tetrandrine suppressed delayed-type -hypersensitivity in mice, effect being more pronounced when treatment was begun two days prior to immunisation with sheep erythrocytes. Similar dosage regimen delayed rejection of cardiac transplants. These immunomodulatory properties of tetrandrine may partly account for its potential in treatment of diseases where immunological mechanism play major role in pathogenesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Tetrandrine inhibited increase in cytoplasmic free Ca2+ Induced by calcimycin, LTB4 and platelet -activating factor in rat neutrophils due to decrease of Ca2+ influx. Apparently tetrandrine acts as Ca2+ antagonist which may be related to its antiallergic actions. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

Tetrandrine at 0.1 g/day was effective in treatment of malaria. Its IC50 value against chloroquine- resistant Plasmodium falciparum in human erythrocyte in Vitro was 197.5 nM. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.

Tetrandrine (0.1 to 5.0 mg/ml) inhibited capacity of human mononuclear leucocytes to produce tumor necrosis factor alpha in culture when stimulated with killed Staphylococcus aureus. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.

Tetrandrine mobilized Ca2+ from intracellular stores of HL-60 cells in concentration-dependent manner, with EC50 value 20.0 mM. Tetrandrine-induced intracellular Ca2+ release was independent of protein kinase C activity and a-adrenoceptor activation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)j.

Tetrandrine, in addition to its well-known vasodilator effect via blockade of Ca2+ channels, produced a novel contractile effect on dog saphenous vein which was specifically associated with activation of postjunctional a2-adrenoceptors functionally characteristic in venous smooth muscle. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)k.

Study of relaxant action of (1S, 1'S) tetrandrine and isotetrandrineon rat aortic strips in presence and absence of extracellular calcium showed that contractile response elicited by depolarising solution (KCl 80 mM) or noradrenaline (1.0 mM), is concentration dependent. In Ca2+ - free solution both alkaloids inhibited contraction induced by noradrenaline. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)l.

The inhibitory effect of the root of Cyclea peltata Lam. on nephrolithiasis induced in rats by feeding with ethylene glycolated water (1%) for 35 days was summarized. Ethylene glycol administration led to oxalate stone formation, as indicated by its high level in urine. Complementary to this anion, the cation calcium level in urine was elevated. These two ions may have contributed to the formation of calcium oxalate stones. In addition to high serum potassium, a low serum magnesium level contributed to stone formation. Simultaneous administration of the powdered root of Cyclea peltata resulted in decreased urinary oxalate and calcium. Likewise, serum potassium was lowered and magnesium was elevated. (Christina et al,2002)
