DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An erect branched, annual herb, 0.3-0.9 m high; branches sharply quadrangular. Leaves linear-obovate to lanceolate, 5-8 x 1-2 cm, acuminate at both ends, slightly undulate at margin, glabrous, pale beneath, main nerves 4-6 pairs, slender; petiole to 1.5 cm.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence  solitary in panicles ; Flower: bracteate,pedicillate, pubescent small,red;  Calyx: Sepals five, linear, lanceolate and pubscent ; Corolla: hairy, white, spotted, rose purple in colour light,hairy and bilipped ;   Androecium:  didynamous, stamens inserted in corolla tube alternate with lobes,filaments hairy and erect, exerted, dithecous, opening by longitudinal slits.;Gynoecium:   Ovary and the base of flower sub glabrous or very sparesly hairy ;Fruit  capsule linear, oblong and acute at both the ends ; Seed numerous, yellowish brown, glabrous, minute, sub-cordate. 

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting: August - November Pollination by insects. Seeds are dispersed by the rupture mechanism of capsules.

GERMPLASM CHARACTERIZATION:

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: It is propagated through scattering of seeds.

Soil type:  Plant can be cultivated in wide variety of soils from loam to lateritic soils with moderate fertility. It can also be cultivated in shady wastelands.

Nutrient requirements: Plant requires nitrogen and P205 to increase the herb yield. Rotten farmyard manure is also beneficial for the plant.

Water management: In the areas with well distributed rainfall no irrigation is required but regular irrigations are required in the areas of scanty rainfall.

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is harvested  for maximum biomass after 90-100 days of planting  beyond this leaves start shredding. If the crop is raised as an annual crop it is harvested when flowering initiates.

Pharmacology:
    Used as curative and preventive in snake venom poisoning. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.

Neither leaf and stem extract of kalmegh administered orally nor andrographolide given s.c. or orally changed blood sugar level of normal or diabetic rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Kalmegh increased biliary flow and liver weight in rat and decreased hexobarbital-induced sleeping time. It was less potent then phenobarbital. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Apigenin-7,4’-di-O-methyl ether produced significant dose-dependent antiulcer activity in shay rats, histamine- induced ulcer in guinea pigs and in aspirin-induced ulcers in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)k.

Pretreatment with a single dose of Kalmegh leaf (500mg/kg, p.o.) or andrographolide (5mg/kg, p.o.) prevented carbon tetrachloride- induced increase of serum glutamate-oxaloacetate transminase (SGOT) and glutamate-pyruvate transminase (SGPT) but decreased liver levels of these enzymes in dog. These changes were not observed by simultaneous treatment with either of the drugs. Pretreatment of rats with kalmegh leaf or andrographolide, however, failed to arrest carbon tetrachloride-induced changes of SGOT and SGPT in blood serum and liver. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)l.

Total root flavonoids effective against experimental myocardial ischemic necrosis induced by isoprenaline in rats and by ligation of left descending coronary artery in rabbits; decreased infraction size, and pathological changes in ST segment and on wave inhibited; LD50 of flavonoids in mice, 1.15g/kg, i.v. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Andrographolide (5.0mg/kg and 10.0 mg/kg) or single dose of leaf extract (0.5g/kg and 1.0g/kg) given orally to adult male rats produced dose-related and time-dependent characteristic activation of brush-border membrane bound hydrolase, lactase, maltase and sucrase in duodenum, jejunum and ileum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.

Alcoholic extract of plant exhibited antidiarrhoeal activity against E.Coli enterotoxins in animal models. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Leaf extract showed significant cytotoxicity against KB and P388 cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Andrographolide, neoandrographolide, deoxyandrographolide and andrographiside tested for antidiarrhoeal activity against E. coli enterotoxin in vivo. Andrographolide and neoandrographolide showed activity similar to that of loperamide against E. coli LT and LT/ST enterotoxins. Andrographolide had greater activity against ST enterotoxin, the most common cause of epidemics of neonatal diarrhoea. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

Andrograholide exhibited potent cytotoxic effect in KB cell cultures and P-388 lymphocytic leukaemia whereas 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroandrographolide and neoandrographolide did not show any cytotoxicity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.

Andrographolide exhibited strong choleretic action when administered i.p. to rats. It induced increase in bile flow together with change in physical properties of bile secretion. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.

It also produced significant dose-dependent (1.5 to 12.0 mg/kg) Choleretic effect (4.8 to 73.0%) as shown by increase in bile flow, bile salt and bile acids in conscious rats and anaesthetized guinea pigs. Paracetamol-induced decrease in volume of bile was prevented significantly by andrographolide, pretreatment. It was found to be the more potent than silymarin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)j.

Andrographolide and neoandrographolide significantly stimulated antibody and delayed-type hypersensitivity response to sheep red blood cells (SRBC) in mice. Plant extract also stimulated non-specific immune response of animals. Stimulation of antigen-specific and non-specific immune responses with andrographolide, was less than with ethanol extracts of plant, suggesting there by that constituents other than andrographolide present in extract may also be contributing immunostimulation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)k.

Administration of andrographolide, andrographiside and neoandrographolide (0.1g/kg, i.p. for 7 days) caused significant elevation of different enzymes of cellular antioxidant defense mechanism and reduction in lipid per oxidation in (Cl4-treated mice, revealing that antioxidant action may play a role in antihepatoxic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)l.

Andrographolide found to be almost devoid of antihepatitis B virus surface antigen (anti-HBS) like activity whereas picroliv showed promising activity antihepatotoxic activity of 0.1g/kg, i.p., andrographolide was compared with 861.33 mg/kg, i.p. of total methanolic extract and 761.33mg/kg, i.p., of andrographolide free methanolic extract of plant using CCl4 intoxicated rats. Inhibition of CCl4 induced increase in biochemical parameters (GOT, GPT, SAP, serum bilirubin and hepatic triglycerides) was found to be 48.6% (andrographolide), 32.0% (methanolic extract) and 15.0% (andrographo-lide-free methanolic extract). Further andrographolid at some dose completely normalize CCl4 induced increased in pentobarbitone- induced sleep time to mice. Its LD50 (i.p.) inmice was 11.46g/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)l.

Hepatoprotective effect of andrographolide was studied on acute hepatitis induced in rats by single dose of galactosamine or paracetamol. Treatment of rats with 0.4 g/kg i.p., or 0.8g/kg, p.o., 48,24 and 2hr. before galactosamine administration or with 0.2g/kg, i.p., 1,4 and 7hr. after paracetamol challenge, caused complete normalization of toxin induced increase in levels of five biochemical parameters and significantly ameliorated toxin induced histopathological change in liver of experimental animals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)m.

Andrographolide, andrographiside and neoandrographolide investigated for their protective effect against hepatotoxicity induced in mice by CCl4 or tertiary butyl hydroperoxide. Pretreatment of mice with these substance (0.1g/kg, i.p.) for 3 consecutive days decreased malondialdehyde formation, reduced glutathione depletion and enzyme leakage of glutamic pyruvate transaminase and alkaline phosphate in both groups of toxin-treated animals. Andrographolide exhibited lower protectivity than andrographiside and neoandrographolide which were as effective as silymarin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)n.

Single dose pretreatment with andrographolide (0.02-0.2g/kg, i.p.) or aqueous extract of leaves (0.3-1.00 g/kg, p.o.) 48hr. and 4hr. respectively before reduced toxicity induced by ethanol. Similar results obtained by 7 days pretreatment with andrographolide (0.1g/kg/day, i.p.) or aqueous extract of leaves (0.5g/kg/day, p.o.). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)n.

Andrographis paniculata showed potent cell differentiation- including activity on mouse myeloid leukemia cells. (Matmaa et al, 1994).

Antihepatotoxic activity of diterpenes of Andrographis paniculata (Kal-megh) against Plasmodium beghe -» induced hepatic damages in Mastomys matalensis was studied, which shows significant, result. (Chander et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 74860k.

Patients receiving Andrographis paniculata showed that post Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy pyuria and hematuria were reduced to 0.69 and 0.55 time of pre Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy value. (Maungman et al, 1995).

Mechanism of Andrographis paniculata in alleviating the Ca(2+)- overloading in dog model during the process of ischemic reperfusion was studied. The findings indicate the Andrographis paniculata may improve the activity of sarcolemma ATPase in alleviating the Ca(2+) and Na+- overloading by decreasing the harmful effect of oxygen free radicals. (Guo et al, 1995).

Crude extract from Andrographis paniculata could prevent myocardial ischemia and reperfusion injury. (Guo et al, 1996).

The study indicates that the aqueous extract of Andrographis paniculata lowers Systolic Blood Pressure in the Spontaneously Hypertensive rats possibly by reducing circulating Angriotensin- Converting Enzyme in plasma as well as by reducing free radical levels in the kidneys. (Zhang and Tan, 1996).

Andrographolide, a diterpene lactone isolated from Andrographis paniculata showed anti-inflammatory activity in different models of inflammation in rats after oral administration at doses of 30,100 and 300mg/kg. (Madav et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 194999w.

An antifeedant compound against the diamondback moth (DBM) from the hexane layer of the extract of Andrographis paniculata has been isolated. The compound was identified as 14-deoxyandrographolide. (Wawan et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 71971q.

Andrographis pniculata possesses the effect of antioxidentation, preserving endothelial function and maintaing the balance of nitric oxide/enothelin. (Wang et al, 1997)Andrographis paniculata (API0134) possesses the effects of antioxidation, preserving endothelial function, and maintaining the balance of Nitric oxide/endothelin. (Wang et al, 1997).

Skin lightening cosmetics contain 0.005-20.0 wt% Andrographis paniculata extracts showing melanin formation- inhibiting and tyrosinase inhibitors activities. (Tanaka et al, 1997) C.A. 1997, 126, 216445s.

Andrographolide inhibits nitrite synthesis by suppressing expressions of inducible nitric oxide synthase protein in vitro. This inhibition of inducible nitric oxide synthase synthesis may contribute to the beneficial haemodynamic effects of andrographolide in endotoxic shock. (Chiou et al, 1998).

Andrographolide, a diterpene isolated from Andrographis paniculata, has been shown to have several biological activities including analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory effects. (Habtemariam, 1998).

Andrographis paniculata used in number of clinical conditions including allergic manifestations. Two diterpenes, andrographholide and neoandrographolide, isolated from the plant, were evaluated for their antiallergic activity. These were tested for anti-PCA (Passive cutaneous anaphylaxis) and mast cell stabilizing activities in rats, significant anti-PCA activity was observed. (Gupta et al, 1998).

The cardiovascular activity of 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroandrographolide (DDA) from Andrographis paniculata was elucidated in anaesthetized Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats. In anaesthetized rats, 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroandrographolide produced significant falls in mean arterial blood pressure (MAP) and heart rate in a dose-dependent manner with the maximum decrease of 37.6+/- 2.6% and 18.1+/- 4.8% respectively. In the isolated right atria, 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroandrographolide caused negative chronotropic action and antagonized isoproterenol-induced chronotropic actions in a non-competitive and dose-dependent manner. These results further supported the bradycardia-inducing and beta-adrenoceptor antagonistic properties of 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroandrographolide in vivo. (Zhang et al, 1998).

Diterpenes, andrographolide and neoandrographolide, isolated from Andrographis paniculata were tested for allergic activity. These were tested for anti-Passive cutaneous anaphylaxis and mast cell stabilizing activity against compound 48/80 and in sensitized mast cells, against egg elbumin induced degranulation. The activities were found to be comparable to disodium cromoglycate. (Gupta et al, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9806, 3343.

Andrographolide shows antiallergic activity. (Madav et al, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9806, 3366.

The aqueous extract was given orally to the animals suffering from liver damage, ultimately leading to tumor induced by hexachlorocyclohexane (BHC). The histopathology and the various biochemical constituents were monitored. The parameters like protein and liver p marker enzymes like SGPT, SGOT and alkaline phosphatase were analysed in time intervals. All these enzymes were found to be significantly high and the protein concentrations were reduced in BHC treated animals. The liver marker enzymes were found to be lowered in animals which were supplemented with the AP extract. Protein levels, which were found decrease on BHC treatment, have shown an increase with AP supplementation. The results confirm the hepatoprotective property of AP and its probable role in delaying hepatic tumorgenic conditions. (Trivedi and Rawal, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9902, 1062.

Andrographolide, a diterpene isolated from Andrographis paniculata, exhibited several biological activities including analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory effects. Incubation of endothelial cells with non-toxic concentrations of andrographolide attenuated the tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF)-induced intercellular adhesion molecule-I expression. Similar concentration ranges of andrographolide also inhibited the TNF-induced endothelial-monocyte adhesion in a concentration dependent manner. (Habtemariam, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9903, 1711.

Twelve animals were administered Afanil (containing Andrographis paniculata with other plant drugs) 100ml intraruminally (10 cases) or orally (2cases) and the treatment was repeated the next day or if required for 3rd day. Buffaloes were treated with Timpol powder 80gm orally or intraruminally. The results reveled that release of gases started rapidly, distension was relieved in 26hr. along with restoration of normal appearance of the flank. Eleven of the 12 buffaloes treated with afanil recovered (1 died) whereas only 5 of the 8 animals treated with Timpol showed recovery (3 died). Afanil being in emulsion form was easily given intraruminally. It appeared more potent for quicker eructation of the gases and the recovery rate was faster. Relief was comparatively slower with Timpol as two animals recovered after two days treatment and the other 3 after three days treatment. (Bhardwaj, 1998). MAPa 1998, 9903, 1653.

Two diterpenes, andrographolide and neoandrographolide, isolated from the plant and evaluated for their antiallergic activity showed significant anti-PCA activity. The compounds also possessed mast cells stabilizing activity against compound 48/80 and in sensitized mast cells, against egg albumin induced degranulation. The activities were found to be comparable to disodium cromoglycate. (Gupta et al, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9902, 0974.

Neoandrographolide exhibited the highest inhibitory action with an IC50 of 53.5 mM against furin. (Basak et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 348934t.

Effect of Andrographis paniculata extract on progesterone in blood plasma of pregnant rats was studied. (Panossian et al, 1999).

Andrographis paniculata have been screened out systematically for their effectiveness in inhibition of bacterial population of raw water at variable pH level i.e., 6.0, 6.5, 7.0, 7.5 and 8.0. (Kumar and Gopal, 1999).

Andrographis paniculata showed inhibitory effect of andrographolide on platelet activating factor induced platelet aggregation. (Amroyan, 1999).

HD-03 a multi-herbal formulation, consisting of Andrographis paniculata with other plant parts was investigated for its anticholestatic activity in TAA-induced cholestasis in anaesthetized guinea pigs. (Mitra et al, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9904-2439.

Hepatogard contain powder of 11 plants including Andrographis paniculata, increased the breaking strength of granulation tissue but not of incised wound. It reversed the dexamethasone induced decrease in breaking strength in both incised wound and granulation tissue. Even on hydroxyproline concentration it reversed the dexamethasone induced decrease in the hydroxy-proline content of granulation time and thus has the potential for antagonizing the antihealing activity of steroids in patients receiving steroid therapy. (Nadig and Rao, 1999). MAPA, 1999, 9904, 2445.

In vitro and in vivo studies revealed that Andrographis paniculata produced considerable antimalarial effect. Chloroform extract in vitro did show better effect than methanol extract. The chloroform extract showed complete parasite growth inhibition as low as 0.05mg/ml drug dose within 24hr. incubation period (Andrographis paniculata) as compared to methanol extract of drug dose of 2.5mg/ml but under incubation time of 48hr. of the same plant species. In vivo activity of Andrographis paniculata also demonstrated higher antimalarial effect than other two plant species. (Rahman et al, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9904, 2574.

Carbon tetrachloride was unable to produce detriminal effect on the liver of albino rats treated with Andrographis paniculata. In a clinical trial the drugs were found to exhibit a curative action. (Singh and Dubey, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9902, 1053.

Andrographis paniculata showed antioxidant activity. (Huang et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 132281w.

The contents of andrographolide and neoandrographolie contained in Andrographis paniculata were analyzed by reverse phase HPLC using a Merch-lechroma KP-C18 column with acetonitrate water (35:65) as the mobile phase. The recoveries for andrographolide and neoandrographolide were 102.62% and 98.6% respectively. (Chen et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 163433b.

Reversed phase liquid chromatography method for the simultaneous determination of three major andrographolides and neoandrograholide, 14-deoxy 11,12-didehydroandrographolide in Andrographis paniculata leaf extract with U.V. detection at 230 nm was described. (Jain et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 132, 339469j.

A rapid and simple HPTLC method has been developed for the simultaneous quantitative estimation of the biologically active diterpenoids, 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroxyandrographolide, andrographolide, neoandrographolide and andrographiside in Andrographis paniculata. The analysis combines the isolation and separation of andrographolide derivatives on silica gel 60F254 HPTLC plates with spot visualization of scanning at 540nm, Methanol was found to be visualizing and scanning at 540nm. (Saxena et al, 2000). C.A 2000, 132, 248100z.

A simple and rapid method for isolation of andrographolide from the leaves of Andrographis paniculata is reported. This involves extraction of the leaf powder by cold maceration in a 1:1 mixture of dichloromethane and methanol and isolation of andrographolide directly from the resulting extract by recrystallisation. The identity of the compound was confirmed through IR, UV, mass and melting point and co-chromatography with a reference standard on TLC. (Rajini et al, 2000). MAPA 2000, 06, 3692.

Andrographolide showed male reproductive toxicity. The study also confirms the possible prospective use of andrographolide in male contraception. (Akbarsha and Murugaian, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 217315b.

Andrographis paniculata and other plant extract which is useful for treatment of rough skin, give loss to the hair and cause smooth hair combing. (Toki et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 132, 325852s.

Andrographis paniculata had an antipyretic effect in rabbits and it synergized the action of promethazine.(Wang et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 274042t.

The medicinal composition containing Andrographis paniculata may be used as anti-infectious, anti-pyretic and medicine. (Wang, 2000). C.A 2001, 135, 372764w. CN-1, 293, 955 (Cl. A61K31/34), 9 May 2001, App. 2000, 120, 573, 27 Oct 2000; 9 pp (ch).

Andrographis paniculata has the effect of anti oxidation, can protect the mesangial cells, maintain Nitric Oxide/ Endothelin balance and prevent the renal tissue from pathological changes. (Wu et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 247027f.

 The effect of a standardized extract (SH-10). Of Andrographis paniculata fixed combination (Kan jang) in the treatment of uncomplicated upper-respiratory tract infections was investigated. (Melchior et al, 2000).

A phase I trial of andrographolide in HIV positive patients and normal volunteers was carried out which has an main objective to assess safety and tolerability and secondarily to assess effects on plasma virion HIV-I RNA levels and CD4(+) lymphocite levels. (Calabrese et al, 2000).

Dried extract of Andrographis paniculata show blockage of voltage operated calcium channels in rats. (Burgos et al, 2000).

Andrographolide, a diterpenoid lactone has an ability to be an antiflammatory drug by preventing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and nutrophils adhesion. (Shen et al, 2000).

The ethanolic extract of Andrographis paniculata not only possesses an antihyperglycaemic property, but may also reduce oxidative stress in diabetic rats. (Zhang and Tan, 2000).

Andrographis paniculata was tested on the hydraulic permeability of water in the presence of bile salt through a transport cell model. The data on hydraulic permeability were calculated as t (time). JV=Lp x AP where Lp= hydraulic conductivity and AP is the pressure difference. It was observed that the value of controlled hydraulic permeability (0.49 x 10(-8) M3 S(-1) N(-1)) decreased in the presence of indigenous drug and bile salt. The results suggest that these drugs might have the cell membrane stabilizing property which may lead to prevention of the toxic effect of bile salts in various hepatic disorders. (Upadhyay et al, 2001).

Andrographis paniculata aqueous extracts of dried leaves was tested in vitro against adult worms of subperiodic Brugia malayi. The relative movability (RM) value of the adult worms over the 24-h observation period was used as a measure of the antifilarial activity of the aqueous extracts. The strongest activity, followed by the LA extract of Andrographis paniculata. (Zaridah et al, 2001).

Andrographis paniculata shows chemopreventive potential against chemotoxicity including carcinogenicity. (Singh et al, 2001).

 The extract of Andrographis paniculata exhibited a significant alteration in behaviour pattern and a reduction in spontaneous motility. The extract also produced a prolongation of the pentobarbitone-induced sleeping time and lowered the body temperature in different experimental animal models. (Mandal et al, 2001).

Andrographis paniculata induces relaxation of uterus by blocking voltage operated calcium channels and inhibits Ca(+2) influx. (Burgos et al, 2001).

Andrographolide prevent oxygen radical production by human neutrophils. (Shen et al, 2002).
