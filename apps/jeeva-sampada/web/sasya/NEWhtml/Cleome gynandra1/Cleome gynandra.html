HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, erect herb, 20-80 cm tall, glandular hairy. It is found in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves compound, 3-5 foliate, lower leaves petiolate, upper ones sessile; leaflets obovate, elliptic-obovate or elliptic-lanceolate, 2-7 x 1.5-3.5 cm, margin crenate or dentate. Flowers in terminal corymbose raceme, 5-8 cm long, white. Sepals pubescent. Petals narrow, clawed; gynandrophore c 1 cm long. Fruits capsule, cylindrical, viscidly pubescent, obliquely striated, 3-10 x 0.3-0.5 cm, many seeded. Seeds spherical, blackish brown.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Long raceme ; Flower: Yellow, bracteate, complete, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Sepals 4, spreading; Corolla: Petals 4, regular ;Androecium: Stamens 4-8, sessile on the disk; Gynoecium: Ovary with short gynophore, style short, ovules many, placentation parietal; Fruit: Oblong capsule ; Seed: Reniform . Flowering & Fruiting : October- January. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  The whole plant is used in scorpion sting, snake bite, eye diseases, fever and wound treatment. Leaves are rubifacient, vesicant and used in rheumatism. The juice of the leaves is used for the relief of otalgia and catarrhal inflammation of the middle ear. Leaves are also used externally to boils to prevent the formation of pus, headache, neural diseases, pyorrhea, stiff neck etc. Vermicides are prepared from leaves. Decoction of root is febrifuge and used in fever. Sedds are anthelmintic and rubifacient and used in cough, headache, round worms and skin diseases. Seed powder with sugar is applied internally to treat burning sensation of ear.


Chemistry:
b-sitosterol-b-D-glucoside and kaempferol from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Glucoiberine, glucocapparine, neoglucobrassicin and glucobrassicin were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Methyl glucosinolate isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

5,7-Dihydroxychromone, 5-hydroxy-3,7,4’-trimethoxyflavone and luteolin isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Leaves of Egyptian Cleome plant afforded centaureidin (fig. 1), Kaempferol and its 3-O-diglucoside, quercitrin, myricitrin, a- and b-ayrins, taraxasterol, b-sitosterol and capric,lauric, myristic, palmitic, palmitoleic, stearic, oleic and linoleic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Crystal structure and conformation of centaureidin was determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Rutin was isolated from the fresh flower petals of Cleome gynandra and was identified by comparison of spectral data with authentic sample. (Ragunathan et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 99854c.

Chemical examination of Cleome gynandra (whole plant excluding seeds) led to the isolation and identification of a novel (20S, 24S)-epoxy-19,25-dihydroxydammarane-3-one hemiketal (1). The structure of the new compound, designated cleogynol, was determined using spectral and chemical methods. (Das et al, 1999).
