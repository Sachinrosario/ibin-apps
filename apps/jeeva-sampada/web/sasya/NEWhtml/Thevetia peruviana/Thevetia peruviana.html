HABIT & HABITAT

Yellow oleander is a large, evergreen shrub and grows to 4.5-6.0 m tall, in its native habitat of tropical America and West Indies. The plant prefers fertile, well-drained soils, but can survive on most soils. Can be invasive on dryer sites. (1, 5, 6, 8, 11, 12)

Distribution : This plant is native of Central & South America, but now frequently grown throughout the tropical and sub- tropical regions. This plant is naturalized and cultivated in gardens and in the plains throughout India. (1, 4, 5, 6)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

The leaves are spirally arranged, linear-lanceolate, acute or subobtuse, acute at base, coriaceous, dark green somewhat glossy above, paler beneath, petiole very short (3 mm), blade 8-14 cm long, less than 1 cm wide, glabrous and the leaf is about 13 to 15 cm in length. (1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12)

Flowers are fragrant bright yellow and funnel-shaped with 5 petals spirally twisted. Flowers in few-flowered terminal clusters (cymes). cymes many-flowered; calyx 1-1.3 cm long, lobes acute-acuminate; corolla 5-7 cm long, yellow (rarely light orange); fruit a juicy black drupe 4-5.5 cm broad globular in shape. The fruits, which are green in colour, become black on ripening. Each fruit contains a nut which is longitudinally and transversely divided. (1, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12)

Seed Biology :

Flowers give way to black seed pods, each containing 1-2 nut-like seeds and the seeds are 1 cm broad. (1, 5, 6, 11, 12)

Cultivation:

The plant is Propagated by seeds, cuttings and transplants. Planting is done during the starting of the warmer season, spacing 6-8 feet apart for mass plantings. These shallow-rooted trees should be protected from strong winds. They grow fast and need occasional pruning. Seeds and flowers create litter. Plants thrive in heat and full sun. Grows well in average, medium wet soils in full sun to part shade. Thrives in rich, sandy soils. Water regularly but let plant soils dry out between watering. (1, 7, 12)

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:

An angiospermic parasite, Cuscuta sp occurs on this plant. (1)

Diseases:
Virus: A viral disease of this shrub, showing severe smalling and curling of the leaves, has been reported. It shows a high incidence and is transferred by grafting; no insect-vector has so far been identified. (1)

Economic Potential and Uses

The seed kernel yields oil which has been used in India as an external application in skin diseases. The solvent extracted oil is free from cardiac glycosides and appears to be suitable for soap-making and edible purpose. (1, 7)

Parts used

Stem, Bark, Leaves, Roots, Seeds. (1, 3, 9, 11)

Back to Top

 

Uses

Stem: The wood is brownish grey, soft to moderately hard, fibrous, light, easy to work on and resistant to decay. It is sometimes used to make axe-handles. It is reported to be poisonous to fish.

Bark: A tincture of the bark is a bitter cathartic and emetic; it is also used as a febrifuge. In large doses, it is poisonous.

Leaves: Leaves also posses purgative and emetic properties and are said to be poisonous to the animals. It is also dried and smoked sometimes.

Roots: The roots is made into a plaster and applied on the tumors.

Seeds: Seeds are used as an abortifacient and as a purgative in rheumatism and dropsy; they are also used as an alexiteric. The seed is also used in extraction of non drying oil.

(1, 3, 9, 11)

Miscellaneous Information

As with many of the dogbane family members, plant stems exude a milky sap when cut and all parts of the plant are poisonous if ingested. Plant saps can cause allergic skin reactions in some people. Smoke from burning plant material can also be toxic. (1, 3, 8, 11, 12)
