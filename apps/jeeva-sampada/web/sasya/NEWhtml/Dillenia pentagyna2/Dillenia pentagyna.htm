Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Large deciduous tree with straight cylindrical bole. Frost sensitive. Saplings stand jungle fires to a remarkable degree.

D. pentagyna is found almost throughout India. It occurs along the foothills of the Himalayas from Oudh eastwards in Nepal, Bihar, Bengal and Assam and extends southwards to Chota Nagpur, Madhya Pradesh, southern and western India and Andaman Islands.

It is found in regions with min. temp., 320 – 600 F., max. shade temp., 960 – 1150 F., and annual rainfall, 30-180 in. where the annual rainfall is less than 45 in. the growth is stunted. It is common in the sal forests of Bengal and Assam. It is confined mostly to low ground in Chota Nagpur but occasionally ascends up to 2,000 ft. It is common in the moist deciduous forests of south India.

The tree is a light demander and reproduces freely in burnt savanahs often forming pure patches.

Saplings stand jungle fires to a remarkable degree. It is sensitive to frost.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tree with erect trunk; branches strangling, numerous, ascending, with drooping ends. Leaves at the extremities of the branches, very large reaching 2 ft. and upwards in length, by 1 ft. in breadth (the leaves of young trees larger than those of older often attaining a length of 4 ft. or more), smooth and shining when old, downy when young, the nerves of very young leaves densely clothed with white hairs, nerves many, often forking towards the margins, margins with sharp villous teeth at the extremities of the main nerves and of each of the furcations; petioles channelled, sheathing.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in March-April and fruits ripen about May-June. Flowers scarcely 1" in diameter, fragrant, appearing before the leaves, in fascicles from tuberosities on the naked, woody, 2 3 year old branchlets. Pedicels erect, terete, glabrous. Sepals ovate, obtuse. Petals oblong-lanceolate, yellow. Filaments numerous, the outer erect, the inner spreading, twice as long as the outer. Ovaries 5, styles short, stigmas spreading. Fruit edible, pendulous, size of a small nutmeg, the fleshy sepals entirely enclosing 5 small soft capsules. Seeds reniform, 1 (rarely 2) ripening in each capsule.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

High calorific value. Sapwood 5176 cal, 9317 B.t.u.; heartwood 5176 cal, 9315 B.t.u. Source of charcoal.

Wood: Sapwood yellowish-brown gradually merging into light brown heartwood. Occasional white lines occur due to chalky deposits in the vessels. It is somewhat lustrous with a rough feel. Moderately hard to hard. Moderately heavy to heavy. Average unit weight at 12 per cent moisture content 640 kg/m3. Specific gravity 0.58 – 0.82 air dry, 45-48 lb. /c ft. Usually twisted or interlocked-grained. Coarse and even textured.

Wood used for house-posts, rafters and planking. When quarter-sawn, used for almirahs, cupboards and panelling. Most effective when used in inlays against darker wood. Easy to peel after boiling treatment but veneers should be dried mechanically, not in open air. It yields a second grade plywood for interior work.

The wood suitable for match making and reported to resemble beech.


Harvesting Practices:

Trees felled 2-2½ years after girdling and seasoned under shelter for 6 months, yield excellent boards and rafters. Kiln-seasoning gives satisfactory results. The timber has a marked tendency to warp and twist when plain-sawn, but little tendency to do so when quarter sawn. It presents no difficulty in sawing when cut green, but it is almost impossible to cut after kiln-seasoning.

Processing Techniques:

The timber seasons more rapidly than the most other timbers. Wood moderately refractory to air seasoning. Slight tendency to split at the ends and warp. Kiln seasoning offers no difficulties. Timber comes out in excellent condition provided that it is well sawn and carefully piled. Also seasons well when cut from girdled logs.

 


Other uses:

Buds and fruits eaten raw or cooked.

Leaves used as green manure (nitrogen 1.34, potash 3.20, and phosphoric acid 0.5%). Also used for thatching and as platters. Dry leaves used for polishing ivory and horn.

Large leaves used to form a substratum for roof thatch.

Bark contains tannin and said to yield a fiber suitable for cordage.
