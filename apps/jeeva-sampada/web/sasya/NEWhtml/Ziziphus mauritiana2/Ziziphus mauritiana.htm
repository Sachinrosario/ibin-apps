Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Fruits are used in preparation of sweet drinks. ( Jain 1991).
Preparation of the beverage: One kilogram (Ziziphus mauritiana) of washed dried ber of variety ‘Katha’ are cooked with 61iters of water for 60-80 min and the cooked mass is extracted through a basket press. This procedure is adopted from Hoofman who found that cooking the dried prune with water, helped maximum extraction of soluble solids and fruit colour. The extract was allowed to settle and the clear juice was separated.

The average yield of juice was 200 ml per 100 g of dehydrated fruit. The juice has a pH of 3.75; total soluble solids (TSS), 19.60 Brix and 0.56 per cent acidity as anhydrous citric acid. The juice possesses slight acrid taste, a typical dried ber flavour and reddish brown colour and as such it is not relished. Therefore, a ready-to-serve beverage of different formulations can be prepared. Organoleptic evaluation using Hedonic scale showed that a ready-to-serve beverage having 33.3 percent juice and 20.80 Brix and an acidity of 0.51 per cent was liked moderately by the panel of ten judges.

Ber (Ziziphus mauritiana Lamk) is a hardy crop which can be grown on marginal lands and needs minimum inputs as compared to other fruit crops (Parekh, 1983). Ber fruit is rich in vitamin C, minerals, calcium, phosphorus and carolene (Khurdiya and Singh, 1975). The cultivation of ber is becoming increasingly popular due to low cost of cultivation and good returns (Khurdiya and Singh, 1975).. The ber fruit has shelf-life of 4-5 days which generally results in market glut. Considering the fast increasing area under ber cultivation, methods of its preservation and processing, technology needs to be developed to regulate the prices of fresh fruit and safe-guard the interest of growers during glut period.

Methods of preparation of the beverage: Juice Extraction: The washed and cleaned fruits are destoned by using cork borer (Khurdiya and Singh (1975) and then used for juice extraction. The pulp of the destoned fruits is extracted in juicer (Electrocom-solid state control) and is allowed to stand at 40C for 2 hrs. The scum formed at the surface of the juice is removed by stainless steel spoon and the juice passed through four layers of muslin cloth and filtered through ordinary filter paper. This yields very clear and sparkling juice. Alternately, similar type of juice can be easily obtained by centrifugation of pulp at 5000 rpm for 5 min. This juice is used for preparation of ready-to-serve beverage.
Preparation of Ready-to-serve (RTS) Beverage: The RTS beverage is prepared by using 10% ber juice, which contains 15% total soluble solids and 0.25% acidity. 

Preparation of Candy: the healthy and bold size matured fruits are picked with stainless steel pricker (fork) and fruits destonned by using cork borer. The destonned fruits are blanched in boiling water for 2-3 minutes and subjected to sulphur treatment (2 g/kg for 2 hrs.). the syrup containing 40% sugar and 1% citric acid is prepared and treated fruits were dipped in sugar syrup for 24 hrs.

Produce and Products:


Chemistry: The chemical analysis was done for reducing sugars and total sugar by Hulme’s ferricyanide micro-method (1931), ascorbic, total titratable acidity and moisture content by AOAC methods (1970). 


The juice of Umaran has 16% total Soluble Solids (T.S.S.) and 0.24% acidity whereas juice of Kadaka variety shows 10.4% T.S.S. and 0.36% acidity. The processing cost of RTS prepared from ber juice is Rs. 1.03/200 ml bottle. It is evident that good quality RTS from ber can be prepared at reasonably low cost. It seems that this may open new avenue for utilization of ber fruits in beverage industry.
