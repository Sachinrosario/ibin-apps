REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Flower: Minute on the inner walls of the fleshy receptacles which is sessile or  in pair, axillary, globose, or pendulous, with broad basal bracts 3, coriaceous ; Male flower: sessile, globose, near the mouth of receptacles, sepals 3, broadly ovate, stamens 1, anther ovate-rotund, filament short ; Gall and Fertile flower: Sessile, pedicellate, the gall flower much more numerous than fertile females, many of them without perianth, sepals 5, lanceolate, style short lateral, stigma rotund ; Fruit: Achenes minute, tubercled, mucilagenous ; Seed: Pendulous . 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : May-July . 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=26.

Pharmacology:

 The bark extract has varying degree of relaxant and spasmolytic effects on various smooth muscles in different species of animals. Its spasmolytic ED50 against acetylcholine, histamine, barium chloride and serotonin was in the ratio of 1:4.5:12:21. The extract blocked cardiovascular effects of acetylcholine but not those of histamine and was also vagolytic. It had no antiacetylcholine action at autonomic ganglia and skeletal myoneural junctions. Its LD50 in rats was 2.24 and 0.80 g/kg by oral and i.v. routes respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
b-sitosterol-D-glucoside injected i.v. into rabbits at 5.0 and 7.5 mg/kg, decreased blood sugar by 23.2 and 33.86% respectively. It produced CNS stimulation, convulsions and reversal of reserpine depression in mice at toxic doses (40-400 mg/kg); LD50 was 62 mg/kg, i.p. in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.


Ethnobotany: 

Medicine for abortifacient (Leaf, Lal and Lata, 1980); Asthma (Fruit, Bhalla et al, 1982); Blisters, boils, ulcer (Bark, Maheshwari and Singh, 1984); Carbuncle (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Cholera (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Dysentery (Part not mentioned, Shah, 1981; Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Fever (Branch, Shah, 1982; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Gonorrhoea (Bark, Bhalla et al, 1982; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Haematuria (Bark, Molla and Roy, 1985); Laxative (Fruit, Shah, 1982); Rheumatism (Part not mentioned, Shah, 1981; Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Scabies (Bark, Bhalla et al, 1982); Skin disease (Leaf, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973; Singh and Pandey, 1980; Shah, 1982; Shoot, Sharma et al, 1979); Small pox (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Snake bite (Leaf, Maheshwari et al, 1986; Bark, Murthy, 1986); Sores, sores in mouth (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Tonic (Latex, Rai, 1985); Toothache (Leaf, Shekhawat and Anand, 1984); Urinary complaints (Seed, Vir Jee et al, 1984); Whooping cough (Bark, Vir Jee et al, 1984); Edible (Fruit, Ball, 1967; Maheshwari et al, 1986; Datr and Vartak, 1975; Gupta, 1962; Kanodia and Gupta, 1968); Vegetable (Leaf, Jain, 1963; Fruit, Singh, 1945); Fibre (Bark, Jain and De, 1966); Symbolic sacred worshipped (Sen Gupta, 1965); A curse of Parvati turned Vishnu into this tree (Sen Gupta, 1965); Bhil clan name Pipli (Joshi, 1982); Tree of fertility (Sen Gupta, 1965); In folk songs, associated with Vishnu in many states, with Sun in Bhojpur, for Kubair (Lord of wealth) in Rajasthan, with Buddha in Tibet (Agarwal, 1981); Burning in Yagna taboo, cutting tree more sinful than killing a saint (Agarwal, 1981); Taller this tree in a temple in Kerala more revered the temple (Sen Gupta, 1965)
