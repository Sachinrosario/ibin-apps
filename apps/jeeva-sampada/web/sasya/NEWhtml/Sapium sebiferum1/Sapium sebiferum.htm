Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A small glabrous, fast growing tree a native of China. Introduced at various elevations in northern India, chiefly in ravine lands in the foothills.

It is frost hardy and useful for fixing the sides of ravines, banks of rivers and waterlogged areas. It grows well on moist soils and therefore, localities with heavy rainfall are best suited. On poor soils tree remains stunted. Cultivated tree.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Small glabrous tree. Leaves rhomboid, entire, on long slender petioles.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in terminal spiciform racemes. Ovary glabrous. Styles 3, long, spreading, recurved. Capsule coriaceous, subglobose, shortly pointed. Seeds enclosed in a thick layer of a white fatty substance attached to a central column which splits into 3 slender divisions.

Other uses:

Fruits - Fruits provide two types of fats: (I) the Chinese vegetable tallow (of low iodine value) obtained from the waxy mass covering the seeds and (II) the kernel oil (of high iodine value),, known in commerce as the Stillingia oil.

Seeds - when whole seeds are crushed, without removal of the waxy seed coating, a softer, low-melting tallow, consisting of a mixture of the vegetable tallow and Stillingia oil is obtained. Seed has fatty covering - caruncle, which has about 54.5% tallow, used for candle wax and soap. Employed as antiseptic.

Stillingia oil - Oil is drying oil. It is considered superior to linseed oil in its drying and polymerizing properties. It is used in the formulation of paints and varnishes and in plastics. It does not polymerize as easily as tung oil. Varnishes are found to be as good as those prepared from tung oil. It is also used as an illuminant. It is reported to possess vulnerary, emetic, purgative and hydrogogue properties and is used for skin disorders.

Chinese tallow - It is used in the manufacture of candles, cosmetics and soaps, and for dressing cloth. It gives an indorous, smokeless flame. The tallow resembles cacao butter and, if properly refined, is edible. It is used as a substitute for animal tallow and is employed in small quantities for stiffening softer edible fats.

Seed meal - Seed meal, left after the extraction of oil, possesses a high content of protein, and is a valuable feed and fertilizer. It can be processed into a refined flour, containing 75% protein, fit for animal consumption.

Leaves - The leaves yield a black dye.

Eri silkworms are reported to feed upon the leaves.

Roots - Root bark is medicinally valuable.
