 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, woody shrub or small tree, up to 10 m tall; it is found to occur in Pondicherry, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Goa and Maharashtra.


Availability :  
Specimen examined:  Tamil Nadu: Karian Shola, 14.03.1974, K.N. Subramanian, 4775 (DD); Goa: Panagi N.I.O. campus, 15.11.1980, P.S.H. Khan & party, 12318 (LWG); Maharashtra: Kandala, Bombay, 597 m, 16.06.1959, G.S. Srivastava & Uma Shankar, 61738 (LWG).
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant grows wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, woody shrub or small tree, up to 10 m tall; twigs flagellate, brown, puberulous. Thorns recurrved, 2-3 mm long. Leaves elliptic-oblong, 5-18 x 3.0-5.5 cm, with a twisted acumen at apex, coriaceous, glabrous, midrib sunken. Flowers white, purplish during shading in 6-12 flowered terminal or subterminal corymb, showy, fragrant. Outer pair of sepals concave, cup shaped. Petals veined. Torus 5-8 mm across. Stamens 100-170, filaments white, turning rosy or brick-red. Gynophore 5-8 cm long. Ovary ellipsoid, green with purple tinge. Pericarp woody, green, turning reddish brown; endocarp pulpy, yellow. Seeds 50-60, pink.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Terminal corymb; Flower: Bracteate, complete, usually hermaphrodite, zygomorphic, tetramerous, hypogynous, white ; Calyx: Sepals 4, free at the very base in 2 series, imbricate, deeply cupped, orbicular, inner broadly oblong, finely tomentose outside ; Corolla: Petals 4, imbricate, spreading, pubescent on the upper surface; Androecium: Stamens many, inserted at the base of a long gynophore , filaments filiform, anthers dithecous, introrse, dehiscing longitudinally; Gynoecium: Ovary ovoid,  glabrous or very minutely pubescent; Fruit: Berry, subglobose, pointed,  seated on woody stalk ; Seed: numerous, reniform.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : December - January.   
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by cattles.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Barries are used in skin treatment, jaundice, tuberculosis, etc.


Chemistry:
Plant extract showed little effect in vivo and in vitro on growth of Myobacterium tuberculosis or its lesions in susceptible animals; it did not show any bcteriostatic property in vitro. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Rutin, b-sitosterol and 1-stachydrine (fig. 1), m.p. 232°C, isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Medicine for cough, spiting, weakness in tuberculosis (Fruit, Kamble and Pradhan, 1980)
