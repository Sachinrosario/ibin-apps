Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large deciduous tree occurs naturally in India (including Andamans). Found in subtropical regions like East Indian hills and parts of Western Ghats, in the tropical high rainfall areas of Western Ghats, West Bengal and Assam (wet tropical). Also found in the sub-Himalayn tracts from Yamuna eastwards to West Bengal, Satpura range, Gujrat, South India and in the Andamans. Grows along streams and moist ravines characteristic of western Gangetic moist mixed deciduous forest and low alluvial savanna woodlands. Suitable for growing in village commons, marginal lands and roadside.

The species are generally frost-tender especially in early stages.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Unarmed tree, young shoots pale, lenticellate. Leaves abruptly bipinnate, main rachis glabrous, 12-18" long, with a large gland on the petiole a little above its base; pinnae 2-6 pairs, 5-6" long, rachises glabrous and with an oval gland below the uppermost pair of leaflets. Leaflets 6-12 pairs, obtuse or subacute, dark green and glabrous above, more or less gray-pubescent beneath, base obliquely cuneate on the upper, rounded on the lower margin.

Flowers sessile, in numerous small 12-20 flowered heads, peduncles in fascicles of 3-5 arranged in terminal panicles. Calyx glabrous, teeth distinct, triangular. Corolla pubescent outside, teeth lanceolate. Stamens 11/2 as long as corolla; filaments greenish-yellow, connate at the base into a tube as long as the corolla-tube.

Pods scarcely stalked, at length dehiscent, thin, flexible, bright orange-brown, smooth and glossy, faintly reticulate above the seeds. Seeds broadly ovate, much flattened, pale-brown.

Floral and seed biology:

Grown by entire transplanting and direct sowing. Direct sowing is generally the common method for raising plantations. Well drained alluvial and sandy soils are best for its growth.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood excellent fuel. Calorific value of sapwood and heartwood is 4780 and 4865 calories, respectively. Calorific value 86.9 compared to pure carbon and hence it makes good charcoal.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, pale yellowish-white. Heartwood light brown to golden brown or dark brown with darker streaks. Wood moderately hard to hard, moderately heavy, average weight 640 kg/m3 (40 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; lustrous; coarse and even-textured with straight to somewhat wavy or interlocked grain.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings distinct to indistinct. Pores large to very large, clearly visible to the eye, scanty, solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3, occasionally filled with dark, gummy deposits; vessel lines prominent on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly aliform forming eyelets round the pores, rarely connecting adjacent pores. Rays fine to very fine, indistinct to just visible to the eye, distinct under the lens as pinkish or light-coloured lines of varying thickness, usually somewhat widely-spaced.

Wood is similar to kokko (Albizia odoratissima Benth.) and safed siris (Albizia lebbeck Benth.) from which it is not possible to distinguish it under hand lens.

Timber is used for construction, carriage, carts, furniture, posts, beams, planks, boards and bridge work, dug outs, oars, cane crushers, rice pounders and agricultural implements.

The wood gave 50.3 per cent yield of bleached pulp by the sulphate method. Since, it is short fibred, it requires to be mixed with a suitable proportion of a long-fibred pulp such as bamboo pulp for the manufacture of paper on a commercial paper machine. The pulp is suitable for writing and printing paper.

White siris along with other forest wastes has been found to be useful for making chipboards of satisfactory strength properties. It is good substitute for teak and sal after suitable seasoning and preservative treatment.

Processing Techniques:

Timber kiln seasons well. As the sapwood is easily perishable it should be removed as soon as possible. The heartwood is durable even in exposed conditions.


Other uses:

Tree grown as an ornamental tree. It is used for afforestation and is also grown as a shade tree in tea-gardens in Doon valley.

Fodder

Medicinal - Plant is used for stomach and intestinal diseases, and during pregnancy. All parts of the plant are reported to show anti-cancer activity. Decoction of bark is given in rheumatism and haemorrhage. It is used as fish poison.
