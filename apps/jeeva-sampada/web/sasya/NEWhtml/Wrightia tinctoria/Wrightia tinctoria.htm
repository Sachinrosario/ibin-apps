Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT: 
A small, deciduous tree, generally up to 1.8 m. tall and often under 60 cm. in girth, sometimes up to 7. 5 m. high, distributed in Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and the peninsular India, ascending to an altitude of c. 1,200 m. in the hills.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES :
A small, deciduous tree, generally up to 1.8 m. tall and often under 60 cm. In girth, sometimes up to 7. 5 m. high,  Bark light gray, scaly, smooth; leaves elliptic ovate or obovate-oblong, 7.5-12.5 cm. Long; flowers white, fragrant, in lax, terminal cymes; follicles in pairs, pendulous, 15-50 cm. Long, cylindrical, tips adhering; seed linear, with basal coma.  The tree is a moderate light-demander, and is often found as an undergrowth species in deciduous forests. The wood is white like ivory, hard, close grained; weight 49 lb per cubic foot.

Cultivation:
Soil and Propagation:- It requires a mixture of peat, loam and sand and is propagated by seeds or cuttings which readily root in sand under the sun. The growth is slow to moderate, the annual increase in girth being 1.16-2.3cm.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests: 

  • A beetle, Xylorrhia adusta attacks the trees producing ring on the stem and branches. 
  • The green bug of coffee also feed on this tree.

Diseases: 

  • The tree is subjected to many fungal diseases
  • Cercospora wrightiae found on the leaves of the plant from Bangalore and Bhopal. 
  • Hemileia wrightiae infects the tree in many parts of Tamil Nadu.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Bark, Leaves, seeds, flowers.

Uses: 

  1. Several amino acids have been isolated from the bark. 
  2. The juice of the fresh unripe fruits is also used for coagulating milk, and, as the latex, the presence of a proteolytic enzymes has been reported. 
  3. The flowers are used as a vegetable; they are slightly bitter and require thorough washing before use. 
  4. The tender leaves, pods and seeds are also eaten. 
  5. The leaves are eaten by the cattle, sheep and goats.

Other Uses
  1. The wood is extensively used for all classes of turnery. 
  2. It is made into cups, plates, combs, chessmen. 
  3. The bark and seeds are used in flatulence and bilious affections. 
  4. A decoction of the leaves and bark is taken as a stomachic. 
  5. The dried and ground bark is rubbed over the body in dropsy.
  6.  The seeds are said to posses aphordisiac and anthelmintic properties. 
  7. The fresh leaves are very pungent and are chewed for relief from tooth ache. 
  8. The plant yields a cream-coloured latex, with rubber content varying from 2.4 to 28.4 per cent. The latex containing more than five per cent rubber or rather its coagulum. The coagulum used in articles as code-wire insulations, floor furnishings, adhesives etc. 
  9. In South India, the plant is used for green-manuring the rice fields, branches being trampled into the puddle soil. 
  10. The leaves are a source of blue dye, indigo called Mysore pala Indigo. 
  11. The tree bears handsome clusters of white, jasmine scented, star-shaped flowers in profusion, which are much asteemed by Hindus for offerings at the temples.
  12. The wood is used for carving, turnery and building.
