DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves elliptic, oblong, obovate or oblanceolate, 7.0-14.5 x 3.0-5.5 cm, acuminate at apex, rounded or acute at base, entire along margin; main lateral nerves 6-12 pairs. Flowers in dichotomous, terminal, lax cymes, fragrant, 1.2-1.5 cm across; bracts ovate. Calyx pubescent outside, glandular inside; segments broadly ovate, 2-3 mm long, obtuse with ciliate membranous margins. Corolla tube 4.5-5.5 mm long; lobes oblong, 1.0-1.5 cm long, rounded at apex; corona orange, scales fimbriate. Follicles cohering at the tip only, cylindrical, 25-40 cm long, slender, curved, pendulous, glabrous. Seeds slender, 1.2-1.4 cm long with a deciduous white coma at the lower ends.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The stem bark and root bark are believed to be useful in snake bite and scorpion stings.
Chemistry:

·        b-Sitosterol, b-amyrin and its acetate and lupeol benzoate from bark. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra 1960- 1969).

·        Isolation of quercetin and rutin from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985- 1989)a.

·        New sterol -14a-methylzymosterol isolated from seeds along with desmosterol, clerosterol, 24-methylene-25-methylcholesterol and 24-dehydropollinastanol. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra, 1985- 1989)b.

·        Analytical values of ‘777oil’preperad from fresh whole, fresh cut and dry leaves of  Wrightia tinctoria have been reported. The oil prepared by varying ingredients did not show any different in chromatography. The variation of qualities of leaf and oil indicated high acid number when leaves were double the weight of oil. (Alam, 1994). MAPA 9603-1297.

·        Tryptonthrin 0.012% and triacontonol of 0.25% were isolated from W. tintoria.           (George et al., 1996). MAPA 9704-2368, BA 1997 (103)8, 116640, CA 1997, 126, 222833h.

·        Butylhydroxyoctadecenamide – a derivative of unusual fatty acid  (hydroxyoctadecenoic acid) of Wrightia tintoria seed oil has been oil has been prepared (yield 46%) by oxidation of the oil  with n-butylamine. Butyl amines serves as reactant and solvent temperature of 45 0C has been found to be optimal. Dioxon has been to stop the reaction. (Banerjee et al., 1999).

·        Indigotin, indirubin, tryptanthrin, and rutin have been isolated and identified as major constituents of W. tinctoria. The identities of these compounds have been established by comprehensive chromatography (HPTLC, HPLC) and spectroscopic (UV-VIS, IR, EL-MS) techniques using markers and by synthesis where possible. While indigotin is found to be an artifact formed only during drying. (Muruganandam and Bhattacharya, 2000).

Pharmacology:

·        Flower extract showed anti-inflammatory activity against carrageenin – induced rat paw oedema. (Rastogi and  Mehrotra 1960- 1969).

·        A pharmaceutical preparation for topical treatment of skin disorder particularly psoriasis, comprising the ingredients of a latex extract from leaves of the  Wrightia tinctoria plant urea  and polyethylene glycol . The pharmaceutical preparation is also a hydrophilic ointment that is capable of delivering the active drug without being greasy or irritating to the skin. (Jacob et al., 1997). CA 130, 100642g.

·        Wrightia tinctoria when applied to mouse tails showed nearly 90 % reversal of parakeratosis in orthokeratosis when compared with treatment using retinoids and betamethasone. (Venkataranganna et al., 1998). MAPA 9901-0345.

·        The  ethyl acetate, acetone and methanol extract of Wrightia tinctoria bark showed antinociceptive activity on acetic acid induced writhing  test in mice their effects being comparable to that of acetylsalicylic acid.( Reddy et al., 2002). BA 2002 (21) 314284.

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for abdomen pain (Bark, Joshi et al., 1980); antidote to scorpion, snake bite (bark + root, Goel and  Mudgal, 1988); asthma (Leaf,  Shah et al., 1983); bleedible injuries (Bark, Joshi et al., 1980); bronchitis (Bark, Joshi et al., 1980); dysentery (part not mentioned,  Jain, 1963); galactagogue (bark Joshi et al., 1980); laxative (root, Shah et al., 1983); piles (bark 61 F); throat pain, toothache (Leaf, Shah et al., 1983); wounds (latex,  Shah et al., 198; Joshi et al., 1980). Vetsnake bite (bark,  Sebastian, 1984).

Miscellaneous Information

·        Antipirytrosporum activity of a herbal drug combination of Wrightia tinctoria was tested in vitro against the isolates of Pirytrosporum ovale recovered from dandruff. The drug combination exhibited fungicidal activity at a concentration ranging between 500 to 1000 mg/ml. (Krishnamurthy et al., 2000). MAPA 2000-06-3544.

·        W. tinctoria may be used testing leach bite, snake bite, scorpion sting rat bite and insert bite. (Thangadurai et al., 1998).
