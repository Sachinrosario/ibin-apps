HABIT & HABITAT
Erect, much-branched, annual herbs or undershrubs, 30-150 cm high, throughout India ascending up to 1800 m, in the Himalayas and in Andaman and Nicobar Islands. It is commonly planted or cultivated in garden.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, much-branched, annual herbs or undershrubs, 30-150 cm high. Stem and branches subquadrangular, purplish in colour, woody below on maturity, covered with dense white hairs. Leaves petiolate, elliptic-oblong, 1-5 x 0.5-2.0 cm, acute or obtuse at apex, truncate at base, distantly serrate along margin, closely gland dotted with long hairs on both surfaces; veinlets 5-6 pairs; petioles slender, 0.3-2.0 cm long, pubescent. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Flowers pale pink or pinkish purple in compact whorls of simple or panicled racemes; bracts broadly ovate. Calyx broadly campanulate, purplish, lobes 5, oblique; upper lip ovate, 2 mm long, acute; lower lip 1.5 mm long, hispid without. Corolla white, purplish within, bilabiate, c 5 mm long, lobes 5; upper lip pubescent on back; lower lip 2.5 mm long, scarious, obtuse. Stamens 4, tetradynamous. Nutlets broadly ellipsoid, 0.1 x 0.1 cm, smooth, reddish or yellow with small black markings.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The leaves possess disphoretic, antiperiodic, stimulating properties. It is used in cattarah, bronchitis and applied to the skin in ringworm and other cutaneous diseases and dropped into the ear to relieve earache. Infusion of the leaves is used as stomachic and gastric disorders of children. A decoction of the root is given as a diaphoretic in malarial fever. The seeds are mucilaginous and demulcent, and are used in disorder of genito-urinary system. The leaves are also used in cholagogue, anthelmintic, alexiteric and antipyretic. It is also useful in heart and blood diseases, leucoderma, asthma, lumbago and vomiting.
Parts used:Leaf-expectorant, diaphoretic, antiperiodic, in catarrh, bronchitis, earache; seed-demulcent, in genitourinary diseases; root-in malarial fever, plant-antidote for snakebite and scorpion sting.
Chemistry:
NBPGR:  The major components of the essential oil are eugenol, carvacrol, nerol and engenolmethylether, other included caryophyllene, terpinen-4-ol, decylaldehyd r-selinene, α and β-pinene and camphor.  The leaves have been reported to contain ursolic acid, apigenin-7 o-glucuronide, luteolin-7-o glucusonide, orientin and nrolludisin.
