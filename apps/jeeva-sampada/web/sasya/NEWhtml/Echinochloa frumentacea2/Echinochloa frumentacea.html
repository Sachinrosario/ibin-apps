DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herbs, tall, robust. Culms up to 1.5 m tall. Leaves linear-lanceolate, 11-17 x 0.5-1.2 cm, midrib prominent white; sheath loose, membranous, glabrous; ligules absent.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence of racemes, 2-nate or 3-nate; rachis angled, scabrid. Spikelets ovoid, 3-4 mm long, cuspidate, awnless. Lower glume c 2.5 mm long, apiculate, minutely hispid, 5-nerved; upper glume similar to spikelet, compressed, 5-7-nerved. Lower lemma barren; upper fertile, elliptic, minutely ciliolate. Caryopsis ellipsoid, 2.0-2.5 mm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plant is sweet, acrid. It is cooling, digestible. It is useful in biliousness and constipation (Ayurveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).


Chemistry:
Gelatinised sample of Echinochloa frumentacea (var. K2) showed minimal of starch by porcine pancreatic alpha-amylase. Gelatinised starch was highly susceptible to enzymic digestion when compared to ungelatinised starch. The extent of starch degradation varied from 71 to 85 percent in gelatinised samples and starch degradation in ungelatinised sample varied from 10 to 18 percent. (Krishnakumari and Thayumanavan, 1995)

Miscellaneous Information
Other information:    

Weaning foods were formulated from Echinochloa frumentacen with other locally available cereals and pulses sanwak (Echinochloa frumentacea). Cereals and pulses were mixed in the proportion of 7:3. Nutrient composition of developed weaning foods was within range prescribed by Indian Standard Institute. Roasting was the processing technique employed in developing weaning foods which resulted in significant increase in HC1-extractable minerals, an index of their bioavailability to humans. The higher HC1-extractability of the minerals may be ascribed to the decreased phytic acid in the processed home made weaning foods. (Gahlawat and Sehgal, 1995).
