 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Availability : 
The plant is distributed throughout the country between 1650-3400 m. altitude.  In Himachal Pradesh it is present in chamba, Kinnaur, Sirmour, Kullu, Mandi and Shimla.  Found in rare and sporadic conditions in shady ravines, field borders and depressions between 1800-2800 m.  The population of this species has decreased alarmingly.  Its population patches can be found in Pakha-Dindi, Khabal, Dodra Kawar and Tikkri; Bagi, Palthari, Chiuni in Rohru (2000-2800 m.); Chherna, Derri Bardhar in Chhota Bhangal area; Barot in Mandi, Parvati valley in Kullu, Mcleodgari, Dharamsala (1800-2600 m.)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A large, thorny, deciduous shrub nearly 2-5 m. tall with pole rough, furrowed bark; leaves stalked obovate or elliptic, fascicled in the axils of simple or obovate or branched spines, leathery, often with sharply toothed margin; flowers yellow in compound racemes with stout pedicles; racemes are longer than the leaves; Fruits: 1.8 cm. X 0.6 cm., ovoid, grey-white when young bluish when ripe.  Wood and roots are yellow from inside
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Inflorescence: Compound, corymbose racemes ; Flower: yellow, hermaphrodite, fascicled, complete, regular; Calyx: Sepals 6, with 2-3 apressed bracts, imbricate in two series; Corolla: petals 6, usually imbricate, in 2 series, usually with 2 basal glands inside ;Androecium: Stamens 6, free, anther-cells opening by recurved valves; Gynoecium: Ovary simple, ovules few, basal, style short, stigma small, globose; Fruit: Berry few seeded; Seed: Seeds with crustaceous fleshy testa.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
 The wood, root bark and extract known as rasount, rasount is a dark brown extract of the consistency of opium, having a bitter, astringent taste readily soluble in water forming a rich yellowish brown colour. The wood, root bark and extract has been used in Hindu medicines from a very remote period. Its properties are set to be analogous to turmeric. Rasount and the plant are used as alternative deovstruent and are used in skin diseases, menorrhagia, diarrhoea, jaundice and above all in affections of the eye. In painful uricturition from bilious or acrid urine, a decoction of the Indian barberry and amla is given with honey. A decoction of the root bark is used as a wash for unhealthy ulser and is said to improve their approach and promotes cicatrization. Resount mixed  with honey is an useful application of aphthous sores, it is regarded efficacious in chronic uterine troubles, piles and specific for cholera. Ripe fruit are edible and are also gives as a mild laxative to children. Berberine hydrochloride and berbein sulfide is used in dyspepsia, diarrhoea, malarial affections, amenorrhoea, enlargement of spleen, anorexia, womiting of pregnency and intestinal catarrh. Infusion of berberis is bactericidal power
Chemistry:

·         A protoberberine alkaloid – karachine (fig. 1) – isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·         Taxilamine (fig. 2) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·         Synthesis of karachine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

·         Isolation and characterisation of a new aporphine base –O-methylcorydine N-oxide – along with berberine, palmatine, jatrorrhizine and oxyacanthine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·         Hentriacontane, triacontane, acetyl alcohol, b-sitosterol, g-sitosterol, dihydrokaempferol, quercetin and oleic, stearic, palmitic and linoleic acids isolated from roots; three alkaloids – chitrians A, B and C – isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·         Isolation and structure elucidation of a new alkaloid – dihydropalmatine N-oxide (fig. 3) - from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

·         Quercetin, E-caffeic acid, rutin, chlorogenic and meratin isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·         Structure of antimalarial alkaloid –jatrorrhizine – determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

·         A method for isolating berberine comprising: a) soaking dried ground stems and/or roots of Berberis aristata in water or a dilute aqueous solution of an acid for 24-74 hours at room temperature; b) filtering and extracting the filtrate with an organic solvent comprising dichloromethane at a ratio in the range of 1:5 to 1:100 of filtrate to organic solvent; c) repeating step b) at least two times; d) separating the organic solvent phase and drying said phase; e) washing with diethylether for a minimum of 30 minutes; and f) repeating step e) at least two times to obtain berberine. (Upadhyay and Yadav, 1999).

·         A high performance thin layer chromatography and spectrophotometric method developed for the estimation of berberine isolated from Berberis aristata has been described. The sensitivity by HPTLC method was 0.01 mg/ml and the linearity was observed in the range of 0.01mg to 0.10mg, while the spectrophotometric method showed the sensitivity of 0.02mg/ml and the linearity was observed between 0.02mg to 0.10mg. An average berberine content of 2.76% by HPTLC and 2.58% by spectrophotometric method was observed in Berberis aristata. Both the proposed methods reported to be simple; precise and sensitive, have been recommended for detection, monitoring and quantification of berberine in Berberis aristata. (Chauhan et al, 2000). MAPA 2000-04-2571.


Pharmacology:

·         Root extract showed CNS depressant activity in albino mice and antimicrobial activity against Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus proteus, Salmonellatyphi and pseudomonas pyocyanae. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

·         Jatrorrhizine showed negative chronotropic and positive inotropic effects concentration-dependently on isolated guinea pig atria. Time to peak tension and relaxation time were increased. At 50.0 mM it prolonged functional refractory period of left atrium but excitability was not affected. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

·         Pre-treatment of mice with Berberis aristata fruits, crude extract (500 mg/kg) reduced the death rate to 10%. Pre-treatment of rats with fruit extract (500 mg/kg, orally twice daily for 2 days) prevented (p less than 0.05) the paracetamol (640 mg/kg) as well as CCl4 (1.5 mL/kg)-induced rise in serum transaminases (GOT and GPT). Post-treatment with three successive doses of extract (500mg/kg, 6h) restricted the hepatic damage induced by acetaminophen (p less than 0.01) but CCl4-induced hepatotoxicity was not altered (p greater than 0.05). plant extract (500 mg/kg) caused significant prolongation 9p less than 0.01) in pentobarbital (75 mg/kg)- induced sleep as well as increased strychnine-induced lethality in mice suggestive of inhibitory effect on microsomal drug metabolizing enzymes (MDME). Hepatoprotective action of the crude extract of Berberis aristata fruits partly through MDME inhibitory action has been indicated. (Gilani and Janbaz, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9603, 1408.

·         The efficacy of Diaroak, a herbal drug containing plant ingredients Berberis aristata with other plant drugs was examined in ruminants suffering from non-specific entritis. Over all 42 (68.9%) of the 61 cases recovered fully with an average of 3.92 dose per animal. The response in goats and sheep was the best (74.0%) requiring only 3 doses Diaroak was effective (73.3%) in treating calf scours. (Raja, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9604, 2168.

·         A formulation comprising of five medicinal herbs with Berberis aristata is prescribed for intestinal disorders. The dried and pulverized plants were extracted in ethanol together and individually. In vitro amoebicidal activity was studied to determine the minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) values of all the constituents extracts as well as the whole formulation. The formulation had a MIC of 1000 microg/ml as compared with 10 microg/ml for metronidazole. In experimental caecal amoebiasis in rats the formulation had a curative rate of 89% with the average degree of infection (ADI) reduced to 0.4 in a group treated with 500 mg/kg per day as compared with ADI of 3.8 for the sham treated control group of rats. (Sahnim et al, 1995). MAPA 9504, 2356.Extract of ‘Lycovin’ capsules having Berberis aristata with other plant parts were tested for their antioxidant activity. (Joy and Kuttan, 1995). 1996, 9603, 1425.

·         The alcoholic extract of root of Berberis aristata on rabbit inhibited the PAF (Platelet activating factor) induced aggregration of platelets in a dose dependent manner in the microgram range. (Tripathi and Shukla, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9703, 1589.

·         Crude extract formulation was evaluated in experimental amoebic liver abscess in golden hamsters and in immunomodulation studies. The formulation comprises Berberis aristata with other plants. The formulation had a maximum cure rate of 73% at a dose of 800mg/kg/day in hepatic amoebiasis reducing the average degree of infection (ADI) to 1.3 as compared to 4.2 for sham-treated controls. In immunomodulation studies humoral immunity was enhanced as evidenced by the haemaggulutination titre. The T-cell counts remained unaffected in the animals treated with stimulated as observed in the leukocyte migration inhibition (LMI) tests. (Sohni and Bhatt, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9703, 1576.

·         Rajnyadi churna containing Berberis aristata with other plants has been clinically investigated in 80 cases of diarrhoea (dose 125mg, 250mg or 750mg four timea a day depending upon age of the child). 87.5% children were cured in 3-5 days. (Jadav, 1999). MAPA, 1999, 9902, 0894.

·         Berberis aristata is an edible plant used as a tonic remedy for liver and heart. In isolated cardiac tissues, Berberis aristata fruit extract exhibits a positive inotropic action. (Gilani et al, 1999).

·         Kamilari is a hepatoprotective drug composing of Berberis aristata with other plant was given to ten cirrhotic patients for 4 months and biochemical parameters analysed in serum. Levels of serum enzymes, cholesterol and bilirubin were decreased and that of proteins increased. (Rajesh et al, 2000). MAPA 2001-01-0157.

·         Different extracts from the stem bark of Berberis aristata, and the alkaloid fraction derived from the commonly used aqueous extract, were tested for their putative antimicrobial activity against a battery of microorganisms by disk diffusion and agar dilution methods. The activity of the fraction was compared with that of berberine, the major alkaloid of the plant. While the methanolic extract was found to be most active, other fractions were also found to be superior to berberine, which suggests that in addition to berberine other antimicrobial agents are present in the plant. (Bhandari et al, 2000). MAPA 2001-02-0888.

·         Oral administration of multiherbal composite drug (Containing Berberis aristata with other plant) to 22 patients showed significant decrease in bleeding and constipation by the fourth week of therapy. (Paranjpe et al, 2000). MAPA 2000-05-2764.

·         Pretreatment of animals with berberine from Berberis aristata, 4 mg/kg orally twice daily for 2 days, prevented the acetaminophen- or CCl4- induced rats in serum levels of alkaline phosphatase and aminotransaminases, suggestive of hepatoprotection. Post-treatment with three successive oral doses of berberine (4 mg/kg every 6h) 73 reduced the hepatic damage induced by acetaminophen, while CCl4 induced hepatotoxicity was not modified, suggesting a selective curative effect against acetaminophen. Pretreatment of animals with a single oral dose of berberine (4 mg/kg) induced prolongation of the pentobarbital induced sleeping time as well as increased strychnine induced toxicity. (Janbaz and Gilani, 2000). MAPA 2000-05-2837.

·         Berberis aristata is an edible plant, its fruit being used as a tonic remedy for liver and heart. In this berberine a known compound from this plant, was studied for its possible antihepatotoxic action in rats. (Janbaz and Gilani, 2000).

·         The effect of commonly used indigenous drugs for hepatic disorders i.e. Berberis aristata was tested on the hydraulic permeability of water in the presence of bile salt through a transport cell model. The data on hydraulic permeability were calculated as t (time). JV = Lp x AP, where Lp= hydraulic conductivity and AP is the pressure difference. It was observes that the value of controlled hydraulic permeability (0.49 x 10 (-8) M3 S(-1) N(-1)) decreased in the presence of indigenous drugs and bile salt. The results suggest that these drugs might have the cell membrane stabilizing property which may lead to prevention of the toxic effect of bile salts in various hepatic disorders. (Upadhyay et al, 2001).

·         Berberine is quaternary alkaloid derived from the plant Berberis aristata having antibacterial, antiamoebic, antifungal, antihelminthic, leishmanicidal and tuberculostatic properties. The effect of berberine sulphate salt on the growth of Trichomonas vaginalis in vitro was compared to the efficacy of metronidazole as a reference drug. Results showed that berberine sulphate was comparable to metronidazole as regards potency with the advantage of being more safe and possible replacement in metronidazole resistant cases. (Soffar et al, 2001).

·         Berberine, an alkaloid isolated from the plant Berberis aristata, has been found to inhibit significantly the carcinogenesis induced by 20-methylcholanthrene or N-nitrosodiethylamine in a dose-dependent manner in small animals. Administration of berberine also reduce significantly the incidence of tumour in animals after injection of 20-methylcholanthrene and increased their life span compared with the control. When berberine was administered simultaneously with NDEA, the markers of liver injury were reduced significantly compared with animals treated with NDEA only, which resulted in all the values being elevated. A similar decrease was noted in the serum levels of lipid peroxide, bilirubin and glutamate pyruvate transminase. Morphology of liver tissue and levels of marker enzymes indicated that berberine offered protection against chemical carcinogenesis. (Anis et al, 2001).
