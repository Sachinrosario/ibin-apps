REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Umbellate cymes; Flower: Complete, heemaphrodite, pedicels shorter than peduncles, bracteate, regular; Calyx: Sepals linear-oblong, acute, pubescent outside; Corolla: Petals yellow, oblong or ovate-oblong, about 2/3 the length of sepals glandular; Androceium: Stamens indefinite, inserted on the turbinate often glandular torus, filaments free, anther cells 2, parallel, contiguous, dehiscing longitudinally; Gynoecium: Ovary free, villous, 2-4 celled, ovules 2 in each cell, style subulate, stigma short 2-5lobed ; Fruit: globose, 1-2 lobed, 1-celled, pillose; Seed: Ascending, fleshy;

Chemistry:
Physical characteristics of seed oil described. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Taraxasterol, b-sitosterol and erythrodiol isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

b-amyrin and betulin from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Lupeol, lupenone, and friedelin isolated from plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

b-sitosteroi, quercetin, its 3-O-glucoside, naringenin and its 7-O-glucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

A new lactone - 3,21,24-trimethyl-5,7-dihydroxyhentriacontanoic acid d-lactone (fig. 1) isolated from flowers and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Grewinol (fig. 2) isolated from flowers and identified as tetratriacontan-22-ol-13-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Pelargonisin-3,5-diglucoside, quercetin, its 3-glucoside and naringenin-7-O-b-D-glucoside isolated from fruits; proline,lysine, glutaric acid and cyaniding-3-glucoside isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Delphindin-3-glucoside isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Quercetin, kaempferol and a mixture of their glycosides isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Oil content in seed coat (2.0) and in kernel (23.7%); oil-free kernels contained 34.9% protein; linoleic acid (51.7%) is major components of oil with lesser amounts of arachidic, heptadecanoic, linolenic, myristic, oleic, palmitic, palmitoleic and stearic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

CIMAP:   Phalsa is a fruit of very poor keeping quality. Mature fruits can be stored upto 48 hr and ripe ones for 24 hrs. The mature fruits resemble ripe fruits after 24 hr of storage at room temp. The sugar content of mature fruits increases tremendously and there was a slight increase in ripe fruits during storage. All sugars in phalsa are reducing sugars1; Foliar mineral compn. of guava and phalsa seedlings grown in artificially salinized sandy loam soil at various salinity levels (1.8, 3.0, 4.5, 7.5, 10.5, 13.5 and 15.0 mmhos/cm for guava and 1.8, 3.0, 6.0, 9.0 and 12.0 mmhos/cm for phalsa) was studied Salinity levels above 7.5 and 6.0 mmhos/ cm caused significant increase in the Ca and Mg contents of leaves in guava and phalsa, resp., whereas significant redn. in the contents of N, P and K were obsd. above these levels of salinity in these 2 fruit species. The Cl and Na content of leaves in both species increased with increasing levels of salinity, and the increase was more pronounced above 7.5 and 6.0 mmhos/ cm in guava and phalsa, resp. High concns. of Cl and Na above these levels of salinity in both the crops species corresponded with leaf injury symptoms and redns. In fresh wt. of shoots. Levels of Cl and Na accumulations at which toxic effects occur were similar in both fruit species. In guava and phalsa specific injury from Cl- and Na accumulation in the foliage and nutrient imbalance caused by the high concn. of salinity ions in the soil and plant foliage seem to be the chief effects of salinity2.
