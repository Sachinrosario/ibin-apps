HABIT & HABITAT:
  Herbaceous plant, found to occur in Native to America; spread all over Uzbekistan, Russia and central Asia. In India it  occurs in Punjab and naturalized in Kashmir and ascending up to 2000m.; Pakistan (Peshwar to Waziristan).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual, glabrous, stem high, furrowed, passing in to an ample spreading branching compound panicle.  Radical leaves long-petiolate in outline triangular-ovate, bipinnatisect; primary segments petiolulate, secondary ones oblong with acute, dentate pinnatipor tite lacinulae.  Primary rhachis without teeth. Floral leaves sessile with linear setaceous teeth. Heads very small, globere, pedicellate, hanging, outer leaves of involucre few, oblong-linear herbaceous; inner ones suborbiculate, scarious.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: terminal compound panicles ; Flowers: arranged in small heads and typically yellow in colour, heterogamous ,2-3 mm wide,globular,bract linear,oval-acuminate or oval,marginal flower are pistillate and central florets are hermaphrodite; Calyx absent ; Corolla of both the floret are synpetalous and tubular with the top split into 5 lobes in hermaphrodite florets and into 2-3 lobes in the pistillate florets ; Androecium stamens 5,epipetalous,alternating with corolla lobes, filaments free but anthers usually connate into the tube around the style, anthers dithecous, introrse and opening by longitudinal slits ; Gynoecium bicarpellary, syncarpous, ovary inferior, unilocular with single ovule in each locule, stigma bifid ; Fruit  is an obovoid  achene . Flowering & fruiting:  July-October.  Plants are cross pollinated by variety of insects and wind action, sometimes self pollination is also seen. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plants are propagated through seeds .

Soil type: Plant is adapted to grow in wide variety of soils.

Nutrient requirements: Nitrogen has been found to give good response and trace elements like molybdenum and boron have been found to affect flower yield and oil contents.

Water management:  It grows well under adequate soil moisture,in many situation frequent light irrigation’s are necessary to ensure good safe establishment.

Harvesting period / Method: For higher yield of active constituents / essential oil plants should be harvested at full bloom.

UTILIZATION:
Parts Used and Uses: (NBPGR)
Plant- Antipyretic and Antimalarial. Chinese chemists isolated Quinghaosu (QHS) which has been successfully used against malaria chloroquine-sensitive and  choroquine-resistant strains of Plasmodium falciparum. QHS is a sesquiterpene lactone-Artemisin. It also yields essential oil of interests to perfumery, cosmetics and dermatology.The oil has specific fungicidal properties.

(56%) (7%) of individuals2; The time course of the levels of artemisinin, its biosynthesis precursor and the biosynthetically related sesquiterpenes was monitored during the vegetation period of A. annua plants of different geog. origin. considerable differences in contents of artemisinin and its direct precursors artemisinic acid and dihydroartemsinic acid were found between these A annua’s. For the 1st time the A. annua  plants different geog. origin were found to belong to different chemotypes. A chemotype with a high artemisisnin level was found to have also a high dihydroartemisinic acid level but a relatively low artemisinic acid level. Reversibly, a chemotype with low levels of artemisisnin and dihydro artemisisnic acid contained a high artemisisnic acid level. Artemisisnic acid is considered to be the direct precursor of dihydroartemisisnic acid in the biosynthetic pathway of artemisinin. The obsd. accumulation of artemisisnic acid in 1 of the A. annua chemotypes may indicate the presence of a ratelimiting step in the biosynthetic pathway of artemisisn. The enzymic redn. of artemisisnic acid into dihydro artemisisnic acid is probably a “bottle neck” in the biosynthetic pathway of artemisinin in varieties with high artemisisnic acid and consequently low artemisinin levels. After a night-frost period, the level of artemisisnin was increased, in the vietnamese A.annua plants, while the dihydroartemisisnic acid level was decreased. The phenomenon is in accordance with the authors hypothesis that stress triggers the conversion of dihydroartemisisnic acid to artemisinin. It is suggested that the presence of high levels of dihydroartemisinic acid may be an adaptation to stress conditions (eg. night-frost), during which relatively high levels of ‘O2 are formed. Dihydroartemisinic acid gives the plant protection by reacting with these reactive oxygen species yielding artemisinin as stable end-product 3.

Pharmacology:
Qinghaosu exerted direct parasiticidal action in vivo and in vitro on plasmodium in erythrocyte stage. It showed good therapeutic results in almost all cases and did not cause adverse reactions in clinical studies.

Artemisinine showed antimalarial activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)u.

Artemisic acid (0.125-0.5 mg/ml) showed effective antibacterial activity against staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Bacillus dysenteriae and typhoid bacillus. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)v.

Arteannuin (0.1mm) suppressed growth of plasmodium falciparum in in vitro culture; Its antimalarial action was different from that of chloroquine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)w.

Phagocytic activity of macrophages from plasmodium in in vitro culture system was increased inpresence of arteannuin. Acid phosphatase activity was also increased in abdominal macrophages from arteannuin-treated mice as compared with those from control mice. Antimalarial activity of arteannuin may be associated with its macrophage phagocytic enhancing effect. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)x.

ED50 of artemisnin for inhibiting Plasmodium falciparum strain FCC-1/HN in vitro was 1.99ng/ml as compared to ED50 of 1.24 mg/ml for chloroquine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)y.

Artemisinin showed potent inhibitory activity in vitro comparable to that of mefloquine,against chloroquine-susceptible and resistant strains of Plasmodium falciparum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)z.

Qinghaosu caused significant changes, notably swelling of mitochondria in parasites after 2.5hr of administration in Plasmodium inui-infected monkeys. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a2.

Artemisinin acted rapidly as blood schizontocide against P. berghei and P. yoelii nigeriensis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a3.

Artemisinin at 150.0mg/kg, b.i.d for 7 days, reduced level of serum IgG and hemolysin-forming capacity of mice sensitized with sheep red blood cells (SRBC). It reduced weight of thymus in SRBC-sensitised and PHA-treated mice but increased weight of spleen in these mice. On the other hand, artemisinin at same dosage enhanced PHA-induced lymphocyte transformation rate in mice and increased T cells in SRBC-sensitised mice. At a dose of 150.0 mg/kg for 5-days, artemisinin reduced percentage of phagocytosis of peritoneal macrophages. Thus artemisinin showed both immunosuppressive and immunostimulant activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a4.

Single i.m. injection of 5.0mg/kg artemisinin in rhesus monkeys carrying gametocytes of plasmodium cynomolgi resulted in complete loss of mosquito infectivity within 24hr of drug administration. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a5.

Bis-(1-hydroxy-2-methylpropyl) phthalate inhibited germination of wheat coleoptle and mustard seed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a1.

Cytotoxicity of artemisinin, artemetin, deoxyartemisinin, artemisinic acid,arteannuin B and quercetagetin 6,7,3’,4’-tetramethyl ether was tested in vitro on several human tumor cell lines; artemisinin and quercetagetin-6,7,3’,4’-tetramethyl ether exhibited significant cytotoxicity against P-388, A-549, HT-29, MCF-7 and K B Tumor cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a2.

Plant extract exhibited high inhibition of tobacco viruses. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a3.

Various extracts of plant showed marked antipyretic and anti-inflammatory activities and also some antinociceptive and antibacterial actions. Artemisic acid is identified as antibacterial and scopoletin as anti-inflammatory agents. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a4.
