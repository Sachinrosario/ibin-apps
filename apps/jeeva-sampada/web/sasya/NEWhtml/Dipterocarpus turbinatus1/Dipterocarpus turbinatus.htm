Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Lofty resinous tree. Evergreen or semi-evergreen moist tropical forests of Assam and Andamans. Cachar and Silchar in Assam, mostly found in the Surma Valley, and in Manipur, Tripura, Meghalaya (Garo Hills), Mizoram, extending into Myanmar, Chttagong Hills (Bangladesh). Mostly on low undulating hills below 300 m. Its occurance in the Andamans is doubtful.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tall tree, trunk straight to the top; bark light grey, deeply cracked. Leaves leathery, ovate, acute or very shortly acuminate, margins entire or sinuate-crenate, glabrous on both surfaces, shining above, base rounded or acute, drying to coppery brown; lateral nerves prominent, 14-18 pairs, oblique or parallel; stipules oblong-ovate, acute, gray-velvety, with a tuft of soft hairs at the apex.

Flowers in axillary, 3-8 flowered racemes. Calyx tube abconic, hoary, puberulous, the mouth contracted in fruit, two enlarged lobes, oblong, obtuse, strongly 3-5 nerved and reticulately veined, the other lobes deltoid. Petals white, tinged with red, linear, obtuse, puberulous without, twisted in bud. Stamens numerous, filaments yellow, linear, subulate, terminated by a long slender bristle.

Fruit subglobose, pubescent, without ribs, spindle shaped, with linear marks on surface on maturing; fruit-belly 2-3 X 3-3.8 cm, smooth, ovoid or ellipsoid, very shortly stalked, wings two, terminal, 11.4-17.8 X.5-3.8 cm, with three nerves in the lower half, middle prominent, the two veins disappearing about half way up the wings.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Old leaves are generally shed early in the hot season. Flowers January to March, occasionally up to April. Fruit ripen and fall from May to June. 


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

When the fruit drop they rotate in the air like propeller of an aircraft, so that they are blown far and wide. The seeds have a poor viability and are therefore sown within 7-10 days of collection.

Seedling Germination: A burnt area forms a favourable germinating bed for seeds which fell after the season of fires. The treatment necessary for ensuring natural reproduction of this species is to clean the ground of all cover, retaining sufficient seed-bearers. The area is burnt and thereafter strict fire protection measures are enforced. Necessary cleanings and thinnings are regularly carried out.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Good fuel. 

Wood used for constructional purposes. Also used for heavy packing cases and for rough furniture. It can be used for railway sleepers after treatment. Valuable timber commercially used for boat building, planking, flooring and dugout canoes. Timber is slightly stronger and harder than teak. Wood is used for making packing cases and wagon construction.

Other uses:

Gurjun oil - Wood is the principal source of gurjun oil in Bengal, which is used as an adulterant of copaiba balsam. It is an ingredient of lithographic inks and anti-corosive coating compositions for iron. It is also used as a preservative for timber and bamboo, and for caulking boats.baking varnishes giving films with satisfactory flexibility and resistance to water have been prepared in combination with heat-treated castor oil and linseed oil. The oil obtained by the distillation of the oleo-resin has been used from time to time for adulterating palmarosa oil. It has no value as a perfume material. It has good solvent properties but is inferior to turpentine.

Oleo-resin - Used as an external application for ulcers, ring-worm and other cutaneous affections. It is given with malt extract as an expectorant. Gurjum oil - It is medicinally important.

Miscellaneous Information

 Wood: Sapwood and heartwood fairly well demarcated. Sapwood dirty white to greyish to pale yellowish-brown, heartwood pale red to reddish-brown, sometimes with orange tinge darkening to red on exposure occasionally with reddish streaks, often with gummy exudation on the end surface. Moderately hard; moderately heavy to heavy, average weight 785 kg/m3 (49 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content. Mostly moderately heavy. Specific gravity 0.59 – 0.91 air dry. Straight to interlocked or irregular-grained, coarse textured.

Wood diffuse-porous. Growth rings indistinct. Pores very large to moderately large, visible to the eye, prominent under the lens, few to moderately numerous, exclusively or mostly solitary, occasionally filled with tyloses; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues visible only under the lens, as thin sheaths round the pores and also diffuse-in-aggregates occurring as short broken lines between the rays. Rays moderately broad to fine, the former visible to the eye, few, widely-spaced. Gum ducts (vertical) always present, much smaller than the pores, usually arranged in short tangential groups of 2-6, occasionally more, and filled with white deposits. The tree is sensitive to fire and the power of recovery from fire injury is poor.
