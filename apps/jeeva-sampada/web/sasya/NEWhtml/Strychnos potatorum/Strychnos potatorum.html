Chemistry:

·        An alkaloid – diaboline (fig.1) isolated. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1970-1979)a

·        b-sitosterol and stigmasterol isolated from seeds. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1970-1979)b

·        Oleanolic acid and its 3b-acetate and a saponin containing oleanolic acid, galactose and mannose isolated from seeds; galactose and mannose also also isolated as free sugars. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1970-1979)c

·         A new triterpene isomotiol  (fern-8-en-3b-ol) (fig.2)isolated from leaves; mixture of sitosterol, stigmasterol and campsterol obtained from leaves and bark. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1970-1979)d

·        Quercetin and vanillic acid, syringic, 2-hydroxy-4-methoxybenzoic, chlorogenic, and sinapic acids found in plant. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1970-1979)e

·        Structure earlier proposed for mannogalctan from seeds revised. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1990-1994)a

·        Harmane carboxamide, norharmane, cantleyine, 18, 19-dihydrousambarensine, polyneuridine, akuammidine, nor-C-fluorocurarine, ochrolifuanine A, bisnordehydrotoxiferine, ochrolifuanine E, normacusine B, normavacurine, henningsamine, 11-methoxyhenningsamine, dihydrolongicaudatine, dihydrolongicaudatine Y, antirhine, 20(R)- and 20(S)-dihydroantirhine, 11-methoxy-12-hydroxydiaboline, diaboline, its N-oxide, 11-methoxydiaboline and deacetylretuline isolted from root bark. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1990-1994)b

·        The composition of the coagulant polysaccharide fraction from Strychnos potatorum seeds is described. This fraction comprises a 1:1.7 mixture of a galactomannan and a galactan. The structure of these polysaccharides is also discussed. (Adinolfi et al., 1994)

Pharmacology:

·        Cholesterol and triglycerides reducing effect of mannogalactan was one-tenth and one-fifth respectively as compared to that of clofibrate. (Rastogi And Mehrotra , 1990-1994)b

·        The antidiarrhoeal activity of the methanol extract of the dried seeds of Strychnos potatorum (MESP) has been evaluated out in rats using different models (castor oil-induced diarrhoea, effects on gastrointestinal motility and on PGE(2)-induced gastric enteropooling. MESP (100, 200 and 400 mg/kg, p.o.) significantly (P<0.001) inhibited the frequency of defaecation and reduced the wetness of faecal droppings in castor oil-induced diarrhoea, decreased the propulsion of charcoal meal through the gastrointestinal tract, and also reduced the PGE(2)-induced enteropooling. (Biswas et al., 2002).

·        The diuretic effect of  Methanolic extract S.potatorum was comparable with that of the standard drug Furosemide. The increase of cations in the urine on treatment with S. potatorum seed extract (SPSE) was dose-dependent. This effect supports the use of the Strychnos potatorum seeds as a diuretic in folk remedies. (Biswas et al., 2001)
