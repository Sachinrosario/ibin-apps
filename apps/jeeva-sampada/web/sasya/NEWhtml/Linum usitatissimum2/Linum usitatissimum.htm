HABIT & HABITAT

Flax is an erect annual and is cultivated throughout the plains of India and upto an altitude of 1800 mt. Flax is chiefly found in temperate parts of the world. The native country of flaxis unknown but is probably thought to be originated in oriental regions. Some of the important flax growing countries are France, Ukraine, Netherlands, Egypt, Lithumia, Poland, Argentina, Chlie, Ireland.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Flax is an annual with long and slender stem upto 60-12 cm. tall. It has long and slender but fibrous roots, which penetrate to a considerable distance into the soil, where this is coarse and friable. The stem is smooth, simple and erect; ranched, or, as usually cultivated, branching only towards the top; from one and a half to three feet in height. It consists of a pith and woody part, with the layer of bark fibers covered with cuticle on the outside.

The leaves are alternate, sessile, linear-lanceolate, and smooth. The flowers are small, blue in colour, and arranged in a corymbose panicle. The fruit is an indehiscent capsule enclosed by the persistent calyx slightly ridged and sharply pointed at the apex. The fruit/capsule contains 10 seeds, one in each of the ten division.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
The inflorescece is a corymbose panicle. The flowers are small, blue in colour, sepals are five in number, ovate acute, slightly ciliated, nearly equal to the capsule in length. The petals are blue, five in number, obscurely crenate, comparatively large and deciduous. The stamens are equal in number to the petal and alternate with them, having their filaments united together near thin bases into a kind of ring. The ovary or young seed vessel is divided into 5 cells and is surmounted by five stigmata.

Capsule or ball roundish but rather pointed at the apex divided into five perfect cells, each of which is again subdivided by an imperfect partition thus forming 10 divisions, each of them containing a single seed. The seeds are exotestal, exotegnic, oval in shape flattened or plump, smooth and shining, of a brownish colour externally, but sometimes white, always white internally, the seed coat mucilaginous and the kernel of the seed oily and farinaceous.

Development of endosperm is of nuclear type. Haploid diploid twins are reported in Flax.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Flax is a self-pollinated plant. Cytoplasmic male sterility is known in Flax. If warranted the expenses, certainly hybrid flax seed could be produced though the species is normally self-pollinating. The species show trimorphic heterostyly but functions as dimorphic heterostyly.

Phenology:


Pollinators: Since flax is bisexual, it normally is self pollinated, although some natural crossing may be carried out by various insects such as thrips (Thysanoptera). Some varietal crossing has been reported by workers in India, England, the Netherlands, Germany, France, Ireland, the US, the crossing has usually represented chance occurrences that happened in fence than 1 of the plants (Robinson, 1937). The large blue-flowered varieties are more susceptible to outering than small white flowered ones.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:

Diseases:

Heat canker is a now parasitic disease caused due to heat injury of the cortical tissues of hypocotyls and stem near the soil. Preventing excessive soil temperature by early planting, by drilling the rows worth and south so as to secure maximum shading, by higher rates of seeding, by use of a nurse crop, by mulching the soil surface and by irrigations can reduce the damage of such type.

Viral diseases caused by curly top virus and Aster yellow viruses.
Seed rot, seedling blight and root rot caused by Pythium spp. other pathogens like Polyspora lini Laff., Colletotricum linicolum Pethyb. & Laff., Fusarium spp., Coniothyrium olivaceum Bom., Rhizoctonia spp., cause and ortting and seedling blight of flax. Erysiphe cichoracearum DC or Erysiphe communis Wallr. causes powdery mildew on leaves, stems and floral structures.

Pasmo disease - caused by Septoria linicola (Spreg.) Gar., Mycosphaerella linorum (Cur.) Garcia-Rada causes defoliation, reduces yield of seed, and damages the fire. Control measures include rotation and removal of straw or covering the straw in plowin helps reduce the inoculum seed treatment reduces seed borne conidia and also the use of resistant varieties can control the disease.

Polysporaline Laff. causes browning and stem break control measure is the use of disease free seed, seed treatments and use of resistant varieties are important in control of browning.

Anthracnose is caused by Colletotrichum linicolum Pethyb. & Laff. controlled largely by the use of sound seed, seed treatment, rotation of crops and resistant varieties.

Fusarium wilt of leaves caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. lini. (Bolley) Snyder and Hansen. Controlled by using with resistant varieties.

Rust is caused by Melanospora lini (Pers.) Lev. on stems, leaves and capsules. It is controlled by crop rotation, removal/plowing under of the flax refuse.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
fiber

fibers are woven into fine fabrics such as Cambrids, Hollands, Crashes, Damasks and Sheetings. Also used for linen stitching, paulin stitching, fishing twines, binding twines, ropes, fishing nets, carpet making, house furnishing, sails, wrapping cloth, handkerchiefs, dress interlinings, men's suitings, towels, draperies, upholstery, covers, surgical dressings, fire fighting hoses and water holding bags.

Linseed fiber used as textile, manufacture of twines ropes, canvas as substitute for jute in manufacture of carpet backing and as a substitute for coir in matting.

Cottonized fiber is used for making rugs, blankets, suiting and skirting cloth, bed spreads, curtains, carpets, and canvas.

PAPER PULP

Used in making high grade paper, cigarette paper, air mail, bible and other high priced books and writing papers, safety paper used for currency notes.

LINSEED OIL

Used in paints, varnishes, linoleum, oil-cloth, printing and lithographic inks, soft soap, linings and packing, caulking component, lubricants and grease, polishes, plasticizers, pyrotechnic compositions.

Linseed oil is also used as a solvent for industrial stains and for seasoning bobbins and cricket bats and other sports goods.

MEDICINAL

Raw linseed oil is used in pharmaceuticals as an emollient, demulcent, expectorant and diuretic. Also used as a laxative.

FEED

Oil cake is a palatable, protein rich livestock feed. Leaves also makes a low goods feed for cattle. (5)

Harvesting Practices:
To get maximum length of undamaged fbre, the crop is harvested in autumn, by pulling it out by the roots. If harvested too early, the fibers are fine and silky but lacking in strength and low in outturn. Late harvesting, on the other hand, yields fibers that are of inferior quality and fineness owing to liquifaction. Generally, the harvesting is carried out when the lower two thirds of the stem have turned yellow and the leaves have fallen off. This takes place about one month after the appearance of the first bloom on the plant. Unlike other fiber crops, flax are pulled from the filed and not cut. Pulling is considered better them cutting as the cut straw tends to deteriorate at the cut. Pulling by hand is still practiced in some regions, but it is an expensive and slow process. Numerous machines have been devised for pulling fiber flax.

Processing Techniques:
Harvested flax is first dried, by stacking it in upright heaps, and then retted by retting it thoroughly and then allowing the soft tissues of the stem to rot partially. The retted stems are thoroughly dried and then seutched that is beaten to free the softer tissues (non fibrous) from the fibers, which form the fast, on outer layer of the stem fartching was once done by and beating, but now a days it is achieved by passing the stems between rotating rollers and drawn. A combining called hackling follows.
Other uses:

Raw linseed oil is used for the preparation of water paints. Woody materials and short fibers after. fiber extraction are converted into a useful pulp for paper production. Oil cake has a slightly laxative action. Boiled oils dry at a faster rate and form a smooth and lustrous film and are preferred for most industrial uses, e.g., in the paint, lacquer and varnish industries. They are also used in the manufacture of waterproofing materials and patent leather and for imparting special finishes to cotton and silk fabric.

Oil cake, either. alone or with other inorganic manures, can be used for increasing soil fertility.
