HABIT & HABITAT
Stout and robust, erect, much branched, pilose to glabrescent shrubs, monocarpic, 1-3 m tall, from a massive taproot crowned with persistent fibrous leaf bases. It is found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves triangular-ovate, 20-40 cm in diam., ternate-pinnately or pinnately- compound, leaflets narrowly oblong to oblong-elliptic, 5-15 x 1.5-5 cm, obtuse at apex, finely crenate-serrate along margin, glabrous above, pilose beneath, decurrent on rachis; petioles stout, 10-25 cm long, dilated into an oblong sheath; cauline leaves reduced upward, with prominent inflated sheaths. Inflorescence large, paniculate or corymbose; pedicles terminal and lateral, 15 cm long to obsolete. Involucre and involucel lacking. Rays 8-25, stout, subequal, 2.5-5 cm long, spreading. Umbellets 10-20 flowered, matured pedicels stout, spreading ascending, 2-3 mm long. Flower polygamous, yellow; petals lanceolate; calyx obsolete; stylopodium depressed, flat, much shorter than slender, reflexed style. Fruit oblong to oval, 15-20 x 6-10 mm, rounded at apex and base, dorsal ribs filiform, lateral winged, wings much narrower than body. Vittae solitary in intervals, 2-6 on commissure.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
      Inflorescence: Pedunculate umbel; Flower: Yellow, polygamous ; Calyx: Teeth obsolete; Corolla:petal ovate, obtuse or emarginate ;Androecium: Stamens 5, epigynous; Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 2-celled, disk 3-lobed, styles 2, stigma capettilate ; Fruit: Longer and thickened, carpophore 2-partite; Seed:  Dorsally compressed.

Chemistry:

Oil from roots collected in May, contained a-pinene (9.5), b-pinene (5.2%) and limonene (tr) whereas oil from roots collected in July contained chamazulene (6.0-7.0%); fruit oil contained a-pinene (30.0), b-pinene (16.2%), limonene and car-3-ene as major components. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Determination of car-3-ene (18.78), limonene (10.21), a-pinene (8.32), geranyl acetate (7.45), terpinyl acetate (6.26), a-terpineol (5.23), geraniol (4.07), g-terpinene (3.86), camphene (2.56), p-cymene (2.16), myrcene (1.21) and unidentified sesquiterpenes (7.44% ) in seed and stalk oil from Pakistani plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Two new sesquiterpenoids - feruginidin (fig. 3) and ferugin (fig. 2) isolated from rhizomes and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Isolation of a new carotane derivative -feruginin (fig. 5) from rhizomes and its structure elucidation; jaeschkeanadiol, its 5a-(4-hydroxybenzoyl) and 5a-(3-methoxy-4-hydroxy- benzoyl) derivatives also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.
New sesquiterpenes -feruone (fig. 4) and 5a-(p-hydroxybenzoyl)ferutriol (fig. 6) isolated from rhizomes and their structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.
Isolation and characterisation of three new compounds - p-menth- 3-en-1,2-diol (fig. 7), p-menth-4-en-1,2-diol (fig. 8) and p-menth-8-en-1,2-diol (fig. 10) from rhizome oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)h.
Five new sesquiterpene esters - jaeskeanin (fig. 12), jaeskeanidin (fig. 15b), ferutinone (fig. 15a), ferutinianin (fig. 15c) and angeloylferutinianin (fig. 15d) isolated from roots and their structures elucidated; in addition, teferidine, ferutinin, epoxyfarnochrol, drimatol, jaeskeanadiol, its salicylate, angelate and 3,4-dihydroxybenzoate isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Isolation of a new isocarotane - ferujaesenol (fig. 16) and three new carotenes - akichenol (fig. 17a), 9b-O-angeloylakichenol (fig. 17c) and 5a-O-p-hydroxybenzoylakichenol (fig. 17b) from rhizomes and their characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Rhizomes afforded two new jaeschkeanadiol derivatives characterisd as jaeschkenol (fig. 13) and 2a,3a-dihydroxy-4-keto-5a-p-hydroxybenzoyl-jaeschkeanadiol (fig. 14). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Two new jaeschkeanadiol derivatives have been isolated from Ferula jaeschkeana rhizomes. These have been identified as a dichloro compound, jaeschkenol [1], and 2 alpha,3 alpha-dihydroxy-4-keto-5 alpha-p-hydroxybenzoyl-jaeschkeanadiol [2], on the basis of spectral data and the X-ray analysis of the former. (Garg et al, 1993)
Two new isocarotane esters have been isolated from the methanolic extract of Ferula jaeschkeana rhizome. These have been characterized as 5a - 4'-hydroxybenzoyl ferujaesenol and 5a - 3' - methoxy - 4' - hydroxybenzoyl ferujaesenol on the basis of spectral and chemical analysis. (Garget al, 1998) C.A. 1999, 130, 12406c.

Pharmacology: 

Effect of administration of ethanolic extract (0.2 g/kg for 5 days) on biochemical constituents in vital organs of pregnant rats studied. Protein content in kidney was decreased but increased in adrenal and glycogen content in kidney and adrenal was decreased. Significant increase in acid phosphatase was observed with simultaneous decrease of alkaline phosphatase in liver and kidney; however, spleen and adrenal showed increased alkaline phosphatase. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)i.
The hexane extract of Ferula jaeschkeana aerial parts was studied at an oral dose of 25 mg/kg per day for its postcoital effects in pregnant rats. Ovaries of treated rats remained in a cyclic state rather than undergoing pregnancy as demonstrated by constant ovulation accompanied by newly formed corpora lutea. Follicles were present in different stages of development. Uterine histoarchitecture of treated rats appeared non-receptive for implantation. No decidcoma were observed on day 5 of pregnancy and the luminal epithelium remained unresponsive. Uterus was non-oedematous and lumen was considerably wider. Administration of the extract caused increases in the protein and glycogen content of ovary and uterus, while the activity of acid phosphatase remained essentially unchanged and the activity of alkaline phosphatase was increased. The volume of uterine fluid in the treated rats was increased considerably on day 5 post coitum. It appears that the histological and biochemical modifications in the ovary and uterus of treated pregnant rats do not support the preparation of uterus for implantation. (Prakash et al, 1991)
Effect of hexane extract of Ferula jaeschkeana has been studied on corpora lutea of adult cyclic guinea-pigs. Administration of extract showed duration dependent luteolytic changes in the corpora lutea. Its administration for first three days from the onset of estrus caused significant decrease in the diameter of corpora lutea in the ovary at day 10 of cycle. Ovarian wet weight, proteins and glycogen contents were decreased while the activity of acid phosphatase in the ovary was increased. These luteolytic changes in the ovary have also been observed in the histological findings. Administration of extract for other durations (4-6, 7-9 or 10-12 of cycle) did not cause any change at 10th and 16th day. (Patakh et al, 1995)
