Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Dill is an erect, fragrant, annual or biennial herb.

Climate and soil
A winter crop, dill requires a well-drained, sandy soil with full exposure to sunlight. Grown as annual crop during rabi in India. Dill can resist frost in limited extend where as dry and warm temperature favours seed production. It is grown as rainfed crop in black soils and irrigated crop in light soils. In hills, it is grown as summer crop. Dill requires sandy loam soil for good growth and it tolerates slight alkalinity (upto pH 8.5). In India dill is grown as a winter crop, sowing around mid October, harvesting around mid April. In tropics, it can successfully be grown up to 2000 m with an annual rainfall requirement of 500-1700 mm. It does not tolerate wet conditions or frost, and thrives in full light at temperature range of 16-18°C (1).


Availability :

Native to Asia and Mediterranean region of Europe and Africa, growing wild in Southern Russia, France, North Africa. Germany, Hungary, Netherlands, India, Pakistan and USA. European dill is native to Mediterranean Europe and is extensively cultivated in West and East European countries, Russia and USA. Indian dill is closely related to the former and is cultivated extensively in Northern India. European dill, though introduced to India by beginning of the present century did not become popular because of its poor seed set here. Dill is cultivated as cold weather crop in many parts of India. Dill is grown in Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Assam and West Bengal. The sub tropical and temperate regions of India, particularly the northern parts, produce high quality dill (1).
Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild and cultivated

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An erect, glabrous herb growing to about 1 m in height with hollow finely grooved stem, striped dark green and white with bluish spots. Leaves tripinnate-partite having clasping base up to 50 cm length and 20 cm spread, bluish green, decompound, segments filiform, leaf sheath surrounds the stem, ultimate segments being 3-5 mm long. Flowers are pale yellow, organized into compound umbels, accrescent, terminal umbels being larger. Stem is smooth, shining, hollow and bears flat terminal compound umbels with numerous yellow flowers.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
As dill flowers are strongly protoandrous, cross pollination and fertilization is normal. They are yellow, in flat compound umbels, regular, small yellow petals are rolled inwards, bisexual, pentamerous, dichogamous.

Fruit:
Fruits oblong, dorsally compressed, indehiscent lens shaped, seeded carpel schizocarp, 3-4 mm long, 1-3 mm broad, ridged, with five ridges, three dorsal ridges are brown in colour and two lateral ridges are yellow and like, winged having two mericarps that separate easily, with six large and conspicuous vittae.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Raised easily from seeds.

Phenology: Plants come to flowering in around 45-60 days after sowing and flowering continues for about two months (5).


Pollinators:Flowers well visited by bees and flies.

Floral and seed biology:
Seed Biology
Dill seed are oval and brown to tan color, flavour similar to caraway.


CULTIVATION ASPECT:
Seed germination:
Seeds sown in rows takes 7-10 days for germination. Remains viable for 2-3 years without special storage measures and germination rate is about 75% (5).

Related taxa:
Indian dill or Japanese Dill - A. sowa (very similar to European dill) - leaves decompound, ultimate segments about 8 mm long, flowers pale yellow in compound umbels, fruits subelliptical, dorsally compressed, 3-5 mm long, 1.25-2.5 mm broad with three longitudinal ridges, winged with two mericarps which do not separate easily, vittae with irregular marginal walls. Grown as a cold weather crop. Used in Asian cuisines.

Much intraspecific variability occurs in Indian dill. In Gujarat two prominent landraces occur - 'Ghoda sowa' and 'Varyali sowa', differing both in morphology and in chemical composition. In latter, mericarps remain firmly united. Two main chemical races are also distinguished by the pale and dark coloured ones. A third common cultivated variety is the Vizag sowa.

Among the two different botanical species of dill or sowa cultivated in the country major area is occupied by Indian dill Anethum sowa. Cultivation is spread mainly in the states of Rajasthan (Chittor and Bhilwara) and adjoining areas of Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat. It is longer, but narrower, than its European counterparts. Indian dill finds extensive use in foods, beverages and medicines. Its essential oil constitutes an important flavouring for the pickles industry (3).

Cultivars:
Improved cultivars are NP-127, NP-179, NP-115 and NP-15. Their average yield is around 1.5 t/ha.

Released varieties
Gujarat dillseed-1, Gujarat dillseed-2 and RSP-11.

Chromosome number:
2n=20

Selection of planting material:
Seeds are used for planting.

Planting:
Grown as rainfed and irrigated condition. Seeds are sown in March-April in hills and October-November in subtropical plains. Seed rate: 3-4 kg/ha. Spacing: 30 cm apart. Indian dill: Cultivation is similar to European dill. Seed rate is 8-10 kg/ha, sown in rows 40 cm apart and within rows spacing 30-40 cm and 3-6 mm depth by thinning. Before sowing, soil is enriched with farmyard manure.

Management after planting:
Weeding: 2-3 weeding at 30 days interval, first weeding and hoeing is done after 3-4 weeks after sowing followed by thinning of the plant. Plant to plant distance: 10-15 cm.
Irrigation: 2-3 irrigation in light soils grown under rainfed condition in black cotton soils. For good yield high soil moisture should be ensured. Low soil moisture as well as late sowing leads to early flowering and poor yield. Recommended fertilizer dose is 60 kg P2O5/ha. Dill is sometimes intercropped with onions, parsley or carrots. To maximize essential oil yield, an application of 80 kg N, 30 kg P and 30 kg K per ha is recommended. Wind breaks should be established to prevent lodging (1).


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:
Aphids: Myzus persicae
Symptoms: Aphids attack during flowering stage.
Control: Spray Endosulphan (0.07%) or Dimethiote (0.04%) or Monocrotophos (0.04%) or Phosphamidon (0.03%).

Catworms: Agrotis gegetum is a polyphagus insect, caterpillars of catworm cause damage to plants.
Control: Spary Monocrotophos (0.01%) once/twice.

Diseases:

Powdery mildew: Caused by Erysiphe anethi
Symptoms: White powdery patches on leaves, stem, flower and fruits, shriveling of seeds with decrease in oil content.
Control: Spray wettable sulphur (0.2%).

Root knot: Caused by Fusarium oxysporum
Symptoms: Brown spots on collar region and death of plant.
Control: Adopt phytosanitary measures, seed treatment with Afrosan @ 3g/1kg seed foliar spray of Bavistin (0.1%).
Ramularia foeniculi Sibilia (infects in the early stages, especially when humidity rises).
Symptoms: Dirty-brown spindle shaped lesions appear on the growing shoot, resulting in partial or complete sterility.
Other organisms are: Botrytis sp. and Alternaria sp. (both are fungi) that prevent fruit set. Systole albipennis Walker infests the seeds of sowa (5).
Viral infections are possible.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Dill has a distinctive but mild caraway-like flavour (3). Used as a condiment, flavouring and as a pickling spice (2). Green parts and fruits of dill have use both as culinary and medicinal herb. It has a pleasant odour, slightly pungent and aromatic. Seeds are used both whole and ground. Dill is an essential ingredient in many western and continental dishes. Chopped leaves of dill enhance the natural flavours of salads, any green vegetable, potatoes, soups and stews of meat, poultry and fish. Inflorescence and ripe fruits are used to flavour pickled cucumbers, onions, vinegars, sauces, pastries and bread. In India, fruits are ingredient of curry powder. Leaves are best when added to dishes just before they are removed from heat. Dill seed as well as the plant can be used to make dill vinegar and pickles (called dill pickles). Dill stem and blossom heads are used for dill pickles for flavouring soups ground seed is an ingredient in seasoning. Essential oil produced is used in the manufacture of soaps. Extracted dill parts are used as cattle fodder. Fresh and dried inflorescence and infructescences are increasingly being used in ornamental flowering industry (1). Dill is an important flavouring agent in the pickling of cucumbers. Some dill oil is used in cosmetics and perfumes. Dried dill foliage is commonly called dill weed (6).

Parts Used:

Seed

Harvesting Practices:
is usually harvested in 2-3 installments depending on the end products. Winter crop is ready for harvest by middle of March. Crop is prone to shattering therefore harvesting should be done at right stage and in the early morning hours. Plants are pulled out when they become pale yellow and seeds light dark brown, i.e., 7-9 days after petal drop. For foliage, plants are harvested before flowering, about 40-50 days after sowing.

Post Harvest Handling
Harvested plants are thrashed by beating of umbels. Seed crop should be harvested when seeds are fully developed and still retain the green colour, which happens 7-9 days after petals fall. If the seeds are harvested when they are greenish brown they start shattering and decrease in oil content. Seeds are dried in sun, cleared and stored in gunny bags for marketing. In market the produce of dill has wide difference in seed size and shape as well as usable oil content because European dill seeds are longer and posses more volatile oil content with lesser percent of undesirable substance 'apiole' and Indian dill seed have almost equal length and breadth and look flat and less volatile oil content. Average yield is around 1 ton/ha. For marketing dill herb, fresh herb is put in sealed polythene bags in cartons and kept in low temperatures (6-12°C).

Produce and Products:

Dill seed, dill seed oil, dill herb oil and seed powder.
Dill oil is a pale yellow or almost colourless (when fresh) and very mobile with warm-spicy odour reminiscent of caraway and spearmint; hydrodistilled oil has a flavour superior to steam-distilled oil. Taste is warm and slightly but pleasant. Suggested level for use is 2-6 mg/100 g (Guenther, IV, 619; Gupta in Atal & Kapur, Aromatic Plants, 545) (5).

Medicinal uses
Dill infusion in water has a reputation for settling indigestion and curing stomach aches, for inducing sleep and stimulating appetite and milk production in nursing mothers. Dill water is given to babies for curing flatulence and gas production. Seeds are well known for their medicinal properties mostly due to the essential oil contained in the seed. Both seed and oil are used in preparation of various medicines. Dill seeds possess aromatic stimulant, febrifuge, resolvent and diuretic pharmacological properties. Dill oil is strongly antiseptic, has anticarinogenic activities in mice. Dill fruits are antispasmolytic and bacteriostatic for dyspepic disorders (1). Fruits are bitter, acrid, thermogenic, deodorant, digestive, carminative, stomachic, anthelmintic, anodyne, anti-inflammatory, emmenagogue, galactagogue, expectorant, cardiotonic, anaphrodisiac, sudorific, antispasmodic, antidysenteric, alexeteric and vulnerary. Useful in halitosis, flatulence, colic, dyspepsia, intestinal worms, odontalgia, arthralgia, inflammation, strangury, ammenorrhoea, dysmennorrhoea, fever, ulcers, skin diseases, splenopathy, hepatopathy, uropathy, gleet, syphilis, haemorrhoids and cardiac debility (4). Dill promotes menstruation, so it can be used to encourage a late period. Dill seed is for children, what fennel seed is for adults. Called "the secret of British nannies," dill, combined with fennel, is the active ingredient in the famous "gripe water," the miraculous infant colic remedy taken around the world in the British Empire. This plant is also used to treat bad breath, kidney and urinary problems, sleep disorders, intestinal problems, and spasms. For adults, dill, along with fennel, treats heartburn. Dill oil has been resear

ched at Cairo University for its effects in glucose and insulin levels and supporting pancreatic function (6).

Chemistry:
Herb and fruits oil posses different flavour constituents. Dried fruits are distilled for dill oil, colourless or pale yellow oil, which is present to the extent of 1.5-4%. European dill is richer in oil (2.5-4.5%) and some of the elite lines contain upto 6.4% oil. Dill oil contains mainly carvone which varies from 20-70% in various lines. The other important components are dihydrocarvone (Ca. 14%), d-limonene (Ca. 21%), d-phellandrene (Ca. 11%), a-pinene (Ca. 5%), a-terpene (Ca. 4%), Caryophyllene (Ca.4%), myristincin (Ca. 1%), apiole (Ca. 6%) and dill apiole (Ca. 9%). Wide variations occur in the concentrations of various components. Dill apiole is toxic, and its concentration reported to be high in Jammu Variety (upto 30%) and in Allahabad variety (upto 40%). Vizag variety of sowa is reported to be apiole free and this variety has 5-6% oil content also. Dill oil cake left after oil extraction contains about 22% protein and around 32% carbohydrates, used as cattle feed. Dill seed oil (from India) contains: 45.0% lemonene, 23.1% carvone, 20.7% dill apiole, 5.2% cis-dihydrocarvone, 4.2% trans-dihydrocarvone, 0.1% myrcene, 0.1% a-phellandrene, 0.1% para-cymene, 0.1% (Z)-ß-ocimene, 0.1% ß-caryophyllene, 0.1% a,p-dimethylstyrene, 0.1% a-pinene, 0.1% ß-pinene, 0.1% a-terpinene, 0.1% ?-terpinene, 0.1% linalool, 0.1% borneol, 0.1% terpinen-4-ol, 0.1% a-terpineol, 0.1% thymol, 0.1% nerolidol (unknown isomer), 0.1% elemol, 0.1% (E)-anethole, 0.1% cis-carveol, 0.1% ß-eudesmol, 0.1% dodecane, 0.1% pentadecane, 0.1% heptadecane, 0.1% myristicin, 0.1% elemicin, 0.1% viridiflorol, 0.1% neoisodihydrocarveol, 0.1% decane, 0.1% tridecane, 0.1% tetradecane, 0.1% hexadecane, 0.1% octadecane, 0.1% nonadecene/ninadecane (Source: Lawrence, 1980) (7).

Steam distillation of green dill plant yields about 0.1-0.2% of an essential oil - dill herb oil. For this purpose they are harvested just before flowering. Herb oil contains d-a-phellandrene, eugenol, thymol, isoeugenol, and phellandral. Dill apiole is absent in herb oil. Herb oil is preferred over seed oil by Western culinary experts because of its delicate aroma and absence of toxic apiole. The regulatory status of GRAS has been accorded to dill (GRAS 2382), dill herb oil (GRAS 2383) and dill essential oil (GRAS 2384).

Per 100 g edible portion of dry dill herb contains approx.: water - 7 g, protein - 20 g, fat - 4 g, carbohydrates - 44 g, fibre - 12 g, ash - 12 g, ascorbic acid - 60 mg. Energy value is 1060 kJ/100 g. Essential oil - 0.1-1.5%.
Per 100 g edible portion of dry dill fruit contains approx.: water - 8 g, protein - 16 g, fat - 14 g, carbohydrates - 34 g, fibre - 21 g, ash - 7 g. Energy value is 1275 kJ/100 g. Essential oil - 2-6% (1).

Economic potential to the country

India is a major producer and exporter of dill. Rajasthan (Chittor and Bhilwara district) and Gujarat are the major growing states. Also grown in small scale in Madhya Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, New Delhi, Orissa, Maharashtra, and Andhra Pradesh.
Area: 2000 ha
Production: 1650 T
Export: 700t (1999) Rs. 312 lakhs
Yield: 5-7 q/ha (rainfed) 10-15 q/ha (irrigated)

Export potential:
India exports dill (whole as well as its essential oils) to USA, the UK, Canada and Japan. Importing countries: Bangladesh, USA, Israel, Japan, Brazil, UK, and Canada.
Export: 984 t (source: Spices Statistics, Spices Board, 1996-97).
