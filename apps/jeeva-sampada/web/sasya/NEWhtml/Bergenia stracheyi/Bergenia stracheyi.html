 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Availability :  
The plant is reported from Afghanistan to Uttar Pradesh between 3300-4500 m. in alpine slopes.  This is common alpine species of western Himalaya.  In Himachal Pradesh, it forms vast patches in Sub-alpine and alpines areas in Chhota and Bara Bhangal area of Kangra,Pangi-Bharmour of chamba, lahaul spiti, Kinnaur, Dodra-Kwar, Khahadhar and Rohru Ranges of Shimla districts.
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant grows wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
The rhizomatic herb on rocky and shady places.  Root stock is thick, dark brown with short stem, thick and fleshy.  Leaves are stalked and sheathing at the base; ovate or orbicular cordate margin fringed with short and stiff hairs.  Flowers are culuite, pink or purplish in terminal corymbose cymes.  Fruit is a conical capsule having minute seeds.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: Pink in drooping clusters, regular, hermaphrodite; Calyx:  Sepals 5, hairy,, with marginal bristles; Corolla:Petals 5 ;Androecium: Stamens equaling the petals in number; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, frequently fused at their bases and tapering into the stigma, with flat cup shaped receptacle encircling the ovary, ovules many; Fruit: Capsule spillting along the inner edges of carpels ;Flowering & Fruiting : June- August. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts Used: Rhizomes and roots: bitter, astringent, diuretic aphrodisiac, toxic, also used in fever and applied to boils and ophthalmria.  Bark is antiseptic, carminative, and is used to heal up the wounds.
Chemistry:
Bergenin, its C-glycoside, b-sitosterol and catechin-3-gallate isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 - 1979)


Pharmacology:
The effect of aqueous and alcohol extracts of the roots of Saxifraga strachyi. Both extracts caused the inhibition of spontaneous movements of the whole worm and the nerve-muscle preparation of Setaria cervi, characteised by an increase in the amplitude and a decrease in the rate of contractions, while the tone of contractions remained visibly unaffected. The concentration required to inhibit the movements of the whole worm preparation was 140 mg/ml for the aqueous and 250 mg/ml for the alcohol extract. The concentration of S. strachegi extracts required to produce an equivalent effect on the nerve-muscle preparation was 30 mg/ml for aqueous, and 20 mg/ml for the alcohol extract, suggesting a cuticular permeability barrier. (Singh et al, 2000).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine antiscorbutic, astringent, diuretic, febrifuge, ophthalmia, tonic (Root, Gaur et al, 1983); Joint disease (Part not mentioned, Koelz, 1979); Cuts, wounds (Root, Rawat and pangtey, 1987)
