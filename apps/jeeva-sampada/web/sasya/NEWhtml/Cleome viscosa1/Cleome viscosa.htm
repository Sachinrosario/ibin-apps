HABIT & HABITAT :                                                                                       
An erect herb; leaves trifoliolate (or rarely with 5 leaflets), long-petiolate; foliage with rank odor; glandular-pubescent; flowers in bracteate terminal racemes, yellow; petals 4; fruit pod-like, slender, 3-4 cm long, dehiscent; seeds numerous, reniform, reddish-brown, small

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 In Fiji, "often locally abundant as a naturalized weed near sea level in cultivated fields, along roadsides, and especially in canefields" .  "Common as a weed of disturbed places in Samoa, uncommon on Niue" .
 The brown-black seeds are occasionally used for culinary purposes. Both the seeds and the leaves have rubefacient and vesicant properties  The bark is irritant and acrid; externally it is rubefacient and vesicant. The bruised plant is used in western Africa and elsewhere as a counter-irritant and vesicant

The seeds contain glucocapparin from which the mustard oil methyl isothiocyanate is released when the plant material is crushed.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Root, Leaf, Seed.

Uses: 

1.Headache: Grind the seed of Cleome viscosa and make a paste. Apply this paste on the forehead. (K-7) [OR-1-1-139]

2.Lumbago: grind the root of Cleome viscosa and make a paste. Massage this paste on the waist.
