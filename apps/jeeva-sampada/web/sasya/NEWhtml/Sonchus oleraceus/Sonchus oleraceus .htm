HABIT & HABITAT:

Other Uses
  1. Milk thistle was once highly valued as a galactagogue and in the treatment of diseases of the liver. 
  2. Its leaves and roots are used in indigestion and as a febrifuge. Stems are prescribed as a sedative and tonic. Roots are used as a vermifuge. 
  3. An ointment made from the decoction is used to dress wounds and ulcers. 
  4. The brownish gum formed by evaporation of the latex of milk thistle is a powerful cathartic and is used in the treatment of ascetics and hydrothorax.
  5. Milk thistle is eaten cooked as a vegetable or as salad. It is also used as fodder for cattle, sheep and goats though the plant is suspected to be poisonous to horses. It forms a good feed for fowls. 
  6. Milk thistle may sometimes become a weed; its infes tation can be controlled by spraying 2,4-D at 2 kg./ hectare 
  7. A narcotic is reported to be present in the plant and the Chinese in California arc reported to use the plant
  8. It is an anti-opiate. Sub-cutaneous injection of aqueous or oil suspension, or alcoholic or acid extract of the plant material has been reported to produce histologically demonstrable damage to Sarcoma-37 cells 
  9. Young leaves are reported to contain 4.1 mg./l00 g. of vitamin C.   Rubber to the extent of 0-14 per cent is pre sent in the stems of plants from Spain. Taraxasterol has been reported in the plant
