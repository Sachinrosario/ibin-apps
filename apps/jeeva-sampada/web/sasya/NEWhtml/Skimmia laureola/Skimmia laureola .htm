Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
 A glabrous shrub, found throughout the Temperate Himalaya from Marri to Mishmi, at attitudes between 6,000ft and 10.000 ft, and o the Khasia mountains between 5,000 and 6,000 ft. It can be seen in forests.
BioticZone: Himalaya and Temperate Himalayas.

Availability : 
Misc: C. Nepal to N. China, Fagu, Gulmarg, Kanara-Mohankhal, Kashmir to Mishmi, Khasia hills, Khelanmarg, Khiraum valley, Lata-Belta (Nanda devi), Marri, Mishmi, Pahlgam and Tosh maidan.
Country: Afghanistan, Ceylon and India.
Zone: Central, East, North and North East.
State: Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Meghalaya, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.
District: Chamba, Chamoli, Darjeeling, Khasi hills, Kinnaur and Simla.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
An erect glabrous shrub 0.6-1.2 m high with stems upto 2.5 cm diam. leaves crowded towards the ends of the branches, 7.5-15 by 2-3.8 cm., closely gland dotted, oblong lanceolate or oblanceolate, acute or acuminate, thick, softly coriacous, lateral nerves obscure. Petiole 2.5-7.5 mm long, stout, flattened above. Flowers 7.5-12 mm diam., pale greenish yellow, fragrant, in compact, erect, terminal panicles 3.8-5 cm long. Stamens yellow, as long as the petals. Drupe ovoid, 1.2-1.8 cm long, red when ripe.  The wood is soft, white with distinct concentric rings, when fresh has an aromatic scent. Weight 42 lb per cubic foot.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Leaves, Timber.

Uses

  1. The leaves when crushed have an orange-like smell, and are burned near small-pox patients with a view to curative effects.
  2. The hill tribes often eat the leaves in curries.
  3. The timber is used to make hoe and axe handles.
  4. The leaves are burnt as incense.
