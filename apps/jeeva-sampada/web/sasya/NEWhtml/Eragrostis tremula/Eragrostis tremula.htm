HABIT & HABITAT
 It is a herbaceous annual with narrow tapering leaves. These grasses are found nearly throughout India , from Punjab to Bengal and southward to Karnataka. It is also fairly common in the plains of Assam and in rice fields in Manipur. It occurs mostly in light sandy soils, especially on poorly cultivated ground.

Miscellaneous Information

Other sps.
Besides the species described above, a few others .are considered useful for fodder purposes.

E. cilianensis (All.) Link ex Lutati syn. E. major
Host; E. megastachya Link (KAN.-Bettada akabu- hullu; BOMBAY-Ran pohe, kaodia) is an annual grass with culms 3 ft. high, found through out India, up to 7,000 ft. in the Himalayas. It is eaten by cattle. The straw is used in West Africa for matting and thatching and the grain is eaten by some tribes .

(E. gangetica Steud. syn. E. elegantula Steud. (BOMBAY-Todha, asara, kaluargi; V.P.-Jenkua, khan) is a perennial grass, often glaucous, found throughout India, usually on low-lying swampy ground. It is eaten by cattle either green or as hay. In some parts of Madhya Pradesh, it is used for brooms .

E. unioloides Nees syn. E. amabilis Wight & Arn. non Linn. (TEL.-Udara gaddi; BOMBAY-Poi, poke, motichava) is an annual grass found throughout the plains in India, especially in wet places. It is grazed by cattle and horses. It is reported to be ploughed in as green manure when it occurs/plentifully in paddy fields .

Other minor species of Eragrostis grazed or eaten by cattle are : E. ciliaris Link, E. coarctata Stapf ex Hook.f., E. diarrhena Steud. syn. E. interrupta Beauv. var. diarrhena Stapf, E. japonica . Trin. syn. E. interrupta var. tenuissima Stapf, E. nigra Nees ex Steud., E. nutans Nees ex Steud. syn. E. stenophylla Hochst. ex Miq., E. riparia Nees syn. E. tenella var. riparia Stapf, E. spicata Vasey, E. tenuifolia Hochst., E. uiscosa Trin. syn. E. tenella var. uiscoso-Stapf and E. willdeno- viana Nees .

A few exotic species have been introduced into India for soil conservation purposes. Of these E. curvula Nees (Weeping love grass) and E. lehmanniana Nees (Lehman love grass), introduced from S. Africa and U.S.A. respectively, have proved useful. The love grasses form well developed clumps with strong root systems. With the exception of E. curvula, others are poor in flowering and seeding.
