DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Branches spreading; bark yellowish grey, thick. Leaves pinnate, 30 cm long; leaflets 7–9, elliptic to elliptic-oblong, 7–10 x 3.5 – 5.0 cm, acuminate at apex, unequal at base, entire or occasionally serrulate along margin, glabrous, caudate. Flowers fragrant, in lax terminal, glabrous panicles; bracts minute. Calyx campanulate, c 0.9 cm long, purplish, glabrous. Corolla campanulate to tubular, c 2.5 cm long; lobes 5, rounded. Stamens 4, subequal. Capsules elongated, 30–60 x 1.2 cm, curved, 4– angled.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Roots, leaves and flowers are used as febrifuge. Leaf juice mixed with limejuice used in maniacal cases. Flowers and fruits are also used in scorpion bites.


Chemistry:

·        Structure of flavonoid glycoside proposed earlier. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Found to be incorrect; it is designated as stereolensin and its structure established as 5, 7, 3’, 4’-tetrahydroxy-6-O-b-D-glucopyranosylflavone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:

·        Medicine for asthma (root, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973, Sharma, et. al., 1979, part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); cholera (bark, Rao and Jamir, 1982); dysentery (bark, Rao and Jamir, 1982, part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); indigestion (bark, Megoneitso, Rao, 1983); liver complaint (bark, Megoneitso, Rao, 1983); malaria (bark, Rao and Jamir, 1982); phthisis (part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); rheumatism (seed, Tribedi, 1985) stomachache (bark, Rao and Jamir, 1982). Dye (bark, Rao, 1990). Symbolic keeps snakes away (wood, Jain, 1963).
