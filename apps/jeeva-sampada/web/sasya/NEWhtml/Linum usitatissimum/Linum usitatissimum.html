Chemistry:

·        Cholesterol (2), campesterol (26), stigmasterol (7), sitosterol (41), 5-dehydro-avenasterol (13), cycloartenol (9) and 24-methylenecycloartanol (2%) in seed characterized by GC-MS.(Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)a.

·        Two new cyanogenic glycosides-linustatin and neolinustatin-isolated and characterized as 2-[(6-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-β-glucopyransyl)oxy]-2- methylpropanitrile and(2R)](6-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-β-Dglucopyranosyl)oxy] 2-methylbutanenitrile respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984)a.

·        Plant contained HCN (0.0486%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994)a.

·        Isolation of oleic, linoleic and linolenic acids and isofucosterol from seed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994)b.

·        A new phenylpropanoid glucoside -linusitamarin- isolated from   defatted seeds and its structure elucidated.(Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994)c.

·        A method for allergen preparation comprises extraction with 2.5% NaCl of pollen of jute, flax and ambary. (Zaritskaya et. al., 1993).

·        A two-phase solvent extraction process, developed laboratory for rapeseed, was used to simultaneously extract oil and toxic, antinutritional components from flaxseed (Linum usitatissimum). to produce a meal suitable for animal feed. The most effective solvent systems consisted of hexane in combination with a solution of methanol that contained 10 percent (vol/vol) water and 2.5-5 percent (w/w) ammonia, or methanol that contained 10 percent water (vol/vol) and 0.8 percent (w/w) NaOH. The Success of the test with a pilot-scale Karr liquid-liquid extraction column suggested that this process could be readily carried out on an industrial scale. The resulting flax meal had a high protein content (40-47 percent) and low levels of cyanogenic glycosides (reduced by 90-100 percent from the starting material). The methanol-ammonia extraction reduced the total polyphenol content by 20 percent. (Varga and Diosady, 1994).

·        Dimer, trimer and tetramer compounds of phenolic nature were extracted from lignin of the bast and woody portion of flax straw. Comparison between the chemical structure of dimers and trimers from flax lignin and firwood lignin indicated that flax lignin has a more complex structure. (Karpunin, 1995).

·        A crude enzyme extracted from flaxseed by acetone precipitation was used for the hydrolysis of cyanogenic glycosides to determine total cyanide in flaxseed and flaxseed-derived products. The hydrolysis of cyanogenic glycosides as well as the endogenous substrate was dependent on the crude enzyme concentration and followed a first-order relationship. Two colorimetric methods using the crude enzyme extract and an established high-performance liquid chromatographic (HPLC) method were compared using 28 flaxseed samples. Total HCN values obtained by all three methods were not statistically different, although those obtained by the HPLC method were higher than those from the colorimetric methods. (Kobaisy et. al., 1996).

·        Extracts of 13 Jordonanian medicinal plants were evaluated against hexane fractions of Linum usitatissimum showed the strongest activity against S. pneumonia. (Ahmad et. al., 1996).

·        Analysis of phenols in cell walls from cells of flax stem xylem and phloem was carried out by GLC. Coniferyl and sinapyl alcohols accounted for about 80% of cell wall phenols in flax xylem. The cell wall phenols from flax phloem contained more than 80% ferulic acid. (Lozovaya et. al, 1996).

·        The seeds of linseed extracted with chloroform-methanol (2:1, v/v) yielded the lipids. The TL were fractionated by silicic acid column chromatography into neutral lipids (NL) (87.8-89.6 percent), glycolipids (GL) (5.8-6.6 percent) and phospholipids (PL) (3.8-5.8 percent). The NL, upon being subjected to preparative TLC, were separated into monoacylglycerol, diacylglycerol, triacylglycerol, free fatty acids, sterols, steryl esters and hydrocarbons. The fatty acid composition of all the lipid materials, as determined by GLC, revealed the major fatty acids to be linolenic, linoleic, oleic, stearic and palmitic acids. (Khotpal et. al., 1997).

·        A new cyclic nonapeptide, cyclolinopeptide B: cyclo (-Pro-Pro-Phe-Phe-Val-lle-Met-Leu-Ile-), has been isolated from seeds of Linum usitatissimum and its structure was elucidated by extensive 2D NMR methods and chemical degradations. Cyclolinopeptide B showeed potent immunosuppressive activity comparable with that of cyclosporin A. (Morita et. al., 1997).

·        The antioxidant activities and total phenolics of flaxseeds were determined. The total phenolic content, determined according to the Folin-Ciocalteu method, varied from 169 to 10548 mg/100g of dry product. Antioxidant activity of the methanolic extract evaluated according to the beta-carotene bleaching method expressed as AOX, AA, ORR, AAC ranged from 0.05, 53.7, 0.009, and 51.7 to 0.26, 99.1, 0.46, and 969.3, respectively. (Velioglu et. al., 1998).

·        The seeds of Linum usitatissimum were extracted with chloroform-methanol (2:1, v/v) to yield the total lipids (42.1-46.3 percent), which were separated into neutral lipids (87.8-89.6 percent), glycolipids (5.8-6.6 percent) and phospholipids (3.8-5.8 percent) by silicic acid column chromatography. Glycolipids were separated by TLC into individual components viz., monogalactosyldiacylglycerol, digalactosyldiacylglycerol, a acylatedsterylgalactoside and sterylgalactoside. The fatty acid composition of total glycolipids and its components as determined by GLC showed linolenic, oleic and linoleic acids, as the major fatty acids. (Kulkarni ey. al., 1998).

·        Cyanogenic glucosides were quantified in different organs in oil flax plant at different stages of development. Monoglucosides and diglucosides appeared in developing embryos soon after anthesis but mature seeds accumulated only diglucosides. Monoglucosides were also found in germinating seeds. Young seedlings, leaves (Throughout the germination period) and in very high quantities in flowers. Very small quantities were found in roots or stems. (Niedzwiedz and Irena, 1998).

·        Experiments were carried out to estimate the amounts and nature of bonding of ferulic acid to cell walls of flax phloem and xylem. Isolated cell walls were oxidized with CuSO4-NaOH, with phenol aldehyde products and ferulic acid produced estimated by GC. Ferulic acid content was also analyzed in the cell wall fraction extracted by hot concentrated alkali, which cleaves their linkages. Overall, bulk of the cell wall ferulic acid (60-80%) was found to be ether linked to cell-wall components. Room temperature alkali treatment may release from the cell wall a portion of the ferulic acid that is esterified to cell wall components via sapon. This treatment, however also extracts a portion of the etherified ferulic acid that is bound to some cell wall components like proteins or glycoproteins that are acid precipitable. Our results demonstrate that hydroxycinnamic acids can form a significant part (0.01-0.19% of cell wall dry wt.) of primary cell wall phenolics of dicots and the nature of linkages between ferulic acid and polymers of the primary cell wall varies in different plant materials. (Lozoyaya et. al., 1998).

·        The isolation and characterization of lignans, isolariciresinol, Pinoresinol, secoisolariciresinol, and matairesinol, potent phytoestrogens, from flaxseed meal are described. The extraction method selected combined the removal of the lignan glycosides from the plant matrix with an alcoholic solvent system, followed by hydrolysis to release the aglycons. A reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography with diode array detection system was used for initial separation and detection of the lignans at 280 nm in the acid hydrolyzed methanolic extract. Lignan trimethylsilyl ether derivatives were characterized by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry. Secoisolariciresinol is the major lignan in flaxseed; isolariciresinol, pinoresinol, and matairesinol were identified as minor lignan components. (Meagher et. al., 1999).

·        New cyclic nona and octapeptides, cyclolino peptides B-E, were isolated from the seeds of Linum usitatissimum and their structures were elucidated as cyclo (-pro-pro-phe-phe-val-Ile-Met-Leu-Ile), cyclo (pro-pro-phe-phe-val-Ile-Mso-Leu-Ile), cyclo (-pro-phe-phe-try-Ile-Mso-Leu-Leu-) and cyclo (-pro-Leu-phe-phe-Ile-Mso-Leu-val-phe-) respectively by extensive 2D NMR methods and chemical degradations cyclolino peptides B and E showed immuno suppressive activity. (Morita et. al., 1999).

·        A series of flax fiber and yarn samples that had been com. graded low, medium, and high quality were analyzed by light microscopy, wet chemical analysis, Raman spectroscopy, and NMR (NMR) spectroscopy to determine charecteristics which could be related to quality ratings for each sample type. Chemical evaluation showed that, as quality of the yarns increased, amounts of fattty acid and long-chain alcohols and as well as dihydroxy fatty acids decreased. Chemical data on fiber did not show constituent trends with quality. Raman spectroscopy showed increasing amounts of cellulose and decreasing amounts of aroms. and hydrocarbons with increasing quality which paralleled the chemical data. NMR analysis showed nearly equal amounts of crystalline cellulose regardless of quality for both fiber and yarn samples. (Morrison, et. al., 1999).

·        Flax bast tissue was sequentially extracted using hexane, propanol, methanol and water as solvent and extracts were analyzed using RP HPLC and 13NMR. Results indicated a large variety of aromatic constituents including flavanoids and hydroxy cinnamic acids linked to oligosaccharides and hydroxy acids through glycosidicn linkages. The extracts inhibited cellulose and pactinase activities and can thus influence retting. (Biotechol, 2000). 

·        A method was developed for the analysis of secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG) in flaxseeds. The analytical method involves extraction of defatted flaxseed flour with dioxane/ethanol, aqueous basehydrolysis, solid-phase purification of an SDG-containing fraction, and quantitative analysis by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Pure SDG was isolated from flaxseed using different column chromatographic methods and used, as external standard, for the calibration of the method and for quantification. (Johnsson et. al., 2000).

·         A review with 106 references, summarizing data available on the chemical compound of flax, biological activity of flax components (polyunsaturated fatty acids, proteins, phenolic acids etc.) and their application in medicine and nutrition for humans and animals. (Tolkachev and Zhuchenko, 2000).

·        Four cyclic peptides, cyclolinopeptides F-I were isolated from seeds of Linum usitatissimum. Their structures were elucidated by extensive 2D NMR spectroscopic methods and by chemical degradation. Further their immuno suppressive activity is examined. (Matsumoto et. al., 2001).

·        Aporsibility of preparation of cellulose from flax waste and its use for lacquer colloxylin was investigated. The process cationic boiling of flax waste using NaOH, 8,12 and 16% and 1% Na2CO3 wt. from absolute dry fiber was studied at 50-1800 C, hydromodulus 6:1 and 1-5 hr. of boiling. Yield of cellulose was 52-73% exhibiting 8.7-22.1 delignification. Kapper units end containing 92-95% a-cellulose. (Berbash and Deikun, 2001).

·        Components from the bast region of flax (Linum usitatissimum) was removed by hand from seed flax and fiber flax and analyzed by gas chromatography and mass spectrometry. Four main constituent groups were investigated: dihydroxy fatty acids, long-chain fatty acids and alcohols, sterols, and aroms. Long-chain fatty acids and alcohols located in the outer layer accounted for 80-92% of the total found in both fractions. Aroms. and sterols in the outer layer accounted for 29-72 and 27-67%, respectively, of the total. The main dihydroxy fatty acids were a mixture of 8,16-and 9,16-dihydroxyhexadecanoic acids and represented 87-89% of the total dihydroxy fatty acids measured. As a constituent of cuticle, this compound may serve as an excellent marker for indicating the degree of retting as well as a possible marker for fiber quality because this compound is almost always exclusively associated with the outer layer and not the fiber. Morrison et. al., 2001).


Pharmacology:

·        Methanol extracts of the seed of Linum usitatissimum were given orally in dose of 300, 500 and 1000 mg/kg body weight to mice. Analgesic activity was assessed on intact mice by tail flick latency via the tail immersion method. Linum usitatissimum exhibited highly significant analgesic activity. (Ahmad et. al., 1996).

·        The emulsion consists of animal or vegetable oil (Linum oil) 20-55 alongwith lime water 30-75 and borneol 0.1-1.5%. The emulsion showed anti-itching activity, prevented infections, alleviated tissue damage and promoted wound healing. (Su Kejian et. al., 1997).

·        The effects of maceratio seminis lini (MSL) bioflax (defatted specimen of flax seed) on healing chronic gastric ulcerations in rats has meen investigated. Administration of MSL or bioflax significantly affected the area of gastric ulcerations. In MSL treated group, the area of ulcers decreased to 10 percent in bioflax treated group to 30 percent. Two specimens of flaxseed, MSL and bioflax accelerated the healing rate of gastric ulcers in rats. (Bobkiewicz et. al., 1998).

·        Gastric and intestinal infections with H. pylori are eradicated by oral treatment with a powdered preparation containing an essentially H2O insoluble bi-salt, a chamonile dry extract and defatted linseed powder. (Pascoe et. al, 1998).

·        The mammalian lignans enterolactone (EL) and enterodiol (ED) derived from precursors in foods, particularly flaxseed, have been shown to reduce the mammary tumor growth due to their antiestrogenic properties. At 100 mM concentration, both lignans significantly reduced the proliferation of all cell lines. EL was more than twice as effective 17b-estradiol implying that these cells are not estrogen-sensitive. Lignans are growth inhibitors of colon tumor cells and they may act through mechanism(s) other than antiestrogenic activity. (Sung et. al., 1998).

·        A compound is prepared by extracting phytochemicals from plant matter. It is enriched preferably in two or more isoflavones, lignans, saponins, catechins and phenolic acids. The compound is a dietary supplement for treatment of various cancers, pre-and-post-menstrual syndromes, and various other disorders. (Empie and Gugger, 1998).

·        Chemopreventative properties of flax in post menopausal women. (Hutchins and Andrea, 1999).

·        A compound is prepared is enriched in isoflavones, lepens, saponins, catechins and phenolic acids. Flax is a good source for these chemicals. The compound is a dietary supplement for treatment of various cancers, pre and post-menstrual syndronus and various other disorders. (Empie and Gugger, 1999).

·        To evaluate the possible effect on insulin function, medicinal plant extracts were tested in the insulin-dependent utilization of glucose using a rat epididymal adipocyte assay. Flaxseed has been reported to be effective antidiabetic agent. This technique measures direct stimulation of cellular glucose metabolism, so it may be that the active phytochem., in this plant improves glucose metabolism via other mechanisms or that this in vitro screening is not a reliable predictor of hypoglycemic effect in vivo for some products. (Broadhurst et. al., 2000). 

·        Abnormal blood fat value such as high blood fat value in hyperlipemid is controlled with the oil/fat compound that contains mainly triglycerides. The triglycerides have n-3 long chain fatty acid 2-40. The n-3 long chain fatty acid is selected from a linolenic acid or arachidonic acid. Medium chain fatty acid is selected from C6-12 saturated fatty acid n-3 fatty acid being obtained from Linum usitatissimum. Antioxidants 0.5% weight are added. The oil fat compound has good flavour and is also useful for cooking. (Zkuina et. al., 2001).

·        Dietary flaxseed increased total polyunsaturated fatty acids and decreased monounsaturated fatty acids in hen egg yolk. Alpha-Linolenic acid was linearly increased in response to levels of ground flaxseed, but whole flaxseed resulted in reduced alpha-linolenic acid availability for yolk deposition. Similar deposition profiles were exhibited by docosapentaenoic and docosahexaenoic acids. These alternations in yolk composition resulted in n-6 to n-3 fatty acid ratio values as low as 2.92 and 1.94 for 5 and 10 percent ground flaxseed and 2.49 for 10 percent whole flaxseed feeding, respectively. Dietary flaxseed had no effect on the cholesterol level of eggs, which showed a mean value of 188 mg/egg or 1247 mg/100g of yolk. (Botsoglou, 1998).

·        Phenolic resin coatings could be prepared by mixing phenolic resin residues with tung oil, flax oil, solvent and Pbo powder. (Wang et. al., 1999).

·        The agents and topical preparation contain polar solvent extracts of Linum usitatissimum. A skin lotion containing Linum usitatissimum extract showed anti- wrinkle, anti-ageing, anti-inflammatory and wound healing effects in mice. (Yahagi and Tsurumi, 2001).

·        This composition relates to cosmetics comprising (1).
