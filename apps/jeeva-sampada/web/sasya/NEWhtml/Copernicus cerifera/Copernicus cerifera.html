Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Palm exhibiting an erect, single trunk, the palm grows up to a height of 30- 50 feet high (9 m-15m) and up to 14 feet in diameter (4 m) with scaly bark (since covered with the remains of leaf sheaths) and fan shaped leaf. The palm is a solitary palm. . Its leaves, borne on an erect petiole, are palmate, split half their length into approximately 60 acuminate, bright green segments, covered with a waxy substance. It proliferates along river banks, streams and damp lowlands.

Distribution: Northeastern Brazil, it grows in smaller quantities in the south of this country and in the northern regions of Paraguay and Argentina, Ceylon, and Equatorial Africa. Due to irregular rainy seasons in other locations, the Carnauba Palm only produces wax in northern Brazil. In India it is grown in some gardens.
(1, 2, 11,12,14,23,25,26)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
The ‘ Copernica cerifera ’ is a straight-trunked palm of slow growth, attaining an average height of 25 to 35 feet (7 to 11 meters), although in some isolated cases it can reach more than 40 to 50 feet (15 meters). The bark is scaly, rough and thick, and the leaves grow out from yard-long petioles in wide fan shapes. Leaf is Costapalmate, 20 to 30 very waxy, blue green leaves. 30 to 60 narrow leaflets split at their tips, which are 3 to 4 feet wide and 3 feet long (90 cm). Trunk is gray, characteristical pattern of leaf bases persist. (1, 2,11,12,17,18,20)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY

The flowers are Brown or Yellowish-Green, usually in groups of four, bell shaped, bisexual flowers or monoecious in nature. Clustered inflorescences as long as the leaves i.e 5 to 7 feet long, three times branched, paniculate, each branch protected by by a spathe. Its fruits are subrounded and brownish in colour and 1 inch long (2.5 cm). The tree reproduces prolifically. (1, 2,11,12,20)

Breeding System :

The plant is monoecious in nature. (14)

Phenology :

The copernica palm requires an optimum temperature of 26-270 C and rainfall of 1,250 mm during rainy season and dry summers. (1,2,11)

Seed Biology :

Seedling Germination :

Germination takes upto 3 months after sowing. (12)

Cultivation:

Seeds germinate readily in ground softened by the heavy rain and the floods of the rainy season. But the tree needs very little water to grow once it germinates, is very prolific grower, and attains a height 40-50 feet after fifty years. And it is grown under shade. (1,2)

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used and Uses

Leaves: To protect itself against dehydration during the period of drought that severely affects the Northeast region of Brazil every year for more than six months, the Carnauba covers its leaves with a thick layer of wax consisting basically of esters, alcohols and fatty acids of high molecular weight. This wax is called the Carnauba wax which is used all over the world by various industries, such as in the manufacture of polishing wax (floors, shoes, cars etc.), finish coating of fine furniture, tobacco pipes and produce (apples, pears, etc. are sprayed with carnauba to achieve their polished look), in the paper and packaging industries, in making paints and other more resistant continuous coatings, in the cosmetic industry (lipsticks, enamels), Investment Casting, in the food industry (bubble gum, chocolate, fruits), as well as in the pharmaceutical industry as vehicle and recipient. Before the advent of vinyl it was used extensively in producing phonograph records. The leaves are used to make fiber which is locally used to make hats, baskets and mats.

Dried fruits and seeds: Used to extract cooking oil and the roasted fruits are said to impart a coffee-like flavour.

(1,2,10,16,25,26,27)

Harvesting Practices

The cutting of the leaves and sprouts takes place during the dry months of September through February. Workers use knives on long poles to trim the leaves from mature trees. (1,2)

Produce and Products

Paper Industry: Used in the past only to manufacture carbon paper, the paper industry now uses refined Carnauba wax to produce among other types of material.

Packaging Industry: Its insulating properties the Carnauba Wax is widely used to manufacture cartons and foils to protect foods.

Lumber Industry: Excellent for waterproofing laminated plywood.

Aeronautic Industry: Used for covering exposed parts. Gives lasting protection and lessens surface friction allowing greater speeds.

Cosmetic Industry: It is used in making Lipsticks and other waxy cosmetic stuffs. It is used as a texture producing agent in many makeup's.

Pharmaceutical Industry: Uses to Finish medicinal capsules and in various pharmaceutical formulas. Refined Carnauba Wax meets all relevant requirements of this industry.

Cleansing Products Industry: It is main component of many pastes and emulsions used to polish floors, furniture and shoes.

Food Industry: Carnauba Wax is widely used to coat cheeses, sausages and fruits.

Automobile Industry: Used in a variety of anti-corrosive paints applied to car's bodywork to prevent rusting.

Electric and Electronic: The excellent insulating properties of the refined carnauba Wax makes it of great use by this industry.

Peneumatic Industry: Carnauba Wax give tires extra-resistance, used as a plasticiser to prevent sun-cracking.

Printing Industry: The Carnauba Wax is a main component in some types of printing ink, varnishes and additives.

Textile Industry: The Carnauba Wax is used to water-proof fabrics, lubrificate knitting yarns and impart a glossy surface to linens.

It is used in URAD cream because of the protection it offers and for its exceptionally brilliant long-lasting shine.

Additional areas of use the Carnauba Wax:

  • Foundries: used to melt waxes and produce galvanized coatings.

  • Lubrificants: Machine and cable greases.

  • Dental floss: it gives extra resilience.

  • Roofing Material: increases their durability and water-resistance.

  • Miscellaneous: confection of ointments, safety matches, ceramics, metal casting, crayons, concrete curing, glues, modeling compounds, and fiber reinforced plastics.

(1, 2, 5, 6, 9, 16, 18, 19, 21, 24, 25, 26, 27, 32, 33, 34, 38)

The Carnauba wax contains mainly fatty esters (80-85%), free alcohols (10-15%), acids (3-6%) and hydrocarbons (1-3%). As a peculiarity, carnauba wax contains esterified fatty dialcohols (diols, about 20%), hydroxylated fatty acids (about 6%) and cinnamic acid (about 10%). This last phenolic acid compound (antioxidant in free form) may be hydroxylated or methoxylated. It is believed to consist largely of myricyl ceretate and myricyl alcohol (C 29 H 59 . CH 2 . OH).

Processing Techniques

Carnauba Wax is obtained from the leaves of a palm tree known as Copernica cerifera, the tree exudes a wax through the petioles of its fan-shaped leaves, preventing dehydration from the equatorial climate. The unopened heart leaves of the carnauba palm are harvested from September to March, to obtain the wax, the fronds are sun-dried for several days or on steam heated racks and then fed into beaters where the crude wax is removed and the wax powder is separated from the rest of the frond. Carnauba wax is a natural product obtained from the processing of the powder. After going through a refining process that includes filtering, distillation, centrifugation and bleaching (with hydrogen peroxide) the finished product is obtained which is melted and made into desired shape and whose colour may range from grey-greenish to pale yellow to even whitish if the wax has been decolorised.

Carnauba is available in various grades of purity and clarity: #1 yellow is the top grade going downward to #2 yellow, #3 yellow, and various other commercial grades. #1 yellow is exclusively, the finest grade of carnauba available.

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

With a maximum cutting of twenty leaves per year from a tree, the average yield of wax for each tree is about one kilo per cutting. Each plant bears some hundred leaves; from each of them about 7 kg wax are extracted. The color and quality of the wax are governed by the age of the leaves and care used in processing of this hard, brittle, lustrous wax. (2)
