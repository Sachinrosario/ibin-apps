DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect much-branched undershrubs, up to 1 m high, with an unpleasant smell; stems, petioles, and pedicel stellate-densely wooly, intermingled with simple hairs. Leaves ovate to oblong or disc-shaped, 0.5-7 x 0.4-5.0 cm, shallowly cordate at base, sharp, occasionally rounded or truncate at apex, shallowly round-toothed-saw-toothed, 5-7 nerved at base, densely velvety indumentum with minute stellate hairs on both surfaces particularly on veins; Petioles 4-5  mm long, filiform, densely stellate hairs mixed with some simple hairs.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers axillary, solitary or 2-5 in clusters particularly towards apex of branchlets; pedicels 1-10 mm long, accrescent upto 2cm long, pointed towards apex. Calyx bell-shaped, 5-9 mm across, persistent and increasing in size; lobes triangular, sharp or sharp with cocave side, densely star-shaped hair with some simple hairs outside, sparsely stellate hairy towards apex inside. Corolla yellow or yellowish white, c 15 mm across; petals obliquely obovate, truncate at apex; ciliate at base; staminal column c 2.5-3.0 mm long; simple hairy or glabrous. Schizocarp 7-8 mm across; mericarp 8-10, c 3.5 mm long excluding awans, radially c 2 mm, both dorsal and ventral surfaces with prominent reticulations, and apical portion with stellate hairs, awans 3.0-4.5 mm long, bent hairy. Seeds flattened reniform, c 2 mm across, glabrous except for a few short hairs near hilum, dark brown or black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Whole plant is used in gonorrhoea, dysentery and rheumatism. Leaves are used as demulcent, febrifuge and in dysentry. Infusion of roots is given in urinary troubles. Powdered root is used with milk in leucorrhoea and frequent micronutrition.
NBPGR: The roots and leaves are bitter, sweet, emollient, cooling, aphrodisiac, strengthening and promote sexual vigour and vital factor. They are good for rheumatism, flatulence, colic, haemothermia, emaciation, vitiated condition of tridosa, seminal weakness, arthritis and diarrhoea.


Chemistry:
 Roots contain alkaloids-β-phenethylamine, N- methyl- β-phenethylamine, vasicinol, vasicinone, vasicine, choline and betain. These alkaloids are also present in aerial parts. Roots also contain alkaloids-S-(+)- methyltryptophan methyl ether and hypaphorine methyl ester. Alkaloids- emphdrine, Ψ- ephedrine and cryptolepine are reported from aerial parts. Sterculic, malvalic and coronaric acids isolated from seed oil along with other fatty acids (C14:0, C16:0, C18:0, C18:1, C18:2, C18:3). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Ephedrine isolated from stems and roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Identification of palmitic, stearic and hexacosanoic acids and b-sitosterol in aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Pharmacology:  

Treatment of female fertility by Sida cordifolia (Karmarkar et al., 1996.) MAPA. 1998, 9803: 1526.
Assessment of hepatoprotective activity of fumaric acid, isolated from the bioactive total aqueous extract of the whole plants of Sida cordifolia Linn. (Malvaceae), showed significant activity, in-vitro against galactosamine- and thioacetamide-induced hepatic cytotoxicities in isolated rat hepatocyte and in-vivo against carbon tetrachloride-, paracetamol-, and rifampicin-induced hepatotoxicities in albino rats similar to that of silymarin, a known hepatoprotective agent. (Rao and Mishra, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 188597.
Whole plant showed antihepatotoxicity activity. (Rao and Mishra, 1998). MAPA. 1998, 9805: 2857.
Sida cordifolia extracts of the aerial and root parts showed good analgesic, antiinflammatory and hypoglycaemic activities. The ethyl acetate extract of root showed comparable antiinflammatory activity with indomethacin and possessed significantly higher activity when compared with that of the methanol extract of the root part. The ethyl acetate extract of both root and aerial parts of Sida cordifolia showed very good central and peripheral analgesic activities at a dose of 600 mg/kg. The methanol extract of root was found to possess significant hypoglycaemic activity. (Kanth and Diwan, 1999).
Ethyl acetate extract showed analgesic and anti-inflammatory activity and methanolic extract showed hypoglycemic activity. ( Ravinath and Diwan, 1999) MAPA. 1999, 9905: 3124.
The anti-inflammatory, analgesic effects and acute toxicity of an aqueous extract of S. cordifolia were evaluated in animal models. The aqueous extract (AE) showed a significant inhibition of carrageenin-induced rat paw edema at a dose of 400 mg/kg administered orally, but did not block the edema induced by arachidonic acid. The AE also increased the latency period for mice in the hot plate test, and inhibited the number of writhes produced by acetic acid at the oral dose of 400 mg/kg. The aqueous extract of S. cordifolia showed low acute toxicity in mice. (Franzotti et al, 2000).
Ethanolic extract of Sida cordifolia was found to be most potent antioxidant activity (IC50 16.07 microg/ml), water infusion of S. cordifolia on lipid peroxidation inhibition (IC50 126.78 microg/ml). (Auddy et al, 2003).
