Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Hydnocarpus Kurzii is consiedered to be native of tropical areas of Malaysia and the Indian subcontinent. In India, it is commonly found in evergreen forests throughout upper Assam, Tripura, Chittagong, and in Minbu district of Upper Burma, often forming gregarious patches (1, 2, 3).

Availability :  

Status : Wild

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A tree up to 40-50 ft. high with tall trunk and narrow crown of hanging branches. Leaves spiral, simple, leathery, very asymmetric at the base (usual), or symmetric at the base, smooth margined. Leaves 4-10 inch., long, oblong, ovate or lanceolate, acuminate, entire or serrate. Venation pinnate. Secondary veins visible. Intramarginal vein absent. Stalk of blade less than a quarter the length of the blade to a quarter to one half the length of the blade, attached to base of leaf blade. Stipules present; free from one another; laterally placed; not encircling the twig; scale-like, small, not persistent (1, 2, 3, 4).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Inflorescences represented by solitary flowers only (rare), or present; axillary,the flowers from a single point on the stem or on an unbranched axis or on a branched axis; specialized inflorescence units absent; numerous and persistent inflorescence bracts absent. Some flowers unisexual; with male and female flowers on different plants; stalked; about 0.5 cm long or across; round; with male or female parts raised on a column, or not raised on a column; with the female parts of the flower raised on a stalk. Hypanthium absent. Perianth present; with distinct calyx and corolla whorls. Sepals 4-5; free; not persistent in fruit; not growing any further after flowering. Petals present; 4-5; all joined; not fleshy; deciduous; not stalked; entire. Stamens 20-32 , filaments present; all equal in size; isomerous, or polystemonous; opposite the sepals (when isomerous). Anthers 2 loculed; attached by the base; dehiscing by short slits, or dehiscing by long slits. Ovary superior. Carpels joined; 3 or 5. Locules 3-5. Styles absent. Stigmas 3-5 lobed; distinctive, or not distinctive; disk-like (1, 2, 3, 4).

Seed Biology :
The seeds (weighs 1-2 g) resemble brown pebbles in appearance and are of varying size and shape. Seeds are ovoid, irregular and angular, 1 to 1.25 inches long, 1 inch wide, skin smooth, grey, brittle; kernel oily and dark brown. The seed kernel 73-85% of the weight of seed (1, 3. 5, 6).

Economic Potential and Uses

A fatty oil is obtained from chaulmoogra seeds is officially known as Gynocardia oil in Britain, as Oleum Chaulmoograe in the U.S.A. Chaulmoogra oil is yellow or brownish yellow in colour with a characteristic odour resembling that of rancid butter and somewhat acrid taste. It is a soft solid fat at temperatures below 25°, sparingly soluble in 90% alcohol, but soluble in benzene, chloroform and ether. The oil is traditionally used in India and possess antiseptic, regenerative and restructuring properties and is used as a ingradient in various skin products for irritated skin due to severe cold or a too dry atmosphere products for mature, tired or dull skin body and hand products.


A physician is reported to have used chaulmoogra oil in ancient Egypt. Another early reference to the use of oil was made in the writings of the Sushrata Samhitas, dating back to 600 B.C. in India. The oil has long been widely employed as an accepted treatment for leprosy in China and India. Chaulmoogra contains strongly antibacterial chemicals, two of which, hydnocarpic and chaulmoogric acids, are responsible for destroying the bacterium, Mycobacterium leprae, that causes leprosy. In Indian medicine, the oil has also been used to treat intestinal worms and skin diseases. In many places today, the ingredients of chaulmoogra oil, modified by chemists, are still used to cure early cases of leprosy-advanced cases do not yield to the treatment but they have generally been replaced by synthesized sulfones. The seed cake is unfit for use as cattle feed because of the presence of a cyanogenetic glycoside, but it can be used as manure (1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8).

Parts used

Seeds

Uses

In Sikkim, the fruit pulp is sometimes eaten after boiling with water. The bark is rich in tannin and is reported to be used as febrifuge. An infusion of the bark has the odour of the essential oil of bitter almonds. Timber and other branches of the tree are used as firewood (3).

Harvesting Practices
The tree bears irregularly and fruits are gathered once in 2-3 years when a full crop is expected. There is no organised way of seed collection. The yield of fruits per tree is reported to be about 300 (3).

Produce and Products

H. kurzii seeds are the source of the true chaulmoogra oil. Timber and other parts of the tree are used as firewood.

The oils of the chaulmoogra group are characterised by the presence, in predominating amounts of unsaturated cyclic fatty acids mainly chaulmoogric and hydnocarpic; lower homologues are also reported to be present in some oils. The acids contain a cyclopentene ring at the end of the carbon chain and an asymmetric carbon and are dextrorotatory. They possess marked therapeutic value, especially in the treatment of leprosy.


Major fatty acid composition of seed oil: Palmitic acid 4.0%, Oleic acid 14.6%, Gorlic acid 22.6 %, lower homologues of Choulmoogric acid 0.4%, Hydnocarpic acid 34.9% and Choulmoogric acid 22.5 % (1, 3, 7, 9).

Processing Techniques

Kernels contains about 48-55 % of oil. The oil is obtained from the chaulmoogra or hydnocarpus seeds by hot or cold extraction. The seeds yield 30-40 per cent of the oil, according to the method used. By hydrulic pressure about 30.9 per cent of the oil is obtained, while by ether extraction the yield amounts to 38.1 per cent. On hydrolysis the fatty oil yields fatty acids which are mixtures of several constituents and these can be seperated by fractionation (1, 3, 5).

Other Uses
Fruit pulp is used as fish poison however, fish killed by the use of the seed pulp should not be eaten. Employed internally and externally in the treatment of skin diseases, scrofula, rheumatism, eczema, also in leprosy, as a counterirritant for bruises, sprains, etc., and sometimes applied to open wounds and sores. Also used in veterinary practice (1, 3, 8, 10, 11).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

Chaulmoogra belongs to the Hydnocarpus family, quite common in southern Asia, particularly in India. In southern India, chaulmoogra oil has been used for a long time to heal chronic skin diseases, wounds and ulcers. Applied locally, it also relieves rheumatism and other pains. The ripe and fresh seeds give a medicinal oil reputed to be a specific remedy against leprosy quite efficient when the disease has just started to develop (8).

Export Potential

Small quantities of seed oil of Hydnocapus kurzii and Hydnocapus laurifolia was exported during 1950's to Belgium, France and Borneo. The total quantitiy exported was 7, 057 gal., valued at Rs. 57,852. At present, there are no available records of seed oil exports from India (3).
