Chemistry:

 Polysaccharide complex was isolated which consisted mainly of galacturonic acid and arabinose; galactose, glucose, xylose and rhamnose were other sugars of water-soluble polysaccharides. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
Quercetin, avicularin, quercitrin, umbelliferone and scopoletin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Aerial parts contained pyrogallic tannins (2.0%), formic, acetic, pyruvic, valeric, glucuronic and galacturonic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Kaempferol-3-arabinoside, luteolin, quercetin-3-galactoside, rhamnetin-3-galactoside, vitexin and isovitexin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
A new lignan glycoside - aviculin (fig. 1) isolated and its structure determined; in addition, juglanin, avicularin, astragalin and betmidin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
The volume constituents of Polygonum aviculare were investigated using capillary GC-MS-DC. a-caphor (8.29%), linalool (7.62%), spathulenol (4.63%) and camphene (4.04%) were found to be major. (Zheng et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 105291t.
Five flavone compound isolated from the alcoholic extract of the whole plant of Polygonum aviculare are - 5,7-dihydroxy-6-methoxyflavone (1); 5,7-dihydroxy flavone (2), 5,7-dimethoxy-4'-hydroxy flavone (3), morin-7-O-b-D-glucoside (4) and 5-hydroxy-3'-methoxy flavone-7-O-rutinoside (5). (Zheng et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 182317n.
The flavonoid aglycones were studied in Polygonum aviculare by means of the RP-HPLC method.The content of taxifolin, quercetin, quercetin-3-methyl ether, kaempferol, myritcetin, luteolin, isorhamnetin and rhamnetin were determined. (Smolarz, 2002).
A new naphthoquinone, 6-methoxyplumbagin was isolated from the acetone extracts of Polygonum aviculare. Its structure was elucidated by various spectroscopic methods. The known compounds sitosterol, oleanolic acid and 5,6,7,4'-tetramethoxyflavanone were also obtained from this natural source. (Al-Hazimi and Haque, 2002).

Pharmacology:  

Vitexin and rhamnetin-3-galactoside inhibited aggregation of human blood platelets whereas luteolin and kaempferol-3-arabinoside retarded or stimulated aggregation depending upon experimental conditions. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Reatival is a herbal formulation having Polygonum aviculare as one of the ingredients with other plants, decreases O2-, OH and lipid peroxidation. (Stajner et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 156694f.
EtOAc fraction of the overground portion of Polygonum aviculare exhibited the anti-lipid peroxidation and the liver protective effect in intoxicated rats. Two flavanoids, avicularin and juglanis are isolated as active components. Avicularin as a main principle of Polygonum aviculare significantly exhibited liver protective activities by decreasing s-GOT s-LDH levels which represent for the hepatotoxicity induced by CCl4 in rats. (Choi et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 129, 85881t.
Extract of Polygonum aviculare showed the protective effects on BNL CL2 cells damaged by CCl4 with values of less than 100mg/ml. Silymarin had been used as a positive control. (Kang et al, 1999). MAPA 9905-3083.
The antifibrotic effect of the methanol extract from Polygonum aviculare (PA), Artemisia capillaris (AC) and an aqueous solution of biphenyl dimethyl dicarboxylate (DDB) on liver fibrosis were studied. Liver fibrosis was induced by a bile duct ligation and scission (BDL/S) operation, duration of 4 weeks in rats. In BDL/S rats, the levels of aspartate transaminase (AST), alanine transaminase (ALT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), total bilirubin in serum and hydroxyproline content in liver were dramatically increased. The PA treatment in BDL/S rats reduced the serum AST, ALT and ALP levels significantly (p<0.01). Liver hydroxyproline content in PA treated BDL/S group was also reduced to 40% that of BDL/S control group (p<0.01). The morphological characteristics of fibrotic liver, which appeared in BDL/S control group were improved in the PA treated BDL/S group. (Nan et al, 2000).
Mexican Sanguinaria extract of Polygonum aviculare against gingivitis, was assessed in 60 male dentistry students between the ages of 18 and 25 years. Over a period of 2 weeks, these students used the Sanguinaria extract (1mg/ml) in oral rinse twice daily as a unique oral health measurement (no tooth-brushing was allowed). The O'Leary Plaque Index and the Loe and Silness Gingivitis Index were recorded at baseline (day 0) in all the subjects. Also, a complete prophylaxis was performed after both indices were obtained. The antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects of the extract were evaluated on days 7, 11 and 14. The results showed that the Mexican Sanguinaria extract in oral rinse significantly decreased gingivitis from day 0 to day 14 (P greater less 0.05). In contrast, a significant -increase in dental plaque was observed from day 0 to day 14 (P more less 0.05). Mexican Sanguinaria extract in oral rinse has been suggested to be employed supportively in the therapy of gingivitis. (Begne et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-05-2402.
