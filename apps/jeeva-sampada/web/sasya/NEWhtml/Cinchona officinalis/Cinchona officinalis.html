DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 7.5-14.5 cm long, acute or shortly acuminate, petiolate; petiole 2.5-3.8 cm long, secondary nerves 8-10 pairs, hairy pits in their axils. 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers red in short terminal and axillary corymbo form compound cymes. Corolla tube 1.3-1.7 cm long, densely silky with white appressed hairs, slightly pentagonal. Capsules ovoid-oblong, 1.5-2.0 cm long.
Inflorescence: Terminal panicles; Flower: Pentamerous, scented,  ; Calyx:  Pubescent; Corolla: Tubular, pubescent outside ;Androecium: Anthers linear, filaments free; Gynoecium: Ovary 2-celled, style slender with 2-short ovules ; Fruit: Capsule 2-seeded ; Seed: Numerous, peltate. Flowering & Fruiting : July- December. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and stem cuttings.

Soil type: Plant prefers well drained rich loamy soil.

Nutrient requirements: Plant requires nitrogenous fertilizers for its proper growth.

Water management:    It requires good irrigation facities.

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is valued for its bark so it is harvested after 12 years.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The bark is the sources of quinine.


Chemistry:
A rapid, accurate and inexpensive quantity HPTLC/ densitometry procedure was developed for the simultaneous determination of the 4 major alkaloids found in the bark of Cinchona species: quinine, quinidine, cinchoninc and chinchonidine. (Dayrit et al, 1994). C.A. 1995, 123, 51382s.

Avicularin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

A method for the estimation of quinine (Qn), cinchonine (Cn), and cinchonidine (Cnd) and a new method based on the fluorescence enhancement and detection and quantification of quiinidine (Qnd) from Cinchona stem bark. And its formulations, using HPTLC has been reported. (Ravishankara et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-06-3490.
