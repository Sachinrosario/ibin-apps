Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
R. commmunis is believed to be a native of tropical Africa and most probably originated in Ethopia. Its occurrence in the scrubby jungles of the outer Himalayas in truly wild state, together with the undoubted antiquity of its usage as drug, as revealed in Sanskrit literature, are held to point to its being a native of India as well as of Africa. It is said to be under cultivation from ancient times in both these areas. Castor is a tall glabrous annual or perennial shrub or a small tree. The main castor-growing countries are Brazil, India, USSR and Argentina. India ranks first in area (44,8000 hectares) but second in production (140,000 tonnes), Brazil being the, first. Castor is distributed throughout India. Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Orissa and Tamil nadu account for over 90% of the area and also production.

Castor is said to be essentially a crop of the tropics, though it is cultivated in the sub-tropics to some extent. It can be grown even at high altitudes up to 2,000 m. and, in fact, perennial types are said to no best at altitudes above 850 m. Castor can withstand dry arid climates, as also heavy rains and floods, it is, however, susceptible to damage by frost (10). The crop comes up well on all types of soils, but it is generally grown on red sandy loams in peninsular India and on light alluvial soils in the northern states. Inferior soils, not fit for valuable commercial and food crops, are often used for growing castor. (1,29)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Stems green or reddish, erect, circular in section, partially hollow, smooth, glabrous, with good branching. The stem is marked by a number of well-defined nodes, from each of which a leaf arises. The lower internodes are shorter and their length increases with the height. A single stem is first produced which terminates in an inflorescence at 6th-10th node in dwarf early cultivars, at 8th-16th node in later-maturing cultivars, and at 40th or more nodes in tall and wild plants Leaves are spirally arranged with a phyllotaxis of 2/5, peltate, stipules 1-3 cm long, united, sheathing bud, deciduous, petiole pale green or reddish, round, 8-50 cm long, with 2 nectiferous glands at junction to lamina, 2 glands on either side at base and 1 or more glands on upper surface towards base, lamina orbicular, 10-75 cm in diameter, palmately 5-11 partite for about half length, segments ovate or lanceolate, acuminate, serrate, dark green or reddish above, paler green beneath (11) . Leaves are carried on long stout petioles. Petioles are 15-30 cm long. The inflorescence are borne terminally on the main and lateral branches (3).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers are large, borne in terminal, many-flowered panicles, 10-40 cm long, unisexual, monoecious, apetalous, with male flowers at the base and female flowers on the top 30-50 per cent of inflorescence, some commercial hybrids entirely female. Male flowers in 3-16 flowered cymes; pedicels 0,5-1.5 cm long; sepals 3-5, ovate, spreading, green, 5-7 mm long; petals absent; stamens numerous, 5-10 mm long, with much branched filaments, each branch ending in a small, spherical, pale yellow anther. Female flowers in 1-7 flowered cymes; pedicels 4-5 mm long, sepals 3-5, green, 3-5 mm long, connate and bursting irregularly, quickly falling; petals absent, ovary superior, 3-celled, 1 ovule per cell, ovary wall covered with fleshy soft green spines each terminating in a transparent bristle which breaks off as fruit develops, style very short; stigmas 3, fleshy, red, papillate, long persistent (11). Inflorescence in panicle. Stamens many, filaments much branched, anthers are sub globose, ovary is superior 3 carpelled, 3 celled and each cell having a single ovule. Style short, spreading large and often bifid at the tip. The fruit is a roundish glaucous capsule, with three projecting sides covered with tough spines or smooth, three-loculed and three-seeded. (3). Fruits have elongated pedicel, 3 1obed, 1.5-2,5 cm in diameter, usually spiney, unripe flowers green, but red in some cultivars, turning brown on ripening, woody remains of pistil at apex. Dehisce by woody pericarp splitting along dorsal suture, in wild plants and some cultivars splitting is violent ejecting seeds, most modern cultivors are indehiscent and seed is held in capsule for several weeks.

Breeding System :
Castor is an often-cross pollinated crop. Anthesis takes place between 4 a.m. to 8 a.m. The excertion of stigmatic lobes occur at all hours, and they attain receptivity at the time of opening of pistilate flowers. Stigmas remain receptive for 24-36 hours, the pollen grain remain viable for 2-3 days. After fertilization it takes 35-50 days for development of capsules and seeds (3).

Phenology :
Castor is sown mostly in the month of June-July as rainfed annual crop and to a limited extent in Aug-Sep. The taller varieties will bear ripe seed 7 months after sowing, while for shorter varieties the growth period is 4-5 months and castor is indeterminate in flowering (2).

Pollinators:
Castor is an often-cross pollinated crop. Cross-pollination by wind varies from 5 to 36 per cent depending on climatic conditions. It is usually stated that castor is mainly wind pollinated. At the same time as the flowers are opening the glands on the young leaves on the sympodial branches below the inflorescence exude copious nectar, so it seems probable that insects play some part in pollination (11).

Seed Biology :
Seeds are roughly oval in shape, somewhat flattened and with a marked caruncle, vary in colour according to the varieties from pale buff to almost black (2). Seeds contained one per locule in a trilocular fruit. Seeds are albuminous, anatropous, broad, oval, compressed with a marked caruncle and longitudinal raphe. The testa is thin, brittle, varying in colour and mottling. Below the testa is the thin tegmen, covering the whitish oily endosperm containing the embryo. About 8-12 kg of seeds are required for one hectare.

Seedling Germination :
Sound seed will retain their viability for 2-3 years. More even germination may be obtained by pouring boiling water on the seeds and leaving them to soak for 24 hours. Some castor seeds show dormancy, but this may be broken by removing the caruncle and cutting a small hole in the testa. Germination is epigeal and emergence takes 7-10 days, sometimes longer (11).

Cultivation:

The land is repeatedly ploughed in summer, as and when the rains are received, and with the onset of monsoon rains the clods are crushed by working a country plough or harrow. In clay loams, only harrowing is done with a blade-harrow two to three times to bring the soil into proper tilth. Sowing is done mostly in June-July and to a limited extent in August-September. The seed is sown either in the plough furrow or with a seed-drill or by hand dibbling. The seed-rate used for sowing behind the plough is 12 kg per hectare. For hand dibbling, 8 to 10 kg of seed will be sufficient per hectare. Before sowing, castor seed should be treated with Thiram 3 g/kg of seed to prevent the attack of root-rot and Alternaria blight. The spacing adopted generally ranges from 90-120 cm between rows and 45-60 cm between plants in the row (2). One or two weedings and hoeings with bullock-drawn implements are given and sometimes, the plants are earthed up. The fertilizer dose recommended for castor is 40 N, 40 P, 20 K kg/ha. The entire dose of P and K and half of N is applied basally and the remaining half of N about 30 days after sowing, as and when the soil moisture is adequate.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

pests:

1. Castor semilooper (Achoe janata)

Symptoms: The caterpillars defoliate the plants and in the case of severe attack in the early stage, the loss is great.

Control measures: Collection and destruction of infested material along with caterpillars. Spraying Endrin 0.5% in 900 litres of water per hectare. Ploughing the field to expose the pupae.


2.Shoot and capsule borer (Dichocrocis punctifaralis)


Symptoms: The insects bores into seed capsule or inflorescence stalk. External indication of the presence of this insect are the existence of trashy matter on the bored shoots and webbed condition of seed capsule, which are covered with brown fecal matter.

Control measures: Spray Metacid combi 0.05% or Endrin at 0.05%.


3. Jassids (Empoasca formosana)


Symptoms: Yellowing along the veins, spotting and yellowing in patches, drying from edges and curling, resulting in the shedding of leaves.

Control measures: Spray Endrin, Malathion, and Endosulfan all at 0.05% in 900 litres of water.


4. Gall-midge (Asphondylia ricini)

Symptoms: The maggots attack and feed on flowers, causing malformed gall like buds, owing to which capsules remain undeveloped, dry up and fall.

Control measures: Spray Matacid combi 0.95% or Endrin 0.05% during flowering (1).


Diseases:

1. Bacterial blight (Phytopthera parasitica)

Symptoms: The blight appears only in seedling stage and rarely on older plants. Dull green round patches appear on cotyledonary leaves, leaves hang down and rot finally killing the seedlings.

Control measures: Spray Dithane Z-78 @ 2 g/lit of water.

2. Alternaria blight (Alternaria ricini)

Symptoms: At seedling stage dirty, brown irregular patches on cotyledonary leaves appear, leading to the death of seedling. The disease also attacks panicle leading to the improper seed setting.

Control measures: Spray Dithane Z-78 @ 2 g/lit of water.

3. Leaf spot (Cercospora ricinella)

Symptoms: Minute black spots, surrounded by pale green areas appear on leaves later on the colour of the spots turns pale brown and then greyish white, with deep brown margins, with tiny dot like black structures at the centre of these spots.

Control measures: Spray Copper oxychloride @ 1g/lit of water.

4. Powdery mildew (Leveillula taurica)

Symptoms: Whitish powdery growth on leaves, which become discoloured and turn brown, leading to necrosis.

Control measures: Spray Coasan or Thiovit @ 2 g/lit of water.


5. Rust (Melampsora ricini)

Symptoms: Rust pastules, formed in small circles, chiefly on the lower surface of leaves, containing orange-yellow spore masses covered by epidermis.

Control measures: Spray Coasan or Thiovit @ 2 g/lit of water (1, 16).

Economic Potential and Uses

Castor wax: It is mainly used for coatings and greases where resistance to moisture, oils and other petrochemical products is required. Castor wax is used primarily in the formulation of cosmetics. It is generally grown for its oil-yielding seeds. (22)

Castor seed oil: The oil content of the seeds varies from 35-58 per cent in different varieties, the average being about 47 per cent (1). Castor oil is being used widely for various purposes. Castor oil finds applications in manufacture of nylon, plasticisers, soaps, lubricants in high-speed engines and aeroplanes, rubber treatment, polishes, varnishes, linoleum, serves as a replacement for mineral oil and also used as a raw material in the manufacture of various chemicals e.g. sebacic acid and undecylenic acid. It is also used for medicinal and lighting purposes. Castor oil is used in the production of Heveacrumb rubbers (10,13).

Harvesting Practices
The improved varieties cultivated at present are early maturing and take about 150 to 180 days. When one or two capsules in a bunch show signs of drying, the whole cluster is generally removed and stacked, covered and weighed in a corner of the field or in a pit. The harvesting of unripe capsules in this manner has an adverse effect on the oil content of the seed. It is preferable to collect the fruits, as and when they ripen. When the whole crop is gathered, it is dried in the sun for a few days and the threshing is done either by beating the dried capsules with a stick or by treading them under the feet of bullocks. Winnowing is done in the usual manner (1).

The average yield of rainfed castor varies from 200 to 500 kg per hectare, that of the mixed crop from 100 to 200 kg and of the irrigated crop from 500 to 800 kg.

Produce and Products

Castor seed oil: Oil is used in the manufacture of cosmetics, soaps, paints, varnishes etc

Oilcake: used as manure which is the residue after oil extraction.

Castor Wax: These white hard waxy flakes are vegetable derived and used in hair styling products, lip sticks, lip balms, make-up pencils, deodorants sticks, soaps, etc.  An excellent alternative to beeswax for vegan formulas  A great non-comedogen emollient that also helps stabilize emulsions. Derivatives of castor wax are used as surfactants and plastics additives.

Export Potential

There are no reports of Castor wax exported from India. While, Castor oil is exported in considerable quantities.


EXPORTS OF CASTOR OIL


Since India is the largest producer of castor oil in the world, it exports large quantities to many countries around the world. Brazil and China are India's competitors in castor oil exports. However, during the last few years, due to various reasons, India has emerged as the largest and most consistent exporter of good quality castor oil. In the global castor oil trade, India is a dominating player with 80-90% market share. Major exporters of castor oil are Hindustan Lever Ltd. (HLL) and Jayant Oil Mills (JOM). For HLL, castor oil and its derivatives form a significant part of its agricultural business exports. Several other companies also produce and export castor oil to many countries around the world (4).

Miscellaneous Information

Processing technique for oil extraction:

Pretreatment

  • Seed or bean separated from the spiny outer husk by mechanical methods or by sun drying in the open until the casing splits.

  • Dehusked beans are cleaned by screening.

  • Dehulled from the hard shells by an impact or disc mill decorticator:

  • Hulls and kernels are separated by aspiration.


Extraction

  • Under closely controlled conditions a plant may operate for the production of No.1 grade oil from the entire supply. The kernels are conditioned by cooking at 79OC and 15% moisture, followed by drying to 4% moisture at 990C. The kernels are then pressed in a continuous double screw press, which removes about 90% of the oil content. The press cake contains about 5.5% oil at 10% moisture. The expressed oil is cooled, screened to remove larger particles of cake and filtered in filter presses. The product is No.1 oil. The press cake is either ground and sold as it is or subjected to solvent extraction for recovery of the residual oil content .

  • Cake or oil can be produced from the castor bean by mechanical expression or by a combination of mechanical expression followed by solvent extraction of the press cake.

  • Under usual operating conditions cold pressing of castor bean, followed by bleaching and filtration with activated carbon or earths, yields high quality oil, which is suitable for medicinal purposes.

  • Hydrocarbon solvent extraction of the press cake from No. I oil pressing, yields No.3 oil, which is unsuitable for medicinal purposes (2).


Castor cake, though rich in protein content, is unsuitable for feeding purposes due to toxic components of seed. Solvent extracted cake is reported to be more toxic than expeller cake. Horses when fed on the cake either died or got badly disabled. Fowls tolerated the cake slightly better but tended to lose weight. Various methods have been employed for the detoxification of ricin and destruction or inactivation of the allergenic property of castor cake. These include processes like autoclaving, boiling or cooking with water or with water and lime, and steaming, or treatment of the cake with solutions of salt, ammonium sulphate, sodium sulphate and bisulphate, hydrochloric acid, formaldehyde or dilute alkali. The ricin component was completely detoxified by a mild moist-cooking of the cake with or without the addition of alkali, while moist cooking of the cake or meat with sodium hydroxide or with a mixture of formaldehyde and caustic soda or hydrochloric acid, resulted in 98-100 per cent reduction of the allergens. Dry heating of the cake at 2050 also for destroyed the allergens completely. Castor cake has been rendered suitable for human consumption by a patented process in which it is treated with proteolytic enzymes, autolysed yeast and then with autolysed bacteria (10).
