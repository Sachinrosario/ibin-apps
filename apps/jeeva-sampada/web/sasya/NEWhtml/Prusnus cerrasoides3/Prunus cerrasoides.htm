Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry: Synthesis of padmakastein and its derivatives. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
b-sitosterol behenate, tectochrysin, genistein,leucocyanidin and 4'-glucoside of genkwanin (fig. 1) from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Two new flavanoid glycosides - naringenin-5-O-a-L-rhamnopyranoside and 4'-O-methylliquiritigenin-7-O-a-Lrhamnopyranoside - isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Naringenin-4'-methylether-7-xyloside isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
Isolation of naringenin, apigenin, b-sitosterol, sakuranetin, prunetin and genkwanin from stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.
Chrysophanol, emodin and their 8b-D-glucosides, orientalone, physcion and b-sitosterol glucoside isolated from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
A new flavanone glycoside - puddumin A (fig. 2) isolated from stem sapwood and characterized; genistein and prunetin also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
N-pentacosane, triacontane, n-octacosanol, b-sitosterol and ursolic acid identified in unsaponifiable part of petrol extract of sapwood whereas oleic, palmitic and stearic acids identified insaponifiable part; afzelin, kaempferitrin, naringenin and b-sitosterol-b-D-glucoside isolated from ethanol extract. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
A new flavanone glycoside - puddumin B (fig. 3) isolated and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
b-sitosterol, stigmasterol, ursolic acid, prunetinoside, glucogenkwanin and neosakuranin isolated from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany: 

Medicine for bone dislocation, fracture (Bark, Uniyal, 1968); Burns, cuts, wounds (Bark, Shah and Joshi, 1971); Diarrhoea, fever (Bark, Megoneitso, 1983); Hemicrania (Bark, Uniyal, 1968); Venereal disease (Bark, Rao and Jamir, 1982); Veterinary hoof disease (Leaf, Negi et al, 1985); Edible (Fruit, Arora, 1981; Pal, 1984; Negi et al, 1985; Gupta, 1962); Hair oil (Bark, Uniyal, 1968); Rosaries (stone, Mehra et al, 1975)
