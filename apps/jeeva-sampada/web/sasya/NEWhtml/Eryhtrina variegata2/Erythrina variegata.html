REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Dense racemes ; Flower: Large, corel red ; Calyx: Oblique, spathaceous, finally spillting down the base; Corolla:  Very unequal, exserted; Androecium:  Upper stamens free, down near to the base, anthers uniform; Gynoecium: Ovary stalked, many ovuled, style incurved, stigma capitate ; Fruit: Pods torulose, containing 12 seeds ; Seed: Oblong, smooth, red to brown.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting :March-May. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Chemistry:
Docosyl alcohol, b-sitosterol, g-sitosterol, mp. 148°C, d-sitosterol, mp. 147°C, three other substances having mp. 135°, 142°, and 126°C, along with two unidentified products isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Erythraline (fig. 7) and erysovine isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Erysotrine (fig. 8), erysodine, erysopine, erysopitine (fig. 3), erysodienone (fig. 4) and erysonine along with three carboxylated indole-3-alkylamines -hypaphorine, its methyl ester and N,N-dimethyltryptophan methyl ester -isolated from various parts of plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Scoulerine, (+)coreximine, 1-reticuline and erybidine (fig. 2) isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Three new isoflavones - erythrinins A (fig. 1a), B (fig. 5) and C (fig. 6) in addition to osajin, alpinum isoflavone (fig. 1b), oxyresveratol and dihydroxyresveratol isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Erythrartine (fig. 7) isolated from flowers; choline isolated from seds and flowers; absolute configuration of C-11 in erythrinine and erythrascine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

A D-galactose-binding lectin agglutinating human erythrocytes has been purified from the seeds of the Indian coral tree (Erythrina variegata (Linn.) var. orientalis (Linn.) Merrill] by affinity chromatography on acid-treated Sepharose-6B gel. It has a higher reactivity for O-group erythrocytes. The lectin is a glycoprotein having a leucoagglutinating property. (Datta and Basu, 1981)

Synthesis of erysotrine and erythraline. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Erysopine, erysodine and erysovin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Ferulic and caffeic acid, isoharmnetin-3-O-rhamnoglucoside, rutin, quercetin and its 4-O-glucosyl-3-O-rhamnoglucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Synthesis of erythrinin A. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Erysotine, erythratidine, epierythratidine and 11-hydroxyepierythratidine isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

A new cinnamylphenol – eryvariestyrene (fig. 9) isolated from roots and its structure elucidated; warangalone. 5,7,4’-trihydroxy-6,8-diprenylisoflavone, erycristagallin, erythrabyssin II, phaseollin, phaseollidin and isobavachin also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of a new isoquinoline alkaloid – isococcolinine (fig. 11) isolated from flowers and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Another isoquinoline alkaloid – erythritol (fig. 10) isolated from flowers and itsstructure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Flowers afforded 3b-acetoxy-B-norcholest-5-ene, capric acid, docosanoic acid methyl ester, 29-norcycloartenol and b-sitosterol arachidate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Two new isoflavonoids, eryvarin A (1) and eryvarin B (2) were isolated from the wood of Erythrina variegata and their structures were elucidated on the basis of spectroscopic evidence. (Tanaka et al, 2000).

Three new isoflavonoids, eryvarins C-E, together with fourteen known compounds were isolated from the roots of Erythrina variegata (Leguminosae) and their structures were elucidated on the basis of spectroscopic evidence. (Tanaka, 2001).

Three isolectins were isolated from seeds of Erythrina variegata (Linn.) var. Orientalis by ion-exchange chromatography, followed by affinity chromatography on lactose-Sepharose 4B and acid-treated Sepharose 4B columns. The purified isolectins (EVLI, EVLII and EVLIII) are all specific for galactopyranosides and N-acetylgalactosamine, and their affinities for simple sugars are EVLIII greater than EVLII greater than EVLI. EVLI and EVLIII are homodimers made up of an A-subunit of molecular mass 36,000 and a B-subunit of molecular mass 33,000, whereas EVLII is a heterodimer composed of the A- and B-subunits. (Yamasaki, 1992).

Acetone extract of the Erythrina variegata flowers has revealed the presence of stigmasterol, cycloartenol and erysotramidine and new isoquinolines were isolated and characterized as erythrosotidineone and erythromotidienone. (Sharma, and Chawala, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9904-2709.

Three inhibitors of Na+/H+ exchange system erythrene B, euchrenone b 10and 1,3,5-trihydroxy-4(3-methylbut-2-enyl)-xanthen-9-one were isolated from methanol extract of Erythrina variegata bark. (Kobayashi et al, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9802-0838.

Three flavanoids phospholipase A2 (PLA2) inhibitors, 4’-hydroxy-3’,5’-diprenylisoflavanone (abyssinone-V), 3,9-dihydroxy-2,10-pterocarp-6a-en and 4’-hydroxy-6,3’,5’-triprenyl isoflavanone were isolated from a MeOH extract of Erythrina variegata bark. (Hegde et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9801-0167.

Cytotoxicity induced by Erythrina variegata serine proteinase inhibitorsin tumor hematopoietic stem cell lines was studied. (Ohba et al, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9902-1028.

Isolation of known prenylated isoflavone along with some known phytosteroids and triterpenoids from the stem bark of Erythrina variegata is described. (Huang and Yen, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 356990m.

Two prenylated isoflavone erythrivarones A and B were isolated from the stem bark along with known compounds alpinumisoflavone. Structure of erythrivaron A and B were characterized as dihydroalpinumisoflavone and 4’-hydroxy (6’’, 6’’ – dimethyldihydropyrano (2’’, 3’’ : 5, 6)) - (6’’’, 6’’’ -  dimethyldihydropyrano (2’’’,3’’’:7,8)) isoflavone respectively. (Huang and Yen, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 142021b.


Pharmacology:

Total alkaloidal fraction from bark caused smooth muscle relaxation of isolated rabbit and inhibited spontaneous rhythmic contraction of isolated rat uterus in concentration 0.5-2.0 mg/ml. These alkaloids showed CNS depressant and anticonvulsant actions and clinically useful as muscle relaxants and as sedatives. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Total alkaIoid fraction from bark showed neuromuscular blocking, smooth muscle relaxant, CNS depressant and anticonvulsant effects. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Through bioassay-guided separation of the methanol extracts of Euphorbia variegata the Na+/H+ exchange system, erythrinin B (2), euchrenone b10 (3), and 1,3,5-trihydroxy-4-(3-methylbut-2-enyl)xanthen-9-one (4), were isolated from the bark (Kobayashi et al, 1997).

Inhibitory potency of Erythrina variegata proteinase inhibitors toward serine proteinases in the blood coagulation and fibrinolytic systems was studied. (Nakagaki et al, 1996)

Novel anti-inflammatory compounds have been isolated from and Erythrina variegata, (Cox, 1993)


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for anthelmintic (Bark, Nautiyal, 1981); cold (Bark, Jain and De, 1966); Convulsions (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarfder, 1970); Cough (Leaf, Bark, Jain and De, 1966); Eye complaints (Bark, Jain and De, 1966); fever (Bark, Nautiyal, 1981); Menorrhagia, paralysis, pimples (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarfder, 1970); Rheumatism, skin disease (Bark, Nautiyal, 1981); Snake bite (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarfder, 1970); Ulcers (Leaf, Apparantham and Chelladurai, 1986); Edible vegetable (Fruit, Nagar, 1985); Beads (Seed, Mehra et al, 1975); symbolic essential in cremation of Garos (Wood, Hajra, 1987).
