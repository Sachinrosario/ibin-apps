Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
 A medium or large-sized deciduous tree, up to 100 ft. high and 11. ft. in girth, found throughout the sub-Himalayan tract, from Chamba to Bhutan, up to an elevation of 6,500 ft. and in North Bengal, Assam and Khasi and Garo hills.
The tree is found usually in moist shady places, often bordering streams. In North Bengal, it grows best above 2,000 ft., in jhoras and valleys with good soil. In China and Japan A large genus of ornamental herbs and shrubs, sometimes epiphytic, distributed in tropical and sub-tropical Asia and Australia. About 32 species occur in India

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 The bark of youngtrees is smooth, pale ashy or whitish ; that of old trees pale brown, rough, with deep and regular vertical fissures ; leaves 4-6 in. long and 2-3 in. broad. ovate, acuminate, glabrascent above and hairy on nerves beneath . Flowers greenish white, in pedun- culate, many-flowered axillary or terminal cymes ; fruits 0.3 in. diam., globose, succulent, obscurely 3-lobed, becoming embedded when ripe in thefleshy, swollen peduncle.

Other Uses

1.It is extensively cultivated for its ornamental foliage and edible fleshy peduncles. In Russia , it has been found to thrive well under humid sub-tropical conditions.

2.The tree is much esteemed in China and Japan for its edible peduncles, which are sweet and fleshy and taste like ripe pears. The sweetness is due to the presence of glucose (11.14%), fructose (4.74%), and sucrose (12.59%). The fruit extract contains potassium nitrate and potassium malate and is strongly diuretic.

3.In China , dried peduncles are used medicinally.

4.Seeds are also used for relieving intoxication due to wine.

5.The wood of the tree is dull white, light and porous. It is not used, except as fuel. In the lower hill forests of Assam and North Bengal , where it occurs in good proportion, it is considered to be a fair timber.
