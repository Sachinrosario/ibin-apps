DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Climbers, stout; tubers solitary, globose to pyriform with long roots produced above; bulbils numerous, irregular in shape, 2.5 cm or more across, brown, warted. Leaves alternate, broadly ovate, 5-16 x 3.5-13.5 cm, apex acuminate, base more or less deeply cordate, 7-11 nerved.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in axillary, pendulous spikes; male spikes 6.5-10.0 cm long and female spikes 10-25 cm long. Capsules oblong, 1.7-2.0 x 0.5 cm, reflexed, wings rounded at both ends. Seeds winged at base, brown. It flowers between July and March.
CIMAP:
Male Flower: Variable in size, green or purplish, in short slender spikes closed together or scattered on the branches; perianth sessile, segments subvalvate, fleshy, lanceolate, inner small and narrower; stamens 6, filaments short, anther minute, didynamous, pistillode 3-lobed; Female flower: Axillary, solitary or fascicled pendulous spikes; perianth sessile, segments subvalvate, fleshy, lanceolate; bracts below the ovary minute, style short conical, stigma 3, very short recurved   ; Fruit: Capsule quadrately oblong, rather broader upwards ; Seed: Seeds winged at lower end. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : August- October. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through bulbils.

Soil type: Plant thrives best in deep sandy loam with good drainage.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to application of manure.

Water management:   Plant is irrigated 8-10 times during growing periods.                               

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is harvested when the tubers become mature.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Tuber has a sharp bitter taste and used as expectorant, astringent to the bowels and in asthma, bronchitis, pain in the abdomen (Ayurveda). Tuber is tonic, aphrodisiac, stomachic, anthelmintic; used in dyspepsia, urinary discharges, leucoderma, bronchitis, piles, tumors and strangury.


Chemistry:
Three new furanoid norditerpenes – diosbulbins A, m.p. 265°C, B (fig. 1), m.p. 285°C and C, m.p. 247°C – from root tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Diosbulbin isolated from bulb. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Two new compounds – 4-hydroxy-2-(trans 3’,7’-dimethylocta-2’,6’-dienyl)-6-methoxy - acetophenone (fig. 2) and 4, 6 – dihydroxy – 2 – O - (4’ -hydroxybutyl) acetophenone (fig. 3) were isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Three compounds were obtained and elucidated as 3,7-dimethoxy-5,4'-dihydroxyflavone; 3,7-dimethoxy-5,3',4'-trihydroxyflavone and emodin. (Li et al, 2000)

Pharmacology:
The ethyl acetate soluble fraction of 75% ethanol extract of the rhizomes of Dioscorea bulbifera L. showed an inhibitory effect against the tumor promotion of JB6 (Cl 22 and Cl 41) cells induced by a promoter, 12-O-tetradecanoylphorbol-13-acetate (TPA). Further investigation on the constituents of the EtOAc fraction from the rhizomes revealed the chemical structure to be kaempferol-3,5-dimethyl ether (1), caryatin (2), (+)-catechin (3), myricetin (4), quercetin-3-O-galactopyranoside (5), myricetin-3-O-galactopyranoside (6), myricetin-3-O-glucopyranoside (7) and diosbulbin B (8). Constituent antitumor-promoting activities were also examined in the same way. Compounds 1-7, characterized as flavonoids with the two hydroxyl groups at C-7 and C-4', showed the most potent inhibitory effect, but there seemed to be differences in the inhibitory effect between flavonol aglycones and flavonol glycosides. (Gao et al, 2002)
