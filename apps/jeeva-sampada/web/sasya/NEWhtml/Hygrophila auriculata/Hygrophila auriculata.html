HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual or perennial herbs, erect, up to 1 m high; stem stout, thickened at nodes and axillary whorls of 1.5-3.0 cm long, hispid, spines yellow. It is found through out India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves in whorls of 6 at a node, oblong or linear-lanceolate, 3.5-18.5 x 1.0-2.5 cm, acute at apex, cuneate at base, minutely dentate at margin, scabrous, subsessile. Flowers in dense clusters at leaf axils, usually 8 in each whorls, showy; bracts 2-seriate, linear-lanceolate, up to 2.5 cm long, ciliate; bracteoles linear-lanceolate, narrow, smaller. Calyx 4-partite; outer segments 1.5-2.0 cm long, broader than other 3, all segments linear-lanceolate, hairy. Corolla blue-purple, glabrous; tube c 1.2 cm long, abruptly widened; lips subequal, 1.6 cm long. Capsules linear-oblong, c 0.8 cm long, pointed, glabrous, 4-8 seeded. Seeds orbicular, c 0.3 cm across.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
The plant is referred for the treatment of jaundice, dropsy and urinogenital diseases. Roots are diuretic. Seeds are given in gonorrhoea. Mixed with milk and sugar it is sometimes prescribed in spermatorrhoea.


Chemistry:
Decoction of whole plant as also the water extract of its ash showed diuretic actions in rats, which was attributed to presence of potassium salts in high concentration. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Xylose and uronic acids from oil of seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Stigmaterol and straight-chain hydrocarbons from lipid extract of plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Detection of palmitic (7.2), stearic (0.8), oleic (11.9) and linoleic (80.1%) acids in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Apigenin-7-O-glucuronide and apigenin-7-O-glucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Lupeol, betulin and stigmasterol isolated from plant collected in Saharanpur; betulin found absent in aerial parts and stigmasterol in roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for aphrodisiac (Stem, Jaina, 1965; Jainb, 1965); Body pain (Leaf, Chaudhuri et al, 1975); Cancer (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Diuretic (Part not mentioned, Gupta, 1981); Dropsy (Root, Singh and Pandey, 1980; Part not mentioned, Gupta, 1981); Flatulence (Whole plant, Jaina, 1965); Impotence (Seed, Saxena and Vyas, 1983, Saxena and Vyas, 1986); Jaundice (Leaf, Chaudhuri et al, 1975; Root, Singh and Pandey, 1980); Malaria (Whole plant, Saxena and Vyas, 1983); Rheumatism (Root, Sharma and Malhotra, 1987; Singh and Pandey, 1980); Swelling in face, body (Whole plant, Jaina, 1965; Leaf, Chaudhuri et al, 1975); Tonic (Seed, Jaina, 1965; Jainb, 1965); Tubercular fistula (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Edible as vegetable (Leaf, Arora, 1987; Stem, Chaudhuri et al, 1975, Leaf + Twig, Chaudhuri et al, 1985).
