HABIT & HABITAT:
A medium sized, deciduous tree, found throughout India, it is found in Sub Himalayan tracts from the Indus eastwards and throughout the dry forests of India, ascending to 1300 m.; cultivated throughout the plains.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A medium sized, deciduous tree, found throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 1,300 m. in the Himalayas.  Dark grey with longitudinal cracks, pale pink inside. Leaves: Broader than deep, rigidly sub-coriaceous, deeply cordate; flowers variously coloured in few-flowered, lateral sessile or short peduncled corymbs; pods long hard, flat, glabrous dehiscent, 10-15 seeded.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Panicle chyme ; Flower: Bracteate, bracteolate, pedicillate, complete, zygomorphic, hermaphrodite, pentamerous, hypogynous and cyclic ; Calyx:  Sepals 5, gamosepalous, valvate, odd sepals anterior, margins membranous; Corolla:  Petals 5, polypetalous, ascending imbricate, pink coloured with prominent red veins ;Androecium: Stamens 5, polyandrous, filaments long, dithecous, versatile, introrse, bent in bud conditions; Gynoecium: Mono-carpellary, ovary superior, unilocular, ovules many, placentation marginal, curved and raised on small gynophore, style short, stigma simple and sticky  ; Fruit: Pods flat, frequently variegated with reddich brown streaks ; Seed: albuminous. Flowering & Fruiting :.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Bark alternative tonic, antileprotic, antirheumatic in skin diseases ulcers, scrofula; bud- antidysentence in piles; roots- carminative, antitote for snake bite in dyspepsia; Bark and bud- astringent, vermifuge.
Uses:
In Ayurveda system of medicine bark is used as laxative, astringent, appetiser, cooling and tonic. It is uesful in ulcer, tuberculous gland, leprosy, leucoderma, cough and asthma The flowers are useful in blood diseases, bronchitis, vaginal discharges and headache. In Unani system of medicine bark is astringent and used as tonic. It is useful in leucoderma, leprosy, dysmenorrhoea, asthma and ulcers. Buds are used in piles, cough and liver complaints.
Chemistry:
NBPGR:
Flowers contain flavonoids kaempferol 3-galactoside and kaempferol –3- shamnoglucoside, stem-bark yields hentriacontane, octacosanol and stigmasterol stem yields b-sitosterol, lupeol and a flavanone glycoside 5-7 dimethoxy flavorone 4’-0-a-L-shamnopyranogyl –b-D-glucopyranoside.

NBRI:
Quercitroside, isoquercitroside and rutoside isolated from plant; myricetol glycosides in seeds and kaempferol glycosides in flowers also present. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

5,7-dihydroxyflavanone-4’-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig. 1) isolated from stem. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

b-sitosterol, lupeol and 5,7-dimethoxyflavanone-4’-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-b-D-glucopyranoside isolated from stem. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Trypsin inhibitors of two varitiesof Bauhinia verigata seeds have been isolated and characterized. Bauhinia verigata candida trypsin inhibitor and Bauhinia verigata lilac trypsin ihibitor are proteins with Mr of about 20,000 without free sulfhydryl groups. Amino acid analysis shows a high content of aspartic acid, glutamic acid, serine, and glycine and low content of histadine, tyrosine, methionine, and lysine in both inhibitors. (Di Ciero, et al, 1998).

Bauhinia verigata was analysed for proximate and fibre composition, nitrogen solubility, fibre bound nitrogen, total phenolics, hydrolysable tannins and condensed tannins. (Sharma et al, 2000). MAPA 2000, 04, 2284.

Lectin is present in the seed and young seedling of B. variegata. (Peng and Chen, 2000). C.A. 2001, 135, 16736m.

A new flavone glycoside m.f. C(30) H(36)O(15) m.p. 252-253°C, [M]+ 636 (EIMS) was isolated from the acetone soluble fraction of the concentrated 95% ethanolic extract of the seeds of Bauhinia verigata. It was identified as 5-hydroxy 7,3’,4’,5’-tetra-methoxyflavone 5-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à2)-a-L-rhamnopyranoside (1) by various colour reactions, chemical degraditions and spectral techniques. (Yadav and Reddy, 2001).


Pharmacology:
Methanolic extracts of the leave of Bauhinia verigata were tested for anti microbial activity. Antifungal activity was shown against Aspergillus fumigatus, A. niger. The activity maxima was displaced by A. fumigatus, Bacillus anthracis, Streptococcus agalacties. (Sharma and saxena, 1996).
