Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A large handsome tree of subtropical and tropical climate, in west Himalayan region. Found in deciduous forests throughout the greater part of India, but not in arid regions. It occurs most frequently in moist vallyes.

It is a light demander, but can withstand slight shade when young. It is decidedly frost sensitive, leaves being usually affected more readily than those of almost any of its associates. It is somewhat drought hardy, but it does not occur in dry localities. Grows on variety of soils, best on deep sandy loam with good moisture supply and good drainage.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

A large handsome tree. Stems straight, frequently buttressed when large. Leaves collected about the extremities of the branches, alternate, coriaceous, broadly elliptic or elliptic-obovate, rounded or rarely subacute or shortly acuminate at the apex, both surfaces puberulous when young, glabrous and reticulate when old, the margins entire, pellucid, base narrowed. Main nerves 6-8 pairs, spreading, prominent, the midrib prominent on both the sides. Petioles without glands at the apex.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers pale greenish-yellow, with an offensive odor, in axillary slender spikes longer than the petioles but shorter than the leaves, those in the upper part of the spike male, very shortly pedicelled, those in the lower part hermaphrodite, sessile. Calyx pubescent outside, densely villous within, the teeth broadly triangular, acute. Drupe suddenly narrowed into a very short stalk, clothed with minute pale tomentum, obscurely angled when dried.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowering during March - May when tree bears new leaves. Old leaves fall in November. Fruits ripen during November - February.

Floral and seed biology:


Seed Germination & Propagation: Proapgated through seeds. Best time for plantation is July.

Natural regeneration is through seeds. Healthy seeds have high germinative capacity, germinate easily and quickly. Seedlings thrive under shade for first one or two years.

For artificial regeneration seed sowing, transplanting the nursery-raised entire seedling of one year growth is practised. Stump plants are not satisfactory. Seeds are sown in nursery from March to May. The two to four-month-old seedlings are ready for transplantation.
Stump planting is not satisfactory. However, 12-15 month-old-plant gives satisfactory stump plantation.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Cercospora terminaliae Syd.

Trametes lactinae Berk.

Tree is not subject to any serious pests, although many insects have been recorded on it.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used for fuel and for making charcoal. Calorifi value: sapwood - 4,972 cal, 8950 B.t.u.

Wood: Wood is yellowish grey with no separation of heartwood. It is fairly straight-grained, sometimes curly-grained in the radial plane, very coarse-structured and moderately heavy to heavy (sp. gr. 0.60-0.77; wt. 593-769 kg./ cu.m.) hard and strong. The timber is not durable in exposed situations as it is non-restant against wood-rotting fungi. It shows 4-12 rings per 2.5 cm of radius, giving a mean annual girth increment of 1.3-4.0 cm.

Suitable for rough shafts and in coal mines as pit props, for house building, chiefly as rafters, and boards and packing cases. Also used for ploughs, sieves, plywood and tea chests and the pulp for paper. It is used for dugouts and side planks of boats and small crafts and cataramans as it is fairly durable under water. It is also useful for grain-measurers, ploughs, turning and cooper's work and coffee boxes. It is suitable for the manufacture of plywood for general purposes and for tea-chests.

The wood in mixture with other hardwoods of Chandrapur district, Maharashtra, has been found to be suitable for the manufacture of chemical pulps for writing-, printing- and wrapping-papers. Tree yields good timber.

Other uses:

Belliric myrobalan - Fruit one of those exported from India under the name of Myrobalan and used for dyeing. An ingredient of "Triphala" – ayurvedic medicine. Fruits are also canned. Ripe fruits eaten by animals. From seed protein adhesive can be developed.

Soap - the soap made from bellirica oil is reported to be pure white instead of the conventional pale yellow, and can be employed in the preparation of toilet-soaps of attractive appearance.

Leaves - Leaves used as feed for tasar silk worms. Also used as fodder for livestock in Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Himachal and Uttar Pradesh. Leaves used for tannin.

Medicinal - Kernels, ripe fruits, pulp is medicinally useful.

Oil - Oil extracted from fruit is considered good application for the hair. In some parts of Madhya Pradesh, it is used by poor classes as a substitute for ghee. Also used medicinally.

Fruit - Fruit is a source of tannin. It is also a source of black dye and ink.
