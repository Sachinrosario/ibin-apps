Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

large deciduous tree with straight cylindrical bole. Frost sensitive. Saplings stand jungle fires to a remarkable degree.

D. pentagyna is found almost throughout India. It occurs along the foothills of the Himalayas from Oudh eastwards in Nepal, Bihar, Bengal and Assam and extends southwards to Chota Nagpur, Madhya Pradesh, southern and western India and Andaman Islands.

It is found in regions with min. temp., 320 – 600 F., max. shade temp., 960 – 1150 F., and annual rainfall, 30-180 in. where the annual rainfall is less than 45 in. the growth is stunted. It is common in the sal forests of Bengal and Assam. It is confined mostly to low ground in Chota Nagpur but occasionally ascends up to 2,000 ft. It is common in the moist deciduous forests of south India.

The tree is a light demander and reproduces freely in burnt savanahs often forming pure patches.

Saplings stand jungle fires to a remarkable degree. It is sensitive to frost.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Middle sized deciduous tree, bark dark gray or black, exfoliating in rectangular scales; young part and inflorescence clothed with gray or tawny tomentum. Leaves alternate or subopposite, coriaceous, elliptic or elliptic-oblong, obtuse or subacute, softly tawny-tomentose on both sides when young, glabrous above and pubescent beneath when mature, base usually acute, rarely rounded; main nerves 6-10 pairs with reticulate veins between.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers dioecious, in cymes, which are axillary, or along the old branches, or sometimes (the female often) reduced to a single flower.

Male flowers: 4-6 merous, 3-12 together, in tawny tomentose panicled drooping cymes longer than the petioles. Calyx campanulate, hairy on both sides, lobes 4-6 shorter than the tube, subacute. Corolla more or less tubular, about twice as long as the calyx, pubescent outside, glabrous inside, lobes 4-6, broadly ovate, acute. Stamens 12-16 (in pairs when 16); anthers linear-oblong, acuminate.

Female flowers: rather longer than the male, solitary, subsessile, 4-5 merous. Calyx hairy on both sides, 4-5-gonal. Corolla as in the male. Staminodes 8-10. Ovary densely hairy, 4-8-celled; ovule solitary in each cell; styles 2, bifid.

Fruit yellow when ripe, ovoid or globose, fruiting calyx thickly coriaceous, nearly flat, the lobes undulate, often with reflexed with margins. Seeds 2-8, compressed, oblong, testa rugose, shining, albumen ruminate.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Under natural conditions seeds germinate in the rainy season and seedling reproduction is often plentiful. Observations on artificial reproduction show that transplantation of seedling raised in nurseries is not always satisfactory owing to the large size of the taproot. It is best to raise seedlings in long narrow baskets during the second rains. Direct sowing in lines is preferable to transplanting, and lines should be weeded during the first 2 or 3 years. Propagation by this method, along with field crops, has been carried out in the Amraoti Forest Division in Berar. The rate of growth is slow; coppice growth is also slow.

D. melanoxylon grows mixed with other valuable species like teak and no special silvicultural treatment is prescribed for it.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Boring insects – Plocaederus ferrugineus Linn., Stromatium barbatum F and Xyleborus noxius Samps. – and defoliators – Hypocala rostrata F. and Lamida carbonifera Mevr. – are found on this species. The leaves of young plants are frequently damaged by the scale insect, Psylla obsoleta Buckton. Basidiomycte fungi – Daedalea flavida Lev., Lenzites repanda (Mont.) Fr., Stereum lobatum Fr. and Thelephora sp. have been recorded.

Cercospora kaki Ellis & Everhart – leaf spot in Banglore and Nandi Hill (Karnataka)

Aecidium rhytismoideum Berk. & Br. – leaf spot in Sirsi (Karnataka)

Aecidium miliare Berk. & Br. – leaf spot from Chanda (Maharashtra)

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:Good fuel wood. Calorific value: sapwood – 4,957 cal, 8,923 Btu; heartwood – 5,030 cal, 9,055 Btu.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood. Sapwood light roseate grey aging to light roseate brown. Heartwood small, black, often streaked with purple or brown bands. Somewhat lustrous with smooth feel, heavy. Wood hard to very hard, heavy to very heavy, average weight 835 kg/m3 (52 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content. Specific gravity 0.79 – 0.87; wt. 51-56 lb./c ft. straight or curly grained in radial plane, medium fine and even textured. Growth rings inconspicuous or absent.

A diffuse porous wood. Growth rings usually indistinct. Pores small indistinct or barely visible to the eye, distinct under the lens, moderately numerous, mostly arranged in short or long radial multiples of 2-6, occasionally more; vessel lines indistinct on longitudinal surface. Soft tissues usually indistinct to eye, but distinct under the lens, as fine, interrupted or broken tangential lines forming a net work with the rays. Rays very fine, not visible to eye, but seen under the lens as fine closely-spaced lines.

Lighter part or the sapwood considered as valuable as the black heartwood, used in the place of true ebony. The sapwood extensively used for posts, rafters, shafts and poles of carts. Suitable for picking arms, billiard cues, plumbers’ tools and agricultural implements. The sapwood is the best Indian timber so far tested for shuttles; it gives about 50% of the life of the imported timbers. The wood also used for mine works and pit props.

The blackwood used for butts of billiard cues, carved walking sticks, brush backs, picture frames, scale beams, combs, toys and snuff boxes.


Harvesting Practices:

Coppices and pollards well, though the growth of pollard shoots is slow. Experience in Madhya Pradesh shows that the coppicing power of the tree is poor after April, the percentage of coppice shoots observed in different months as compared to those appearing in April (100) being: May, 30 and August, nil.

Processing Techniques:

Processing of logs soon after felling, followed by open stacking under cover gives satisfactory results. Sapwood not difficult to season, but black portions liable to end splitting and develop wavy surface cracks.

Seasoning trails have shown that conversion of logs soon after felling, followed by open stacking under cover gives satisfactory results. The sapwood is not difficult to season, but black portions are liable to end splitting and develop wavy surface cracks.

Timber can be sawn without difficulty in green state, but seasoned timber is difficult to saw. It takes good lasting polish but requires initial grain-filling with a thin polish.


Other uses:

Tree of importance for afforestation purpose.

Leaves are highly esteemed for wrapping bidis. Their flavour, flexibility and resistance to decay are properties which are particularly valued for this use. Coppice shoots produce fine wrapper leaves and are plucked when freshly expanded. Leaves from Himachal Pradesh are preferred in trade.

Fruit, very astringent, eaten and much appreciated in hot season.

Leaves are diuretic, carminative, laxative and styptic. Dried flowers are reported to be useful in urinary, skin and blood diseases. The bark is astringent and its decoction is used in diarrhoea and dyspepsia. Dilute extract is used as an astringent lotion for the eyes.
