HABIT & HABITAT
A gigantic perennial climber and one of the most abundant. Climbers with opposite circinate tendrils of Indian climbing Bauhinia spp. It is distributed in the sub-Himalayan region ascending to 3,000', also in Assam, Bihar and Tenasserim. .Tolerably common through out the Bombay presidency. Moist forests of the Konkan Ghats, also found in hilly districts of entire India. Along the stream, dense forest, dry mixed forests, Foot hills.,

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An immense climber with opposite circinate tendrils; young parts clothed with fulvous or rusty tomentum. Leaves 4-10 inches, suborbicular, scarcely broader than long, divided about 1/3 of the way down in to 2 obtuse lobes with a broad sinus, reticulately veined, glabrous or nearly so above, densely tomentose beneath, base deeply cordate, main nerves 11-15, fulvous hairy on the underside. Flowers in long peduncled terminal corymbose racemes; bracts beneath the pedicels 1/8-1/6 inches long, linear spathulate ferrugenous hairy, persistent. Calyx densely villous with fulvous or rufous hairs; to be very slender, 1/4-1/3 inch long, limb 3/8-7/16 inch long splitting into two broadly ovate lobes. Petals white, ¾- 1 inch long, obovate-cuneate, clothed along the middle of the back with appressed silky hairs and with crisped margins.

MAJOR PESTS AND DISEASES


Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used :
Bark, Stem, Seeds, nuts

Uses: 

  1. The seeds are eaten, raw or fried. When ripe, they taste like cashew nuts. 

  2. The young pods are used as a vegetable by the hill tribes.

Other Uses

  1. Grown as an ornamental plant. 

  2. Seeds used as a tonic and aphrodisiac. 

  3. The fiber from the inner bark is used for making ropes for domestic purposes. The outer bark yields 17% tannin, and 19% non-tans. The stem contains 8% tannin (Indian For. Leafl., loc. cit.). 
  4. The stem produces a tan which penetrates hides quickly and produces a soft, pale, cream-coloured leather. The tannin is present in the cementing material between the fibres and has to be combed out to make it marketable .
  5. The leaves are used for thatching and for making umbrellas,- etc., The poorer natives use the large leaves as plates.
