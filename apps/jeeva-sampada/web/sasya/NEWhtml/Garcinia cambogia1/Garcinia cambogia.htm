Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
A small or medium sized tree with riunded crown and horizontal or drooping branches; leaves dark green and shining, elliptic obovate, 2-5 in. long and 1-3 in. broad; fruits ovoid, 2 in. in dia., yellow or red when ripe, with 6-8 grooves; seeds 6-8 surrounded by succulent aril. Tree is found commonly in Evergreen forest,Forests, Gardens and Roadsides, Hill slopes, Hilly regions, shola, Slopes and Western Ghats of India.

Availability :  
It is available in Ceylon,India and ,Sri Lanka. In states like Goa Daman Diu,Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra,none,Tamil Nadu

Cultivation:
It is commonly propogated by seedling.Being an androdioecious crop, the seedlings segregate into producting bisexual and  unproductive male tress.Softwood grafting has benn standardised in Kerala Agriculture University.
Seedlings are planted at a spacing of 10x10 m and grafts at 5x 5m.Application of 40 kg of FYM/tree/year gives high yield and keeps the tree healthy.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used :
Fruits and Fruit pulp.

Uses:

1 It is used in India as a substitute for tamarind in columbu curry. Of late it has gained importance for its anti-obesant property. It contains an alkaloid called (Hydroxy Citric Acid) which helps in controlling the formation of fat in the body. It also helps in digestion and has antiseptic properties.

2. The fruits are edible, but too acid to be eaten raw. They are valued for their dried rind which is used in travancore-Cochin and malabar as acondiment for flavourig curries in place of tamarind or lime. The dried material is used along with salt in the curring of fish. It is rich in acids and and possesses marked antiseptic properties. It contains tartaric acid, 10.6; reducing sugars, 15.0; and phosphoric acid( as calcium triphosphate), 1.52%. The dried rind is also used for polishing gold and silver and as a substitute for acetic and formic acids in the coagulation of rubber latex.

3. The seeds yield 31% of an edible fat, resembling kokam butter. the fat has a grannular structure and the following constants: m.p., 29.5 deg.; acid value, 5.0; saponification value, 203.5; acetate value, nil; iodine value, 52.5; R.M value, 0.2; unsaponifiable matter, 1 %; and titre, 52.1 deg., The fat is rich in oleic acid. The tree yields a translucent yellow resin which does not form an emulsion with water. It is soluble in turpentine and gives a yellow varnish. A decoction of fruit rind is given in rheumatism and bowel complaints. It is also employed in veternary medicine as a rinse for disease of the mouth in cattle. The resin possesses purghative properties.

Harvesting Practices
A well maintained bearing tree gives an yield of 300 to 400 kg,fresh fruits.Immediately after harvest,fruits are cut opened and the seeds along with the pulp are separated.Then the rind can be dried under sun or smoke.

Other Uses
Medicinal use : A decoction of the fruit rind is given in rheumatism and bowel complaints. The organic acid known as (-)HCA is the primary acid found in the fruit and rind of G. cambogia. The fruit rind and extracts of G. cambogiaare used in may traditional recipes. In the Ayurvedic system of medicine, some flavours are said to activate digestion and are used as purgatives, in the treatment of worms and parasites, tumuors and dysentry. Neither acute nor chronic toxicity is reported with regular consumption of Garcinia products as either food or tonics. These products have been used routinely in the coastal areas of South Asia for centuries and they continue to be consumed in large amounts.
