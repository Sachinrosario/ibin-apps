· Subclass : Rosidae

Synonyms:

Common Names: 

English : Caraway

Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT                                                                                                                         

An erect, herbaceous, perennial plant with tuberous roots. Caraway is the dried fruit of Carum carvi an important umbelliferous spice.

Climate and soil:
A temperate plant and is cultivated as a summer crop in temperate region of India and also grown as a winter crop in subtropical areas. It is also growing in the temperate areas at 1800-3500 MSL. Caraway grows well in variety of soils. Sandy loam soils such as organic matter are preferred.

Availability :

A native of North-central Europe and West Asia. Cultivated limited extent in Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttaranchal.


Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild and cultivated

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An erect, herbaceous, annual or biennial, bisexual plant with thick tuberous rootstock, plant height varies from 0.5 m to 1.0 m. taproot fusiform to cylindrical, strong, thick, fleshy and long. Stem terete, up to 2 cm in diameter, hollow, cylindrical, robust, aromatic, striate, branching in upper part. Leaves are alternate, pinnately compound, bright green and ultimately segments of lower leaves are lanceolate; petiole up to 13 cm long, upper ones gradually shorter, uppermost absent, all of them with a sheath with membranous margin and auriculate apex; blade subtriangular in outline, 6-15 cm x 2-8 cm, 2-3-pinnate, lowest leaf segments at least twice as long as wide, ultimate lobes linear-lanceolate to linear, 3-25 mm long (2).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence a compound umbel, 4-8 cm in diameter, terminal; peduncle up to 11 cm long; bracts and bracteoles absent or few; primary rays 3-16, unequal, 0.5-6 cm long; secondary rays 6-16, unequal, up to 1 cm long; umbellets about 1 cm in diameter; flowers bisexual, protoandrous, usually white, sometimes pinkish; calyx absent; petals 5, obcordate with short inflexed apex, about 1.5 mm x 1 mm; stamens 5; styles 2, recurved, with enlarged base forming stylopodium, stigma capitate (2).

Fruit:
Fruit a schizocarp, ellipsoidal, laterally compressed, 3-5 mm long, splitting into 2 mericarps; mericarp often falcate, 5-ribbed, brown, with wide, solitary vittae (2).

Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Propagated through seeds.

Phenology: Flowering is during May-June. Dry, sunny weather is optimal for insect activity and good seed set (2).


Pollinators: Wind and insect.

Floral and seed biology:
Seed Biology:
Seeds dorsally flattened, slightly grooved.


CUTIVATION ASPECT:
Seed germination:
Germination is immediate.

Seedling development:
But seedling development is rather slow (2).

Related taxa:
Two types of caraway occur. A winter (biennial) type - indigenous to the eastern part of the Mediterranean and a spring (annual) type - native to mountains of Eurasia.

Chromosome number:
2n=20

Selection of planting material:
Seeds are selected for planting. Vegetative propagation is by suckers; micropropagation using in vitro culture of tissues derived from petiole, hypocotyls and seedling shoot tip has been reported.

Planting:
Seeds sown in rows at a distance of 30-40 cm, during March-April for temperate and hilly areas. Grown as winter crop in subtropical areas the seeds are planted in October-November and the crop flowers in the same year. About 6-8kg seed is required for one hectare. Seeds are mixed with fine soil or ash and sown in lines or broadcasted.

Management after planting:
Irrigation: Number of irrigations varies with soil and weather conditions. When cultivated in subtropical areas as winter crop, 3-4 irrigations are required from October-March. Irrigation should be preferably given either weeding operation or at peak flowering and grain formation stages.
Manure and fertilizer: 25-30 tons of FYM application to soil at the time of field preparation and 30 kg N at 20 kg P2O5 should be applied as the basal dose. Another dose of 30 kg N should be top dressed in two equal splits, each at an interval of 30-45 days from sowing. In the temperate areas, an additional dose of 15 kg N per ha should be top dressed twice in the spring season and at flowering stage.
Weeding: Field should be kept weed free in the initial two months of sowing. 2-3 weedings are necessary.

When propagated vegetatively suckers are taken out after the 3rd year crop. Bulbs/suckers are uprooted and transplanted during October-November.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:
White grubs: Grubs harbouring in soil causes heavy loss and damage to sprouts.
Control: Apply well decomposed FYM, apply Phorate (Thimet) 10 g to soil @ 25 kg/ha during ploughing or incorporate Chloropyriphos @ 4 ml/kg of soil during ploughing.

Hairy caterpillar and Semi loopers: Feed on foliage, stems and inflorescence of the plants and causes considerable damage to the crop.
Control: Collect and destroy caterpillars. Spray the crop at 15 days interval with 0.05 % Methyl parathion or 0.05 % Endosulphan or 0.05 % Malathion.

Diseases:

Blight: Caused by Alternaria sp.
Symptoms: Affected plants parts turns brown to dark and the plant dies in severe infection.
Control: Application of Bavistin (1.5 g/l), two sprays at 15 days interval.

Powdery mildew: Caused by Erysiphae polygoni, is a menace which can occurs in an epidemic proportion.
Symptoms: White patches usually appear on the flower buds and developing seeds.
Control: When infection is noted sulphur should dusted @ 20-25 Kg/ha. Spray the crop at fortnight intervals with 0.2 % Dithane M-45 or Bliotx-50 WP from the first appearance of the disease.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Seed has a characteristic distinct taste and pleasant smell, and is mainly used as a spice in various food preparations. Is one of the spicy fruits of Indian cooking and an ingredient of garam masala. Oil is used mainly in flavouring food products viz. meats, sausages, canned foods, cheese, pickles and in seasoning. Also used for flavouring certain alcoholic beverages, used in cosmetic products like soap, perfume and toothpaste. Carvone from the oil is effective as an anti-sprouting agent in stored potatoes, controls storage fungi and used as a disinfectant and insect repellent. In Europe and North America the fruit is used in bakery products, flavouring meat and various dishes. The oil of caraway is put to a variety of uses from food flavouring to scenting soaps and perfumes to medicine (2). Oil is often used as a domestic spice in bread. The oil of Caraway is used to flavour the Russian liqueur Kummel and the Scandinavian liqueur Aquavit (3).

Parts Used:

Seed

Harvesting Practices:
Harvesting
Harvesting starts by the end of June (after 4-5 months of sowing) and continues throughout July. Winter crop grown in subtropical areas become ready for harvest in March-April. Crop grown in temperate areas flower only after winter and matures in July next year, after 15 months. Crop is harvested when the oldest seeds start turning brown and in some cases when fruits are still green. The average yield is 800-1000 kg/ha.

Post Harvest Handling:
The harvested umbels / or plants are dried in the shade and then beaten with sticks for separating the seed. Seeds are then cleaned, dried and packed in gunny bags.

Produce and Products:

Caraway seeds and caraway seed oil.

Medicinal uses
In the pharmacology industry it is mostly used as a carminative and stomachic. Carvone isolated from caraway seed is used for the treatment of cancer. Essential oil is spasmolytic, anti-microbial, and applied for dyspeptic complaints such as mild gastro-intestinal spasm, bloating and fullness. An old-fashioned remedy for earache was a poultice of ground seeds and breadcrumbs moistened with spirit and to relieve the pain and discolouration of bruising, ground seeds were mixed with vinegar. Caraway julep was taken for relief of nervous indigestion, flatulence and settles stomach, and sometimes given in cases of hysteria. Dioscorides, the Greek physician, mentions the use of oil of caraway as a tonic for pale girls (1). Caraway belongs to a class of herbs called carminatives, which are plants helpful in easing gastrointestinal discomfort, including gas. The essential oils derived from this group of plants help alleviate bowel spasm. In addition, studies have shown that caraway oil has antibacterial and larvicidal properties. A combination of caraway with the other carminative herbs anise and fennel has shown to be helpful in dealing with conditions of flatulence, especially in children. It's actions are analgesic, anaesthetic, anodyne, anti-anxiety, antibacterial, anti-parasitic, antiseptic, diuretic, mildly expectorant, fungicidal, muscle relaxant, soporific, stimulant, tonic, urinary antiseptic. Caraway strengthens the urinary organs, soothes irritation and expels stones. It is also well known that this oil can help with menstrual cramps, is good for the skin and decreases bruising, as well as to increase the appetite and help with dyspepsia. Laryngitis, bronchitis and coughs are easy targets for the soothing vapors of caraway. This oil has traditionally been used as a remedy for colds and can also promote milk secretion. The actions of caraway are very similar to fennel seed, to which it is closely related. Caraway is used to improve the flavour of several medicinal compounds. The seeds are frequently added to cakes and confectioneries, to render them more agreeable, whilst gently exciting the digestive powers (3).


Chemistry:
Seed is sweet slightly and has sharp taste and flavour. Mature seeds after crushing are used for distillation. Steam distillation is practiced which is continued for about 6-8 hours. About 3-6 % of oil yield is obtained. Oil of caraway is a colourless or pale yellow liquid having a complex chemical composition. d-carvone (46.0-81.0% - responsible for the characteristic flavour and biological properties) and d-limonene (about 49.0-50.0%) as the major constituents. Minor constituents are a-thugene, a-pinene, camphene, sabenene, myricin, a-phellandrene, b-phellandrene, octanal, linalool, terpine- 4-ol etc.

Per 100 g edible portion seed contains approx.: water - 10 g, protein - 20 g, fat 14 g, carbohydrates - 37 g, fibre - 13 g, ash - 6 g, Ca - 689 mg, Fe - 16 mg, K - 1.4 g, Mg - 258 mg, Na - 17 mg, P - 568 mg, Zn - 6 mg, Vitamin A - 363 IU. The energy value is about 1395 kJ/100 g. Caraway fruit contains 1.5-5.0% of essential oil, depending on the year and soil conditions. 1000 fruit weight is 4-5 g. fruit of winter type has 3.0-7.0% essential oil and 1000 fruit weight is 3.0-4.5 g. on account of its superior oil content the biennial fruit has the better quality i.e., stronger taste (2). The immature fruits are rich in tannin. Resin, wax, mucilage, sugar, and a greenish fixed oil, are shown to exist in the fruit (3).

Miscellaneous Information

 
· Consumption of caraway enhances lactation of breast-feeding women.
· Old Roman custom to chew caraway seeds to aid digestion.
· Caraway cake, eaten now solely for its pleasant aromatic taste, was traditionally served to farm labourers to celebrate seedtime when the last grain had been sown (1).
· Monograph on physiological properties of caraway oil has been published by Research Institute for Fragrance Materials (RIFM).
· Often adulterated with synthetic carvone (1).
