HABIT & HABITAT:
  It is a shrub, In India it is found in deciduous forests, Uttar Pradesh, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamilnadu. Plains from the coast, in scrub jungles, up to 1000m. Locally abundant.  Srilanka, S.E. Asia, Malesia to Queensland, Celebes and New Guinea.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrubs, 2-6 m high. Leaves obovate-spathulate, 2.5-14.5 x1.5-9.0 cm, rounded or emarginate at apex, attenuate at base, round-toothed at margin, papery to leathery texture, stalk 2-12 mm long, nerves 4-6 pairs.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Tingenone, 3-O-acetyloleanolic acid, hexacosane, betulin, b-amyrin, hexacosanol and b-sitosterol isolated from stem bark, root bark and leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Terpeneol, geraniol, linalool, ferulic acid, vanillic acid, ephedrine and neoephedrine present in plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A new lupane triterpenoid – 3-oxolup-20(29)-en-30-al (fig. 1) – isolated from stem bark along with 3b-hydroxylup-20(29)-en-30-al and 3-hydroxylup-20(29)-en-3-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

b-amyrin (0.2%) and triacontanol (0.107%) from leaves whereas root contained (-)epigallocatechin (0.3%).(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Iguesterin,pristimerin, tingenone, b-amyrin, b-sitosterol, lupenone and dulcitol isolated from stem, root bark and leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Maytenonic acid also isolated from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

A new sesquiterpene pyridine alkaloid – emarginatine A-isolated and its structure and stereochemistry determined by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of another related alkaloid – emarginatine B – and its characterization as 9-benzoate of C-8-epimer of emarginatine A; maytansine also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Friedelan-3-one, b-amyrin, 29-norcycloartanol, quercetin, myricetin and isorhamnetin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Leaves contained palmitic acid. Isolation of b-sitosterol, b- and d-amyrins from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Four new sesquiterpene alkaloids – emarginatines C, D, and E and emarginatinine – isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Two more new alkaloids – emarginatines F and G – isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Two new macrolide sesquiterpene pyridine alkaloids, emarginatine F [1] and emarginatine G [2], were isolated from Maytenus emarginata. The structural determinations of 1 and 2 by 2D nmr techniques and spectral comparison with a related compound, emarginatine A [3], are discussed. Biological evaluation showed that emarginatine F [1] demonstrated strong cytotoxicity against human epidermoid carcinoma of the nasopharynx (KB), ileocecal adenocarcinoma (HCT-8), melanoma (RPMI-7951) and medulloblastoma (TE-671) tumor cells, and against murine leukemia (P-388). (Kuo et al, 1994)


Pharmacology:

Emarginatine B showed potent cytotoxicity against human KB cells with ED50 0.4 mg/ml. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Cytotoxicity against human KB cells was also shown by emarginatine E and emarginatinine with ED50 2.5 and 2.1 mg/ml respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

Emarginatine F was strongly cytotoxic to human epidermoid carcinoma of nasopharynx (KB), ileocecal adenocarcinoma (HCT-8), melanoma (RPMI-7951), medulloblastoma (TE-671) and murine leukaemia (P-388) cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for blood purification (Fruit, Shekhawat and Anand, 1984; Shah et al, 1981; Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984a); Cough (Leaf, Jain, 1965); Eye complaints (Leaf, Jain, 1965); Edible (Fruit, Jain and De, 1966); Veterinary medicine as rejuvenator (Root, Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984b); Symbolic twigs in religious ceremonies.
