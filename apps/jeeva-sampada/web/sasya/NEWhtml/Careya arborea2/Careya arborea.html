REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence:hick swollen, hard terminal spike; Flower: yellowish-white, ill smelling, sessile; Calyx: Calyx tube campanulte, segments rather shorter than the tube, oblong, obtuse, stiff, erect ; Corolla: Petals 4, imbricate, elliptic-oblong, obtuse or subacute ;Androecium: Stamens numerous, epigynous, in innermost and outermost rows often without anthers, the anthers perfect, anther versatile, dehiscing longitudinallyFilaments equaling or slightly longer than the petals, red; Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 4-celled, crowned by annular disk, ovules numerous, in two rows on axile placentation; Fruit: globular, green, glabrous, somewhat like an apple appearance ; Seed: Numerous, nesting in fleshy pulp.


   
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting: March-April.   
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Chemistry:
Lupeol, -sitosterol, betulin and an unidentified compound from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

a-spinasterol and a-spinasterone isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Careyagenol D (fig. 1) isolated from acid hydrolysate of seed saponin and its structure established as olean-12,15-diene-3b,21a,22a,28-tetrol; barringtogenol C, barringtogenol D and 16- barringtogenol C also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

A new sapogenol – careyagenol E (fig. 2) isolated form seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Hexcosnol, taraxerol, taraxerylcacetate, b-sitosterol, quercetin and ellagic acid isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Sapogenol A isolated from acid hydrolysate of crude glycosides found identical with barringtogenol B (fig. 3) for which revised structure proposed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

A new tripterpene ester – careaborin (fig. 4), mp. 257°C – isolated from leaves along with ellagic acid 4,4’-dimethyl ether and b-amyrin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Careyagenolide (fig. 5) isolated from leaves and its structure determined; maslinic and 2a-hydroxyursolic acids also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Bioactivity guided isolation of the bark of Careya arborea afforded piperine - an alkaloid chemically known as 1-[5-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-1-oxo-2,4-pentadienyl] piperidine.  (Ahmed et al., 2002)

The methanol extract of the Careya arborea Roxb. bark significantly reduced castor oil-induced diarrhoea in mice. This effect supports the local traditional use of the plant against diarrhoea. (Rahman et al., 2003)

Pharmacology:

Bioactivity guided isolation of the bark of Careya arborea afforded piperine--an alkaloid chemically known as 1-[5-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-1-oxo-2,4-pentadienyl]piperidine, which was found to possess significant central and peripheral analgesic activity. (Ahmed et al., 2002)

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for blood dysentery (Bark, Saxena and Dutta, 1975); Cold, cough (Flower + Bark, Das et al, 1983; Calyx Bedi, 1978); Cuts, wounds (Bark, Chaudhuri et al., 1975); Diarrhoea (Bark, Jain, 1965); Fistula (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Skin disease (Bark, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973; Sharma et al., 1979); Swelling of face (Leaf, Maheshwari et al., 1986); Snake bite (Bark, Roy and Chaturvedi, 1986; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Edible (Rot + Bark, Sen et al., 1985; Vartak, 1981); Cordage (Fibre, Vishnu Mittre, 1981); Fish poison (Root, Jain and De, 1966)
