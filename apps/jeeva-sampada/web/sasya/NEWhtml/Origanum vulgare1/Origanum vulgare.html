DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect herbs, 0.3-0.9 m high, clothed with short hairs. Stem 4-angled, internodes long and nodes swollen. Leaves opposite, broadly ovate, c 2.5 x 2 cm, acute at apex, entire or rarely toothed along margin, puberulous on both surfaces; petioles 0.4-1.1 cm long.  
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers small, purple or pink, crowded in numerous, 4-sided spikes in clusters or heads at the ends of the branches, sometimes forming panicles; bracts green or purple. Calyx campanulate, 5-toothed, equal with numerous long, white silky hairs on the throat. Corolla white or pink or purple; tube longer than the calyx, bilabiate (2+3); upper lip notched; lower lip 3-lobed. Nutlets brown, smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The plant is useful in inflammations, catarrh, headache, paralysis. The leaves are good for earache, bronchitis, asthma. It enriches blood. Flowers  are applied in hemicrania. Volatile oil is applied in rheumatism. The oil is stimulant and rubefacient. It is often used  as a liniment and as tonic in colic, diarrhoea and hysteria. It is also applied in toothache. 
NBPGR:
Volatile oil from herb-stimulant, anti-diarrheal, antithysteric, tonic, rubefacient, in colic as liniment in rheumatism, toothache and earache, infusion of herb-promotes menses.


Chemistry:
Essential oils contain P-cymene, r-terpinene and carvocrol as major components. Other constituents include α and β pinene, camphene, α – phellandrene, thymol, β -bisabolene and β -caryophyllene.  

·        Presence of a-thujene, camphene, b-pinene, myrcene, a-phellandrene, a-terpinene and ocimene in essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Laempferol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        1, 8-cineole, limonene, terpineol and thymol identified in essential oil by TLC and spectrometrically. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Leptosidin, naringin, 5-hydroxy-3, 3/,4/, 7-tetramethoxyflavone, luteolin, apigenin, peonidin and catechol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Detection of a-pinene (2.44), p-cymene (32.37), g-terpinene (14. 76), thymol (12.12), carvacrol (13.63), b-caryophyllene (7.45) and b-bisabolene (3.81%) in oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new antioxidative glucoside from leaves and characterized as 4-(3, 4-dihydroxybenzoyl oxymethyl) phenyl-b-D-glucopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Isolation and characterization of a new phenolic acid-2-caffeoyloxy-3-[2-(4-hydroxybenzyl)-4, 5-dihydroxy] phenylpropionic acid (I) and a new glucoside (II) from leaves; caffeic protocatechuic and rosmarinic acids also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Cinnamic, p-hydroxybenzoic, vanillic, syringic, protocatechuic, caffeic and chlorogenic acids isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        P-cymene, carvacrol and g-terpinene identified in essential oil of plant grown in Chile. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Identification of terpinen-4-ol, germacrene D and b-caryophyllene in oil of plant grown in Chile. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Identification of terpinen-4-ol, germacrene D and b-caryophyllene in oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Caffeic, chlorgenic and rosmarinic acids and tannin (9.7%) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        A novel flavone glycoside, 5-hydroxy-7-methoxy-6-O-[a-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1 à 2)-b-D-fucopyranosyl]flavone, was isolated along with four known flavones and their structures were deduced on the basis of spectral data and chemical studies. (Zheng et al., 1997) CA.127:92675

·        The chemical composition  of oil from two clones of Origanum vulgare ssp. gracile and ssp. virens was determined.  Forty compounds were identified by GC and GC/MS in both oils.  The oils from O. vulgare ssp. gracile and O. vulgare ssp. virens were characterized by high levels of sabinene (6.5-26.0%) and germacrene D (13.7-25.7%). (Chalchat and  Pasquier, 1999) CA. 132:291016.

·        The glycosidically bound volatiles amounted to 20 mg/kg in dried leaves and flowers of oregano.  Fourteen volatile aglycons were identified with  thymoquinone as the major component. Other important aglycons were benzyl alc., eugenol, 2-phenylethanol, thymol, 3-hexen-1-ol and carvacrol.  It was found that all of the aglycons have an antioxidant effect when tested by measuring peroxide values of lard stored at 60°C.  These results were compared to the antioxidative activity of oregano essential oil, pure thymol, thymoquinone and also to a-tocopherol which is well known among natural antioxidant compounds. (Milos et al., 2000) CA.133:265870

·        Eight compds. ursolic acid, oleanolic acid , protocatechuic acid, tilianin , sagittatoside A, daucosterol, b-sitosterol and stigmasterol were isolated from the plant Origanum vulgare L.  Their structures were established on the basis of spectral data (Wu et al., 2000) CA. 135:2915

·        Volatile components of samples of a population of Spanish O. vulgare were analyzed by direct thermal desorption coupled to GC-MS.  The method is fast and reliable and requires a low amt. of sample, allowing analysis of leaves and flowers from a single individual plant.  Volatile yield is highly variable among individual plants and concentration also presents a high variation for most Origanum volatile compounds., linalool being the main component in most samples.  (Garcia and Sanz , 2001) CA.135:208152.

·        The oil obtained from Origanum vulgare L. ssp. vulgare was examined  by GC and GC/MS.  Eleven compds. were identified of which g-muurolene (62.04%) was found to be the major constituent. (Pande and Mathela, 2001) CA. 134:15229

·        Assay of flavone aglycons in extracts.of O. vulgare from Macedonia including identification and isolation is performed.  Hydrolyzed extract were analyzed by TLC and HPLC.  Three aglycons were identified and then isolated by column chromatoghraphy and TLC.  Further TLC, HPLC, and UV/VIS spectrophotometric anal. showed that the isolated compounds are luteolin (the most abundant), apigenin, and diosmetin.  A new and rapid HPLC method was developed only for determination of luteolin, which can serve as an appropriate criterion for evaluation of the flavonoid content in the drug.  (Kulevanova et al., 2001) C.A. 135:278090.

·        The main constituents of the essential oils from 8 localities were beta-ocimene (14.9-21.6%), germacrene D (10.0-16.2), beta-caryophyllene (10.8- 15.7%) and sabinene (6.6- 4.2%). The essential oils from two localities contained only three above compounds as major components: germacrene D, beta-ocimene and sabinene or beta-caryophyllene, beta-ocimene and germacrene D. Three chemotypes of essential oils were identified. The main chemotype was beta-ocimene germacrene D-beta-caryophyllene. The terpenic hydrocarbons made up 52.8-80.6% of the essential oils. (Mockute et al., 2001)

·        The glycosidically bound volatiles were isolated from dried plant material by percolation with Ethyl acetate and by extraction with water during hydro distillation of essential oil.  Fifteen volatile aglycons were identified by GC-MS on 2 columns with different polarity of the stationary phases.  The main aglycons were: thymoquinone, benzyl alcohol, thymol, 2-phenylethanol, carvacrol and 1-octen-3-ol.  The content of aglycons was 19 and 21 mg/kg with respect to the method of isolation.  The chemical composition of aglycons was compared with the chem. compn. Of essential oil, which consisted mainly of thymol and carvacrol.  After the hydrolysis of glycosides, D-(+)-glucose and unknown disaccharide were identified.  The hydrolysis of disaccharide, D-(+)-glucose and D-(+)-galactose were detected by TLC and GC-MS as trimethylsilyl ethers. (Mastelic et al., 2000)

Pharmacology:

·        Aristolochic acid I, aristolochic acid II, and D-(+)-raffinose were isolated from Origanum vulgare. Their inhibition of thrombin and activity against leukemia were evaluated. Aristolochic acid I and II have high inhibition of thrombin activity and were confirmed to possess activity against cancer.  (Goun et al., 2002)


Ethnobotany:

·        Medicine for cold (leaf,  Shah and Joshi, 1971); diarrhoea (whole plant, Gaur et al., 1983); fever (leaf, Shah and Joshi, 1971); hysteria (whole plant, Gaur et al., 1983); influenza (leaf, Shah and Joshi, 1971); menstrual complaint (whole plant, Gaur et al., 1983, seed, Gupta, 1981); stimulant (whole plant, Gaur et al., 1983); tonic (seed, Gupta, 1981).
