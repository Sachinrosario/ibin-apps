HABIT & HABITAT

A small much branched unarmed tree occurs throughout the country in dry forests. Distributed in the drier regions of South India and few localities of Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A moderate-sized, much-branched, unarmed, deciduous tree. Bole crooked, up to 3.0 m in height and 0.6-0.9 m in girth. Young shoots clothed with yellowish-gray pubescence. Leaves abruptly bipinnate; main rachis 2-4" long, densely pubescent, with a small gland about the middle of the petiole and another between the uppermost pair of pinnae; stipule minute, pinnae 6-15 pairs, 1-2" long, rachises pubescent. Leaflets 10-30 pairs, sessile, linear-oblong, obtuse or subacute, the midrib nearly in center, green and glabrous above, glaucous and more or less pubescent beneath. 

Flowers subsessile, fragrant, in 12-20-flowered heads, peduncles solitary or in fascicles of 2-4 in the axils of the upper leaves, not forming a panicle, pubescent. Calyx funnel shaped, pubescent, scarcely pedicelled; teeth short. Corolla yellow, nearly 3 times as long as the calyx, pubescent outside, lobes linear-lanceolate. Stamen filaments pinkish. Pods 4-7, stalked, thin, flat, abruptly pointed, slightly transversely veined. Seeds 6-8.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

It is excellent fuelwood. Calorific value of the sapwood and the heartwood is 5,049 and 5,306 calories, respectively.

Wood: Wood is heavy (sp gr 0.84; wt 865 kg/cu m), straight grained and medium- textured. Sapwood is white and heartwood light brown, often with a purplish cast mottled with dark and light shades. Growth rings are inconspicuous.

The wood is used for tool handles, mallett heads and agricultural implements. It is commonly used for carving and turnery. It is considered a first class wood for cabinet making.


Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:

Gum - The tree yields a good gum which is a cooling effect, and useful in erysipelas, eye diseases, inflammation and ulcers.


Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:

Other uses:

Fruits used as emetic and to treat cough and malaria.

Leaves - Leaves are used in ophthalmia and as hair wash, and also as adulterant for tea. Also used as fodder. Leaves are used as green manure.

Flowers - These are considered a cooling medicine and externally applied to boils, eruptions and swellings.

Roots - Roots contain saponin, providing a substitute for soap.

Ornamental - In southern Kerala, it is planted as an avenue tree. The tree is useful for shelter-belts, wind-breaks and for afforestation.
