DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
A small, erect and compact shrub, covered with cottony tomentum up to 5.4 m. in height. Bark: soft, corky. Leaves: subsessile, broadly ovate, ovate oblong elliptic or obovate, mucrovate, cottony pubescent when young 5.0-23.0 cm. X 4.2-8.2 cm. Flowers: white, purple spotted or pink with erect petals scented in long pedunculated cottony umbellate cymes which become glabrous. Fruit: follicles subglobose, ellipsoid or ovoid, recurved, 7.5-10.0 cm. X 5.0-7.5 cm. Seeds: broadly ovate acute, flattened, narrowly margined, light brown, corna 3.2 cm. Comprising a tuft of silky hairs.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Sessile, solitary in the lower axils, becoming spicate above; Flower: Bracts foliaceous, oblong or oblong lanceolate, acute, bristle tipped, nearly glabrous, bracteoles narrowly linear subulate, bristle tipped ; Calyx:  divided almost upto the base, one of the two outer sepal rahter more broad, both oblong lanceolate,  with long mucro, the two inner sepal as long as the shorter of the outer one, linear lanceolate, mucronate; Corolla:  Yellow slightly pubescent outside, glabrous inside, bilipped, deeply 4-lobed, ;Androecium:  Stamens 2 fertile and 2 staminodes, filaments of fertile stamens exerted beyond the corolla tube, those of staminode are very short; Gynoecium:  Ovary 2-ceeled, ovules 2 in each cell, style long, stigma bifid; Fruit: Capsule ovoid with long tapering solid beak, 2-seeded; Seed:  Compressed, clothed with silky appressed hairs. Flowering & Fruiting : October-March. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
Plant- wound healing, anticatarrhal:leaf-expectorant, diaphoretic, antisyphilitic, febrifuge,antidiarrhoeal in infants, prevents lacerationand cracking of the sole, antiodontalgic, spermatorrhoea, bleeding gums, otitis: roots- in boils and glandular swellings: bark-bechic, anasarca.
Latex : in toothache, in extracting thorn or spine-inserted deeply in any muscle, in fever caused by spleen disorder; to mothers lacking milk and on rheumatic swellings; Leaf : against abdominal pain; to swellings due to sprain. flowers – stomachic toxic, bechie, anti-asthmatic, milky juice-purgative, gastso-intestinal irritant.
Modern use: Same as Calotropis gigantea.  


Chemistry:
NBPGR:
Leaves contain colotropin and calotropagenin plant and its latex contain calotropic, calotoxin, uscharin, uscharidin, a and b-amyrin and b-sitosterol.  Latex also contain uzarigenin syziogenin, proceroside, sterols, calactin and voruscharin.
Flower contains flavonoides: glycosides-scutellarein-7-neohesperidoside: roots gave favonoids glycosides-luteolin-7-0-2”-(p-o-coumaroyl)-b-d-glucopyraoside, leaves and stems gave iridoids-barlerin and acetyl barlerin.

NBRI:
Scutellarein-7-rhamnosylglucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Structures of new iridoids – barlerin (fig. 1) and acetylbarlerin (fig. 2)– isolated from leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structure of a flavone glycoside, assigned earlier as scutellarein-7-O-rhamnoglucoside, has been modified as 5,6,4¢-trihydroxy-7-O-neohesperidosyl-flavone (scutellarein-7-neohesperidoside) and confirmed by its synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

A new acetylated flavanoid – luteolin-7-O-(2’’-p-coumaroyl)-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig. 3)– isolated from roots and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Two new iridoid glycosides (1 and 2), together with the known compounds barlerin (3) (fig. 4) and verbascoside (4) (fig. 5), were isolated from Barleria prionitis. The new iridoid glycosides were determined to be 6-O-trans-p-coumaroyl-8-O-acetylshanzhiside methyl ester (1) (fig. 6a) and its cis isomer (2) (fig. 6b) by using spectroscopic, especially 2D NMR, data. A 3:1 mixture of 1 and 2 was shown to have potent in vitro activity against respiratory. (Chen et al, 1998).


Pharmacology:
Root extract of Berleria prionitis brought about an interference with spermatogenesis. The round spermatids were decreased by 73.6% (P< or =0.001). No significant change was found in the population of secondary spermatocytes. However, the population of preleptotene spermatocytes was decreased by 41.9%. The extract reduced the fertility of male rats by 100%. Cross sectional surface area of Sertoli cells and mature Leydig cell numbers were significantly reduced (36.9%). The total protein, sialic acid contents of the testes, epididymides, seminal vesicle and prostate were reduced. Testicular glycogen content was low. Antifertility effects of Berleria seemed to be mediated by disturbances in testicular somatic cells functions resulting in Physico-morphological events of spermatogenesis. (Gupta et al, 2000).

Antidiabetic activity of Berleria prionitis was evaluated using alloxan induced hyperglycaemic rats. The potency of alcoholic and aqueous extracts of leaf, stem, and root was compared with that of chloropropamide at a dose of 200 and 100 mg/kg, respectively. Alcoholic and aqueous extracts of leaf and root caused significant fall in blood glucose level in diabetic rats. (Geetha and Wahi, 2001). MAPA 2001, 05, 2469.
