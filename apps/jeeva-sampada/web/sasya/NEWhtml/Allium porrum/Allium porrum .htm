HABIT & HABITAT
The leek (Allium porrum L.) is a tall, hardy, biennial with white, narrowly ovoid bulbs and broadleaves. It resembles the green onion but is larger. It is a non-bulb-forming member of the onion family and is grown for its blanched stems and leaves. Leek is believed to be originated in the Mediterranean region where it has been in cultivation since prehistoric times. It was known by ancient Greeks and Romans. Leeks are mainly grown in northern Europe and less frequently in India, the United States, and Canada. Leeks are especially important in northern European countries such as Belgium, Denmark, and the Netherlands.
It is commonly found in the South and Western zones, in the States Goa Daman Diu and Tamil Nadu in the districts of Goa and Palni hills,

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Breeding System :
Cross-pollinated. Leek is propagated through seeds or by division; the former is preferred. The objective for leek breeding usually emphasize a long shaft length and a dark green leaf blade in addition to high yield. Programmes for crop improvement currently used are
1. simple selection of desirable individuals from the populations to use in intercrossing and seed production.
2. the use of replicated trials and statistically based measurements of the average performance of different individuals to choose those to be retained and combined in an improved cultivar.
3. development of F1- and triple-cross hybrid cultivars.
In leeks breeding for resistance to yellow stripe virus and leek rust are important. Even breeding for less variability is also important.

Phenology : 
Flowering occurs during spring- early summer.

Pollinators :
Pollination occurs mainly through insects, honeybees being the most important. Wind and gravity have less influence.

Seed Biology:
The fruit is a capsule, Seeds somewhat semi- circular or oval, compressed. Leek does not usually seed in the plains as the mature plants should be exposed to low temperatures for a period before they form flowering stalks. the seed is produced at higher altitudes in the hills. In order to obtain pure seed, true to type plants should be selected and other off-types should be rogued out. As it is a cross pollinated crop, isolation distance like that of onion should be maintained for production of pure seed.

Seed Germination :
Leek is propagated through seeds. Seed germination depends upon temperature, with 18-22 ºC being the optimum. In India seeds are sown during august to October in the nursery beds, and seedlings are ready to plant when they attain a height of 15cm. 5-7 kg/ha of seeds is required.

Cultivation:
Leeks are best grown in areas with cool to moderate temperature. The seeds are sown in nurseries during the end of September to October; seed rate is 5 kg per hectare. The transplanting is done in trenches about 30-45 cm deep. the soil is worked up to the plants as they grow. Manuring is heavier compared to onions. A yield of 30 tones leek per hectare removes 100 kg of nitrogen, 65 kg of phosphorous and 130 kg of potash from the soil. For the seed production, since it is a biennial crop, seeds are produced in India at higher altitudes in the hills with cooler climate.

Raising Nursery/Transplanting

The method of transplanting often is same as that for onion except that greater spacing is maintained (15 cm). The plants are often balanced by earthing it up with soil. The transplanting is done in trenches about 30 to 45 cm deep. The soil is worked up to the plants gradually as they grow; care being taken not to start this process too early when plants are young.

Manuring
As the plants of leek being larger than those of onion, the requirements of manures and fertilizers are higher. A yield of 30 tonnes leek per hectare removes 100 kg of nitrogen, 65 kg of phosphorus and 130 kg of potash from the soil. Other operations are same as for onion.

Soil and climate: leek can be grown in variety of soils but grows luxuriantly on medium soils that are rich in plant nutrients and organic matter. loosening of sandy clayloam subsoil helps in easy penetration of roots. the most favourable soil pH for leek is 6-8. Leek withstands heat and cold better than onion. although leek is a cool season crop temperature is more important than the length of the day in seed stalk development. In India leek thrives well at higher altitudes.
MAJOR PESTS AND DISEASES
Diseases like downy mildew, purple blotch, pink rot, smut, smudge, Heterosporium blight, and white tip are the common diseases of leek. White tip of leek has been long known in the British Isles. The disease is caused by the fungus Phytophthora pori Foister. The tips of leaves become yellow, die, and turn white for a distance up to 15 cm. Most affected leaves turn backward. Water-soaked areas in the vicinity of the midrib may appear halfway down the leaf or near the base. The disease can be effectively controlled by spraying with Bordeaux mixture. Purple blotch (Alternaria pori) and powdery mildew (Leveillula taurrcd) observed in leeks were effectively controlled by Prochlorz and Fenpropimorp chemicals. Leek moth (Acrolepiopsis assectelld) is a serious pest in Italy. The moth had five overlapping generations in one year. The third generation, which occurred in July, was the most injurious. The diseases are almost same with that of onion.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Stems and Leaves

Uses:  

1. When tender, it is eaten raw. 
2. It is also cooked with other vegetables. 
3. Like the green bulb onion, stone leeks are used for flavouring or for livening dull starchy diets of eastern peoples. 
4. They are also used in salads or eaten raw alone or used as a flavouring agent in soups and stews. They are mild flavoured.

Harvesting Practices and yeild:
 At the time of harvest, the leeks should have green fresh tops and medium-sized necks. The entire plant is pulled and the leeks are packed in a crate in a manner similar to that used for celery.
Yield
Leek is also harvested like green onions and marketed in bunches. Yield depends upon several factors, however an average yield of 30 ton/ ha can be obtained from well managed crops.

Leek Essential Oil.
New 85 volatile compounds have recently been isolated from leek essential oil in Germany. Six samples of leek essential oil from Turkey were analyzed in Germany for aroma compounds. To recover as wide a range of aroma compounds as possible, aroma compounds were collected by steam distillation, static headspace analyses, Freon distdlation and cryotrapping in ether. The aroma GC/MS.85 compounds were reported for the first time in leek essential oil; 28 of these have already been recorded in other Album spp. Compounds identified include thiols, mono-,di-, tri-tetra-and pentasulphides, alkylthiocarboxylic acid, S-alkyi esters, thiophenes, trithiolanes, other S-contammg compounds, acetals, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, esters, acids, furans, other oxygen containing compounds and hydrocarbons.

Processing Techniques:
Since Leeks are perishable they should be processed as soon as possible after harvest. If storage is necessary, a-temperature of 32°C, and a relative humidity of 90 to 95% is recommended.
The leeks are unloaded onto a continuous conveyer-belt, and so oriented, that, as they pass by a revolving blade, the roots are sliced off. A thorough washing is important to remove dirt and silt that collects in the crotch of the leaves. The bulbs and leaves are diced together into 3 inch dice. These are loaded onto trays at 181b or less per square foot A sulphite/bisulphite mixture is sprayed over the dice in a concentration to give 500 to 1,000 ppm of SO, in the dehydrated product. Drying temperatures are similar to those previously discussed for Onions.

Economical Potential to the country

It is mostly used as salad or in soups, etc. It is quite popular in European and
other western countries. Thus, there is a scope for its export from India, if its production is gradually increased. According to APEDA export statistics (1994-95), a good beginning has been made in the export of fresh leeks from India during the last 3 years. The exports earning was up to Rs 3.04 lakh during 1992-93, where after it declined gradually till 1994-95.

Dehydrated leeks constitute the fifth most important bulb crop imported into
several European countries like Belgium, Luxemburg, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, etc., totalling about 1,300 tonnes in 1979-80, as per the ITC Geneva survey. Albinia supplied 17.5% of this world supply, followed by Kenya (13.2%), Egypt (9.5%), Spain (9.5%). It is further estimated that the imports of leeks alone into the eight important world markets accounted for about 3.5% of the total imports of dehydrated vegetables (ITC, 1981). Thus, dehydrated leeks appear to be important foreign exchange earning commodity. Its cultivation should be encouraged in India wherever feasible.
