DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual erect, monoecious herb. Leaves long-petioled, ovate or rhomboid -ovate, acute at apex, rounded or shortly cuneate at base, glabrous; petioles variable in length, 2-10 cm, slender, pubescent; stipules minute. Spikes 5-15m long, axillary, terminating in an allomorphic flower. Male flowers: minute, in clusters of 3- 7 towards upper part of spikes; stamens with curved, swollen, hyaline filaments. Female flowers: scattered, in lower parts of spikes, 3-5 together in the axil of bracts; bract concave, ovate, cuneiform, truncate, crenate; calyx of 3 minute hyaline scales with gland tipped cilia; style arms with 3 slender branches. Fruits schizocarp, small, hispid, concealed by bracts. Seeds ovoid, smooth, pale brown.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Uses: Expectorant, diuretic, purgative, anthelmintic. Root is bruised in hot water and employed as cathartic. Decoction of leaf mixed with common salt is used as laxative. Mixture with chunam the leaf is used in scabies and effective in cutaneous diseases in. Decoction or dry powder of leaf mixed with little garlic is employed to children as anthelmintic. Mixture of leaf juice and margosa oil is rubbed on tongue of infants for purpose of sickening them and clearing their stomachs of viscid phlegm. Leaf juice is emetic and increases the secretion of the pulmonary organs. Cataplasms of leaves have local application to syphilitic ulcers. Fresh leaves ground into paste, made into ball to the size of a large marble are introduced into the rectum of children to relief obstinate constipation. Plants are used in congestive headaches; a leaf-juiced saturated cotton is inserted in each nostril to relieve head symptoms by causing hemorrhage from the nose. Dry leaf powder is prescribed for the treatment of bedsores and wounds attacked by worms. Mixture of leaf-paste and limejuice cures ringworm. Mixture of common salt or quicklime and leaf juice is antiparasiticide.
Chemistry:
  • Sitosterol isolated form dried and whole plant extract. (Mukherjee, 1967).
  • A amide designated acalyphamide (Fig. 1.1) [isolated as its acetate Fig. 1.2], along with the modified dipeptide aurantiamide (Fig. 1.3) and its acetate (Fig. 1.4), succinimide, 2-methylanthraquinone, tri-O-methylellagic acid (Fig. 1.5) [isolated as its acetate Fig. 1.6], b-sitosterol and b-sitosterol- b-D-glucoside have been isolated from leaves and twigs of A. indica L. Acalyphamide (Fig. 1.1) has been characterized as an amide of tyramine (p-hydroxy- b-phenethyamine) and dotriacontanoic acid (C31H63COOH). The occurrence of the amides and the modified dipeptides in this plant is of considerable biogenetic and chemotaxonomic significance. (Talapatra et al.,1981).
  • A new cyanogenic glucoside, acaclyphin (Fig. 2), was isolated from the aerial parts of A. indica and its structure was identified by NMR studies. (Nahrsted et al., 1982).
  • Stigmasterol isolated from roots and leaves; acalypholacetle isolated from leaves. (Manzoor et al., 1985). C. A. 1988, 108, 52780x.
  • Two flavonoids 5, 7-dihydroxyflavone (chrysin) and 3,5,7-trihydroxyflavone (galangin), have been isolated from the ethanolic extract of whole plants Acalypha indica. (Hiremath et al., 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 110082p.  

Pharmacology:

  • Plant useful against pneumonia and asthma. (Mukherjee, 1967).
  • Aqueous extract was given to 38 patients giving cardial symptoms of symptoms of wheezing cough; 18% cases showed good clinical response from all symptoms, 22% showed no improvement while 60% were relieved of wheezing within two weeks. The raised eosinophil. (Raman et al., 1979).
