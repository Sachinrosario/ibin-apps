DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Leaves 1/8 to 1/2 inch broad and seeds resemble onion seeds in shape and size with angular edges. It bears floral nectarines and each sepal has 3 veins.

Phenology :  
When well developed plants with fleshy roots were planted both in nature as well as in the field, it produces inflorescence with the emergence of its sprouting within 4-6 days of receipt of first shower. However, when plant is grown in the field from individual fleshy roots, then only those fleshy roots will produce inflorescence which are well developed but the inflorescence emerging from individual fleshy root may not have as good number of flowers as in the case of inflorescence produced from the fleshy root bunches started earlier. Generally, one plant produces only one inflorescence but sometimes 3-4 inflorescences have been observed depending upon the age of the mother plant or thickness and broadness of the stem.

Cultivation:
Soil and climate: Safed Musli requires well drained loamy to sandy loam soils rich in organic matter. Warm and humid climatic with good amount of soil moisture during the growing season favour luxuriant vegetative growth and facilitate fleshy root development. Suitably sized beds should be prepared before receipt of rains. If land is slopy, planting may be done in the flat beds otherwise ridges of 15-20 cm height at 30 cm apart are advocated to drain excess of rain water or irrigation water.
Propagation: It could be propagated by seeds as well as by vegetative means (fleshy roots bearing shoot buds).
i) By seed: The seeds remain under dormant condition for about 10 months, i.e., up to June. Seeds take about 12-16 days to sprout and about 13% germination is reported in one year old seeds.
ii) By vegetative means: The initiation of sprouts of fleshy roots in mid May but sometimes it could be early as the last week of April in stored material under laboratory conditions. In teh forest, seedlings emerge out from the ground within 4-6 days after receipt of rains. However, for the purpose of raising plants in the field, either the sprouted seedlings should be collected from thr forest between 10-30 days after receipt of rains and transplanted in the field or fleshy root bunches should be taken out from the ground or storage place in mid of May which may be mixed with equal amount of moist soil and then kept in perforated polythene bags of one kg capacity, the mouth of polythene bag may be tied and kept at a cool place in the room. Even these fleshy roots are separated from the bunch, the shoot buds remain adhered with the stem disc portion attached with separated fleshy roots.
iii) Experiments on in vitro micro propagation of Safed musli indicated that sprouts can be obtained from stem discs.
Manures and fertilizers: Use of 10-15 tonnes of FYM/ha is found to be optimum for good yield. It can easily be intercropped with maize.
Irrigation and Interculture: If there is no rain after transplanting of fleshy roots, then one irrigation be provided immediately. The crop may be sown after receipt of rains. Later, when soil moisture has receded in the fields, one irrigation may be provided after an interval of 10 to 15 days. In all, one to two weeding-cum-hoeing are needed to keep the soil porous and free of weedy growth.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests: In storage, fleshy roots were found to become hollow and infection of Aspergillus and Fusarium species were observed
Diseases: Chlorosis in foliage , viz. yellowing and whitening of leaves in clay loams calcarious soils were observed and causes serious losses.

Harvesting Practices
The crop matures in about 90 days under cultivation. At maturity, the leaves start yellowing and ultimately these dry up from the collar part and fall down. The crop could thus be harvested when leaves have dried which occurs in the month of September/October. During digging of plants, fleshy root bunches should be lifted from the soil.
Yield: A yield of one tone of fleshy roots/ha have been recorded. This after processing and drying, it will be reduced to 200 kg of Safed musli.

Chemistry
Safed Musli roots contain albumin but no starch. Chemical composition of Safed Musli (market sample) gave the following results: Carbohydrates-42.00%; Protein-8.5%; Saponin-2-3%; Sapogenin (Hecogenin)-0.17%; Aqueous extract-40.00% and root fibre-3-4%.

Processing Techniques
The harvested fleshy roots should be spread in the shade for about 4-7 days to dry-out its moisture. Then, these are collected in heaps, which are then covered with leaves of gunny bags. The heap is stirred at 3-4 days intervals for next 10-15 days. Afterwards, fleshy roots are separated from the bunches. Slight pressure should be exerted with fingers and thumb on teh skin of the individual roots so that they get separated and milky white fleshy roots will come out. These should be cleaned and dried in the sun by spreading in thin layers on cloth or mats. These roots take a further period of 7-10 days for drying, depending upon weather conditions. The dry roots are white in colour and hard and bony in appearance. The dry roots possess less than 5% moisture. This is Safed Musli of commerce. The yellowish brown coloured Musli results from immature crops and fetches lower price.
