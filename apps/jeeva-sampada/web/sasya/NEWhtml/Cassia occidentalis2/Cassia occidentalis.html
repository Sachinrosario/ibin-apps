REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence: short peduncled few-flowered racemes, corymbose axillary forming a terminal panicles; Flower: Bracteate, pedicillate, acuminate, caudacous, white with pink tinge; Calyx: Divided at the base, glabrous, segments white, slightly tinged with pink, oblong, obtuse, membranous; Corolla: Petals 5, subequal, ovate-oblong, obtuse, yellow, faintly veined with orange; Androecium: Stamens 10, the three upper reduced to minute staminodes , the remaining seven perfect, the 3 longer with larger anthers than the four lateral ones; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, at the bottom of the calyx, ovules many, style short stigma many ; Fruit: Pods 4-5, recurved, glabrous, septate, distinctly torulose, compressed ; Seed:  20-30, ovoid, compressed, acute at one end and rounded at the other. 
   
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : Januarary - March.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.
 
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 26, 28.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type:   It can grow on wide variety of soils i.e. on poor shallow soil  ,on dry slopes of Himalayas, trap, granite and sandstone soil or even frequently on khurda laterite.

Nutrient requirements:  Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers. It requires nitrogenous fertilizers especially during the period of growth.

Water management: It requires annual rainfall from 300-1000 mm or even more Harvesting period / Method: Leaves and pods are harvested when it is mature.

Pharmacology:

Cassia occidentalis Linn. exerts its antimutagenic activity by modulating the xenobiotic activation and detoxification mechanisms. (Sharma et al., 1999).

The hepatoprotective effect of aqueous-ethanolic extract (50%, v/v) of leaves of Cassia occidentalis was studied on rat liver damage induced by paracetamol and ethyl alcohol by monitoring serum transaminase (aspartate amino transferase and serum alanine amino transferase), alkaline posphatase, serum cholesterol, serum total lipids and histopathological alterations. The extract of leaves of the plant produced significant hepatoprotection. (Jafri et al., 1999).

Aqueous extracts of Cassia occidentalis Linn. screened for effectiveness in inhibiting mutagenicity of aflatoxin B1 (AFB1) and benzo[a]pyrene (B[a]P) in the Ames test. Antimutagenicity was evaluated using Salmonella typhimurium strains TA 98 and TA 100. In the assay, metabolic activation of AFB1 (0.5 microg/plate) and B[a]P (1 microg/plate) was mediated by rat liver S9 preparation. Although both plants inhibited mutagenicity. (Sharma et al., 2000)

The ethanolic, dichloromethane and lyophilized aqueous extracts of Cassia occidentalis root bark evaluated for their antimalarial actvity in vivo, in 4-day, suppressive assays against Plasmodium berghei ANKA in mice. (Tona et al., 2001).

Cassia occidentalis L. possesses antimutagenic activity against benzo[a]pyrene (BaP) and cyclophosphamide (CP)-induced mutagenicity in mice.  C. occidentalis modulated hepatic drug metabolizing enzymes. It is suggested that by a similar mechanism, it may be influencing the hematotoxic and immunotoxic responses of cyclophosphamide. (Bin-Hafeez et al., 2001)

Administration of seeds or parts of the seed of Senna occidentalis (coffee senna) for long periods, induces histochemical changes in the skeletal muscles of hens and rats that are characteristic of a mitochondrial myopathy--as decrease of SDH and COX activity, with some COX negative fibers suggested that the COX mitochondrial activity is heterogeneously impaired in myofibers of rats intoxicated with S. occidentalis. These abnormalities remember those observed in some types of human mitochondrial myopathies. (Calore et al., 2002).
