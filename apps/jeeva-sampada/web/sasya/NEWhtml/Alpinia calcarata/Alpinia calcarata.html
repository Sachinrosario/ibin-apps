DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs, c 1.5 m high with perennial rootstock. Leaves sessile, linear-lanceolate, 15-35 x 2.5 – 5.0 cm, acuminate at apex, narrowing towards base, glabrous on both surfaces; ligule up to 1 cm long, membranous, entire, ciliate.

Inflorescence: Dense panicles; Flower: numerous, bract small, ovate, complete, hermaphrodite, regular ; Calyx: funnel shaped, loosely tubular, 3-toothed; Corolla: tube cylindric, rarely longer than the calyx, lip ovate-oblong, sessile, yellow, streaked with purple veins, emarginate Androecium: Stamen 1, perfect, filament flattened, anther cell diverging at the top, lateral staminode minute; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled,densely pubescent, ovules many in each cell, style filiform, stigma sub-globose; Fruit: Capsules globose red; Seed: Globose.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers in panicles, 1-1.5 cm long. Calyx c 6 mm long, 3-lobed, sparsely pubescent. Corolla tube shorter than or equalling the calyx, pubescent at the throat; lobes oblong,  1-1.5 cm long, yellow, streaked with purple vein. Labellum obovate, 2-3 cm long, 1.50- 2.0 cm wide in lower half, apex emarginate, white. Capsule globose, orange-red.

Chemistry:
Physico-chemico chatacteristics have been analyzed from the essential oils from leaves and roots of A. calcarata. (Rath et al, 1994).

The essential oil compound of the rhizome and leaf oil of Alpinia calcarata  Rosc. of Indian origin has been analyzed by the combination of capillary G.C and GC/MS. In all 31 constituents were identified in rhizome oil and 28 constituents in the leaf and rhizome oil respectively. (Tewari et al, 1999).

Four new labdane-type diteroenoids, calcaratarins A-D (fig. 1, calcaratarin A) along with six known labdane-type diterpanoids, a known coumarin, were isolated from the rhizomes of Alpinia calcarata. The structures of calcaratarins A-D were elucidated on the basis of spectroscopic studies. (Kong et al, 2000).
