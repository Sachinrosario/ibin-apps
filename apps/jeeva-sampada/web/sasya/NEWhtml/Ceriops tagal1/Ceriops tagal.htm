Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Tree or shrub. Coastal forests of India. Common along the intertidal banks of mangrove swamps, more so in areas nearer to and under estuarine influence. Distributed in almost all the tidal swamps along both the coasts of Indian peninsula and Andamans and Nicobar Islands. It is widely distributed from East Africa to Australia and the Pacific.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Glabrous shrub, branchlets marked with the scars of fallen leaves and stipules. Leaves 2-4 by 1.5-2", coriaceous, obovate-oblong, rounded at the apex, sometimes slightly emarginate, glabrous, much attenuated at the base; petioles rugose, glabrous.

 

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in axillary, peduncled, branched cymes; pedicels short, stout. Calyx 5 lobes, oblong, subobtuse. Petals 5, white, shorter than the calyx, oblong-obovate, truncate or emarginate, furnished at the apex with 3 short clavate appendages, glabrous, with a strong mid nerve at the back of the petal. Stamens 10, about as long as the petals, alternately long and short. Fruit slightly conical, protruded radicle sometimes reaching 1 ft. before falling, gradually thickening to near the apex and then becoming acute, deeply grooved and angled, reddish-brown.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Fuel:Wood has high calorific value (5,150 cal, 9,272 B.t.u.). Good fuelwood. Useful either for direct use as fuel or for conversion into charcoal.

Wood: Orange red, without ant sharp line of distinction between sapwood and heartwood. Timber heavy. 55-65 lb./ c ft. Hard and durable. 1.1% ash.

Wood used for boat building. Timber is the most durable of all mangroves.



Harvesting Practices:

 

Produce and Products:

A red cutch, Mangrove Cutch, obtained from bark and cortex and used along with indigo for obtaining different shades of black, brown or purple.

Other uses:

Leaves suitable for direct application in the tanning industry and give a strong, supple, pinkish leather free from any crack of the grain.

Decoction of bark used to cure haemorrhage, and a decoction of shoots a substitute for quinine. Bark an ingredient of lotions for malignant ulcers.

Wood used in house building and for pit props. Branches used for the knees of boats.

It yields an adhesive.

Flowers are a rich source of honey and bee wax. Bark - It contains 45% tannin, which is used for tanning.
