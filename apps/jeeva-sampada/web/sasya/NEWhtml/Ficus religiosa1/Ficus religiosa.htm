Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large tree suitable for growing as single tree or as avenue. Occurs at the Himalayan foothills from Pakistan through India to Bhutan to Myanmar. Wild throughout the subcontinent. Fast growing tree, usually an epiphyte in young stage of development. The tree is found wild or cultivated nearly throughout India, up to 1500 m in sub-Himalayan forests, districts of Bihar and Orissa, upper Gangetic plain, West Bengal and central India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large tree with a fluted trunk and spreading branches without aerial roots. Epiphytic when young. Bark smooth, pale grey. Leaves alternate, 11-17 X 7.5-12.5 cm, very shiny on the upper surface with a heart shaped base, abruptly narrowed at the apex into a long tail like tip, leaf stalk slender 7.5-10 cm long, which causes leaves to sway in a breeze.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Fig globular 1.3 cm, in stalkless pairs, dark pur[ple when ripe. Male flowers stalkless, scanty at the mouth of the fig, sepals 3, stamen 1 stalkless or stalked. Gall and fertile flower stalkless or stalked, sepals 5.


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology: In the north indai new leaves appear about March. Old leavs are shed in the winter. Fruit ripens in April, also in October-November.


Pollinators:

As the fig of 'pipal' is small the insect associated with it is smaller than insects associated with the species with bigger fruits. Each species of ficus usually has its particular insect. The insect can not licve without the fig and the ficus tree can not produce seed without the insect. The insects enter through a hole at the tip of the fig and lay eggs in the flowers, which are known as gall flowers. The eggs hatch and mature into new insects which on leaving their home become dusted with pollen from the male flowers. They then make their way into another fig and repeat the process, thus ensuring pollination of other female flowers. Figs turn dark purple on ripening and are much sought after by birds, notably by flocks of sterlings in April. Also by green pigeons and monkeys which aid into dispersal of seeds.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by branch cutting. Natural regeneration takes place through seeds. Dispersed mostly by birds and monkeys. Seeds that lodge in crevices of walls or rocks germinate readily.
Artificial regeneration can be done by seedlings, wildlings, stump or branch cuttings. The most convenient method is the planting out of self grown natural seedlings.

Other uses:

Leaves - used as fodder. Leaves are used for miniature paintings.
Leaves and young shoots - These are purgative and used against wounds and skin diseases.
Stem bark - It is medicinally important and used against a variety of ailments. Also yields fibre.
Fruits - Medicinally important as laxative and digestive. Fruits are edible.
Seeds - Medicinally used as refrigerant and laxative.
Latex - It is medicinally used against immflamation and hemorrhages.
Gum of the tree is used as a source of sealing wax.

Miscellaneous Information

Tree begins life as an epiphyte developing from seeds dropped by birds in forks of other trees, sometimes in the trunk of a date palm. Two co-exist for a long time, perhaps one hundred years or more. It sometimes strangles the host tree, which gradually dies. It is destructive to buildings. It is a long-lived tree. A tree in Sri Lanka is reported to be over 2200 years old.

This is the Bodhi Tree under ehich Buddha sat and received enlightenment at Bodh Gaya. A sapling of the original tree from here was taken to Sri Lanka by Mahindra, son of emperor Ashoka and flourishes there today.

Tree serves as a host for lac insects and some silkworms.
The plants have been micropropagated. Plantlets have been obtained from mature stem.
Sacred tree for Hindus and Budhists.
Plant is a good climate purifier.
