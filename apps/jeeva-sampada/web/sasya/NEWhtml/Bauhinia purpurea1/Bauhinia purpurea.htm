Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Medium sized ornamental tree suitable for open lands and field edges. Subtropical climate up to 1500 m in the Himalayan hills and Central Indian hills, East Indian hills and parts of Western Ghats, tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (moist tropical). Found throughout India, ascending to an altitude of 1,300 m in the sub-Himalayan tract.

It is a moderate light demander and can stand frost well.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Erect tree with stout branches, glabrescent. Bark dark grey or brown, pink to yellow inside. Leaves 4-5" in diameter, roundish, about as broad as long, divided 1/3 to ½ the way down into 2 obtuse or subacute lobes, glabrous above, glabrous or faintly puberulous beneath, base cordate; main nerves 9-11, petioles glabrous, stipules triangular-oblong, acute, pubescent, deciduous.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers fragrant varying in colour from white to purple,in terminal and axillary few flowered corymbose racemes; bracts beneath the pedicels triangular, acute, pubescent; pedicels pubescent, 2-bracteolate below the middle; buds fusiform, pubescent.

Calyx fulvous-pubescent, tube slightly dilated upwards, limb splitting in 2 coriaceous segments slightly divided at the apex into 5 short teeth. Petals oblanceolate, acute, with a long claw, white, rose or purple. Fertile stamens 3-4, filaments as long as the petals. Ovary with a very long stalk, puberulous; ovules 16-20, stigma oblique, peltate. Pods sub-woody, flat, pointed at both the ends, glabrous.

Seeds 12-16, oblong-ellipsoid, compressed, brown.

Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Grown by entire transplanting and direct sowing.

It is raised from seeds and the seedlings are then transplanted. It can be raised at site by line sowing. Regular weeding is necessary.



MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:


Polyporus zonalis – root rot and Hypoxylon ustulatum Bulliard – butt rot frequently occur together in New Forest, Dehra Dun (U.P.).

Cercospora bauhinae H. & Hp. Sydow – leaf spot recorded from Banglore (Karnataka)

Phyllosticta bauhinae Cke. – leaf spot in Pusa (Bihar) and Bandra (Maharashtra)

Exosporium bauhiniae Garud – leaf spot, recorded from Dapoli (Maharashtra)

Hypoxylon ustulatum Bulliard - root rot

Fomes lignosus (Klotzsch) Bres - root rot

Dendrophthoe falcata (Linn.) Ettingsh - mistletoe

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Wood as fuelwood

Wood: Wood is greyish red, soft and light (wt. 576 kg/m3). For small timber. It is used for the manufacture of agricultural implements and matches.

Processing Techniques:

Timber seasons without cracking and should be converted green and open stacked under cover.



Other uses:

Leaf fodder

Medicinal - Root is carminative but the root bark is poisonous. Stem bark is astringent, used in diarrhoea. Decoction of bark is used to wash ulcers.

Bark - Bark is also used in dyeing and tanning industries. Inner portion of bark yields a fiber.

Flower - Flower buds are eaten as a vegetable and also pickled. They are reported to be laxative and anthelmintic. Flowers also used for ornamentation.
