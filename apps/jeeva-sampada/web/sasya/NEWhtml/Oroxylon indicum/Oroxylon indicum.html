Chemistry:

·        Bark extract decreased capillary permeability in rats sensitized by egg albumin. It suppressed inflammation caused by egg albumin in intact and adrenalectomised rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Baicalein, its 6-glucuronide and 7-glucuronide, mp.2200, scutellarein, its 7-glucuronide, mp. 2050, isolated from leaves and stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        In addition, oroxylin A, chrysin and scutellarein -7-rutinoside isolated from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Prunetin and b-sitosterol isolated from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        A new flavone glucuronide- orxindin – isolated from seeds and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Syntheses of baicalein-6-glucuronide and -7-glucuronide. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Seeds yielded fixed oil (25.0) and crude protein (7.9%); fromer contained caprylic (2.23), lauric (0.18), myristic (0.15), palmitic (2.48), palmitoleic (0.34), stearic (0.28), oleic (89.42) and linoleic (4.92%) acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Baicalein-7-O-b-gentiobioside isolated from seeds of Nepalese plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        From the stem bark of Oroxylum indicum four new pterocarpans, namely Me oroxylopterocarpan (I), dodecanyl oroxylopterocarpan (II), hexyl oroxylopterocarpan (III), and heptyl oroxylopterocarpan (IV) have been isolated and characterized as 2',3'-dihydro-2',2'-dimethyl-2,3-furanylpterocarpan; 2',3'-dihydro-2'-methyl-2'-dodecanyl-2,3-furanylpterocarpan;2',3'-dihydro-2'-hexyl-2,3-furanyl-8-hydroxypterocarpan and 2',3'- dihydro-2'-heptyl-2,3-furanyl-8-hydroxypterocarpan on the basis of spectral data and chem. anal. (Ali et al., 1999) CA. 132:163510

·        Isolation of ellagic acid from root wood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).Four flavonoids, chrysin, baicalein, baicalein-7-O-glucoside, baicalein-7-O-diglucoside (Oroxylin B) and one unknown flavonoid have been isolated and purified for the first time in the seeds of Oroxylum indicum by high-speed counter-current chromatography with a two-phase solvent system composed of chloroform-methanol-water (8:10:5, v/v). Then, 50 mg baicalein-7-O-glucoside, 10.5 mg baicalein-7-O-diglucoside, 4.5 mg chrysin-7-O-diglucoside, 25 mg baicalein and 45 mg chrysin could be obtained after injecting 20 mg/ml sample extract ten times and their purities were 96, 90, 85, 95 and 98%, respectively. All these constituents were identified by high-performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance. (Chen et al., 2003)


Pharmacology:

·        A methanolic extract of Oroxylum indicum strongly inhibited the mutagenicity of Trp-P-1 in an Ames test. The major antimutagenic constituent was identified as baicalein with an IC50 value of 2.78±0.15 microM. The potent antimutagenicity of the extract was correlated with the high content (3.95±0.43%, dry weight) of baicalein. Baicalein acted as a desmutagen since it inhibited the N-hydroxylation of Trp-P-2. (Nakahara et al., 2001)


Ethnobotany:

·        Medicine for antidote to snake bite (root, Maheshwari, et. al., 1986); body chestem pain (Stem bark, Jain, 1965), Gupta, 1981); cholera, colic (part not mentioned,  Jain and Tarafder, 1970); diarrhoea (root bark, Nagendra and Abraham, 1984, Shah, et. al., 1983, Baruah et. al., 1987, part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); dysentery (root bark, Nagendra and Abraham, 1984, Shah, et. al., 1983, Baruah et. al., 1987; root, Maheshwari, et. al., 1986); dyspepsia  dysur, epilepsy,  fever (part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); headache (Leaf, Baruah et. al., 1987); induce conception (root bark, Tarafder, 1983, Saxena, et. al., 1981); jaundice (stem bark, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987, Gopal and Shah,  Hemadri and Rao, 1984);  madness (part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); miscarriage (root bark, Tarafder, 1984); oedema (stem bark Joshi et al 1980); piles (seed, Saxena, 1986); purgative (seed, Baruah et. al., 1987); pimples, small-pox, snake bite, sores, spleen complaint (part not mentioned,  Jain and Tarafder, 1970); scabies (stem bark, Jain, et. al., 1973); stomachache (Baruah et. al., 1987, root, Maheshwari, et. al., 1986); tonic (root bark, Baruah et. al., 1987); urticaria {part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); vermicidal (stem bark, Bedi, 1978), Veterinary wounds (stem bark, Bedi, 1978, root bark, Nagendra and Abraham, 1984). Edible (fruit, Lal and Yadav, 1983, Bhargava, 1981):  vegetable (flower, Bhujel, et. al. 1984). Black dye (bark young fruit, Jain and Borthakur, 1980, Borthakur, 1981, Vishnu Mittre, et. al., 1984). Symbolic garlands of idols (seed, Mehra, et. al., 1975).
