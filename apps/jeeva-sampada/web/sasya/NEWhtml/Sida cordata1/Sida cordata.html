HABIT & HABITAT
It is herbaceous plant occuring in INDIA: In wastelands, humid and shady places upto 1500 m. Throughout. Pantropical. Bihar:  Jhumri Telaiya, Ghanghree, Katkamosandi- bad; Sirohi: Abu road. Very common in roadsides, waste places, gardens, scrub-jungles and forest edges.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbs, prostrate or ascending, up to 1 m high, branched throughout or mostly towards base, rarely rooting at nodes in trailing condition; stem petioles with scattered, spreading long simple and minute stellate hairs. Leaves ovate, 0.5-8 x 0.3-5.5 cm, shallowly cordate, sharp to a sharp apex with concurve sides, shallowly rounded toothed to saw toothed margins, 5-7 nerved at base, appressed simple and stellate hairy on both surfaces; petioles 0.8-3.0 cm long. Stipules 1-3 mm long linear, hairy.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers axillary, solitary, ultimately in few-flowered racemes or loose panicles; pedicles 0.8-2.5 cm long, slender, jointed above the middle. Calyx bell-shaped, 8-5 mm across, slightly accrescent, spreading hairy outside, erect, margin glabrous inside, 5-fid; lobes joint up to just above the middle, deltoid or triangular, 4-6 x 0.2 mm, sharp apex,  curve margin, prominantly uninerved, simple and some stellate-hairy outside, glabrous inside except along margin. Corolla yellow, c 10 mm across, Petals obovate, c 5 x 4 mm, ciliate at base, staminal column c 3 mm long, basal portion conical, tubular part-short hairless or with some short, simple hairs. Schizocarp globose, c 4 x 3 mm, enclosed in persistent calyx, brownish black; mericarps 5, tetrahedral with rounded angles, ownless, hairless or short-hairy at apex. Seeds ovoid, brown-black, hairless, 1.8-2 mm long; dispersed by withering of walls.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plants are useful in asthma, boils, bone fractures, dysentery, gonorrhoea, leucorrhoea, piles, urine complaints, nerval and venereal diseases. Stems, leaves and root-barks are used in fever and urinary complaints. Root bark is used in leucorrhoex, micturition and gonorrhoea. Leaves in form of poultice are applied to cuts and bruises
Chemistry: N-methyly-ephedrine, N-methylephedrine, b-phenethylamine, ephedrine, y-ephedrine, vasicinol, vascinone, vascine, choline and betaine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).
Cholesterol and stigmasterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany: Medicine for asthma (Whole plant, Rajwar, 1983); Boils (Whole plant, Srivastava and Varma, 1981); Bone fracture (Whole plant, Sharma and Malhotra, 1984); cuts, wounds (Leaf, Singh and Pandey, 1980); Debility (Root, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Dysentery (Whole plant, Singh and Maheshwari, 1983; Rajwar, 1983); Gonorrhoea and venereal disease (Whole plant, Singh and Maheshwari, 1983; Rajwar, 1983); Impotency (Whole plant, Singh and Pandey, 1980); Leucorrhoea, nerve disease, piles, urinary complaints (Whole plant, Rajwar, 1983); Tonic (Root, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Veterinary galactogogue (Whole plant, Sebastian, 1984); Fibre (Part not mentioned, Goel et al, 1984)
