DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Subshrubs, erect, ca. 1 m tall; branchlets densely stellato-pilose and velutinous, hairs 3 mm. Stipule filiform, ca. 5 mm, densely stellato-pilose; petiole 1-2.5 cm, densely stellato-pilose and velutinous; leaf blade ovate, 1.5-5 ´ 1-4 cm, densely stellato-pilose, velutinous on veins abaxially, base minutely cordate or round, margin crenate apex obtuse to round. Flower solitary or fascicled in axils of leaves or at branch tip; pedicel 5-15 mm, densely stellato-pilose and with long hairs, articulate in upper part. Calyx cup-shaped, lobes 5, triangular, 5-6 mm, densely stellato-pilose and with long hairs. Corolla yellow, ca. 1.5 cm in diam.; petals oblong, 6-8 mm. Staminal column ca. 6 mm, hirsute. Fruit ca. 6-8 in diam., mericarpss 10, apically 2-awned, awn 3-4 mm, exceeding calyx, reversely hispid. Seeds long ovoid, apically hairy.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Palnt,Seeds, Roots, Root bark

Uses

  1. Seeds are aphrodisiac and are administered in gonorrhoea. Also given for colic and tenesmus in Punjab.
  2. Roor is cooling, astringent and tonic, and a decoction combined with ginger is given in intermittent fevers. 
  3. The root is combined with asafoetida and rock salt is given for many nervous diseases such as hemiplegia and facial paralysis.
  4. Stong decoction is mixed with sesmum oil and milk, is used as an external application in facial paralysis.
  5. A powder of the root bark together with milk and sugar is given for relief of frequent mcturition and leucorrhoea.
