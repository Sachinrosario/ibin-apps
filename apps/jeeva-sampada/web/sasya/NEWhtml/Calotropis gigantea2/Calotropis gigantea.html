 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
 A much-branched, handy erect, woody shrub, 1-5 m. in height. It is common in plains of west Bengal, Punjab, Assam, South Indian, ascending up to 650 m. in the Himalaya


Availability :  
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant is common .

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem:- woody, round tender ones covered with soft, loosely appressed, whitish waxy or sometimes powdery pubescence. Bark: thick, light yellow or ash-grey, soft, corky, deeply fissured. Leaves: fleshy, cuneate-obovate or obovate oblong, with a narrow cordate or often amplex – icaule base, 10.0 – 20.0 cm. X 2.5 - 7.5 cm., smooth above cottony below. Flowers:- Lilac or pale rose or purple rarely light greenish, yellow or white, in simple or compound cymose-corymbs. Fruit:- follicles 2 or 1 fleshy, recurved, 7-10 cm. Long seeds brown, numerous, broadly ovate, flattened with 2.5 – 3.2 cm. Long, white tuft of silky hairs at the pointed end.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation:  Plant is propagated through seeds and vegetatively through root and shoot cuttings.

Soil type: Plant grows well in wastelands where nothing else can grow such as sandy barren lands.

Nutrient requirements: Plant does not have any specific nutrient requirements.

Water management: Plants grow well in dry areas and do not have any specific water requirements.

Harvesting period / Method: It is usually harvested during March-May.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Parts uses and uses:
Root bark: in dysentery; Latex: as purgative; Leaf: in swelling, in burns, in bodyache, skin diseases, in enlarged liver ; Root-powder (with country liquor): in leprotic wounds; Root-decoction (with paste of long peppers): in leucorrhoea; Root and Latex : in body pain, dropsy, as a purgative, febrifuge, in ansarca, ailments of spleen, carbuncle, cholera, convulsions, convulsions with tongue drawn in, dropsy, hemiplagia, hydrocele, lumbago, muscular pain, neuralgia, orchitis, osteomyelitis, palsy, pleurisy, pneumonia, post-natal complaints, protracted labour, rabies, rheumatism, ringworm, scabies, scores, strangulation of intestine, syphilis; Bark: in cholera, epilepsy, small pox, in boil, chest pain, dysentery, kala-azar, stings, toothache, in leprosy. Homeopathy: diaphoretic, expectorant, emetic and valuable remedy for skin diseases; aqueous and alcoholic extract caused marked contraction of isolated rat uterus; the contractions was not altered by atropine but were blocked by papaverine.

Modern Use: Plant: shows anticancer properties.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:
Latex contains proteases calotropins DI and DII, calotropains FI and FII.  Plant contains b-Sitosterol, bark – saponins and leaves and stems – sapogenius.  The root bark contains two isomeric alcohols – giganteol and iso-giganteol.

NBRI:
a-amyrin, b-amyrin, taraxasterol, y- taraxasterol and b-sitosterol were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Calotoxin, uscharin and calactin identified. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Two new triterpene esters-3’,-methylbutanoates of a-amyrin and y-taraxasterol  -- isolated from latex. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

A new flavanol trisaccharide – calotropiside (fig. 1) was isolated from aerial parts and its structure determined; taraxasteryl acetate, 3-O-glucoside and 3-O-rutinoside of isorhamnetin was also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of two new oxypregnane oligoglycosides, Calotroposides A (fig. 2a) and B (fig. 2b), from roots of Indonesian plant and their characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Indonesian plant afforded five new oxypregnane oligoglycosides – Calotroposides C (fig. 2c), D (fig. 2d), E (fig. 2e), F (fig. 2f) and G (fig. 2g) – from roots and their structures was elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Giganticine (fig. 3) a novel antifeedant protein amino acid has been isolated from a methanol extract of the root bark of Calotropis gigantea. (Pari et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 32352u.

Phytochemical studies on the roots of Calotropis gigantea resulted in the isolation of one new lupene type triterpene identified as lup-13(18),20-(29)-dien-9a-yl acetate and lupeol acetate and two ursane type triterpenes characteised as  urs-18bH-12,20(30)-dien-3b-yl acetate and 17b-hydroxy-28-norurs-18aH-12,20(30)-dien-3b-yl acetate on the basis of spectral data analysis and chemical reaction. (Ali and Gupta, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 33209x.


Pharmacology:
Leaves of Calotropis gigantea were extracted with ethylacetate and cytotoxic compound (CGE-1) was isolated as a white semi crystal ppt. The tumor as inhibiting activity of CGE-1 (ED50=< 0.09 mg/ml) was studied in rats. (Anand et al, 1998).

Three cardenolide, glycosides, calotropin, frugoside and 4-O-b-D-glucopyranosylfrugoside were obtained as the cytotoxic principles of roots of C. gigantea. (Kiuchi et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 278754s.
