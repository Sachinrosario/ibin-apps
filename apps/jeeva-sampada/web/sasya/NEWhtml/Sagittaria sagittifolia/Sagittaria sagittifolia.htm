HABIT & HABITAT
A common scapigerous plant with a stoloniferous rhizome occurring throughout India in tanks and ponds; A genus of perennial aquatic plants distributed mostly in temperate and tropical America with a few species in the Old World. Two species occur in India.
Generally it lies in Marshy Areas, marshy tracts, Nulls, Ponds, Puddles, rice fields, Rivers, Streams, Swamps,Swamps, ditches and riverbanks.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Emerged leaves sagittate with narrow divergent basal lobes; scape 15-45 cm. long with 3-S whorls of 3-5 flowers each, lower of female and upper of male flowers; petals white, often with a purple claw; achenes flattened, with broad, entire or sub crenate wings.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used:
Plant, Tubers.

Uses

  1. Tubers when eaten raw produce fluxes, weakness and haemorrhoids, and sometimes induce premature birth in women. 
  2. In North America the fleshy corms of this plant are used as an article of food by the native population & In China it is cultivated as food plant.

Chemistry
Analysis of the edible portion of the tubers gave the following values : moisture, 69-5; protein, 5-0; fat, 0-2; total carbohydrates, 23.7; fibre, 0-8; and ash, 1-6%; calcium, 16; phosphorus, 216; iron, 1-4; and ascorbic acid, 2 mg./l00 g. The plant is reported to contain 75-550 p.p.m. of manganese. Glucose, fructose and raffinose are present in the tubers. The plant is a good oxygenator and useful in ponds where fish are bred. It is also grown as an ornament. S. guayanensis H.B. & K. is an aquatic plant similar to the above species found in fresh water tanks and marshes throughout the country. It is said to become sometimes a troublesome weed in waters where fish farming is carried on and in such cases manual removal of the weed in July just before the peak period of flowering is recommended as a method of control. The plant is often ploughed in with other weeds in rice fields as green manure.

Other Uses
  1. A form of this species which is considered by some to be a separate species, S. sinensis Sims., is cultivated in China partly as fodder for pigs and partly for its globose starchy edible tubers which are produced on the ends of stolons.
    The plant is somewhat acrid and astringent and is used as a discutient and to check the flow of milk in nursing mothers. 
  2. It is also applied to ocdematous limbs. 
  3. Tubers when eaten raw produce fluxes, weakness and hemorrhoids, and sometimes induce premature birth in women. They are recommended for skin diseases and lochia, and mixed with vinegar as a poultice for boils and abscesses. 
  4. Leaves are acrid and are applied to foul sores and in dispersing scrofulous ulcers. They are said to be used as antidote to snake bite and insect bite, and as powder to relieve itching. Mashed with molasses, the leaves are used in sore-throat and inflammation of the breast.
