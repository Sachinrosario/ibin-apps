 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Small tree with small and few or no prickle; It is found in Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Kerala, Maharashtra.


Availability :  
Tamilnadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and West Bengal, usually as a cultivated hedge plant.
Specimen examined:  Uttar Pradesh: Forest colony, Dehra Dun, September 1913, s. legit, 30451 (DD); West Bengal: Kamarkundu, Hooghly dist., 11.03.1968, Subir Sen, s.n. (CAL); Kerala: Palamvi hill, Travancore dist., 10.09.1913, C.C. Calder & M.S. Ramaswami, 789 (CAL); Mahatrashtra: Empreece garden, Poona dist., August 1891, A.R. Lesley, 134211 (CAL).
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant is grown as ornamental.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves: pinnately compound, pinnae 8-12 pairs, leaflets close, sessile, oblong, oblique and falcate, 10-12 pairs. Flowers: yellow, racemes panicled. Pods: prominently beaked, polished, indeliscent seeds: 3-4.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary and terminal panicles ; Flower: bracteate, pedicillate, hermaphrodite ; Calyx: long, leathery, glabrous, imbricate ; Corolla: Petals orbicular, sub-equal, imbricate, yellow, the upper with red spot at the base ;Androecium: Stamens 10, declinate, equaling the petals, alternately long and short; filament, waxy-white, densely wooly at the base ; Gynoecium: Ovary grey-velvety, sesile, ovules few, style filiform, sometimes clavate at the apex, stigma terminal ; Fruit: Pods, woody, obliquely oblong, sub-compressed polished, indehiscent, with hard recurved short recurved beak at the upper angle of the obtuse apex; Seed:  Seeds 3-4.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : September-October .
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
 Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind, birds or animals.  
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 24.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
Decoction of wood – emmenagogue, anti-diarrhoeal, dysentery and in skin disease. EtOH(50%) extract of stem: semen coagulant, anticancer; heart wood (MeOH extract): antiinflamatory in rats: essential oil from leaves: antimicrobial.
In Ayurveda system of medicine wood is bitter and used in fever, delirium, ulcers, urinary concretions and blood complaints. In Unani system of medicine wood is used in chest and lungs bleeding and also used to cure wounds, ulcers and rheumatism


Chemistry:
NBPGR:

Heartwood: a-amylrin, aminoacids, b sitisterol, brasilin essintial oil, b-amyrin flucoside, aminoacids alanive, aspaltic acid, glycine, proline, valine, leveive and threomine and carbohydrates – galactose gorbose etc.
NBRI:
b-Amyrin glucosdie, alanine, asparatic acid, glycine, proline, valine, leucine, threonine, norvaline, lactose, glucose, galactose, sorbose, 4-methylgalactose, 3,4-dimethylgalactose and 2-deoxyribose from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Palmitic (27.62), stearic (44.15), linoleic (25.94) and oleic acids (2.82) in heartwood oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Caesalpin J (fig. 1) and Caesalpin P (fig. 2) isolated from heartwood and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Crystal structure of caesalpin J determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Two new aromatic compounds (I fig. 3 and J fig. 4) isolated from heartwood and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Protosaponin A isolated from heartwood and its structure determined and confirmed by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Isolation and structure elucidation of protosaponin B (fig. 6a) from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

A novel dibenzoxocin derivative (III fig. 6b) isolated from heartwood and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Octacosanol, b-sitosterol and taraxerol isolated from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)g.

Three new homoisoflavanoids – 7-hydroxy-3-(4’-hydroxybenzylidene)chroman-4-one (fig. 8), 3,7-dihydroxy-3-(4-hydroxybenzyl
)chroman-4-one (fig. 9a) and 3,7-trihydroxy-3-(4’-hydroxybenzyl)chroman (fig. 9b) – isolated from heartwood along with 4,4’-dihydroxy-2’-methoxychalcone, 8-methoxubonducellin, quercetin, rhamnetin and ombuin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)h.

Three new homoisoaflavonoids – 3’-O-methylsppanol (fig. 11a), 3’-O-methyl-episappanol (fig. 11b) and 3’-O-methylbrazilin (fig. 10) isolated from heartwood and absolute configurations of new compounds as well as sappanol, episappanol and 3’-deoxysappanol determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)i.

Protosaponin C (fig. 7) isolated from heartwood and characterized; its absolute configuration at C-7 determined as R. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)j.

An inseperable mixture of two new dibenz[b,d]oxocins – protosaponins E-1 (fig. 12a) and E-2 (fig. 12b) isolated from heartwood, chemical studies revealed that both protosaponins consists of Protosaponin C combined with 11b-hydroxybrazilin through acetal linkage and are stereoisomeric in respect of acetal carbon. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Caesalpin P, sappanchalcone, 3-deoxysappanone, protosappanone A, brazilin and two aromatic compounds (I and II) isolated as active ingredients from wood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Isolation of n-triacontane, lupeol, b-amyrin, b-sitosterol and stigmasterol from unsaponifiable matter (11.2%) of seed fixed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

The anticomplementary activity of compounds isolated from the heartwood of Caesalpinia sappan L. (Leguminosae) was investigated. The sterol mixture (campesterol 11.2%, stigmasterol 18.9% and beta-sitosterol 69.9%) was most potent and brazilin, brazilein, and protosappanin E showed a new anticomplementary activity on the complement system in vitro. (Oh et al, 1998).


Pharmacology:
Compunds I and II possessed antihypercholesterolaemic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)k.

Protosaponin A showed weak sedative effect at 42.0 mg/kg, i.p., in mice induced by hexobarbital. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)l.

Sappanchalcone (10.0 mM) inhibited aldose reductase (83.9%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Methanolic extract of Caesalpinia sappan inhibited more than 70% of PEP activity at concentration of 40ppm. (Lee et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 70669q.

Methanolic extract and two purified compounds (brazilin and hematoxylin) from Caesalpinia sappan may be responsible for the vascular relaxtant effects of C. sappan, via endogenousNO and subsequent cGMP formation. The vascular relaxtant effects of the plant may contribute to its therapeutic actions. (Xie et al, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 329320k.

80% aqueous MeOH extracts from the wood of Caesalpinia sappan which showed remarkable anticonvulsant activity, were fractionated using EtOH, n-BuOH and H2O. Among them EtOAc fraction significantly inhibited yhe activities of two 9ABA degradative enzymes, Succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase and succinic semialdehyde reductase (SSAR). Repeated column chromatography for the fracton guided by activity test led to the isolation of the two active principal components, structures were determined to be Sappanchalcone. (Baek et al, 2000). C.A 2000, 133, 305543j.
