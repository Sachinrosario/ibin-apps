HABIT & HABITAT
Large deciduous tree, found in moist evergreen forests of eastern India, on the west coast in Karnataka and Kerala, and in the Tamil Nadu, up to an altitude of 1600 m in the western ghats and in the Andaman islands. Sometimes planted for shade in coffee plantation in Karnataka.

Naturally found in evergreen forests.
It grows in regions of moderate to high rainfall and at moderate elevations of 500m.

 

Back to Top



Availability :  
The tree is found scattered in the evergreen forest of W.Ghats of India.  It is also found in Assam and Meghalaya.  Nothing very clearly indicates its preference for a locality.  Common about Coutrallum in the Tirnelvelly district.  Within India, it occurs in the states of Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Maharashtra, Goa, Rajasthan, Tripura, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.  In Karnataka, the districts of Hassan, Chickmagalur, Coorg, Mysore, Shimoga, North Kanara and Kodagu have reported its presence.  It has been recorded in the Cannanore, Palakkad, Trivandrum, Thiruvanthapuram and Thrissur districts of Kerala and coimbatore, Madurai, Niligiri, Salem and Tirunelveli districts of Tamil Nadu.

In India, it is found in western ghats, shevoroys(Endemic) and in Western ghats, it is found all over the hills, <19 degree north, <1200m.  
  
Globally the species is recorded in India, Srilanka, upper Myanmar (earlier Burma) and Bangladesh.  

Status : 
The Red List Status of this species has been assessed as "Vulnerable" for the three states of Southern India i.e. Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.  Moderately threatened for western ghats, since people burn trees for obtaining large amount of resin in short time in silent valley area.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A tall handsome, evergreen buttressed tree, up to 50 m tall with a straight, white, cylindrical stem; bark hard, light greenish-gray, thin, rough, somewhat aromatic, exfoliating yielding a black resin  when cut.  The undersurfaces of both the twigs and leaflets are covered with reddish hairs. The young foliage is bright yellow, later  turning crimson. Leaves alternate , large 0.6-1.2 m long, impari-pinnate; leaflets 7-15, opposite or alternate, ovate-oblong, ovate-lanceolate or unequel-sided,10-20 cm long and 4-10 cm wide, apex acuminate, margin finely serrate, lateral nerves 10-15 pairs, very prominent beneath, petioles 2.5-8.0 cm long .Flowers small, white or bright yellow, faintly fragrant in terminal or axillary panicles shorter  than the leaves, male and female flowers separate, pedicel very short, calyx usually 4-lobed, cupular, rusty tomentose outside, tube 3 mm long, lobes triangular, 1 mm long; petals 6 cm long and 2 cm broad, oblong-obovate, apex rounded. Fruit (drupes) ellipsoid or obovoid, 3.8-5.0 cm long, resinous with a hard stone.

Breeding System :

Phenology :

Leaf fall: December to January
Flowering: January-March

Fruiting : November-January  Almost all the trees bears fruits once in 2-3year.  


Pollinators:

Seed Biology :
The fruit can be collected from the tree or from the ground.  The fruit has very hard seed coat.  The seed can be stored for one year.  However it is not known whether it will loose viability.  About 120-160 seeds weigh one kg.  Germination starts in about 2 months and is over within 4 months. Germination percentage is 50-80%

Back to Top

 


Seedling Germination :

Propagation by seeds.
Fruits are soaked in water for 2 to 4 days and thoroughly washed to remove the pericarp.  It is desirable to dibble the seed in polybags which are kept under shade and water regularly.  Germination starts in 2 months and is complete in 4 months.  50 80% germination is generally obtained.  Close spacing is not necessary due to its fast rate of growth and ability to clean its bole. The seedlings remain in the cotyledonary leaf stage for 2 to 3 weeks. It grows to a height of 2 to 2.5m in the first year itself.  .  The seedlings require shade during summer months.  They will be available for planting during the next season after 12-14months. 

Cultivation:
The development of root is fast hence transplanting of bare root seedlings is only partly successful.  The seedlings raised in polybags may be planted in pits.  The species requires lateral shade in the initial stages, particularly during summer months.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Bark

Uses
Black dammar: It hardens into a translucent stalactic mass having a bright shining colour and breaking with a glassy fracture.  The resin can easily be ground into a light, dust coloured powder.  

  • It is used in the manufacture of varnishes, bottling wax and as a substitute for Burgundy pitch in plasters.  
  • Black dammar is used as an incense and illuminant. Mixed with fish oil, it is used for caulking boats and preserving fishing lines; dissolved in turpentine, it makes a good dark-coloured varnish.  
  • The resin also possess some medicinal properties. It is used in treating skin diseases such as psoriasis and pityriasis. As a liniment with sesame oil is used to soothe rheumatic pains. As a decoction or in powered form, the resin  is used to treat rheumatism, fever, cough, epilepsy and asthma.  


Harvesting Practices

Black dammar is obtained by making vertical cuts in the bark and setting fire to the bottom of the stem.  This result is effected by lighting firewood piled to the height of a yard round the base of the trunk.  The dammar exudes from the stem as high as the flames reached commencing about two years after the above operation.  The flow is said to continue for ten years. On exudation, the resin hardens to a bright shining black coloured mass which breaks with a glassy fracture.

Chemistry
The resin occurs in stalactitic masses of a bright shining colour when viewed en masse, but translucent and of a deep reddish-brown colour when held between the eye and the light; homogeneous, with a vitreous fracture; partially soluble in boiling alcohol and completely so in oil of turpentine.   Its colour when in solution is pale, if compared with its dark tint when in mass.  Thus, though insoluble in spirit, its solution in turpentine forms a tolerable varnish.  When submitted to dry distillation, it yields  about6-7% of combustible gas containing methane, ammonia and carbon dioxide. It is partly soluble in alcohol and acetone and completely soluble in benzene and turpentine.

Processing Techniques
Resin reaches the market in the form of black, brittle, nodular lumps which can be easily ground to a light dust-coloured powder

Other Uses
  1. Wood is moderately durable and can be worked easily.  Used as boards for ceiling, flooring and partitions.  As guides or runners in down cast shafts.  Since the wood has a glue holding capacity, it is used as plywood tea-boxes.
