HABIT & HABITAT:

Maharukh is a large, deciduous tree, indigenous to central and southern India and found throughout Madhya Pradesh in Broach and Panchmal districts in Gujarat, some coastal districts in Andhra Pradesh and Panchamal districts in Gujarat some coastal districts in Andhra Pradesh and Ganjam and Puri districts in Orissa.(1)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Maharukh is a fast growing tree with a height upto 24m in height and 2.5m in girth with a straight cylindrical bole. The bark is light grey and smooth in young trees with large leaf scars, rough, granular and greyish brown in older trees; leaves pinnately compound upto 90 cm long with 8-14 pairs of leaflets. Flowers are small, yellowish in panicles; fruits one-seeded samara.(1)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Ovules are hemianatropous. The vascular strand extend beyond the chalaza. Integuments are separated by a space in chalazal region, micropyle is zigzag.(2)

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:
It is controlled by Atteva fabrciella Swed., a defoliator almost round the year. Spraying with Anthio, Chlordimeform, BHC, DDT and Endrin dust over the foliage usually controls the pest. Diboma procera Pascoe, a borer, is reported to infest drying twigs or branches after heavy defoliation by A. fabriciella, but does not attack the main stem.(1)

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
WOOD - The wood is white, soft, light but fairly strong. It is perishable in the open but not so in water, it is susceptible to white ant and insect attack. Because of its lightness, it is used especially for making catamarans and small boats. It is also used for veneers and plywood packing cases, sword handles, spear-sheaths, toys and poor quality matchwood.

Also a source of writing and printing papers.(1)

MEDICINAL - The bark is bitter, astringent, febrifuge and anthelmintic. It has anti-spasmodic and expectorant properties and is used for asthma, bronchiis and dysentry. It is also used for dyspepsia and earache, and has antiseptic properties. The bark is a good substitute for Kurchi (Holarrhena antidysenterica Wall.) bark, and is used in indegenous veterinary practices.

Quassinoids separated from the root bark have shown substantial anti-tumour and cytotoxic activities against the P388 lymphocytotic leukemia and KB test systems, respectively.(1)

Parts Used:

Barks, leaves, wood, twigs.


Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:
Bassora or Hog gum, paper, feeds, wooden articles (toys, small boats, matchwood), Quassinoids (1).

Chemistry:
The proximate chemical analysis of the wood gave cellulose, 51.6; pentosans, 14.3, lignin, 30.1; and ash 2.1%.

The bark contains quassinoids, ailantic acid, 2,6-dimethoxybenzoquinone, B-sitosterol, malanthin; triacentane and hexatriacontane(1).

Processing Techniques:
Chemical pulps, suitable for writing and printing papers, can be prepared from the wood by sulphate process. Easy bleaching pulp with satisfactory strength properties can be prepared under suitable conditions of digestion. A excelsa bring short-fiberd, admixture with long-fiberd pulp, such as bamboo pulp, is necessary for the manufacture of paper on a commercial paper machine(1).
Other uses:
The tree yields an inferior quality of Bassora or Hog gum. It serves as one of the hosts 'Eri' silkworms, and as a bee pasturage. The leaves along with the twigs are found to be a suitable fodder for catlte, sheep and goats. The leaves when fed to rams over 4-7 weeks resulted in toxic manifestations which included grunting, spasmodic bleats, icteric conjunctiva and blood-stained faeces (1).
