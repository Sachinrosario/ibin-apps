DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Inflorescence:Compound generally from the center of the axis, terminal; Flower:  Fleshy, each with rough bract, pale blue with sheen of faint purple; Calyx:  Sepals 3, wide acuminate, closely imbricate; Corolla: Petals 3, erect, imbricate, adnate below thw filaments Androecium:Stamens 6, 3 alternating with petal, filaments free, 3 opposite to petals ;Gynoecium: Carpels 3, connate in an inferior, 3-celled ovary, style filiform, stigma linear; Fruit: Aggregation of fruits form sorosis called eyes, shell dark brown, orange yellow when ripe; Seed: Seeds few ovoid, compressed. 
   
Floral and seed biology:

Flowering & Fruiting: July-october. 

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects and seeds are dispersed by wind.

 
Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 50,75,100

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through suckers, stips and crowns.

Soil Type: It flourishes well in wide variety of soil from sandy to clayey and also gravely soil, provided they possess good drainage, aeration,low percentage of lime and pH ranging from 4.5-5.4.

Irrigation: Plant requires well distributed rainfall of 100-150 cm throughout the year.  Although it is drought resistant but watering once in 10-12 days in summers and 15-20 days in winters is required.

Nutrient requirements:  Plant requires bulky of organic manure, application of neem cake, bone meal, wood ash and NPK fertilizers are recommended.

Harvesting:Fruits are harvested when they are mature, soft, less angular and greenish-yellow.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:
Ergosterol peroxide (fig. 1), 5-stigmastene-3b,7a-diol isolated from leaves besides b-sitosterol, campesterol, stigmastanol and campestanol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

5-hydroxytryptamine from leaves of crown of pineapple fruit. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Method for isolation of biologically active (anti-inflammatory, haemolytic) peptides from roots and juice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Ethyl acetate and ethyl-3-(methylthio)propanoate in volatile oil of green pineapple and ethyl acetate and butane-2,3-diol diacetate in that from ripe pineapple, identified by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Purified enzyme, bromelain, from Korean plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Cinnamic, p-coumaric and ferulic acids were isolated from pineapple stems (Ananas comosus var. Cayenne). Twenty-four kinds of esters were prepared from these acids, alcohols. Isopropyl 4-hydroxycinnamate and butyl 4-hydroxycinnamate were found to have almost the same effectiveness in antifungal activity against Pythium sp. at 10 ppm. (Tawata et al., 1996).

Phenolic compounds were found in pineapple (Ananas comosus L. Merill) juice in substantial concentrations and were isolated by semipreparative reverse phase HPLC. The structures were elucidated from UV spectra, acid hydrolysis, and subsequent amino acid analysis, mass spectrometry, and two-dimensional NMR spectroscopy. The compounds are identified as S-sinapyl-L-cysteine, N-L-[Gamma]-glutamyl-S-sinapyl- L-cysteine, and S-sinapylglutathione. (Wen et al., 1999).

Glucuronoarabinoxylans are the major non-cellulosic polysaccharides in the pineapple cell walls. (Smith and Harris, 2001).

GC/FID and GC/MS study showed that among the 47 newly detected volatile constituents, five free phenolic compounds, four sulfur compounds and a family of furanoid compounds were observed (Teai et al., 2001 )

Pharmacology:

Ananas cosmosus showed diuretic activity. (Sripanidkulchai et al., 2001)

Bromelain, an extract of stem, inhibited growth of Lewis lung carcinoma, YC-8lymphoma and MCA-1 ascites tumor cells in vitro cytotoxic effects abolished by heating at 100°C or by addition of aspirin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for abortifacient (Fruit, Maji and Sikdar, 1982; Rao and Jamir, 1982; Leaf, Tarafder, 1983); Anthelmintic (Leaf, Bhattacharjee et al, 1980); Edible (Fruit all over India)
