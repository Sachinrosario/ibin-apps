HABIT & HABITAT
A moderately-sized, much-branched deciduous tree, with  a crooked bole  up  to  8-10'  and a girth of 2-3'.  It is found throughout the drier regions of south India and Ceylon, and is also met with in a few localities in the Central Provinces. It is sometimes grown as an avenue tree. It is usually found places like at lower elevations in the hills, common, dry deciduous forest, Dry forest, dry mixed deciduous forests, Forests, Hill forests, Mixed Deciduous Forest, moist deciduous forest, Scrub forests, Scrub jungles, Slopes, villages,

Altitude: 400-1 500 m, Mean annual temperature: 10-47 deg. C, Mean annual rainfall: 400-1 000 mm.
Soil type: Tolerates clays and is often found near streams, where it can reach more water.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
The wood is heavy, straight-grained and medium textured (sp. gr., 0-84 ; air-dry wt., 54 Ib.  per c. ft.), sapwood is white, and heartwood is light brown, often with a purplish cast  mottled with dark and light shades.  Anatomically it is featured by inconspicuous growth rings, fairly large pores, surrounded or. Occasionally bridged  by parenchyma and very fine rays, which form a faint fleck on the radial surface.
 Albizia amara is a small to moderate-sized, much-branched deciduous tree with smooth, dark green, scaly bark. It resembles the acacias but lacks thorns. Its root system is shallow and spreading.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Flowers pedicelled, yellow, fragrant, in 12-20 globose heads. Fruits are oblong pods, about 10-28 x 2-5 cm, light brown, puberulous, thin, and 6-8 seeded; seeds flattened, 8-13 x 7-8 mm. The yellow or pinkish-white, fragrant flower heads appear mainly in the rainy season, and the fruits ripen in the cold season

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination :
Natural reproduction by seed is good in areas protected from fire and grazing. It reproduces very freely from coppice; it throws a large number of coppice shoots in the first instance, producing as many a 50-100 shoots.

Artificially, the best method is through direct seeding. Seed pretreatment involves immersion in boiling water for 5 minutes followed by soaking for 12 hours. The treated seed can be then sown and will germinate within 7-10 days. Germination is about 80%.

Cultivation:
Spacing generally adopted is 9-10 m apart along contour lines; plants are thinned when 2-3 m high in the first year and 5-8 m in the third or fourth year depending on the rate of growth. Young seedlings should be protected from fire and grazing livestock. Though natural thinning is universal, the best shoots left in an unthinned stump are in no way inferior to those on a thinned stump. On this account, therefore, yield of firewood is likely to be greater at the end of the rotation.

Other Uses:
  1. The tree is cultivated in S. Travancore chiefly for green manure.  
  2. The timber is seasons well .It should be converted green and open-steaked under cover. It works to a fine, smooth surface and takes polish well. It is very strong and moderately hard is therefore useful in making tool handles, mallet heads break blocks etc. It is commonly used for carving turnery and agricultural implements. It is first class cabinet making wood.The leaves are said to be used for adulterating tea.According to Bourdillon the trees are cultivated in S. Travancore chiefly for green manure.
  3. Erosion control: Its spreading root system makes it a good soil binder, deterring soil erosion.
  4. Shade or shelter: Albizias are popular as shade trees for tea and coffee plantations.
  5. Reclamation: This is a very good species for afforestation of degraded hilly areas in dry and semi-arid tracts in mixture with other species such as Acacia catechu, A. planifrons, Anogeissus latifolia and Azadirachta indica.
  6. Ornamental: Albizia amara can be planted in urban areas as an ornamental and avenue tree.
  7. Intercropping: In India, Indonesia and other countries, it is usually incorporated into smallholding, rainfed agriculture and diversified with corn, cassava and fruit trees such as papaya, mango and orange.
  8. Fuel: The branches are suited to both firewood and charcoal.
  9. Timber: The wood from this species is darkish in colour, fine grained and hard. It can therefore be used for furniture making, agricultural implements and construction.
  10. Tannin or dyestuff: Tannins may be obtained from the bark.
  11. Poison: It is alleged that the seeds are poisonous.
  12. Medicine: The tree yields a gum used against ulcers; fruits are said to cure malaria and coughs.
  13. Other products: It is reported that soap can be made from the roots and leaves can be used for washing hair.
