DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 A perennial herb with an erect stout rootstock. leaves alternate, radical, 2.5-12.5 cm. long, of variable width, ovate or ovate-oblong, obtuse or subacute, entire or toothed, nearly glabrous, base tapering and decurrent into the petiole 3-7 (commonly 5) nerved; petioles usually longer than the leaf blade, broad, sheathing at the base.  Flowers scattered or crowded in long slender rather lax spikes 5-15 cm, long; bracts 1.5-2mm. long, shorter than the calyx, broadly ovate-oblong, obtuse, with scarious margins.  Calyx 3mm long, glabrous; sepals oblong, obtuse or subacute, obtusely keeled on the back and with broad scarious margins.  Corolla 4 mm. long, glabrous, lobes lanceolate, acute, reflexed.  Capsules ovoid, 3-4 mm. long, glabrous, dehiscing circumscisilely a little above the base, the top coming off as a conical lid tipped with the remains of the style.  Seeds 4-8, angled, rugulose, dullblack, 0.85 mm. long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used:
 The leaves and roots are astringent and used in fevers, leaves are cooling, alterative, diuretic, diarrhoea, piles, wounds, toothache, malaria, earache.  Seeds-diarrhoea, dysentery, whooping cough.


Chemistry:

 Baicalein, baicalin, scutellarein, luteolin, chlorogenic and neochlorogenic acids isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Apigenin, luteolin-7-O-b-D-glucoside and luteolin-7-O-b-D-glucuronide isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Aucuboside present in leaves and seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
9-hydroxy-cis-11-octadecanoic acid (an isomer of ricinoleic acid) which constituted 1.5% of seed oil, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Two new iridoid glucosides - 3,4-dihydroaucubin and 6'-O-b-glucosylaucubin - isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.
C26, C28 and C30 primary alcohols isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.
A phenylpropanoid glycoside - plantamajoside (fig. 1) isolated and characterized as 3,4-dihydroxy-b-phenethy-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-4-O-caffeoyl-b-D-glucopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Oleanolic acid, acetoside, luteolin, its 7-O-glucoside, 6-hydroxyluteolin and its 7-O-glucoside, b-sitosterol, campesterol and stigmasterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Four new iridoid glycosides - plantarenaloside (fig. 2b), ixoroside (fig. 2a), asperuloside (fig. 3) and melampyroside (fig. 4) isolated and characterized; aucubin also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
PMII isolated from the leaves of Plantago major is a pectin type polysaccharide with anti-complementary activity. (Samsuelsen et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 72605u.
Low amounts of erucic acid were found in Plantago major - 3.45%. (Guil et al, 1997). MAPA 9803-1770.
The composition of the leaf cuticular waxes of Plantago major was determined. The major components of leaf wax of Plantago major are the free polar triterpene acids, oleanolic and ursolic acid, and the linear alkanes C27H56-C33H58. (Bakker et al, 1998). MAPA 9905-3245.
The seed mucilage of Plantago major contains acidic heteroxylan polysaccharides. For further structural analysis, oligosaccharides were generated by partial acid hydrolysis and then isolated byhigh pH anion-exchange chromatography (HPAEC). Each HPEAC fraction was shown by ESMS to contain one major oligosaccharide and several minor components. Partial structures of the oligosaccharides were determined by using GC-MS, ESMS and ES tandem mass spectrometry (ESMS/MS). A (1à4) - linked xylan trisaccharide and (1à3) - linked xylan oligosaccharides with DP 6-11 suggested that the backbone of the heteroxylan polysaccharide consisted of blocks of (1à4) - linked and (1à3) linked Xylp residues. A (1à2) - linked Xylp disaccharide and a branched tetrasaccharide were also found, revealing that single Xylp residues are linked to the O-2 of some of the (1à4) - linked Xylp residues in the backbone. In addition results confirm the presence of of side chains consisting of the disaccharide GlcpA - (1à3) - Araf. (Samuelsen et al, 1999).
NBPGR:  Seeds contain holoside planteose; leaves contain glucoside, saponins, bitter compounds;  10% oinment of powdered leaves in Vaseline quickly stops pus formation in impetigo.

Pharmacology:  

A preparation of flavanoids, saponins, steroids and alkaloids showed anti-inflammatory activity in rats and strengthened capillary vessels. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.
Primary alcohols, C26-C30, showed wound-healing effect in rabbits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.
Aucubin, having liver protective effect, was shown to be antidote for fatal mushroom poisoning caused by Amantia phalloides. It exhibited low toxicity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.
Plantamajoside showed antibacterial activity against Escherichia coli and Staphylococcis aureus. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Biological activities of components of Plantago major were studied for their antibacterial activity, enzyme inhibitory activities of cAMP phosphodiesterase and 5-lipoxygenase, anti-histamine release activity from mast cells, anti-allergic effect, anti-inflammatory effect, antioxidative activity, inhibitory effect on acetic acid induced-writhing in mice and anti-HIV effect. The result suggest that the therapeutic effects of Plantago herb might be mainly ascribed to the biological activities of the major components, plantamajoside, acetoside, plantaginin, aucubin and catalpol etc. (Nishibe and Murai, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 82136q.
Extracts of plantago major is used for various immune disorders and are applied either topically or taken orally as a herbal tea. (Basaran et al, 1996).
The extracts of Plantago major used for various disorders in Anatolia were investigated in vitro for their neutrophil chemotactic and intracellular killing activities. (Basaran et al, 1997). MAPA 9802-0779.
Aqueous extract of Plantago major showed effective antiinflamatory and analgesic activity. (Nunez et al, 1997). MAPA 9803-1565.
Extract of Plantago major was found to have antioxidant activity. (Bol'shakova et al, 1998).
A hexane extract of the entire plant Plantago major investigated by bioactivity-guided fractionation, using an in vitro cycloxygenase-2(COX-2) catalyzed prostlandin biosynthesis inhibition assay. The isolated diterpenoid, ursolic acid (1) showed significant COX-2 inhibitory effect, directly on the enzyme activity. The structural isomer oleanolic acid (2) was found to be less active than 1,but showed a similar COX-2/COX-1 selectivity ratio. The triterpenoid, 18b-glycyrrhetenic acid showed no significant inhibition on COX-2 or COX-1.the direct inhibitory effect of 1 and 2 on COX-2 catalyzed prostaglandin biosynthesis increased with preincubation, indicating a time-dependent inhibition, while the effect on COX-1 was found to be independent of preincubation time. (Ringbom et al, 1998). MAPA 9902-0839.
Plantago major leaves have been associated with various biological properties ranging from antiinflammatory, antimicrobial and antitumor to wound healing. Result found that endotoxin-free methanol extracts from Plantago major leaves at doses 50, 100, 250 and 500 microg/ml, were associated with 4.4+/- 1, 6 +/- 1, 12 +/- 0.4 and 18 +/- 0.4 fold increases of nitric ooxide (NO) production and increased TNF-a production (621 +/- 31, 721 +/- 36, 727 +/- 36 and 1.56 +/- 52 U/mL respectively) by rat peritoneal macrophages in the absenc of 1FN-gamma or LPS. NO and TNF a-production by untreated macrophages was negligible. In addition, Plantago major extracts potentiated C on A - induced lymphoproliferation (3- to 12-fold increases) in a dose-dependent fashion, compared with the effect of C on A alone. The regulation of parameters induced by plant extracts may be clinically relevant in numerous diseases including chronic viral infections, tuberculosis, AIDS and cancer. (Gomez-Flores et al, 2000).
Several natural fatty acids from Plantago major and as well as all of the semi-synthetic thioester containing fatty acids inhibited COX-2-catalyzed prostaglandin biosynthesis where a-LNA and compound all-(Z)5-thia-8,11,14,17-eicosatetracnoic acid showed COX-2. (Ringbom et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-06-3203.
Alcoholoic extract of Plantago major leaves inhibited IgE-dependent histamine release from RBL-2H3 cells. The inhibitory effects were found to be more than 80% for extract concentrations of 0.5 mg/ml. The results indicated that the extracts contain active compounds that inhibit mast-cell degranulation and provide insight into the development of new drugs for treating asthma and/or disease. (Ikawati et al, 2001). MAPA 2001-06-3141.
Plantago major presented some degree of antibacterial activity. (Holetz et al., 2002).
Plantago major have been used as folk medicine in Canada and was evaluated for their anti-hepatoma activity on the five human liver-cell lines i.e. HepG2/C3A, SK-HEP-1, HA22T/VGH, Hep3B and PLC/PRF/5. The samples were examined by in vitro evaluation for their cytotoxicity. The result showed that the effects of crude drugs on hepatitis B virus genome - containing cell lines were different from those against non hepatitis-B virus genome containing cell-line. (Lin et al., 2002).
Plantago major was found to inhibit bovine viral diarrhoea virus (BVDV) replication. (Ruffa et al., 2002).
Aqueous extract of Plantago major possessed only a slight anti-herpes virus activity. In contrast, certain pure cojmpounds belonging to the five different classes of chemicals found in extracts of this plant exhibited potent antiviral activity. Among them caffeic acid exhibted the strongest activity against HSV-1 (EC50 = 15.3 microg/ml, Sl = 671), HSV-2 (EC50 = 87.3 microg.ml, Sl = 118) and ADV-3 (EC50 = 14.2 microg/ml, Sl = 727), whereas chlorogenic acid possessed the strongest anti-ADV-11 (EC50 = 13.3 microg/ml, Sl = 301) activity. Study concludes that pure compounds of Plantago major, which possess antiviral activity are mainly derived from the phenolic compounds, especially caffeic acid. Its mode of action against HSV-2 and ADV-3 was found to be at multiplication stages (postinfection of HSV-1; 0-12 h; ADV-3; 0-2h), and with Sl values greater than 400, suggesting the potential use of this compound for treatment of the infection by these two viruses. (Chiang et al, 2002).
Methanolic extract from Plantago major showed cytotoxic activity against a human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line. (Ruffa et al., 2002).


Ethnobotany: 

Medicine for burns (Whole plant, Rao, 1981); Constipation (Seed, Gaur et al, 1983); Cuts and wounds as haemostat (Leaf + Twig, Rao and Jamir, 1982; Kumar and Rao, 1987); Diarrhoea, dysentery (Seed, Maheshwari and Singh, 1984, Gaur et al, 1983); Fever (Seed, Rawat and Pangtey, 1987); Inflammation (Whole plant, Tiwari et al, 1979); Weakness (Seed, Rawat and Pangtey, 1987); edible as vegetable (Whole plant, Singh and Singh, 1985); Cutaneous leishmaniasis (Franca et al., 1996); Wound healing (Leaf, Samuelsen, 2000)

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:  

Nutritional, antinutritional and toxic components were determined in Plantago major which is collected for human consumption in South east spain. (Guil et al, 1996).
