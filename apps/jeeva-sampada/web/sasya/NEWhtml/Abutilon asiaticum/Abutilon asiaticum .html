 Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
A tomentose annual herb frequent along roadsides, banks of rivers in the vicinity of the villages. chiefly met with in India and Ceylon, along road sides and river banks. In India it is found in East Coast, Peninsular India and Western peninsular India. A small bush found in north-western India, Sindh and Kashmir. It occurs naturally from the Mediterranean to China, and as an introduced plant in the warmer parts of the United States, where it grows wild, as if it were a native plant.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Plant

Uses
Stem contains a good fibre suitable for cordage.

Harvesting Practices
After planting the plants are harvested 4-5 months and the fibres are easily separated from the stems by retting them in water for 4-5 days. They can be dyed easily and are much used in China in rug - making.

Produce and Products

Back to Top

Chemistry: 
The plant seeds contain fatty oil 15.5%, and crude protein, 23.6%. The dried plant gave crud fiber, 23%. The plants of Indian mallow cultivated in Manchuria contain: Total cellulose, 70.5 and a-cellulose, 50.6 per cent.

Back to Top

Processing Techniques :
Stems are allowed for rotting in water to remove fibre.

Other Uses
It is the chiefly fiber- yielding species and the fiber called ' China jute' or ' Tientsin Jute' has long been a commercial product of Manchuria exported from Tientsin.
