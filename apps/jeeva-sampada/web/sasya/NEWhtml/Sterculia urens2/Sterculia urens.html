DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Trees, up to 8 m tall; young parts more or less pubescent; trunk straight; bark white, smooth, outer papery peeling off, inner fibrous. Leaves simple, 40 x 40 cm, palmately 3-5 lobed, cordate at base, caudate-acuminate at apex, entire at margin, pubescent to tomentose beneath, 6-nerved from the base; petioles 8.0-9.5 cm long, terete, tomentose; stipules caducous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers numerous, small yellow, mixed in much branched, glandular-pubescent, terminal panicles, appearing before the leaves at the ends of branchlets, 5-9 mm across. Bracts lanceolate, deciduous. Calyx campanulate, c 5 mm long, tube as long as the lobes; lobes spreading, oblong-lanceolate, acute, glandular hairy inside at base. Follicles 5, spreading, usually oblong or ovoid-oblong, 3-4 x 1.0-1.5 cm, densely pubescent, often mixed with stinging hairs. Seeds 3-6 in each follicle, oblong, c 7 x 5 mm, black, glossy.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Plants are used in blood dysentery, labour and sores. Roots are useful in bone dislocation and fractures. Gum s are used as tonic and also against blisters, joint pain, dysentery.


Chemistry:

·        Stercurensin isolated from leaves and characterized as 2’, 4’-dihydroxy-6’-methoxy-3’-methylchalcone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Leaves afforded two new flavonol glycosides characterized as quercetin-3-O-(6”-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl)-b-D-glucoside and kaempferol-3-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl)-b-D-glucoside; in addition, b-amyrin, its 3-acetyl derivative, b-sitosterol and an ester of terephthalic acid isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
