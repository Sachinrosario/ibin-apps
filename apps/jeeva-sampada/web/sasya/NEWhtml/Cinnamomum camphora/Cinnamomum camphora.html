DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Much branched, glabrous, perennial trees. Leaves  alternate, coriaceous, ovate to oblong or lanceolate, 5.0-12.5 x2.5-5.0 cm, attenuate at both  ends, acute, 3-nerved, shining.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers arranged in axillary panicles. Peduncle this branching at the apex, branches 1-3-flowered. Calyx yellow. Fruits ovoid to globose, c 0.7 cm in diam., dark green, rather dry.
 Inflorescence: Axillary branched clustered panicles; Flower: Globular, hermaphrodite, trimerous, bracteate; Perianth: With oblong lobes short tubes ; Androecium: Stamens 9, fertile; Gynoecium:  Ovary superior, hypogynous, one chambered with single ovule, style slender; Fruit: Globular berry with thikened club shaped base . Flowering & Fruiting : April-May. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and by stem cuttings.

Soil type: It prefers well drained clayey-loamy soil.

Nutrient requirements: Plant requires nitrogenous fertilizers and manure for better growth.

Water management: Plant are irrigated periodically at the interval of 15-20 days.                                      

Harvesting period / Method: Plants is valued for its bark so it is harvested after 10 years, leaves are collected from plant after 5-years.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The plant is used in case of cold, chills, diarrhoea. Externally it is used in inflammation, and sprains. Essential oil and camphor are effective as  anthelmintic (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933). The plant is considered sedative, antiseptic, diaphoretric, anthelmintic, stimulent and carminative. It is also used in insecticidal preparations (Chopra et al., 1956).
