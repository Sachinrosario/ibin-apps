HABIT & HABITAT
INDIA: Evergreen & semievergreen forest and also frequently in open places, between sea level to 900 m. Throughout mainland and Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Nepal, Myanmar, Bangladesh, China, Thailand, Philippines, Sumatra, Java and Srilanka.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Shrubs or small trees, up to 7 m high; branchlets woody, cylindric, glabrous; bark greenish to grey-brownish. Leaves pinnate; leaflets 3-5,rarely 1 or even 6 or 7,  oblong-lanceolate, 7-20 x 2-6 cm, obtuse or rounded at apex, margin entire or obscurely crenate, cuneate or obtuse at base, glabrous; petiole and rachis angular or cylindric, glabrous. 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Erect axillary panicles; Flower: Small, pedicels very short, bracts beneath the calyx triangular; Calyx: Small, lobes ovate-orbicular, margins membranous, ciliolate, with glands on outside of each lobe a little below its apex; Corolla: Petals imbricate, white, very broadly obovate, the margins membranous; Androceium: Stamens 8-10, free, inserted round the disk, filaments flattened, anthers with glands at the apex and sometimes gland at the back of the anther; Gynoecium: Ovary on a short disk, 2-5 celled, covered all over with mammillate glands, style as wide as ovary in bud; Fruit: Berry  globose, apiculate, smooth pinkish-white or cream coloured; Seed: Oblong,;           .
Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in axillary and terminal, panicle, subsessile, white, fragrant. Sepals 5, deltoid to suborbicular, acute, margin ciliolate. Petals 5, elliptic-ovate, obtuse at apex, glabrous. Berries subglobose; cream to crimson red or pinkish when ripe, mamillate. Seeds plano-convex, green. Its phenology is distributed throughout the year.

Flowering & Fruiting time: August-September. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by   wind, birds or animals.   
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome numbers -16, 18.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Pounded root mixed with sugar is used for the treatment of fever. A juice extracted from leaves is used in fever and liver complaints and also against skin diseases especially eczema. Ripe fruits are edible. Stem twigs are used as tooth-sticks.


Chemistry:  

Presence of dictamnine, g-fagarine, mp. 142°, skimmianine, lip. 176°, b-sitosterol and a Coumarin in roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a. 
Isolation of stigmasterol, myricyl alcohol, a new base -glyborine (0.004%) -two new triterpenes- arborinol A, lip. 270°, (0.022%) and arborinol B (0.003% ). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.


Structure of arborine (fig. 5a) (glycosine) elucidated as 2-benzyl-1-liethyl-4-quinazolone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
Structure of arborinine (fig. 1) (0.1%), mp. 176°, confirmed as 2,3- dimethoxy-1-hydroxy-10-liethylacridone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.
Three new quinazolone bases -glycosminine (fig. 3) (glycosmine), mp. 249°, glycorine HC1, lip. 242°, glycosmicine (fig. 2), lip. 265°- and also g-fagarine and skililiianine isolated from leaves and their structures described. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.


Two new epimeric pentacyclic triterpenes -arborinol (fig. 4a), mp. 274° and isoarborinol, mp. 294° -isolated from leaves and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.
Structures of glycosmicine, glycorine (fig. 5b) and glycosminine elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)g.
Complete structure of arborinol by X-ray crystallography. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)h.
Three new acridone alkaloids -noracronycine (fig. 6a), de-N-Methylacronycine (fig. 6b) and de-N-Methylnoracronycine (fig. 6c) from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)i.
Arborinone (fig. 4b), mp 216° (0.00028%), along with arborinol and isoarborinol (fig. 4c) from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)j.


Interrelationships among quinazolone alkaloids; demethylation of glycorine and arborine to 4-quinazolone and glycosminine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)k.
Synthesis of glycozolidine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
A quinazolone alkaloid glycophymine (fig. 7) and an amide - glycomide (fig. 8) - isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
A new quinoline alkaloid - glycosolone (fig. 9) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
A quinoline alkaloid - glycosolone - isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.
Glycophymoline (fig. 11) isolated and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.
Glycosolone isolated from root bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.


Glycozolinine isolated from seeds and characterized as 6-hydroxy-3-methylcarbazole methyl. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.
A new alkaloid - glycozolinol - isolated and found identical to glycozolinine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.
A new alkaloid - glycophylone (fig. 10) isolated from seeds and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.
A quinolone alkaloid -glycolone (fig. 12) isolated from leaves and characterized as 4,8-dimethoxy-3-(3-methyl-but-2-enyl)-2(1H)-quinolinone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Isolation of a new carbazole alkaloid - glycozolidol (fig. 13) from roots and characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.


Homoglycosolone (fig. 14) isolated from root bark and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
A new 2-qunolone alkaloid isolated and characterized as 3-(but-2'-enyl)-4-ethoxy-8-hydroxy-1-methylquinolin-2(1H)-one (fig. 16, also named as glycolone). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.


A carbazole alkaloid - mupamine (fig. 15) isolated and its structure determined and confirmed by partial synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

Pharmacology:

 Arborine (glycosine) in doses of 10-160 mg/kg, i.v. caused no mortality inmice except in 1 of 3 animals receiving 160 mg/kg; a dose of 80mg/kg, i.p. caused death in mouse and tremor in another. In rats doses upto 80 mg.kg,i.p. caused no mortality. In anesthetized rats in situ, doses of 1-4 mg/kg, i.v. caused brief fall in blood pressure which was unaffected by atropine and/or mepryamine. In isolated rabbit duodenum, arborine in doses of above 10 mg/ml caused increased peristalsis and mild contraction. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)l.
Glycozolidol exhibited antibacterial activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.
