DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem terete, striate, branched above. Lower leaves are reniform, upper leaves pinnate, ovate or obovate, dentate, cauline leaves 2-3 pinnate, lobes linear-lanceolate, narrow;  petioles sheathing. Flowers small in compound umbels, white, rays 7-15, sparsely pubescent, ebracteate or bract 1; bracteoles few, filiform; petals white, fruit schizocarp or cremocarp, 3-5 x 2-3 mm, ovoid to oblong, shortly appressed-setose, greyish green to greyish brown, mericarp broadly ovoid, 5-ridged with short hairs and numerous vittae.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Fruits are used as expectorant, stimulatant, carminative, diuretic and diaphoratic. It is useful in flatulent, colic and asthma.
Chemistry:

·        Its major constituent is trans-anethol (1-methoxy-4-(1-propenyl) benzene. (Karaali et. al., 1990).

·        Tissue culture of Pimpinella anisum (PAD) that selectively promoted the production of epoxypseudoisoeugenol-(2-methylbutyrate), termed EPB. This compound served as the final mol. of the biosynthetic pathway in all labeling experiments. (Reichling et. al., 1995).

·        Sterol compound in 9 plant glyceride oils from family apiaceae including Pimpinella anisum (anise) were studied using GLC, sterol content varies from 0.2-0.7%, sitosterol 33.0-58.4% and stigmasterol 26.6-47.5%. The minor components identified are cholesterol, brassicasterol, campesterol, D7 compesterol, D5 avenasterol, D7 stigmasterol and D7 avenasterol. (Zlatanov and Ivanov, 1995).

·        Ethereal oils of onise fruit were determined to introduce a new micro hydrodestination device. Compared to the (Deutsches Arzneibuch 1996) DAB 1996, method, microhydrodestination by the microdistiller is less time consuming and a 50 fold less amount of sample can be analyzed. (Briechle et. al., 1997).

·        Different isolation methods for the volatile components of anise are compared in terms of different of components obtained with each and procedure. These methods include headspace (purge and trap). Sampling procedure, simultaneous distillation extraction (SDE), steam distillation and solvent extraction. 43 components have been identified by comparing GC retention time and mass spectral data. Different isolation techniques result in compositionally different isolates. The headspace sampling method is found to be the best qualitative analytical technique. (Lee et. al., 1997). 

·        The seed oil content and composition of Pimpinella anisum and other plants of Apiaceae family were studied. The compositions were as following, oil content (10.9-23.8) tocopherols 206.7-1550.9 mg/kg mainly g tocotrienol, sterols 0.2-0.7%, sitosterol (33.0-58.0%) and stigmasterol derivatives 29.8%-53.3%. The oil contained 1.7-3.7% phospholipids. The oils mainly contained phospholipids-phosphate dycholines phosphatide cholines, phosphatidylinositols and phosphatidyl ethanolamines. (Zvanor et. al., 1998).

·        Linear, unsaturated hydrocarbons were detected in samples treated with g-rays or microwaves. (Bendini, et. al., 1998).

·        Hairy root cultures of Pimpinella anisum were established following inoculation of aseptically grown plantlets with an A4 pRiA4 70 GUS strain of Agrobacterium rhizogenes. The essential oils from the hairy roots, maintained in four different media, and from the fruits and the roots of the parent plant were analyzed and their compounds compared by GC and GC-mass spectrometry. The major components of the essential oils from the hairy root cultures were trans-epoxypseudoiseugenyl 2-methylbutyrate, geijerene, pregeijerene, zingiberene and b-bisabolene, in varying amounts depending on the light and dark growth conditions and on the culture media tested. Trans-Epoxypseudoisoeugenyl 2-methylbutyrate, b-bisabolene and pregeijerene were the major components of the essential oil from the roots of the parent plant, whereas the main component of the fruit oil was trans-anethole.  Geijerenes were not detected in the fruit oil. The essential oil yield of the transformed roots grown in one of the media was comparable with that obtained for the roots of the parent plant and, calculated on a dry weight basis, the oil yield of these hairy roots was comparable with that of the fruits. (Santos et. al., 1998).

·        The major components of the essential oils from the hairy root cultures of Pimpinella anisum were trans-epoxypseudoisoeugenyl 2-methylbutyrate, geijerene, pregeijerene, zingiberene and beta-bisabolene, in varying amounts depending on the light or dark growth conditions and on the culture media tested. trans-Epoxypseudoisoeugenyl 2-methylbutyrate, beta-bisabolene and pregeijerene were the major components of the essential oil from the roots of the parent plant, whereas the main component of the fruit oil was trans-anethole. Geijerenes were not detected in the fruit oil. (Santos et. al., 1998).

·        Aniseed was extracted with diethyl ether (LSM), simultaneous. (Kollmannsberger et. al., 2000).

Transdermal devices comprising essential oils containing Anise for aromatherapy. (Fotinos and Spiros, 2000).


Pharmacology:

·        Oil of anise appears to show oestrogen like activity and is useful in menopausal problems, menstrual disorders. It is cautioned that it may be prudent to avoid essential oils suspected of estrogenic activity in the case of prepubertal girls. (Balacs, 1993).

·        A topical massage preparation which induces vasodilation and ameliorates varices is prepared by combining (1) a decoction of orange blossoms containing nlemon rind, (2) a decoction of rosenary containing Mediterranean red poppy, (3) a decoction of thyme containing anise and (4) olive extract. (Serra and Genoveva, 1996).

·        These granules contain bi alluminate 200, NaHCO3 100-300, heavy 300-500, licorice extract 200-400, frangila 15-35, arise powder 1-20 and ethanol or water 100-1500 g. The granules may contain 0.5-1508 CMC. Effectiveness was clin-tested. (Yingheng, et. al., 1997).

·        A new pharmaceutical compounds oral use containing diclofenac and alkali metal carbonates nd bicarbonates and particular flavouring substances are described. These compounds are palatable, being free from any unpleasant taste or side effects. An effervescent compounds contains dielofenac K salt 50, KHCO3 24; mint flavouring on vaterdextrin (1:2000) 50, aniseed flavouring on natudrotrin (2:1000)120, (KHCO3), saccharin 5, mannitol 65, citric acid 500, aspartanie 9, and saccharose 3000 mg. (Alberto and Gioregio, 1997).

·        Methods and compounds for promoting hair growth, preventing or minimizing hair loss, enhancing or restoring hair color or remelanization and treating other hair and skin conditions are disclosed. The methods include topical application of the components to the skin or hair follicles being treated. The components include as an essential component a treatment agent in an amount effective for treating the condition and selected from the class of compounds consisting of anol, anethole, fenchone, and mixtures thereof. Various combinations of these compounds may be found in herb families including Umbeliferae, Magnoliaceae, Labiatae and Rutaceae. The invention preferably contemplates selecting the treatment agent from the class of herbs consisting of Pimpinella anisum (anise). (Eliaz and Gonen, 1997). 

·        Anisaldehyde characterized in the seeds of Pimpinella anisum (aniseed) inhibits the oxidation of L-3, 4,-dihydroxyphenylalanine (L-DOPA) by mushroom tyrosinase with an ID50 of 43 mg/mL (0.32mM) (Kupo and Ikuyo, 1998).

·        Anti convulsant effects of an essential oil of the fruits of Pimpinella anisum against seizures induced by pentylenetetiazole (PTZ) or maximal electroshock (MES) in male mice were studied. The essential oil produced motor impairment, though this effect did not occur at the doses and time courses needed for anticonovulsant activity. (Pourgholami et. al., 1999).

·        The essential oil of the fruits of Pimpinella anisum suppressed tonic convulsions induced by pentylenetetrazole (PTZ) or maximal electroshock in male mice. The essential oil produced motor impairment. (Pourgholami et. al., 1999).

·        A novel pharmaceuitic compounds for the treatment of constipation is claimed comprising senna rich in sennosideses laxatives and provider of fruits of anise as carminative with appropriate excipients. A capsule contained senna provider 12, arise fruit provider 12, lactose 4-o and colloidal silica 36 mg. (Chicouri, et. al., 2000).

·        Pimpinella anisum is one of the ingredients in hair growth promoting formulation. (Eliaz and Gonen, 2001).

 

Ethnobotany:

·        Anise (Pimpinella anisum) have long been used for medical therapeutical purposes, as a flavour component in bakery products and as a condiment in many delicious traditional dishes. Its major industrial use in Turkey has been in aromatizing the Turkish raki, a traditional distilled liquor with 45 percent alcohol content. (Karaali et. al., 1990).

·        Spray parts are removed from porous and non-absorbent surfaces by sprayed glycerol solution of Pimpinella anisum and oil of Inula graveolens. (Wlges and Wilhelm, 1997).

·        Beverage with radioprotective properties formulated from banana peel and extracts or juices of anise, seberian fir, cherry, apricot, plumes, peach, grape, mountain ash, blackcurrant or raspberry. (Valdimir et. al., 1997).

·        The low cost and highly efficient emulsifiable solution is prepared from H2O 100, pepper 4-8, aniseed 3-6, cenia bark 3-6, myricia 3-6, herb perillae 2-4 parts. The emulsifiable solution is manufactured by grinding flavour, soaking with water for 0.25-27 hour extracting, emulsifying, diluting, precipitating and filtering. (Xiao et. al., 1997).

·        The ant soy sauce is a mixture of hydrolyzed or proteniase digested edible ant powder and normal soy sauce adding with glycyrrhiza, garlic, apple, aniseed cassia bark and liquor. The ant soy sauce contains N by NH, higher than 0.8 and NaCl 19%. (Wang and Houde, 1997).

·        A flavouring compound is prepared containing (1) ≥ 1 flavour selected from the group:-benzaldehyde, cuminaldehyde, peril aldehyde, cumin alc., g undecalactone, iso-pr. formate, anisaldehyde, anethol, estragol, vanillin and Kt. vanillin, and (2) RN=C=S [R=C1-16 alkyl, aryl, Ph, containing C alkyl substituents and benzyl]. The (1) flavour may be selected extracts from bitter almond, spricot, cumin shiso, peach, orange, plum, apple, vanilla, anise, fennel and tarragon. (Iwahata et. al., 1998).

·        Essential oil extracted from spice material has been tested to prevent butter deterioration stored at room temperature. Butter oxidation and lypolysis were determined by measuring peroxide and acid values respectively. Volatile oil isolated from anise is found to posses’ excellent preservative activity. (Singh et. al., 1998).



Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

·        Essential oil of Pimpinella anisum achieved high toxicity (over 94% mortality) as vapour to at least two insect species tested with doses mostly in the range of 108-135 mh/L air and at exposure between 24-144h. The essential oil caused 95% mortality in Tribohium confusum sitophilus oryzae and Ephestia Kuchhiella within short exposures. (Sarac and Tunc, 1995).

·        Pimpinella anisum - a herbal toothpaste is toxic causing discomfort and dryness of mouth. Then loss of sense of taste alongwith sources of perioral skin and cracking of lips. Discontinued use of herbal toothpaste restores original condition. Anethole is a natural compound from Pimpinella anisum. (Franks, 1998).
