Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:                                                                  
Common bamboo in the Eastern Himalaya, from Kumaon to Assam, central Himalayas,     Eastern Himalayas, Khasia hills, Naga Hills, Tropical Himalayas and Western Himalayas.

Availability :
This species is distributed in the North-West Himalaya, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura. This species occurs in finer textured soil in semi-evergreen forests in lesser rainfall area
Status : 
Misc: Deban forest, Namdapha, Khasia hills, Silhet, Someswar hills, Uttar Pradesh to Arunachal Pradesh.
Country: Burma, India, Nepal, Pakistan and S.E.Asia.
Zone: Central, East, ,North and North East.
State: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Meghalaya, Sikkim and Uttar Pradesh.
District: Champaran and Garhwal.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES                                                             
A large caespitose bamboo growing tall and erect often culms at an angle or curved downwards. Culms large, 12-20 m or upto 25 m tall, usually naked below, much branched ablove, greyish-white when young and dull green when old. lower nodes marked with root scars. Culm sheaths are long and stiff, variable in size, shining within, rough and glabrous outside. Leaves variable, small on side branches, rounded at the base , broadly lanceolate, smooth above and rough beneath finely serrate on the margins. Inflorescence is much branched large panicle with many whorls of branches, bearing half verticillate semi globular heads of purple flowers supported by round scarious bracts. Spikelets purple, ovoid, depressed, glabrous; empty glumes, lemmas broad, paleas of lower flowers 2-keeled, bifid. Stamens pendulus, anthers purpler, connective produced into a long black hairy twisted point. Ovary sub orbicular, hairy; style long, hairy; stigmas 3, plumose. Caryopsis braodly ovoid, beaked, glabrous or hairy above.
The distinguished features of this species are brown pubescent culm with bent top in mature culms, root verticils are seen in almost all the nodes of the culm; largely broad ovate branch buds; zig-zag internodes in some culms.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Breeding System :
Aneuploid, chromosome number 2n=70.
Phenology :
Flowering cycle is reported to be 30-40 years. The entire clump flowers profusely and dies soon after seeding.

Cultivation:
About 153-210 seedlings per square meter were observed. The species can multiplied from seedlings by separating tillers. Also can be propagated by rooting culm cuttings using growth regulators. Horizontal planting was found better than vertical planting.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:
Aphids attack on stems and attack of Oregma bambusae also observed.
Diseases:
Chlorosis caused by Paecilomyces lilacinus was reported from North-East India.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tender shoot, skin of Bamboo, Culms

Uses

  1. Used for walls of native huts, construction purposes, basket making, mats, water and milk vessels, fuel, floats for timber rafts.
  2. The tribals of Arunachal Pradesh use the tender shoot for preparation of 'hiyup' a sour pickle and young shoots are boiled and eaten in Sikkim.
  3. Skin of the Bamboo can be used in cottage industry for binding and caning chairs.

    Harvesting Practices


    Produce and Products
    Baskets, mats, vessels, timber, timber rafts, Pickle

Chemistry
Leaves contain 40.9 percent dry matter, 22.6 percent crude protein, 4.04 percent eather extract, 33.12 percent grade fibre, 11.12 percent total ash, 7.26 percent soluble ash, 0.82 percent Calcium, 0.23 percent Phosphorous and 0.40 percent tannins.
