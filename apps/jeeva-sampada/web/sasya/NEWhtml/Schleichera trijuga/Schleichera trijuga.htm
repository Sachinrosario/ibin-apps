Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large deciduous (sometimes evergreen) tree occurs in medium rainfall area of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (Moist Tropical). Found in the sub-Himalayan tract from Kashmir to West Bengal and in the central and peninsular India, up to an altitude of 900 m., especially in the moist localities.

In the sub-Himalayan tract and the outer hills, it is common on well-drained boulder deposits, often occurring in large numbers along the ravines or on the edges of the terraces. It is common in Siwalik range on the sides of ravines on sandstone or on boulder beds. In central India, it occurs scattered in mixed forests chiefly near the banks of streams.

The tree is typical of mixed deciduous forests, often of somewhat dry type. The absolute maximum shade temperature in its natural habitat varies from 350 to 47.50 and the absolute minimum from -2.50 to 17.50. The rainfall varies from 75 to 250 cm. It thrives best on light well-drained, gravelly or loamy soils, although it also occurs on sandy and laterite soils. It is shade bearer, and is frost- and drought-hardy.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large tree. Bark smooth grey or brown, reddish inside, exfoliating in small, round, irregular flakes. Leaves abruptly pinnate; petioles and rachis terete, pubescent. Leaflets 2-4 pairs, opposite or nearly so, usually very obtuse (rarely shortly acuminate), entire, glabrous, base rounded or tapering; lateral nerves 12-20 pairs with reticulate venation. Petiolules very short, articulated with the rachis.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers white or yellowish, fascicled, on interrupted often branched racemes which are axillary or below the leaves, often several on short branchlets. Calyx lobes acute. Fruit size of a small plum, pointed, often echinate with stout rather blunt prickles. Seeds 1 or 2, enclosed in a pulpy edible aril which has a pleasant acid taste. Testa smooth brown.

Phenology:

Old leaves fall from December - February, turning pale or golden yellow, leafless for a short time. New leaves of various shades of red, light green and dark green appear in March - April. Flowers in March - April. Fruits ripen during June - July and quickly fall to the ground.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by entire transplanting.

Natural regeneration of the tree is through seeds and root-suckers. The fruits fall towards the end of the summer or at the beginning of the rainy season. Under natural conditions the germination of the seed and establishment of seedling appear to depend upon the quickness of the seeds becoming covered by the earth and debris; if not, they perish due to exposure to the sun. The seeds are viable only for a short period. They can be stored for a year in gunny bags and two years in sealed tins. Germinating capacity of the seed is reported to be 58 per cent in Uttar Pradesh.

Artificial regeneration is done by direct sowing of fresh seed in thoroughly prepared soil or by stump planting. The nursery-raised seedlings do not stand transplanting well. For obtaining stumps, the seeds may be sown in the nursery 5.0-7.5 cm apart immediately after their collection. The stumps are prepared from the seedlings in next transplanting season.

The seedlings are frost-tender in early stages. Stump planting appears to give best results in moist climate as on the west coast.

 

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Rosellinia bunodes (Berk. & Br.) - stem blight

Polyporus webwrianus Bres. & P. Henn. - Yellow cork rot

Daedalea flavida Lev - white spongy rot

Hexagonia apiaria Pers. - white spongy rot

Irpex flavus Koltz. - White fibrous rot

Several defoliators, borers and sapsuckers attack the tree.

Serinetha augur Fabr. - Bug, pierces the testa of seed and feeds upon the oily cotyledons.

Laccifer lacca (q.v.) - Lac insect, most important.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:It makes good fuel and excellent charcoal. Calorific value: sapwood - 4,950 cal., 8,910 B.t.u.; heartwood - 4,928 cal., 8,872 B.t.u.

Wood: The sapwood is white or pale-brownish white and heartwood uniform light-reddish brown. The wood is dull, very heavy (sp. gr. Approximately 0.91-1.08; wt. 945 - 1,185 kg./ cu. m.), straight- to irregularly-interlocked grained, even- and medium-textured. It is hard and durable.

It is suitable where hardness, bending strength and toughness are required. It is commonly used for oil and sugar mills, rice pounders, pestles, axles and hubs, felloes and stock of cart wheels, shafts, mortars, violin bows and agricultural implements, like yokes, ploughs and harrows and for turnery articles. It is suitable for tool handles, required for heavy working, such as hammers, felling axes, pick axes, etc.

The treated timber is durable and is suitable for construction, cabinet-work, beams, rafters, purlins, trusses, posts, sleepers and for wagon building. It is also used for road paving, block flooring in mills and godowns and as pit-props, side-props in shafts and galleries in mines.

In a mixture with other woods, such as Terminalia chebula Retz., T. alata Heyne ex Roth, Ougeinia oojeinensis, Tectona grandis Linn. f., kusum wood yields pulp suitable for the manufacture of wrapping and printing papers.

Plant is a source of timber. It is very hard. Used to make mortars.

Other uses:

Seed - Oil, Macassar oil, obtained from the seeds has been employed for the cure of itch,which is used to make candles, soaps, for hair dressing, batik work and illumination.

Oil could be utilized for soap manufacture and as a textile lubricant. But due to difficulties in processing the oil, its direct use is considered inadvisable. Oil cake has good manurial value.

Fruits - Raw fruits are pickled. The ripe fruits possess whitish pulp and pleasant, acidic taste and are often eaten during summer. The aril is reported to possess cooling properties. Seeds also are eaten raw or roasted. Young shoots are eaten. Unripe fruits are pickled.

Shoots - Young shoots are lopped for fodder.

Flowers - Flowers are said to yield a dye. Also, source of nectar for honey-bees.

Powdered seeds are applied to remove maggots from ulcers and wounds of animals. Bark is astringent. A maceration or infusion of the bark is prescribed in malaria.

Bark - Bark yields tanin.
Leaves - Leaves are used as vegetable.

Miscellaneous Information

Suitable for open land around village, boundaries and village commons.

The tree is sometimes known as a lac tree. It is an important host for Kusumi lac and the finest quality of lac is obtained from this tree. The lac which is produced on it being the most highly prized quality. Tree serves a host for lac insects.
