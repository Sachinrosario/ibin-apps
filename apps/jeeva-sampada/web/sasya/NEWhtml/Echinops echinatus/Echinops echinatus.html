DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herb, 30-55 cm high, branched from the base; branches wide spreading. Leaves sessile, oblong, 7-19 cm long, pinnatifid; lobes triangular or oblong, sinuate, spinescent; spine often c 4 cm long. Heads white, 2-3 cm across, with many stout spines; spines 1-3 cm long. Involucral surrounded by strong, white bristles; outer involucral bracts 6-8, oblanceolate, glabrous; inner c 8 mm long. Achenes obconic, c 4 mm long, silky villous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: Heads white, one flowered, with many stout spines; Involucre surrounded by strong, white bristles; outer involucral bracts oblanceolate, glabrous; Receptacles thick hard, hairy; Corolla deeply cleft; Anthers sagittate at the base, appendages elongated; style branches short, commonly united upto the tip  ; Fruit: Achenes obconic , silky, villous ;

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : March- September. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind or birds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: Plants are used in hoarse cough and hysteria. The powdered roots mixed with Acacia gum is applied to kill hair lice.


Chemistry:
Isolation of b-amyrin and lupeol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

A new acylated flavone – apigenin-7-O-b-D-(4’’-cis-p-coumaroyl)glucoside – isolated together with apigenin-7-O-glucoside and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation and structure elucidation of echinacin (fig. 1a) and echinaticin (fig. 1b). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

A new alkaloid – echinozolinone (fig. 2)– isolated along with echinopsine and echinopsidine and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

N-hentriacontane, n-hentriacontanol, lupeol and its acetate, b-amyrin and its acetate, b-sitosterol, palmitic acid, betulininc acid, apigenin, its 7-O-glucoside and 7-O-b-D-(4’’-p-coumaroyl)glucoside, luteolin, quercetin and echinopsine identified in flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Allophonic acid and w-methylallophanic acid (fig. 3) from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

a- and b-amyrin acetates, b-amyrin caproate, betulininc acid, lupeol acetate, ethyl palmitate, apigenin, b-sitosterol and its acetate isolated from twigs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Taraxasterol, its acetate, lupeol, b-amyrin, b-sitosterol, its glucoside, apigenin and its 7-O-glucoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

A new alkaloid – 7-hydroxyechinozolinone – isolated from flowers and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Isolation of echinacin and echinatin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.


Pharmacology:
Echinacin and apigenin exhibited antifungal activity against Alternaria tenuissima. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for boneache (Root, Saxena, 1986); Facilitates child birth (Root, Maheshwari et al, 1986)2; Fever (Whole plant, Saxena and Vyas, 1981; Leaf, Tarafder, 1985); Malaria (Whole plant, Saxena and Vyas, 1981); Skin disease (Root, Maheshwari et al, 1986)1; Veterinary wounds (Root, Uniyal and Chauhan, 1973).
