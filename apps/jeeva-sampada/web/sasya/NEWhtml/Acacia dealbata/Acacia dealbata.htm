Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Medium trees suitable for marginal lands and cut up land around villages. It was introduced in the Nilgiris and Palni Hills in Tamil Nadu, where it was naturalised. The species is also planted in the Himalayas between 2000 to 2700 m. The tree has been planted in Simla, Nainital and Almora hills between altitudes of 1,800 and 2,400 m. Elsewhere, silver wattle was planted in Ceylon, Cyprus, Kenya, New Zealand, Rhodesia, South Africa, Tanzania and Uganda but now is replaced by black wattle due to high tannin content. 

It is susceptible to frost.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An evergreen tree with grey, somewhat silvery bark. Leaves bipinnate, pinnae 14-18 pairs.

Flowers yellow, fragrant, in profuse axillary and terminal, panicled globose racemes.

Pods straight or curved, flattened, 5.0-7.5 cm long, slightly constricted between the seeds.

Produce and Products:

Tannin

Gum - Tree yields a viscous gum of high quality, which closely resembles gum arabic, but differs from it in having a strongly astringent taste and analysable quantity of tannin. It has proved to be suitable for pharmaceutical purposes and has been used as a remedy in bronchial diseases and as an antidote to poisoning by alcohol and ammonia.

Fodder.

Soil conservation.

Flowers are used for the preparation of Mimosa perfume which resemble Ylang-Ylang, but is slightly coarser.

Bark from Indian trees contains 9-17 per cent tannin and is a useful tanning material.
