DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Scandent, branching, climbing shrubs. Leaves 4-8.5 x 2-3 cm, elliptic or oblong, acuminate at apex, cuneate at base, slightly coriaceous, dark-green; Petiole, about 1 cm long. Flowers white or greenish yellow interminal or axillary racemes, 5-merous. Calyx segments 5, Corolla lobes 5, pubescent. Drupes rounded, black when ripe.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
A new compound – vilangin (0.06%, fig. 1), m.p. 264°, identified as methylenebis-(2,5-diydroxy-4-undecyl-3,6-benzoquinone) – isolated from berries. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Isolation and synthesis of vilangin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Embelic acid, the active constituent of Embelia ribes was isolated and identified new method for the estimation of embolic acid was developed by HPTLC. Vilangin, a dimer of embelic acid was prepared from ether extract of Embelia ribes and embelic acid was determined gravimetrically. HPTLC method was compared with gravimetric method. (Sharma et al, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9501-0619.

Tincture of (Embelia ribes) berries were prepared from different strengths of alcohol by maceration process. Seventy percent alcohol produced after 21 days of maceration a maximum solid content of 9.49 + 0.13 percent and showed pH3.2 (Pal et al, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9502-0739.

A simple colorimetric method using Potassium Hydroxide was developed for embelin determination in Embelia ribes. This method was compared with the available method and was found to be more reliable. (Patel et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 351335s.

A simple and reproducible HPTLC method for determination of Embellinin Embelia ribes was developed and described. The sensitivity was found to be 0.20 mg/ml and the linearity was observed in the range of 0.20 m to 2.00 mg. The embellin content of 4.82% wt./wt. was found in test sample. The proposed method being precise and sensitive can be used for detection, monitoring and quantification of Embelin in Embelia ribes. (Chauhan et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 322480s.

Embelin, a major constituent of Embelia ribes Burm. (Myrsinaceae) was transformed into different types of compounds through dimerization during preparation of a decoction. Two of the products are proposed to have furanylidene benzofuranone and 1,4-dibenzofurandione skeletons on the basis of spectroscopic means. The transformation of embelin in boiling water is markedly accelerated by the presence of fatty acids. (Kiuchi et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 275777m.


Pharmacology:
Extract of berries showed antifertility activity in rats and also anti-oestrogenic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Alcoholic extract of plant significantly increased glycogen protein and nonprotein nitrogen contents in uterus of normal and ovariectomised rats. It also showed encouraging antifertility activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Embelin, a p-quinone, is derived from Embelia ribes. The analgesic effect of potassium embelate has been studied in rats and mice. The test drug was found to be effective by oral, i.m. and i.c.v. routes and the results compared well with  morphine. (Atal, 1984).

The naturally occurring anthelmintics, Embelia ribes have been to possibly cause optic atrophy among the Ethiopian population. (Low et al, 1985).

Embelin, extracted from Embelia ribes Burm. berries, altered the testicular histology and glycogen, gametogenic counts and accessory sex gland fructose at the dose levels 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 mg/kg body weight administered subcutaneously for 35 days. The compound is suggested to possess antiandrogenic activity. (Low et al, 1986).

50% ethanolic and benzene extracts of Embelia ribes, dried berries, has been studied histochemically. 75 and 150 mg/kg doses of 50% ethanolic extracts of E. ribes increased the intensity of reaction for alkaline phosphatase in both luminal and glandular epithelium. (Mathur, 1986).

The in vivo studies have carried out in the rat brain. The results indicate that mixed m and kappa binding sites in the brain may be involved in the analgesic action of this compound. (Zutshi et al, 1989).

Embelin, the active principle of the seeds of Embelia ribes Burm, has been isolated and the purity established. Daily subcutaneous administration of the compound at a dose of 20 mg/kg body weight to male albino rats for 15 or 30 days revealed an inhibition of: a) epididymal motile sperm count, b) fertility parameters such as pregnancy attainment and litter size, and c) the activities of the enzymes of glycolysis and energy metabolism. These changes were reversible, as seen after 15 and 30 days of recovery. Addition of embelin to epididymal sperm suspensions caused a dose- and duration-dependent inhibition of spermatozoal motility and the activities of the enzymes of carbohydrate metabolism. Light and scanning electron microscopy showed that both in vivo and in vitro treatment with the drug causes profound morphological changes in spermatozoa such as: a) decapitation of the spermatozoal head, b) discontinuity of the outer membranous sheath in the mid-piece and the tail region, and c) alteration in the shape of the cytoplasmic droplet in the tail. (Gupta et al, 1989).

Embelin (Isolated from Embelia ribes) when administered orally to male albino rats (50 mg/kg/day for 20 days), revealed significantly tumor regression and prolonged survival time. To evaluate the possible mode of action of embelin, the antioxidant status of the drug was studied on fibrosarcoma – bearing rats, which confirmed the potential of the compound to possess significant free radical scavenging property. The biological defense system constituting the superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase, glutathione-S-transferase and glutathione was significantly enhanced whereby the lipid peroxide content was restricted to a large extent on embelin treatment. (Chitra et al, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9501-0174.

Embelic acid (2,5-dhydroxy-3-undecyl-p-benzouinone), isolated from Embelia ribes was found to be an inhibitor of trypsin. Kinetic analysis revealed that the inhibition was non-competitive, reversible on dilution. The inhibitory effect of Embelic acid on trypsin was compared with potent trypsin inhibitors such as phenyl methane sulphonyl fluoride and soyabean trypsin inhibitor. (Vijaya and Vasudevan, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9501-0119.

Pipplayadi Yoga containing Embelia ribes with Piper longum showed antifertility activity due to its antioestrogenic property. (PremKishore, 1994-1995).  MAPA 1996, 9603-1348.

Embelia ribes regulate fertility. (Chaudhury, 1995). MAPA, 1996, 9602-0741.

A herbal preparation containing Embelia ribes with other plant species were found to check the effect of flyash influenced enzyme increase in GOT, GPT and Ca-Mg-ATPase activities at the end of one week. There was no significant alteration of protein, carbohydrate and sialic acid contents of liver and lung tissues of rats after the treatment by L. Preparation and was also found effective against the flyash to the rat serum parameters. (Farooq and Pathak, 1997). MAPA 198, 9802-0801.

Embelia ribes as anthelmintic with mixture of other plants. (Devi et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 135, 13882v.

Embelia ribes with other materials was used as contraceptive. (Chaudhury et al, 2001).


Ethnobotany:
Stomach disease (Kothari and Moorthy, 1994, MAPA 195, 9503-1912).


Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

Potassium embelate, 2,5-dihydroxy, 3-undecyl-1, 4-benzoquinone, from Embelia ribes was subjected to toxicity evaluation which included subacute, chronic, reproductive toxicity testing and teratological investigations in laboratory animals. The results did not indicate adverse effects suggesting that potassium embelate is a safe compound. (Johri et al, 1990).

The toxicity of embelin (a constituent of Embelia-ribes) has been assessed in female cyclic rats. Its administration at a dose of 120 mg/kg body weight of liver, kidney and spleen, however the wet weight of the adrenals showed remarkable increase. Biochemical constituents such as protein and glycogen did not show any change in these organs except in the adrenal where a significant increase was observed. The activity of acid and alkaline phosphatase was increase in the kidney and adrenal. Administration of embelin for 6 weeks caused severe pathological changes in liver and kidney which mainly included disintegration, necrotic changes and perinuclear vacuolation. Marked tubular damage was observed in the kidneys. The adrenals showed hypotropy and histological features of the spleen remain unchanged. (Prakash, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9501-0241.
