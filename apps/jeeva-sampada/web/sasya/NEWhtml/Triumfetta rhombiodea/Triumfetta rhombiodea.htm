HABIT & HABITAT : 
A herbaceous perennial, up to 1.5 m: tall, distributed throughout tropical and sub-tropical India, ascending to an altitude of 1,200 m. in the Himalayas.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES : 
Leaves ovate, rhomboid or cordate, usually 3-lobed, irregularly serrate; flowers yellow, in dense cymes; fruits globose or ovoid, with smooth, hooked spines, pea-sized.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used : 
Fresh leaves, bark, seeds and roots.

Uses

  1. The plant is used as a pot herb in times of scarcity.
  2. The cattle eat the leaves which are somewhat mucilaginous. 

Harvesting Practices


Produce and Products : 
The inner bark of the stem yields a soft and glossy fibre (av. length of best fibre, 2.027 mm.; diam. 0.016mm.), much used in Tamil Nadu. The fibre can be used as a substitute for jute, if it is carefully prepared.

Chemistry
The amino acid make-up of the leaf proteins (17.5%) from plants from tropical West Africa was as follows (g./16 g.N): aspartic acid, 9.1; threonine, 4.4; serine,4.2; glutamic acid, 10.4; proline, 6.4; glycine, 5.5; alanine, 6.0; valine, 5.9; cystine, 1.3; methionine, 2.2;isoleucine, 4.5; leucine, 8.8; tyrosine, 3.3; phenylalanine, 5.4; lysine, 5.3; histidine, 2.1; and arginine, 5.0. The seeds yield a greenish yellow, odourless, fatty oil, having the following characteristics: n 40, 1.4708; acid val., 18.7; sap. val., 196.4; and iod. val., 105.4

Back to Top

Processing Techniques : 
The stems, which contain c. 3.5 per cent fiber by weight, are retted in running or stagnant water, or sometimes the fiber is stripped off the stem in two or three ribbons which are then scraped with a knife to remove extraneous matter. The ribbons are made into hunting- and fishing-nets and ropes, the latter being sometimes used even for capturing wild elephants.

Back to Top

Other Uses : 
  1. The root is used in dysentery and the bark and fresh leaves in diarrohea. Pounded roots are given for the treatment of intestinal ulcers and their hot infusion is said to hasten parturition. 
  2. The leaves and the flowers are used against leprosy.
Back to Top

 

Economical Potential to the country

It is extracted on a commercial scale in certain parts of West Africa where the plant is found in a wild state, and is sometimes cultivated. The yield of fiber is reported to be 500-800 kg./ha.
