DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect or scandent, woody climber or shrub, rarely tree, 2-6 m tall, much branched; indumentum ash-white. Stipular thorns recurved, 3-4 mm long. Leaves oblong-elliptic or obovate, rarely linear, 2.0-5.5 x 1.2-3.5 cm, acute, emarginate or retuse at apex, acute at base, glabrous, pubescent when young. Flowers creamy-white or greenish white, in 8-10 flowered terminal corymb or umbel, fragrant, 6-9 mm in diam. Inner pair of sepals smaller than outer, membranous. Petals spathulate, white, puberulous at base. Stamens 25-40; anthers brown. Gynophore 5-6 mm, rarely 10 mm long. Ovary glabrous. Fruits globose, 8-12 mm across, yellow, turning black or dark violet. Seeds embedded in pulp, brown, discoid.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Solitary axillary corymb; Flower: Bracteate, complete, usually hermaphrodite, zygomorphic, tetramerous, hypogynous  ; Calyx: Sepals 4, free at the very base in 2 series, imbricate ; Corolla: Petals 4, imbricate; Androecium: Stamens many, inserted at the base of a long gynophore , filaments filiform, anthers dithecous, introrse, dehiscing longitudinally; Gynoecium: Ovary ovoid, pointed, glabrous or very minutely pubescent ; Fruit: Berry globose, smooth, black when ripe; Seed:  reniform.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : Febuarary-May. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by cattles.     
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 40.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
In Ayurveda system of medicine plant is used as tonic and stomachic.


Chemistry:
a-amyrin, b-amyrin, taraxasterol and erythrodiol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

b-sitosterol, betulin and a triterpenoid alcohol, m.p. 164°C, isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Betulin-28-acetate isolated and characterized; in addition, octacosanol, b-sitosterol, its glucoside and a-amyrin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine as antidote to snake bite (Seed, Apparanantham and Chelladurai, 1986); Dropsy (Bark + Root, Shah et al, 1983); Earache (Root, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); Gout (Bark + Root, Shah et al, 1983); Mumps (Root, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); Skin disease (Leaf, Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984); Swelling (Whole plant, Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984)
