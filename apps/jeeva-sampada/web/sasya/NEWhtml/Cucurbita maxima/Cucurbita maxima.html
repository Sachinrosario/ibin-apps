HABIT & HABITAT
Annual prostrate or twining, hispid herb. It is found throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves orbicular or reniform, 6-20 x 7-30 cm, shallowly 5-lobed, margin dentate. Petioles as long as blade, pubescent. Tendrils 2 to 6-fid. Flowers in axillary solitary cymose, yellow. Corolla with soft obtuse, revolute or hanging lobes. Filaments glandulioform. Fruits large, of various size and shape; pulp reddish, more or less fibrous. Seeds ovate, obscurely marginate, whitish or pale yellowish.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence:  Solitary, monoecious ;  Male Flower:  Calyx tube campanulate, lobes 5, linear,; corolla 5-lobed hairy; stamens 3, inserted low in the calyx tube, anthers connate; Female Flower: Calyx tube campanulate, lobes 5, linear,; corolla 5-lobed hairy;; ovary oblong, style short, stigma 3, bifid, ovules many; Fruit: Fleshy indehicent, often large ; Seed: Ovoid or oblong, compressed. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : Most of the part of year. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome numbers=24, 40.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant requires well drained loamy soil.

Nutrient requirements: Require tonnes of farmyard manure at the time of planting and  nitrogenous fertilizers and potassium and phosphorous at growing period give good yield.

Water management: Plant requires good irrigation facilities.                                       

Harvesting period / Method: Fruits are harvested when they are fully mature.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine the fruit allays thirst and indigestible. It causes biliousness and loss of appetite.


Chemistry:

·        Stigmasta-7,24(28)-dien-3b-ol (fig. 1) and stigmasta-7,25-dienol acetate isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

·        Euglobulins from seed was a mixture of three proteins. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

·        Seed extract produced positive inotropic effect on frog heart; ECG in dog revealed ivation of QRS complex and decrease in heart rate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·        Total organic acids in fruit, 6.7 mequiv./l00g of which nonvolatile acids were 91.2%; vitamin C content 17.7 mg/100 g. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·        a-spinasterol, stigmasta- 7,22,25-trienol and stigmasta- 7,25-dienol isolated from seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

·        Linoleic (52.0), oleic (29.0%) and linolenic (tr) acids and b- and g-tocopherols (33.8mg/ 100g) in seed oil; total carotenoid content was 15 ppm, of which luteolin constituted (71.0) and b-carotene (12.0%); a-carotene, cryptoxanthin and flavoxanthin present in smaller amounts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

·        Isorhamnetin-3-O-rutinoside, kaempferol-3-O-rutinoside and kaempferol-3-O-robinobioside as well two minor flavonol glycosides from pollen whereas stigma contained former three compounds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

·        Pharmacognostic studies of seeds; isolation of cholesterol, ergosterol, campesterol and b-sitosterol from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·        24a-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7E,22-dien-3b-yl acetate, 24b-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7E,22-dien-3b-yl acetate, 24Z-ethylidene-5a-cholesta-7en-3b-yl acetate, 24b-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7,22,25-trien-3b-yl acetate, 24-ethyl-5a-cholesta-3b-yl acetate, 24x-methyl-5a-cholesta-3b-yl acetate and 24x-methyl-5a-cholesta-7-en-3b-yl acetate isolated from seeds and seedlings. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·        Isolation of 24b-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7,22,25(27)-trien-3b-ol, 24b-ethyl-5a-cholesta-7,25 (27)-dien-3b-ol, avenasterol, spinasterol, 24-dihydrospinasterol, 24x-methyllathosterol and 25(27)-dehydrofungisterol isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·        25(27)-dehydroporiferasterol, clerosterol, isofucosterol, stigmasterol, sitosterol, campesterol and codisterol isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·        13C-NMR of chondrillasterol studied. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

·        Isolation of two new carotenoids - cucurbitaxanthins A (fig. 2) and B (fig. 3) - and their structure elucidation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

·        Antifungal compd., trans-p-coumaryl aldehyde, was isolated from fruit tissue of Cucurbita maxima which had been elicited with pectinase.  Its chemical structure was identified by NMR and MS.  This is the first report of a phytoalexin from a member of the Cucurbitaceae. (Stange et al., 1999) CA.131:269547

·        The oil of Cucurbita maxima seeds, which were used as anthelmintics in Turkey was analyzed for phys. and chem. properties.  Methylated fatty acids were analyzed by using gas chromatog. and the major compds. were detd. as linoleic (%42.6), oleic (%32.6), palmitic (%15.1) and stearic acid (%6.5) and the total amt. of unsatd. fatty acids was found as 75%.  The I value was also a good indicator for the unsaturation of the oil b-sitosterol was isolated from unsaponifiable fraction of the oil and identified by chromatography and spectroscopic methods comparing with the authentic sample. (Basaran et al., 1998) CA.128:326292

·        Pectin was obtained from the fruit of Cucurbita maxima (calabash), from the peel, pulp and seeds. the pectin obtained from seeds was of the best quality, this was verified by methoxylation and by IR spectroscopy. (Lily and Antonio, 2001) CA.137:3111

·        Two new analogs of trypsin inhibitor CMTI-III substituted with D-Arg or D-Lys in position 5 (P1) were synthesized by the solid-phase method.  The first analog ([D-Arg5] CMTI-III) displayed assocn. equil. consts. (Ka) with bovine b-trypsin by about three orders of magnitude lower than did wild CMTI-III.  The second analog ([D-Lys5] CMTI-III) displayed Ka by about four orders of magnitude lower than [Lys5] CMTI-III.  The configuration of basic amino acid residue (Arg or Lys) in the reactive site (position P1) of CMTI-III and its analogs played an important role for the stabilization of the inhibitors active structure. (Fraszczak et al., 2002)CA. 137:63461


Pharmacology:

·        Aqueous and alcoholic extracts of seeds possessed anthelmintic properties. Aqueous extract caused marked reduction in contraction amplitude and rate of frog heart which was not blocked by atropine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.


Ethnobotany:

Medicine for intestinal worms (Seed, Kamble and Pradhan, 1980); Edible as vegetable (Fruit, Dam and Hajra, 1981)
