HABIT & HABITAT
The plant is common in temperate to alpine zone of the Himalayas from Indus to kumaon, occurring at altitudes between 3000-5000m. It is found growing in the field border of villages at and around 2400m elevation.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect herb growing up to 1m in height; stem simple and branched from the base, glabrous below and puberulous above; leaves  simple,5-10 cm in length, ovate or orbicular, cordate, more or less 5 leaved, toothed, upper leaves entire; flowers in panicled racemes, bracteate - bracts sharpy toothed, flowers more than 2.5 cm in length, bright blue or greenish blue with purple veins, helmet half as light as long and shortly beaked; follicles downy; Fruits is a 5-celled capsule. Seeds smooth and dark browns

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
 The daughter tubers are usually preferred. Shrivelled mother tubers are often sold as inferior grade drug. The dried roots are generally ovoid-conical in form and tapering down-wards; 2.5-4 cm. long and about 0.5 cm in diameter at the upper end. Externally they have a greyish or light ash colour and internally they are milky-white. Root cures acute dysentery and chronic enteritis, used in splenic fever and gastric troubles, good astringent and tonic, but its efficacy as anti-periodic is disputed; considered as anti-fertility agent, used externally for rheumatism.
Chemistry:
  1. Apsimon and Edward; 1962 gave the structural proof of the atisine family of alkaloids (Fig. 1). Irradiation of azides of diterpenoid acids with 1 a and 1 b carboxyl groups gave the corresponding isocyanates and ca. 25% yields of lactams. The azids of hexanoic acid under comparable conditions gave 8% of hexanamide. The reaction has been used to convert podocarpic acid to the enatiomer of the phenol III derived from atisine.   
  2.   The structure of alkaloids heteratisine (A. heterophyllum) the only lactone-type diterpene alkaloid (Fig. 2) was proposed by Aneja and Pelletier in 1964.
  3.  Heterophyllisine (Fig. 3a), heterophylline (Fig. 3b) and heterophyllidine (Fig. 3c) were isolated from the rhizome of Aconitum heterophyllum, they were lactone type diterpene skelton and unique to heteratisine. (Pelletier and Aneja; 1967).
  4. The “weak base fraction” yielded the lactone alkaloids like heteratisine, heterophyllisine, heterophylline and heterophylline. The “strong base fraction” yielded atisine, atidine and F-dihydroatisine. The “very strong base fraction” yielded hetisine, hetidine, hetisinone (Pelletier et al., 1968).
  5.   Stereo-structure of heteratisine (C22H33NO5), a diterpene lactone alkaloid occurring as a monobenzoyl ester in roots of A. heterophyllum has been established by chemical and spectroscopic studies. Heteratisine (Fig. 4) thus bears a closer structural relationship to the highly toxic and polyoxygenated alkaloids of lycoctonine-aconitine type than to its companion bases of A. heterophyllum atisine, atidine and hetisine (Aneja et al,.1973).
  6.   A new diterpenoid lactone atisenol a neutral compound (Fig. 5) isolated from “weak base fraction” of Aconitum heterophyllum roots which is related to the C20-diterpenoid alkaloids like atisine. (Pelletier et al., 1982).
  7. Atisine (Fig. 6) was synthesized by Shichido et al., 1987.
  8. Molecular and crystal structure of hetisine (Fig. 7) was determined (Tashkhodzaev 1993; CA 1993, 119, 160603.c).
    Chemical constituents:(NBPGR)
    Aconitine (0.4%) is the chief alkaloid of this crop.The root contain non toxic amorphous alkaloids, atisine (0.4%), dehydroatisine, geteratisine and hestisine. It also contains aconitic acid, tonnic acid, pectin and Starch. Atisine is physilogically relatively inactive substances.

Pharmacology:

Ethanolic extract of Aconitum heterophyllum act as immunomodulator by stimulating phagocytic function while inhibiting the humoral component of immune system. (Atal, 1986).
