DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An erect, much branched, perennial aromatic herbs, 5-60 cm high. Stems and branches 4- angular, striate, young branches pubescent; internodes 3-6 cm long. Leaves elliptic or elliptic-lanceolate, 2.5-3.8 x 1-1.3 cm, acute at apex, truncate or narrowed at base, shallowly serrate along margin, gland dotted, slightly hairy or glabrous; petiole slender, 1.0-2.5 cm long, hairy. Flowers white, in  closed-whorled racemes; pedicels erect, closely appressed to the peduncles; bracts stalked, elliptic-lanceolate, acute. Calyx lobes 5; upper lip ovate, 2 mm long; lower lip 2 mm long, scarious outside and villous within. Corolla white, pink or purplish, bilabiate, 4-6 x 1 mm; upper lip 2.5 mm long; lower lip 3.0 mm long, acute. Stamens 4, much exserted. Ovary 0.5 mm long; style gynobasic, 5.5 mm long. Nutlets oblong-elliptic, 1.5 – 2.5 mm long, black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 A paste obtained from leaves is applied on fingers, toe and nails during fever by Santals. It is also used during parasitical diseases. The leaves and seeds are tonic, febrifuge, anticatarrhal, antirheumatismal. The juice and powder obtained from leaves and seeds are taken as an errhine in migraine. Infusion of leaves is used as a drink for curing fever. Plant is used to cure dysentry. Dried leaves are burned to inhale to stop haemorrpage from the nose, or powdered leaves are used as ointment.
Parts used: Leaf-febrifuge, bechic, in skin diseases, cold, catarrh and bronchitis in children; plant – carminative, diaphoretic and stimulant.
Chemistry:
NBPGR: Essential oil at the flowering stage contains citral as a major component along with methylheptenone, methylnonyl ketone and camphor.  The G.L.C. of oil from the linalool type plant showed linalool and caryophyllene as major components.  Other compounds present were a-pinene camphene, myrcene, 1, 8-cineole, camphor, farnesene, terpineol, methyl chavicol, iso-borneol acetate, geraniol, methyl eugenol, humulene and g-cadinene, leaves yielded b-sitosterol betulinic acid and ursolic acid and the flavonoids pectolinarigenin-7- methylether and nevadensin.  A chemotype rich in camphor (26.7 per cent) is also abundantly available in eastern Uttar Pradesh.

·      A new neolignan – ocimin – isolated from essential oil and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·      Methylchavicol (77.0) and linalool (18.0%) isolated from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·      Nevadensin, salvigenin, ursolic and oleanolic acids isolated from leaves and flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·      Acidic polysaccharide, composed of xylose, arabinose, rhamnose and galacturonic acid together with traces of galactose and glucose, isolated form seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·      Camphor (60.0) and limonene (13.0%) identified in oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·      Linalool (60.90%), bergamotene, b-caryophyllene, 1-octen-3-ol and 3-octanol identified by GLC in oil of plant grown in Rwanda. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·      Eugenol identified as major constituent in leaf oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·      Identification of a-pinene, b-pinene, camphene, myrcene, p-cymene, 1, 8-cineole, terpinolene, linalool, camphor, citronellal, borneol, isoborneol, eugenol methyleugenol, isoeugenol, caryophyllene, humulene, acetoeugenol, methyl chavicol, nerol, geraniol and thymol in essential oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·       Four new sesquiterpene alcohols – copan -3-ol, cop-11(12)-en-3-ol, cop-3 (15)-en-11-ol and coppinene, sabinene, myrcene, car-3-ene, limonene, a-phellandrene, p-cymene, g-terpinene, epoxytagetone, camphor, a-(-) gurjunene, a-humulene, isohumulene, eremophilene, allo-aromadendrene, borneol, g-patchoulene, gselinene, a-copaene and myrtenol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Pharmacology:

·      Antimicrobial activity of essential oil of Ocimum canum. (Janssen et al., 1989)

·      Aqueous extract of Ocimum canum Sim, (Lamiaceae) is used by some Ghanaians to manage diabetes mellitus. In vivo modulation of levels of fasting blood glucose by O. canum extract was evaluated in type-II diabetes mellitus using the C57BL/KsJ db/db genetically diabetic animal model, and its effects on glucose-stimulated insulin release in vitro were monitored using isolated rat pancreatic beta-islet cells. The results showed that fasting blood glucose levels and body weight decreased significantly (p < 0.05) in diabetic and non-diabetic C57BL/KsJ mice, which were administered aqueous extract of O. canum. In vitro, the O. canum extract significantly enhanced insulin release from isolated rat pancreatic beta-islet cells. Insulin release was found to be dependent on glucose concentration and increased with increasing O. canum concentration in the incubation medium up to an optimum extract concentration of 0.03 mg/ml. Release of the hormone decreased beyond this concentration of extract in the medium. (Nyarko et al., 2002).
