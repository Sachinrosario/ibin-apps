HABIT & HABITAT
Tall tree, c 25-30 m, deciduous, branches whorled, horizontal, buttressed at base; It is found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Assam, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Daman& Diu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Laccadeves, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
  Bark with long fissures; branchlets with persistent leaf scars. Leaves clustered towards top of the branchlets, obovate or elliptic-obovate, 15-30 x 10-15 cm, retuse at apex, cuneate or subcordate at base, softly hairy when young, shiny glabrous above, turn red before falling. Flowers sessile, in solitary or spike; male flowers towards upper part and hermaphrodite at lower part of inflorescence; bracts minute. Fruits drupe, elliptic, compressed, glabrous, reddish, 2-ridged at maturity.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine fruits are bitter, astringent and used in biliousness and bronchitis.
Parts used; Bark-astringent, wild diuretic, cardiotonic, antidysenteric, oil from Kernel substitute for almond oil; leaf-sudorfic, antirheumatic, antileprotic, anticephalagic in scabies, colic.


Chemistry:
Husk and endocarp contain tannins and pentosans; oil from kernel contains leic, linoleic, palmitic and stearic acids, heart wood and stem-bark contain β-sitosterol and its palmitate; heartwood in addition contains terminolic acid and triterpenic methyl esters.

·         Gallic acid, ellagic acid, corilagin and unidentified flavonoids isolated from leaves, fruits and bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·         Oil from fruits resembled almond oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·         Palmitic and linoleic acids found to be predominant in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Four new hydrolysable tannins – terflavins A and B, tergallagin and tercatain – isolated from leaves and their structures established; punicalin, punicalagin, chebulagic acid, geraniin, granatin B, 1-desgalloyl eugeniin, corilagin and 2, 3-[(S)4, 4’, 5, 5’, 6, 6’-hexahydroxydiphenoyl]-D-glucose also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·         Chemical investigation of the dichloromethane and n-butanol soluble Fraction from the methanol extracts of the bark of Terminalia catappa gave b-sitosterol, oleanolic acid, betulinic acid, b-sitosterol-D-glucoside, arjunolic acid and 28-O-b-D-glucopyranosylarjunolic acid, all six compounds being reported for the first time from the plant. (Diyabalanage et al., 1997) CA.128: 215523.

·         Punicalagin and punicalin, isolated from the leaves of Terminalia catappa L., are used to treat dermatitis and hepatitis.  Both compounds have strong antioxidative activity.  The anti-hepatotoxic activity of punicalagin and punicalin on carbon tetrachloride (CCl4)-induced toxicity in the rat liver was evaluated. (Lin et al., 1998) CA.129: 225537

·         The structures of the new tannin and the known compounds were established on the basis of 1H and NMR analysis.  The new tannin was found to have high activity against Ehrlish ascita carcinoma cells. (Kandil et al., 1999)CA. 131:211577

·         Four flavan-3-ols and two complex type tannins from the bark of Terminalia catappa. (Lin et al., 1999) 131:297610

·         Chemical and spectral evidences are presented for the isolation and characterization of a new triterpenoidal glycoside from the roots of Terminalia catappa, which has been assigned as lup-20 (29)-en-3-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl (1à4)-a-L-rhamnopyranoside. (Srivastava and Chouksey, 2000) CA.135: 369239.

·         Under the inhibition of Cu+2-induced LDL oxidn.-guided fractionation, two new flavone glycosides with galloyl substitution were isolated from the dried fallen leaves of Terminalia catappa L.  Their structures were established as apigenin 6-C-(2"-O-galloyl)-b-D-glucopyranoside (I) and apigenin 8-C-(2"-O-galloyl)-b-D-glucopyranoside (II), together with four known flavone glycosides, isovitexin, vitexin, isoorientin, and rutin, on the basis of spectroscopic method.  Compounds I and II showed significant antioxidative effects.(Lin et al., 2000) CA. 132:332061

·         Squalene was identified by gas chromatog.-mass spectrometry and high-performance liq. chromatog. (HPLC) spiking analyses in the supercrit. CO2 extracts of freeze-dried abscisic leaves of Terminalia catappa. The seed exts. only exhibited potent inhibition of CDHP formation and very low DPPH scavenging activity. (Ko et al., 2002) CA. 137:278156

·         Water, acetone, and chloroform extracts of Terminalia bellerica for their antimutagenic potency using the Ames Salmonella/microsome assay. Acetone extract exhibited variable inhibitory activity of 65.6%, and 69.7% with 4-O-nitrophenylenediamine (NPD) and sodium azide, respectively (as direct-acting mutagens), and 81.4% with 2-aminofluorene (2AF) (an S9-dependent mutagen), in the preincubation mode of experimentation. Inhibition with chloroform and water extracts was rather insignificant. Studies are well underway to isolate and identify the active polyphenolic compounds from acetone extract, which could be used as effective chemopreventive agents. (Kaur et al. 2003).

Pharmacology:

·         LD 50 of chebulin was 550 mg/kg in mice. It exhibited antispasmodic action on smooth muscle similar to that of papaverine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·         Protective effects of T. catappa leaf water ext. (TCE) and its major tannin component, punicalagin, on bleomycin-induced genotoxicity incultured Chinese hamster ovary cells.  Pre-treatment with TCE or punicalagin prevented bleomycin-induced hgprt gene mutations and DNA strand breaks.  TCE and punicalagin suppressed the generation of bleomycin-induced intracellular free radicals, identified as superoxides and hydrogen peroxides.  The effectiveness of TCE and punicalagin against bleomycin-induced genotoxicity could be, at least in part, due to their antioxidative potentials. (Chen et al., 2000) CA.133:84205

·         Essential oils of T. catappa leaves may possess antioxidant activity and hydroxyl radical scavenging activity. (Wang et al., 2000) CA. 133:155225

·         The effect against bacteria of petroleum ether (60-80 degrees C), chloroform and methanolic extract of dried root of Terminalia catappa Linn. (combrataceae) was employed by cup plate agar diffusion method. The chloroform extract showed prominent antimicrobial activity against S. aureus and E. coli as compared to other tested microorganisms, while petroleum ether extract was devoid of antimicrobial activity. The methanolic: extract exhibited MIC of 0.065 mg/ml against E. coli. and chloroform extract exhibited MIC of 0.4 mg/ml against S. aureus The chloroform has well as methanolic extracts showed good antimicrobial activity against Gram positive and Gram negative microorganisms. (Pawar et al., 2002)

·         Solvent extracts were prepared from green, yellow fallen and red fallen leaves of Terminalia catappa L. and their antioxidant activities were evaluated.  (Chyau et al., 2002) 137:384039

·         Effect of the tannin components of T. catappa L., their ability to prevent lipid peroxidn superoxide formation and their free radical scavenging activity were evaluated.  The results indicated that all of these components showed potent antioxidant activity.  Punicalagin and punicalin were the most abundant components and had the strongest antioxidative effects of this group of tannins. (Lin et al., 2001) CA.135: 221225
