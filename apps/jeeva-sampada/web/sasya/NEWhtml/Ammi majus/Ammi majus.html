 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Slender, erect, branched herb, 20-150 cm tall. It is widely cultivated and advented.


Availability :

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves oblong to triangular-ovate, 4-20 x 2-15 cm, 1-2 pinnate or ternate pinnate; leaflets lanceolate to oblanceolate, spinulose-serrate with appressed teeth, ocassionally lobed towards base, 1-7 x 5-20 mm; petioles slender, 3-13 cm long, narrowly sheathing; cauline leaves similar to basal or so. Peduncles slender, 3-15 cm long. Involucre of 10-25 ovate pinnately or ternate bracts with linear division, 1-3 cm long, usually reflexed. Rays 20-60, subfiliform, spreading –ascending, subequal, slightly scaberulous, 1.5-8 cm long. Umbellets 30-50 flowered, matured pedicels slender, unequal, spreading-ascending to somewhat contracted, 3-10 mm long. Involucel of 6-15, linear-lanceolate, acuminate, broadly scarious-margine, bracteoles 3-6 mm long. Calyx obsolete; stylopodium low conical, shorter than refelexed styles. Fruit oblong-ovate, 1.5-2 mm long, c 1 mm broad, ribs prominent, acute. Carpophore bipartite, halves weak, filiform.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Axillary or terminal umbels; Flower: White, small, hermaphrodite, pentamerous, epigynous ; Calyx: Represented by 5 incospicuous teeth; Corolla: Petals 5, alternating with sepals Androecium: Stamens 5, alternating with petals, filaments inflexed in bud, anthers dithecous, open length lengthwise ;Gynoecium: Bicarpellary, syncarpous, inferior bilocular ovary, placentation axile; Fruit: Ribbed, ellipsoid, green to greenish brown when immature and reddish brown at maturity.; Seed: hard with oily endosperm. Flowering & Fruiting: November – March. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated by seed.

Soil Type: It responds well in wide variety of soil srom sandy loam to clay loams however, well drained loamy soil is prefered.

Irrigation: one to two irrigations are required during winters.

(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-94) c.

Seven diterpenes were isolated from the higher boiling fraction of the essential oil from the aerial parts of Ammi majus. Their structures were proved based on novel irregular acyclic hydrocarbon skeleton which is named ammimajane. Structure elucidation was accomplished by pectroscopic methods. Aside from ammimajanes phytol, isophytol and isoelemicin were also identified. (Abraham et al, 1996). C.A., 1996, 125, 323035j.

Ground seeds of A. majus. were extracted with haxane, yielding 11.7% of crude oil. Physiochemical characterization of fatty acid composition of the oil were determined; only two acids were found in significative proportions. 18:1 (72.1% of the total); 18:2 (19.7%). The residual seed meal contained 27.5% crude protein (dry basis) with 5.1g/16gN of available lysine. Total and phytic acid phosphorus, phytic acid, calcium, ash, crude fibre, sugar polysaccharide and residual lipids are reported. (Qiroga et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 268230d.

Two new acetylated flavanol triglycosides: kaempferol and isorhamnetin 3-O-[2¢¢-(4¢¢¢-acetylrhamnosyl)-6¢¢-glucosyl] glucosides, were identified from the aerial parts of Ammi majus L. In addition, three known flavanol glycosides namely; isorhamnetin-3-O-rutinoside, kaempferol-3-O-glucoside and isorhamnetin-3-O-glucoside were also detected. (Singab, 1998).

Seeds from Ammi majus harvested and were yielded11.7% of crude oil. Physiochemical characteristics of fatty acid compound of the oil were also determinated, with only three acids in significant proportions: 16:0, 18:1, 18:2. The residual seed meals contained low levels of crude protein and high levels of available lysine in the Ammi majus. Values of total phosphorus, phytic acid, ash,crude fibre, calcium, polysaccharides (starch absent) and residual lipid were also reported. (Nolasco et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 132, 33176j.


Pharmacology:

The LD50 of xanthotoxin, imperatorin and bergapten injected into ventral lymph sac of toad, was 13.75, 14 and 32 mg/100g and intramuscularly in rat was 16, 33.5 and 94.5 mg/kg respectively. In acute experiments all three compounds produced convulsions, gastrointestinal inflammation and congestion especially of right heart. Death resulted from respiratory failure. (Rastogi and mehrotra, 1960-69) e.

Effect of imperatorin and isoimperatorin on drug metabolizing enzymes evaluated by study of their effect on hexobarbital- induced sleeping time and strychnine mortality in mice, as well as on hexobarbital and aminopyrine metabolism. Both compounds exhibited potent inhibition of hexabarbital hydroxylation and aminopyrine N-demethylation in in vivo and in vitro experiments. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-84)f. C.A. 1983, 99, 63863y.

Khellin inhibited noradrenaline and high K+ - induced contractions in rat isol;ated aorta. At high dose (0.32 mM) it increased camp levels and reduced Ca2+ influx in resting tissues and in tissues stimulated by noradrenaline and high K+. It is concluded that khellin caused non-specific inhibition of Ca2+ influx and may also exhibit intracellular actions, thus decreasing availability of Ca2+ required for activation in isolated aorta of rat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-94) d.

The effects of CuSO4 and crude extracts of the different parts of Ammi majus i.e., leaves, stems, roots, and flowers, on adult Biomphalaria alexandrina and bulinus truncates were investigated. Leaf and flowers of plant exhibited marked potency in killing the snail vectors of schistosomiasis. (Rawi et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 140964n.

Photoirriant effects of the five constituents of [A. majus] seeds (i.e. xanthotoxin, imperatorin, isopimpinellin, bergaptene and isoimperatorin) were investigated on albino mice. The photoirriant behaviour was evaluated by mouse ear assay, while their potencies were compared with photoirriant dose in 50 percent individuals (Ph. ID50). Isoimperatorin was the strongest photoirriant compound (Ph.ID50=0.0072 mg after five days), while imperatorin showed minimal photoirriant reaction (PhID50=0.3823mg after six days). The other three compounds exhibited intermediate persistant photoirriant reaction on mice skin. (Saeed and Khan, 1994). MAPA 9502, 0926, 1995.

Successive extracts of Ammi majus L. fruits were comparatively analysed with authentic coumarins by HPLC. This was followed by investigation of molluscidal activity of these extracts on Biomphalaria alexandrina snails using the bait formulation technique. A mong successive extracts of the plant under study ppt. of hexane ext (1), ppt. of petroleum ether (3) and ML of ether (6), free coumarin fraction (F2) and esopimpinellin (c) were found to be the most effective and most toxic for snails. So, the snail hosts of Schistosomiasis might be controlled by using hexane extract (1) and free coumarin fraction (F2) of A. majus L. as potential molluscicide of plant origin inside attractant food pellets (AFP). Furthermore, GC/MS of the volatile oil of the fruits of Ammi majus L. was found to contain F27 components. Coumarins were found to be the major constituents of the oil (51.09%). The antibacterial and antifungal activity of the oil were thus investigated. (Shalaby and Abdul, 1999). C.A. 2000, 13.2, 61630w.

Phytochemical fractionation of Ammi majus fruits resulted in the isolation of eight coumarin from the chloroformic extract namely, imperatorin, begaptin, oxypeucedanin, pabulenol, marmesin, xanthotoxin, isopimpinellin and heraclenin. They are identified as U.V. spectra, mass spectrometry and infrared spectra as well as mixed m.p. The molluscicidal activity of the isolated compounds against Biomphalaria alexandrina snails showed that bergapten was the most potent with LC50 and LC90 of 0.31 and 1.02ppm. Respectively. In addition isopimpinellin, oxypeucedanin and pabulenol also possessed marked molluscicidal activity and their 24 h LC50 values were also 3.89, 5.98 and 6.39ppm, respectively. However, marmesin showed the lowest molluscicidal activity and xanthotoxin, imperatorin and heraclenin were found to be inactive in concentrations up to 1500ppm. (Rizk and Hassan, 1999). C.A. 2001, 134, 337121g

The effect of Ammi majus fruit on experimentally induced kidney stones. Oxalate urolithiasis in male rats was produced by the addition of 3% glycolic acid to their diet. But the effects obtained by A. majus were, however, not significant. (Ahsan et al, 1989).

Ammi majus L. was found to possess a certain antischistosomal effect in mice infected with S. mansoni. This was proved by applying two techniques, the worm burden and the Oogram change. Bergapten, a pure furocoumarin naturally present in Ammi majus was found to have certain schistosomicidal effect. (Abdulla et al, 1978).

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

Psoralens derived from the fruit of Ammi majus in combination with exposure of the skin to the sun caused a severe phototoxic dermatitis in a Moroccan patient with vitiligo. The identification of the herbs by microscopic, chromatographic and biological techniques demonstrated that they contained the linear furanocoumarins (psoralens) 5-MOP, 8_MOP and oxypeucedanin each in a concentration of approximately 1%. (Ossenkoppele et al, 1991).

Ammi majus is known to induce toxic phytophotodermatitis i.e. allergic rhinitis and contact urticaria. (Kiistala et al, 1999).

Ammi majus has been known for centuries as phytotoxic plant. The photoreactivity of the plants depends on their furocoumarins (psoralens) content. These cause skin damage by phytotoxic mechanism, when activated by long wave ultraviolet radiation (UVA). The results are cilinical changes such as eryhtema, bullae in the skin 24-72 hr later. Followed by hyperpigmentation. (Kavli and Volden, 1984).

Ammi majus causes severe ophthalmic changes, in particular pigmentary retinopathy in photosensitized domestic fowl. But the photoactive principle is currently used in the treatment of psoriasis in man, and such patient should be examined for possible ocular toxicity. (Shlosberg and Egyed, 1983).

Ammi majus grows on the coastal region of southern United States and in other parts of the world. This plant causes severe photosensitization in livestock and probably contributes to the severe photosensitization outbreak seen in Texas. Sheep were fed finely ground seed of Ammi majus via stomach tube at dose rates of 1,2,4, and 8 g/kg of body weight and exposed to sunlight. The single dose of 8g/kg produced severe clinical signs ( in 24 to 48 hours): cloudy cornea, conjunctivokeratitis, photophobia, and edema of the muzzle, ears, and vulva. Daily dosing at 2 and 4 g/kg produced (in 72 to 96 hours) similar signs, whereas the smallest dose (1g/kg) produced mild irritation of the muzzle. Patholgic changes included (1) corneal edema and marked neutrophilic infiltration of the cornea and corneal/ciliary process, (2) subacute ulcerative and exudative dermatitis of the skin of ears, muzzle, and vulva and (3) mild focal tubular degeneration (Vacuolar type) of the kidney. (Witzel et al, 1978).

Feeding Ammi majus to cattle and sheep caused photosensitization in both species. It also caused photosensitization in human beings who had dermal contact with the plant and subsequent exposure to sunlight. (Dollahite et al, 1978).

Chronic lesions of the beak, footweb, and eye of geese photosensitized by the consumption of Ammi majus (L.), eighty-nine percent of the affected birds showed ocular involvement, comprising chemosis, lepharoconjuncitvitis, keratitis, symblepharon, ankyloblepharon, cicratricial ectropium and mydriasis. (Egyed et al, 1975).

Ammi majus induced photosensitization are described. The acute changes were inflammatory in nature whereas the chronic changes included severe deformities of the beak and footwebs, mydriasis and eccentric location of the pupil. (Egyed et al, 1975).

Photoirritant effects of the five constituents of [Ammi majus] seeds (i.e. xanthotoxin, imperatorin, isopimpinellin, bergaptene and isoimperatorin) were investigated on albino mice. The photoirritant behaviour was evaluated by mouse ear assay, while their potencies were compared with photoirritant dose in 50 percent individuals (Ph. ID50). Isoimperatorin was the strongest photoirritant compound (Ph.ID50- 0.0072 mg after five days), while imperatorin showed minimal photoirritant reaction (PhID50=0.3823 mg after six days). The other three compounds exhibited intermediated persistent photoirritant reaction on mice skin. (Saeed and Khan, 1994).
