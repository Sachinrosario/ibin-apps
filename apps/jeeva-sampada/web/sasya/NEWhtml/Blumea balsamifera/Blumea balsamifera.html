 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennial shrub or undershrub, evergreen. It is found in West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam and Andaman & Nicobar Islands. In dry fields, grasslands, on mountains slopes and along river banks, ascending up to 1200 m.
Availability :  

Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant grows wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem woody with grayish brown bark, simple at base, corymbosely branched above, densely wooly with yellowish white hairs. Leaves usually narrowly oblong-lanceolate or oblong-obovate, ovate, 5.0-28.5 x 1.5-10.5 cm, acute to apiculate at apex, entire or pinnately lobed, serrate-serrulate along margin, coriaceous, upper surface rugose and pilose, lower densely silky-wooly; petioles up to 3 cm long. Head 4-8 mm in diam. in axillary and terminal lax panicles; peduncles 3-10 mm long. Involucral bracts sometimes larger than florets, linear, 1-9 mm long, acute, densely wooly on dorsal surfaces, herbaceous. Receptacles 2-4 mm in diam., slightly convex, glabrous. Corolla of bisexual florets yellow, tubular, 4-7 mm long, 5-lobed; lobes acute, papillate, pubescent. Corolla of female florets filiform, 6 mm long, 2-4 lobed, glabrous. Achenes brown, oblong, 10-ribbed, silky. Pappus 4-6 mm long.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Heads sessile or peduncled in rounded clusters on the stout branches of large spreading or pyramidal panicles; Flower: Outer flower multiseriate, pistillate, fertile; inner disk-Flowers hermaphrodite few, fertile ; Pappus red;  ; Fruit: Achenes 10-ribbed, silky,; Seed: . Flowering & Fruiting :.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  A distillated infusion of leaves is used medicinally to cure throat and chest ailments, dropsy and high fever.


Chemistry:
Two new flavonoids – 2R,3R-dihydroquercetin-4’-methyl ether (fig. 1) and 2R,3R-dihydroquercetin-4’,7-dimethyl ether (fig. 2) – isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Cryptomeridiol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

A new flavone – 5,3’,5’-trimethoxydihydroflavone isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Isolateion and characterization of three new sesquiterpene lactones – bluemealactones A (fig. 3a), B (fig. 3b) and C (fig. 3c) from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

The chloroform extract of the above ground parts of Blumea balsamifera yielded a (2R,3R)-7,5,-dimethoxy-3,5,2’-trihydroxyflavone. (Barua and Sharma, 1992).

Two new flavonoids isolated from Blumea balsamifera were identified as 3,5,3-trihydroxy-7,4-dimethoxy-flavone, and 3,5,3,4-tetrahydroxy-7-methoxyflavone respectively on the basis of data of NMR, MS, IR and UV. (Deng et al, 1996). C.A1996, 125, 297101f.


Pharmacology:
New sesquiterpene lactones exhibited the antitumour activity against Yoshida sarcoma cells in tissue culture. (Fujimoto et al, 1988).

Blumea balsamifera with other plants was tested for allergic reaction on scalp but does not showed any allergic reaction and thus it can be used as active ingredient for hair restoration preparation. (Nakaguchi et al, 1999). C.A. 2001, 134, 136450q.

Pharmacognosy:
Microscopic, chemical and physical Studies of Blumea balsamifera (Linn.) D.C. leaves revealed certain diagnostic characters. The upper epidermis consisted of slightly wavy walled cells with glandular trichromes but without stomata. The lower epidermis consisted of a layer of slightly wavy walled cells with glandular trichomes and anomocytic stomata, a multicellular covering, and glandular trichomes. The lamina contained one to two layers of palisade cells, 4-5 layers of spongy cells; the midrib and petioles consisted of epidermis, collenchyma, and parenchyma vessels and fibres. In chemical and physical studies, it was found that the values of loss upon drying, acid-insoluble ash, and total ash were not more than 8,4, and 16% respectively while the values of alcoholic- soluble extractives, water-soluble extractives, and ether-soluble extractives were not less than 17, 13 and 7% respectively. Chemical tests showed the presence of saponin, triterpenoids, steroids, reducing sugars, and proteins. (Chuakul et al, 1997). C.A 1999, 130, 194269r.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine in stomach upset of infants due to defect in mother’s milk (Leaf, Borthakur, 1976)
