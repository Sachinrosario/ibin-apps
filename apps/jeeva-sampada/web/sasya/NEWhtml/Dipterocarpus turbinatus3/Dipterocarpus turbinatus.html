 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A lofty tree. It occurs in the ever-green or semi-evergreen moist tropical forests of Assam and Andamans.
It requires humid climate and mean annual rainfall varying from 2500mm to 9000mm or even more.  The most abundant growth is found between the altitudes of 300m and 760m.  It is shade tolerant in its younger stages.

Availability :  
Evergreen tree of Eastern Bengal, Chittagong, Burma and of the Andaman Islands.  Distributed to Singapore.

It is endemic to Western Ghats of Karnataka, Keral and Tamil Nadu.

Status : 

The Red List Status of this species has been assessed as "Endangered (Globally)".

Exploitation of the tree is prohibited owing to the large number of trees are already killed by tapping.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

This tree is said to be one of the loftiest of Indian trees, individual specimens being sometimes seen 250ft in height. It has an unbranchable pale grey trunk and small symmetrical crown;  Bark yellowish brown with grey and white patches.  A resin is exuded from the bark when cut.  Leaves entire or undulate; stipules large, softly tomentose, covering terminal bud, caducous.  Inflorescence of axillary, simple or branched few flowered recemes or panicles.  Flowers white and pink shaded, fragrant, Fruit winged, nut like brown, with 2 prominent calyx lobes and three smaller ones.

Seed Biology :

Seedling Germination :
These are generally propagated by seeds, which are recalcitrant and lose their viability within a few days.

Seeds are collected in May or June.  Only plump and heavy seeds should be sown.  The seed viability is low.  Seeds are dibbled at 10 x 10cm, with their apex downwards, in well drained nursery beds free of litter and kept shaded.  Germination varies from 40 to 90 percent.  The seedling is sensitive to drought.


Cultivation:
The seedlings are transplanted in the middle of August.  Six month old seedlings are planted out.  About 33 percent of seedlings establish.  This tree is sensitive to fire and requires successful protection.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Wood, twig bark

Uses

Oleo-resin-- Considerable confusion exists in the literature of Garjan and Wood-oil.  Apparently several species of Dipterocarpus yield balsamic products to which the same name garjan oil is assigned.  In Burma one set of oils is, however, collectively spoken of as Kanyin-oils another as In-oils.  The term garjan appears to be unknown to Burmans.

  • Oleo-resin is used as external application for  ulcers, ringworm and other cutaneous  affections. It is used in gonorrhoea and gleet and as an expectorant.
  • Oleo-resin of this tree is used with asafoetida and coconut oil as an application for large ulcers.  It is also used for treating rheumatism.
  • The oleo-resin is used in lithographic inks and for providing an anti-corrosive coating to iron.  
  • It is also used for the preservation of timber and bamboo; caulking boats and preparation of baking varnishes and as medicine.
  • Twig bark-contain 9% tannin and 7.3% soluble non-tans.
  • The kanyin oils,  are obtainable only after the cut surface of wood has been charred. The thick dry deposit that forms on the wood clogs the pores and prevents the escape of the oil, and that this is fired in preference to being chipped off as a matter of convenience.  It burns readily and quickly, thus exposing the pores, whereas it would take some time to effect the same result by chipping or paring the surface.

Harvesting Practices
A cone –shaped cavity is cut into the trunk of the tree, 2-3 ft from the ground and fire lighted to char the surface of the wound when the Oleo –resin starts flowing out and is periodically removed. When the flow stops, the wound surface is burnt or scraped or a fresh wound made to induce further flow. The season of collection is November- May.  The upper surface of the hollow are rechipped three or four times during the season. On an average, an healthy and mature tree will yield up to 10kg of resin each year.  

Produce and Products
Oil, Resin, timber

Chemistry
Garjan or wood oil:

This class of substances, called wood-oils, forms the connecting link between the oils and resins of the vegetable kingdom.  They consist of a volatile oil holding in solution a resin and are generally classed under the head of balsams.  It  is reported to act as solvent to caoutchouc.

As found in the bazaar, this substance generally occurs as brown oily-looking, semi-transparent liquid, in dour strongly resembling a mixture of balsam of copaiba with a small portion of naptha.

Other Uses

  • Wood exudes an oil when heated. Garjan oil- adulterant of copaiba balsam, ingredient  of lithographic ink    and anti-corrosive coating , composition for iron, lamp oil, and largely employed in  preserving bamboo wicker-work from the attacks of insects and in paving the bottoms of boats.  It is also used as varnish.

  • Wood is used for internal construction work, packaging cases, tea boxes, paneling, flooring and carriage and wagon construction. Wood is also used for canoes, but more generally for planking.

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country
Garjan oil of Burma and Chittagong make the usual admission that, owing to the  cheap price of kerosene, the trade in wood-oil has very considerably declined.  It is now mainly used for torches and in water proofing.  The trade in the medicinal garjan oil must be very limited indeed.  It appears to be mainly from the Andaman Islands and to be produce of D. alatus and possibly D. turbinatus.
