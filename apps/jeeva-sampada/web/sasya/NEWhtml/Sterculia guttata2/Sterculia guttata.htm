HABIT & HABITAT

A tall tree occurs in the western ghats from Konkan to kerala, hills of south India and in Assam ascending up to an altitude of 600 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A tall tree, trunk straight. Bark much cracked. Young shoots clothed with stellate down. Leaves ovate-oblong, acute or acuminate, glabrous above, stellately pubescent, prominently nerved and reticulately veined beneath, base rounded or subcordate. Petioles terete, stout, pubescent. Stipules ensiform, caducous.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers yellow spotted with purple, generally arranged in fascicles of 3, along the branches of short rusty-pubescent racemes, the pedicel of the central flower of each fascicle longer than the others. Bracts lanceolate, beneath each flower, that beneath the central flower of each fascicle the larger. Calyx companulate, densely pubescent without, clothed with stellate hairs within, lobes oblong, subacute, about equalling the tube, ultimately reflexed.

Male flowers : Staminal-column slender, anthers 10-12.

Hermaphrodite flowers : Ovary globose, strigose with stellate hairs; gynophore stout, round. Style stout, deflexed. Follicles 1-5, obovoid, deep orange coloured, villous without, smooth and pink within. Seeds large, size of a bean, oblong. Testa long, brittle.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:Wood used as fuel.

Wood: Sapwood and heartwood not distinct. Wood pale yellowish or greyish white to light greyish brown, often discoloured. Extremely soft to soft and often spongy and easily dealt with a finger nail, extremely light to light. Specific gravity 0.16 – 0.46 air dry. Somewhat lustrous on the quarter sawn or radial surface. Straight grained even and coarse textured. Easily perishable.


Parts Used:


Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:

Wood used as fuelwood. Calorific value : 4,860 cal., 8,748 Btu.

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:

Timber liable to stain. Quick conversion and stacking in open under cover recommended to avoid discolouration.


Other uses:


The bark yields a good fibre, used for cordage and rough fabrics.

Roasted seeds eaten, especially in times of scarcity.
