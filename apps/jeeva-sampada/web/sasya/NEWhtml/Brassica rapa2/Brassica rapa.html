REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Inflorescence: Racemose raceme; Flower: Large, bright yellow, pedicillate, complete, hermaphrodite, actinomorphic, tetramerous and hypogynous ; Calyx: Sepals 4, in two whorls of two each, polesepalous, petaloid ; Corolla: Petals 4, polypetalous, cruciform, each petals distinguished into claw and limb, deciduous ;Androecium: Stamens 6, in two whorls, polyandrous, tetradynamous, 4 inner long amd two outer short, dithecous, basifixed, and introrse; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, syncarpous, ovary superior unilocular but become bilocular later on due development of false septa, ovles many in each locule, placentation parietal, style and stigma bilobed; Fruit: Pods glabrous, sub-erect, valves with mid-rib and flexuous veins; Seed: Small, erect, pale or dark .


Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : January-April .
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  birds, cattles, wind or water.   
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 20.

Chemistry:
The methanol extract from herb of Brassiaca rapa was found to inhibit HSV-1 through bioassay directed fractionations the extract anti HSV1 agent was isolated by chromatographic separation using Amberlite XAD-4 and Sephadex LH-20 compound was identified as 3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside which showed active against HSV-1 with 50% effective concentration of 0.42 mg/ml and 50% cytotoxicity of 5.0 mg/ml. (Kim et la, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 90127b.

Unambiguous and complete assignments of1H and 13C NMR chem. shifts for stigma-5-en-3-O-b-glucoside, isolated from Brassica rapa sb. sp. Campestris and its acetyl derivatives are presented based on DEPT, HETCOR, COLOC, HMBC, COSY-45°, NOE SY and J-resolved experiments. (Faizi et al, 2001).

Twelve compounds were isolated from the pollen of Brassica campestris L. Six of them were identified as nonacosane, b-sitosterol, sucrose, hexadecanoic acid, glyceroloctadcyl ether and daueosterol. (Peng and Yang, 1998). C.A. 199, 130, 49769z.


Pharmacology:

5-Methyl-L-cysteine sulfoxide decreased blood cholesterol elevation induced in rats by a high cholesterol diet, but had no effect on phospholipid levels. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)

Mustard is an important source of pollen alergan, responsible for type I hypersensitivity disorders. In the present study, BC pollen extract was characterized by TLIEF, SDS-PAGE and immunoprinting. Immunoblot analysis with individual patients sera detected four allergenic proteins of 90, 67, 60 and 14 Kda. A protein of 90 Kda isolated by gel filtration was the most allergic protein ELISA inhibition. The findings shall be applicable in standardization of future batches of BC pollen extract to be used for allergy diagonosis. (Singh et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 312159k.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for boils (Seed oil, Daret al, 1984); Migraine (Whole plant, Tiwari et al, 1979); Muscle pain (Seed, Majumdar, 1978); Skin disease (Seed, Purohit, 1985); Bronchitis, cough, leprosy, pneumonia, scabies, syphilis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970)
