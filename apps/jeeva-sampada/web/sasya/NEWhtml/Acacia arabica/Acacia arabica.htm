Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Small tree with prickly stem. Cosmopolitan in the tropics. Dry to moist tropical areas are suitable for this species but can be grown in lower hills. They are tender to frost when young. Best plantation is up to 500 m altitude. Occurs throughout the country, except in moist regions near the coast. Widely planted in the Deccan, Gujrat and Sind. Throughout the greater part of India.


Availability :

Throughout the Presidency, except in moist regions near the coast. Widely planted in Deccan, Gujrat and Sind. Throughout the greater part of India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tree moderately tall about 8 m with spread of 6-7 m like an umbrella. Main trunk may be 60-75 cm in diameter. Dark brown or black longitudinally fissured bark, branchlets slender, terete, pubescent when young.

Leaves compound, bipinnate, 2–4" long, main rachis downy, often with glands, stipular spines variable, sharp, straight, often absent, pinnae 4–9 pairs. Leaflets subsessile 10–25 pairs, linear-oblong, subobtuse, glabrous or nearly so. Each leaf has a pair of sharp spines at the base.

Flowers small, fragrant, yellow, borne in spikes, peduncle axillary. Calyx companulate divided about 1/3 of the way down. Teeth distinct, deltoid. Corolla white, twice as long as the calyx, divided about ½ way down, lobes lanceolate, acute. Filaments white. Anthers yellow.

Pods shortly stalked, linear-oblong, thin, flat, strap like, flexible, attenuated at both the ends, reticulately veined, green when fresh, pale brown and glabrous when ripe. Seeds 5 to 6, circular and brown.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling germination: Fresh seeds are sown in February in polybags or seedbeds. They can be sown in situ, 2-3 seeds in each pit in rainy season. Stored seeds are soaked in boiled water for 24 hours before sowing to expedite their germination. These are transplanted during the monsoon season.

Spacing may be 6X6 m but for hedge it may be 3X2 m.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood hard and heavy. Good for fuel. Used for charcoal purpose.

Wood: Timber is hard and durable. Average unit weight at 12% moisture content 835 kg/m3. Mostly used for wheels, well-curbs.

Other uses:

Dried seeds used as vegetable to form a dry preparation called panchakuta in Rajasthan.

Twigs are used as tooth brushes, fodder for animals, particularly for goats and sheep.

Foliage and pods rich in proteins and used to feed animals such as goats, sheep and cows.

Gum is used medicinally and by calico printer. Form an inferior substitute for true Gum Arabic.

Gum arabic used in foods and confectionery and also in pharmaceutical preparations.

Bark and pods supply valuable tanning material. It fixes atmospheric nitrogen, thus improving soil fertility.

Plant can be used in desertification control to re-establish a vegetation cover over degraded areas as well as for sand dune fixation and wind erosion control works.

Miscellaneous Information

 
Var 1. Cupressiformis, Stewart. – branches closing up to the trunk like a cypress. Var. cupressina. Aitch Var. Ramkanta, Grah. This is a rather doubtful variety. The peculiar growth of the branches may, according to Stewart, have been caused by ages of ruthless lopping of the side branches. Edgeworth states that near Multan, the variety is sometimes seen on the same tree with ordinary form. It is common in Deccan and Gujrat.

Var 2. Vediana ; Bark more deeply cracked and exfoliating; crown formed of twisted angular interlacing branches; spines usually white, stout, up to 2 ½ in. long; pods flat, shortly stalked, ¾ in broad, little constricted between the seeds.

‘Indian Forester’ v. 23 (1898) p. 359, Sir D. Brandis called attention to a variety of babhal called "Kaulia babhal" in vernacular in Berar, where it was found. This is also common in the Deccan (Pune, Sholapur, etc.) where it is called "Vedi babhal". Wood of this babhal is considered useless for building or agricultural implements and fit only for firewood.

Ordinary form with moniliform pods is called "Telia babhal" in Berar as well as in Khandesh on the confine of Berar but called as "Godi babhal". Wood is used for every purpose for which strong timber is needed.

Pods of both are equally valuable as food for sheep and goats.
