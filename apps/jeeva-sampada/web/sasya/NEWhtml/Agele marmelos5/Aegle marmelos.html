HABIT & HABITAT
In India it is reported to occur in the Sub-Himalayan tracts from Jhelum eastward and also Southward to the Central and Southern India. In the Penninsular India, it is restricted to the dry and moist deciduous forests of the Eastern and Western Ghats. It is cultivated in interior Karnataka (Bangalore, Belgaum, Bidar, Dharwar, Hassan, Mysore, Raichur, Tumkur, Uttara Kannada) as well as other regions.
Moist deciduous forests in the subtropical W. Himalayas at elevation up to 1200 m; also in C. & S. India. Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Bihar, West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh & Andaman Islands. It also occurs in Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Nepal. Widely cultivated in S.E.Asia, Malaysia, Tropical Africa and United States.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
It is a small to moderate-sized tree with branches armed with strong axillary spine 1-3 cm long. Bark dark grey, slightly corky, leaves alternate, 3-foliate, rarely 5-foliate. Deciduous tree, up to 12-20 m high, armed; main trunk ca 30 cm across; spine 2-3 cm long; bark bluish-grey. Leaves pinnately trifoliate, dimorphic; leaflets ovate-elliptic to elliptic-lanceolate, terminal ones ca 13x 6.5 cm and lateral ca 7 x 4 cm, apex blunt, base oblique, margin shallowly crenate-serrate; lateral sessile and terminal stalked; petioles slender, ca 6 cm long, glabrous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Axillary panicles ; Flower: Bracteate, bracteolate,, hermaphrodite, greenish-white scented ; Calyx: flat, pubescent, four-lobed,  sometimes obscure, ciliolate ; Corolla: Petals 4, much imbricate, free, oblong, coriaceous ; Androecium: Stamens numerous, sometimes 50 or more, filaments, subulate, glabrous, free or united, anthers linear-oblong, apiculate ; Gynoecium:  Ovary 11 celled, glabrous, ovoid-oblong, faintly ribbed, seated on elevated disk, cells many ovuled, ovules biseriate; Fruit:  Berry with woody rind.  Flowering & Fruiting : May-July. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by animals or human agency.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Root and bark are highly medicinal and used for the cure of intermittent fever. Leaves are used in the treatment of opthalmia. Diluted fresh juice extracted from leaves is applied in catarrhs and fever. Unripe fruits are astringent, stomachic, digestive and good remedies for chronic diarrhoea and dysentery. Ripe fruits are sweet, nutritious and cooling. The pulp of ripe fruits is used for preparing a sherbet, which is effective in dyspepsia. The mucilaginous substance surrounding the seeds is an adhesive, which is used as a varnish for pictures and water colour paints. Leaves, stem bark, root, fruit and seeds contains essential oils, chiefly ‘marmalosin’. The Hindus regard this plant as sacred and its leaves are used for various rituals. It is often grown in and around Shiva Temples in India.


Chemistry:
Umbeliferone, skimmianine, marmin (fig.1) and g-sitosterol have been isolated from immature bark of Aegle marmelos. The constitution of marmin has been established as 7-(3:7-didydroxy-3: 7-dimethyloctyloxy) coumarin. (Chatterjee and Bhattacharya, 1959).

Auraptene (fig.2), (C19H22O3) umbelliferone, marmin, lupeol and skimmianine have been isolated from Root of Aegle  marmelos. (Chatterjee and Chardhary, 1960).

Aegeline was isolated from leaves of A. marmelos (m.p. 176°) and identified as N-b-hydroxy-b-methoxyphenylethylcinnamide  (Chatterjee et al., 1959).

b-sitosterol, alkaloid A (m.p. 172°-3°C) and alkaloid B (m.p. 136-7°) well separated from defatted ethanolic fromtion of Aegle marmolos bark. And they confirmed that alkaloid A was not rutacine. (Khaleque et al., 1970) C.A. 1973,78:1973S.

Aegelenine (fig.3), (Phenolic base containing an oxazole and pyridine moiety) the minor alkaloid was isolated form leaves of Aegle marmelos  and structure has been elucidated and is found to be identical with halfordinol. (Chatterjee and Majumder, 1971).

Essential oil was obtained by steam distillation from leaves Aegle marmelos, which contains a-phellandren, P-cymene, cineole, d-limonene, ethyl n-amylketone, methyl n-heptyl ketone, citronellal, linalool, citral, eugenol, methyl eugenol, caryophyllene and cuminyl alcohol. (Bhadari and Gupta, 1972). C.A. 1974, 80: 40922k.

Psoralen (C11H6O3, m.p. 169-70°), xanthotoxin (C12H8O4, m.p. 145-6°), O-methylscopoletin (C11H10O4 m.p. 145°), scopoletin (C10H6O4 m.p. 204°), tembamide (C10H17NO3, m.p. 156-7°), skimmin were isolated from Root of Aegle marmelos. (Shoebet al., 1973).

Leaves of A. marmelos contained condensed tannins, Phlobatannins, flavan-3-ols, leucoanthocyanins, anthocyanins and flavonoid glycosides, flavan-3-ols are conoverted into leucoanthocyanins which are then finally converted into phlobatanins. (Bajaj et al., 1975). C.A. 1975, 83:144519n.

 Water soluble polysaccharide was isolated from the Fruit pulp of A. marmelos, on hydrolysis yielded 20.4% galactose, 10.7% arabinose, 25.2% uronic acid and trace of L. rahomnose. (Haq and Awal, 1977). C.A., 1977, 87: 197267t. 

Lactonic constituent Aeglelinol (fig.4) (m.p. 175-177°C) was isolated from the Root and stem-bark, aegelinol is 3’ epimer of decursinol. (Chatterjee et al., 1978).

Four alkaloids, O-(3,3-dimethy)-halfordinol (fig.5), N-2-ethoxy-2-C4-methoxyphenyl) ethylcinnamamide (fig.5a), N-2-methoxy-2-[4-(3’,3’-dimethylallyloxy) phenyl] cinnamamide (fig.5b), and N-2-methoxy-2-(4-methoxyphenyl)-ethylcinnamamide (fig.5c) have been isolated from the leaves of Aegle marmelos. (Manandhar et al., 1978).

Aurapten, marmin, umbelliferone and lupeol from the bark and lupeol, sitosterol and ageline was isolated from the leaves carbon-13 NBR spectal shifts of aurapten and marmin well discussed. (Patra et al., 1979).

Lupeol and (S) – (+) – marmesin were isolated and identified by elementary analysis, and spectral data from mature bark. (Khaleque et al., 1980). C.A. 1981, 95, 200604y.

The carbohydrate was isolated from bael seeds was resolved into 4 pure glycoprotein fractions, on these contained galactose, glucose, arabinoe and rhamnose in the molar ratios of 6:2:8:3.  (Mandal and Mukherjee, 1981). C.A. 1981, 94, 99823w.

A alkaloid marmelin (fig.6) was isolated from unripe Fruits of Aegle marmelos  and identified as N-2-hydroxy-2-[4-(3’-3’dimethallyloxy) phenyl] ethyl cinnamamide, Ageline, imperatorin, alloimperatorin and xanthoxoxol well also isolated. (Sharma et al., 1981).  

Neutral polysaccharide of the isolated from the crude polysaccharide of the Fruit pulp from bael cotained arabinose, galactose and glucose in molar rations of 2 : 3 : 14. (Basak et al., (1982). C.A. 1982, 96, 48985j.

Alkaloids, N-2-[4-(3’-3’-dimethyllyloxy) phenyl] ethyl cinnamide (fig.7a), N-2-hydroxy-2-[4-(3’,3’-dimethylallyloxy) phenyl] ethyl cinnamide (fig.7b), N-4-methoxy styryl cinnamide (fig.7c) and N-2-hydroxy-2-(4-hydroxy phenyl) ethylcinnamide (fig.7d) were isolated and characterized. (Govindachari and Premila, 1983).

Umbelliferone, dictamine, xanthotoxol, scoparone, xanthotoxin, isopimpinellin, isoimperatorin and marmin along with amides N-2-methoxy-2-(-4-methoxy phenyl) ethylcinnamide, mameline, marmeline methylether, bergapten marmesin, osthol, isoimperation and auraptin were isolated and identified by HPLC and GC-MS. (Reisch et al., 1985). C.A. 1985, 103,175468f.

Coumarin, 6’,7’ –epoxyauraptene (fig.8) (C19H22O4,51o) was isolated and structure was determined by spectral evidence. (Bhattacharya and Jash,1989). 

Marmesin –1”- a-L-g rhamnopyranoside (fig.9a) and 1,5 – dihydroxy-6-methoxy-2-methyl anthraquinone (fig.9b) were isolated and characterized. Lupeol and b-sitostenol also isolated from the stem bark.(Nema and Srivastva,1991) CA, 1992,117:44563z

Two new compounds,2-(2-hydroxy-4-methoxy phenyl)vinyl acetate (fig.10) and xanthotoxol-8-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig.11) have been isolated from the heartwood of Aegle marmelos along with b-sitosterol and lupeol.(Jain etal 1991).

Bael seedoil content were determined with 40.7% oil. The components of fatty acids are palmatic acid(22.7%) Linoleic(22.6%) and linolenic(19.6%) which showed beneficial effects in the regeneration of two types of tumour. (Pal et al 1993) CA. 1994.120: 212605x

Two lignan gluosides, (-)-lyoniresinol 2a - O - b - D glucopyranoside and (-) – 4-epi-lyoniresinol 3a - O -b - D – glucopyranoside have isolated together with two known lignan glucosides,(+) – lyniresinol 3a - O - b - D glucopyranoside and (-)-lyoniresinol 3a - O - b - D glucopyranoside. (Ohashi and Kitgawa,1994) C.A. 1995, 122: 261031g,

Two new 7-geranyloxy coumarins, chloramarmin (fig.12a) and aeglin (fig.12b)were isolated from the bark of Aegle marmelos and there structure were assigned on the basis of NMR data (Ohashi et al) C.A. 1995,123: 310350 s

Two new anthroquinones 7,8 – dimethoxy-1-hydroxy-2-methyl anthraquinone and 6- hydroxy-2-methoxy-3-methyl anthraquinone were isolated from heartwood and structures were determined by spectral and chemical methods (Srivastava et al 1996).

Two new coumarin were isolated and identified from the methanol extract of the Root of Aegle marmelos and named as marminal and 7’-O-methyl marmin.(Yang et al 1996).

A C-glycosylated propelargonidin has been isolated from the aqueous extract of the pulp of unripe Fruit of A. marmelos.
