 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, annual herb with strong anise odour from subfusiform roots; branching, glabrous and glaucous. It is found throughout tropical and subtropical India.


Availability :  
Conservation/ Threat Status : Plants are commercially cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves pinnately decompoud, with filiform ultimate divisions, petiolate, membranous; petioles slender, sheathing; cauline leaves reduced upward. Inflorescence of lax, terminal and lateral compoud umbels; peduncles elongate. Involucre and involucel lacking. Rays numerous, subequal, spreading-ascending, umbellets numerous flowered. Flowers yellow; petals suborbicular with a narrower obtuse inflexed apex; calyx obsolete; stylopodium conical, styles short, reflexed. Pedicels slender, unequal, spreading-ascending. Fruit ovoid to elliptic-oblong, compressed dorsally, mericarps pentagonal in transection, glabraous. Vittae solitary in intervals, 2-4 on commissure, commissural face plane to slightly concave. Carpophore bipartite.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence:
Compound umbel; Flower: Small, actinomorphic, hermaphrodite, pentamerous and epigynous; Calyx: represented by 5 inconspicuous teeth; Corolla: Petals 5 alternating with sepals, inflexed in bud and they fall off soon ; Androecium: Stamens 5, alternating with petals, filaments are influxed in bud, anthers dithecous, introrse and opening lengthwise.; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, syncarpous with an inferior ovary, placentation axile, epigynous disc is pesent at the top of the ovary ; Fruit: Cremocarp ; Seed: convex and flat. Flowering & Fruiting: October-March. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by birds and animals.

Chemical variability:
Pentane exts. Of the seeds (fruits) of 35 cultivars of dill have been examd. By GC.  Three broad chemo types could be distinguished by the presence of carvone, and the presence or absence of myristicin and dillapiole.  The major components of the cultivars of type 1 were limonene (36.9-46.7%), carvone (17.8-45.6%), myristicin (0.2-20.3%), and dillapiole (8.0-22.3%), where as in type 2 they were limonene (31.0-40.9%), carvone (25.1-47.4%), and dillapiole (6.3-31.8%).  No myristicin was found in the second type.  In the third chemo type, neither myristicin nor dillapiole were characterized; instead, limonene (39.5-50.7%) and carvone (43.7-57.7%) were only major constituents.  Although chemotype-3 (27- cultivars) possessed a chem. compn. Typical of European dill seed, the oil content was found to vary from 1.75-5.8%1; Dill types representing 34 varieties and locations were compared with respect to plant characteristics, field yield, variability, oil content, and o-phellandrene, limonene, anethoffuran, and carvone contents.  The seed oil contents ranged between 5 and 30%.  Drug industry dill types tended to be higher in phellandrene while giant and  tetraploid varieties contained higher levels of carvone 2;  The essential oils of the seeds  (fruits) of 66 cultivars of dill, which were obtained by hydrodistn. And hexane extn., have been examd. By GC.  The oil content was found to vary from 1.91% to 7.25%.  The Three known chemo types were observed., but there were also transition types.  In addn., a new chemotype which contained limonene (43.7%), dihydrocarvone (3.1%), carvone (41.2%) and Myristicin (11.7%) was found in the assortment of dill cultivars.  No dillapiole was found in this type3.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Sandy loam soil that are well drained are suitable for cultivation of this crop.

Nutrient requirements: Dill responds well to application of fertilizers.Farmyard manures are applicable during planting.

Water management: It is moisture loving plant and requires at least 2-3 irrigations between planting and harvesting.                                       

Harvesting period / Method: When the plant is cultivated for herb oil, it is harvested after 3-31/2 months of planting when the plant are in bloom and the plant are in milky stage. For seeds, the crop is harvested when seeds are fully developed and still green in colour.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    
Fruits are acrid, bitter and used as deodorant, digestive, carminative, stomachic, anthelmintic, anodyne, anti-inflammatory, diuretic, emmenagogue, galactagogue, expectorant, cardiotonic, anaphrodisiac, febrifuge, sudorific, antidysenteric, alexiteric and vulnerary. They are useful in halitosis, flatulence, colic, dyspepsia, intestinal worms, odontalgia, arthralgia, inflammation, strangury, amenorrhoea, agalactia, cough, asthma, bronchitis, fevers, ulcers, skin diseases, uropathy, gleet, syphilis and cardiac debility.

Pharmacology:
Isodillapiole tribromide showed fungicidal activity against Helminthosporium oryzae and Aspergillus niger. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Mutagenesis inhibitors containing ³ 1 compound selected from dill apiole, apiole and myristicin as active ingredients. The mutagenesis inhibitors are used as pharmaceuticals and food as prophylactic agents against cancers. Dill apiole at 10-4 M showed 43.4% inhibition on mutation detected by modified Ames test. (Nakasugi, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, P25668v

Hexane fractions from methanolic extracts of Anethum graveolons revealed potent inhibitory activities against the resistance of multi-drug resistant staphylococcus aureus SA2 when combined with ampicillin or chloramphenicol (Carvone) showed resistance inhibition at the level of 20-50 mg/ml when combined with 100/150 mg/ml of Am or Cm respectively. (Kim et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 191467t.

Indian dill oil, which was obtained from the seeds of Anethum sowa is called Anethi oil. The oil is used in perfumery as a replacement for flavouring purposes as it contains dillapiol, which is abortifacient and toxic to both liver and kidneys. (Ranade, 1998). MAPA 1998, 9804-2405.
