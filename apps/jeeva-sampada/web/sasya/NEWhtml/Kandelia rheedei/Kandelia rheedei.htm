Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Small tree found in tidal swamps along the coast. Gregarious along the banks of intertidal creeks and channels in the mangroves forests. In India the species is found in all the tidal forests on the east and west coasts and Andaman and Nicobar Islands. It is distributed in the mangrove forests of Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Burma and Malaysia.


Availability : Tidal shores of India and Ceylon, Malaya.


Conservation/ Threat Status :

Lower risk - Least concern

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small tree, up to 6 m tall, much branched, branches erect, thick, brittle, stem base buttressed with stilt roots. Leaves 2½-5 by 1-2¼ in., elliptic-oblong, obtuse, dark green and polished above, reddish-brown beneath, glabrous, base narrowed. Flowers in axillary cymes; peduncles longer than the petioles, 4-9 flowered. Calyx externally rugose; lobes narrow-linear, acute. Petals bifid; lobes divided into numerous capillary segments. Stamens indefinite, of unequal lengths. Fruit obclavate, girt above the middle with the reflexed calyx-lobes; protruded radicle, cylindric, acuminate, usually not ridged nor grooved.

Other uses:

Leaves used as fodder.

Bark - The bark of the tree is rich in tannins and is suitable for heavy leather tannage. The bark may also be used for dyeing: it produces red and brown colours. The bark is reported to be used, along with dried ginger or long pepper and rose water, for diabetes; it has been shown, however, that aqueous or alcoholic extracts of the bark do not have any effect on the blood sugar of normal or alloxan-diabetic rabbits.

Miscellaneous Information

Mangrove plant. The tree is recommended for planting along the sides of backwaters for green manure purposes.

Wood: Wood (wt. 35-40 lb./cu.ft.) is reddish brown, soft and close-grained.
