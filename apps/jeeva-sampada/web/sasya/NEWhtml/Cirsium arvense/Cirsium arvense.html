DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Heads solitary, corymbose or fascicled ; Flower: Involucral bract glabrate or cobwebby; outer most short, ovate or triangular, ending in a short spine, inner gradually longer with undulate often recurved tips, innermost narrow linear-lanceolate; Corolla purpulish; Fruit: Achenes smooth and shining and Pappus brownish white ;  Flowering & Fruiting : February-May. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Other uses: New isocoumarins from an endophytic fungus isolated from the Cirsium arvense. (Krohn et al, 2001).


Chemistry:
Alkane mixture (C18-C31), b-amyrin, taraxasterol, taraxesterol acetate, tetracosanol, hexacosanol, octacosanol and stigmasterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

3-O-methylkaempferol from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Apigenin-7-b-D-glucuronidopyranoside and acetin-7-b-D-glucuronidopyranoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Flavonol glycoside – linarin – isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Aplotaxsene (Z,Z,Z-1,8,11,14-heptadecatetraene) isolated from roots along with pentadecane, 1-pentadecene and 1-heptadecene and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Two new aplotaxene derivatives isolated from roots and characterized as 8,9-epoxy-heptadeca-1,11,14-triene and 8,9-dihydroxyheptadeca-1,11,14-triene. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Volatile components of Cirsium arvense roots were identified by gas chromatography-mass spectrometery. Among 24-compounds identified, there were seven C13 polyacetylenes, seven unsaturated C15-C17 straight chain hydrocarbons, and five epoxides derived from the C16 and C17 hydrocarbons. The major components are cis-8,9-dihydroxyheptadeca-1,11,14-triene and 8,9-dihydroxyheptadeca-1,11,14-triene. Sesquiterpenes g-humulene and b-selinene are present.
(Binder and French, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9503, 1670.

CIMAP:
Canada thistle (Cirsium arvense) root samples were collected at montly intervals over a 2-yr period from 2 locations to det. Seasonal fluctuations in the presence and growth potensial of root buds. Root bud growth was highest during late fall and winter months. Following death of the areial shoots. Root fragments incubated at a constant 15˚ for 2 wk in continous light consistantly had more buds than non-incubated roots, which indicated active bud differentiation during the incubation period. Incubated roots produced 3-9 cm of new shoot length/ cm root length. There were no obvious seasonal patterns in the presence of root buds or their ability to elongate at different times of the year. Carbohydrate reserves were stored preferentially in roots rather than in developing root buds or the bases of shoots. These reserves ranged from ≥ 3 % of root fresh wt. during spring months to ≤ 26 % in late fall months, although the levels did not increase consistently during summer months over the locations and years of this study1.
Pharmacology:
Aplotaxene (25.0 m/l) stimulated teliospore germination of Puccinia punctiformis by 50% in 7 days. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
