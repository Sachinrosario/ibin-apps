Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Arenga palms are found in moist lowland and mountain rainforests. They are usually monoecious (rarely dioecious), and hapaxanthic, but some are pleonanthic.
Grows naturally in the moist hill forest particularly on the slopes between 150-1000 m.

Availability :

India: Western Ghats to Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka.
Conservation/ Threat Status : Endemic to India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Solitary or sometimes cluster forming hapaxanthic palm. Stem about 4 m long, with leafsheath about 30 cm in width near base; upper part of the stem covered with persistent leafsheath conspicuous by their fibrous outgrowths from the margins. Leaves pinnate about 5 m long; petiole roundish, to 1 m long; leaflets alternate to sub-opposite, in one plane, linear ensiform, 1-1.5 m long, 3-4 cm broad at middle; upper surface light green, lower surface whitish. Inflorescence terminal and axillary, basipetal in emergence, to 1.5 m long; male flowers oblong, 20 mm x 8 mm; sepals transversely orbicular, 5 mm x 8 mm; petals elliptic-oblong, 18 mm x 6 mm; stamens with very short filaments; anthers bilobed, 9 m x 1 mm. Female flowers subglobose, 13 mm x 12 mm; sepals broadly orbicular, 4 mm x 6 mm, imbricate; petals obcordate, obtuse, 8 mm x 8 mm, 1/3 connate from base; ovary subglobose, with 3 distinct upper ridges. Fruits subglobose, slightly trigonous, to 3 cm in diameter, yellowish green in colour when ripe; seed convex, equally bifacing; endosperm horny; embryo on the outer end of the convex face.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Male and Female flowers are separate, fruits are globose to oblong and the juice contains calcium oxalate crystals. This palm propagates through seeds.

Phenology: The inflorescence usually arisen from among the leaves, some times from below. The inflorescences are usually simple, branched.
