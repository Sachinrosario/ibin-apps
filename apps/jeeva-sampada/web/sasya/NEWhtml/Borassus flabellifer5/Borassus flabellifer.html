DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Trunk solitary, upto 30 m high, 45-75 cm in diam. near the ground, black. Leaves flabelliform, 0.8-1.5 m in diam.; leaflets 60-80, linear-lanceolate, folded along the midrib; petioles 0.5-1.2 m long. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Spadix large, simple branched, sheathed with numerous open spathes. Male flowers: in spikes, small. Female flowers: large, globose, ca 2.5 cm in diam.. Drupes subglobose, brown, ca 15 cm in diam. or more with 1-3 fibrous pyrenes.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Fruits are stomachic, aphrodisiac, antibilious. Fermented juice is tonic, fattening, aphrodisiac, intoxicating, expectorent (Unani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).  


Chemistry:
An antilipid-mobilizing protein isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Aqueous infusion of roots produced initial stimulation followed by depression of spontaneous motor activity in mice; its LD50 value > 2.0 g/kg, i.p. Ethanolic extract inhibited granulation tissue formation in cotton pellet implanted rats. It also produced hypoglycaemic and hypocholesterolaemic effects in rats; LD50 1.68 g/kg, i.p. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

A polysaccharide having a linear chain of D-monnosyl residues linked (1à4) in the polysaccharide was isolated palmyra palm nut and its structure by chemical means. The structure of the repeating unit of the polysaccharide is given. (Awal et al, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 50635k.

Four saponins from palmyrah were separated and isolted by flash chromatography. One flabelliferin (F.II) correspons to the bitter flabelliferin tetraglycoside previously isolated from the fruits. The other three flabelliferins isolated were FB, FC and FD of these FB and FC were (steroidal triglycosides of mol. Wt. 868) and FD an impure steroidal diglycoside. It appears that the steroidal aglycon was similar to that isolated previously (MW = 414) of the four flabelliferins isolated, FB was most effective in foam stabilization. (Nikawela et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 165478c.

Water extraction of pretreated matured seeds of B. flabellifer Linn afforded a homogenous polysaccharide (D. mannose and D-galactose). Polysaccharide may be regarded as a galactomanon with manose as the repeating units. (Awal, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 83461k.


Pharmacology:
While FB and FC had the highest hemolytic action as judged by spectrometry. (Nikawela et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 165478c.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for abortifacient (Wood pulp, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Burns, convulsions, earache, headache, heat stroke (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); epilepsy (Petioles Goel et al, 1984; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Gonorrhoea (Sap, Tarafder, 1984); Hysteria (Petiole, Goel et al, 1984); Menorrhagia, nausea (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Rheumatism (Inflorescence, Saxena and Vyas, 1983); Scabies, sores, Syphilis, ulcers (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970). Edible (Fruit, Maji and sikdar, 1982; Ramchandaran and Nair, 1982; Jain, 1964; Goel et al, 1984; Nagar, 1985; Seed, Maji and Sikdar, 1982; Oil, Goel et al, 1984); In drinks (Sap, Shah, 1984; Goel et al, 1984); Cordage (Fibre, Jain and De, 1966); Miscellaneous articles, thatch (Leaf, Goel et al, 1984); Symbolic in sculpture on Vishnu temples (Whole plant, Gupta, 1987)
