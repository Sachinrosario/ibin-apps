REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Axillary cymose ; Flower: regular, hypogynous, cyclic, complete, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Sepals 5, connate near the base ; Corolla: Petals 5, concave below, above onto large spoon shaped lamina, imbriacte in bud, deciduous; Androecium: Staminal cup of 5 fertile as many sterile divisions, filaments oppose the petals, 3-antheriferous, lobes divergent, staminode longer than fertile filaments; Gynoecium: Ovary free, sessile, pyramidal lobed, cells many ovuled, styles 5, ovules attached to inner angle of carpels; Fruit: Capsule, membranous, 5-angled, 5-winged; Seed: Seeds numerous albuminous. Flowering & Fruiting :  Januarary.Flowers are pollinated by insects.  Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Chemistry:
  • Taraxeryl acetate, taraxerol, b-sitoserol and a low melting neutral compound from the petroleum ether extract of the air dried leaves of A. augusta (Chaudhary and Gupta, 1969).

  •   Air dried powdered bark of the plant was successively extracted with petroleum ether (60 - 80 °) and benzene. b-sitoserol   was identified in petroleum ether extract. Friedelin was identified in benzene extract. (Mukherjee and Badruddoja, 1977).

  •  The concentrated chloroform extract of the heartwood isolated Octacosane-1, 28-diol (Lir. m.p. 112 °) and its structure was established by chemical and spectroscopic studies. (Mukherjee and Badruddoja,1978).

  • Air dried leaves of A. augusta were extracted with hot petroleum ether (60-80 °). The extract on repeated column chromatography over silica gel yielded, taraxenol and lupeol based on spectral analysis and comparison with authentic samples was performed. (Maiti et al., 1992). C.A. 1993, 119, 266502j.   

  •   Maslinic acid and a-amyrin isolated from root bark; protocatechuic, vanillic and caffeic acids in their free, glycosidic and ester forms identified in root bark extract. (Aman et al. 1993) C.A. 1994, 120, 4566h.

  • The mucilage was isolated from fresh bark of A. augusta by cold water extraction which contains xylose, mannose, galactose, glucose, galacturonic acid and glucuronic acid. (Majumder et al.1994).

  •     An acidic polysaccharide containing rhamnose, galacturonic acid and glucuronic acid isolated from root bark and partially characterized. (Nahar et al.  1994), C. A. 1994, 121. 276760 x.; B.A. 1995, 99, 25300.

Pharmacology:

  • The root and root bark of A. augusta Linn. F. are used as emmenagogue, uterine tonic and in dysmenorrhoea. (Chopra. et al. 1956).
  • The leaves, stem bark and heartwood of this plant are used in treatment of gonorrhoea and in painful menstruation (Chaudhary & Gupta, 1969, Das and Basu, 1970).
  • A. augusta shows antibacterial activity. (Quader et al. 1998). BA 1998, 105, 355087.
  • Fibre (Bark, Mittre, 1981); Fishing net (Fibre, Mittre, 1981).
