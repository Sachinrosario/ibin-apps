HABIT & HABITAT
Annual herbs. Bulbs with membranous outer scales in axils of which 10-12 fleshy bulbs commonly found to be cultivated throughout India.


Availability :

Conservation/ Threat Status : Plant is commercially cultivated.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herbs. Bulbs with membranous outer scales in axils of which 10-12 fleshy bulbs. Leaves flat, linear, 20-40 cm long; sheaths ca half as long as blades. Umbels small, dense with long scarious beaked bracts; scape slender, upto 60 cm long, terete.

Inflorescence: Many flowered globular umbel; Flower: bulbils, enclosed by thin membranous spathe, pedicles 4-5 times longer than that of flower, spathe composed of 2-3 reflexed valves; Perianth: 6 in number in whorls of three each, imbricate, white, segments ovate-oblong, acutish; Androecium: 6 arranged in two alternate whorls of three members each, always opposite to the tepals, filaments longer than the perianth, connate with each other and the perianth at its base, anthers dithecous, basifixed, introrse and opening by longitudinal slits; Gynoecium: tricarpellary, trilocular superior ovary with numerous ovules in two rows in each ovule,style with distinct stigma, a nectariferous disc is present below the ovary; Fruit: Capsule. 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers white, often replaced by bulbils, usually sterile; pedicels longer than the flowers. Perianth segments lanceolate, acuminate, white or pinkish. Capsules oblong-ovoid, membranous.


Floral and seed biology:
It flowers between December - April.


Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Plants are pollinated by insects. Seeds: are dispersed by animals and wind.
 
Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 16

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and bulbs.

Soil type: It grows well in wide variety of soils but well drained clayey loam are best suited for its cultivation.

Nutrient requirements: Finely powdered fish manure, sulphates of ammonia and potash and nitrate of soda and super phosphate are required.

Water management: Periodic irrigation ater 5-10 days are  highly recommended for the crop.                                       

Harvesting: Plant is harvested when the fot the bulbs when the leaves turn yellow and the bulbs are exposed above the ground.

Chemical Variability: Bulbs and leaves of garlic contained throughout 5.5 months of development raffinose, malt hose, sucrose, glucose and fructose; the canons of sugars varied throughout the growing period.  Polysaccharides decreased in leaves and increased in bulbs and total monosaccharides decreased during development; glucose and fructose increased at early stages and decreased with wilting of bulbs. Dry matter in cloves decreased from time of planting (April 21) till the middle of July; it rose from bulb formation to shrinking.  In leaves, dry matter increased till July 10, and decreased till the end of development1; the dry matter, ask, P, K, Ca, Cu, Mn, and volatile substance contents of 20 garlic varieties were 30.92-55.5, 2.46-5.05% (on dry matter basis), 295-615, 989-1912, 39.4-230.2, 0.6-1.41, 0.6-1.73 mg% (on dry matter basis), and 0.32-0j.7%, resp. UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Bulb is pungent, heating, oleaginous, tonic, aphrodisiac, fattening, digestive, anthelmintic. It improves appetite, voice complexion. It also useful in diseases of the eye and the heart, low fevers, bronchitis, inflammation, piles, leucoderma, asthma, lumbago, tumors, thrist and earache (Ayurveda) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
