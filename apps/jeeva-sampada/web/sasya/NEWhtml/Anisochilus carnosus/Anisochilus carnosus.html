DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An erect, annual or perennial,  sub-succulent herbs, 20-60 cm high. Stems tetrangular, stout, woody, densely tomentose or glabrous, dull pinkish red. Leaves petioled, ovate to lanceolate, 1.5-6.0 x 1.2-4.0 cm, acute or sub-acute at apex, rounded or subcordate at base, crenate-dentate or serrate at margin, glabrous above, slightly pubescent beneath, lateral veinlets 3-5 pairs, more prominent on the lower surfaces, fleshy; petiole slender, 1.0-6.6 cm long, puberulous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Cylindric spikes; Flower:  Sessile, bracteate, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Pubescent, upper lip in fruit ovate-lanceolate, acute, ciliolate; Corolla: Pale purple, pubescent outside, tube narrow below, inflated above Androecium: Stamens 4, didynamous, declinate, filaments free ;Gynoecium: Ovary 4-partite, style 2-fid at the apex; Fruit: Nutlets; Seed: Smooth, brown. Flowering & Fruiting: September-October. Flowers are pollinated by insect and seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers pale-purple, 4-5 mm long, arranged in terminal heads or cylindrical spikes; bracts ovate. Calyx tubular; tube c 2 mm long; upper lip deltoid-lanceolate, c 2.5 x 2 mm, acute; lower lip slightly truncate. Corolla purple, 5 x 1.5 mm across; tube truncate; lobes 5, bilabiate (4+1); upper lip entire; lower lip 4.5 mm long, pubescent. Stamens 4. Ovary suborbicular, 1.5 mm long.  Nutlets suborbicular, 0.1 cm in across, compressed, smooth, shining, brownish.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The plant is mild  stimulant, expectorant and is used for the treatment of cough of children. The fresh juice of the leaves mixed with sugar and gingelly-oil is used as a cooling liniment for head. The paste obtained from the root and tuber is used to poison the arrows.
Chemistry:
Diosmetin and diosmin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)


Pharmacology:

Ethnobotany:
Medicine on itching (leaf, Saxena and Dutta, 1975)
