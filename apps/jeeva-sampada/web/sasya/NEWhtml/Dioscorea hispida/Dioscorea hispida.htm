HABIT & HABITAT
A climber with prickly stem twining to the left. This species is a native of tropical Asia and occurs in the rain forest regions from India to Formosa, Philippine Islands and New Guinea. It is found nearly throughout India up to an altitude of 4,000 ft. in Sikkim Himalayas and Khasi hills. It is rarely cultivated. About 5 varieties are recognised of which var. daemona
It is also seen in the Damp forests, Deciduous forest, Forests, Hill forests, low elevations, open forest, Primary & Secondary forests, Scrub forest, semi evergreen forest, Wet deciduous to semi-evergreen forests, Wet forest area,

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Leaves alternate. Bulbils absent. Tubers more or less depressed globose, often lobed, sometimes slightly elongated, occasionally of considerable size (wt., up to 35 kg.), borne close to the surface of the soil; skin straw-colored to gray; flesh white to lemon yellow, poisonous. Male flowers in cylindric pedunculate spikes, female flowers in solitary distant spikes, seeds winged at base.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers.

Uses

  1. On account of their large size, high starch content and the comparative ease with which they can be dug out, the tubers of the species are used as famine food in parts of India.
  2. Great care is needed in preparing them for food as they are acrid and poisonous. The Lepchas of Sikkim are reported to soak them in running streams for 7 days or more before cooking. (The toxic principle is dioscorine which is distributed throughout the plant. The tubers yield a milky juice used by hill tribes along with the juice of Antiaris toxicaria Lesch., as arrow poison. They are not, however, effective against leeches (Hooper, loc. cit.; Prain & Burkill, The possibility of utilising the tubers for the production of industrial starch and edible flour has been recently investigated. A clean white starch is easily prepared. Dried and debarked tubers contain 0.19% of poisonous alkaloids and 1.14% of a yellow colouring matter. The dried material is powdered (80 mesh) and suspended in 4-5 .times its weight of saturated lime water containing potassium permanganate (0.005%) and thoroughly agitated for an hour. The suspension is then acidified with hydrochloric acid and treated with sodium bisulphite. The flour which settles down on standing is separated, washed and air- dried. The product obtained is free from alkaloids.
