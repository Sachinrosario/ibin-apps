DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A much-branched tree, c. 9 m. high, found up to an altitude of 1,350 m. in Assam and Meghalaya and cultivated in Bengal. Bark smooth, ash-coloured; leaves opposite, ovate-oblong or ovate-lanceolate, 3.75-5.6 cm.x2.5-3.75 cm., coriaceous, gland-dotted; flowers in terminal or axillary cymes, large, white, red or purple; fruits as big as a small apple, turbinate, flattened at both ends, white or pale-rose coloured, with faintly fragrant, juicy, spongy pulp; seeds 3-6. There are two varieties of the tree, one which bears white fruits and the other, fruits of pale-rose colour.

Seed Germination:
The tree is propagated by seeds during rains in South India. Elsewhere, it is propagated by marcottage or budding on understocks of Semarang Rose-Apple (5. samarangense) or Kopo.
