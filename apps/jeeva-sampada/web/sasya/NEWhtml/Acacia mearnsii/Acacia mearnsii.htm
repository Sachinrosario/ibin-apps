Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Middle-sized evergreen tree, native to Tasmania, introduced in India in the mid 19th century near Kodaikanal. Tree is widely planted in the high altitude locations in the Nilgiris and Palni Hills in Tamil Nadu and in Kerala in South India and in Shilong (Assam) and Kohima (Nagaland). In its native habit, i.e. in Australia, the species prefers cool, moist winters, moist soils in valleys or on hill sides and grows often as an understorey of moist type of eucalyptus. Suitable for marginal lands and cut up land around villages.

Crown in solitary species spreads out but when crowded is erect and slender. Root system superficial. Grows on a variety of soils, but its growth is rapid on loose, deep, well-drained soils. Best plantations develop on gentle slopes with deep red, dolerite soils, particularly where there is an admixture of granite detritus.

Prefers moderate climate exhibiting great tolerance to the extremes of heat and cold. It thrives best at elevations of 1,500-2,000 m, in areas where there is an annual rainfall of 150 cm, an average maximum shade temperature of 23.90c and an average minimum of 1.60 C    .Strong light demander. Tolerant to light fires but susceptible if fire is hot and severe.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Middle sized evergreen tree. Feathery foliage, dark green and the trunk appears black. Leaves bipinnate: pinnae 15-20 pairs.

Flowers yellowish, in heads, distributed in simple or panicled terminal or axillary racemes. Pods dark, pubescent, having 8 black seeds with club shaped arils.

Floral and seed biology:

 

Seed germination & Propagation: Regeneration is by seeds or rootsuckers. The seeds are hard-coated and hence, are steeped in hot water for 12-24 hours or stirred in conc. sulphuric acid for 1 hr. recent studies have shown that steeping the seeds in hot water for 5 min is sufficient to break their dormancy. Direct or broadcast sowings are suitable. Seed germination is 50-65 per cent. Two-month-old seedlings are pricked out from the bed and planted in small polythene bags filled with good soil and watered twice daily for the first two months. After 3-4 months when the seedlings are 25-30 cm tall they are planted out in the field. One square meter of bed provides for 400-500 seedlings.

Grown by direct sowing and entire transplanting. Planting is preferred through 6-month-old nursery seedling in India.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Excellent plant for fuelwood. Stem and branches used as fuel. Excellent plant for charcoal. Calorific value: 3,500 kcal/kg for wood and 6,600 kcal/kg for charcoal.

Wood: Suitable for pulp, having good strength properties for wrapping paper.

Wood serves as a fibrous raw material for the pulp, paper and board industry. The spent bark and wood can be used for straw boards. The spent bark also can be usefully employed in the activated carbon industry.

Produce and Products:

Yield good tan bark.

Tannin - The bark is one of the richest vegetable tanning material known to the tanning industry. The amount of non-tan is proportionately low which makes the bark superior to other tanning materials.

Miscellaneous Information

 
The seedlings and trees, up to five years of age, are susceptible to frost. Fertilizing with superphosphate makes the trees frost-resistant. The damaging effects of frost on wattle seedlings may be minimized by clear weeding around them and maintaining the soil surface undisturbed. A frost shelterwood of c 250 large-crowned. Dominant trees per hectare is effective for natural regeneration.

Tree is a nitrogen fixer
