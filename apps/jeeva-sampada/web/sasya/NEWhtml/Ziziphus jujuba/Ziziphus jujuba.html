HABIT & HABITAT
Small trees or shrubs, found in dry slopes and forest openings. Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh , Punjab, West Bengal, Orissa, Gujarat, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Small trees or shrubs, 7.5-9 m tall, glabrous; spines unequal, one straight, c 3 cm long, other recurved, c 7 mm long. Leaves obliquely ovate- lanceolate, 1.3-5 x 0.7-7.3 cm, apex obtusely acute, base slightly acute or rounded, glabrous, basally 3-nerved.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers c 5 mm across, in 2-12 flowered axillary fascicles.  Calyx lobes deltoid. Petals ca 1.2 mm long.  Drupes ovoid- oblong, c 1.7 x 1-1.5 cm, dark red or black, shining. Seeds 2, black.

Chemistry:

·        Leucocyanidin from bark; leucopelargonidin, betulinic and ceanothic acids from wood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Rutin from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Stepharine, N-nornuciferine and asimilobine isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Sativanines A and B isolated along with frangulanine, nummularine B and mucronine D; structures of first two determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Presence of alkaloids detected. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new saponin isolated from leaves and stems and characterised as 3-O-[2-O-a-L-fucopyranosyl-3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-a-L-arabinopyranosyl] jujubogenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Cyclic AMP isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Three flavone-C-glucosides-6''-sinapoylspinosin, 6''-feruloylspinosin and 6''-p-coumaroylspinosin-isolated and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Zizyphus saponins I, II and III and jujuboside B isolated from fruits and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        13C-NMR of frangulanine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Sativanine C isolated from bark and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984). 

·        Bark used in healing ulcers and wounds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        New cyclopeptide alkaloid-sativanine D-isolated and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Another alkaloid -sativanine E- and its characterisation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Cyclopeptide alkaloid - sativanine F- from bark and its structure determination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        A neolignan (I) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        A 13-membered N-formylcyclopeptiide alkaloid,nummularine-T, has been isolated from the bark of Zizyphus nummularia and its structure established by spectroscopic and chemical methods. It provides the fourth example of a natural occurring N-formyl cyclopeptide alkaloid. (Singh, 1995).

·        Investigation of the non-alkaloid water insoluble fraction of the EtOH extract from Zizyphus jujuba led to the characterization of seven triterpene betulin (1) Betulinic acid, (2) Ursolic acid, (3)Ceanothic acid, (4) 2 ahydroxyursolic acid, (5) mixture of b-sitosterol 3-O-b-glucoside, (6) Stigmasterol 3-Ob-glucoside, (7) One unsual ester 24-O feruloye-lignoceric acid. (Lee et. al., 1995).

·        The fruit of Z. jujuba was found enriched with calcium, potassium, bromine, rubidium and lanthanum. Fruit extracts showed very high biological activity in the normalization of the macro and microelement homeostasis of the solid tissues of the teeth of white rats. (Zhumatov, 1996). MAPA 9801-0257.

·        New dammarane- type triterpene oilgolycosides,jujubosides A1 and C and acetyljujuboside B1 were isolated from Zizyphi spinosi Semen, the seeds of Zizyphus jujuba together with three known saponins. The structures of jujuboside A1 and C and acetyljujuboside B were determined on the basis of chemical and   physicochemical evidence. Jujubosides A1 and C and acetyljujuboside B were found to inhibit the histamine release from rat peritoneal exudates cells induced by antigen-antibody-reaction. (Yoshikawa M, 1997).

·        A triterpenoid A-nor-lupa-2,20-diene-14, 17-dicarboxylic acid has been isolated from the leaves of Zizyphus jujuba. (Mondal, 1998). MAPA 9903-2016.

·        Dentifrices food and beverages contain substances having the glucosyltransferase inhibitory activity extracted from Z. jujuba leaf. Z. jujuba leaf was refluxed with H2O for 2h to given a solid extract in 30.7 wt % yield. The extract inhibited glucosyltransferase with 1C50 of 680 mg/ml. (Takagaki, 1998). CA: 129 23454x.

·        Flavonoid glycosides, 2-glucosidase inhibitors from leaves of Zizyphus jujuba Mill. var. inermis REHD have been studied. (Nakamura et. al., 1998).

·        Following the elucidation of jujubosides A1 and C and acetyljujuboside B, novel protojujubogenin type triterpene bisdesmosides, protojujubosides A, B, and B1 were isolated from Zizyphi spinosi semen the seed of Zizphus jujuba Mill.var.spinosa Hu. The structures of protojujubosides A, B and B1 were determined on the basis of chemical and physiochemical evidence. The conversion of protojujubosides to know jujubosides using enzymatic hydrolysis. Protojujubosides A jujubosides A, B, and C were found to show potent immunological adjuvant activity. (Matsuda H, 1999).

·        Eight flavonoid compounds were isolated from the seeds of Zizyphus jujuba Mill var. Spinosa. On the basis of chemical and spectral analyses their structures were elucidated as swertish (1), puerarin (2), 6”’-feruloylisospinosin (3), apigenin-6-C-b-D-glucopyranoside (4), spinosin (5), 6”’-feruloylisospinosin (6), isopinosin (7), and isovitexin-2”-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (8), Flavonoids 6 and 7 are novel compounds. Rotamers exist for compounds 1, 3 and 5, which are reported for the first time. Compounds 2, 4 and 8 were isolated from this plant for the first time. Spinosin and swertish possess significant sedative activity. (Cheng, et. al., 2000).

·        The leaves of Zizyphus mauritiana yielded two new long chain compounds charecterised as undecyl-hexadecanoate 1 and 12-hydroxy-hentriacontane 2 using 1R, 1H NMR and mass spectral data. (Agarwal et. al., 200-2001).

·        A new cyclopeptide alkaloid, Jubanine-C(1), together with known alkaloids scutianine-C(4) and  zizyphine-A(5),Have been isolated  from  the stem  bark of Zizyphus Jujuba and identified by spectral  analysis. (Tripathi M, 2001).

·        Bioactivity-guided fractionation of petroleum ether-and EtOAc-soluble extracts of the seed of Ziziphus jujuba using a cyclooxygenase-2 assay as a monitor indicated that the triglyceride, 1,3-di-O-[9(Z)-octadecenoy-1]-2-O-[9(Z)-octadecadienoy1] glycerol(3), and  a fatty acid mixture of linoleic, oleic and  stearic acids were the  major activity  components. A new pentacyclic lupane- type triterpene derivative, 3-O-[9(Z)-octadecenoyl]betulinic acid (1) and betulinic acid (2) were also isolated and identified. All isolates were evaluated for their inhibitory effects against both cyclo-oxygenases-1 (Cox-1) and –2(Cox-2). (Su B N, 2002).


Pharmacology:

·        Neolignan (I) showed significant induction effect on release of endogenous PG12 from rat aorta. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Zizyphus jujuba leaves have been widely used as a hypoglycaemic agent by diabetics in some regions of Turkey. In this study the effects of Zizyphus jujuba leaves on plasma glucose levels in normo-and hyperglycaemic rats were investigated. In addition, the chronic toxicity of Zizyphus jujuba leaves was investigated in normally caemic rats. When 3 and 6% decoctions of Zizyphus jujuba leaves were administered to rats plasma glucose levels decreased significantly (P<0.001). Biochemical and haematological parameters were not different from control groups (p>0.05) and these parameters were within normal limits. MO-drug related changes were observed in rats and there were no pathologic changes attributable to the drug in histopthologic examination of all tissues. (Erenmemisoglu, et. al., 1995).

·        The induced anxiolytic effect of the ethanolic extract of semen Ziziphi jujuba (SZJE) was studied. The SZJE was administered to male ICR mice at 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 g/kg, 30 min before the behavioral evaluation in the black and white test and elevated plus maze. Results suggest that SZJE has anxiolytic effect at lower dose and sedative effect at higher dose. (Peng et. al., 2000).

Pharmaceutical and Toxicology:

Jujuboside A is a glycoside extracted from the seed of Z. jujuba, which has long been known as a sedative-hypnotic drug. The present study used the method of intracerebroventricular jujuboside A in vivo to detect the effect of   jujuboside A on paired-pulse responses of dentate gyrus granule cells in urethane-anaestetized rats, and the methodof hippocampal slice bathing in jujuboside A in vitroto detect the effects of jujuboside A on the responses of CA1 pyramidal cells. The results showed that jujuboside A significantly decreased the slope of excitatory postsynaptic potential and the amplitudes of population spike excitatory postsynaptic potential in the first responses of granule cells and significantly decreased excitatory postsynaptic potential and population spike in response of CA1pyramidal cells. The results indicate that the study accessed the inhibitory effect of jujuboside A on hippocampal formation in vivo and in vitro in rats central nervous system. ( Shou C et. al., 2002).

At 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 g/kg, 30 min before the behavioral evaluation in the black and white test (BWT) and elevated plus maze (EMP). The SZJE at the dosage of 1.0 g/Kg prolonged the hexobarbital-induced sleeping time in mice and decreased the locomotor activity in rats.
