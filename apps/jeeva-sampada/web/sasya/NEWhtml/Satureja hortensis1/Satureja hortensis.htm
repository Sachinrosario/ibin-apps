Uses:
Chemistry: Terpenoids (2.6-3.2%) from aerial parts during flowering, consisted of thymol (46.0), linalool (26.0), geraniol (1.2), a-thujene (1.1), caryophyllene (2.5), a-terpinene (0.6), Cymol (16.0) and citronellol (0.6%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).
Leaves contained apigenin-7-O-glucuronoside, luteolin-7-O-glucoside andluteolin-7-O-glucuronoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Diosmin and 5,3',4'-trihydroxyflavone-7-O-rutinoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Two flavonoids, diosmine and 7-rutinosyl-3',4',5,7-tetrahydroxyflavone were isolated from Satureja hortensis L. herb. (Maria and Cisowski, 1991). C.A. 116, 252136.
The composition of the essential oil obtained in 0.30% by hydrodistillation of the seeds of Satureja hortensis L. was investigated by GC and GC/MS. Forty-two components were characterized, representing 96.7% of the total oil components detected. Carvacrol (59.7%), g-terpinene (12.8%) and p-cymene (9.3%) were the major constituents of the oil. (Ghannadi, 2002). C.A. 2002, 136, 314739.
The major component of savory extracts with antioxidant activity was found phenolic monoterpene carvacrol. Its content in crude extracts is approx. 16%. However, this compd. causes only approx. 30% of antioxidant activity of these extracts. The composition of savory is very similar to that of thyme, which contains very effective antioxidant p-cymene-2,3-diol. The content of p-cymene-2,3-diol in crude extracts is approx. 0.04%. This compound has only low influence upon antioxidant activity of savory extracts (approx. 1%). The savory extracts content also tocopherols (lower then 0.2%) and phospholipids (lower then 1%), these compds. cause approx. 2.5% and 5.5% of antioxidant activity, respectively. (Trojakova et al, 2001). C.A. 2002, 136, 262148.

Pharmacology: The antioxidant activity (AA) of acetone, savory (Satureja hortensis L.) were tested in refined, bleached, and deodorized rapeseed oil applying the Schaal Oven Test and wt. gain methods at 80 °C and the Rancimat method at 120 °C. (Bandoniene et al, 2002). C.A. 2002, 137, 62413.

Miscellaneous Information
Any Other

The effect of the ethanol ext. from Satureja hortensis L on the oxidative and thermal processes occurring in sunflower oil during its high-temperature treatment (180°C) was investigated. The ethanol ext. from S. hortensis improved the oxidative stability of sunflower oil even after 50 h at 180°C and it inhibited the oxidative processes more than the thermal processes under these conditions. (Yanishlieva et al, 1997). C.A. 1998,127, 233729
