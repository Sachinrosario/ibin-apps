Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
Jatropha curcas seems to have originated from South America (Brazil) from where it got disseminated to Africa and India. It is commercially grown in Angola, Guiana, Mexico etc., The physic nut is a small tree or large shrub which can reach a height of up to 5 m. In India it is not grown as crop but it is commonly found in Aravalli hills in wild form. It is grown as hedge plant in Rajasthan and in some other places. It can adapt to different climatic conditions as it has been found growing in equitorial humid regions and also in tropical places. It is normally cultivated in poor soils or marginal lands. It can withstand alkaline soils and grows well in arid and semi arid zones. Physic nut is reported to tolerate annual precipitation of 4.8 to 23.8 dm and annual temperature of 18.0 to 28.5°C (3, 4, 5, 6).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
The plant has spreading branches and stubby twigs, showing articulated growth, with a morphological discontinuity at each increment. The branches contain latex. Normally, five roots are formed from seedlings, one central and four peripheral. A tap root is not usually formed by vegetatively propagated plants. The physic nut has 5 to 7 shallow lobed leaves, which are arranged alternately but apically crowded. Leaves deciduous, ovate, acute to acuminate, basally cordate, 6–40 cm long, 6–35 cm broad, the petioles 2.5–7.5 cm long (3, 5, 7, 8, 9).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Inflorescences are formed terminally on branches and are complex, possessing main and co-florescences with paracladia. Botanically, it can be described as a cyme. The plant is monoecious and flowers are unisexual; occasionally hermaphrodite flowers occur. Flowers several to many in greenish cymes, yellowish, bell-shaped; sepals 5, broadly deltoid. Male flowers many with ten stamens are arranged in two distinct whorls of five each in a single column in the androecium, and in close proximity to each other. Female flowers borne singly (gynoecium), with elliptic 3-celled, triovulate ovary. The three slender styles are connate to about two-thirds of their length, dilating to massive bifurcate stigmata (3, 5, 7, 8, 9).

Breeding System :
Staminate flowers open later than pistillate flowers in the same inflorescence and this mechanism promotes cross-pollination (5).

Phenology :
Two flowering peaks have been observed by workers, one in November and the other in May. In permanently humid equatorial regions, flowering occurs throughout the year. Fruit development needs 90 days from flowering until seeds mature (3, 10, 11).

Pollinators:
Pollination of the physic nut is by insects. Flowers are pollinated by moths because of its sweet, heavy perfume, attractive flowers, versatile anthers and protruding sexual organs and copious nectar.

Seed Biology :
Seeds one, two or three and they are separated from one another by the septums of an ellipsoid, scarcely lobed capsule about 2.5 cm. long and black in colour at maturity (3).

Seedling Germination :
Under favourable conditions, seeds germinate in 10 days after planting. The seed shell splits, the radicle emerges and four little peripheral roots are formed. Soon after the development of the first leaves, the cotyledons wither up and fall off. Further growth is sympodial (3).

Cultivation:

Several workers have described several traditional propagation methods such as direct seeding, precultivation of seedlings, transplanting of spontaneous wild plants and direct planting of cuttings.

Factors influencing crop establishment of plants propagated by different methods are:

Generative propagation (seeds)

  • direct seeding- seeding depth, date and quality of the seed.

  • transplanting- type and length of precultivation, planting date.

Vegetative propagation (cuttings)

  • direct planting character of cuttings (length, diameter, age), cutting time,storage, fungicide treatment, planting time and depth

  • transplanting as with direct planting of cuttings and precultivation of seeds.

Not all factors are of equal importance. Successful precultivation is characterized by high germination rates of seeds, high sprouting rates of cuttings and survival. Basing the propagation method on rainfall conditions plays a decisive role in the survival and properties of the plant in the field. The plants obtained from seeds are more robust, with the initial production starting after four years. The plants grown from cuttings have weak branches. The plants are short lived but the production starts in the second year itself. If the plant is left to itself, it can keep yielding upto 30- 40 years. Better yields can be obtained when poor soils are given treatment with organic manure of fertilizer. It is reported that calcium, magnesium and sulphur in small quantities are useful. However, excess use of fertizer seems to reduce yield of plant. Crop can be cultivated sucessfully without much difficulty as it is drought hardy and requires much less attention (3, 5, 8, 10, 11).

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES
Jatropha curcas is a neglected crop, so not much work has been done on this crop. Some of the pests and diseases are listed below:

Pests:

Julus sp. (millipede) causes total loss of seedlings, Lepidopterae larvae make galleries in leaves. Oedaleus senegalensis (locust) feeds on leaves and seedlings. Pinnaspis strachani (cushion scale) causes die-back of branches, Ferrisia virgata (wooly aphid) causes die-back of branches, Calidea dregei (blue bug) and Nezara viridula (green stink bug) feeds on fruits, Spodoptera litura larvaes feeds on leaves.

Diseases:

Damping off, root rot caused by Phytophthora spp., Pythium spp., Fusarium spp., etc. Leaf spots caused by Helminthosporium tetramera, Pestalotiopsis paraguarensis, Cercospora jatrophae-curces (5, 10).

Economic Potential and Uses

The oil is semi-drying and may be employed for the preparation of non- or semi-drying alkyds. In China, a varnish is prepared by boiling the oil with iron oxide. The oil is used as an illuminant, it burns without emitting smoke. It can be used also as a lubricant and for making soaps and candles. It is used in wool-spinning in England. In Senegal, it is said to have been used as an adulterant of groundnut oil. It is used as an external application for skin diseases and rheumatism, it is reported to be abortifacient and also efficacious in dropsy, sciatica and paralysis. In Java, the oil is applied to hair as growth stimulant. It is also used as an application for sores on domestic stock. The use of physic nut seed oil in car engines is reported in the literature and in unpublished research reports. Jatropha oil may however be modified by transesterification; heating with alcohol and a catalyst; after which it can be directly substituted for diesel fuel. The seed cake contains toxic principles and is unfit for use as cattle feed. It is rich in nitrogen and phosphorus and can be used as manure. The cake protein may be employed as a raw material for plastics and synthetic fibers (3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11).

Parts used

Seeds, leaves and bark.

Uses

The plant is widely cultivated in the tropics as a living fence in fields and settlements. This is mainly because the species is not browsed by cattle. It is also cultivated as a drought resistant plant in marginal areas to prevent soil erosion. The young leaves may be safely eaten, steamed or stewed. They are favoured for cooking with goat meat. In India, pounded leaves are applied near horse's eyes to repel flies. Though purgative, the nuts are sometimes roasted and eaten. All parts of the plant exude a sticky, opalescent, acrid and astringent latex, containing resinous substances. The bark yields a dark blue dye reported to be used in the Philippines for colouring cloth, fishing nets and lines. Bark used as a fish poison.Tender twigs of the plant are used for cleaning teeth and juice is reported to relieve toothace and strenghen gums. Mexicans grow the shrub as a host for the Lac insect. Ashes of the burned root are used as a salt substitute. The latex dries to a bright reddish brown, brittle substance, resembling shellac, it stains linen indelibly and can be used as marking ink. The latex was strongly inhibitory to watermelon mosaic virus. In South Sudan, the seed as well as the fruit is used as a contraceptive. The wood is very flexible if thinly split and is used for basket making (3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11).

Harvesting Practices
The seeds even when dry remain on plant for a long time. The sign of maturation is the tellowing of capsules. Usually there are two harvests, one in June-July and a second in October-November. They are allowed to fall to the ground and are then collected, or more rarely the trees are climbed (3, 10, 11).

Produce and Products

Seeds yield oil of industrial importance such as soap making, candles etc. Oil cake is used as manure. Plant is valued more for its medicinal properties.

Processing Techniques

Pretreatment

  • After harvesting, the fruits are put into heaps to dry, where they dehisce spontaneously.

  • The seeds are then separated from the pericarp and put to dry.

  • They are then roasted to ground.

Extraction

  • The ground seeds are boiled with water, oil is collected when the it floats on the surface.

  • The oil is obtained from decoricated seeds by expression or solvent extraction (7, 10, 11).

Other Uses
Reported to be abortifacient, anodyne, antiseptic, cicatrizant, depurative, diuretic, emetic, hemostat, lactagogue, narcotic, purgative, rubefacient, styptic, vermifuge, and vulnerary, physic nut is a folk remedy for alopecia, anasorca, ascites, burns, carbuncles, convulsions, cough, dermatitis, diarrhea, dropsy, dysentery, dyspepsia, eczema, erysipelas, fever, gonorrhea, hernia, incontinence, inflammation, jaundice, neuralgia, paralysis, parturition, pleurisy, pneumonia, rash, rheumatism, scabies, sciatica, sores, stomachache, syphilis, tetanus, thrush, tumors, ulcers, uterosis, whitlows, yaws, and yellow fever. Latex applied topically to bee and wasp stings and it is also used to dress sores and ulcers and inflamed tongues. Homeopathically used for cold sweats, colic, collapse, cramps, cyanosis, diarrhea, leg cramps. Seed is viewed as aperient. Seeds are used also for dropsy, gout, paralysis, and skin ailments Root is used in decoction as a mouthwash for bleeding gums and toothache. Otherwise used for eczema, ringworm, and scabies. Leaves are regarded as antiparasitic, applied to scabies; rubefacient for paralysis, rheumatism; also applied to hard tumors.

Mauritians massage ascitic limbs with the oil. Cameroon natives apply the leaf decoction in arthritis. Colombians drink the leaf decoction for venereal disease. Bahamans drink the decoction for heartburn. Costa Ricans poultice leaves onto erysipelas and splenosis. Guatemalans place heated leaves on the breast as a lactagogue. Cubans apply the latex to toothache. Barbadians use the leaf tea for marasmus, Panamanians for jaundice. Venezuelans take the root decoction for dysentery. The extracts are used in folk remedies for cancer (3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 13).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

Physic nut is well adapted to marginal areas with poor soils and low rainfall, where it grows without competing with annual food crops, thus filling an ecological niche. It is widely distributed in the tropics and used to a certain extent. The species has numerous uses and in their combination lies the potential of this crop. The most important is the combination of erosion control and oil production. The use of the oil as a substitute for diesel fuel and for soap production in rural areas would improve the living conditions of the people and would offer additional income. All parts of the plant are used in traditional medicine and active components are being investigated in scientific trials. Several ingredients appear to have promising applications both in medicine and as a plant protectant (5).
