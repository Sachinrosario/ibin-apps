AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated by seeds, shoot cuttings and root cuttings, sprouted old root stocks..

Soil Type: A well drained slit loam to clay loam, rich in humus and slightly acidic in reaction are suitable for cultivation.

Irrigation: The crop requires frequent irrigation  after 10-15 days  to the depth of 3-5 cm during summers depending upon the soil texture and rainfall at the locality..

Nutrient requirements:  The crop responds well to application of FYM and organic fertilizers. A basal dose of 25 kg/ha N, 60kg/ha P2O5  and 30 kg/ha K2O. Organic manures are also recommended.

Harvesting: Maximum yield is obtained from leaf crop during flowering which possess highest alkaloid contents and contains more active principle when collected after clear sunny day than under cloudy weather.

Kaempferol-7-monoglucoside and quercetin-7-monoglucoside from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Atropine (69%) and other alkaloids determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Hyoscyamine N-oxide isolated from seeds, roots, leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Maximum atropine level in plant reached atstage of ripening fruits; scopolamine was highest in young leaves at top and poor in leaves at bottom. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Pharmacognostic study of leaf; ratio of atropine and scopolamine in total tropine alkaloids of leaf was 0.8. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A process for extracting (-)hyosayamine developed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Total enantioselective synthesis of (-) calystegin B2. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Eight steroidal glycosides of the spirostane type, tentatively named atropisides A – H were isolated from the methanolic extract of Atropa bellodona seeds.The structures of atropisides A,C,E and G were identified as glycosides of (25R)-5a-spirostan-3b-ol and the others as glycosides of (25R)-spirost-5-ene-3bol.  (Shvets et al, 1996). Pharmacology:
Atropine inhibited cholinesterase and thus, in small doses it potentiated toxicity of acetyl choline both in vivo in mice and in vitro on guinea pig ileum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.

Acute toxicity (LD50) and ED50 (convulsions) of optically active isomer of hyoscine and hyoscyamine in mice (i.v.) determined. Dextro and laevo forms of hyoscine and hyoscamine are equi-active only at toxic dose levels. Quantitative differences in central activity of both isomers at doses far below toxic levels observed either by measuring direct effects or in test involving drug antagonism in CNS. (-) Hyoscine effective at 0.01 to 0.25 mg/kg and (-) hyoscamine at 0.04 to 0.4 mg/kg in rats and mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)g.

Metabolism of tropane alkaloids measured by exhalation of PL402 after i.p. injection in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)h.

Scopolamine (1mg/kg, single dose) markedly increased spontaneous activity of rats which was blocked by a-methyl-p-tyrosine or lithium chloride. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Effect of atropine in rats was similar to that of strychnine or d-turbocurarine and was blocked after eserinisation for 60min. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Atropine sulphate (40mg/kg i.v.) in pregnant women increased foetal heart rate after 17th week of gestation and the effect increase with increasing gestational time. At all stage of pregnancy maternal heart rate increased about 50 beats/min. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Atropine decreased lower oesophageal sphincter pressure significantly at 5 min. after i.v. injection and this change was sustained for 1hr in healthy volunteers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

In normal adult volunteers, decrease in leftward lower oesphageal sphincter pressure with atropine was more marked (40%) than rightward (20%). The result of this differential response to atropine was to reduce right-left pressure asymmetry. Mean highest leftward pressure fell 15-30 min post injection from 35 to 21 mm and mean highest rightward pressure fell from 22 to 18mm. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Atropine sulphate (0.1-0.8m Mol) injected into rat hind limb perfusion circuit, showed dose-dependent decrease in perfusion pressure, which was immediate in onset, and always preceded systemic pressure changes. Duration of vasodilation lengthened with increasing doses of atropine and ranged from 1.5 to 9 min. During atropine-induced vasodilation, challenging doses of acetylcholine did not alter perfusion pressure. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

Peripheral vascular effects of intraarterially (i.a.) injected atropine sulphate and atropine methylbromide in autoperfused hind limbs of rats, showed that the former (0.1-0.8m Mol) decreased perfusion pressure whereas the latter (0.1-1.6m Mol) increased it. Responses were immediate on i.a. injection and were dose-related. Data indicated that vasoconstrictor action of both drugs did not appear by activation of vascular a-adrenergic receptors. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

Atropine (4mg/kg/day, i.p.) for 10-15 days after 33 weeks of chronic barbital treatment in rats, showed increased tolerance to hexo barbital. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

Atropine (20 and 50mg/kg, i.p.) given to rats 20 min before or immediately after training markedly inhibited learning and/or retention in training and memory testing experiments; further, after atropine administration central stimulants did not exert their memory improving effect. Methylatropine, on the other hand, in similar doses applied alone or together with central stimulants influenced neither memory processes nor memory-improving effect of central stimulants. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)h.

In normal subjects, atropine (0.85mg, p.o.) impaired short-term memory, flicker recognition, coordination and standing steadiness, whereas higher dose of atropine (1.7mg) had greater effect on these parameters and also affected reaction speed. Central effects of atropine were maximal at 1 hr after its administration, while peripheral effects were maximal after 2-4hr. Its acute psychomotor effects are less than those of anxiolytics, neuroleptics or antidepressants. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)i.

Atropine was injected i.v. into rabbits, rats and guinea pigs and their effects on atrioventricular node were studied. In most cases it dose-dependently repressed vagal bradycardia but in some animals it was increased; duration of repression varied with animal species. Arrhythmia was also produced by atropine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)j.

Effect of pretreatment with inhaled atropine methonitrate (1.5mg) and placebo was compared on standardized inhalation challenges with histamine in normal and asthmatic human subjects and with antigen in atopic asthmatics. It was concluded that atropine was a powerful bronchodilator, particularly in atopic asthmatics with increased bronchometer tone. Histamine- and antigen- induced bronchoconstriction was inhibited by atropine but extent of thi inhibition varied between subjects. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)k.

Atropine inhibited cholinergically-induced gastric acid secretion by atropine occurred at dose-range of 1-2nM. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)l.

In isolated rat diaphragm-nerve preparation atropine (0.01-0.25 gm%) slightly stimulated contraction induced by indirect stimulation followed by gradual decrease and finally blockade; atropine effect was reversed by washing with drug-free solution. Atropine induced increased in outward movement of Na+ in muscle followed by subsequent decrease. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)m.

Scopolamine exhibited central nervous system depressant but atropine showed stimulant activity in conscious rabbits after intracerebroventricular injection. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)n.

Atropine and scopolamine at minimal i.p. doses markedly increased foot-pad skin temperature of rats due to vasodilatory action. Atropine was more potent vasodilator in denervated than in intact limb. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Sopolamine exhibited dose-dependent anti-ulcer activity against gastric ulcer induced by cold-restraint stress, indomethacin and acetic acid in rats. It inhibited gastric acid secretion and pepsin activity and increase gastric barrier, mucus and serum gastrin suggesting its anti-ulcer effect. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Extensive clinical trials were made on the schizophrenia cases, where the violent delirium patterns were exactly like those produced symptoms of Bellodona. (Banerji et al, 1994 – 1995.). MAPA 1996, 9603, 1320.Belladona extract are used to lower the skin irritation. (Manabe, 1998).

Spirostane glycosides from Bellodona as anticholsteremic agents. (Kiptya et al, 1998), C.A. 2000, 133, 23825a.

The hypolipidemic effect of spirostan F was observed. (Lattsers et al, 1997). C.A. 2001, 134, 788055j.
