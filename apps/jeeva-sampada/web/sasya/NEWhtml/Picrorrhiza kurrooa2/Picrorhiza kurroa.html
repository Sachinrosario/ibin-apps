HABIT & HABITAT
A small nearly hairy perennial herb with a woody elongate creeping root stock found in the Himalayas from Kashmir to Sikkim at an elevation of 2700-4500 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves spathulate, serrate; flowers white or bluish in dense terminal spicate raceme; fruits ovoid capsules. The dried rhizome is cylindrical, deep grayish brown in colour and longitudinally wrinkled with annulations at the tip. Rootstock as thick as the little finger, 15-25 cm long, clothed with withered leaf-bases. Leaves 5-10 cm, rather coriaceous tip rounded, base narrowed into a winged sheathing petiole. Flowering stem or scapes are ascending, stout, longer then the leaves, naked or with a few bracts below the inflorescence. Spikes 5-10 cm long, sub cylindrical, obtuse, many flower red, bracts oblong or lanceolate, as long as the calyx. Sepals 6mm long, ciliate. Corolla of short stamened from 6-8 mm long, with longer filaments 8 mm long, of the longer stamened from 6 mm, with filaments 2 cm long; capsules 1-3 cm long.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The rhizomes are bitter, acrid, cooling, laxative, carminative, digestive, stomachic, anthelmintic, anti-inflammatory, depurative, cardiotonic, galactopurifier, expectorant, antipyretic and antiperiodic, and purgative in large doses. They are useful in vitiated conditions of kapha and pitta, burning sensation, constipation, gastropathy, flatulance, colic, anorexia, verminosis, inflammations, leucoderma, leprosy, skin diseases, cordiac disorders, hypotension, cough, asthma, bronchitis, hiccough, fever, bilious fever, intermittent fever, diabetes, jaundice, haemorrhoids, impurity of breast milk and general debility.

Chemistry:
Roots yield a glycosidal biter principle kutkin, found to be mixture of two iridoid glycosides – picroside 1(6’ - o - trans-cinnamoylcatalpol) and kutkoside characterised as 10 – o – vanilloy – 1 catalpol. Also, D-mannitol, Kutkiol, Kotkistrol and a Ketone subsequently found identical with apocynin .
Attempted synthesis of proposed structure of kutkin (6-O-cinnamoyl-b-D-glucosylvanillate) starting with 1,2,3,4-tetra-O-acetyl-6-tosyl-glucose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Pharmacology: Kutaki increased bile flow in dog. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)h.
Alcoholic extractive was effective in chronic carbon tetrachloride-induced hepatotoxicity in rat. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)i.
Apocynin showed choleretic activity. It produced moderate to marked relaxation of rabbit ileum and contracting rat uterus and antagonized spasmogen-induced spasms on guinea pig ileum. It had depressant action on frog heart but showed no significant effect on blood pressure. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)j.
Alcoholic extract decreased alkaline phosphatase, SGOT, SGPT and thymol turbidity. It increased significantly body weight of rats, concomitantly DNA and RNA concentrations in liver were found to be elevated significantly. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Root and rhizome extract exhibited hepatoprotective activity in rat and mastomys. Active principles identified as kutkin and kutkin-free fraction found to be devoid of any activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
Oral administration of plant extract to guinea pigs before or after vaccination markedly reduced rise in body temperature, loss in body weight, food intake and mortality induced by vaccinia virus. The drugs may be acting directly as virucide because it prevented many effects of virus when administered after vaccination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.
Plant extract suppressed inflammatory oedema at 1 hr, 3 hr and 5 hr post-insult in rats. Macrophage depletion in animals did not affect anti-inflammatory effect of the extract. Neutrophil depletion significantly inhibited carrageenin-inflammation assayed 3 hr after insult. Mast cell depletion counteracted anti-inflammatory effect of extract at 1 hr post-insult, but significant activity was apparent at 3 hr. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.
Kutkin showed significant hepatoprotective activity in hepatic damage induced by galactosamine in rats and mastomys. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.
Alcoholic extract of roots exhibited anti-inflammatory activity in adjuvant -induced and formaldehyde arthritis in rats and mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Testing of picroliv (a standardized fraction of alcoholic extract of plant) and its major components picroside I, catalpol, kutkoside I and kutkoside for anti hepatitis B virus surface antigen (anti-HBs)-like activity, showed promising anti-HBsAg-like activity in picroliv and catalpol which was different from classical viral neutralisation. Picroliv also inhibited purified hepatitis B virus antigens prepared from healthy HBsAg carriers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.
Administration of picroliv (3.0 to 12.0 mg/kg/day for two weeks) to Mastomys natalensis simultaneously following Plasmodium berghei infection provided significant protection against hepatic damage; increased levels of serum glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase, glutamate pyruvate transaminase, alkaline phosphatase, liporotein-X and bilirubin in infected animals were markedly reduced; picroliv decreased levels of lipid peroxides and hydroperoxides in liver and facilitated recovery of superoxide dismutase. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.
Picroliv in doses of 6.0 and 12.0 mg/kg provided significant protection against most of the hepatic and serum biochemical alterations produced by CCl4 in rats. Degree of protection afforded by picroliv when administered simultaneously with infection or as pretreatment was almost equal. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)j.
Kutkin, picroside I and kutikoside exhibited significant anti-inflammatory activity in adjuvant-induced and formaldehyde arthritis in rats and mice. Kutkin significantly inhibited dextran-induced oedema in rats, acetic acid-induced vascular permeability in mice and leukocyte migration in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)k.
Methanol extracts of Picrorhiza kurrooa exhibited a significant protection against the ulcer. (Roy et al., 1996) MAPA. 1998, 9803: 1587.
Androsin, apocynin from rhizomes of Picrorhiza kurrooa were identified as antiasthamtic agent. (Muller et al., 1996) MAPA. 1998, 9803: 1557. Daily administration of ethyl alcohol (3.76 g/kg, p.o.) for 45 days resulted in significant changes in several biochemical parameters of the liver and serum of albino rats. After exposure to alcohol for 30 days when Picroliv (12 mg/kg, p.o.), an iridoid glycoside fraction of Picrorhiza kurroa, was administered for 15 days along with alcohol, the degree of change in most of the parameters was reduced. (Rastogi et al., 1996)
Picroside I is a major active constituent of picroliv. A sensitive high-performance liquid chromatographic assay method in plasma has been developed and validated for the determination of picroside I in plasma. The method consists of extraction of the drug, after protein precipitation with methanol, from rabbit plasma samples. Separation was achieved using a C18 endcapped reversed-phase column coupled with a photodiode array detector and acetonitrile-0.1 M acetic acid (25:75) as mobile phase. (Dwivedi et al., 1997)
Alcoholic axtract of roots of Picrorhiza kurrooa and active constituents kutkin, picroside-1 and kutkoside demonstrated antiinflammatory activity in a variety of test models. Significant antiinflammatory activity was recorded in adjuvant-induced and formaldehyde arthritis in rats and mice. In carrageenan-induced edema inhibitory activity was remarkably enhanced upon i.p. treatment in rats and mice. Kutkin exhibited significant action in dextran-induced edema in rats. It inhibited acetic acid induced vascular permeability in mice and leukocyte migration in rats. Kutkin lacked any analgesic, antipyretic or ulcerogenic effect. (Singh et al., 1993) CA. 1997,120:260821.
Picroliv, the active constituent of P. kurrooa, showed a dose dependent (1.5-12 mg/kg, po for 7 days) hepatoprotective activity against oxytetracycline induced hepatic damage in rat. It increased the number of viable hepatocytes (ex-vivo) significantly. Increase in bile volume and its contents in conscious rat suggests potent anticholestatic property. Picroliv also antagonised alterations in enzyme levels (GOT, GPT, and alkaline phosphatase) in isolated hepatocytes and serum, induced by oxytetracycline (200 mg/kg, i.p.) feeding. Picroliv was more potent than silymarin a known hepatoprotective drug. (Saraswat et al., 1997).
The extract appears to offer significant protection against liver damage by CCl4. It probably acts as free-radical scavenger and inhibitor of lipid peroxidation of liver plasma membrane. (Santra et al., 1998)
Picroliv, the active constituent of P. kurrooa, showed a dose dependent (1.5-12 mg/kg, orally for 7 days) hepatoprotective activity against oxytetracycline induced hepatic damage in rat. It increased the no. of viable hepatocytes (ex-vivo) significantly. Increase in bile vol. And its contents in conscious rat suggest potent anticholestatic property. Picroliv also antagonized alterations in enzyme levels (GOT, GPT and alk. phosphatase) in isolated hepatocytes and serum, induced by oxytetracycline (200 mg/kg, i.p.) feeding. Picroliv was more potent than silymarin a known hepatoprotective drug. (Saraswat et al., 1997) CA.1999,129: 90203.
Picroliv, an iridoid glycoside mixture from the root and rhizome of Picrorhiza kurrooa, at the dose of 6 mg/kg po for two weeks provided significant protection against the generation of lipid peroxidation. Products in serum b-lipoproteins of P. berghei infected M. coucha. Incubation of normal rat hepatocytes with very low d. lipoprotein or low d. lipoprotein isolated from infected animals caused significant generation of lipid peroxides followed by a decrease in the viability of these cells, however these effects were partially reversed with the lipoproteins from infected and picroliv treated groups. High d. lipoprotein from infected animals was not toxic to hepatocytes in vitro. (Chander et al., 1998) CA.1999, 129:49602.
Picroliv, a standardised iridoid glycoside fraction from the root and rhizome of Picrorhiza kurroa at a dose of 25 mg kg-1 p.o. inhibited passive cutaneous anaphylaxis in mice (82%) and rats (50-85%) and protected mast cells from degranulation (60-80%) in a concentration-dependent manner. (Baruah et al., 1998)
Effect of Picroliv, a standardised fraction of root and rhizome of Picrorhiza kurroa, on proliferative T cell response to the mycobacterial 'Purified Protein Derivative (PPD)' antigen in subjects infected with or exposed to mycobacteria (tuberculoid leprosy patients and endemic normals). Coculture of their peripheral blood mononuclear cells with the optimal concentration of Picroliv (0.5 microgram/ml) significantly enhanced the proliferative response to 1/10 optimal PPD dose, as determined by [3H] thymidine incorporation, in the group of 'low' responders. The response to PPD of cells from 'high responders' and to PHA (phytohaemagglutinin, a non-specific T cell mitogen) remained unaffected by Picroliv which did also not induce cell proliferation on its own. The selective, antigen specific augmentation of human T cell response suggests that Picroliv could be useful as an adjunct to chemotherapy or as a short term prophylactic agent. (Sinha et al., 1998)
Chronic administration of the extract significantly reduced the blood sugar in alloxan-induced diabetic rats for several days (10 days). The extract was also found to reduce the increased blood urea nitrogen and serum lipid peroxides in alloxan-induced diabetic animals and to inhibit the body weight reduction and leukopenia induced by alloxan administration. (Joy and Kuttan, 1999).
Picroliv, the active constituent isolated from the plant Picrorrhiza kurrooa, was evaluated as a hepatoprotective agent against ethanol-induced hepatic injury in rats. (Sarawat et al., 1999).
Picroliv modulates the expression of insulin-like growth factor (IGF)-I, IGF-II and IGF-I receptor during hypoxia in rats. (Gaddipati et al., 1999).
A potent antioxidant derived from the plant Picrorrhiza kurrooa, in protecting against hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury in vivo. Picroliv was fed to male Sprague Dawley rats in a dose of 12 mg/kg once daily by oral gavage for 7 days prior to hepatic ischemia. Picroliv pretreatment resulted in better hepatocyte glycogen preservation and reduced apoptosis. Reduction in apoptosis was associated with decreased mRNA expression of caspase-3 and Fas. Oxidant induced cellular damage as measured by tissue malondialdehyde (MDA) levels was significantly less following picroliv pretreatment. Picroliv to be a promising agent for ameliorating injury following ischemia-reperfusion. (Singh met al.,2000)
Picroliv, an iridoid glycoside mixture prepared from the roots and rhizomes of Picrorrhiza kurrooa was found to be an effective inhibitor of hepatocarcinogenesis induced by N-Nitrosodiethylamine (NDEA) in rats. chemopreventive potential of Picroliv against chemically-induced liver tumours. (Rajeshkumar and Kuttan, 2000). Iridoid glycoside isolated from the roots and rhizomes of Picrorhiza kurroa, significantly prevented the biochemical changes induced by aflatoxin B1. (Rastogi et al., 2001)
Anti-tumour and anti-carcinogenic activity of Picrorrhiza kurroa extract were studied in mice. Administration of 20-methylcholanthrene (20 MC) produced 100% induction of sarcoma in control mice, whereas the tumour incidence and tumour related deaths were significantly inhibited by the oral administration of P. kurroa extract 150 and 750 mg/kg body weight, respectively. The extract was also found to reduce the volume of transplanted solid tumours induced by Dalton's lymphoma ascites (DLA) tumour cell lines and increased the life span of ascites tumour bearing mice. P. kurroa extract inhibited yeast topoisomerase I and II enzyme activity when tested on Saccharomyces cerevisiae mutant cell cultures. The extract did not inhibit the enzyme involved in the activation of carcinogen and the cell cycle regulatory enzyme cdc2 kinase. (Joy et al., 2000)
The cardioprotective effect of the ethanol extract of Picrorrhiza kurrooa rhizomes and roots (PK) on isoproterenol-induced myocardial infarction in rats with respect to lipid metabolism in serum and heart tissue has been investigated. Oral pre-treatment with PK (80 mg kg(-1) day(-1) for 15 days) significantly prevented the isoproterenol-induced myocardial infarction and maintained the rats at near normal status. (Kumar et al., 2001)
Protective effect of Picroliv, the active constituent of Picrorrhiza kurrooa, against chemical carcinogenesis in mice. (Rajeshkumar and Kuttan, 2001).
Picroliv has the potential to be developed as a protective agent against hemorrhage-resuscitation injury. (Seth et al., 2003)
