Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Gynocardia odorata is an evergreen, glabrous, dioecious tree with thickly-spreading branches that grows to 30-50 ft. in height and 3-6 ft. in girth, with a clear bole up to 20 ft. Its leaves are dark green, coriaceous, and oblong. The tree can be easily recognised by its hard, round, dark-gray, rough-textured fruit, growing on its stems and main branches and the branches are slender; bark greenish grey and lenticellate. The tree is distributed in north-east India (forests of eastern Himalayas, Sikkim, Khasia Hills, Chittagong, Martaban Hills, Assam and Burma. (1, 3, 4, 5, 8)

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Leaves are oblong to elliptically lanceolate, acute at base, on a rather slender, 6-8.5 mm. long petiole, the leaves are glossy, entire, alternate, abruptly acuminate, almost coriaceous and glabrous.(1, 3, 4, 5, 8)

Flowers are dioecious, pale yellow and sweet-scented, almost 3.8 cm. in diameter, the female flowers are larger, fragrant, on a 3.8 cm. long peduncles, fascicled, arising from tuberosities from the trunk and larger branches; stamens in males very numerous, with woolly filaments; staminodes in females 10, pinnatified, villous; berries as large as an orange and when mature, 10 to 20 pounds in weight, on a rather slender peduncle 2.5-3.8 cm. long, globular, with a thick" ash-coloured, roughish bark. Seeds numerous, imbedded in pulp.(1, 3, 4, 5, 8)

The seeds are about 3 cm. long and 1-5 cm. in breadth, elongated ovoid, somewhay flattened and irregularly angular, the hilium is at the pointed end, skin smooth, brittle; kernel oily and dark brown. The surface is dull yellowish gray, and somewhat scurfy to the touch from an adhering thin layer of pulp.(1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8)

Seed Biology :

The seeds weigh about 3 gm., of which the integument is 0.8 gm. The latter consists of an outer and inner layer of stone cells placed parallel to the surface, and between these two layers of two or three rows of stone cells, the long axes of which are nearly at right angles with the former cells. The inner surface of the sclerenchymatous tissue is covered with a delecate and partially broken layer of elongated parenchyma cells. The endosperm consists of tolerably thick-walled cells filled with solid colorless fat and large yellow spherical or irregularly rounded bodies, insoluble in alcohol and benzol. Starch, tannin and Calcium oxalate crystals were absent. (1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8)

Cultivation:

Gynocardia odorata species is light-demanding and needs little care. It grows mostly on sloping land at higher elevations or on stream banks and gullies where there are no other tall trees. Plantations can be established by direct sowing or by planting seedlings. Seedlings are grown in raised nursery beds or in polythene bags. (5)

Economic Potential and Uses

The seed kernel yeilds a pale yellow drying oil which possess insecticidal properties. The seeds are pounded and mixed with oil and used in skin diseases. (1, 5)

Back to Top

 

Parts used

Seed, Fruit, Trunk and Bark. (1, 3, 4, 5, 8)

Uses

Fruit: The fruit pulp is reported to be eaten by Lepchas (Nepal) after boiling.

Trunk: The wood is pale yellow to light brown, frequently discoloured by fungal sap stain, lustrous when first exposed, moderately hard, tough, light (sp. gr., c. 0.46; wt., 29 lb./cu. ft.), interlocked- grained and fine-textured. The timber seasons slowly and is generally converted green. It is fairly durable as posts, which remain green for 10 years or more and are not liable to insect or fungus attack. It is easily sawn when green; conversion of seasoned wood is more difficult. Though not a typical turnery wood, it turns well on the lathe and finishes to a good surface. It is a good constructional timber, especially for planking. It is also used for wall boards and dugouts.

Bark: The bark has medicinal value.

Major fatty acid of seed oil: Palmitic acid (63 %), Oleic acid , Linoleic acid (Isolinolenic), a new acid, gynocardic acid, hypogaeic acid and cocinic acid. These acids exist in the form of glycerides, as fats, the first two acids also in a free condition. Gynocardic acid (C14H24O2), however, is the important constituent (11.7 per cent), and gives the burning taste to the oil. Chaulmoogra oil strikes a green colour with sulphuric acid.

Processing Techniques

Oil is extracted from the seeds. The seeds are beaten into a pulp, heated, then compressed in tightly-woven bamboo baskets. Raw oil is boiled until it no longer foams. After cooling, the oil becomes white and semi-solid. (5)

Other Uses

Seed: Chaulmugra oil is used both internally and externally in leprosy, secondary syphilis, rheumatism, scrofula, and in phthisis. The seeds, when powdered, are reputed more active than the oil. The oil has relieved the dyspepsia and bronchitis occurring in lepers. Externally, it has been successfully applied in the above-named diseases, likewise in herpes, tinea, stiffness of joints, ulcers, and various cutaneous eruptions. In the latter class it generally proves too irritating except in eczemas and psoriasis. It is said to destroy pediculi and the itch insect. It is usually triturated with from 4 to 6 parts of simple ointment, and thoroughly rubbed in with the palm of the hand, or with the fingers' ends. Chaulmoogra oil in the anemia of syphilis, remedy if used early, to abort the constitutional effects of syphilis and states that by its action as a deobstruant, it averts indurations and banishes skin and mucous patches.

Fruit: The tree's fruit pulp is poisonous and is often used to kill fish. The seed is covered with a thick, edible pulp. The fruit is also used in Ayurvedic medicine. The fruit is hot; anthelmintic; useful in bronchitis, ulcers, skin diseases, small tumors and slight inflammations, leprosy, diabetes, gonorrhoea, fever, piles (Ayurveda).
Bark: The bark is said to be used as a febifuge.
(1, 3, 4, 5, 8)

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

The Oil has premium market in India because of its medicinal values, but of late, as a consequence of its high price, it is extensively adulterated by the natives of India, and so the physicians in India have discontinued its use. The oil imported from Nepal is sold under the trade name "Chulmugra," common in India's Ayurvedic medicine market. (5, 7, 8)
