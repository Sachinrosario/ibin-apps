 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
A small or medium sized, deciduous, soft-wooded tree, occurs all over India from Garhwal, Bundelkhand and West Sub-Himalayan tracts to Bengal, Bihar, Central India and Deccan peninsula. Less common in western coastal districts.


Availability :  

The tree occurs all over India from Garhwal, Bundelkhand and west sub-Himalayan tracts to Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Central India and Deccan Peninsula.  It is particularly common in hot dry and stony regions.  

Status : Found in dry deciduous forests and hilly forests. It is one of the few species capable of withstanding forest fires and is considered useful in afforestation of bare, rocky and denuded hills.  It is often cultivated in gardens and near temples for its beautiful flowers.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

A small deciduous tree 8-18 ft. in height and up to 1 ft. in diameter with  smooth, deeply furrowed, fibrous, ash-gray bark. palmately 5-lobed leaves, apex acuminate, with a blunt tip, base acute, glabrous above, tomentose beneath; petiole usually 10-18 cm long; stipules linear, 5-7 cm long, flowers bright yellow, 7-13 cm in diameter in terminal panicles with tomentose peduncles; pedicels stout 5-13 cm long, grooved and twisted pubescent, sepals unequal, oblong, concave, silky outline, petals obovate, deeply emarginate. Flowers appear before the new leaves. Fruits (capsule) 5-7.5 cm long, obovoid, striate outside, 5-valved. Seeds 5.5 mm long covered with white silky wood.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Bark, leaves, flowers, floss covering the seeds

Uses

The tree yields a gum which exudes from the fibrous, deeply furrowed bark.  The gum is similar in appearance and properties to Kadiya(karaya) gum obtained from Sterculia urens Roxb. and is often known by the same name as hog gum and used as a substitute for gum tragacanth.  The gum is pale and semi-transparent, transversely striated, with a tendency to split into flat scales.  It gives off acetic acid slowly on exposure to moist air.  It is insoluble in water, but swells into a pasty transparent mass in contact with water.  A mixture of 1 part of katira and 3 parts of gum arabic forms an adhesive paste with water.  It is neutral and yields with alkalies a thick mucilage of a pinkish colour and is not precipitated by acids. 

  •  The gum is  considered sweetish, cooling, stomachic and sedative.
  • The gum used in the cigar paste and ice-cream industry, calico-printing, marbling paper, leather dressing and to polish  tussar silk. 
  • It is also used in Unani medicine to treat gonorrhoea, syphilis, asthma , eye troubles and trachoma and cosmetically to soften skin. 
  • It is not soluble readily and become diffused into minute particles through a large quantity of water; hence its applicability to the art of marbling paper. 
  • The gum is largely used by Indian shoemakers and like that from Sterculia, could doubtless be employed to impart a polish to tasar silk.
  • It is used for edible and various purposes.  It is hygroscopic and when water is added, it swells like karaya gum.  It has commercial importance.

Chemistry
It occurs in striated and twisted pieces, of a pale semi-transparent colour, transversely fissured with a tendency to split up into flat scales.  It is neutral and yields with alkalies a thick mucilage of a pinkish colour and is not precipitated by acids. 

The gum contains over 50% pentosans and galactans, and on hydrolysis with mineral acids yields 14% acetic acid, gondi acid, C23H26O21, i-cochlospermic acid, xylose and galactose.

Other Uses
  1. It is capable of withstanding forest fires and considered useful in afforestation of tree on denuded hills.
  2. Dried leaves and flowers are used as stimulant in Punjab.
  3. Floss covering the seeds are used for stuffing mattresses, pillows and cushions and for life-belts.
  4. Non-drying oil is used for making soaps. The residual cake are used as cattle feed or manure.
  5. Wood is soft and light and has little value as timber.
  6. Bark yields a cordage fibre.

Economic Potential To The Country

This gum and the gum derived from Sterculia urens have assumed great importance in recent years, and several million lb. are exported annually from India for use in cigar paste and ice-cream industry.
