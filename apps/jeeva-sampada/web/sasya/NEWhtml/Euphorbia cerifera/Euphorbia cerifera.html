Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

The plant is a mass of erect pencil-like succulent stems. The stems are leafless during the dry season, but put on new leaves following rain. The stems have a natural vegetable wax that occurs as an outer coating of the shrub which helps retain moisture and protect it from the hot sun and winds. Candelilla is native to the Chihuahuan Desert in northern Mexico and western Texas. (1, 2,3,4,11,12,13,19,20,25)

Distribution India: Rajasthan, Jhabua District in Madhya Pradesh. (28,33) 

Availability :  

Status : Wild

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Euphorbia cerifera is a Cespitose perennial suffruticose herb or shrub, about 1m tall; rhizomes fleshy, 3-1-mm thick, dark-barked, branched, leading into numerous erect stems. Aerial stems mostly simple, 2-5(-10) dm tall, 3-4 mm thick, grayish-green, terete or longitudinally wrinkled when dry, glaucous with a waxy bloom; nodes enlarged, purplish-brown minutely pubescent, early deciduous, present only on new growth. (2)

Cultivation:

ECOLOGY: Candelilla grows best on rocky, sandy slopes, poor in humus, with very good drainage. Waxy coat is reported to be greater in the drier months or in more arid areas than in wetter areas.

All commercial candelilla Wax obtained from wild plants, and most of that is from Mexico. Under natural conditions, the plant is propagated by seed and possibly by root material. Few experimental plantings have been started with transplants from wild plants. Wax production from transplants has been lower than from natural growth.

(2)

Economic Potential and Uses

Candelillas extraordinary molding qualities capable of reproducing fine detail make it a superior precision casting wax.

Parts used and Uses

Whole Plants are source of Candelilla Wax; which, when refined, is used for polishes and creams, for foot and leather wear, for floor and furniture varnishes; when mixed with rubber, used in electric insulating materials and varnishes; for making waterproof fabrics, dental moldings; paper sizers, paint removers, metal lacquers, lithographic color, sealing waxes, records, shoe polishes, chewing gums (though bitterness of wax!is a problem) and Flavoring Agent or Adjuvant. . When mixed with paraffin, made into candles; used as a hardener for soft waxes; and spent candelilla plants make an acceptable source of paper pulp. (1,2,6,11,12,13)

Harvesting Practices

In manual harvesting pull the entire shrub from the soil, leaving most of the root system in the soil that within three years grows into a mature plant. Together with scattered seeds, roots remaining partially in the soil guarantee the growth of new strands, which takes several years. Harvested candelilla plants are transported to the field extraction facility. (2,3,11,12,13,19)


Produce and Products

Pharmaceutical Industry: This hard vegetable wax is mainly used for cosmetic and pharmaceutical stick products. It is also suitable for W/O emulsions.

Cosmetic Industry: Candelilla wax has extraordinary oil retention properties and improves the stability and texture of cosmetic products.

Food Industry/Confectionaries: Candelilla has food additive status. Widely used in the coating of soft candy, chocolate, fruit and in industrial bakeries as a release agent. Candelilla is the plasticizer used with chicle to make chewing gum. The oil retention properties of Candelilla allow the gum to hold flavors well and for this application has proven to be superior to most synthetic waxes.

Lumber Industry: Candelilla is softer than carnauba wax and used with carnauba and other waxes for furniture wax formulations.

Candle Industry: The wax on the stems has been used in candle-making.

Cleansing Products Industry: The wax is also used commercially in many other products, including candy, crayons and polishing waxes.

Electric and Electronic: Properties, such as chemical stability, water repellency, high melting point, and good electrical resistance, make Candelilla useful in electronic and electrical products, liquid and paste polishes and waterproofing compounds.

Paper Industry: Used in paper making additives.

Other applications include paints, sealing waxes, paint removers, Precision Casting, dyes and pyrotechnics. In sticks Candelilla wax provides good mould release, firmness, pay-off and surface gloss. It is often used in combination with carnauba wax, beeswax and other waxes where it improves skin feel and adhesion and pay-off properties. Because of its hardness and relatively high melting point Candelilla wax increases the softening point of sticks.

(1, 3, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 18, 19, 20, 23, 25)

Specifications

Characteristics:

Hard vegetable wax, brittle, lustrous, slightly tacky. Color varies from light brown to light yellow slightly tacky and lustrous. Low coefficient of expansion and contraction. Candelilla is softer than carnauba.

  • Candelilla plant yields from as low as 1 % to as high as 5 % wax, wax yield may be 1,000 kg/ha of which 6.57% might be fatty acids, 0.34% ash, and 84.2-91.17% nonsaponifiables. Candelilla wax consists of hydrocarbons (ca. 50 %, C29-C33), esters, free fatty acids and alcohols and resins. The wax is soluble in acetone, benzene; carbon disulfide, carbon tetrachloride, chloroform, dekalin, ether, gasoline, and tetralin. It is poorly soluble in absolute alcohol but very soluble in a 2:3 alcohol:benzene mixture.

Processing Techniques

Candelilla Wax Extraction:
The crude wax is extracted in the field by heating the plants in water, adding sulfuric acid, and skimming off the wax. The hot wax called cerote is allowed to cool. When no more wax is left the plants are removed from the solution with pitchforks and allowed to dry in the hot sun. These dried plants are used as fuel to heat the water for the next wax extraction. In this manner, everything from the plant is recycled with no waste.

Refining and Processing:
After the cerote has cooled, this crude wax is transported to the refinery where it is reheated, and the impurities removed by passing the hot, liquid wax through a filter press. The filtered Candelilla wax can be bleached into a light yellow color. The refined or bleached Candelilla is then processed into flakes, slabs, lumps, powder, or pastilles. These processed Candelilla waxes are then ready for use.

  (1,3,11,12,13,19)
