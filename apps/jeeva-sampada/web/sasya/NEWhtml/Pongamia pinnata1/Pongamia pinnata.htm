Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

A medium-sized, glabrous tree considered to be the native of Indian subcontinent - western ghats, now widely distributed throughout the world’s humid lowland tropics. It can be found in the Philippines, Malaysia, Australia, Oceania, the Seychelles, Florida and Hawaii. Tree is chiefly found along the banks of streams and rivers or near sea coast in beach and tidal forests. Occurs in tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plain and the Peninsula (moist tropical). It is not exacting in its soil and climatic requirements and grows in dry places far in the interior, up to an elevation of 1,000 m. mature trees withstand temperatures from slightly below 00 to 500 C. Tree grows wild on sandy and rocky soils (including oolitic limestone). It will grow in moist soil types; it is so highly tolerant of salinity that it can survive even with its roots in salt water. Suitable for growing around ponds, on water logged and alkaline sites and on roadsides.

It resists drought well, is moderately frost-hardy and highly tolerant of salinity. Distributed along the base and in outer vallyes of Himalayas from Ravi eastwards to Chittagong.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A medium-sized, glabrous tree with spreading or drooping branches. Bark soft, greyish-green. Leaves pale green, stipules small, oblong, obtuse, caducous. Leaflets opposite, 5-9 (usually 5), ovate-oblong or elliptic, acute or (usually) shortly acuminate, glabrous, base acute or rounded; main nerves 6-8 pairs, prominent beneath.

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in short axillary racemes shorter than the leaves, pedicels in fascicles of 2-4 along a glabrous or puberulous rachis, bracteoles ovate, acute. Calyx purplish, brown-pubescent, truncate or obscurely toothed. Corolla pinkish-white, standard suborbicular, emarginate, appendiculate at the base, the claw very short. Pods oblong or slightly obovoid, compressed, narrowed at the base and with a short decurved mucro at the apex, glabrous.

Floral and seed biology:

Grown by entire transplanting or direct sowing.

Natural reproduction is through seed or by root suckers. The seeds are said to retain viability for a year. Direct seed sowing is usually successful; nursery production is therefore often not needed.

Artificial regeneration is carried out through direct sowings or transplanting one-year old seedlings raised in nursery. Root or shoot cuttings can also be used for this purpose. It is a good coppicer, and is regarded as a fast growing species.

In West Bengal, a rotation of 30 years is considered suitable for fuel plantations. In South India, the tree is often pollarded for fodder or for green manuring purposes.

Classed as a fast-growing species, the trees often reach adult height in 4 or 5 years. They coppice very well.

 

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

The tree is attacked by a large number of insects and a few fungi. It is a host for the lac insect.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood commonly used as a fuel. Calorific value - 4,839 cal., 8,710 Btu. It burns well and is commonly employed for fuel in India. Seed oil is a potent alternative to diesel, which can be used as a fuel for tractors by farmers in Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka.

Wood: White, somewhat lustrous, aging to cream colour and often becomes dull, irregular, interlocked grained, medium coarse textured, moderately strong, tough, fibrous, hard and light to heavy. Average unit weight at 12 per cent moisture content 755 kg/m3. Specific gravity 0.747, 593-865 kg/ c m. Not durable.

Wood used for yokes of bullock carts, ploughs, solid cart-wheels, rafters of thatched cottages, oil mills, furniture, small turnery articles, agricultural implements, posts. Its use as pattern wood and as veneer for plywork has been suggested. Often employed for cabinet work.

and for boat building. Wood suitable for turnery, joinery work, bobbins and boot lasts. Used for manufacture of plywood, matches. Timber is used for boats and carpentry.

Produce and Products:

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques: Water seasoning reported to reduce insect attack. Wood liable to split and warp badly in seasoning. But water seasoning improves it.

Other uses:

Oil extracted from seeds, it is yellow or red-brown (30-40 per cent). It is not edible, but it can be used as a lubricant or, because it burns well, as a fuel in kerosene lamps. Mainly used in tanning industry and in the anufacture of soap, varnish and paints. Cake left after extraction of oil has high nitrogen content and mostly used as manure. It is reputed to have some ntiseptic value in treating human and animal skin diseases.

Fibre – The bark fibre is made into string and rope.

Fodder - Leaves lopped for fodder, especially in arid regions. Since pasture grasses grow well in its shade, it could be a valuable tree fodder. Its foliage is probably rich in protein.

Stem bark used for cordage.

Leaves, roots, stem bark of medicinal use.

It is a shade bearer and is considered to be a good tree for planting in pastures, as grass grows well in its shade. The tree is used for afforestation, especially in water sheds, in the drier parts of the country.

Pest control – When dried, pongam leaves are used to protect stored grains because their odour repels insects. They are often ploughed into the soil as a green manure and are thought to reduce nematode infestations. The presscake also has insecticidal and pesticidal properties and is sought after by Indian farmers. Researchers in India have shown recently that the presscake makes a useful poultry feed.

Seeds - Seeds yield oil, which is used in skin treatment. Seeds are medicinally important. It is also used for making candles.
Flower - Flowers are employed to quench dipsia in diabetes.

Miscellaneous Information


 

The plant has a wide-spreading, aggressive surface root system. It sheds leaves, flowers, and pods heavily and is therefore classed as a "trachy" tree. Moreover, because of its spontaneous seedlings and root suckers it may run wild and create serious weed problems. Tree is a nitrogen fixer.
