Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Deciduous tree, considered to be a native of Iran, Afghanistan and Baluchistan. Found growing wild in the warm vallyes and outer hills of the Himalayas between 900 and 1800 m. and cultivated throughout the India. Small tree or large shrub. Found in sub-tropical and tropical regions where climate is moist and hot but these trees do not produce good fruits in cold regions.

It stands considerable frost, thrives even in desert conditions. It is highly drought resistant. Gives very good returns in a deep, loamy soil. Thrives well on comparatively poor soils where other fruits fail to flourish.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small tree of 3-6 m height, shrubby, hardy. Bark is brown and scaly. Branching is weak, slender, branchlets sometimes spiniscent. Leaves are oblong, glossy, dark green, entire, short petioled, opposite.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers are scarlet red, sometimes yellow, mostly solitary or 2-4 together, medium sized, 2-3 cm across. Petals are leathery, bell shaped to cup shaped.

Fruit is bright red, off-white or cream. Berry, looks like a pome, round, 6-10 cm in diameter, skin leathery, tough peel, inside many loculii, each having many seeds enclosed in a rich pulp of yellow to red colour, closely oppressed.

Seeds round, pink, 0.25X2.5 cm, edible.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Seed are sown 0.5 cm deep in nursery or polybags. They complete germination in about 4-5 weeks. Cuttings are also used which is an easy way of propagation. Hard wood cutting method is universally used for propagation.

Best time for plantation is July. Transplanted in rainy season. Hardwood cuttings are planted in beds leaving only one or two buds exposed. One-year-old rooted cuttings along with the ball of earth around the roots are transplanted.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Pleuroplaconema punicae Petrak. - dieback of twigs

Aspergillus castaneus Patterson - Discolouration of fruits and seeds

Sphaceloma punicae - Disfiguration of fruits

Phomopsis sp. - Dry rot

Zythia versoniana Sacc. - Dry rot

Cercospora - leaf and fruit spot

Pestalotia sp. - leaf spot

Virachola isocrates Fb. - pomegrasnate butterfly, grub is most common pest

Other uses:

Fruits - Fruits are edible. Largely used as a dessert. The seeds along with the fleshy portion are dried and commercially marketed as anardana.

Fruit juice - Juice is prepared from the fruits. Fully riped fruits yield sweet, deep coloured juice with a rich flavour. It blends well with other juices. It is used in the preparation of true granadine. May be used for the production of wines.

Tannin - Fruit rind constitutes one of the important tanning materials in Jammu & Kashmir state.

Dye - Rind of pomegranate is a source of a dye which gives yellowish brown to khaki shades and has been used for dyeing wool and silk. In Almora district, Uttar Pradesh, it is largely used to dye wool.

Flowers yield a light red dye, which has been used to dye cloth.

Medicinal - Plant parts are medicinally useful. Dried bark of root and stem, has been used in treatment of tapeworms.

Insecticide - Insecticidal is prepared from the bark.

Antibacterial - Extracts of different parts of the tree exhibits antibiotic properties.

Fruits - Plant is grown for its refreshing fruits. Pulp contains 17 mg oestrone/ kg/ pulp is easily fermented to grenadine - a cordial. Extract is effective against HIV.
Fruit rind - It is medicinally important.
Seeds are medicinally important.

Leaves are used in conjunctivitis.
Bark - It is medicinally useful due to the presence of alkaloids. Bark is used to cure intermittent fever, diarrhoea.
Bark & fruit - Bark and fruit pericarp is used in tanning leather.
Flowers - Flowers are medicinal

Miscellaneous Information

 Planted as soil stabilizer. Grows on dry hilly slopes. Trees may be pruned to have good shape.

Pomegranate has been classified into two subspecies, chlorocarpa and porphyrocarpa, each having two varities. These subspecies have been established on the basis of the colouring of the ovary, a stable feature, which is retained ehwn they are reproduced by seeds.
