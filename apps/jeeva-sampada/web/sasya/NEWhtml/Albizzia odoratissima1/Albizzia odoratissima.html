DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Inflorescence: Solitary or 2-4 together in terminal panicles; Flower: Fragrant, sessile, hermaphrodite, complete, pentamerous; Calyx: Pubescent, teeth -obsolete; Corolla: White, grey-silky outside, teeth ovate-lanceolate, acute; Androecium:  Stamens indefinite, twice as long as corolla, filaments pale yellow, connate at the base half as long as the corolla tube ; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, style filiform, stigma minute, capitate ; Fruit: Pods shortly stalked, thin, flexible, glabrous, slightly reticulately veined, brown; Seed: 8-12, broadly ovate, much flattened, yellow . Flowering & Fruiting : April-June.

Chemistry:

Acacic acid m.p. 267° (Fig. 4). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960 – 1969).

The saponin obtained from the seeds of Albizzia odoratissima Benth from Maharashtra state on hydrolysis gives two triterpenic acid. The acid m.p. 266-270°C, acetate m.p. 260-265°C, acetyl methyl ester m.p. 279-280°C, and methyl ester m.p. 224-225°C has been identified as machaerinic acid (3b,21b-dihydroxy-D12-18-b-oleane-28-oic acid) (Fig. 1) by mixed melting point and infrared spectra with authentic sample. The acetate of the second acid genin melted at 228-234°C. (Varshney and Khan, 1961).

The seeds of Albizzia odoratissima Benth. from Uttar Pradesh yield a colourless saponin, m.p. 227-8°C, tentatively named as odoratissimin. On hydrolysis it yields and acid sapogenin, m.p. 296-9°C, which has been identified as echinocystic acid (3b,16a-dihydroxy-D12-oleaene-28-oic acid) (Fig. 2). (Varshney and Khan, 1962).

From the heartwood of A. odoratissima Benth. a dihydroflavonol was isolated as its methyl ether. From its degradation reactions, it was shown to be (+) O-pentamethyl dihydro-melanoxetin (Fig. 3). The synthesis of a racemate of this constitution is also reported. (Rao and Sastry, 1963).

Linoleic (29.70), oleic (24.01), palmitic (22.55), behenic (7.17), stearic (6.85), arachidic (3.86), myostic (2.63) capric (1.09), lauric (1.07) and lignoceric (1.07%) acids determined in seed oil (5.0%) by GC-MS; vitamin A (24 I.U.) and vitamin D (a I.U.) also present. (Ahmad and Akhtar, 1991). C.A. 1992, 116, 170218h, (Rudrappa and Revadi, 1991) C.A. 1992, 117, 86756v

 


Pharmacology:
Leguminous seeds of Albizzia odoratissima was investigated for its hypoglycaemic activity. It showed marked hypoglycaemic activity on normal albino rats but not on alloxan produced diabetic albino rats. The hypoglycaemic effect is probably due to direct or indirect stimulation of b-cells of islets of Langerhans to secrete more insulin so as to produce hypoglycaemia in normal young albino rats. (Singh et al.,1976).


Ethnobotany:
Fish poison (bark, Bedi, 1978).
