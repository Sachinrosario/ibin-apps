 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT :
A large, straggling shrub or a small tree, armed with spines, up to 4 m. in height, found in North-estern India, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and Central and South India. The tree is quite common in certain types of dry, open deciduous or scrub forests and grasslands, often forming impenetrable thickets when young, and in the sub- Himaiayan tracts it springs up in the blanks in the sal forests. In its natural habitat the absolute maximum shade-temperature varies from 38 to 48°, the absolute minimum from 4 to 15°, and the rainfall from 50 to 190 cm.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Branches, inflorescence and fruits covered with short greyish tomentum; leaves broadly elliptic, obovate or orbicular, serrulate, glabrous, dark above and covered with soft and pale tomentum beneath, oblique; flowers in compact cymes; fruits globose, 3-, rarely 2- or 4-celled, with usually a seed in each cell, very hard and woody.

Phenology : 
The old leaves begin to fall in February and the new leaves appear during April-May, followed by flowers during April- June. The young fruits begin to form soon after flowering and in North India they become fully ripe during January-April of the following year. 

Seed Biology :
The seed within the stone retains its viability for c. 30 months from the time of ripening, but tests have shown that at any time the proportion of viable seed is comparatively low, and the viability decreases as the time passes.

Seedling Germination:
Tests have shown that at any time the proportion of viable seed is comparatively low, and the viability decreases as the time passes. The rate of growth of seedling trees is slow. The rate of growth of seedling trees is slow, but that of coppice shoots is fairly rapid 

Cultivation :The tree is a light demander, growing typically in open situations, but is capable of standing a light degree of shade. It withstands drought well.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tree, Leaves, Bark.

Uses:
  1. The tree is one of the chief hosts for the propagation of lac, and is important in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and Punjab. 
  2. It is one of the major lac hosts in Madhya Pradesh for the production of Rangeeni lac. It has also been reported as a new host for Mitteriella ziziphina Syd. Systematic studies on various aspects of lac cultivation on this valuable regional host were made at the Regional Field research Station for Lac at Damoh (Madhya Pradesh).
  3. The leaves are used for fodder.

Other Uses
  1. The wood (wt, 960 kg./cu.m.) is durable and ea work. It is used for agricultural implements, cart-wheel and all classes of handles, and is suitable for pan beading, inlay work, frames, and other ornan purposes. It is used as fuel and for charcoal.
