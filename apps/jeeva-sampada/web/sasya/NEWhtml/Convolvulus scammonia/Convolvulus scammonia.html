DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Perennial, prostrate herb. Leaves linear to oblong, 1-3 x 0.5-0.8 cm, subacute to obtuse at apex, villous, sessile. Flowers 1-5 in axillary head, sessile to subsessile; bracts linear to oblanceolate, acute; bracteoles filiform. Sepals ovate-lanceolate to lanceolate, 4-7 mm long, acuminate, villous; outer sepals long; inner short. Corolla 10-12 mm long. Capsules subglobose, glabrous, 4-seeded. Seeds brown-black, sparsely to densely white pubescent.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flower: Solitary axillary, pedicillate, complete, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Sepals 5, equal, broadly elliptic; Corolla: infundibuliform,  glabrous; Androecium: Stamens 5, attached near the base of corolla, included,  ; Gynoecium: Ovary 2-celled, ovules 4, style filiform, stigma two; Fruit: Globose, capsule; Seed: dark reddish brown. 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : November- December. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind. 
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=24.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The whole plant is used as brain tonic. It improves memory.


Chemistry:
Scammonic aic A (fig. 1a), isobutryic, 2(S)-methylbutyric and tiglic acids obtained by alkaline ydrolysis of resin glycosides isolated from roots; two resin glycosides – scammonin I (fig. 2a) and scammonin II (fig. 2b) isolated and characterized as 11(S)-hydroxyhexadecanoic acid-11-{[O-6-deoxy-4-O-(2(E)-methyl-1-oxy-2-butenyl)-b-D-glucosyl(1®4)-O-6-deoxy-2-O-(2-methyl-1-oxybutyl)-a-L-mannosyl (1®2)-O-b-D-glucosyl(1®2)-6-deoxy-b-D-glu-cosyl]oxy}-intramolecular 1,3’’- ester and 11(S)-hydroxyhexadecanoic acid-11-{[O-b-D-glucosyl(1®4)-O-6-deoxy-2-O-(2-methyl-1-oxybutyl)-a-L-mannosyl(1®2)-O-b-D-glucosyl(1®2)-6-deoxy-b-D-glucosyl]oxy}-intramolecular 1,3’’-ester respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation and structure elucidation of four resin glycosides – scammonins III (fig. 2c), IV (fig. 2d), V (fig. 2e), and VI (fig. 2f) from roots; all glycosides have a common glycosidic acid, scammonic acid A and intramolecular macrocyclic ester structure. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

In addition, two minor resin glycosides – scammonin VII (fig. 3) and scammonin VIII (fig. 4) – having orizabic acid A (fig. 1c) and scammonic acid B (fig. 1b) respectively, isolated from roots and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
