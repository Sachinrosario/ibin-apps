 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Availability :  
Marshes throughout India, ascending up to 1300 m; Bangladesh, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant is common near canals, ponds and other water logged areas. It is also cultivated to limited extent.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual creeper, rooting at each node; stem soft, succulent, soft hairs present; branches 10-25 cm long; leaves alternate, simple,1.25-1.8cm long, sessile or subsessile,oval-shaped; flowers light blue or white,0.8-1.5cm long; capsules 2-grooved, values separating from entire column, many-seeded.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Solitary axillary; Flower: Pedicillate, bracteate, bracteolate,perfect, hermaphrodite, and hypogynous, solitary,axillary, white or lilac, campanulate; Calyx: Pentamerous, persistant, imbricate; Corolla: Gamopetalous, pentamerous, imbricate;Androecium: Stamens 5, epipetalous, arising from the base of corolla tube,anthers dithecous,opening lenghtwise; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, syncarpous, with superior ovary, placentation axile, a nectariferous disc is present at the base of ovary, stigma capitate; Fruit: Ovoid capsule with numerous seeds; Seed: ridged, brown to dark brown in colour .Flowers are pollinated by insects.  Birds and animals disperse seeds.

Floral and seed biology:
It flowers between April-June, the fruiting is observed between June-December.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Genetic variability:
Plant has chromosome number 2n=64.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation:  Plant is propagated by seeds as well as by cuttings of leaf, nodes and roots.

Soil type:  Plant responds well in poorly drained soils and water logged areas under sub-tropical conditions.

Nutrient requirements: Decomposed farmyard manure is required at the time of field preparation and NPK applied as basal dose at the time of planting.

Water management: Plant is immediately irrigated after planting and subsequently after 7-8 days as per requirement                                       

Harvesting period / Method: The plants are harvested after 7-8 months of planting after which senescence sets in and there is loss of plant biomass and bacoside yield.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used and uses:
(a) Plant : bitter, diuretic, blood-purifier, invigorates sex, poison-killer; (b) Plant-extract : used in purification of blood, for anaemia, dermatitis,diabetes, filaria; (c) Plant- juice (along with ginger, sugar and bark extract of Moringa oleifera), good for epilepsy. : to children in stomach disorder; (fried in ghee) : as a brain tonic, in nervous weakness, hysteria, epilepsy,insanity, anxiety neurosis, and to sharpen dull memory.
Plant-extract : In experiments with mice, rat and dog, it has been proved to be tranquilizer,musculature relaxant, antispasmodic, anticancer; Powdered dried leaf : satisfactory results obtained in man in cases of asthenia, nervous breakdown, and other low adynamic conditions.
Chemistry:
Plant : Bacoside A and B, betulic acid, betulinic acid, d-mannitol, stigmasterol, b-sitosterol, saponin, stigmastanol, hersaponic, monnierin, nicotine, luteoline and its glucosides.
NBPGR:
Commonly known as Brahmi, contains tetracyclic triterpenoid glycosides (bacoside-A and B) which are reported to be memory enhancers. Four B. monnieri accessions differing in bacoside-A content were evaluated for ontogenic and seasonal variation in accumulation of bacoside-A. The bacoside-A content in general increased with increase in leaf dry wt. Seasonal variation was found to induce changes in leaf-stem ratio, percent leaf dry wt. and bacoside-A content. The leaf stem ratio increased from July till Nov., remained more or less the same till Jan. and then decreased considerably. The percent leaf dry wt. decreased from Sept. till Jan. and then increased, whereas bacoside –A content decresed from July till Nov. and then increased. Though the leaf stem ratio was maximal during Nov. and Jan., the leaf percent dry wt. and the bacoside-A content were minimal during this period 1; wide variation in growth parameters and bacoside-A content and yield was observed between fifteen accessions of Bacopa monnieri collected from different geog. locations in India in a study conducted at the Central Institute of Medicinal and Aromatic Plants during 1996 and 1997. The accessions in general responded differently to the environmental conditions of winter (Sept. to Dec.) summer (Feb. to May) and monsoon (June to Sept.) seasons of North Indian plains. On av. Basis, the expression of vegitative growth and bacoside-A yield related characters was higher in the monsoon season as compared to the other seasons. Four accessions, BmTv, BmM, BmC2 and BmBp2 produced largest amount of shoot herbage; among these the last had the longest shoot. The shoot of the three accessions BmC1, BmC2 and BmBp1 contained higest concentration of bacoside-A. The yield of bacoside-A was significantly higher among six accessions, which included BmTv, BmBp2 and BmC2 that has large shoots and BmBp1 were identified as high bacoside-A yielding for the crop harvested in the summer and the monsoon seasons. On the other hand, the accession BmTv was found to be high bacoside-A yielding in all the three seasons 2.

Total alkaloidal fraction, LD50 8.5mg/100g in mice (i.p.), stimulated autonomic ganglia followed by blockade, produced spasm of the skeletal muscle, stimulated respiration followed by depression, showed tachyphylaxis and produced rigidity and convulsions in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Behavioural effects of active fraction suggested that it had tranquillising property, which was much weaker than that of chloropromazine. It did not block significantly conditioned avoidance response in rats even at high doses but offered graded protection against audiogenic seizure in mice and antagonized mescaline scratch response in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

An amorphous substance, m.p. 71°, betulinic acid, D-mannitol, and a glycoside, m.p. 275°, containing glucose and arabinose, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Two saponins- bacoside A, m.p. 250° and bacoside B, m.p. 203°- stigmastenol, b-sitosterol, stigmasterol in addition to mannitol and betulinic acid isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

Structures of bacoside A (fig. 1) and bacoside B assigned as 3-(a-L-arabinopyranosyl)-O-b-D-glucopyranoside-10,20-dihydroxy-16-keto-dammar-24-ene; these differ only in optical rotation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Another saponin- monnierin- isolated and its structure assigned. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)f.

Nicotine detected by PC in alcoholic extract. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)g.

Nicotine, 3-formyl-4-hydroxy-2H-pyran, luteolin and leutolin-7-glucoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Bacogenin A1 (fig. 2) obtained from acid hydrolysate of bacoside A, characterized as 3, 18-dihydroxy-20à25-epoxy-22 (or 23)-methyl-24-nor-dammar-22-en-16-one. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Isolation and structure of Bacogenin A3 (fig. 3). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Bacogenin A4 (fig. 4), m.p. 175°, obtained from acid hydrolysate of bacoside A identified as ebelin lactone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Bacogenin A2 characterised as isomer of bacogenin A1 differing either in configuration at C-20 or disposition of vinylic Me in side chain. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Structure and absolute configuration of jujubogenin (fig. 5) and pseudojujubogenin (fig. 6) obtained by degradation of bacoside A. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Isolation of apigenin-7-glucuronide and luteolin-7-glucuronide from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

A new minor saponin- bacoside A1 (fig. 7)- isolated and characterized as 3-O-a-L-arabinofuranosyl(1à3)-a-L-arabinosyl-jujubogenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Isolation of another saponin- bacoside A3 (fig. 8)- and its structure elucidated as 3-O-b-D-glucosyl(1à3)-[O-a-L-arabinofuranosyl(1à2)]-O-b-D-glucosyl- jujubogenin; revision of structure of cis-isomer of ebelin lactone (fig. 9), obtained during acid hydrolysis as another artifact of jujubogenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

A dammarane type pseudojujubogenin glycoside, bacopasaponin D (m.p. 250°C) has been isolated from the leaves of Bacopa monnieri and defined as 3-O- {alpha-Larabinofuranosyl(1 to 2)beta-D-glucopyranosyl} pseudojujubogenin  by spectroscopic methods and chemical transformation. (Garai et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0351.

Three new dammarane-type triterpenoid saponins, bacosaponin A, B and C, of biological interest have been isolated from Bacopa monnieri and identified as 3-O-alpha-L-arabinopyranosyl-20-O-alpha-L-arabinopyranosyl-jujubogenin. 3-O-{alpha-arabinofuranosyl (1 to 2) alpha-L-arabinopyranosyl}pseudojujubogenin and 3-O-{beta-D-glucopyranosyl (1 to 3)-{alpha-L-arabinofuranosyl (1 to 2)} alpha-L-arabinopyranosyl} pseudojujubogenin by spectroscopic methods and some chemical transformations. (Garai et al, 1996). MAPA 1996, 9606, 3640.

Structure of bacosine (fig. 10) (m.p. 284-5°C) isolated from the aerial parts of Bacopa monnieri was elucidated lup-20(29)-ene-3alpha-ol-27-oic acid. (Vohora et al, 1997). MAPA 1998. 9801, 0243.

A new saponin, bacoside A2, has been isolated from B. monniera and its structure was elucidated as 3b-[(O-a-L-arabinofuranosyl-(1à6)-O-[a-L-arabinopyranosyl (1à5)]-O-a-D-glucopyranosyl)oxy]-pseudojujubogenin by chemical and spectral analysis. (Rastogi and Kulshreshtha, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 226098y.

The phytochemical investigation of aerial parts of Bacopa monniera has resulted the isolation of a sterol 13,14-seco-stigma 9(11), 14(15)-diene-3b-ol designated as bacosterol and a triterpene lup 20(29)-ene-3a-ol-27-oic acid named as bacosine. Their structures have been elucidated by spectroscopic and chemical methods. (Ahmad and Rahman, 2000). MAPA 2000, 05, 3035.

A procedure to evaluate bacoside-A biosynthetic potential by tracer technique using labeled carbon dioxide has been standardized. The technique involved (1) incorporation of 14C labeled carbon dioxide into bacoside (2), isolation of bacoside-A (3) and hydrolysis of bacoside-A to yield bacogenin. The label incorporated into bacogenin served as an indicator of the bacoside biosynthetic potential. Using the procedure Bacopa monniera accessions differing in bacoside-A content were screened for their biosynthetic potential to synthesize bacoside-A and to find out any relationship between bacoside-A synthersis and accumulation. (Ganjewala et al, 2000). MAPA 2001, 01, 0573.

Two new dammarane-type jujubogenin bisdesmosides, bacopasaponins E (fig. 11) and F (fig. 11a) of biological interest have been isolated from Bacopa monniera and defined as 3-O-{b-D-glucopyranosyl(1 à 3)(a-L-arabinofuranosyl(1à2)}, a-L-arabinopyranosyl}-2-O-(a-L-arabinopyranosyl) jujubogenin and 3-O-{b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à3)(a-L-arabinofuranosyl(1à2)} -b-D-glucopyranosyl}-20-O-a-L-arabinopyranosyl) jujubogenin respectively by spectroscopic methods and some chemical transformation. (Mahto et al, 2000). MAPA 2000, 05, 3109.

Four new saponins from Bacopa monniera reputed for its use as a nervine tonic for memory improvement with eight other saponins has been confirmed. (Mahato, 2000). C.A.2001, 134, 353178m. 


Pharmacology:

Hersaponin mainly exhibited sedative action in mice (i.p.). It did not protect rats against electric shock and mice against metrazole seizures nor did it diminish metrazole toxicity. It reduced amphetamine toxicity in mice. It potentiated hypnotic effects of hexobarbital, pentobarbital and EtOH in mice; maximum potentiation observed when it was administered 15 min. prior to other compounds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)h.

Plant extract showed significant barbiturate hypnosis potentiation in rats.

Saponins showed significant change in locomotor activity and learning behaviour of normal rats and also significant barbiturate hypnosis potentiation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Effect of administering alcoholic extract of brahmi (40mg/kg, p.o. for 3 or more days) on learning performance of rats studied in three different conditioning schedules. Drug-treated animals showed better acquisition, improved retention and delayed extinction. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Treatment with plant extract for one month brought about reduction in level of anxiety, adjustment disability, leading to improved mental function. Levels of urinary V.M.A. and corticoids were also reduced. Thus, drug appeared to have anti-anxiety agent property. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

An alcoholic extract (40 mg/kg p.o. x 3 day) of Bacopa monnieri, was found to significantly enhance the acquisition, consolidation and retention parameters in a foot-shock motivated brightness discrimination reaction in a Y-maze. Further fractionation yielded bacosides A and B, and a mixture of these bacosides (10mg/kg p.o.), when administered 90min. prior to training, significantly enhanced the acquisition and retention of the brightness discrimination reaction during later and also early phases of consodilation (20mg/kg p.o.). A dose of 10 mg/kg p.o. when given every alternate days significantly improved the facilitation of procedural, declarative and spontaneous memories in an active conditioned avoidance response. Bacosides also significantly enhanced the number of responses in a Sidman Continous Avoidance Response. In EEG, it produced concomitant changes. Bacosides (20 mg/kg p.o. x 3 d) prevented the amnesic effects of Ecs (25 ma, 50Hz, 0.5 sec.) and 18hrs. immobilization sleep deprivation stress. (Singh and Dhawan, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9505, 2922.

Analysis of the plant reveals that glutamic acids and aspartic acids are present in high concentration in the drug and are also required by brain in high concentration to keep itself in higher gears. Both these amino acids are used as neurotransmitters and also in stabilizing and stimulating the activities of brain. (Ahmad et al, 1994). C.A. 1995, 123, 266272s.

Efficacy of Smritsagararasa, a herbomineral preparation containing Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri) with other plant drug in the dosage of 250 mg tablet, given three time a day with honey has been reported. Eleven patients out of thirty (36.67%) showed significant improvement and approximately similar number (33.33%). Showed moderate improvement after months treatment in case of residual Schizophrenia. (Tripathi and Singh, 1994). MAPA, 1996, 9603, 1357.

Bacopa monnieri plant extract has been reported to have anticancer property. Different concentrations of ethanolic extract of Bacopa monnieri were tested with sarcoma-180 cell culture. The cell growth, viability and 3H-thymidine incorporation into the DNA were assessed. Sarcom-180 cell growth was significantly inhibited with increasing concentrations of Bacopa monnieri. The site of action of the drug has been suggested to be at DNA replication stage. Hence the alcoholic extract of Bacopa monnieri can be considered as the anticancer drug. (Elangovan et al, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9601, 0146.

Action of Bacopa monnieri on free radical induced lipid peroxidation was examined. Different fractions of the plants were tested in vitro and in vivo experiments by using different tissues. Lipid peroxidation was produced by different chemicals, specific for the generation of free radicals. As a result Bacopa monnieri exhibit anti-lipid peroxidation property. (Tripathi et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0079.

Bacopa monnieri used for memory enhancing epilepsy, insomnia, and as mild sedative. The alcoholic and hexane fraction of Brahmi has been studied on FeSO4 and cumene hydroperoxide induced lipid peroxidation. Alcoholic fraction showed greater protection with both inducers. Results were compared with known antioxidant tris, EDTA and a natural antioxidant vitamin E. the effect of Brahmi was also examined on hepatic glutathione content. The mechanism of action could be through metal chelation at the initiation level and also as chain breaker. The results suggested that Brahmi is a potent antioxidant. (Tripathi et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 212635k.

Brahmi in crystalline form slows down memory loss in aged rats. (Gupta et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0128.

Medhya rasayan drug (brain tonic) Aindri, identified as Bacopa monnieri was selected for the evaluation. Thirty six patients of Cittodvega vis-à-vis anxiety neurosis were selected for the clinical study and were randomly divided into two groups. Extract of the drug Aindri (Bacopa monnieri0was administerd both the groups in dose of 1.5 gms, representing 7.5 gms of dry crude drug daily for a period of four weeks. The trial treatment produced significant improvement in the level of anxiety and depression, mental fatigue, memory span, along with positive effect on systolic blood pressure and rate of respiration. The drug also cause significant improvement in symptoms of anxiety, nervousness, palpitation, headache, insomnia etc. Aindri also showed barbiturate hypnosis potentiation effect with improved learning behaviour in albino rats. (Yadav and Singh, 1996). MAPA 2000, 06, 3391.

The antioxidant property of Bacopa monnieri was evaluated by determining FeSO4 induced lipid peroxidation in rat brain homogenate (in vitro). Significant protection against lipid peroxidation was observed at the dose of 500 mg/ml, measured as reduction in the level of malondialdehyde induced by 4mM Fe2+. The effect of water decoction of Bacopa monnieri is more potent. The mechanism of action could be through metal chelation at the initiation level and also as a chain breaker. (Tripathi and Tripathi, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0195.

Neuro psycho-pharmacological effect of the noortropic Bacopa monnieri was carried out. (Singh and Dhawan, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9802, 0765.

The relaxant action of an ethanol extract of Bacopa monnieri was examined on ring segments of pulmonary arteries (guinea-pig and rabbit), aorta (rabbit) and tracheal preparations (guinea-pig). The plant extract induced relaxation in all the tissues in a dose dependent manner. Guinea pig main pulmonary artery was found to be the most responsive to the plant extract, however, complete relaxation was obtained in the tracheal preparations. The results of the study suggest that relaxation induced by Bacopa monnieri possibly involves prostacyclin compounds (in all the tissues) and beta-adrenoceptors (in tracheal preparations). Further, this relaxation is independent of endothelium and muscarinic receptors activations. (Dar and Channa, 1997). MAPA 1997, 9705, 2803.

Reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography seperation coupled to (structurally informative) spectroscopic methods like NMR and MS and an efficient bioassay have been used to determine the active compounds from crude fraction of Bacopa monniera. The fraction containing a mixture of saponins with closely related structures was found to show significant anthelminitc ativity against Caenorhabditis elegans. (Renukappa et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 225791g.

A herbal preparation having Bacopa monnieri as its one ingredient [15.0-25.0] shows improvement in estrogen deficiency related cognitive dysfunction- in woman. (Grose, 2000). C.A. 2001, 135, 127166v.

Bacopa monnieri (BM) was evaluated alone and in combination with phenytoin for its effect on passive-avoidance (PA) task; maximal electroshockl seizures; and locomotor activity in mice. BM extract (40 mg/kg x 7 days), given along with phenytoin (PHT) in the second week of the two-week regimen, significantly reversed PHT-induced impairment. Both acquisition and retention of memory showed improvement without affecting its anticonvulsant activity. The cognitive effects of PHT and BM were found to be independent of motor simulation. Potential corrective effect of BM in cognitive deficit associated with PHT therapy have been suggested. (Vohora et al, 2000). MAPA, 2000, 06, 3520.

Bacopa monnieri (BM) extract (bacoside A content 82% ± 0.5%), administered in doses of 5 and 10 mg/kg, orally, induced a dose-related increase in SOD, CAT and GPX activities, in all the brain regions investigated, after 14 and 21 days of drug administration. On the contrary, deprenyl induced an increase in SOD, CAT and GPX activities in the frontal cortex and striatum, but not in the hippocampus, after treatment for 14 to 21 days. Bacopa monniera like deprenyl, exhibited a significant antioxidant effect after subchronic administration which, unlike the latter, extended to the hippocampus as well. The increase in oxidative free radical scavenging activity by Bacopa monniera has been suggested to explain, at least in part, the cognition-facilitating action of Bacopa monniera, recorded in Ayurvedic texts, and demonstrated experimentally and clinically. (Bhattacharya et al, 2000). MAPA 2000, 04, 2164.

Extract from Bacopa monnieri, to children with mental retardation, was reported to significantly improve short-term and long-term memory. (Lodha and Bagga, 2000).

Brahmyadi Ghana Vati, consist of plant Bacopa monnieri, with other plant was tested. Forty patients of hypertention which were divide into trial and control groups of 20 each. Trial group was treated with Brahmyadi Ghana Vati 500 mg tablet twice daily and control group treated with Atenolol 50 mg tablet twice daily. The total effect obtained by Brahmyadi Ghana Vati was, 30% marked improvement, 30% moderate improvement, 25% slight improvement and 15% no improvement. Analysis shows that the trial drug is significantly effective (P values less than 0.001). the total effect obtained by atenolol treatment was, 80% mark improvement and 20% moderate improvement. (Rath et al, 1999). MAPA 2000, 06, 3376.

Successive petroleum ether, chloroform, methanol and water extracts of Bacopa monnieri were tested (In vitro) for mast cell stabilizing effect. The methanolic fraction exhibited potent activity comparable to disodium cromoglycate, a known mast cell stabilizer. (Samiulla et al, 2001).

Effects of Brahmi on human memory was studied and has shown significant effect on a test for the retention of new information , rate of learning and also decreased the rate of newly acquired information. (Roodenrys et al, 2002).

Successive petroleum ether, chloroform, ethanol and water extracts, a saponin rich fraction (SRF) and bacoside A isolated from Bacopa monnieri were tested for brine shrimp lethality. Successively ethanol extracts and SRF showed potent activity. Bacoside A showed the maximum activity with LC(50) of 38.3 microg/mL. The results confirmed the previous reports of an anticancer effect of Bacopa monnieri and suggest bacoside A as the active constituent. (D’Souza et al, 2002).

Pharmacognosy:
Comparative pharmacognostic study and chemical/pharmacological evaluation of Centella asiatica and Bacopa monnieri was carried out and has shown that Centella asiatica is the real plant. (Shakoor et al, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9502, 0786.
