Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Native of Tropical America, naturalized and cultivated in India, especially near the coast. Coastal areas of tropical and sub-tropical nature. Medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plains and the Peninsula (Moist Tropical). Suitable for house compounds, marginal lands and village commons. Naturalized in the warmer parts of India especially near the sea. The cashew grows wild along East and West coasts of South India, as far North of Ratnagiri on the West and Mahanadi delta on the East. It has become thoroughly acclimatized in South India in the forest regions along the West coast. In South Kanara, it grows extensively in a wild state, mixed with mango and other forest trees and shrubs, all along the hill slopes where owing to poor soil and the exposed situation, it attains a low bush form. it is densely scattered over the low hilly regions of Goa. On the east coast, it is abundant in Thanjavar, South Arcot, Chingleput, Nellore, Guntur, Godavari and Vishakhapattanam districts. It is planted in Orissa near the coast and has run wild in some parts of the Mahanadi delta.

Cashew is a hardy drought resistant tree, thriving under a variety of climatic conditions. Mild tropical climate is suitable for cashew cultivation. The crop is very sensitive to temperature extremes. It is less resistant to frost and chilly weather, but is able to blossom and develop fruits during a long dry period preceding the rains, that normally delays the blossoming of other trees. It is highly resistant to strong winds also. Cashew grows well in shallow and impervious soils.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A small tree with usually round canopy, 10-12 m tall and 6-7 m in spread, short thick crooked trunk. Branching upright, glabrous and strong. Leaves are lush green, leathery, coriaceous, oval and alternate, rounded at the apex, finely reticulately veined, base cuneate, main nerves 10-12 pairs, prominent beneath, flattened with long petiole.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers are pinkish white, small and scented, in panicles usually borne on tips of young branches, longer than the leaves, the branches cymose; peduncles lengthening with the age. Bract ovate, very acute, nerved, puberulous outside. Sepals lanceolate, puberulous outside. Petals linear-lanceolate, deflexed from the middle, minutely puberulous outside. Stamens about 9, one longer than the others, the longer one exserted beyond the recurved petals. Ovary glabrescent, attenuated into a subulate style.

Fruits are kidney shaped, dry, white in colour with a greyish coloured pericarp, which is hard and irritant. Flower stalk swells to form a fleshy, sweet edible mass of small apple, which may be white, yellow or scarlet on ripening.

Seeds are kidney shaped, oily, cream.


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowers during January - March. Flowering commences from November and extends up to February, and fruit ripens from March to May.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: The tree is mainly propagated from seeds.

Grown by entire transplanting. Trees are raised by seeds which are collected from February to April from superior mother trees which are regular and heavy bearers, healthy and free from diseases and insects. To remove the seed coat they may be treated with dilute sulphuric acid for 8-10 hours and then washed in water to remove the acid. 2-3 seeds are sown in situ in each pit or place and later the plants are thinned to have only one vigorous plant. Prefers poor soil and is tolerant to drought to some extent. Germination is complete in 4 weeks. Transplanted in monsoon. Spacing is 8X8-10 m.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel. Roasted outer shells of nuts are used as fuel in cashew processing factories.

Wood: Wood is reddish brown, fairly hard and heavy (wt 608 kg/cu m), close grained. Very good for making packing cases and country boats and tea chests.

Other uses:

Ornamental, avenue, shade tree.

Fruit - Fruits are used as nuts. Nuts are eaten roasted. Cashew apple is edible and yields a delicious beverage. The juice can be fermented and made into wine. Wine can be distilled to produce a brandy. It can also be used in pharmaceutical industry. Cashew apple is used for making juice, syrup, candy and pickle after removing the astringent principle. Candy is prepared from the fruit. The cashew apple residue can be used for pectin extraction.

Cashewnut shell liquid - Cashewnut shell yields a vesiscent juice CNSL which is a valuable raw material for the manufacture of numerous industrial products.

Fodder - young leaves and buds are used as a vegetable.

Green manure - Leaves used as green manure.

Cultivation - Cashew trees are used for afforestation. Sites selected for afforestation are the barren, denuded areas which are usually unsuitable for raising any other timber species

.Nut - Nut shell liquid is used in break lining and clutches.
Stem - stem yields gum.
Bark - Bark yields sap from which indelible ink is obtained.
Resin is used for tarring boats, fish nets and for varnishes.

Miscellaneous Information

 The cashew was introduced in India by Portuguese in the latter half of the 16th century. Cristavao da Coasta recorded it as grown in Cochin in 1578, which suggests that Cochin served as a dispersal point for the cashew in India and perhaps, for South-East Asia as well.

The reason for its introduction in India is not documented, although the popular explanation is that it was introduced for the purpose of checking the soil erosion in the coastal areas of the country.

Vegetative propagation could be followed in cashew to ensure better performance. But these plants get easily damaged and fail to establish if not handled properly.

Air-layering, grafting, budding, stooling or mound layering is also practised.

Fruit - Pericarp is hard and acrid. Pericarp oil is irritant due to presence of cardol and anacardic acid.
