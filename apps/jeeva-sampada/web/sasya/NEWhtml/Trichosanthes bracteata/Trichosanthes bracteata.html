HABIT & HABITAT
 Climber; stem robust, woody, grooved, older light grey with scabrous spots, younger green, smooth. It is found in almost throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tendrils 2-3 cleft. Leaves variable, 6.3 x 25 cm in diam., palmately 3-5 lobed, dark green above, paler below, glabrous, sometimes scabrous with small scales, cordate at base; lobes ovate-oblong, acute at apex, dentate or serrate. Male flowers: in axillary, 5-10 flowered raceme, 15-23 cm long, rarely solitary; pedicels very short, erect; bracts ovate, pale green, dotted with dark green spots; calyx longitudinally striate; lobes lanceolate; petals white, wedge-shaped; filaments slightly villous. Female flowers: solitary, axillary. Fruits globose, 3-8 cm in diam., red when ripe with 10 longitudinally orange streaks. Seeds ellipsoid, smooth, attenuate at base.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine fruit is used in asthma, earache and ozoena. In Unani system of medicine fruit lessens inflammation and cures hemicrania, heat of brain and leprosy. It is used in leprosy, rheumatism and toothache. Seeds are purgative.


Chemistry:

·        A new methylsterol – cyclotrichosantol (fig. 1) – isolated from leaves along with cycloeucalenol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·        A triterpene – trichotetrol, mp. 289° - isolated from roots which was not characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·        A new saponin – santholin (fig. 2a) – isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·        A new saponin – trichonin (fig. 2b) – isolated and its structure elucidate; hexacosanoic acid, cycloeucalenol, b-sitosterol and a-spinasterol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·        Isolation of cucurbitacins B and D and bryonolic acid from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        A protease has been purified from sarcocarp of Trichosanthes bracteata (Lam.) Voigt by four steps of chromatography.( Kaneda and Uchikoba, 1994).

·        From the fresh roots of Trichosanthes bracteata Voigt., the following substances were identified: methyl palmitate, palmitic acid, suberic acid, a-spinasterol, stigmast-7-en-3 b-ol, a-spinasterol 3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside, stigmast-7-en-3 b-ol 3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside, glyceryl 1-palmitate, glyceryl 1-stearate, bryonolic acid, cucurbitacin B, isocucurbitacin B, 3-epi-isocucurbitacin B, 23,24-dihydrocucurbitacin B, 23,24-dihydroisocucurbitacin B, 23,24-dihydro-3-epi-isocucurbitacin B, cucurbitacin D, isocucurbitacin D and D-glucose. (Kitajima et al, 1989).

·        From the fruits of Trichosanthes tricuspidata, 14 cucurbitane glycosides (khekadaengosides A-J, M-N, cucurbitacin J 2-O-beta-glucopyranoside and cucurbitacin K 2-O-beta-glucopyranoside), a hexanorcucurbitane glucoside (khekadaengoside K) and octanorcucurbitane (khekadaengoside L) were isolated along with two known cucurbitane glucosides (cucurbitacin 2-O-b-glucopyranoside and 25-O-acetyl-cucurbitacin 2-O-beta-glucopyranoside). (Kanchanapoom et al, 2002).

Pharmacology:

·        Pharmaceutical compositions for HIV virus infection comprise e.g. poly I.C. 1, 10, Trichosanthes bracteata proteins 0.05-0.33 and prifampin 0.1-1mg in 0.1M NaCl– 0.06M phosphate buffer (pH 6.5-7.5). The preparations inhibited growth of HIV-I virus in cultures. (Lu et al, 1995).

·        An extract of the fruits of Trichosanthes tricuspidata collected in North Vietnam was found cytotoxic in KB cells. A bioassay-guided fractionation led to the isolation of a series of cucurbitacins of which two are new: tricuspidatin and 2-O-glucocucurbitacin J.  (Mai et al, 2002).

Miscellaneous Information

·         Antibacterial activity of the nonsaponifiable parts of the fixed oils of Tichosanthes dioca along with other species of Trichosanthes was studies. Petroleum extracts of the seeds were found to possess appreciable antibacterial activity against Bacilis anthracis and Xanthomonaas malracearum. (Hariti and Rathee, 1995). MAPA 9603-1513.

·         Seed of Trichosanthes bracteata were extracted with petroleum ether and they showed antifungal property. (Harit et. al., 1996).
