HABIT & HABITAT
Cultivated in the coastal areas; also naturalised at places on sandy coast and hills near the sea. Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Tripura, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, TamilNadu, Kerala, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.


Availability :  It is also available in Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri lanka, Philippines, Africa and Tropical america.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Small trees. Leaves coriaceous (leathery), obovate, 5-14 x 3.5-8.5 cm, apex blunt or rounded, margin entire, base acute, glabrous; petiole 1-2 cm long. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Terminal panicles ; Flower: Bracteate, hermaphrodite, complete, polygamous, regular ; Calyx:  Calyx 5-partite, deciduous, erect, imbricate,sepals lanceolate, puberulous, ; Corolla: Petals linear-lanceolate, deflexed, from the middle, minutely puberulous outside ; Androecium: Stamens 9, one longer than others, the long exerted, beyond the recurved petals, filaments connate and adnate to the disk ;Gynoecium: Ovary obovoid, glaberescent, attenuated into subulate style,  ovule 1, ; Fruit: Reniform, its pedicle large, fleshy, dark-coloured ; Seed: Reniform, ascending, radicle short hooked ; Flowering & Fruiting: January-March. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by birds,animals or human agencies.
Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in axillary or terminal panicles, yellowish green with reddish centre, fragrant, stalked. Sepals 5, ovate-elliptic, 4-1 mm. Petals 5, thick, linear-lanceolate, 13 x 2 mm. Stamens unequal. Fruit a kidney shaped nut, 2.5-3.5 x 1.5-2.0 cm, seated on enlarged peduncles which are green when young but red or yellow when mature. It usually flowers and fruits between Jan-May.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
 Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and also by vegetative means.

Soil Type: Plant does well in shallow and impervious soil. It also grows well in deep loam.

Irrigation: Plant is seldom irrigated but in raising young plantations in dry tracts, however, it is necessary to water the plant for year or two.

Nutrient requirements: Farm yard manure @ 7-11 tonnes/ha, oil cakes are also applied. Application of suitable doses of fertilizers are also recommended.

Harvesting: The entire crop of nuts does not ripen at one time. The duration of harvest extend from 45-70 days and fruits are picked daily during this period

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Uses:
Bark is used in diarrhoea, leprosy, rubifacient, skin disease, ulcer and vesicant. Roots are also useful in purgative.


Chemistry:

  • Ethyl gallate, hyperoside, methyl gallate, leucocyanidin and leucodelphinidin identified. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969).

  • A degraded polysaccharide obtained from cashewnut shell polysaccharide by mild hydrolysis; its proposed structure composed of galacturonic acid. Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979).

  • p-hydroxybenzoic, protocatechuic, gentisic and  gaillc acids along with glucosides rhamnosides, arabinosides and  xylosides of kaempferol and  quercitol  found  in  leaves ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979).

  • Cyanidin, delphinidin, pelargonidin, quercetin, its 3-galactoside, myricetin and gallic acid isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979).

  • Naringenin and naringenin-7-O-β-D-(6”-O-p-coumaryl) glucoside (prunin-6”-O- p-coumarate) isolated from defatted nut hell and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979).

  • A new biflavonoid glucoside-occidentoside- isolated from nut shells and its structure elucidated. In addition, (-)salipurposide and b-sitosterol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984).

  • Isolation of b-sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol and cholesterol from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989).

  • Myricetin, agathisflavone, robustaflavone, amentoflavone, quercetin, kaempferol, apigenin, quercetin-3-0-rhamnoside and quercetin-3-0-glucoside from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989).

  • The volatile compounds of a largely consumed Brazilian cashew apple variety (Anacardium occidentale L. var. nanum, Anacardiaceae) were recovered by headspace extraction or simultaneous distillation-extraction. Several compounds including esters (29), terpenes (16), hydrocarbons (9), carboxylic acids (7), aldehydes (7), alcohols (3), ketones (2), lactones (2) and norisoprenoids (1) were characterized and quantified by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry analyses. (Bicalho and Rezende, 2001)

  • Separation of anacardic acid, cardol, and cardanol for industrial application. Anacardic acid was selectively isolated as calcium anacardate. The acid-free cashew (Anacardium occidentale L.) nut shell liquid was treated with liquor ammonia and extracted with hexane/ethyl acetate (98:2) to separate the mono phenolic component, cardanol. Subsequently, ammonia solution was extracted with ethyl acetate/hexane (80:20) to obtain cardol. (Paramashivappa et al., 2001)

Pharmacology:

·        In diabetic rats level of blood sugar was markedly reduced by decoction of plant. A dose of 25 mg/kg produced hypoglycaemic effect in dogs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969). 

·        (-)Epicatechin reduced rat paw oedema induced by carrageenin (ED50, 74 mg/kg, i.p.). It produced significant inhibition of accumulation of pouch fluid and granulation tissue formation in carrageenin-induced granuloma pouch in rats, which could be attributed to decreased formation of granulation tissue. It was effective against cotton pellet granuloma and adjuvant-induced chronic arthritis in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984).

·        Essential oil inhibited spontaneous motor activity, potentiated pentobarbitone-induced hypnosis, caused deterioration of rota-rod performance and blocked conditioned avoidance responce in rats; it also had hypothermic action and marked dose-related analgesic effect. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989).

·        Tannins from bark, on i.v. administration, exhibited anti-inflammatory activity in carrageenin and dextran-induced rat paw oedema, cotton pellet granuloma test and adjuvant-induced arthritis in rats. Intravenous administration of tannins inhibited AcooH-induced writhing response in mice, antagonised permeability-increasing effect of inflammation mediators and inhibited leukocyte migration towards inflammation site. High doses of tannins, on oral administration, also had anti-inflammatory activity in carrageenin- induced rat paw oedema and adjuvant arthritis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989) .

·        Cardol showed pronounced antifilarial activity; hydroxyl groups and alkyl side chain were found to be necessary for activity. While carbamazine was active at 3,50 ppm (LC 100), cardol showed activity at low concentration of 3.5 ppm (LC 100). Cardol given orally to rats was tolerated upto 5.0 g/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994).

·        Anacardic acid, cardol and methyl cardol exhibited moderate cytotoxic activity against both BT -20 breast and HeLa epithelial cervix carcinoma cells. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994).

·        Anacardic acid and cardol exhibited characteristic competitive inhibition of oxidation of L-3, 4- dihydroxyphenylalanine by mushroom tyrosinase. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994) .

·        Fruits inhibited tyrosinase activity.( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994).

·        The protective effect of Anacardium occidentale aqueous extract against streptozotocin-induced diabetes was evaluated in rats. (Kamtchouing et al., 1998)

·        Anacardium occidentale bark 60% methanolic extract exhibited antimicrobial activity against 13 out of 15 bacterial isolates at a concentration of 20 mg/ml.(Akinpelu, 2001)  

·        Extracts of Anacardium occidentale (cashew) pollen in patients with allergic bronchial asthma. (Menezes et al., 2002)

·        A. occidentale extract possesses antiinflammatory activity, and thus lend pharmacological support to the folkloric use of the plant in the management and/or control of arthritis and other inflammatory conditions. (Ojewole, 2004).

·        Cashew nut shell oil has been reported to possess tumour promoting property. Therefore an attempt has been made to study the modulatory effect of cashew nut (Anlacardium occidentale) kernel oil on antioxidant potential in liver of Swiss albino mice and also to see whether it has tumour promoting ability like the shell oil. The animals were treated orally with two doses (50 and 100 microl/animal/day) of kernel oil of cashew nut for 10 days. The kernel oil was found to enhance the specific activities of SOD, catalase, GST, methylglyoxalase I and levels of GSH. These results suggested that cashew nut kernel oil had an ability to increase the antioxidant status of animals. The decreased level of lipid peroxidation supported this possibility. The tumour promoting property of the kernel oil was also examined and found that cashew nut kernel oil did not exhibit any solitary carcinogenic activity.(Singh et al., 2004). 

·        Intravenous administration of the hexane extract of the bark of Anacardium occidentale (cashew) in normal, healthy dogs produced a significant lowering of the blood glucose levels. Pursuit of the hypoglycaemic principle(s) in the hexane extract resulted in the isolation and characterization of two compounds, stigmast-4-en-3-ol (1) and stigmast-4-en-3-one (2). These compounds were purified by chromatographic methods and the structures were characterized by spectroscopic methods. Both compounds produced significant hypoglycaemic activity after intravenous administration at a dose of 1.3 mg/kg body weight. The bark of the cashew plant, A. occidentale, exhibited a hypoglycaemic effect probably due to the presence of these compounds. (Alexander et al., 2004)
