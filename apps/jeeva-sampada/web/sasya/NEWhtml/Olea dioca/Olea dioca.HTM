HABIT & HABITAT
It is a medium-sized mostly dioecious tree, usually in height but sometimes reaching to 30 m.,  found in  the eastern Himalayas, Duars, Assam and in Peninsula, chiefly in western ghats. along slopes of ghats, deciduous forests, evergreen forest, Forests, Ghats, Hedges, Hill forests, Midlands, along Rivers and Streams, moist deciduous forest, Plains, ravines, semi evergreen forest, Slopes and in tree farm.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Bark yellowish brown leaves very variable usually elliptic-lanceolate flowers in lax panicles, small, white; drupes ellipsoid, deep purple, 13-17 mm. long.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Bark, Leaves.

Uses
  1.  The leaves (nitrogen content, 1.14%) may be used as green manure
  2. The bark is said to be used as a februfuge in the Central provinces.
  3. Fruit can be eaten in curries and to be also pickled.
  4. Timber is extensively used by the inhabitants of Assam for various domestic purposes.

Other Uses
  1. The wood has a sweet scent when freshly cut and is, therefore, known as Rose Sandalwood in Bombay. The sapwood is reddish grey; heartwood dark yellowish brown, streaked and mottled, straight and close grained, hard, strong, elastic and heavy. The wood is used in Assam for domestic purposes ; it is suitable also for carving and cabinet work.
  2. The fruit is bitter to taste. 
  3. The bark is reported to be used as febrifuge; in Queens land, it is reputed to be an emetic.
