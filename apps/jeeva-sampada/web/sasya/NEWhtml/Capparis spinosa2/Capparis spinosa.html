HABIT & HABITAT:
Armed, diffused, prostrate shrub, 3-5 m tall; It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Branches terete, glabrous or pubescent. Stipules of 2-hooked thorns orange. Leaves variable, orbicular or ovate-obovate, 1-3 cm across, retuse or acute at apex, mucronate, rounded or cuneate at base. Flowers axillary or solitary, 2.5-5.0 mm long, white. Filaments purple. Gynophore 5-7 cm long. Fruits obovoid, 2.5-7.0 cm long, ribbed, red when ripe. Seeds many, globose, smooth, brown.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Solitary axillary corymb; Flower: Bracteate, complete, usually hermaphrodite, zygomorphic, tetramerous, hypogynous ; Calyx: Sepal subequal; Corolla: Petals 4, imbricate, white exceeding the sepals. ; Androecium: Stamens many, inserted at the base of a long gynophore , filaments filiform, anthers dithecous, introrse, dehiscing longitudinally.; Gynoecium: Ovary oblon or pubescent, gynophore present, bicarpellary, syncarpous, style short, stigma capitate, placentation parietal ; Fruit: Berry obovoid, ribbed red when ripe ; Seed: globose, smooth and brown .

Floral and seed biology:

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
In Unani system of medicine the bark is bitter. It is used as tonic, expectorant, analgesic. It is also used in rheumatism, paralysis, toothache, enlarged spleen and tubercular gland. The juice kills worms in the ear.


Chemistry:
Quercetin-3-rutinoside and kaempferol-3-rutinoside isolated from floral buds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Kaempferol-3-O-rhamnorutinoside isolated from floral buds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Leaves contained alkaloids (0.02), reducing sugars (1.68), fats (0.71), resins (2.2%), ascorbic acid (70.8 mg%), and tannins; fruit contained alkaloids (0.074), glycosides (0.083), reducing sugars (32.9), fats (3.75), resins (23.75), total titratable acid (14.1%), and ascorbic acid (135.5 mg%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A new flavanol glycoside-quercetin-7-O-b-D-glucopyranosido-b-L-rhamnopyranoside, m.p. 178°C – isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Capparenol 12,cappaprenol 13 and capparenol 14 isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Glucobrassicin, neoglucobrassicin and 4-methoxyglucobrassicin identified in roots by HPLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

In addition to rutin, quercetin 3-O-glucoside and quercetin 3-O-glucoside-7-O-rhamnoside, the methanolic extract of the aerial parts of Capparis spinosa yielded the new flavonoid quercetin 3-O-[6'"-alpha-L-rhamnosyl-6"-beta-D-glucosyl]-beta-D-glucoside(Sharaf et al, 2000).

Two new (6S)-hydroxy-3-oxo-alpha-ionol glucosides, together with corchoionoside C ((6S,9S)-roseoside) and a prenyl glucoside, were isolated from mature fruits of Capparis spinosa. The structures were established on the basis of spectroscopic, chiroptic and chemical evidence. In addition, the 13C-resonance of C-9 was found to be of particular diagnostic value in assigning the absolute configuration at that center in ionol glycosides. The alpha-ionol derivatives are metabolites of (+)-(S)-abscisic acid. (Calis et al, 2002).


Pharmacology:
Juice and volatile fraction from leaves showed anticystic, fungicidal, bactericidal and phytoncidal activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Cappaprenol 13 inhibited carrageenin-induced (44.0) and oxyphenbutazone-induced (67.0%) paw odema in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Aqueous extract of C. spinosa was found to be most active, while methanolic extract of C. intybus showed lowest antihepatotoxic activity against CCl4 and paracetamol intoxicated liver in rat. (Gadgoli and Mishra, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9603-1404.

p-Methoxy benzoic acid isolated from the methanolic soluble fraction of the aqueous extract of Capparis spinosa (Capparidaceae) was found to possess significant antihepatotoxic activity against carbontetrachloride and paracetamol induced hepatotoxicity in vivo and thioacetamide and galactosamine induced hepatotoxicity in isolated rat hepatocytes, using in vitro technique. HPTLC analysis of methanol soluble fraction indicated that the compound constitutes 33% w/w of the active fraction. (Gadgoli and Mishra, 1999).

 The antioxidant activity of extracts from Capparis spinosa buds was evaluated using different in vitro tests: ascorbate/Fe(2+)-mediated lipid peroxidation of microsomes from rat liver; bleaching of 1,1-diphenyl-2-picryl-hydrazyl radical; and autoxidation of Fe(2+) ion in the presence of bathophenanthroline disulfonate. The methanolic extract showed strong activities in all of these in vitro tests. The amount of total phenols was determined in the methanolic extract. (Germano et al, 2002)
