DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: 1-5 fascicled in the axil of leaves, pedicels hairy, bract ovate, acute, ciliate, brateoles minute, triangular, hermaphrodite, complete, regular; Calyx:  Clothed with white hairs, teeth lanceolate, longer than the campanulate tube ; Corolla: Pink or occasionally white, cuneate, produced into a long slender claw; Androceium: Vexillary stamen entirely free, the other 9 united, anthers uniform; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, ovules infinite, style incurved, stigma terminal, minute, capitate; Fruit: Pods with upper edge straight and lower indented, joints 3-5, as broad as long reticulately veined; Seed: 1;        .

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting time: October-December.     
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by   wind, birds or animals.  
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=22.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:

Indole-3-acetic acid, tyramine, N,N-dimethyltryptophan, hypaphorine, trigonelline, choline and betaine isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Four phenethylamines, five indole-3-alkylamines, stachydrine, trigonelline, choline and betaine isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

2’’-O-glucosylvitexin isolated together with (+)pinitol, apigenin, vitexin and isovitexin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Fucosterol, vitexin and 2-O-b-D-xylosylvitexin isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

24-ethylcholesta-5,22-dien-3b-ol, 24-ethylcholest-5-en-3b-ol and 24methylcholest-5-en-3b-ol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Desmodium triflorum roots were rich in crude protein and amino acids. (Chauhan and Rawat, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 144559x.


Pharmacology:
Leaves are used as remedy for diarrhoea, dysentery and convulsions; roots used for cough and asthma. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for abscess, eruptions (Leaf, Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984); Bodyache (Whole plant, Goel et al, 1984); Breast pain, colic (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Diarrhoea (Leaf, Singh and Pandey, 1980; Puri, 1983; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Dysentery (Leaf, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973; Singh and Pandey, 1980; Sharma et al, 1979); Menorrhagia, sore, spleen complaints (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Nail disease (Leaf, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1981); Swelling (Whole plant, Goel et al, 1984); Toothache (Leaf, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1982); Withlow (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Wounds (Leaf, Singh and Pandey, 1980; Sebastian and Bhandari, 1984; Rajwar, 1983)
