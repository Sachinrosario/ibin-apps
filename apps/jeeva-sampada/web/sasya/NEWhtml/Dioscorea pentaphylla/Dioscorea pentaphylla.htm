HABIT & HABITAT
A tall, slender, prickly climber with stems twining to the left, Dioscorea. pentaphylla is a native of tropical Asia extending eastwards to the remotest islands of the Pacific. It occurs throughout India scending to 5,500 ft. in the Himalayas and in Andaman Islands. About 16 varieties are recognised. Three well marked cultigens are distinguished based on tuber characters.
The first includes varieties with soft, esculent tubers borne close to the surface of the soil, the second with tubers deeply buried, and the third yielding hard tubers, nauseous and unpleasant to eat.
In general it is found along slopes of ghats, Damp forests, Decidious forest, deciduous forest, evergreen forest, Field hedges, Foot hills, Forests, Ghats, Hill forests, Hill slopes, mixed forests, moist deciduous forest, moist places, on forest trees, Open and Dense Forest, Plains, Sal forests, Scrub jungles, Secondary forest, semi evergreen forest, Shaded places amidst grass, tea gardens, Village jungles, Villages, waste fields, Wet deciduous to semi-evergreen forests and wet mixed forests.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  Stems are prickly and furrowed, rather hairy. Leaves alternate, 3-5 foliate. Bulbils globose or cylindrical, usually plentiful. Tubers almost invariably single; texture and shape variable; skin brown, yellow or purplish; flesh, firm in ovoid tubers covered with bristly roots and soft in elongated tubers with few roots; pale cream or lemon yellow with purple flecks.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Tubers, Flowers, Leaves.

Uses

  • The flowers are eaten as a vegetable and the tubers are edible.
  • As the species includes both posionous and innocuous forms, tubers should be consumed only after repeated boiling and washing.The nutritive, value of edible tubers is nearly the same as that of Dioscorea alata.
  • The leaves are eaten during famine and during times of scarcity.

Chemistry
Analysis of tubers of different races gave the following values (on dry matter basis) : albuminoids, 8.68-15.93; ash, 4.91-732; fat, 0.72-1.35; carbohydrates, 71.07-80.77; fibre, 2.22-7.96; and P2O5, 0.44-0.73%. The flowers are often collected 'and used as vegetable. Leaves are also eaten in times of scarcity. (Tubers are used to disperse swellings and as tonic).
