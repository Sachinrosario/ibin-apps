HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennials herbs with fibrous roots. It is found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Goa, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem reddish-yellow, dichotomously branched, erect, up to  1 m high with long, white, silky hairs. Lower leaves subsessile, obovate, 1.5-14.5 x 0.5-4.5 cm, apiculate or obtuse at apex, tapering at abse, irregularly spinulose-serrate along margin; upper sessile, elliptic ovate to oblanceolate, cordate at apex, clasping at base, spinulose serrate along margin. Heads axillary to terminal, solitary or few in cluster, 4.5-5.5 mm in diam. Involucral bracts linear, 1-7 mm long, acute, herbaceous, slightly longer than florets, margin scarious, ciliate. Floret numerous with usually abortive anthers in central florets, marginal florets female. Corolla of central florets yellow, tubular, 3-4 mm long, 5-lobed; tube with white hairs. Corolla of female florets 2.5-3.0 mm long, 2-3 lobed, pubescent. Achenes brown, minute, obscurely angled, sparsely pilose, shining. Pappus white, up to 3 mm long.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
 Inflorescence:  Heads on long slender silky hairy peduncles of dichotomous chymes ; Flower: Outer flower hermaphrodite, fertile; inner disk flower few fertile, involucre ovoid, campanulate, bracts clothed with white silky hairs, imbricate, narrow, acute, herbaceous or soft , the outer gradually smaller ;  Corolla: Corolla of outer flower filiform, minutely 2-3 toothed , those of inner flower regular, hairy, tubular, sender, with slightly enlarged 5-toothed limb ; Pappus 1-seriate, slender, caducous ; Androecium:  Stamens 5, inserted on the corolla and alternating with the segments, filaments usually free above, anther bases obtuse, entire ;Gynoecium: Ovary 1-celled, ovules solitary erect, style arms of pistillate flower truncate, usually penicillate ; tips often connate, in the sterile flower; Fruit: Minute, ellipsoid, smooth, brown; Seed: Erect. Flowering & Fruiting:  Febuarary. Flowers are pollinated by insect. Seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.
