DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herbs. Bulbs compound, large. Leaves subdistichous, fistular, 30-55 cm long, acute, shorter than the inflated scape. Scapes 0.4-1.0 m long, terete, hollow, rounded, with many flowered umbels at apex.

Inflorescence: Many flowered globular umbel; Flower: Greenish white, stellate, with or without bulbils, enclosed by thin membranous spathe, pedicles 4-5 times longer than that of flower, spathe composed of 2-3 reflexed valves; Perianth: 6 in number in whorls of three each, imbricate, white, segments ovate-oblong, acutish; Androecium: 6 arranged in two alternate whorls of three members each, always opposite to the tepals, filaments longer than the perianth, connate with each other and the perianth at its base, anthers dithecous, basifixed, introrse and opening by longitudinal slits; Gynoecium: tricarpellary, syncarpous, trilocular superior ovary with numerous ovules in two rows in each ovule,style with distinct stigma, a nectariferous disc is present below the ovary; Fruit: Capsule. 


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers greenish white, subtended by 2 or 3 reflexed bracts; pedicels upto 2 cm long, shorter than the flowers. Perianth segments linear-oblong, white. Capsules 0.25 cm in diam., membranous. It flowers and fruits between December to March.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and bulbs. Seeds are generally sown in nursery beds and 8-10 weeks old seedlings are transplanted in the field in December or early January. About 8-10 Kg seeds are required for transplanting seedlings in one hectare of land.

Soil type: It grows well in wide variety of soils while sandy loam are well suited.

Nutrient requirements: Finely powdered fish manure, sulphates of ammonia and potash and nitrate of soda and super phosphate are required.

Water management: Copious fortnightly irrigation of crop is highly recommended.                                       

Harvesting period / Method: Harvested when the bulbs are mature. Maturity of the plant is indicated by tops drooping just above the bulb while leaves are still green.

Kaempferal could only be detected in Kale (211 mg/kg) and endive (15-91 mg/kg), leeks (11-56 mg/kg) and turnip tops (31-64 mg/kg). In most fruits the quercetin content averaged 15 mg/kg, except for different apple varieties in which 21-72 mg/kg was found. The content of myricetin, lutcolin and apigenin was below the limit of detection (<1 mg/kg) except fresh broad beans (26 mg/kg myricetin) and sweet red pepper (13-31 mg/kg luteolin). Seasonal variability was low for most vegetables except for leafy vegetables, with the highest flavonoid levels in summer. Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The young plants are commonly used as vegetable. Bulbs are used as salad or for flavouring the dishes. Bulbs contain an acrid, volatile oil, which acts as a stimulent, diuretic and expectorent. Onions are occasionally used in fever, dropsy and catarrh and chronic bronchitis, in colic and scurvy. Onion juice rubbed on insect-bites is said to allay irritation.
