Name: Achyranthus aspera
VERNACULAR NAME: Naurivi, Prickly chaf flower
FAMILY: Amaranthaceae

Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A stiff erect herb, 30.5-91.4 cm in height.  commonly found as a weed of waysides and waste places throughout India, up to an altitude of 2,100m, and in the South Andaman Island. 

Availability: Plenty.


Conservation/ Threat Status : 

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
An erect or procumbent, annual or perennial herb, 1-2m in height, often with a woody base, Branches spreading, with the upper ones thick. Leaves opposite, velvety-tomentose, rigid, 3.8-12.7 cm long and 5.1-7.6 cm wide. Stems angular, ribbed, simple or branched from the base, often tinged with reddish purple color; leaves thick, ovate-elliptic or obovate-rounded, but variable in shape and size; flowers greenish white, numerous in axillary or terminal spikes up to 75 cm long; seeds sub-cylindric, truncate at the apex, rounded at the base reddish brown. 

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers bisexual, greenish-white borne in long spikes, inverted. Stamens with interposed staminodes; anthers 2-celled.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
To control leaf spot disease, leaf roller and ear head bug in paddy.

Parts Used: Seeds, Root: 1 kg of root and 1 kg of bark powder (vela tree bark) to make powder for 1 ha crop. Different parts of the plant including seeds.


Harvesting Practices: Collect fully developed roots.

Produce and Products:


Chemistry: 
Sodium arsenate, Phosphorus, Meithionine, 2,2cystine, Histidine, 4,9 lysine, Threonine, 2,3 Phenylanine, 6,2 leucine, 8,6 Isoleucine, 5,6 tryptophan, and valine.

Processing Techniques: Powder the dried roots and mix it with vela tree bark powder.


Other uses:
Alcoholic and aqueous extracts of the leaves showed antibiotic action against Micrococcus pyogens var. aureus and Escherichia coli.


STATUS: Wild.

Miscellaneous Information

 The roots are astringent; their paste is applied to clear opacity of cornea, and to wounds as an haemostatic; it is reported to be useful in cancer. A decoction of the roots is used for stomach troubles, and an aqueous extract for stones in the bladder. 

The leaves are used as a cure for gonorrhea, and excessive perspiration; their extract, called ‘Achyrol’ for leprosy, and the heated sap for tetanus. 
The leaves made in a paste with water are applied to bites of poisonous insects, wasps, bees, etc. Powdered root, mixed with honey, is given internally in hemorrhoids. Decoction of the root is prescribed in diarrhea. Root paste is given to stop bleeding after abortion.
