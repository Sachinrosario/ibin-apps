HABIT & HABITAT
Bulbous herbs, found in Western Temperate Himalaya from Kumaon to Kashmir,  2600-4000 m.WEST TIBET.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Bulbous herbs. Bulbs small, globose; scales membranous. Stem erects, 15-55 cm high, unbranched, naked below. Leaves 3-6 in a whorl or opposite, linear-lanceolate, 4.5-12.0 x o.3-1.2 cm, lowest ones sometimes 2.5 – 3.5 cm broad, tips of the upper leaves often linear and naked.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers  terminal, solitary or 2-4 in a short racemes nodding. Perianth campanulate, 2.5-3.5 cm long,;  segments 6, yellow-green, each with a large gland at the base. Capsules obovate, 1.2-1.6 mm long, bluntly 6 angled. Seeds many, small, flattened, slightly winged. It flower and fruits between March and July.
CIMAP:
   Inflorescence: Racemes; Flower: Regular, yellowish green, usually checkered, bell shaped, nodding; Corolla: Petals narrow-ovate, with a nectary at the base ;Androecium:  Stamens 6; Gynoecium: Ovary superior, 3-chambered, with a simple style, ovules numerous; Fruit: Capsule ; 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : June -July.  
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=24.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Bulbs are powered and boiled with dried orange skin and administered for Tuberculosis and asthma.


Chemistry:  

An alkaloid - kashmirine - isolated and its crystal structure determined and found identical with that of imperialine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Pharmacology:

 The non-toxic cigarette is manufactured by extracting raw material for medicinal compound with H2O twice under boiling, precipitating with ethanol filtering and spraying the filtrate in the smoking core taking Fritillaria roylei and other medicinal plants. (Yan, 2000).


Ethnobotany: 

Medicine for asthma, bronchitis (Bulb, Gaur et al, 1983); Burns, stomach disease (Bulb, Koul, 1941); Tonic (Bulb, Uniyal, 1968); Edible (Bulb, Koul, 1941)
