Seed Biology :

Seed Germination:
The seed requires about 6 months to germinate and their germination percentage is very poor.  In an experiment:  highest germination percentage of 72.0 was obtained with BA (Benzyl adenine ,growth regulator) at 150ppm.

Cultivation:
Brusera is propagated mainly by 1 meter long cuttings.In order to maintain economic characters only vegetatively propogative material must be used for planting.The best season for rising the cuttiings is during Feb and March.The first sprout appear in above 30-40 days and healthy root system will develop in about 4-6 months.Rooted cuttings are planted during rainy season Jul.-Aug in pits of 60 cm cube at a spacing of 6 m ether way are dug..The pits are filled with the compost and top soil.Hand watering is done at regular intervals.Since Bussera is dioecious plant for every  8 female plants one male plant has to be planted to meet the pollination requirements.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Husk, Fruits.

Uses: 

  • The oil obtained from fruits , used for flavouring food.

Harvesting Practices
In India it occupies an area of 800 acres mostly localized in Karnataka, with an annual average yield of 20 tonnes of dried husks. The production of oil is expected as 300kg worth  over 15-20 lakh rupees.

Produce and Products : 
Essential oil: as base for numerous perfumes, cosmetics ,soap industry and also as flavorings of food and beverages.

Back to Top

Chemistry
The major constituent of bursera oil is linalol (60-70%) with other compunds like, linalyl acetate, gereniol, alpha terpenol, methyl heptenol,  linalol monoxide  etc,, in smaller qunatities. 

Back to Top

Processing Techniques: 

It is the dried husk obtained annually and heartwood obtained after 50-60 years which are used in the extraction of essential oil. The characteristics sweet odour of linaloe oil blends well, and hence used as a fixative for costly essential oils. like rose oil, jasmine oil, tuberose oil, rosemary oil, liliac oil and most other floral bouquets.
