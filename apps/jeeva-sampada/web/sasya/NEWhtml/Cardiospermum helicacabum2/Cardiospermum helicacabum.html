HABIT & HABITAT
Annual or perennial, tendrillar, climbing herbs; and occurs throughout the country in tropical and subtropical regions, mostly amidst hedges, grasses or as undergrowth in forests. Srilanka,Malacca.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual or perennial, tendrillar, climbing herbs; stems and branches slender with longitudinal grooves, pubescent or glabrous. Leaves biternate; uppermost often 3-foliate; leaflets lanceolate-oblong to ovate, 1-7 x 0.5-2.5 (-3) cm, acute at apex, narrowed at base, serrate to pinnatilobed, glabrous except hairy nerves beneath; petiole 0.5-5.0 cm long.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers very small, white in umbellate cymes; cymes resting on 2 opposite circinate tendrils; pedicels 0.2-0.3 cm long. Sepals suborbicular; outer 2 rounded-obovate, 0.15 x 0.12 cm and inner 2 rounded, 0.3 x 0.2 cm, thin. Petals obovate-spathulate, retuse, 0.3 cm long with pubescent scales at base. Stamens 8. Capsules on short stalks, subglobose or broadly pyriform, 1.2-3.0 cm across, trigonous, angles winged. Seeds globose, black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Roots, leaves and seeds are used in earache, eyesore, lungs diseases, rheumatism and stiffness of limbs. Leaves are used in anasarca, reduce bulkyness and wounds. Roots are also useful against dizziness.
Chemistry:

·         Fatty acid content of seed oil 19.5 – 23.5%. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·         (+)pinitol, 7-O-glucuronides of apigenin, chrysoeriol and luteolin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·         An efficient and enantiodivergent synthesis of (+) and (-)pinitols. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

·         Arachidic acid, apigenin and b-sitosterol-b-D-galactoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

·         The lipidic fraction of both the cortical and inner part of the seeds of Cardiospermum halicacabum was analysed by TLC and GC-MS spectrometry. Fractions studied show a different chemical composition, besides which, the fraction rich in triterpenoids shows an anti-inflammatory activity. (Ferrar et al, 1996). MAPA 9703-1491.

·         Two crystalline compounds b-sitosterol-b-D-glucoside and stigmasterol-b-D-glucoside were isolated. (Srinivasan et al., 1998). MAPA 9902-1289.

Pharmacology:

·        Alkaloid fraction from seeds showed in vitro antibacterial action against some pathogenic organisms, caused transient hypotension and cardiac inhibition in anaesthetised dogs. It blocked spasmogenic effects of acetylcholine, histamine and 5-hydroxytryptamine on guinea pig ileum and dog tracheal chain. It had biphasic effect on frog rectus abdominis muscle. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·        Alcoholic extract of leaves produced cholinergic and antihistaminic effects on CVS and smooth muscles and showed significant anti-inflammatory activity in rats. It also produced CNS depression in near lethal doses and analgesic effect in mice and rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·        Plant extract stabilized lysosomal membrane and prevented enzyme leakage. The drug may prevent cellular and extracellular injuries by stabilizing lysosomal membrane. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Alcoholic and aqueous extracts in vivo prevented cellular and extracellular injuries by stabilizing lysosomal membrane and prevented enzyme leakage. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

·        An ethanol extract (95% v/v) from the leaves and stems of Cardiospermum helicacabum when applied at a concentration of 250mg/10 ml concomitantly with croton oil (50mg/10ml) to the ear of mice inhibited the ongoing inflammatory oedema after the successive fractionation of the crude extract with different solvents, the heptane fraction displayed a strong antiphlogistic activity (ED50=232 mg/ear). The heptane fraction did not significantly inhibit the inflammatory response induced by arachidonic acid or capsaicin. (Koch et al., 1996/97). MAPA 9803-1535.

·        The anti-inflammatory activity of Cassia occidentalis leaf powder and an ethanol extract of Cardiospermum halicacabum aerial parts were assayed in male albino rats using carrageenan-induced rat paw edema. C. occidentalis was maximally active at a dose of 2000 mg/kg, while the C. halicacabum extract was maximally effective at a dose of 500 mg/kg. In the cotton pellet granuloma assay, these drugs were able to suppress the transudative, exudative and proliferative components of chronic inflammation. Further, these drugs were able to lower the lipid peroxide content and gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase and phospholipase A2 activity in the exudate of cotton pellet granuloma. The increased alkaline phosphatase activity and decreased A/G ratio of plasma in cotton pellet granulomatous rats were normalized after treatment with these drugs. C. occidentalis powder and C. halicacabum extract were able to stabilize the human erythrocyte membrane against hypotonicity-induced lysis. It is likely that these drugs may exert their anti-inflammatory activity by inhibition of phospholipase A2, resulting in the reduced availability of arachidonic acid, a precursor of prostaglandin biosynthesis, and/or by stabilization of the lysosomal membrane system. (Sadique et al., 1997)

·        The in vitro evaluation of various solvent extracts of Cardiospermum halicacabum leaves against a plant pathogenic and human pathogenic bacteria revealed potent microbial activity. Except ethyl acetate extract other extracts showed no inhibitory activity. Partially purified substance was found to be phenolic in nature. (Raman et al, 1998). MAPA 9802-0938.

·        Pharmacognosy, phytochemistry and pharmacological activities of Cardiospermum helicacabum seed have been reviewed. (Latif et al., 1998). MAPA 9901-0706.

·        The ethanol as well as n-hexane extracts (400 mg/kg) of the whole plant of Cardiospermum halicacabum powder showed potent antipyretic activity. The water extract was devoid of significant activity. The antipyretic activity of the ethanol extract was concentration dependent. (Asha and Pushpangadan, 1999). MAPA 9904-2336.

·        The in vitro effects of ethanol and aqueous extracts of the medicinal plant Cardiospermum halicacabum on adult worms and microfilariae of Brugia pahangi were investigated. With or without the plant extracts in culture medium, the motility of adult worms, microfilariae and microfilarial release from female worms were monitored daily. After 7 days of culture, viability or tissue damage of adult worms was assessed using the MTT assay. At > 500 microg ml-1, the aqueous extract significantly reduced motility of adult females after 24 h of exposure and adult males after 3 days. The aqueous extract, at > 500 microg ml-1, also significantly reduced microfilarial release from female worms, starting on day 2. The reduction in the motility of adult worms and the pattern of microfilarial release from female worms were concentration and time dependent. The MTT assay results revealed that adult worms cultured in the presence of aqueous extracts at > 500 microg ml-1 were damaged. However, the aqueous extract did not affect the motility of microfilariae with the exception of those in higher concentration extracts. Higher concentrations of ethanol extracts (2 mg ml-1) inhibited both the motility of adult worms and the release of microfilariae from females. Little effect of ethanol extracts was detected by the MTT assay, as only slight damage was caused to worms exposed only to the highest concentration (2 mg ml-1). However, ethanol extract at 500 microg ml-1 rapidly reduced the motility of microfilariae on day 2. The result suggested that an aqueous extract of C. halicacabum has mild but definite direct macrofilaricidal action on B. pahangi. (Khunkitti et al., 2000).
