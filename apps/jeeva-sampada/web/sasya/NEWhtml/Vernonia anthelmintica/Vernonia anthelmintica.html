Chemistry:

·        7, (Z)24(28)-stigmastadienol (fig. 1), stigmasterol, 5-stigmasten-3b-ol and 7,22-stigmastadienol isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·        Stigmastatrienol acetate (II) (fig. 3) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·        A new elemanolide – vernodalol (fig. 2), mp. 133° - isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

·        Amino acid composition of seeds determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

·        Two new flavan glucosides – vernovan (fig. 4a) and p-hydroxybenzoylvernovan (fig. 4b) – isolated from seeds and characterized; in addition, vernodalin, vernodalol, butein, 3,4,2’,4’,5’-pentahydroxy-6’-methoxy-2-methylchalcone, b-amyrin, daucosterol, vernolic acid and stigmasterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        The plant has been characterized for alkaloids, glycosides, saponins, cardiac glycoside anthraquinones and flavanoids etc. (Khokhar et al., 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 97376s.

·        The seed oil of Vernonia anthelmintica on reaction with dials (mono-di, tri or tetra ethylene g lgeals) in the prescence of boron trifluoride etherate, followed by saponification and esterification (methanol/H+), gives the digoethylene glycol ethers: methyl 12(13)-hydroxy-13(12) [2-hydroxy-ethyl-1-oxy]-octadec-9-enoate methyl 12(13) hydroxyl-13(12)-[5-hydroxy-3-oxapentyl-1-oxy]-octadec-9-enoate; methyl 12(13)-hydroxy-13(12)-[11-hydroxy methyl 12(13)-hydroxy-13(12)-[11-hydroxy-3,6,9-trixaundecyl-1-oxy]-octadec-9-enoate. Methyl 12, 13-dihydroxy octadec-9-enoate is a co-product in all reactions. (Singh  Sukhprit, 1997).

Pharmacology:

·        Alcoholic extract showed anthelmintic activity on earthworm and tapeworm. It caused relaxation of smooth muscle in isolated guinea pig ileum and had mild laxative action in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

 

Ethnobotany:

·        Medicine for acidity (Seed, Chaudhuri and Pal, 1976); Antihelminthic (Fruit, Shah, 1984; Srivastava and Varma, 1981; Seed, Rajwar, 1983; Joshi et al, 1980); Amtimetic (Leaf, Gupta, 1981); Asthma (Seed, Bedi, 1978); Cholera (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Constipation (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970; Whole plant, Goel and Mudgal, 1988); Cough (Seed, Singh and Pandey, 1980); Pain (Root, Goel and Mudgal, 1988); Paralysis (Fruit, Shah, 1984); Skin disease (Seed, Singh and Pandey, 1980); Swelling (Fruit, Shah, 1984); Syphilis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Urinary complaints (Whole plant, Goel and Mudgal, 1988; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Vermifuge (Fruit, Shah, 1984)

·        Fort-four plants used for twenty-three common diseases have been studied and presented along with their tribal names, locality, ethno-pharmacognistic preparation and dosage. Diseases like dysentery, fever, lumbago, stomachache, rheumatism, snakebite, gastritis are always treated ethno-medicinally. Vernonia anthelmintica is considered to be of high medicinal value even by cultured tribes and are regularly prescribed by tribal herbalists. (Mukherjee, S. K., et. al.; 2000).
