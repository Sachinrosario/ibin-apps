HABIT & HABITAT

Native to Indo-Malaysian region, distributed throughout the tropics, found in wild state in South India and Assam.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

- Handsome evergreen tree, C20m in height.
- Shoots and young leaves stellately puberulous or tomentose, leaves long-petioled, polymorphous, ovate to lanceolate or broadly rhomboid and obtusely acutely 3-7 lobed.
- Flowers white, shortly pedicled in tomentose cymes.
- Fruits 4-5 cm in diam. Sub-globose, fleshy, smooth, olive-coloured, containing one or two large, hard seeds or nuts with a furrowed testa.

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
No serious pests.
Platypus latifinis Walker (beetle) bores newly felled logs and Ayleborus interjectus Blandford (beetle) - bores into trunk of dying trees.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood

Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:
Wood - for packing cases, match sticks, raw material for pulp.
Kernels - oil (lumbang oil or candlenut oil or Indian walnut oil) oil used in paint and varnish industry; soap making, for painting boats and crafts (in Philippines & Java); in medicine, oil is used as a purgative, applied externally in rheumatism and ulcers; used for culinary purposes, as an illuminant and as a hair tonic; oil cake as manure.
Leaves - leaves or young twigs for mulching in Sri Lanka; applied in case of acute rheumatism; black dye is prepared (in Fiji).
Roots - brown dye.
Fruit - tonic, useful in diseases of heart and blood is carminative and expectorant used in piles, hydrophobia and ring worm. Bark and flowers - asthma, sores, ulcers, tumours and as laxative.
