DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Prostrate, much branched herb with small woody rootstock, stem more than 30 cm long, spreading, wiry, clothed with long spreading hairs, sometimes glabrous. Leaves numerous, oblong-elliptic or lanceolate, 1.0-3.5 x 0.3-1.0 cm, obtuse at apex, densely covered with appressed silky hairs; petioles short. Flowers light-blue, solitary, axillary; bracts lanceolate. Sepals 4, lanceolate, densely silky. Capsules globose, 3-4 mm long, 4-valved. Seeds usually 4, glabrous.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: Small, 1-3 together, bracts hairy, pedicellate; Calyx: Sepals unequal, unaltered in fruit; Corolla: Corolla funnel shaped, limb 5, sub-entire ;Androecium: Stamens included, filaments filiform; Gynoecium: Ovary 2 celled, 4-ovuled, styles 2, separate from the base, stigma linear ; Fruit: Capsule globose, 4-valved ; Seed: glabrous. Flowering & Fruiting : July-November. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine the plant is bitter and pungent. It is used as tonic and anthelmintic. It is also used in the treatment of bronchitis, biliousness, epilepsy and leucoderma. Leaves are used in asthma and bronchitis.
Chemistry:
Betaine and a base, mp. 60°C, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Pharmacology:
Effective nootropic agent was isolated from Evolvulus alsinoides. (Kulkarni and Verma, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9605-2799.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for aphrodisiac (Root, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); Asthma (Leaf, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Whole plant, Singh and Pandey, 1980; Whole plant, Jayabharti, 1998); Bronchitis (Leaf, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985); Dysentery (Whole plant, Sharma et al, 1975; Whole plant, Jayabharti, 1998); Fever (Root, Lal and Yadav, 1983); Leucorrhoea, spermatorrhoea (Whole plant, Maheshwari and Singh, 1987); Skin disease (Whole plant, Jayabharti, 1998; Singh and Prakash, 1994); Inflammation, piles, Malarial fever, Jaundice (Whole plant, Jayabharti, 1998); Scorpion sting (Whole plant, Jain and De, 1966); Stomachache (Whole plant, Maheshwari and Singh, 1987); Ulcers (Leaf, Bhatt and Sabnis, 1987); Whitlow (Leaf, Saxena, 1986); Symbolic in Santal religious ceremonies (Whole plant, Goel et al, 1984) .
