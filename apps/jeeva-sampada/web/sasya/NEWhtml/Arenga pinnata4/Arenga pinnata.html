HABIT & HABITAT
A large palm, this palm has a trunk, 20-40' high with long ascending pinnatisect (3m long) leaves forming a diffuse crown. The trunk is covered in a black fibrous hessian like material, while the leaf bases have long spines protecting them. The tree reaches maturity in about 6-10 years. The sugar palm is distributed mainly in the tropical Asian rainforest. A. pinnata, though rare in India, is common, in Malaya, where people cultivate it in the neighbour- hood of their houses and villages. It is found wild in parts of Assam, Burma and south China. (6,8)

Distribution: Assam, Martaban, Tenasserim, and also commonly cultivated in the India-Malay Peninsula and Archipelago. (24) Assam, Pegu, Burma and Malay Peninsula and also East Asia and the Malaya. DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Pinnate leaf, 20 to 25 leaves, very dark green top, silvery bottom, erect, up to 12 meters long. Petiole very stout, channeled at the base, sprinked with blakish scurf. Pinnules generally fasiculate, four or five farious, the midle ones five feet long, four or four and half inches broad, linear-ensiform, coriaceous, dark green above, underneath white, margins with distant spinescent teeth, towards the appex becoming more frequent; apex itself bilobed or bifid, eroso-dentate; base with one, or generally two unequal auricles of which the lowermost is much longer. REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY

The pendulous, solitary inflorescence, 3-4' long, appears first on the topmost leaf-axil and later spreads downwards. Male and female flowers occur generally on separate peduncles; but rarely together. Showy yellow flowers. As most of the Caryotas and Arengas, after the tree finishes blooming, it dies. Flower stalk coming from among the leaves, much longer than the leaves. (1,2,3,4)

Male spadix four or five feet long. Spikes attentuate at the apex, and they are furnished with a few rudimentary or obortive flowers. Flowers very numerous, ablong club shaped, with a rich purple black colour, and a disagreeable smell, of considerable size, often an inch long. Sepals three, rounded, broad. Petals nearly three times longer, oblong, ovate. Stamens; filaments short and slender, anthers nearly as long as the petals, aristato-mucronate.

Female-flowers solitary, large, about an inch across. Sepals three, very broad. Petals three, cordate-ovate, coriaceous. No sterile stamens. Ovarium shrtly obturminate, thrre celled, apex three-lobed, concave in the center. When arise three tooth-shaped, triangular, erect stigmata; substance thick, fleshy. Below the back of the lobes which are opposite to the sepals, runs a slight keel. Ovula erect. (1,2,3,4). It flowers about the tenth year. (18)

Phenology :The palm reaches a height of thirty to fourty feet. Crown oblong, very dense of a sombre aspect. The fruits are obovoid, globose, 2-3 seeded with uniformly thick semi-pulpy kernel. (1,4)

Cultivation:

Taking care of Arenga pinnata trees does not need much work and cost. This trees sprouted naturally from the seeds. According to the rural farmers their spread were indirectly conducted by the civets which eat the Arenga pinnata fruits, digested them and discharged them in some places not far from their main trees. That is why commonly the Arenga pinnata trees are grown in cluster within certain area either a forest or a dry land. These clusters of Arenga pinnata trees are seldom approached by the villagers they try to avoid as far as possible being injured by their thorn which can cause infected wounds, and can lead to death. (1,9)

Economic Potential and Uses

Widely grown in the tropics and is used to produce sugar, sago, and alcoholic drinks. (14,10)

Parts used and Uses

The most economical valued product of Arenga pinnata is infact brown sugar or Jaggery/gur obtained from the sap coming from the male floral axis (it has different taste and quality when we compare it with palm Sugar). (1,14)

From the selected fresh Flower Essence of Arenga Palm tree comes various products of Arenga Palm Sugar ( Arenga Palm Flower Sugar, Arenga Sugar, Palm Suicker ) that has exotic Taste and Aroma. (7)


Harvesting Practices

During flowering starch from the stem is transformed into sugar and is transported to the growing axis of the inflorescence. When the floral, axis emerges from the spathe, after about 70 days, the male spadix is chosen for tapping. Female spadices do not yield any sugar. Tapping is carried out by beating the male floral axis with a wooden rod for 3-4 days to ensure the flow of sap into the wounded part. A small notch is then made in the peduncle by chipping it. The exudate is collected like nira, coconut palm, in earthen pots previously heated and smoked, or smeared with chunam (slaked lime) to sterilize them, and removed every 6 hours. The syrup is collected in this manner, day after day for nearly two months or more, removing fresh chips from the same peduncle. Once flowered, the tree goes on producing spadice for 2-3 years till the lower-most axil of the leaf is utilized the tree is exhausted, On an average each tree yields about 3/4 of a gallon of juice per day. (1,14)

Processing Techniques

The syrupy liquid exuded from the peduncle is rich in sugar and is easily fermented. The unfermented syrup is boiled and cooled to form jaggery The fermented liquid is used as toddy, and the over fermented juice yield sirka or vinegar. (1)

GUR Production:

The harvest is usually done about 3 times a day. The farmer taps the male floral axis , by connecting a large bamboo tube (of 20 cm in diameter and 1 m long) on it, to catch its dripped sweet sap, and let the bamboo tube hanging. It is important to note, that after the sap has been tapped, it is necessary to hammer the whole body of the stalk from where the sap has been tapped with the back of the knife (this hammering act done as long as 15 minutes in order to make the sweet sap of it easily drip out. One tapping gives the farmer approximately 2 litre of sweet sap. The tapping and hammering should continue, especially if the production of the sap decreases.

When the production of sweet sap nearly comes to its end usually the tree produces a new male floral axis. If this new male floral axis arise or protrude, the farmer shifts the tapping activity from the old male floral axis to the new one, and so on, until the production totally comes to its end, which takes about 2 - 3 years since the first tapping of male floral axis. If the tree has only one male floral axis during a year, it means that the harvesting cannot go on.

Primarily, the sap must boil for an instance (to avoid that it will become sour) it can not directly be processed. The sap is collected to harvest so that after the processing it produces an adequate quantity of brown sugar. The production of sweet sap from those 3 times of harvesting in a day, taken from a single Arenga pinnata tree usually reach about 6 litre. The sweet sap for one day is processed to brown sugar by boiling it continuously up to 6 hours. Some times the liquid coagulate, then it must be stirred regularly in order to become well mixed and to avoid burning. Soon after coagulating, the Arenga pinnata sap become brownish and good enough to become brown sugar. Then it is put it into coconut shell moulds. A few hours afterwards the coagulated Arenga pinnata sap become cool and hard to form Jaggery/gur (brown sugar). The last stage of this brown sugar making process is to wrap the already moulded brown sugar with Arenga pinnata or palm leaves.


The sugar can also be produced in a similar manner as done for the other palms.


CRYSTAL SUGAR MAKING:

The initial process of sugar making is practically the same as that of gur. In this case also, juice is delimed, filtered and boiled. The juice when it reaches 110°C temperature is poured in the crystalliser.

Now the mass is stirred, twice or thrice in an hour, for about 3 to 4 hours. On cooling, crystals begin to form. After 24 hours, the mass is removed and poured in small quantities in the cage of the Centrifugal machine which when rotated separates the sugar from the rab. On working the machine with the handle, the cage rotates with a speed of 2,000 revolutions per minute and expels the molasses through the perforated side walls of the cage, fitted with copper gauge filter depositing the sugar crystals on the inner walls of the copper or brass gauge. In order to wash the sugar, water is sprayed on the sugar while centrifuging. This water cleans the sugar and passes through the cage flow, through a spout, down to the bucket kept below. The sugar deposited on the inner walls of the copper gauge is removed and the cage is fed with rab for the next charge.


The quality of sugar obtained by this method is slightly brown in colour and not or well defined crystalline structure. Various experiments are being conducted to manufacture white regular crystalline variety of sugar from Palm juice with the help of small vacuum pans. In the case of vacuum, boiling, the caramalisation of sugar is much reduced as it boils at a low temperature. The size and shape of the sugar crystals can also be better controlled in a vacuum pan.


The various Sugar Products specially made of Essence of Arenga Pinnata’s Flower are not ordinary products of its kind, but the Sugar that has an excellent Taste and Aroma. It is specially produced from the boiled selected fresh Arenga Palm Flower’s Essence Cream. The boiled liquid is then crystallized to form powder. This kind of Sugar is produced through hygienic process without any additives, preservative, chemical substances or other sweeteners. Only a limited quantity of Sugar can be produced from the high grade of boiled selected fresh Palm Cream. From the whole fresh selected Palm Cream used, only 4 - 6 % of them produce the various sugar products as mentioned above. Therefore, the product has great quality with excellent and typical taste. Other Uses

The young leaf can be used to roll a pinch of tobacco.

The leafs ribs can be used as broom for cleaning the house and garden, etc.

The full length of the trunk of an Arenga pinnata tree is completely covered by black fiber. This black fiber has several uses as material for broom, brush (to clean floor, car, etc.), paint brush, septic tank base filter; clear water filter, door mat, carpet, rope, chair/sofa cushion, and for fish nest to hatch its eggs.

The palm Arenga pinnata which consists of very soft brownish fiber which stick on the trunk. This part of the tree can be used as a flammable material.

The Trunk of a mature Arenga pinnata tree has an average diameter of 60 cm. Not all Arenga pinnata could produce sweet sap from their Protruding stalk. Those which are unable to produce sweet sap usually contain more starch material in their trunk heart, as compared to the productive ones. This starch material could be processed into Arenga pinnata starch flour, which further on can be used for various purposes such as foods and cakes, etc. (1,)

Medicinal uses:

The roots is considered pectoral in Cambodia, and administered in Bronchitis. It is also said to be stomachic. The plant is often used as a substitute for Borassus flabellifer.

The juice of the fleshy outer covering of the fruit is highly stimulating and corrosive. If applied to the skin it causes great pain and inflammation. It is used by the Malays to Poison their enemies. (24)
