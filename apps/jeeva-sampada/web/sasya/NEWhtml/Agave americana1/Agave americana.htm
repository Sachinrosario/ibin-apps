SCIENTIFIC NAME: Agave americana

VERNACULAR NAME: Century plant, American Aloe.

LOCAL NAMES: Bara kanwar, nam kanta, rakas-pattah.

Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A stemless plant, native to America, introduced as an ornamental and now naturalized throughout India, often met with as an escape in waste places. Leaves erecto-patent with strong widely patent or recurved marginal spines, glaucous, often with longitudinal, white or yellow streaks or bands; perianth funnel-shaped; capsules oblong clavate, beaked.

Native of S.America, grown in garden for ornamentation Short stemmed half-woody plant, bearing a rosette of long, erect, pointed, fleshy leaves. Planted along railway embankments and roadsides, and suitable for hedging, fencing and checking soil erosion. Also useful for seaside landscaping

A shout, shrubby plant; trunk short, bearing a rosette of leaves. Leaves lanceolate, 60-80 in long, 6-8 in broad, smoot, grey, ascending without curved ends, with a terminal, rather short, stout, recurved, round-grooved spines, obliquely flattened at base and moderately large, grey prickles on prominent, marginal elevations. Inflorescence 20-30 ft. Long, rather slender; flowers 2.5-2.75 in long.

Availability :

Plenty, A native of America, but naturalized throughout India and Pakistan.

Miscellaneous Information

The plant is used as fish poison, and the core of the plant as febrifuge in malaria and other fevers; it is used as an antiseptic and also inascites, venereal sores, and dysentery.
