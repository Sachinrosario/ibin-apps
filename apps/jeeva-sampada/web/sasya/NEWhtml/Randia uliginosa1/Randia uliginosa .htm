DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A deciduous armed tree with quadrangular branches, up to 7.5 m. in height and 1.2 m. in girth, found nearly throughout India, up to 1,000 m. in the hills. Bark reddish brown, scaly ; leaves obovate or obovate-oblong; flowers dimorphic, white, and fragrant ; berry ellipsoid or ovoid, 5-6 cm. long, yellowish brown, crowned with persistent calyx; seeds about12. compressed, smooth, closely packed in pulp. The plant is common in the sub-Himalayan tracts from the Yamuna eastwards and in eastern, central and southern India. It freely produces root suckers, and is hardy against frost and drought. The rate of growth is moderate, with a mean annual girth increment of 14-28 mm. Several defoliators and borers attack this tree. The wood is whitish gray or light-brown, close-grained, hard and fairly heavy (wt., 720-833 kg./cu.m.) and without heartwood. It is not used for any special purpose, but is one of the possible box-wood substitutes : it is suitable for turning small articles.

Uses

  1. The leaves are boiled and eaten. They are used as fodder for deer and cattle. 
  2. Flowers yield an essential oil similar to Gardenia oil, but the perfume is not commercially prepared.

Chemistry
Destructive distillation of wood gave (on dry wt. basis): charcoal, 30.4 ; tar, 9.6 ; pyroligneous acid,39.7 (acid, 5.0 ; ester, 3.7 ; acetone, 2.7 : methanol,1.4) : pitch and losses, 1.7 ; and gas 18.5%.  The fruits ripen in February-March and are eaten boiled or roasted, either alone or in curries.  Analysis of the edible matter gave the following values: moisture, 81.7: protein, 1.0; fat, 0.2 : fibre,3.9; other carbohydrates, 12.5; and mineral matter,0.7 g. : calcium 33 mg. : phosphorus, 13 mg. ; and calorific value, 56 cal./100 g.

Other Uses
  1. The unripe fruit is astringent. The roasted pulp is used as a remedy in diarrohea and dysentery, especially during pregnancy. The unripe fruit is employed as fish-poison. It is used in dyeing as a colour intensifier. The fruits, like those of R. spinosa, contain a saponin of oleanolic acid, which appears to be responsible for their toxicity to fish. They cotain alsocontain mannitol and leucocyanidin.
  2. The roots are considered to possess cooling, diuretic, and tonic properties. They are employed in biliousness and boils in children and in diarrohea and dysentery.
