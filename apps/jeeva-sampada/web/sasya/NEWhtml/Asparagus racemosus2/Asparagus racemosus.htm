Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Climbing shrub growing at foothills to 1400(1800) m: on exposed slopes, on open rocks. It is distributed in Tropical Africa, Australia, Ceylon, Pakistan (Sind), India (throughout tropical and subtropical regions).

Availability :

Almost throughout India.
Conservation/ Threat Status : Common in wild as well as cultivated for medicinal purpose.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An extensive scandent, much branched, spinous, Undershrubs. Roots tuberous, many in large fascicles, each 1-2 dm long. Main stem terete, glabrous, green; branches angular. Leaves scaly membranous, 4-6 mm long on the main stem, smaller on the branches, with a stout, conical, spinous straight or slightly curved, 2-7 mm longspur at back of the leaves. Cladodes 14-25 mm long, falcate, slender, triquetrous, spinous pointed, 2-6 in a cluster. Flowers white, in simple racemes, the latter 3-5 cm long, produced by 1-2, accessory buds on the main stem; pedicels 3 mm long, filiform jointed above the middle 1-3 together in the axil of a membranous, 1.5 mm long, white, ovate-acute scale. Perianth segments 3.5 x 1.5 mm, white, ovate-oblong, obtuse at the apex, spreading. Stamens epiphyllous and opposite to and smaller than perianth lobes; anthers versatile red. Ovary 3-lobed; stigma 3, spreading. Fruit a globose berry, 4 mm in diam., red when ripe. Asparagus racemosus is our endemic species, which can be trained into a nice ornamental specimen. On brownish spiny and wiry stems it has finger lengths of cladophylls arranged in a bottlebrush fashion.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
Flowers are bisexual, in simple racemes. It reproduces by seeds.

Phenology: Flowers white, very fragrant, in peak during May to July. In Desert regions new branches and cladodes appears between July to September; flowers come up later from November to December when the cladodes are withering; often the whole plant is seen in flowers, bearing no cladodes. Fruiting November to January.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
As a Medicinal, Ornamental Climber.

Parts Used:

Roots
The roots are locally known as Satawar: they are dried, powdered and taken with milk as a tonic.


Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:


Chemistry:
Sitosterol, stigmasterol and their glucosides, sarsasapogenin and two spirostanolic and two furostanolic saponins from fruit (Pharmazie 1981, 36, 709; ChemAbstr. 1982, 96, 31698 v).

Processing Techniques:

Other uses:
The tuberous roots are medicinally very important. Root extract caused initial increase in force and rate of amphibian heart at lower dose and cardiac arrest at higher dose. It showed hypotension both in normal and spinal cats (J. Res. Indian Med. 1971, 6, 132).
