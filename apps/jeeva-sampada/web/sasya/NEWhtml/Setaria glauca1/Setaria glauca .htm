HABIT & HABITAT:
A loosely-tufted annual grass with erect or ascendng culms, 30-150 cm. high, distributed throughout India ascending up to an altitude of 1,800 m.

BioticZone: Deccan Plateau, Konkan, Konkan plain and Western Ghats.

Availability : 
Cultivated fields and Plains, Gardens and Roadsides, Grass land, Moist places, Moist shady places, Pasture lands, Rocky habitats, Sandy Rhabitats, Slopes and waste land.
Soil: Moist clayey soils.
Misc: Baroda, Borighat, Corbett National Park, Dasgaon, Dhunvan vidi (Saurashtra), Dolara forest, Karla, Katraj ghat, Khanpur, Kodra dam, Mahan, Marvor beach, Matheran, Mt.Abu, Nagoa, Pachmari, Pen,Raiyi village, Sanegaon, Saurashtra, Shelu-budruk, Tirla, Trombay and Ugem village.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination:
It is sown in June and comes to harvest in October. 

Field is well-prepared and the seed is evenly broadcasted or sown with a seed drill at the rate of 5-7 kg. per h: The crop does not receive either irrigation or manure: It tillers profusely and needs no further attention till harvest time. It flourishes throughout the year but is its best during the monsoon.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Entire plant, Grains.

Uses: 

  • Setaria glauca is regarded as a fairly good green fodder for cattle.
  • The grains of the wild as well as cultivated types are used as food. They are either boiled and eaten whole or more rarely ground to flour. 
  • A bread is made out of these grains. 
  • In Russia, the grains are used as poultry feed and for the preparation of alcohol. It is also con sidered to be a good lawn grass. 
  • The grains are some times used as an adulterant of anise
