HABIT & HABITAT:
It is evergreen tree, 10-20m high and found in INDIA : Maharashtra: In semi-evergreen forests of Ratnagiri, Siadhudurg. Hills of Uttar Pradesh, Assam, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Kerala and Andaman & Nicobar Island.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Evergreen trees, 10-20 m high; young branches, petioles, inflorescence covered with minute, brown deciduous scales; barks brownish-grey, smooth. Leaves 7-25 cm long, imperipinnate; Leaflets 5-7, opposite, ovate or  lanceolate, oblong-lanceolate or ellitic-obtuse, 5-15 x 2.5-8 cm, acutely acuminate at apex, cuneate at base, shortly petiolulate, scaly on both surfaces, leathery, glabrous above, sparsely hairy beneath and densely so on nerves, terminal leaflet longer.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers in axillary, dull or buff-coloured, 7-20 cm long panicles, 0.15-0.20 cm across yellow, young petals scaly. Anthers included. Berries ovoid-pyriform or sub-globose, 2 cm across, velvety-hairy, buff-coloured. Seeds solitary, ellipsoid and enclosed in a mealy, edible pulp.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The fruit is acidic in taste and edible. It is cooling and astringent and employed in inflammation and febrile complaints. The seeds are said to be useful in painful micturition.


Chemistry:

The structure of roxburghiline was determined by IR, NMR and the mass spectral analysis. Pyrrolidine was prepares from proline and possessed spectral properties similar to those of I. (Purushothaman et al., 1978). C.A. 1980, 92, 147000h.

Roxburghilin has been shown to be N-cinnamoyl-Z-(2-methylbutanoylamino) pyrrolidine (fig. 12) by chemical and spectroscopic methods. (Purushothaman et al., 1978). C.A. 1980, 92, 147000.

Two triterpenes-roxburghiadiols A (fig. 1) and B (fig. 2) isolated from leaves and fruits and their structure elucidate as 4-bisnormethyl 24-methylene cycloartan -3b 7a diol (fig. 10) and 4-bisnormethyl 24-methylene cycloartan - 3b 6a diol (fig. 11). Isolation of two new triterpenes and their characterization as 29-norcycloratenol and 28, 29-bisnorcylloartan-24-methylene -3b 6a-diol also isolated. (Purushothaman et al., 1986).

By 2D-nmr studies structure of Roxburghiadiol B was confirmed and Roxburghiadol A was found to be 6b-epimer of Roxburghiadol B. (Balakrishna et al., 1990).

 1H-cyclopentaterahydro[b]bezofuran, two lignans, two diammarane triterpenoids and one limonoid were isolated from the bark of Aglaia elaeagnoides. (Fuzzati at al., 1996).

Synthesis of (+)-Lariciresionol 3a-acetate [(+)-(3S)-acetyloxymethyl-(2S)-(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)- (4R)-[(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)methyl]tetrahydrofuran], a metabolite of A. elaeagnoidea]. (Rajasekhar and Subbaraju, 2000).

Twelve new derivatives of Bisamides [Piriferinol (fig. 3), edulimide (fig. 4)], lignans [lariciresinol acetat (fig. 5)], triterpenes[4-bis-norcycloartane-type triterpenes] (fig. 13 – fig. 20), cyclopenta[b]benzofurans [Pannellin (fig. 6), Pannellin 1-O-acetata (fig. 7), 3’-metoxypannellin] (fig. 8) and an aromatic butyrolactone [agalactone] (fig. 9) were isolated from lipophilic leaf, stem, and root extracts of Aglaia spp. Eleagnoidea. (Brader et al, 1998.)


Pharmacology:


Anti-leukaemic bisamides from aglaia elaegnoides.

Eight flavaglines, six cyclopenta[b]benzofurans, a cyclopenta[bc]benzopyran, and a benzo[b]oxepine, together with an aromatic butyrolactone were isolated from A. elaeagnoidea against the three plant pathogens Pyricularia grisea, Fusarium avenaceum, and Alternaria citri for antifungal properties. The benzofurans displayed strong activity, whereas the benzopyran, benzoxepine, and butyrolactone were inactive at the highest concentration tested. P. grisea, responsible for rice blast disease. (Engelmeier et al, 2000). 

Any Other 

Two cyclopenta[b]benzofurans (flavaglines), pannellin(15) and pannellin I-O-acetate (16), displayed very strong insecticidal activity against Spodoptera littoralis and Lymantria dispar. (Brader et al, 1998).
