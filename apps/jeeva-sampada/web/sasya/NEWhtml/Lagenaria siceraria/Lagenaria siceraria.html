HABIT & HABITAT
Large, softly pubescent, climbing or trailing herb, stems 5-angled. It is cultivated widely in warmer parts of India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Tendrils 2-fid. Leaves soft, 15 cm in diam., 3-5-lobed, acuminate at apex, hairy on both surfaces, basal sinus broad and deep; petioles 6-20 cm long, hollow. Flowers white, large, solitary. Male flowers longer than those of female; calyx tube campanulate, 5-teeth, narrow; petals 5, free, hairy on both surfaces. Female flowers: calyx and corolla as in male. Fruit large, bottle or dumble shaped, thick or woody. Seeds compressed, white, horizontal with a marginal groove or apex bidentate, smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Sweet variety: the stem is sweet and cures biliousness. It cause bronchitis. Fruit is cardiotonic, aphrodisiac and laxative and improves taste and cures leucorrhoes and biliousness. Bitter variety: leaves are diuretic and antibilious. It is used in leucorrhoes, vaginal and uterine problems, and earache. Fruit cures asthma, bronchitis, ulcers and pains.


Chemistry:

  • Two triterpenoids-22-deoxocucurbitacin D (fig.1a) and 22-deoxoisocucurbitacin D (fig.1b) from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1960-1969).

·        Plamitic (42.41), palmitoleic (0.81), stearic (6.7), oleic (9.67) and linoleic acids (40.42%) isolated from  seed  oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)

·        Rhamnose, fructose, glucose, galactose, sucrose, raffinose and stachyose isolated from  seed ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979).

·        Protein (44.68) and nitrogen (7.15%) determined in defatted seed meal; all essential amino acids except tryptophan also present.( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1980-1984).

·        Avenasterol, codisterol, clerosterol, isofucosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol, sitosterol, spinasterol, 25(27)-dehydroporiferasterol, 25(27)-dehydrofungisterol, 25(27)-dehydrochondrillasterol, 24β-ethy-(25)-dehydrolathosterol,24x- methyllathosterol and 22-dihydrospinasterol identified in seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1985-1989).

·        Plamitic, stearic, oleic and  linoleic acids identified in seed oil ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1994).

Pharmacology:

·        The antihepatotoxic activity of different fractions of the ethanolic extract of Lagenaria siceraria fruit, administered orally to different groups of rats was evaluated using the CCl4-induced hepatotoxicity test. All fractions tested, in a dose of 250 mg/kg showed significant activity, with the petroleum ether fraction exhibiting comparatively higher activity. Two steroids were isolated from the petroleum ether fraction and they were identified as fucosterol and campesterol. (Shirwaikar and Sreenivasan, 1996)

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for cracks in sole, emetic (fruit, Singh et al., 1980); jaundice (leaf, Singh et al., 1980; root, Hemadri and Rao, 1980); Purgative (fruit, Singh et al., 1980). Bottles, bowls, containers, utensils (fruit, Jain, 1964)
