Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
A deciduous tree, 18-24 m in height and 1.2-2 m in girth, frequently occuring in the Himalayas from Jammu and Kashmir to Arunachal Pradesh, at altitudes of 1650-2700 m ; also commonly planted for shade and fodder. Found from West Pakistan(Salt ranges) to UP, through Punjab, Nepal from 600 to 2400 mtrs. Height , often cultivated.
Availability :  
India:Himachal Pradesh,Jammu & Kashmir,Tamil Nadu,Uttar Pradesh.
Nepal.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Moderate sized, deciduous tree, Leaves obliquely ovate or elliptic, 6-8 cm x 3-4 cm, sharply toothed; flowers polygamous, male flowers larger, drupes ellipsoid or ovoid, solitary. Wood grey or yellowish grey, with irregular streaks of darker colour, tough and strong. It can be seasoned, susceptible to insects, moderately durable, polishes dull; Wt 47 lb per cubic foot.

Pests:
Diseases:

Chemistry
Analysis of the leaves (dry basis) gave the following results: crude protein-11.9-15.5; crude fibre-16.5-22.0; ether extract-4.5-8.6; N-free extract-17.75; total ash-10.8-15.7; calcium-2.83-4.47; phosphorous-0.11-0.14%. Presence of quebrachitol in the leaves is also reported.
The bark contains betulin, gallic acid, 3,3'-di-O-methylellagic acid and its glycocide and quebrachitol.

Other Uses

  1. The wood is used for making tool and whip handles, cups and spoons, churners, sport goods, oars, canoes, sticks and agricultural implements and for carving; also employed for carriage construction and building purposes. The timber is reported to be good material for paper pulp.

2.      Folk medicine: Used as a remedy for amenorrhoea and colic. The stem and leaves are crushed and given with jaggery in leprosy by bhil tribes in Madhya Pradesh ( India). The tribals of western Himalayas boil the root and give it as a remedy for colic and stomach troubles.

3.      Used in UP for tool handles; in Punjab for oars, sticks , whip handles, in Kashmir for ploughs, cups & spoons. It can be tried for churn sticks & bent part of carriages.
