HABIT & HABITAT:
Small trees or large shrubs with a acrid milky poisonous juice, glabrous; branchlets whorled, stout, marked with leaf-scars.It is found to occur throughout India in salt-swamp or on the sea-coast.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves alternate, lanceolate, oblanceolate or oblong-obovate, 10.5-24.5 x 3-6 cm, suddenly acuminate at apex, tapering at base, glabrous, coriaceous; petioles 2-3 cm long. Flowers white with yellow throat, large, odorous, in terminal paniculate cymes; peduncles 5-15 cm long; pedicels stout; bracts oblong, 1.3-2.5 cm long, acute, caducous. Calyx segments linear-oblong, 2.0-2.5 cm long, very acute, recurved. Corolla tube 1-2 cm long; lobes longer than the tube, broadly elliptic, acute, oblique. Fruit a drupe, subglobose, 5-10 cm long, smooth, green. Seeds usually solitary.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: White, axillary terminal cymes ; Flower: Scarecly fragrant, bracteate, pedicellate; Calyx: Hemispheric, thick lobes, semicircular ; Corolla: White, tubular, cylindric, swollen at the base, limb white ;Androecium: Stamens above the middle of the tube, filaments short, anther half the way down the tube; Gynoecium: Ovary bicarpellary, syncarpous, ovary superior, bilocular; Fruit: Solitary follicles recurved at the both ends, orange yellow; Seed: Many buried in scarlet pulp. Flowering & Fruiting :Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Bark, leaves and milky juice are purgative and emetic. The bark is used for ringworm infestation. Leaves are used for vitiated conditions of ‘vata’ and for skin diseases in children. The fruits are poisonous and used in hydrophobia.


Chemistry:
Thevetin B and monoacetylthevetin B, m.p. 176°C, isolated from seed kernels. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Diacetylneriifolin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Succinic acid, nicotiflorin, rutin, and L(+)bornesitol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

An iridoid – cerbinal (fig. 1) – along with its 10-carboxy derivative (cerberic acid, fig. 2) and 11-carboxy derivative (cerberinic acid, fig. 3) isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Digitoxigenin glycosides (neriifolin, cerberin, thevetin B and its 2’-O-acetyl derivative) and taughinigenin glycosides (deacetyl taughinin and its gentiobioside) identified in seeds; 17-b-H-taughinigenin-b-L-thevetoside and its (1’’à4’)-glucosyl derivative and deacetyltaughinin from root bark and stem; neriifolin, 17-bH-neriifolin, deacetyl-taughinin and 17-bH-deacetyltaughinin from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

A new flavanol tetraglycoside-3-O-(2G-rhamnosylrutinosyl)-7-O-b-glucosylquerctin – isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Five new cardenolides – cerleaside A (fig. 4), 17b-cerdollaside (fig. 5) (8b-hydroxy-17b-digitoxigenin-a-L-thevetoside) and its isomer 17a-cerdollaside (fig. 6), 17a-solanoside (fig. 7) (17a-digitoxigenin-a-L-acofrioside) and tanghinigenin-a-L-acofrioside (fig. 8) – isolated from leaves together with 17b-solanosid  (fig. 9) (17b-digitoxigenin-a-L-acofrioside) 17a- and 17b-neriifolins, 17a- and 17b-deacetyltanghinigenins. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Air-dried leaves afforded new glycosides – 17a-digitoxigenin-b-D-glucos-3-ulosyl(1à4)-a-thevetoside (fig. 10) and 17a-tanghinigenin-b-D-glucos-3-ulosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetoside (fig. 11) along with 3-O-b-D-glucosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetosides of 17a- and 17b-digitoxigenins and 17a- and 17b-tanghinigenins and gentiobiosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetosides of 17b-digitoxigenin and 17b-tanghinigenin; whereas fresh leaves yielded two new glycosides – cerleaside B (fig. 12) (oleagenin-b-glucosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetoside) and 17b-digitoxigenin-b-D-gentiotriosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetoside (fig. 13). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Isolation of three new glycosides – 17a-digitoxigenin-b-D-apiosyl(1à6)-b-D-glucosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetoside named as cerapioside, 17a-digitoxigenin-b-cellobiosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetoside and 17a-digitoxigenin-b-gentiobiosyl(1à4)-a-L-thevetoside – from stems along with  - cerleaside B, thevetin B 2’-O-acetylthevetin B as well as glucosyl-thevetoside of 17a- and 17b-digitoxigenins and 17a- and 17b-tanghinigenins. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Isolation of (-)olivil and (+)cyclo-olivil from stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Three olivil dimers – 5’,5’’’-bisolivil (cerberalignan A, fig. 14a), 5’,5’’-bisolivil (cerberalignan B, fig. 14b) and 5,5’’-bisolivil (cerberalignan C, fig. 14c) – isolated from stems; leaves afforded olivil-4-O-glucoside and olivil-4’-O-glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

Additional cerberalignans D (fig. 14d), E (fig. 14e), F (fig. 1f), G (fig. 14g), H (fig. 14h) and I (fig. 14i) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Three new normonoterpenoids – cerberidol (fig. 15), epoxycerberidol (fig. 16) and cyclocerberidol (fig. 17)– and their four new b-D-allopyranosides (15a, 15b, 15c and 15d) isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation and characterization of five complex lignans – cerberalignans J (fig. 18a) (olivil-4.O.8’’-threo-guaiacylglycerol), K (fig. 18b) (olivil-5’.5’’-cycloolivil), L (fig. 18c) (olivil-5.5’’-cycloolivil), M (fig. 18d) (olivil-5.5’’-olivil-4’’’.O.8’’’’-threoguaiacylglycerol) and N (fig. 18e) (olivil-5’.5’’-olivil-4’’’.O.8’’’’-threo-guaiacylglycerol) from stem. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

10-O-benzoyltheveside and 10-dehydrogeniposide isolated from leaves along with loganin, theviridoside and theveside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

10-Carboxyloganin (fig. 19) was isolated along with b-D-glucosides of cyclopentano-normonoterpenoids (fig. 19a) and dinormonoterpenoids (fig. 19b) from the frozen fresh leaves of Cerbera manghas. Their structures were characterized by means of MS and NMR respectively. (Abe and Yamauchi, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9702, 0982.


Pharmacology:
Effect of deacetyltanghinin on biosynthesis of thromboxane A2 and prostaglandin PG12 inisolated rabbit heart investigated. Due to opposite effects of these eicosanoids on platelets and blood vessels, the equilibrium in their biosynthesis plays a prominent role in cardiovascular haemostasis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Discovery and mechanism of betulic acid and natural product estrogens and antiestrogens. (Pisha, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 197712v.

Evaluation of antioxidant potential from Cerbera manghas. (Lee et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 49604w.

Two new cardenolides, (-)-14-hydroxy-3b-(3-O-methyl-6-deoxy-a-L-rhamnosyl)-11a, 12 a-epoxy-(5b, 14b, 17bH)-card-20 (22)-enolide (1), (-) –14-hydroxy-3b-(3-O-methyl-6-deoxy-a-L-glucopyranosyl)-11a,12a-epoxy-(5b,14b,17bH)-card-20(22)-enolide (2), and a known cardenolide, (-)-17b-neriifolin (3), were isolated from the roots of Cerbera manghas as antiproliferative and antiestrogenic principles when evaluated against a human colon cancer cell line (Col2) and the ishikawa cell line, respectively. Two known lignans, (-)-olivil (4) and (-) cycloolivil (5), were also isolated but were inactive in the assay systems used. (Chang et al, 2000).

The cytotoxic activity of methanol extracts of leaves from Cerbera manghas inhibited the growth of K562 cells. (Masuda et al, 2002).
