Chemistry:

·        Quercetin, kaempferol and cyaniding from whole plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Cyanidin-3,5-diglucoside from petals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        b-Phenethyl-b-D-glucopyranoside (1.0%) isolated from floswers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Detection of citronellol, nerol, geraniol and phenylethanol in essential oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        2-Hydroxyursolic acid, b-amyrin and methyl ursolate isolated from stamen. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Detection of a-copaene, n-nonacosanol, n-dotriacontanol, farnesol, geranyl acetone, nerol oxide, caryophyllene epoxide, neryl laurate and methyl geranate by GLC in Bulgarian rose concrete. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Average and highest oil contents of roses from India and North Africa were estimated as 0.041, 0.051 and 0.051, 0.08% respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Edward rose absolute contained geraniol (11.0), citronellol (4.0) and phenylethyl salcohol (56.0). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Ioslation of citronellol, nerol, geraniol, b-phenylethanol, eugenol, methyleugenol, b-sitosterol, pectolinarigenin, kaempferol-3-O-glucoside and kaempferol-3-O-galactoside from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Five fragrant compounds (I-V) identified in Bulgarian rose oil by GC –MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        The 9-O-b-D-glucopyranoside of 3-hydroxy-7,8-didehydro-b-ionol (1) has been isolated from a glycosidic XAD-2 extract obtained from rose petals. In addition to the b-damascenone-generating compound 1, the following glycoconjugates have been isolated and characterized as peracetates: 3-hydroxy-7,8-dihydro-b-ionol 9-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (2), 3-hydroxy-7,8-dihydro-b-ionol 3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (3), 2-phenylethyl-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (4), 2-phenylethyl-O-b-D-galactopyranoside (5), benzyl-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (6),(2E)-2,6-dimethyl-6-hydroxyocta-2,7-dienyl-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (7),(2Z)-2,6-dimethyl-6-hydroxyocta-2,7-dienyl-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (8), and (2E,6E)-2,6-dimethyl-1-hydroxyocta-2,6-dien-8-yl-O-b-D-glucopyranoside (9). (Straubinger et al., 1997)

·        (S)-3,7-dimethyl-5-octene-1,7-diol and related oxygenated monoterpenoids from petals of Rosa damascena Mill. (Knapp et al., 1998)

·        Clarification of the biosynthetic pathway to 2-phenylethanol (2), the deuterium-labeled putative precursor, [2H8]L-phenylalanine ([2H8-1]), was fed to the flowers of Rosa 'Hoh-Jun' and R. damascena Mill. throughout maturation, ceasing feeding at the commencement of petal unfurling and at full bloom. Based on GC-MS analyses, [2H8]-1 was incorporated into both 2 and 2-phenylethyl b-D-glucopyranoside (3) when the flowers were fed until full bloom, whereas no such incorporation into 2 was apparent when feeding was ceased earlier. In both species of rose, the labeling pattern for 2 was almost identical to that for 3, and indicated the presence of [2H6]-, [2H7]- and [2H8]-2, and [2H6]-, [2H7]- and [2H8]-3. This may be ascribed to the equilibrium between 2 and 3. The labeling pattern for 2 and 3 also indicated that these compounds were produced from 1 via several routes, the route involving phenylpyruvic acid being the major one. (Watanabe et al., 2002).

·        The progenitors of damascenone, the most intensive C13-norisoprenoid volatile aroma constituent of rose essential oil, were surveyed in the flowers of Rosa damascena Mill. Besides 9-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-3-hydroxy-7,8-didehydro-b-ionol , a stable progenitor already isolated from the residual water after steam distillation of flowers of R. damascena Mill., two labile progenitors were identified to be (3S, 9R)- and (3S, 9S)-megastigma-6,7-dien-3,5,9-triol 9-O-b-D-glucopyranosides  based on their synthesis and HPLC-MS analytical data. (Suzuki et al., 2002)


Pharmacology:

·        Water and methanol extracts of Rosa damascena exhibited moderate anti-HIV activity. The anti-viral activities of 9 compounds isolated from the methanol extract were compared. The tetrahydroxyflavanone (kaempferol, 1), was effective in reducing the maturation of infectious progeny virus apparently due to selective inhibition of the viral protease. On the other hand the pentahydroxyflavone (quercetin, 2) and two 3-substituted derivatives of kaempferol appeared to inhibit HIV-infection by preventing binding of gp120 to CD4. 2-Phenylethanol-O-(6-O-galloyl)-b-D-glucopyranoside 8 interacted irreversibly with gp120 and neutralized virus infectivity. The differences in the modes of action of 1 and 8 can account for the apparent synergy of their anti-viral activities. (Mahmood et al., 1996)

·        The essential oil of Rosa damascena petals was evaluated for its antibacterial effects against three strains of Xanthomonas axonopodis spp. vesicatoria. (Basim, 2003)

·        The fresh juice of Rosa damascena Mill, flower exhibited promising in vitro antioxidant potential. The partially purified acetone fraction (AF) from silica gel column chromatography was found to be the active fraction with antioxidant properties. The AF required for 50% inhibition of Superoxide radical production, hydroxyl radical generation and lipid peroxide formation were 13.75, 135 and 410 mug/ml, respectively. Oral administration of AF at 50 mg/kg body weight significantly reduced the serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP), glutamine pyruvate transaminase (GPT) and glutamine oxaloacetate transaminase (GOT) activity and lipid peroxide level in rats receiving an acute dose of CCl4. This indicated that R. damascena could protect against CCl4 induced hepatotoxicity, possibly by its free radical scavenging activity. (Achuthan et al., 2003)
