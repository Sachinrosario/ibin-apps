DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Branched, 15-20 m tall trees. Branches angular, yellowish. Leaves opposite, oblong-elliptic, 3-ribbed, glabrous, young leaves pubescent, yellowish.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers yellow in colour, arranged in panicles, Perianth lobes thick. Berries ellipsoid, covered with persistent perianth lobes. December-March. Inflorescence: Axillary ; Flower: complete, hermaphrodite, small; Perianth: With oblong lobes and short tubes, segments 6 ; Androecium: Stamens 9, fertile, introrse 4-celled anthers; Gynoecium:  Ovary superior,  surrounded by cup shaped base, hypogynous, one chambered with single ovule, ; Fruit: Black  berry  sealed on somewhat enlarged perianth tube . Flowering & Fruiting : May- June .Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:

·         Linalool (92.66), geraniol (2.2) and citronellol (1.47%) determined in leaf oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·         The leaf and stem bark oil of Cinnamomum sulphuratum were investigated by GC/MS. Fort-six and twenty-nine components were identified, representing 96.2% and 94.4% of the oils, respectively. Geranial (27.8%), geraniol (23.2%) and neral (17.6%) were the major constituents of the leaf oil. The bark oil was rich in (E)-cinnamaldehyde (65.6%). (Baruah et al, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9904-2593.
