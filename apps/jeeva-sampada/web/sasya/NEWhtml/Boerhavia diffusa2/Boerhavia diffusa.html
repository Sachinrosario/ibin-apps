DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Herbaceous, diffuse, root large, fusiforms; stems prostrate or ascending, reaching 0.6-0.9 m. long, divaricately branched, slender, cylindric thickened at the nodes, minutely pubescent or nearly glabrous often purplish.  Leaves at each node in unequal pairs, the larger 2.5-3.8 cm., the smaller 1.3-2 cm. Long, both nearly as broad as long, broadly ovate or suborbicular, rounded at the apex, green and glabrous above, usually white with minute scales beneath the margins entire, often coloured pink, somewhat undulate, base rounded or subcordate; petioles nearly as long as the blade, slender.  Flowers very small, shortly stalked or nearly sessile, 4-10 together, in small umbels arranged in slender long stalked corymbose, axillary and terminal panicles; bracteoles small, laceolate, acute perianth 3 mm. Long; ovarial part of tube 1.25 mm. Long contracted above the ovary, glandular-vicid; limb funnel-shaped, dark pink with 5 narrow vertical bands outside.  Stamens 2 or 3, slightly excerted.  Fruit 3 mm. Long, clavate, rounded, broadly and blunty 5 ribbed, very glandular.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Small umbels arranged in slender long-stalked corymbose, axillary and terminal panicles; Flower: Small, shortly stalked, bracteolate, complete, regular ; Perianth:  Perianth tube contracted above the ovary, glandular viscid, limb funnel shaped, dark pinkwith 5-narrow vertical bands outside;Androecium: Stamens 2 or 3 , slightly exertedfilaments capillary, unequal, anthers dorsifixed, didynamous; Gynoecium:Ovary 1-celled, free, ovule solitary, basal, erect, oblique, stipitate, style filiform, stigma peltate ; Fruit: Clavate, rounded, broadly and bluntly 5-ribbed, very glandular ; Seed: with adherent testa. Flowering & Fruiting : November-January.Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts Used and Uses:
The leaves have a sharp taste, appetizer alexiteric used in ophthalmia and for eye wounds; useful in pains of the joints.  The seeds are toxic, expectorant carminative, useful in muscular pain, lumbago, Scabies, and scorpion-sting; purify the blood; hasten delivery. The root is well known for its diuretic properties. It is also a very good expectorant. Taken in large doses it acts as a emetic. It is used in jaundice, ascites, anasarca, scanty with dried ginger it is given in urticaria.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:
Roots have b-ecdysone, triacontanol, a purine nucleoside – hypoxanthine –9-L-arabinofuranoside, 5,7- dihydroxy –3’, 4’- dimetroxy –6, 8 – dimethyl flavone, hentriacontane, b-sitosterol, and ursolic acid, alkaloid punarnavine has also been reported from the plants.
NBRI:
 Ash (11.8), Ca (1.2) and K (2.3%), presence of alkaloids, free and combined amino acids determined in aerial parts of the plant. Similar comparative studies showed them to be different in B. erecta L. syn. B. punarnava saha & Murthy. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Hentriacontane, b-sitosterol and ursolic acid isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A polysaccharide isolated which on hydrolysis yielded glucose, xylose, glucuronic acid, galactose, L-arabinose and L-rhamnose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

A glycoprotein with molecular weight of 16,000-20,000 daltons isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

b-Ecdysone (0.05%), triacontanol and b-sitosterol was isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

5,7-dihydroxy-3’,4’-dimethoxy-6,8-dimethylflavone (fig. 1) isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Two new rotenoids – boeravinone A (fig. 4a) and boeravinone B (fig. 4b) isolated from roots of plant collected from Nepal; sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol and their glucosides and methyl esters of palmitic, heptadecyclic, oleic, stearic, arachidic and behenic acids were also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation of a new 12a-hydroxyrotenoid boeravinone C (fig. 5) from roots and determination of its structure and absolute configuration. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Two new rotenoids – repenone (fig. 2a) and repenol (fig. 2b) isolated and their structures established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Isolation of liriodendrin and syringaresinol-b-D-glucoside from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Isolation and structure determination of three new rotenoids – boeravinones D, E and F (fig. 4) from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Borhavine (fig. 3) isolated from roots and characterized as methyl-3,10-dihydro-11-1-methoxy-4,6-dimethyl-10-oxo-1H-furo[3,4-b]xanthene-3-carboxylate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

Phytochemical and pharmacological investigation on B. diffusa, study affored 2 plant bases; a water soluble base choline hitherto unreported from this source and a CHCl3 soluble alkaloid. (Wahi et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 299410s.

In addition to b-sitosterol a new unsaturated acid from the roots of Boerhavia diffusa has been characterized as triacont-24-en-1-oic acid by spectral data. (Srivastava and Shukla, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 280828bn.

From the roots of Boerhavia diffusa four new compounds namely Boerhavisterol (fig. 6), boerhadiffusene (fig. 7), diffusarptenoid (fig. 8) and boerhavilanostenyl benzoate (fig. 9) and a known rotenoid boervinone A, have been isolated and their structures were elucidated as 9,10-seco-stigmast-5,8(9)-dien-3b-ol, 1-(2’,6’,6’-trimethylcyclohex-1’-enyl)-11-(3’’,3’’-dimethylcyclohexyl)-4,8-dimethyl-dimethyl-indec-1-ene, 4,9-dihydroxy-10-methyl-6a-dehydrorotenoid-6benzoate and 27-O-(4’-benzoyl-b-D-glucopyranosyl)-lanost-5-en-3-one, respectively on the basis of spectral data analysis and chemical means. (Gupta and Ali, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 107586a.


Pharmacology:
Glycoprotein was active against spherical and tubular viruses in hypersensitive and systemic resistance in plants. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Chloroform and methanolic extracts of roots and aerial parts exhibited hepatoprotective activity against CCl-intoxication in rats; a rotenoid, a steroid and a flavone retaining hepatoprotective activity were also obtained. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

Liriodendrin exhibited significant Ca2+ channel antagonistic effect in frog heart single cell. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.

B. Diffusa produced 50% inhibition of lipid peroxidation at a concentration of 2.28 mg/ml in Fe2+ ascorbate system (Joy and Kuttan, 1995). MAPA 1996, 9603-1425.

Punernavine has a powerful diuretic activity. (Wahi et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 299410s.

Pharmacological activities of B. diffusa and their isolates have also been reviewed, hepatoprotective adaptogenic, antifibrinolytic, antiinflammation, diuretic and antiviral were found in the plant. (Srivastava et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 194235b.

The alkaloidal fraction of boerhavia diffusa was studied for its effect on cellular and humoral functions in mice. Oral administration of the fraction (25-100 mg/kg) significantly inhibited SRBC-induced delayed sensitivity reaction in mice. (Mungantiwar et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 179490h.
