HABIT & HABITAT:
 Annual, erect, stellate tomentose herb found throughout India, from Punjab to Kerala. Common in waste places, roadsides, dried up ditches and cultivated fields. Nepal,Indochina,Myanmar, Srilanka, North Africa, Spain.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual, erect, stellate tomentose herb. Leaves lanceolate, variable in shape and size, broadly ovate-orbicular, sometimes obscurely 3-lobed, rounded at apex, truncate to subcordate at base, crenate or undulate at margins, subcoriaceous, rugose or plaited, 3-nerved from base; petioles 2.5–5.0 cm long; stipules subulate, deciduous. Racemes terminal or subterminal, densely stellate hairy. Flowers axillary, bracteate, yellow; male flowers above, subsessile; female flowers below, pedicelled. Male flowers: sepals 5, linear-lanceolate; petals 5, ovate, externally lepidote; stamens 5-15, filaments connate below, disc obscure. Female flowers: sepals subulate; petals 5, narrow or obsolete; disc of 5 glands; styles 3, 2-fid; stigma red. Capsules of 3, 2 valved cocci.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary racemes; Flower: Monoecious, sessile, male flower crowded in upper part and female solitary, pedicellate ; Male Flower: Calyx globose or ovoid, spillting into 5-valved segments; petals 5-short, disk obscure; Stamens 5-15, filaments connate below in whorls, anthers oblong, cells parallel, pistillode ; Male Flower: Sepals narrow, disk glandular short; ovary 3-celled, styled 3-celled, 2-fid, ovule 1 in each cell ; Fruit: Capsule tomentose, 2-valved ; Seed: Estrophiolate . Flowering & Fruiting :. March –July.

Floral and seed biology:
Fl. & Fr: August - January.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: Plant is acrid and poisonous. Ashes of roots are given in cough. Seeds are used as cathartic and purgative. Plants are emetic, drastic and having corrosive properties. Blue colour obtained from capsule is used in formation of litmus for chemical analysis.


Chemistry:
Three new glycosides – 3,5,6,7,8-pentamethoxyxanthone-1-O-rhamnosyl(1à6)-glucopyranoside, 3,5,8-trimethoxyxanthone-1-O-glucopyranoside and 2-acetonyl-5-methyl-7-hydroxy-6C-glucopyranosyl-chromone-2’’-O-glucopyranoside – isolated from roots and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Ethnobotany:
