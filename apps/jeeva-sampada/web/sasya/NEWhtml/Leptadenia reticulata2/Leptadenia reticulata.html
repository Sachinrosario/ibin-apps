HABIT & HABITAT
Twining leafy, much-branched herbs or under shrubs; branches hoary pubescent. It is found to occur in Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Daman & Diu, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves ovate to lanceolate, 3.5-8.5 x 2.5-5.5 cm, acute or acuminate at apex, rounded or slightly cordate at base, pubescent; petioles 1.0-1.7 cm long, pubescent. Flowers in lateral or subaxillary, many flowered, umbelliform cymes; peduncles 1-7 cm long, often in pairs, puberulous; pedicels 3-4 mm long, puberulous. Calyx pubescent outside, divided up to the middle; segments ovate-oblong, 1.25 mm long, subacute. Corolla pale green or white, pubescent outside, 5 mm long; tube very short, glabrous; lobes ovate or oblong, obtuse, thick. Follicles solitary, lanceolate, 7.0-12.5 cm long, terete, tapering into an obtuse curved beak, minutely white dotted. Seeds ovoid or oblong, 4.0-5.5 mm long, acute, flat; coma c 3 cm long.

Pharmacology:  

Aqueous extract showed prolonged and pronounced hypotensive effect both in intact and spinal dogs. It did not black carotid sinus reflex on intracisternal administration and caused marked and prolonged rise in arterial pressure. It did not possess parasympathomimetic or adrenolytic actions but blocked pressor response to nicotine. It showed transient negative inotropic and chronotropic effects in dog heart. It caused significant vasodilation in hind limb of dog. It caused initial stimulation, followed by depression of respiration and slight constriction of bronchial muscle. It did not show any acute or chronic toxicity in rats in doses upto 6 ml/kg. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
Isolation of a new trisaccharide - leptaculatin - and its structure determined as O-b-D-glucosyl(1®4)O-b-D-glucosyl(1®4)-D-cymaropyranose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
