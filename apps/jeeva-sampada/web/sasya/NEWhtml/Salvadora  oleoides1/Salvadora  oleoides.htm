HABIT & HABITAT :
A shrub or a small tree with short twisted trunk and drooping branches, found in the arid regions of Punjab and western India.
 It can be seen near gardens and roadsides, Hard rocky grounds, Hill slopes, Sandy plains, Sea shore,

Bark gray or whitish gray; leaves linear-lanceolate, coriaceous; flowers sessile, greenish white, minute in paniculate spikes; drupes globose, usually yellow when ripe; seeds greenish yellow, c. 3 mm. in diam.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used: 
Leaves , Fruit, Seeds , Wood

Uses

  • Fruits are sweet and edible; the pulp contains glucose, fructose and sucrose. Fed to cattle, they are said to increase their milk yield.
  • Seed-cake or meal left over after expressing the fat is reported to be suitable as livestock feed.

Chemistry
Seed-cake or meal left over after expressing the fat is reported to be suitable as livestock feed. It is also valued as manure for tobacco. A commercial sample of the cake (yield, 38% on air-dried seed kernel) had the following chemical composition : moisture (inclusive of volatile constituents at 105°), 12-4; crude protein, 26.66; carbo hydrates (as reducing sugar), 22-0; total ash, 16-9; nitrogen, 4-16; and phosphorus (P.,0,;), 1-68%. The amino acid composition of processed seed meal protein is as follows glycine, 8-9; alanine, 6-6; aspartic acid, 8-5: glutamic acid, 14-0; serine, 8-3; threonine, 6-0; valine-methionine 5-8; leucines, 14-5; arginine, 6-6; histidinc, 3-9; Ivsinc, 5.5, proline, 5-6; tyrosine, 3-8; and cystine, 2-4% 

The seeds are also reported to contain quercetin, rutin, and an alkaloid. Minor components of the seeds, which make the seed fat non-edible, are probably dibenzyl thiourea, dibcnzyl urea, and a thioglucoside glucotropaeolin (C14H18O9NS2K, m.p. 75- 76°) and its decomposition products

Analysis of seeds from Kutch gave the following values : moisture, 2.80; fat, 45-48; albuminoids, 18.94; carbohydrates, 23-48; fibre, 5-80; and ash, 3-50%. Seeds contain 40-50 per cent of a greenish yellow fat (Karadi, Pilu or Khakan fat).

Processing Techniques
A process for the purification and preferential separation of non-glyceride constituents from the fat has been patented. The process consists of maceration and percolation of the coarsely powdered seed with dilute ethanol at ordinary temperature and subsequent extraction of the fat from the alcohol extracted seed powder. This is followed by the con ventional alkali refining, deodorization and bleaching of the fat. Purified fat is free from foul odour and has an agreeable taste. It has the following characteristics : colour, snow-white; m.p., 42° (solid, p., 39°);-sap. val., 233.0; iod. val., 3.6; acid val., 0-71; and unsapon. matter, 0.5%. It is rich in lauric and myristic acids and resembles coconut oil in composition. A sample of the purified and refined fat contained : capric, 0.77; lauric, 35-60; myristic, 50.75; palmitic, 4.54; oleic, 8-28; and linoleic acid, 0-06%. The unsaponifiable matter contains dibenzyl thiourea, di-benzyl urea, a substance C19H20N2S. ½ H,0 (m.p., 180- 82°) ß-sitosterol, a carotenoid, and probably higher alco hols and hydrocarbons.

Other Uses :
  1. Leaves are used to relieve cough, and are given to horses as a purgative. 
  2. Root bark is used as a vesicant. Fruits are employed in the treatment of enlarged spleen, rheumatism, and low fever. 
  3. Seeds form 44-46 per cent of the whole fruit. Analysis of seeds from Kutch gave the following values : moisture, 2.80; fat, 45-48; albuminoids, 18.94; carbohydrates, 23-48; fibre, 5-80; and ash, 3-50%. Seeds contain 40-50 per cent of a greenish yellow fat (Karadi, Pilu or Khakan fat). Crude fat, obtained by pressing decorticated seeds in high pressure expellers with the accompaniment of heat or in ghanis, is a dark coloured malodoriferous product having a bitter taste and contaminated with considerable quantities of degradation products. Since it contains large amounts of lauric and myristic acids, it could be used for soap making, but the undesirable colour and odour of the fat have prevented its direct use for this purpose. Purified seed fat can be directly used for soap making . and is a potential industrial substitute of coconut oil for this purpose. It is also used for making candles. Medicinally, the fat is used in the treatment of rheumatic pains and is employed in the preparation of suppositories. It is also used as a base for ointments. Gujarat, Punjab and Rajasthan are the main collecting centres with an estimated potential of about 47,000 tones of seed. 
  4. Wood is light red or yellow (wt., 608-865 kg./cu.m.), moderately hard, with a small, irregular, purple heart- wood. It is used for building purposes, agricultural implements, Persian wheels and the knees of boats. It is not a good fuel, but mixed with deodar and pine scrap wood it is used for burning bricks.
