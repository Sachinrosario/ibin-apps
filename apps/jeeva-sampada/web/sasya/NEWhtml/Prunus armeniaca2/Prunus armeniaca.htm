DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 A moderate- sized tree, about 10m tall, with a reddish bark. Leaves ovate to round- ovate or sometimes sub-cordate, 5-9 cm long; flower s pinkish white, borne singly and appearing much in advance of the foliage ; fruits rund, pubescent when young, but nearly glabrous at maturity, with a yellow to yellowish orange, firm and e, mostly free from the flat, ridge stone; kernels sweet in some types and bitter in others

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used:
 Dried fruit – used as laxative and refrigrant in fevers.


Chemistry:  

Quercetin-3-rhamnoglucoside was isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.
Kaempferol (0.13%) from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.
Gum composed of xylose, arabinose and galactose in molar ratio 1:8:8 in addition to mannose, glucuronic acid and 4-O-methylglucuronic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.
24-Dehydrocholesterol, cholesterol, estrone and a-estradiol (estradiol- b-17-ol) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Seed kernels contained myristic, palmitic, stearic, oleic and linoleic acids and b-sitosterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Avenasterol-5-ene, b-sitosterol and stigmasterol isolated from pits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Kernels contained total fat (40.9), total protein (23.1) and mineral constituents (2.8%); oleic acid (51.6) and linoleic acid (36.6%) also present. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Hexane extract of seeds inhibited mutagenicity of benzo[a]pyrene, 3-amino-1,4-dimethyl- 5H-pyrido[4,3-b]indole and 2-(2-furyl)-3-(5-nitro-2-furyl)acrylamide; oleic and linoleic acids identified as inhibitory substances.
Plant contained HCN (0.004%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
p-cymen-8-ol, butanol, isobutanol, isovaleraldehyde, butyl acetate, hexyl acetate, phenylacetaldehyde, 2,6,6-trimethyl-2-vinyltetrahydropyran, ionene, toluene and butyric, palmitic and linoleic acids identified by GC and GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.
Linalool, g-decalactone, hexanal, (E)2-hexenal, (E,E)2,4-decadienal, (E)2-nonenal, g-dodecalactone and b-ionone identified in fruits by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
Dried fruits contained 3,4-dihydroxybenzoic, chlorogenic and vanillic acids; quercetin, quercitrin, rutin, hyperoside and kaempferol identified by HPLC and TLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.
Oleic (0.7) and linoleic (0.4%) acids determined in seeds by HPLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.
Isolation of lilac alcohol, lilac aldehyde, lilac acetate, 6-hydroxy-2,6- dimethyl-2,7-octadienal and 2(Z),6-dimethyl-2, 7-octadien-l,6-diol from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.
Glucose, fructose, citric acid and malic acid identified in fruit pulp. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.
Roots of Prunus armeniaca yielded a proanthocyanidin (C37H28O12) whose structure was assigned ent-epiafzelechin-3-O-p-hydroxybenzoate-(4a®8, 2a®O®7)-epiafzelechin, on the basis of spectral studies. (Prasad et al, 1998). MAPA 9901-0582.
Aromatic glycoside, 4-O-glucosyloxy-2-hydroxy-6-methoxyacetophenone (C15H20O9), has been isolated from Prunus armeniaca roots. (Prasad, 1999). MAPA 9906-3942.
The major allergens of apricot is a protein of molecular mass (Mr) 9000 belonging to the family of lipid transfer protein. Structure of apricot LTP ny micro-sequencing and mass spectroscopic analyses was determined. Apricot LTP is a 91 amino acids protein like peach and almond LTPs with a a sequence identity of 91% and 94% respectively. Out of the 25 amino acid forming the inner surface of the tunnel-like hydrophobic cavity, 16 are identical and 7 similar in the apricot LTP, supporting the hypothesis of a similar function. (Conti et al, 2001).
Two A-type proanthocyanidins, ent-epiafzelechi-(4a®8, 2a®O®7)-(-)-epicatechin and its isomer ent-epifzelechin-(4a®8', 2a®O®7)-catechin, together with other known compounds, have been isolated from the roots of Prunus armeniaca. The concerted use of one- and two-dimensional NMR methods (1H-(1)-H COSY, HMQC, HMBC and NOESY) allowed the identification of these compounds. (Prasad, 2000).

NBPGR:   Seeds contain fatty oil, ethereal oil, enzymes and amygdalin; fruit contains lycopin, α-carotin, Vitamin A.

Pharmacology: Microscopical identification of Prunus armeniaca in Hae dong Gye Meong Hwan a Chinese patent medicine. (Park et al, 1998). MAPA 9901-0193.


Ethnobotany: Edible (Fruit, Bhargava, 1959; Gupta, 1962); Oil (Seed, Sarin, 1989)
