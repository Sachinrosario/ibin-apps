HABIT & HABITAT
Annual, slender, erect herb, 30-70 cm tall, glabrous except fruits. It is cultivated in India.


Availability : Plenty, Throughout India

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves twice or thrice 3-partite, ultimate segments filiform. Umbel compound, rays few, bract and bractioles several, linear, rigid, calyx-teeth small, subulate, unequal. Petals oblong or ovate, emerginate, white, often unequal. Fruits cylindric, tip narrowed; primary ridges filiform, distinct, secondary ridges usually hispidulous; vittae large, solitary under each secondary ridges. Carpophore 3-partite or 2 fid, seeds somewhat dorsally compressed, convex-concave.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed  biology:
Inflorescence compound umbel consisting of many umbellules; Flower bracteate, pedicellate, central flower actinomorphic, peripheral flower zygomorphic due to unequal size of petals, complete, hermaphrodite, pentamerous, epigynous and cyclic; Calyx sepals 5, polysepalous, valvate, persistent; Corolla petals 5,polypetalous, valvate; Androecium  stamens  5, polyandrous, dithecous, filaments long and slender, dorsifixed and introrse; Gynoecium bicarpellary, ovary inferior, bilocular with one pendulous ovule in each locule, placentation axile, style 2,stigma 2 and capitate; Fruit is cremocarp splitting into 2 mericarps; Seeds brown obovate-oblong. December-March.

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal: 
Plant is pollinated by insects and mericarps are dispersed by birds and animals.
Genetic variability:
There are two cultivated varieties i.e irrigated   and dry differing from each other in colour of stem and flowers. Sporophytic chromosome count is 22.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: It is usually grown dry in black cotton soil and in all type of soil under irrigation

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of manures and fertilizers.

Water management: . The first irrigation is given after three days of sowing and a subsequent once in a week in case of irrigated crop and only few irrigations are required for dry crop.

Harvesting period / Method: It is harvested when plant is about three to three and half months old when the fruits get ripe.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine seeds are used as aphrodisiac, anthelmintic, carminative, antidysenteric, stomachic, alexipharmic, antipyretic and uterine stimulant. It increases appetite and improves taste. It is also useful in leucoderma, fever, leprosy and scorpion sting. It cures “vata” tumorus and eye disease.

In Unani system of medicine seeds are used as astrigent, carminative, vulnerary, abortifacient, emmenagogue and tonic to the intestine. It is also useful in corneal opacities, ulcer, asthma, gonorrhoea, hiccough and inflammation. It also cures scabies, and haemolysis.


Chemistry:
b-sitosterol, D-mannitol and flavanoid glycosides from defatted seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

b-sitosterol and a substance, mp. 288°C, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Chlorogenic acid and caffeic acids, rutin, umbelliferone and scopoletin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Detection of a-pinene, limonene, b-phellandrene, 1,8-cineole, linalool, borneol, b-caryophyllene, citronellol, geraniol, thymol, linalyl acetate, geranyl acetate, caryophyllene oxide, elemol and methylheptenone in seed oil by TLC and GLC; triacontane; triacontanol, b-sitosterol, tricosanolo, psoralen, angelicin and coriandrinol (b-sitosterol glucoside) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Linalool (90.0), furfural and geraniol identified in essential oil (0.65-0.73%) of plant from Vietnam. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A process developed for extracting essential oil which contained linalool (68.22) and geraniol (2.87%). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Butylphthalides – neocnidilide and Z-ligustilide – analysed by GLC in different parts of plant from Netherlands. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Coriandrinonediol (fig. 1), mp. 285°C (0.006%) isolated from seeds and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Gnaphaloside A, gnaphsloside B, quercetin, isorhamnetin, rutin and luteolin isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Two new furoisocoumarins – coriandrin (fig. 2) and dihydrococoriandrin (fig. 3) – isolated and characterized by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Nonane, C9-16 alkenals, C7 -17 alkanals, C 10-12 primary alkenols and alkanols identified in fresh green leaves by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Petroselinic acid (10.7%) determined in seed oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Palmitic, petroselinic, oleic and linoleic acids as major fatty acids along With lauric, myristic, myristoleic and palmitoleic acids identified in seeds by GLC; b-sitosterol identified in unsaponifiable fraction. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Isolation of new isocournarins - coriandrones A (fig. 4) and B (fig. 5) from aerial parts and their structure elucidation by X-ray analysis; in addition coriandrin and dihydrocoriandrin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Synthesis of coriandrin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Leaves of coriander were found to contain waxy hydrocarbons, imperatorin, bergapten, scopoletin, umbelliferone and xanthotoxol. (Nassar et al., 1993) CA. 1995, 122:183184t.

Three isocoumarins, coriandrones C-E were isolated from the whole plants of  C.sativum. (Masahiko et al., 1996)

From the water-soluble portion of the methanol extract of coriander, 33 compounds, including two new monoterpenoids, four new monoterpenoid glycosides, two new monoterpenoid glucoside sulfates and two new aromatic compound glycosides were obtained. (Ishikawa et al., 2003).

Pharmacology:

The antiperoxidative effect of coriander seeds was studied in rats administered high fat diet. Significant decrease in the levels of lipid peroxides, free fatty acids and glutathione was observed when compared to control group whereas the activity of antioxidant enzymes showed increase. (Chithra and Leelamma, et al., 1999).

Sequential extraction with solvents revealed insulin-releasing activity in hexane and water fractions indicating a possible cumulative effect of more than one extract constituent. These results demonstrate the presence of antihyperglycaemic, insulin-releasing and insulin-like activity in Coriandrum sativum. (Gray and Flatt, 1999).
