DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves 3-5 foliate; leaflets lanceolate, 7-12 x 2-3 cm, acuminate at apex, acute at base, chartaceous, glabrous above, grey pubescent beneath; petioles 1-1.3 cm long, lateral leaflets smaller with a very short petiolule, common petioles, 2.5-3.8 cm long. Flowers in terminal and upper axillary panicles, c 20 cm long, bracts 1.5-2.5 mm long, lanceolate, caducous. Calyx 3 mm long, white spotted, 5-toothed, teeth triangular, 0.8-1 mm long. Corolla 1 cm purple to violet, hairy inside at the insertion of the stamens, tube c 5 mm long, upper lip 2 mm long. Filaments hairy at base. Ovary glabrous, style glabrous, stigma forked. Drupes c 4 mm across, black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine plants are used as astringent, cephalic, stomachic and  anthelmintic. It is also useful in eye diseases, consumption, inflammations, expectorant lecuderma, enlargement of spleen, bronchits, asthma, and painful teething of childrens. Roots are also used against snake-venome and scorpion sting
Parts used: .Leaf-tonic, antirheumatic, antigonorrhoeic, in catarrh, headache for soaking various metallic powders for making pills, removes foetid discharges and worms from ulcers; oil-applied to sinuses and scrofulous sores.  Plant – astringent, cephalic, stomachic, promotes growth of hair in diseases of eye, consumption, inflammations, leucoderma, enlargement of spleen, bronchitis, asthma, biliousness, painful teething of children; root-tonic, febrifuge, antirheumatic, diuretic, expectorant, in dyspepsia as a demulcent in dysentery; flower-astringent, antidiarrhoeic, febrifuge, in liver complaints, plant, root, leaf and fruit-vermifuge.


Chemistry:
Seeds gave hydrocarbon –n- tritriacontane, n-hentriacontane, n-pentatriacontane and n-nonacosane.  Other constituents of seeds are β-sitosterol, p-hydroxybenzoic acid and 5-oxyisophthalic acid.  Leaves yielded an alkaloid-nishindine, flavonoids –5- hydroxy –3, 6, 7, 3, 4’-pentameth – oxyflavone and casticin, irridoid glycosides – aucubin, angusid and 2-p-hydroxy benzoyl mussaenosidic acid, an essential oil.  Other constituents of leaves are vitamin C, carotene, gluco-nonitol, p-hydroxybenzoic acid, 5-oxy-isophthalic acid, 3, 4-dioxybenzoic acid and sitosterol.  Stem-bark contains a fatty acid, β-Sitosterol, vanillic acid, P-hydroxy benzoic acid and luteolin presence of flavonoid c-glycoside is also indicated.

·            3, 4-dihydroxybenzoic acid also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 – 1979).

·            Vanillic and p-0hydroxybenzoic acids and luteolin isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970 –1979).

·            Two new leucoanthyocyanidins isolated from stem bark and their structures determined as 6, 8-di-O-methylleucodelphinidin and 3’,4’’-di-O-methylleucocyanindin-7-O-rhamnoglucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970– 1979).

·            Two new favonoids -6-C-glucosyl-5-O-rhamnopyranosyltrimethyxywogonin (I) and acerosin-5-O-glucoside monoacetate (II)-isolated from bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·            a-pinene (0.16), limonene (2.84), camphor (0.86), b-phellandrene (17.0), methylheptenone (3.47), p-cymene (0.76), linalool (2.27), camphor (0.96), 4-terpineol (5.79), citral (1.35), caryophyllene oxide (0.33), caryophyllene (2.08), terpinyl acetate (0.590, benzaldehyde (0.18), and cinnamaldehyde (10.95%) identified in essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 –1984).

·             New compounds-3,6,7,3’, 4’,-pentamethoxyflavone-5-O-glucopyranosylrhamnoside, vitexin caffeate and 4’-methoxymyricetin-3-O-[4’’-O-b-D-galactosyl]-b-D-glaactopyranoside – isolated  from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980–1984).

·             Roots contained hentriacontane, b-sitosterol, b-sitosterol acetate and stigmasterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 –1984).

·            Essential oil (0.42%) from leaves yielded a-pinene, camphene, caryophyllene, citral and two unidentified compounds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980–1984).

·            A new iridoid-2’-p-hydroxybenzoylmussaenosidic acid (negundoside)-isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 – 1984).

·            Isolation and structure elucidation of a new furanoeremophilane (III). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980 –1984).

·            6’-p-hydroxybenzoylmusaenosidic acid isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980–1984).

·            Studies on the components of essential oils. III. Studies of chemical constituents of the essential oil from Rhododendron racemosum Franch. (I) Comparison of the constituents of Vitex negundo L. (Fang HJ et. al., 1980).

·            Petrol ether (PE) and n-butanol (BE) extracts of roots produced moderate CNS depression; PE prolonged pentobarbitone sleep and also protected mice against pentylenetetrazole convulsions; chloroform extract (CHE) shortened diazaepam sleep; BE and ethanol extracts produced marked antiparkinsonian effect; CHE markedly suppressed carrageenin pedal oedema in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·            Isolation of 5-hydroxy-3, 6, 7, 3’, 4’-pentamethoxyflavone and 3, 5-dihydroxy-6, 7, 3’, 4’-tetramethoxyflavonol from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·            Structures of 5, 4’-dihydroxy-7, 8, 3’, 5’-tetramethoxyflavone, isomeric 5, 3’-dihydroxy-7,8,4’-trimethoxyflavanone and 5,3’-dihydroxy-6,7,4’-trimethoxyflavanone confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·            The chemical constituents of the essential oils obtained from the leaves of Vitex negundo.  Vitex negundo was analysed by GC-MS. Forty compounds including alpha-pinene, linalool, terpinyl acetate, beta-caryophyllene and caryophyllene oxide, etc. were identified. Their percentages in oils were given. (Pan et. al., 1989).

·            Purified fraction of leaf oil showed repellent activity against Aedes aegypti, giving protection from mosquito bites for 1-3 hr. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·            Isolation and structure determination of a new diterpene-5b-hydro-8, 11, 13-abietatrien-6a-ol (I); lanostan-8, 25-dien-3b-ol and artemetin also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·             A new lignan isolated from seeds and characterized as 6-hydroxy-4-(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)-3-hydroxymethyl-7-methoxy-3, 4-dihydro-2-hydroxymethyl-7-methoxy-3, 4-dihydro-2-naphthaldehyde. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·            The CHCl3 extract of the defatted seeds of Vitex negundo exhibited anti-inflammatory activity and yielded four triterpenoids: 3 beta-acetoxyolean-12-en-27-oic acid [1], 2 alpha, 3 alpha-dihydroxyoleana-5, 12-dien-28-oic acid [2], 2 beta, 3 alpha-diacetoxyoleana-5, 12-dien-28-oic acid [3], and 2 alpha, 3 beta-diacetoxy-18-hydroxyoleana-5, 12-dien-28-oic acid [5]. This is the first report of the isolation of compounds 2, 3, and 5 from a natural source. (Chawla et. al., 1992).

·            The essential oil of the leaf of V. negundo growing wild near Hyderabad has been analysed by capillary GC and GC/MS. The essential oil of the leaf was obtained by hdyrodistillation of the shade-dried leaves in a yield of 0.18 percent. The major component of the oil were sabinene (11.19 %), p-cymene (2.49 percent), beta-phellandrene (1.28 percent), gamma-terpinene (1.62%), terpinen-4-ol (13.25 %) b-caryophyllene (13.65 %), globulol (17.27 %) viridiflorol (1.10 %) etc. (Mallavarapu et al, 1994) MAPA, 1995, 9504-2555.

·            Four iridoids-2’-p-hydroxybenzoyl mussaenosidic acid, 6-p-hydroxybenzoyl mussaenosidic acid, agnuside and lagundinin-were identified in the pharmacologically active fraction of the leaves of V. negundo. (Dayrit and Lagurin 1994) MAPA, 1995, 9505-3151.

·            Volatile constituents of V. negundo leaves were analysed by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry and showed the presence of sixty-six compounds. The main compounds are viridiflorol (19.55 %), b-caryophyllene (16.59 %), sabinene (12.07 %), 4-terpineol (9.65 %), gamma-terpinene (2.21 %), caryophyllene oxide (1.75 %), 1-oceten-3-ol (1.59 %) and globulol (1.05 %). Viridiflorol is being reported for the first time in the oil of Vitex species. (Singh et al, 1999). MAPA, 2000, 2000-05-3158.

·            The chemical constituents of the essential oil of Vitex negundo have been analyzed by GC-MS. 35 components of the essential oil were identified. The major constituents are caryophyllene (33.01%), eucalyptol (13.30%), alloaroma dendrene (6.58%) and b-farnesene (5.56%). (Chen et al., 1999) CA, 1999, 131:254898c.

·            The antioxidant compound in Vitex negundo L. seeds was detd. by UV, 1H NMR, and 13C NMR.  The main compound is flavone with chalcone skeleton, 2 methoxy groups connected with the benzene ring. (Zheng and Luo, 1999a). CA 1999, 131:240448u.

·            The extract of seed of V. negundo was separated by chromatography & purified to obtain 9 compounds of which 6 were shown to have antioxidative activity and identified as flqvanoids. (Zheng et al., 1999b). CA. 1999, 132: 47525j.

·            A chloroform extract of air-dried leaves of V. negundo afforded vitexilactone and casticin after silica gel chromatography. Their structures were elucidated by extensive 1D and 2 D NMR spectroscopy. (Ragasa et al, 1999) CA 1999, 132: 163504a.

·            The chloroform fraction of the aqueous extract of the fresh leaves of Vitex negundo yielded a pure compound, rotundial (C9H12O2), which has been isolated shown potent mosquito repellent activity. (Amancharla et. al., 1999). CA. 131: 140824v.

·            The chemical profile of the volatile oils extracted from the leaves of V. negundo occurring in China, India and Philippines have been reviewed, delta-Elemene, beta-eudesmol, camphor, camphene, carene, beta-caryophyllene, caryophyllene oxide, 1.8-cineole, glublol, linalool, 1-oceten-3-ol, alpha-pinene, sabinene, terpinyl acetate, 4-terpineol, gamma-terpinene, and viridiflorol were found to be the major constituents of the oil. The chief constituents of the oil from these countries are beta-caryophyllene, viridiflorol and beta-eudesmol. A number of other minor constituents have also been identified. (Dayal and Singh, 2000). CA.135:170458

·             Thermogravimetric analysis of lignins isolated from Vitex negundo. (Kosankar et. al., 2001).

·            Two pentacyclic triterpenoids, betulinic acid (3beta-hydroxylup-20-(29)-en-28-oic acid) (3), and ursolic acid (2beta-hydroxyurs-12-en-28-oic acid) (4), were isolated from leaves of Vitex negundo L. along with three other compounds; an aliphatic alcohol n-hentriacontanol (1), beta-sitosterol (2) and p-hydroxybenzoic acid (5).  (Chandramu et. al., 2003).

Pharmacology:

·            Alcoholic extract of the seeds of Vitex negundo Linn. was obtained by cold maceration. A dose of 250 mg/kg (1/6 of LD50) of the extract was selected to study the hepatoprotective action against carbon tetrachloride-induced liver damage. The extract was found to be effective in preventing liver damage which was evident by morphological, biochemical and functional parameters. (Avadhoot and Rana, 1991).

·            A dose related inhibition of 1° and 2° lesions induced by the adjuvant was observed in a suspension of dried powder leaves of V. negundo for antiarthritic activity in rats. (Tamhankar and Saraf, 1994). MAPA, 1995, 9501-0266.

·            V. negundo is used in an Unani polyherbal formulation “Jigrine” which was tested for Hepatoprotective activity.  The activity of jigrine was compared with Liv 52, a known Ayurvedic hepatoprotective formulation. (Kapur et. al., 1994) MAPA 1995, 9501-0209.

·            Ethanolic extract of Vitex negundo inhibits the release of histamine and products of arachidonic acid metabolism. (Nair and Saraf, 1995)

·            Effective treatment for patients having low sperm count by V. negundo. (Dutta and Chakravarty, 1995) MAPA 1996, 9602-0732.

·            Vitex negundo used as ophthalmic drug for night blindness, cataract, conjunctivitis, eye sores and improvement of eye sight. (Sahoo, 1995).

·            Vitex negundo is used for curing rheumatism, arthritis and muscular strains. (Sahoo and Mudgal, 1995).

·            The active fraction of the Vitex negundo extract was separated by column chromatographic technique, and the acute toxicity studies were done using Swiss albino mice of either sex. Three dose levels (125, 167, 250 mg/kg b.w. dried leaves extract of V. negundo were chosen to study analgesic, anti-convulsant  and sedative-hypnotic activities using Swiss albino mice of  either sex. The leaf extract potentiated the sedative-hypnotic effect of pentobarbitone and diazepam significantly (P.001) in a dose dependent manner, while it did not produce sleep in mice itself. The extract showed significant analgesic activity and prolonged the analgesia produced by morphine and pethidine, whereas, the anti-convulsant activity against strychnine and leptazole was found only at high dose levels (250 mg/kg b.w.). (Gupta et al, 1997). MAPA, 1998, 9801-0161.

·            Vitex negundo showed significant antibacterial activity against gram negative bacteria. (Perumal Samy et. al., 1998).

·            Methanolic extract (ME) of V. negundo potentiated significantly the sleeping time induced by pentobarbitone sodium, diazepam and chlorpromazine in mice. ME exhibited analgesic properties and potentiated analgesia induced by morphine and pethidine. ME also showed significant protection against strychnine and leptazole induced convulsions. The results suggest that ME exhits CNS depressant activity in a dose dependent manner. (Gupta et al, 1999) MAPA, 1999, 9904-2632.

·            Analgesic & anti-inflammatory activity of the leaf extract of V. negundo at a dose of 500 & 1000 mg/kg was studied in mice. (Telang et al, 1999). MAPA, 1999, 9906-3783.

·            Ethanolic extract of roots of Vitex negundo exhibited hepatoprotective activity of Vitex negundo. (Srinivas et al., 1999)

·            The Juanxiao Tablet (JXT) has the action of anti-asthma, expectorant, antisepsis and hypoxia tolerance enhancement, its LD50 was (40.48 +/-5.17) g/kg. (Hong et. al., 1999).

·            Methanolic extract (ME) of the leaves of Vitex negundo potentiated significantly the sleeping time induced by pentobarbitone sodium, diazepam and chlorpromazine in mice. ME posseses analgesic properties and potentiated analgesia induced by morphine and pethidine. ME also showed significant protection strychnine and leptazole induced convulsions. The results suggest that ME exhibits CNS depressant activity in a dose dependent manner. (Gupta et. al., 1999).

·            Effect of solvent residues of Vitex negundo L. leaves (0.5 and 1%) was studied on egg laying and adult emergence of Callosobruchus maculates Fab. And on percentage of larval parasitism by Dinarmus vagabundus. Cowpea seeds treated with methanol and aqueous extract of Vitex, at these concentrations (0.5 and 1%), significantly reduced the number of eggs and emergence of F1 adults of C. maculates. Vitex negundo extracts did not affect the percentage of parasitism by D. vagabundus on C. maculates grubs. (Raja et. al., 2000).

·            The anti-inflammatory activity of Vitex negundo on acute inflammatory reaction induced by carrageenin oedema in rats. Vitex negundo also exhibited anti-inflammatory activity on carrageenin induced rat paw oedema which was recorded as 45, 60 and 44 percent at 2nd, 3rd and 4th hours of treatment, respectively. Antiinflammatory activity of Vitex negundo is mediated through histamine and 5-HT which is further maintained during delayed phase. (Srivastava and Sahni, 2000) MAPA, 2000, 2001-01-0295.

·            Anti-ulcerogenic activity of Vitex negundo on piroxican induced gastric ulceration was observed. (Gaithani et. al., 2000).

·            The effect Vitex negundo on gastric ulceration in albino rats induced by NSAIDS like piroxicam and aspirin were investigated. (Sahni et. al., 2000-2001) MAPA 2001, 2001-04-1975.

·            The anti-inflammatory activity of the indigenous medicinal plant- Vitex negundo on acute inflammatory reaction induced by carrageenin oedema in rats was studied. The role of inflammatory mediators in anti-inflammatory activity of Vitex negundo and the possible mode of action on acute inflammatory condition was also observed. (Srivastava et. al., 2000-2001) MAPA, 2001, 2001-01-0295.

·            Vitex negundo was studied for the anti-radical and antilipoperoxidative effect by Srilankan traditional medicinal practitioner for cardi- practitioner. (Munasinghe et. al., 2001).

·            Vitex negundo was administerd in dose of 1000 mg/kg ip. For seven days after implantation of cotton pellet in rats. Vitex negundo exhibited significant (P<0.001) anti-inflammatory activity. Promethazine and cyproheptadine given along with Vitex negundo significantly protentiated the anti-inflammatory activity of Vitex negundo. (Gaidhani et. al., 2002).

·            Vitex negundo showed antifeedant activity against the larvae of an agricultural pest, the castor semilooper, in a no-choice laboratory assay and their antibacterial activity against Bacillus subtilis and Escherichia coli, by the paper disc method, were used. Ursolic acid (4) showed more effective antifeedant activity than the betulinic acid (3). However, both these compounds have shown a very mild antibacterial activity. The other three compounds; n-hentriacontanol (1), beta-sitosterol (2) and p-hydroxybenzoic acid (5) have shown a little antifeedant activity the larvae and did not show any antibacterial activity. (Chandramu et. al., 2003).

 

Ethnobotany:

·            Mosquito repellent activity of oils from Vitex negundo Linn. leaves. Hebbalkar et. al., 1992).

·            Larvicidal activity of partially purified extracts of leaves of Vitex negundo on different ninstars of Culex quinquefasciatus and Anopheles stephensi was estimated. Petroleum ether (PE): Ethyl acetate (EA) 3:1 fraction of Vitex negundo, It inflicted considerable larval mortality and interfered with pupal-adult metamorphosis. At very low concentration the active fractions of these plant extracts extended the duration of the various larval instars and of pupation. In general, I and II instar were more susceptible to the active fractions. Species and stage specific differences in the susceptibility of the mosquitoes to the active fractions of the plant extracts were observed. (Pushpalatha et. al., 1995).

·            Vitex negundo the ingredients of oil Varanashodhan used for as anti-dandruff. (Suru and Kulkarni, 1995).

·            Smoke of leaves of Vitex negundo is more toxic towards filarial vector mosquito. (Culex quinquefasciatus, than synthetic mosquito mats). (Pandian et. al., 1995).

·            Antifungal activities of extracts of Vitex negundo were tested against Ceratocystis paradoxa which causes soft rot of pineapples. Antifungal activities of extract of Xanthium strumarium was the most effective followed by Allium sativum. (Damayanti et. al., 1996).
