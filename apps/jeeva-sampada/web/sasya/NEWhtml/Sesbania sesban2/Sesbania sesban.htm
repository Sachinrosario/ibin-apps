HABIT & HABITAT

Cultivated throughout the plains of India upto an alt. of 1,200 m of pantropical.
Sesban can grow under waterlogged condition and acid soils.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Soft-wooded, quick growing, short-lived shrub, 1.8-6m high.
Leaves 7.5-15 cm long, paripinnate, leaflets 8-20 pairs, linear-oblong, glabrous entire, mucronate to acuminate, 6.0-25.0m x 2.5-6.0m.
Flowers yellow or yellow spotted red to purple or with standard petals coloured purple or brown form outside, in 8-10 flowered, lax, axillary racemes, 2.5-14.0 cm. long.
Pods 12.5-22.5 cm x 0.25 - 0.37 cm.
Pendulous, weak, distinctly torulose, twisted, sharply beaked, 20-30 seeded and septate.

Cultivation aspect:
Seedling establishment :

Sesban is propagated through seeds. When grown as a shade plant in ochards, it is sown parallel to the row of young orchard plants about a foot from their periphery.
As a support to sugarcane, sesban is planted in equidistant rows at the same time as the sugarcane sets are planted and allowed to grow up with it.

Produce and Products:
As green manure crop.
Leaves - eaten in Thailand, flowers eaten in Bihar.
Bark-fiber-ropes. fiber as an av. of 9.1-12.3 N diameter.
Pith - soft pith plaited into mats in Assam.
Wood - for poles in Deccan; toys in Burma.
Plant yields an insecticide, also used in making ink.
Seeds - stimulant, emmenagogue and astringent, useful in checking diarrhoea and reducing enlargement of the spleen. Seed ointment for curing itches and skin eruptions.
Gingelly oil (in which flowers are boiled) used as bath oil for patients suffering from coryza or nasal catarah.


Chemistry:
Sesban seed protein is of poor quality because of the presence of canavanine, which is competitive inhibitor of arginine.

Miscellaneous Information

 Cottage ornament.
Leaves and flowers used in religious offering.
As a shade tree for turmeric, tea, sweet orange and mandarin and S. W. Indies and for cotton in Tamil.Nadu.
As hedge in N. India and paddy field bunds in Tamil.Nadu.
Wind break for betel vines and grape vines in Baroda and Assam.
