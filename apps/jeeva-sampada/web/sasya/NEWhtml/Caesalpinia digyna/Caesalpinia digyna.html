 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Large, scandent prickly shrub or small tree, up to 10 m high; it is found to occur in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Tripura, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu. Srilanka, Nepal, Myanmar, Indo-china, Thailand, Sumatra, Java, Malaya islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Branches minutely velvety; branchlet mostly glossy. Leaves bipinnate, 15-23 cm long, pinni 5-8 pairs, rachis sparingly prickly. Leaflets opposite, 6-10 pairs, unequally oblong, 0.5-1.1 x 0.2-0.4 cm, oblique at base, sessile, glabrous, membranous. Flowers in long, glabrous raceme, yellow, often reddish at the base. Filaments white, wooly. Pods 2.5-5 cm long, torulose, oblong, 1-3 seeded, coriaceous, dehiscing in 2-valves. Seeds 1-3, oblong, c 1.0-1.2 cm, black.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Sessile in dense spicate racemes ; Flower: clothed with soft brown, pubescence, stout more or less grooved ; Calyx: densely puberulous, scarlet, segments oblong or sub-spathulate, obtuse ; Corolla: Petals 5, inserted at the top of calyx tube, obovate-spathulate, subequal, reticulately veined, dark-orange ;Androecium: Stamens 10, free, equaling the petals, alternately long and short; filament flattened and densely pilose at the base  ; Gynoecium: Ovary densely pubescent, ovules 4-6,  style as long as fiaments and the petals, stigma 2-lobed and oblique; Fruit: Pods, linear-oblong, swollen, swollen, above the seeds and constricted between them, with thickened surtures ; Seed: 3-4, obovate-oblong, transverse, thick, bony.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Root is astringent and useful in sores externally and also diabetes.
Chemistry:

Caesalpinine C (fig. 2) isolated and its crystal structure determined by X-ray studes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Tanin was extracted from Caesalpinia digyna and quantified as 58.5%. (Ye et al, 1999).
