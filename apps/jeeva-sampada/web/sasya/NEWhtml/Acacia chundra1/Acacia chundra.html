HABIT & HABITAT
Large shrubs or trees, 2-8m tall found Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:


Uses:
Bark and leaves are used as medicine in abscess. Bark is used in ulcer, gum and toothache. The whole plant is used in leucoderma (Jain, 1991).
Chemistry:

  • Degraded A. chundra gum was prepared by autohydrolysis of the purified gum, thus removing the L-rhamnose and L-arabinose residues. The degraded gum was then methylated first with Me2SO4, then with Ag2O and MeI. Hydrolysis of the resulting Me ester of the methylylated degraded polysaccharide yielded, in 6:1:3:4 molar ratio, 2,3,4-tri-, 2,4,6-tri-, and 2,4-di-O-methyl-D-galactose and 2,3,4-tri-O-methyl-D-glucuronic acid. The degraded gum was reducing in nature, had a mol. wt. of 570, and was composed of average recurring units comprising ten D-galactose residues and four D-glucuronic acid. On the basis of the Me derives, identified, and their molecular proportions, a tentative structure was proposed for the degraded gum. (Mukherjee and Shrivastava, 1959)C. A. 1960, 54, 22386 b.
  • A. chundra gum was obtained as a light buff colored, non-reducing powder by repeated precipitation from its acidified aqueous solution with EtOH. On heating this aqueous solution for about 80 hrs, L-rhamnose, L-arabinose, D-galactose and traces of xylose were obtained. The Ba salt of the degraded gum obtained by MeOH extraction of autohydrolyzed solution on heating for 35 hrs with 0.1 N H2SO4, gave chiefly D-galactose with traces of L-arabinose. While the Ba salt of insoluble MeOH extraction of the aldobiouronic acid on hydrolysis with N H2SO4 gave D-galactose and the MeOH insoluble Ba salt with the calculated amount of H2SO4 gave paper chromatogram indicating the presence of glucuronic acid. Methanolysis of the aldobiouronic acid followed by acid hydrolysis of the methylated product, together with the specific rotation of the methylated product ([a]3D5 – 19.6°) established the structure of the aldobiouronic acid as  6-O-b-D-glucuronopyranosyl-D-galactose, the  same acid that is found in other species of this genus. The degraded gum obtained on autohydrolysis lost the labile sugar residues attached glycosidically to the main structure; it was reducing and consisted of a repeating unit of 10 D-galactose and 4-glucuronic acid. It is concluded that the galactose units of the degraded gum giving rise to the di-Me derivative were linked through C-1, C-3 and C-6 and branching occurring at these galactose units; the 1 that is linked through C-1 and C-3 gave rise to the 2,4,6-tri-Me derivative and those that are linked through C-1 and C-6 gave rise to the 2,3,4-tri-Me derivative The fully methylated glucuronic units were linked through C-1 only to the main structure, and formed the ends. (Shrivastava, 1962), C. A. 1963, 58, 6911f.
