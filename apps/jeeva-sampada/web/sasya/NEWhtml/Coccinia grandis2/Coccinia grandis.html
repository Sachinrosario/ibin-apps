HABIT & HABITAT

Availability :  
 Cultivated in Assam, Bihar, Orissa, West Bengal, Maharashtra, Andhrapradesh and Tamil Nadu, wild in many parts of India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Scandent, perennial, glabrous, much branched, dioecious herb. Tendrils slender, striate, simple. Leaves 5-10 cm long, bright green above, palmately 5-nerved, base cordate, sometimes deeply 5-lobed; lobes broad, acute or obtuse at apex, more or less sinuate-toothed. Flowers ebracteate, white. Male flowers: 1.5-5 cm long; calyx tube campanulate; corolla papillosely hairy within; pistillode 0. Female flowers: staminodes 3, subulate; stigma densely papillose. Fruits ellipsoid, both ends rounded, green with white streaks when unripe, scarlet when ripe. Seeds yellowish, ovoid.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts used: Leaf and root - antidiabetic; leaf - in eruptions of skin: plant-internally antigonorrhoeic; bark-cathartic.
Uses:  Root of sweet variety is cooling. It stops vomiting, urinary losses, burning of head and feets. It is used in uterine discharges. Leaves are astringent to the bowels. Flowers cure itching and jaundice. Fruit cures leprosy, burning sensation of body, bronchitis, asthma, consumption, jaundice and blood diseases. Fruit of bitter variety cure biliousness, foul breath, bad taste, bronchitis, leprosy, anaemia, inflammation and blood diseases. In Unani system of medicine fruit allays thirst. It is used in biliousness and blood diseases.
Chemistry:
b-amyrin, lupeol and a bitter glycoside containing curcurbitacin B, from fruit. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Cephalandrol, mp 81°C, tritriacontane, b-itosterol, cephalandrine A and cephalandrine B from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Stigmast-7-en-3-one (fig. 1), mp 154°C, from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

b-sitosterol and taraxerol from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Seed fat rich in palmitic (16.3%), oleic (22.4%) and linoleic (58.6%) acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

b-carotene, lycopene, cryptoxanthin and apo-6’-lycopenal obtained form fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Fruits afforded taraxerone, taraxerol and 24(R)-24-ethylcholest-5-en-3b-ol glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

A flavanoid glycoside ombuin 3-O-arabinofuranoside was isolated from roots of Coccinia indica. (Vaishnav and Gupta, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 95705v.

The root of Coccinia indica contain 3-O-b-(a-L-arabinopyranosyl)-b-hydroxylup-20(29)-ene-28-oic acid. (Vaishnav and Gupta, 1995). C.A. 1996, 124, 284325n. Aerial part gave heptacosane, cephalandrol, tritiacontane, b-sitosterol, alkaloids-cephalandrines A and B, fruits yielded b-amyrin and its acepate, lupeol and cucurbitacin.
NBPGR: Aerial part gave heptacosane, cephalandrol, tritiacontane, b-sitosterol, alkaloids-cephalandrines A and B, fruits yielded b-amyrin and its acepate, lupeol and cucurbitacin.

Pharmacology:

An orally effective hypoglycaemic principle, comparable to tolbutamide, isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Clinical evaluation of green leaves extract at a dose of 10.0 g/day showed significant inhibition of infective hepatitis starting from third or fourth day of treatment. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1989)b.

The hypoglycemic effect of C. indica is partly due to the repression of the key gluconeogenic enzyme glucose-6-phosphatase. (Hossain et al., 1992).

The pectin isolated from the fruit administration resulted in a significant reduction in blood glucose and an increase in the liver glycogen. Glycogen synthetase activity was highly significant. Incorporation of labelled glucose into hepatic glycogen was also found to be higher. A significant reduction in phosphorylase activity was noted in the pectin-administered groups. (Kumar et al., 1993).

Studies on a chitooligosaccharide-specific lectin from Coccinia indica. Thermodynamics and kinetics of umbelliferyl glycoside binding. (Sanadi and Surolia, 1994).

Pectin from Coccinia showed highly significant hypolipidemic activity in albino rats. (Kumar et al, 1997). C.A. 1997, 127, 49490q.

Ingredients present in the extract of C. indica, act like insulin, correcting the elevated enzymes G-6-p (ase), LDH in glycolytic pathway and restore the LPL activity in lypolytic pathway with the control of hyperglycemia in diabetes. (Kamble et al .,1998)

Hepatoprotective effect of the ethanolic extract of Coccinia indica fruits in rats treated with carbon tetrachloride. (Mohana Rao et al, 2003).

Administration of Coccinia indica  extract for 45 days to STZ diabetic rats significantly reduced the accumulation and cross-linking of collagen. The effects of C. indica (collagen content 23.87 +/- 1.52 mg/100 mg tissue (t value = 6.80), extent of cross-linking 0.893 +/- 0.072 mg hydroxyproline/100 mg tissue (t value = 9.0)) were comparable with those of glibenclamide (collagen content 26.18 +/- 1.65 mg/100 mg tissue (t value = 4.58), extent of cross-linking 0.787 +/- 0.057 mg hydroxyproline/100 mg tissue (t value = 7.1)), a reference drug. (Venkateswaran et al., 2003)
