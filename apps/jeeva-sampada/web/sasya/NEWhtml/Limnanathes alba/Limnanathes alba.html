Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Meadowfoam is an erect low growing winter annual herb with one or more branches arising from the base and grows to a height of 10 to 30 cm in. The natural habitat of meadowfoam is 35° to 45° N latitude.

Distribution: Native to Sierra foothills and adjacent rolling plains from Sacramento to Chico, California.

(1, 7,12,14)

Availability :  

Status :Cultivated

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

Meadowfoam is an annual herbs; stems commonly erect or ascending, 10–30 cm tall; leaves up to 10 cm long. It has a shallow fibrous root system that allows for easy transplanting at any stage of growth. Leaves are pinnately dissected. (1, 2 6, 7,12)

Back to Top

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY

Flowers are regular, perfect, and usually conspicuous on axillary peduncles. The flowers are alternate, petioled, without stipules, dissected or pinnate with 5–9 segments, segments ovate, simple, 3-lobed or 3-parted; flowers pentamerous (5), crateriform to campanulate, on peduncles up to 10 cm long; sepals lanceolate to ovate, acuminate, 7–8 mm long, glabrous to densely villous, not accrescent; petals obovate, truncate to obcordate, 10–15 mm long, white, some aging lilac-purple or pink at tips, a few scattered long hairs from veins and 2 rows or hairs at base; stamens 5–6 mm long, anthers 2 mm long; pistil 5–6 mm long. (3,6,7)

Breeding System :

L. alba is self-compatible and predominantly allogamous. Although naturally self-pollinated progeny are found in certain wild L. alba ssp. versicolor populations, Meadowfoam is not self pollinating because the male reproductive organs mature before the female organs are mature (pollen is released from the anthers before the stigma of the flower is receptive). This plant adaptation is common for enhancing cross pollination. The development of self pollinating varieties should increase yield potential.

The versatility of the mating system of meadowfoam means that diverse breeding methods and schemes can be employed. Several breeding methods are developed: recurrent, mass and family selection and backcross breeding in insect-pollinated populations and backcross, pedigree, single seed descent, and bulk breeding in crosses between self- and cross-pollinated lines. And different breeding practices used are recurrent mass, half-sib family, and S1 family selection. The genetics of self-pollination are poorly understood in meadowfoam. Molecular breeding experiments are underway in laboratories to dissect the genetics of self-pollination. Two inter-fertile Limnanthes alba subspecies have been described (L. alba ssp. alba and L. alba ssp. versicolor). Partially fertile inter-specific hybrids can be made between L. alba and secondary gene pool species of Section Inflexae (e.g., L. gracilis and L. floccosa). Hybrids between L. alba and tertiary gene pool species of Section Reflexae species (e.g., L. douglasii) are sterile. The genetic diversity of several related species can be accessed through inter-specific crosses. (3,15)



Phenology :

Meadowfoam thrives in cool weather (growing temperatures between 4° and 16°C), but is typically killed by ambient air temperatures below 5°C for prolonged periods (more than four days). (2,7)

Pollinators:

Meadowfoam requires insect pollination (Honeybee) to set seed. Two or three colonies of bees per acre of meadowfoam are needed for adequate pollination. Cool, wet, or windy weather during flowering limits the activity of pollinators and therefore reduces the number of fertilized flowers. (3)

Seed Biology :

Nutlets are obovoid, 3–4 mm long, smooth or wrinkled to very rough with broad ridges. (6)

Seedling Germination :

Secondary dormancy of the seed occurs at temperatures over 60 oF when the seed is imbibing water. It also appears that light may be a factor in initiating dormancy. Seed sown on the soil surface shows a higher level of secondary dormancy than seed that is covered with soil. The crop is Propagated from seed. Experimental results shows, seeds germinated at 4.5°C, gave poor germination at 21°C, and went dormant at 26.5°C. (2)

Cultivation:

Climate: The long wet growing season and dry harvest season are ideal for meadowfoam. Meadowfoam thrives in cool weather ( growing temperatures between 4° and 16°C ), but is typically killed by ambient air temperatures below 5°C for prolonged periods of more than four days. The plant can tolerate annual precipitation of 7 to 11 dm, annual temperature of 12 to 19°C.

Soil: Meadowfoam grows well on most soil types, however sandy soils with low water holding capacity are less favorable under dry conditions. And pH of 5.6 to 6. However, it does well on soils with pH 6.2, especially on slopes and in cultivated fields.

Cultural Practices:

Seedbed Preparation: The seedbed should be moderately fine to allow for uniform seed placement.

Seeding Date: It should be planted during the cooler months, otherwise their is the problem of seed dormancy.

Method and Rate of Seeding: Plant densities of 3 to 4 plants/square ft have resulted in the highest yields.

Fertility and Lime Requirements: Soil pH should range between 5.5 and 6.0, with fertilizer requirements of 40 to 60 lb N/acre, 20 lb P2O5/acre.

Weed Control: Competition from weeds can severely reduce the yield of meadowfoam. Two herbicides; propachlor at 4 lb/acre (broad spectrum) and diclofop at 1 lb/acre (post-emergent, grass control) have been shown to be effective for weed control in meadowfoam fields but, are not registered for use at this time.

  1. Mechanical: Begin with a weed free seedbed and avoid fields known to have a wild mustard problem.

  2. Chemical: No herbicides have been approved for use at this time.

(1,3,6,7)

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests: (7)

Insect pests in association with meadowfoam plants include the spotted cucumber beetle, nitidulid beetle, seed bug, and seed-feeding Carabidae beetle.

Diseases: (7)

  1. The most serious pest of meadowfoam is Scaptomyza, a fruit fly that bores into developing buds and crowns, thereby destroying seed or whole plants.

  2. Botrytis cinerea affects stems, leaves, and flowers and results in shriveled seed and reduced yields. Fungicide applied at 40 to 50% bloom has been shown to be effective, however no fungicides are currently approved for commercial use.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used and Uses

Seed:

  • Source of oil, possibly as replacement for spermwhale oil.

  • Meal from seeds may find use as feed component.

  • A versatile raw material, Meadowfoam oil has many potential applications in cosmetics, lubricants, waxes, polymers, surfactants, water repellents, and in textile and leather manufacturing.

  • It has the added benefit of enhancing the properties of other oils when added to them. Less expensive oils can be mixed with without the loss of the qualities of either oil. In fact, it is believed to be more oxidatively stable than any other vegetable oil known, thus it can increase the stability of the oils it is added to.

(1,5,7,8,12)

Harvesting Practices

The crop is cut when 90% of the seeds are mature and the stems are greenish-yellow. Windrowing early in the morning with a dew present helps to prevent shattering. In drier seasons and sites, plants may wither away in flower without seed formation. Shattering may cause 16–54% seed loss in Limnanthes alba. Meadowfoam is allowed to dry in the swath for 7 to 10 days or to a seed moisture content of 12 to 16%. Threshing is more efficient when the moisture content of the seed and other plant material is low. (7)

Produce and Products

Wax: Meadowfoam oil can be chemically transformed into a liquid wax ester that is a substitute for sperm whale oil and jojoba oil. Meadowfoam oil can also be converted to a light colored premium grade solid wax, a sulfur polymer factice potentially valuable to the rubber industry, or used as a lubricant, detergent or plasticizer.

Meadow foam oil: These fatty acids are unique due to very high levels of mono-unsaturation and very low levels of poly-unsaturation. These characteristics make meadowfoam oil very stable, even when heated or exposed to air.These characters has stimulated interest in its use in personal care products such as cosmetics and toiletries.

Applications in cosmetics:

  • When added to cosmetics and creams, Meadowfoam Seed Oil re-moisturizes the skin and hair in ways no other oil can.

  • It adds vitality to hand creams and hair creams by forming a membrane to prevent moisture loss and provides an excellent cushion feeling on the skin.

  • In shampoos and soaps, it helps in adding shine and moisture to hair scalp. It provides natural luster and emollient feeling with hair care products.

  • In lipsticks and lip balms, it helps revitalize dry, cracked lips and keeps them moist for longer.

  • As a liquid with good surface tension, it is conducive to good spread on the skin making an excellent ingredient for all cleaning products, massage creams, and multiple type skin products.

  • It contributes high adhesion onto the lip and skin when used in lipsticks and makeup products.

  • It provides excellent substantivity and lubricity for a silky smooth feeling and appearance.

  • It is a superior color enhancer as a binder in powder products and blended in liquid makeup applications, as well as lipsticks, color makeups, hair colors (permanent or semipermanent), and color shampoos.

Meal: After the oil is removed by crushing the seed and utilizing a solvent extraction process, the remaining meal may be used as a feed source. Meadowfoam meal fed to beef cattle at levels up to 25% of total intake had no negative impact on weight gain. Use of the meal for other livestock may require steam cooking or using a lower percentage of meal in the total feed supply. Further research in this area is necessary.

(6,7,10,21,24)

Seed oil characteristics:

  • Meadowfoam (Limnanthes alba) is an alternative oil seed crop that is an excellent source of C20 and C22 fatty acids. No other seed oil is known to have as high concentration (>90%) of total fatty acids of chain length greater than C18. The seed of meadowfoam can be crushed to yield an oil high in eicosenoic acid (20:1) used for industrial processes. First, over 95% of its acyl groups are longer than C18; secondly, about 90% of these fatty acids have double bonds in the delta-5 position; and thirdly, there are essentially no polyunsaturates. Along with the 60% cis-5-eicosenoic acid, the 8% cis-5-docosenoic acid, and the 10% erucic acid.

Processing Techniques

Natural meadowfoam oil is in the form of triglycerides. However, they can been converted to liquid wax-esters (C:40-C:44 wax esters similar to those of jojoba), by reduction of fatty acids to alcohols and reesterification with the unreacted fatty acids. (17)

Other Uses

There is an ongoing research into the use of oil as a replacement for fossil fuel oils. Meadowfoam is also a renewable plant crop, thus ensuring a continual source of oil far into the foreseeable future. (7)

Export Potential

Seed yield is good, and seed retention is excellent. Yields of 700- 1200 kg/ha of seed is obtained. Given the oil content and fatty acid composition, 1200 kg/ha would be worth $315/ha at rapeseed prices. However, the unique chemistry of meadowfoam oil has prompted recent contracts for $1.10/kg seed, resulting in a crop value of about $1320/ha at the high end of the normal yield range. (7)

Toxicity:

Meadowfoam seed contains significant levels of glucosinolates that upon hydrolysis yield both a volatile mustard oil, m-methoxybenzyl isothiocyanate, and the nonvolatile 5,5-dimethyloxazolidine-2-thione. (7)
