DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves peltate, floating, orbicular or elliptic, acute at one side, shallow notched at the opposite side; prickly along veins above and beneath; petiole densely prickly. Flowers prickly; sepals fleshy, triangular-ovate, concave, obtuse, violet or pinkish in colour on the inner surfaces, green and with recurved prickles outside. Outer petals violets or pinkish-violets at the inner surfaces, green and without prickle, gradually becoming smaller and white towards centre. Stamens at outer side long, inner side short. Fruits globular-ovoid, with tubercles and densely prickly. Seeds subglobose, dark brown, covered by white aril, testa hard and thick.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: 1 flowered scapes; Flower: Bright red inside, green outside, ; Calyx: Sepals 4, erect, inserted on the edge of the torus above the carpels ; Corolla: Petals numerous, seriate, shorter than the sepals ;Androecium: Stamens many, many seriate, fascicled in eight, filaments linear, pollen spheric, 3-nucleate; Gynoecium: Ovary 8-celled, sunk in dilated top of the torus, stigma discoid, depressed, concave, ovules few parietal; Fruit: Berry spongy, crowned with persistent sepals ; Seed: From pea to cherry in size .

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Leaves are useful in rheumatism. Flowers are used as tonic, aphrodisiac, restrain seminal gleet and dysentry. Seeds are astringent, deostruent and useful in dispel nightmares, dysmenorrhoea.


Chemistry:
Isolation of a-, b-, g- and d-tocopherols from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

A new cerebroside isolated from rhizomes with adventitious roots and characterized as an isomeric mixture of N-a-hydroxy-cis-octadecaenoyl-1-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl sphingosine (fig. 1) and its trans isomer. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Pharmacology:

Euryale ferox was able to stimulate humoral immunity to a greater extent than cell-mediated immunity. (Puri et al, 2000).

Euryale ferox has been widely used in traditional oriental medicine to treat a variety of illness. However, very little is known about the cellular actions by which this plant mediates its therapeutic effects. Various aspects of antioxidant activity were evaluated in total extracts and fractions derived from Euryale ferox. Total extracts (IC50 5.6 microg/ml) showed relatively high level radical scavenging activity toward 1, 1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) and also enhanced viability of Chinese hamster lung fibroblast (V79-4) cells under exposure to oxidative agents. Upon further fractionation, the highest levels of DPPH radical scavenging and lipid peroxidation inhibitory activities were found in the ethyl acetate and butanol fractions. The ethyl acetate fractions, the butanol fractions, and total extracts of Euryale ferox also dose-dependently enhanced the activities of superoxide dismutase, catalase and glutathione peroxidase in V79-4 cells. Of these three antioxidant enzymes, glutathione peroxidase activity was most strongly induced. Euryale ferox contains a significant antioxidant activity and that specific components in the ethyl acetate and butanol fractions may play an important role in mediating these antioxidant properties. (Lee et al, 2002).
