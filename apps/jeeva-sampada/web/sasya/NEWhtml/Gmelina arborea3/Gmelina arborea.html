HABIT & HABITAT:
Moderate sized tree, up to 18 m tall, deciduous, unarmed; bark corky, yellowish grey, branches covered with white hairs. It is found throughout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves ovate, 10-20 x 7.5-15 cm, acuminate at apex, glabraous above. Flowers appear before young leaves, arranged in 3-flowered small cymes; buds clavate. Calyx campanulate, densely hairy; teeth triangular, corolla brownish-yellow, hairy outside, up to 3.8 cm long, 5-lobed, 2-lipped; upper lip more than 1 cm long. Drupes 2-2.5 cm long, ovoid smooth, orange-yellow when ripe.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Velvety tomentose panicles of 3-flowerd cymes; Flower: Orange yellow or brownish yellow and dichotomously branched ; Calyx: Small, 5-fid, funnel shaped; Corolla: Corolla tube cylindrical, lobes 5, rounded;Androecium: Stamens 2, perfect, 3 rudimentary, anthers linear ; Gynoecium: Ovary stalked, style long. Stigma 2-lobed ; Fruit: Drupes fleshy ovoid, orange yellow; Seed: 1-2.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting :March-May.

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: In Ayurveda system of medicine roots are used as indigestable, stomachic, laxative and anthelmintic. It is useful in hallucination, thirst, piles, abdominal pains and fever. Flowers are used in leprosy and blood diseases. Fruits are used as diuretic, tonic, astringent to bowels, anaemia, leprosy, ulcers, consumption and vaginal discharges and alsoused to promote hair growth.


Chemistry:
Luteolin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Apigenin, luteolin, quercetin, hentriacontanol and b-sitosterol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A new lignan -arboreol- isolated and its structure elucidated as 1-hydroxy-2methoxy-2,6-bis(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl)-3,7dioxobicyclo[3,3,0]  octane. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structures of arboreal, 2-O-methylarboreol, 2-O-ethylarboreol, isoarboreol and gmelanone determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Six new lignans isolated from heartwood characterised as 6’’-bromoisoarboreol, 4-hydroxysesamin, 4,8-dihydroxysesamin, 1,4-dihydroxysesamin (gummadiol), 2-piperonyl- 3- hydroxymethyl-4-(a-hydroxy- 3,4- methylenedioxybenzyl)-4-hydroxy-tetrahydrofuran (I) and 4-epigummadiol-4-O-glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Hentriacontan-1-ol, ceryl alcohol, b-sitosterol, octacosanol and gmelinol obtained from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Gmelofuran isolated and its crystal structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Nonsaponjifiable fraction of wood extract contained C18-C30 wax alcohols, C28 (33.1) and C30 (14.7%) predominating, along with b-sitosterol, stigmasterol, stigmastanol, campesterol, a2-sitosterol and betulinol; saponifiable fraction contained oleic and linoleic acids a relatively high amount of saturated fatty acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

A new coumarin glycoside – apiosylskimmin – isolated from roots and characterized as umbelliferone-7-apiosyl(1à6)glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation of two new ketlignans – arborone and 7-oxodihydrogmelinol – from heartwood together with paulownin acetate, epieudesmin, methyl trans-p-methoxycinnamate ad trans-p-hydroxycinnamic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Major component linoleic acid (53.0%) identified along with lauric, myristic, palmitic, stearic, oleic, liolenic and arachidic acids in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Leaves afforded premnazole. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Anti-inflammatory study was conducted on 50% ethanolic extracts of stem bark of Streblus aspera and Gmelina arborea and root of Citrullus solocyhthin. All extract showed significant anti-inflammatory activity, out of which C. colocynthis was more potent. (Agarwal et al, 1994). C.A. 1996, 124, 45055s.

Besides the known iridoids 6-O-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosylcatalpol (1), 6-O-(3"-O-trans-feruloyl)-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosylcatalpol (14), 6-O-(2"-O-acetyl-3", 4"-O-di-trans-cinnamoyl)-alpha-L-rhamnopyranosylcatalpol (15) and the known phenylpropanoid glycosides verbascoside (acteoside) and martynoside, 12 new acylated iridoid glycosides named gmelinosides A-L (2-13) have been isolated from the leaves of Gmelina arborea. These compounds were structurally characterized using a variety of spectral methods. (Hosny and Rosazza, 1998)

A furanoresorcinol was identified in the benzene extract of the heartwood of Gmelina arborea. (Olatunji, 1999). C.A. 2001, 135, 34520r.

CIMAP:   Leaf chlorophyll (chl) content, net photosynthetic rate (PN) and biomass prodn. were estd. at monthly intervals in Chukrasia tabularis, Dolichandrone atrovirens, Eugenia jambolana, Gmelina arborea, Lannea coromandelica, Terminalia arjuna and Terminalia bellerica from Sept. 1990 to Aug. 1991. The leaves of all the seven tree species showed significantly higher chl content during summer than in winter, when it was expressed on area and/or fresh mass basis. Chl conc. showed marked variations from month to month and these differed from one plant species to the other. Clear pos. correlations were found between Chl content and PN and/or biomass prodn. in all the seven deciduous forest tree species studied. The scope for screening and selecting the right type of tree species prospective for energy plantations can be based on a simple technique such as estn. Of leaf Chl content and PN.1; changes in the concentrations of Ca, Mg, S and Na were detd. at monthly intervals in leaves, stems and roots of Chukrasia tabularis, A. Juss. (Meliaceae), Dolichandrone atrovirens (Heyne ex Roth), prague (Bignoniaceae), Eugenia jambolana Lam. DC (Myrtacea), Gmelina arborea Roxb. (Verbenaceae), Lannea coromandelica (Houtt.) Merr. (Anacardiaceae), Terminalia arjuna (DC.) Wright & Arn. (Combretaceae) and Terminalia bellerica (Gaertn.) Roxb. (Combretaceae) for a period of one year strating from June, 1990 to May 1991 in all seven tropical deciduous tree species increased slowly throughout the life span of leaves. Concns. of Ca, Mg, S and Na of stems were high at the time of new foliage or prior to emergence of new foliage and gradually increased decreased towards the leaf fall or senescence in all the saplings studied. Further, above four nutrients did not show any appreciable seasonal variation in roots. Seasonal variation was min. in roots compared to the stems and leaves. Concns. of Ca, Mg, S and Na varied from month to month  and species to species. The results of the present study indicate the scope of screening and selecting the right type of tree species for energy plantations, which could be used as fuel wood2.
Pharmacology:
Effect of methanolic extract of Gmelina arborea was studied on the aggregation of human blood platelets induced by ADP and Arachidonic acid, extract which inhibited the platelet aggregation and also showed anti-inflammatory activity. (Hamid and Shehnaz, 1998). C.A. 1998, 129, 184033z.
