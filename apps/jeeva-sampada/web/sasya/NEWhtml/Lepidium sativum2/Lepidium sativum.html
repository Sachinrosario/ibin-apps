DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 A small, herbaceous, glabrous annual, 15-45 cm. high, cultivated as a salad plant throughout India; leaves variable, entire or variously lobed or pinnatisect; radical leaves long-petioled, twice pinnatisect, cauline leaves sessile, linear, linear-oblong or pinnatifid; flowers small, white, in long racemes; ponds small, orbicular-ovate, notched at apex, winged; seeds solitary in each cell.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Plant antiasthamatic, bechic, in bleeding piles, root in secondary syphilis, seed galactagogue, abortifacient, leaf-stimulant, antiscorbutic, diuretic.


Chemistry:
NBPGR: Plant contains carotene and glucosinolate. Seedlings gave benzyl thiocyanate.  Seeds also afforded sinapic acid and sinapin.
A glycoside present in seeds, but absent in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
A new alkaloid - lepidine (fig. 1) isolated from seeds along with sinapic acid ethyl ester, N,N'-dibenzylthiourea and N,N'-dibenzylurea; new compound characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
5,4'-dihydroxy-7,8,3',5'-tetramethoxy-flavone and two new isomeric flavones - 5,3'-dihydroxy-7,8,4'-trimethoxyflavanone and 5,3'-dihydroxy-6,7,4'-trimethoxyflavanone - isolated and their structure determined and confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Lepidimoide (fig. 2) isolated as amorphous powder from mucilage of germinated seeds and characterized as sodium 2-O-rhamnosyl-4-deoxy-threo-hex-4-enopyranosiduronate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
Total lipid content (13.8%) in seeds from central India. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Pharmacology: 

Alcoholic extractive of plant a 10-20 mg/kg, i.v., significantly increased blood pressure in anaesthetized cats and dogs. Hypertensive effect was completely blocked by 5 mg/kg of priscoline. Same dosage also increased and force of auricular and ventricular movements of open heart preparation. At 0.5 - 1.0 mg/ml showed cardiostimulant action on isolated rabbit auricle preparation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
Lepidimoide >3.0 mM promoted at >100.0 mM inhibited growth of etiolated Amaranthus caudatus. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Ethnobotany:  

Medicine for asthma, bronchitis, colic, dysentery, mucus in urine, pain, phthisis, pneumonia, stomachache (Part not mentioned, jain and Tarafder, 1970); edible as vegetable (Leaf, Kaul et al, 1985)
