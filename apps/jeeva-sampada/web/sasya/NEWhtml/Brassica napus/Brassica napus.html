REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Racemose raceme; Flower: Large, bright yellow, pedicillate, complete, hermaphrodite, actinomorphic, tetramerous and hypogynous ; Calyx: Sepals 4, in two whorls of two each, polesepalous, petaloid ; Corolla: Petals 4, polypetalous, cruciform, each petals distinguished into claw and limb ;Androecium: Stamens 6, in two whorls, polyandrous, tetradynamous, 4 inner long amd two outer short, dithecous, basifixed, and introrse; Gynoecium:  Bicarpellary, syncarpous, ovary superior unilocular but become bilocular later on due development of false septa, ovles many in each locule, placentation parietal, style and stigma bilobed; Fruit: Pods erect, valves with mid-rib and flexuous lateral veins, pedulous, 3-4 celled; Seed: Large, globose, pale or dark .

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : January-April .   
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by  birds, cattles, wind or water. 
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 32, 38.

NBRI:

A flavonol glycoside – brassicoside (fig. 1), m.p. 209°C – characterized as isorhamnetin-3-b-[2-O-b-glucopyranosyl-D-glucoside] from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Epi-progoitrin, the major thioglucoside of seeds, was cleaved by ferrous salts to yield (S)-1-cyano-hydroxy-3 butene and (S)-3-hydroxypent-4-enthionamide. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Detection of petroglutamate derivatives in seedlings by differential microbial assay. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A new flavanoid – kaempferol-3-(O-sinapylsophoroside)-7-glucoside (fig. 2)– along with kaempferol-3-sophorosido-7-glucoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

24-methylene-lanost-8-en-3b-ol from seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Two new 4a, methylsterols-4a,14a,24-trimethyl-5-a-cholest-8,24-dien-3b-ol (fig. 3) and 4a,14a,24-trimethyl-9b,19-cyclocholest-24-en-3b-ol (fig. 4) – isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Nine tritepene alcohols, sixteen 4a-methylsterols and ten 4-demethylsterols identified in unsaponifiable fraction of seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Glucosyl fatty acid esters – 6-D-glucopyranosyl linolenate, palmitate, linolenate and oleate were isolated from pollen. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

A plant growth promoter – brassinolide (fig. 5) was isolated from pollen, characterized as (22R, 23R, 24S)2a,3a,22,23-tetrahydroxy-24-methyl-6,7-s-5a-cholestano-6,7-lactone and its structure determined and confirmed by X-ray analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Glucuronoxylan isolated from stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Oleic acid content in summer rape seed oil was considerably high and erucic acid content was very low. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Trans-22-dehydrocholesterol, stigmasta-5,25-dienol and fucosterol isolated from unsaponifiable matter of seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Free and esterified sterols compositions of seed oil was found as brassicasterol (14.1, 6.5), campesterol (31.0, 37.9), sitosterol (54.1, 52.2) and 5-dehydro-avenasterol (0.8, 2.5% respectively). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

Stereospecific synthesis of brassinolide and brassinone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

Kaempferol-3-(O-sinapoylsophorosyl)-7-O-glucoside and Kaempferol-3-(O-sinapoyl-glucosyl)-7-O-sophoroside isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Synthesis of brassinolide. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

A new quaternary base isolated from seeds and characterized as 2,6-dimethoxy-4-(2’-trimethylammonium ethenyl)phenyl-a-glucopyranoside (fig. 6). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Farnesene was identified as major component. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Synthesis of brassinolide. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

9’(Z)neoxanthin, (E)- and 9(Z)violaxanthins and 8’(R) and 8’(S)luteoxanthins isolated from petals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.


Pharmacology:
Brassinolide produced 200% increase in elongation in bean second-internode bioassay. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

Brassinolide and brassinone showed strong growth-promoting activity in rice-lamina inclination test, Raphanus test and tomato test at concentrations of 0.0001-1.0 ppm. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)h.
