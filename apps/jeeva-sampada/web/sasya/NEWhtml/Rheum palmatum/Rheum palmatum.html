Chemistry:

·        Structure of rhein established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Five glucosides – physcion -1- or 8 - b- D – glucoside hydrate, mp. 2350, aloeemodin -1 – or 8 - b - D – glucoside, chrysophanol – 1 or – 8 - b - D – glucoside hydrate, mp. 2450, rheumemodi n – 1 or 8 - b- D glucoside hemihydrate, mp. 1890, and rhein-8-b-D-glucoside, mp. 2660, from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        A new dianthrone – reidin A – from roots composed of emodin anthrone and rhein anthrone (1:1). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Glucogallin and (+) catechol identified in roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Two new phenolic glucosides -4-hydroxyphenyl-2-butanone-b-D-glucoside (I), mp. 1100, and 3,5-dihydroxystilbene-4/-O-b-D-glucopyranoside II, mp. 2350 – isolated from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Sennoside D, citreorosein, laccaic acid D and three unidentified hydroxyanthraquinones isolated from roots of a hybrid variety. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Total anthraquinone content in decoctions of root and rhizome were 3.9, 4.1, 5.5, 4.6 and 4.1 mg/ml in samples boiled for 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 min respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        6’ -O-galloyl-, 4’ -O-galloyl-, 6-O-galloyl-and 1’-O-galloylsucrose isolated from Chinese plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Five new esters – 1-O-galloyl-6-O-cinnamoyl-,1,2-di-O-galloyl-6-O-cinnamoyl-,1,2-di-O-galloyl-6-O-p-coumaroyl-, 1,6-di-O-galloyl-2-O-p-coumaroyl-and 1,2-di-O-galloyl-b-D-glucose –also isolated from Chinese plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Two new stilbene glycosides – 4’-O-methylpiceid and rhapontin – isolated from roots and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Isolation and structure elucidation of a new cytotoxic anthraquinone glucoside – pulmatin – from roots along with its congeners, chrysophanein and physcionin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Two polysaccharides – DHPI and DHP2 – isolated from roots and rhizomes; both polysaccharides contained lyxose, glucose, galactose, xylose, rhamnose, mannose and ribose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        HPLC method for the determination of rhein in the drug processed five different ways has been established. The method is simple, specific and accurate. The recovery is 98.52% and relative standard deviation. (Chen et al., 1994)

·        A micellar electrokinetic capillary chromatographic method for the separation and quantitative determination of aloe-emodin, emodin, and rhein in rhubarb. (Zong and Che, 1995)

·        Rhubarb extract reduces proteinuria and the severity of glomerulosclerosis in rats with remnant kidneys.(Zhang et al., 1996)

·        Hairy root culture of the medicinal plant Rheum palmatum L. was established by genetic transformation with Agrobacterium rhizogenes. The effects of various media with different pH on growth of the hairy roots and biosynthesis of free anthraquinones were investigated. The experimental results showed that MS agar medium with pH 5.5-5.8 is suitable for growth of the hairy roots. Dark condition is favourable and 62.5-fold increase in fresh weight was reached within a culture period of 25 days. Auxin (0.1 mg.L-1 IAA) activated the hairy root growth but inhibited the biosynthesis of free anthraquinones. About 28% of the total free anthraquinones was released into the liquid medium from the rhubarb hairy roots. (Chang et al., 1998)


Pharmacology:

·        The effects of volatile oil from Rheum palmatum (RVO) on immunologic function in mice are studied. The results show that the delayed type hypersensitivity (DTH) induced by 2, 4-dinitrochlorobenzene, lymphocytes transformation of (3H)-TdR induced by PHA, clearance rate of ivcarbon particles, phagocytic function of peritioneal macrophage and production of hemolysin induced by SRBC are markeyly increased with RVO given by oral administration or inhalation. (Zhang et al., 1997)

·        The antiHBV effect of Rheum palmatum Volatile oil was studied by using 2215 cell line transfected with HBV DNA. At the same time MTT method was applied for the detection of cytoxicity of drugs, selecting acyclovir(ACV) as control medicine. It turns out that the toxic concentration of Rheum palmatum Volatile oil for 50% cells was (CD50) > 1.25 x 10(-1) g/L. When concentration was below 0.625 x 10(-1) g/L, the survival rate of cells was over 90%. The maximum inhibitory rates for HBsAg and HBeAg were 70.71 +/- 5.4% and 30.99 +/- 5.3% respectively. This shows Rheum palmatum Volatile oil possesses the effect of antiHBV in vitro. (Zhang et al., 1998)

·        Ser/Thr protein kinases play important roles in signal transduction pathways that control the proliferation and differentiation of eukaryotic cells. present evidence that emodin, an anthraquinone derivative, selectively inhibits casein kinase II (CKII), a Ser/Thr kinase, as a competitive inhibitor. The results with ethyl acetate extracts of the rhizomes of Rheum palmatum showed that emodin significantly inhibited the activity of cyclin B/cdc2 protein kinase (cdc2). We measured IC50 values for emodin on the activities of several Ser/Thr protein kinases, including cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA), protein kinase C (PKC), cdc2, casein kinases I (CKI) and CKII. Interestingly, emodin inhibited CKII activity with an IC50 value of 2 muM, which was two to three orders of magnitude lower than those against the other kinases. Enzyme kinetic assays showed that emodin inhibited CKII activity as a competitive inhibitor against ATP with a Ki value of 7.2 muM. Collectively, we suggest that emodin is a selective CKII inhibitor, whose action mechanism is mediated through competitively binding to the ATP binding site. ( Yim et al., 1999)

·         It is known that constituent herbs of kampo (herbal) medicine contain many kinds of phytoestrogens. Using a reporter gene assay system,  demonstrated that Rheum palmatum contains compounds that cause ER (estrogen receptor) - ERE (estrogen response element) mediated transcriptional activation. Among the compounds in Rheum palmatum, the most potent activity was obtained with emodin. Emodin could not activate transcription of a reporter gene lacking the ERE. It was also demonstrated that the emodin gene could not activate transcription when transfected into HeLa, an ER free cell line. Co-transfection of a reporter gene with ER alpha or ER beta expression plasmid in HeLa cells led to transcriptional activation of both receptor subtypes by emodin. Competitive binding assays demonstrated that the IC50 of emodin was 3.49muM (17 beta estradiol 12.21nM) for ER alpha and 14.07muM (17 beta estradiol 14.10nM) for ER beta. In MCF-7 cells, emodin was also able to induce the synthesis ofPS2 mRNA, a known estrogen inducible gene, and this activity was inhibited by adding the pure ER antagonist, ICI182,780. These results demonstrate that emodin is a functional ligand of ER alpha and ER beta. (Kazuhiro et al., 2000)

·        The antiviral effects of aqueous extract of Rheum palmatum L was examined by a cell culture system using a hepatitis B virus (HBV) producing cell line, HepG2 2.2.15. The extracts were assayed for the inhibition of HBV multiplication by measurement of HBV DNA and surface antigen (HBsAg) levels in the extracellular medium of HepG2 2.2.15 cells after an 8-day treatment. (Kim et al., 2001)

·        Emodin and rhein are major iNOS inhibitors of R. palmatum and may possibly serve as bioactive substances for anti-inflammation effects.(Wang et al., 2002)

·        Extract from Rheum palmatum significantly prevents and treats hepatic encephalopathy in rats with thioacetamide-induced acute liver failure. (Zhang et al., 2002)

·        Flow cytometric analysis of breast cancer cells exposed to one of these herbs (Rheum palmatum) suggested that it arrests cells in the G2/M phase of the cell cycle. These results indicate that many of the herbs used in traditional Chinese medicine for the treatment of cancer have significant growth inhibitory effects on breast cancer cells in vitro. (Campbell et al., 2002)

·        Emodin (1,3,8-trihydroxy-6-methylanthraquinone) is an active constituent of Rheum palmatum, and showed inhibitory activity on lipopolysaccharide-induced NO production. (Chen, et al., 2002)
