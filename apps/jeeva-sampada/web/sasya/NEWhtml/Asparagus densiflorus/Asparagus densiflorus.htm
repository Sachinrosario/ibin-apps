Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Asparagus densiflorus in a rocky embankment.Has the potential to be similar to climbing asparagus which has a capacity to smother forest understory to a height of 2.5 - 5 m; smothers ground cover and can prevent regeneration of canopy species. 


This group of plants are most definitely shade loving plants that require adequate, if not more than adequate, amounts of water for best performance in the landscape. Most prefer damp, moist heavily shady areas but will do well with adequate water in almost any landscape. Some varieties are evergreen, some are semi-evergreen and some are deciduous. 
This evergreen arching perennial has feathery, needle-like, stems giving an open, loose appearance in growth. In summer the plant has small white flowers that are followed by bright red berries. The plant can grow 24 to 36 inches in height and to a width of 3 to 4 feet. It will grow in sun or even fairly dense shade, however, it would prefer some shade to maintain a darker green color leaf. Bright sun tends to wash out the color leaving a more yellow plant. 

Habitat/ecology: Dry to moist forests and openings. In Australia has invaded coastal, littoral rainforest, rainforest, frontal dunes and sclerophyll forest and coastal heath (Csurhes and Edwards, 1998).
Availability :

Location: Native to southern Africa. 
Native range: South Africa, widely grown as an ornamental.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect, perennial herb or somewhat woody, branches gracefully arching and fern-like; leaves alternate and scale-like, terminal branchlets very narrow, flat, needle-like, in clusters of 3; flowers axillary, drooping, 6-parted, bell-shaped, yellow-green; fruit a bright red berry. 

Description: Although commonly called asparagus fern, this plant is actually a woody stemmed plant that produces long vine-like branches. These are equipped with small hook structures which the plant uses to scramble up adjacent plants. The small white flowers, about 1/2" in diameter, mature into small red berries. 
Plants from tuberous roots; stems sprawling to erect, usually armed; cladophylls solitary or in fascicles of 3 or more, linear, usually flattened, slightly curved, 1-veined; flowers perfect, white or pale pink, borne in axillary racemes; fruit red." (Welsh, 1998)

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Species and Cultivars:

• densiflorus 'Myersii' (A. meyeri)-- Myers Asparagus, Foxtail Asparagus/Fern; dense, upright to slightly arching foxtail-like fronds 12-18" long, often used in pots

• densiflorus 'Sprengeri' (A. sprengeri)--Sprenger Asparagus, Emerald Fern; arching, then pendant stems 18-30" long, with widely spaced "leaves" (more so in low light), giving an open or loose appearance, best trailing or hanging, may also be cut

• setaceus (A. plumosus)--Asparagus Fern, very feathery foliage deep green and clustered on stalks, often used as a florist cut green

Phenology:
Flowering and fruiting: 
Flowers: small waxy white flowers scattered throughout plant; not conspicuous; sporadic blooming
Fruit: small red berries, 1/4in in diameter

Pollinators: The Asparagus should be fed sparingly. Use an all purpose fertilizer every two to three weeks during the spring and early summer. Use less that the recommended strength on the package.
Growth habit, uses: hanging, pot or cut depending on species or cultivar 
Other interest: native to sandy and coastal areas of South Africa; grouped with ferns, but as a member of the lily family not actually a fern; related to the edible garden asparagus (officinalis) 
Other culture: may need dividing frequently as the spindle-like tuberous roots become quite potbound and can crack pots; survives drought, but dryness causes needles to fall; prune bare stems as new shoots will develop after needle fall; often plants shed old needles as new shoots form and so are messy indoors


Floral and seed biology: The standard way is through seed. The seed is sown into individual small pots or if large quantities are raised can be seeded in pans. Old plants may be divided, the plant being cut apart with a sharp knife. The fronds or stems should be cut back also. Repot in the mixture above. 

CULTURAL REQUIREMENTS:

  • Exposure: part sun or part shade; takes reflected light (but not reflected heat)
  • Water: moderate; once per week or per two weeks
  • Soil: prepared, amended
  • Propagation: seed or division; roots develop fleshy nodules
  • Maintenance: low; removal of frost damage foliage; okay to mow to ground to control size, but not in heat of summer

Culture: Light: Needs good light without strong direct sun, but can tolerate partial to full shade, especially in hot summer areas. 
Moisture: Asparagus ferns like any fertile soil that is efficiently drained.
Hardiness: USDA Zone 9-10. Asparagus fern is killed to the ground by frosts but recovers quickly.
Propagation: Can be grown from seed or from division. 
Features: This easy to grow houseplant is inexpensive. Grow it for the beauty of its foliage, which make a nice filler for floral arrangements.
Foliage: true leaves are small and scale-like, with feathery leaf-like stems (cladophylls) or modified side shoots (phylloclades) appearing to be leaves, often with spines

Light: bright 
Temperature: cool to average 
Watering: moderate, avoid dryness to prevent needle fall

This soft, fluffy ornamental forms attractive mounds when planted at level ground or cascades over walls, banks, or containers. Produces a softening effect as well as interesting texture. Can be trained on a trellis. Sparse white spring flowers may produce red fall berries. Often used as ground cover or foundation plant. 

Var. "Compacta" has denser, tighter growth. Smilax Asparagus (a. asparagoides) is known for its pointed, glossy leaves, fragant white flowers, and blue berries. Myers Asparagus (a. d. Myers) has stems that are stiff and upright.


Form: mounding, spreading or trailing clump with delicate appearance 
Seasonality: evergreen perennial
Size: 12-24in, spread to 3ft; rapid growth
Leaves: delicate needlelike leaves, ferny; tiny thorns present despite soft appearance of plant; foliage is bright green or yellow green
Stems/Trunks: green branches, arching or cascading appearance; sprawls
Range/Origin: S. Africa
Hardiness: frozen to ground at 24°F; recovers quickly; better in locations protected from frost
The various Asparagus "ferns" are not true ferns, but are members of the lily family, Liliaceae. They are popular house plants and can be used in various ways. There are number of species, including the edible one, but three varieties are the most popular. Asparagus plumosus by florists for the foliage. Asparagus densiflorus "Sprengeri" is probably the most popular form. The plant consist of arching branches covered with fine, light green needle-like foliage. Asparagus densiflorus "Meyeri" the foxtail asparagus is gaining fast popularity. At present it is the most expensive of the Foxtail Asparagus. 
Light: Asparagus ferns need good light for proper development. They need at least three to four hours of bright sunlight a day. These plants will quickly turn yellow and drop their leaves if the light is insufficient. 
Water: The Asparagus should be kept moderately moist for proper development. Watering is done usually when the plant feels dry to the touch. Plants that are overwatered or underwatered usually start to develop yellow leaves at the base and begin to shed leaves, both yellow and green. The root system of these plants is extensive and forms thick growths. Plants that are very old and that have not been repotted for some time may have to be watered several times a day. 
Humidity: Humidity levels should be kept as high as possible. These plants are often grown as hanging baskets and usually kept at high levels in the home where the temperature is warmer and drier. The addition of humidity through a humidifier, frequent syringing or a humidity tray is beneficial. 
Soil: The Asparagus is not particularly fussy over the soil as long as it is a well-drained mix. A soil consisting of 1 part soil; 1 part sharp sand (or perlite); and 1 part peat moss will suit the plant. The addition of small amounts of charcoal (horticultural grade); dried blood and bone meal will improve the fertility of the soil and the drainage.

LANDSCAPE VALUE:

  • good in containers, especially hanging pots
  • houseplant
  • informal groundcover
  • tropical effect
  • best near living and traffic areas

can be difficult to remove litter if planted in a location where leaf drop accumulates 
 Planting & Care
Remove longest stems to maintain pleasing shape and cut out old shoots. Move to sunnier location if plant begins to thin. Fertilize in spring. Needs good drainage. 
Freezes at 24 degrees F but recovers in warm weather. 



MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES: crown rot, leaf spots, rust, anthracnose, canker, slugs, spider mites (often common), aphids, scales (often common) 
Insects & Diseases: Susceptible to iron chlorosis. Maintain deep green color with iron
Never pot Asparagus in fancy or expensive containers. The heavy roots have the capability to break even the best of pots. The Asparagus can be afflicted with several pests. Aphids can be seen on the new growth, and spider mites will attack the leaves. Frequent strong syringing will keep your plant clean of these pests. Occasionally root mealy bugs be encountered. It is best then to wash the soil away completely and repot into fresh soil
