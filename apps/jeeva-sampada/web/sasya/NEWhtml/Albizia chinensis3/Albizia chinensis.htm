HABIT & HABITAT

Occasional in hill regions or deciduous forest of E. ghats (T. Pulliah F. of E. ghats, Vol. II) throughout Himalayan valley upto 1,200m in Assam, W. Bengal, Bihar, South India and Andaman cultivated for shade in coffee and tea gardens.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

- Large, deciduous tree, with a broad spreading and flat topped crown.
- Leaves with 8-15 pairs of pinnate, leaflets 50-80 lanceolate.
- Flowers white with pinkish filaments, in terminal and axillary panicles.
- Pods thin, brown 15-20 cm, 8-12 seeded.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood
Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:
Wood - box-making, teak-boxes, packing cases, for matches, for planking dugouts, well-curbs, small turnery articles, combs, spoons, yokes and agricultural implements.
Pulp - writing and printing paper.
Gum - In Nepal for sizing paper.
Bark - Tannin - its infusion is used as a lotion for cuts, scabies and other skin diseases.
Seeds & leaflets - toxic to melonworn larvae and cotton stainer adults; petioles toxic to melonworm and leaves and twigs - for fodder.

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:
Wood pulp - sulphate process.
Other uses:

The tree is planted to chack soil erosion, as shade in coffee and cardamom plantations and as green manure. It is planted along roadsides and in gardens.

A reddish brown gum exudes from the bark and dries into stalactiform masses. It has a smooth polished surface. It gelatinises with a large amount of water and is not completely soluble. It is used as an adulterant of gum arabic and sometimes as its substitute.

The seeds, leaflets, petioles and pods are toxic to cotton stainer adults, whereas bark, root and seeds are toxic to melonworm larvae.

The leaves are nutritious and palatable, and can be sued as fodder (1). Kalidasa noted women wearing its flowers (400.A.1) (3).
