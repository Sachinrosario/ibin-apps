DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Armed, large shrub or small tree, branches numerous, jointed, green, prominently 3-5 winged, wings repand-sinuate. Stipular spines 2, short, sharp, divaricate, persistent. Leaves small, subsessile, fleshy, obovate-oblong or spathulate, obtuse at apex, cuneate at base, glabrous, caducous, cyathia 3-nate, in short axillary pedumculate cymes; glands 5, large, semicircular. Male flowers: in 5 groups of 8 each, bracteolate; anthers dehiscing vertically. Female flowers: styles free, 2-fid, recurved. Cocci compressed, glabrous.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Cymes from the small peducles ; Flower: Bracteoles numerous, lacinate, monoecious, the central flower sessile, female, the two lateral on long pedicels ; Male Flower: a stalked stamen without floral envelop ; Female Flower: Ovary 3-celled on ultimately exerted stalk in the center of 3-nate involucre, ovules solitary in each cell, style 3, free; Fruit: Capsule of 3 two valved compressed cocci with styles -fid; Seed: Broad, flat. Flowering & Fruiting : May-September  .

Floral and seed biology: 
Fl. & Fr.: November – January.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The juice obtained from branches is used as purgative. It is useful in rheumatism and toothache and also employed in nervine diseases, dropsy, palsy, deafness and amaurosis. A plaster prepared from the roots and mixed with Asafoetida is applied externally to the stomachs of children suffering from worms. Root barks are used as purgative. Stem decoction is effective in gout. The juice mixed with burnt borax and common salt is applied in painful joints and swellings. Used as antidote in snakebite. The plant is supposed to ward off lightening stroke.


Chemistry:
Taraxerol, taraxerone, friedelan-3a-ol, epifriedelinol, euphol and an unidentified compound, mp. 215°, from stems and roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

b-amyrin, euphadienol and euphorbol isolated as acetyl derivatives from latex. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

A new diterpene – antiquorin (fig. 1) isolated along with friedelan-3b-ol and taraxerol and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Isolation of new triterpenoids – friedelan-3b,30-diol diacetate, 30-acetoxyfriedelan-3b-ol and 3b-acetoxyfriedelan-30-ol – from stem; taraxerone, b-amyrin, cycloartenol, euphol, taraxerol, cycloartan-3b,25-diol, C-24 epimers of cycloart-25-en-3b,24-diol and b-sitosterol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Isolation and structure determination of 3,12-di-O-acetyl-8-O-benzoylingol, 3,12-di-O-acetyl-8-O-tigloylingol, 12-O-acetyl-8-O-tigloylingol and 8-O-tigloylingol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Structure of antiquorin revised by X-ray diffraction which showed trans-fused A and B rings. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Three new triterpenes – euphol-3-O-cinnamate (fig. 2), antiquols A (fig. 3) and B (fig. 4) isolated from latex and their structures established; euphol, 24-methylenecycloartanol, cycloeucalenol, (Z)9-nonacosane, sitosterol and p-acetoxyphenol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

The structures of three triterpene alcohols isolated from the latex of Euphorbia antiquorum were established to be eupha-7,9(11),24-trien-3beta-ol (2; antiquol C), 19(10®9)abeo-8alpha,9beta,10alpha-eupha-5,24-dien-3beta-ol (3; antiquol B), and 24-methyltirucalla-8,24(24(1))-dien-3beta-ol (4; euphorbol) on the basis of spectroscopic methods. Compounds 3 and 4 have previously been assigned the erroneous structures of 10alpha-cucurbita-5,24-dien-3alpha-ol and 24-methyleupha-8,24(24(1))-dien-3beta-ol, respectively. (Akihisa et al, 2002).


Pharmacology:

A toxic and skin irritant factor -3-O-angeloylingenol -isolated from latex. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Euphol (i.v.) showed dose-dependent hypotensive activity ranging from slight to marked, in normotensive anaesthetized dogs and rats. It inhibited various autonomic pressor and depressor responses. Hypotensive effect was not affected in dogs by pretreatment with atropine, antistine or b-blockers. Its LD50 was 1.5 g/kg, i.p. and 2.0 g/kg by oral route in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Compounds eupha-7,9(11),24-trien-3beta-ol (2; antiquol C), 19(10®9)abeo-8alpha,9beta,10alpha-eupha-5,24-dien-3beta-ol (3; antiquol B), and 24-methyltirucalla-8,24(24(1))-dien-3beta-ol (4; euphorbol) and four other known compounds isolated from the latex, euphol (1), lemmaphylla-7,21-dien-3beta-ol (5), isohelianol (6), and camelliol C (7), showed potent inhibitory effects on Epstein-Barr virus early antigen (EBV-EA) activation induced by the tumor promoter 12-O-tetradecanoylphorbol-13-acetate (TPA). (Akihisa et al, 202).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for anascarca, asthma, breast pain, bronchitis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Burns (Latex, Borthakur, 1976; Borthakur, 1981); Deafness, dropsy, dysentery, phthisis (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Rheumatism (Latex, Chaudhuri and Pal, 1976); Skin disease (Whole plant, Mishra and Sahu, 1984); Sores, syphilis, whitlow (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970) .
