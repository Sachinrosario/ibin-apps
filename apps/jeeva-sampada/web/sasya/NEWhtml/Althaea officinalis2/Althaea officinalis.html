 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
  Herbaceous under shrub, up to 50-100 cm. 


Availability :  
INDIA: N.W. Himalayas between 900 and 1800 m. Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh. AFGANISTHAN, EUROPE, N. AFRICA.

Specimen examined:
Himachal Pradesh: Lippa, Bashahr dist., 2900 m, 18.08.1890, J.H. lace, 532 (DD).


Conservation/ Threat Status :Plant grows wild.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect herbs or undershrubs, 50-100 cm high; branched throughout, uniformly downy. Leaves ovate, 2.5-7.0 x 1.5-6.0 cm, entire or slightly 8-lobed, scarcely cordate at base, acute at apex, irregularly dentate at margin; petioles 1-4 cm long; stipules 5-10 x 1-2 mm.

Inflorescence: Racemes peduncled in axillary clusters; Flower: Bracteate, bracts connate at the base, rosy, hermaphrodite, regular ; Calyx: Sepals 5, valvate, free; Corolla: Petals 5, twisted-imbricate; Androecium:  Stamens indefinite, adnate to the base of the petals, filaments monoadelphous, anther valves sub-globose, one celled, bursting longitudinally ;Gynoecium: Ovary multicarpellary, ultimately seperating from a short torus, ovule one in each cell, styles filiform, stigma peltate; Fruit:  Dry capsule ; Seed: Solitary in each carpel.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Floral and seed biology:
Pedicels 0.5-1.5 cm long. Epicalyx segments 6-8, 2-3 x 1 mm. Calyx longer than epicalyx, lobes 4-6 x 3-4 mm. Petals 1.5-2.5 x 0.4-1.0 cm, pinkish. Staminal column 0.5-1.0 cm long. Schizocarps 5-7 mm across, partly covered with persistant calyx, mericarps 10-15, wrinkled at sides. Seeds 1.5-2.5 mm in diam., brownish black. August - July.

Flowering & Fruiting: January-March. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 40, 41, 42, 43, 44.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Hemicelluloses were extracted from A. officinalis. Hydrolysis of hemicellulose gave D-xylose as a major component, 4-O-Me-D-glucuronic acid and traces of L-arbinose. (Saeed and Saeed, 1976). C.A. 56475p, 89, 1978.

Mucos polysaccharide, Althaea mucilage O, has been isolated from the roots of Althaea officinalis and composed of L-rhamnose, D-galactose, D-galacturonic acid, D-glucuronic acid in molar artio of 3:2:3:3. (Tomoda et al, 1977).

Althaea-mucilage OL has been isolated from the leaves of Althaea officinalis, polysaccharide was composed of L-rhamnose, D-galacturonic acid, D-gluceronic acid in the molar ratio of 1.5:1.1:1. (Tomoda et al, 1981).

Polyphenolic compounds Scopoletin, p-coumaric, caffeic, ferulic, salicylic, p-hydroxybenzoic, vanillic, syringic and p-hydroxyphenyl acetic acids and  flavonoid heterosides were isolated and tentatively identified as kaempferol-3-glycoside, kaempferol 3-b-D-(6’’-O-p hydroxy cinnamoyl ) glucoside, quercetin 3-glycoside, 8-hydroxydiosmetin 8- glucoside. (Gudej, 1982) CA. 1982, 96, 82724h.

b-sitosterol, Palmitic, myristic, stearic, oleic, linoleic  and lauric acids were detected in Althaea officinalis. (Karawya et al, 1983) CA. 1983, 98: 2737t.

A water soluble L-arabinan isolated from the mucos material of the roots of the marshmallow has a highly branched structure of ~90a-1-arabinofuranose residues variously linked by (1à5), (1à3) and (1à2) bonds. Some of the L-arbinose residues are involved in branches through 0-2, 0-3 and 0-5. (Peter et al, 1983). C.A. 19691s, 99, 1983.

Five crystal flavonoid heterosides isolated from marshmallow (Althaea officinalis) leaves were identified as tiliroside, kaempferol 3-glucoside, quercetin, 3-glucoside, 8-hydroxydiosmetin 8-glucosides and the 3’-So3K derivatives of 8-hydroxydiosmetin 8-glucosides. These are new natural flavonoid heterosides. (Gudej, 1985). C.A. 183294g, 104, 1986.

Scopolin P-coumaric Caffeic, ferulic, vanillic, P-hydroxy benzoic and chlorogenic acids were identified in Althaea officinalis. (Koleva et al, 1986). C.A. 94531k, 105, 1986.

Novel flavonoid glycosides were isolated by chromatography from marsh mallow (Althaea officinalis) leaves. Two of them were identified as hypoletin 8-glucoside and hypoletin 8-b-gentiobioside. (Gudej, 1987). C.A. 89750j, 109, 1988.

Tiliroside naringenin 4’-b-D-glucoside, and dihydrokaempferol 4’-b-D-glucoside were isolated from the methanolic extracts. Kaempferol 3-glucoside, quercetin-3-glucoside, and b-hydroxyluteolin 8-gentiobioside, salicylic, vanillic, ferulic, syringic, caffeic, p-hydroxybenzoic, p-coumaric, and p-hydroxy phenylacetic acids was ascertained by chromatography. (Gudej, 1988). C.A. 209342x, 110, 1989.

Caffeic, p-hydroxybenzoic, ferulic P-coumaric, vanillic and chlorogenic acids, quercetin-3-glucoside (isoquercitrin), Kaempferol-3-glucoside (astragalin), populnin and an unidentified compounds (flavanones and dihydro flavonl glucosides) were isolated from flowers. (Didry et al, 1990).

Althaea officinalis leaves contained 0.68-1.39, flowers 0.32-0.35, root 0.14-0.24% flavonoid aglycons. Leaves and flowers contained 0.130.25% and 0.15-0.19% tyliroside respectively. Leaves contained 0.25-0.42% hypoletine 8-gentiobioside. Flowers contained 0.76-0.84% dihydrokaempferol 4’-glucoside. (Gudej, 1990). C.A. 89137p, 115, 1991.

Dihydrokaempferol 4’-O-glucoside, isoquercitrin hypoletin 8-O-gentiobioside, astragalin and tiliroside were determined by reversed phase HPLC in leaves and flowers of Althaea officinalis. (Gudej et al, 1991). C.A. 187802h, 118, 1993.
