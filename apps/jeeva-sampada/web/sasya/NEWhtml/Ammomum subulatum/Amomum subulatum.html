 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Rootstock perenial widely creeping found to occur in Assam, West Bengal, Sikkim.


Availability :  
 
Conservation/ Threat Status : Plant is commercially cultivated.

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Rootstock perenial, widely creeping. Leaves oblong- lanceolate, 30.0-61.0 x 7.5-10.5 cm, glabraous on both surfaces, green.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: dense spike on short peduncle bearing 40-50 flower buds in an acropetal succession, spikes are radical, compact, obovate echinate with long, subulate pale yellow points on bracts, calyx and upper segments of the exterior border of corolla; Flower: white with large strap shaped lip, bracts numerous, exterior bracts imbricated upwards, dark red, smooth, long subulate, pointed, the inner or inferior calyx spathiform opening on one side, trimerous, hermaphrodite and epigynous; Calyx: superior, three cleft, segment subulate and longer than the tube or corolla; Corolla: Corolla of nearly 3 equal segments the upper ends subulately lip oblong often emarginate, margins are curled and deeper in colour than the segments; Androecium: Anther 2-lobbed and crowned with entire crest, filaments flat.; Gynoecium: Tricarpellary and multilocular, placentation axile, stigma simple and nector secreting glands are present; Fruit: trilocular with many seeds and reddish brown to dark pink in colour; Seed: 30-50 seeds in each fruit. Flowering & Fruiting: April-December. Pollination by insects. Fruits are dispersed by wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and rhizome cuttings.

Soil type: Soils that are well drained , rich in nitrogen contents, with medium phosphrus and potassium,  pH ranging between 5.0-5.5 are reported best for the plant.

Nutrient requirements:  Plants respond well to application of manures and fertilizers.

Water management:   Rainy season is preferred for planting and crop must be irrigated periodically.                                      

Harvesting period / Method:  Crop is harvested when fruits becomes completely mature.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
In Ayurveda system of medicine seeds are used in indigetion, vomitting, enlarge spleen, itching, abdominal pains, disease of the rectum , mouth and head. In unani system of medicine seeds are used for preparation of tonic for heart and liver diseases. It is astringent to the bowels and cause beleching. Outside covering is good for teeth; heads stomatitis.


Chemistry:
Petunidin-3, 5-diglucoside and leucocyanidin-3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Cardmonin and alpinetin from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structure of a new aurone glycoside- subulin (fig. 1)- isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Elemental analysis of the herbal drug, “Intellan” containing Ammomum subulatum reveals the presence of Sodium, Potassium, calcium, lead, cobalt, nickel, copper, zinc, magnesium and cadmium. (Ahmad et al, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9502, 1000.

The steam distilled volatile oil of the large cardamom was analysed by GC-MS, found to consist of monoterpene hydrocarbons (16.3%), oxygenated monoterpenes (75.2%) and sesquiterpenes (6.3%), its major constituents are 1,8-cineole (61.3%), a-terpineol, a- and b-pinene and allo-aromadenrene. (Gurudutt et al, 1996). MAPA 1996, 9606, 3611.

From the ethyl acetate solution fraction four compounds were isolated and their structures were ascribed to protocatechualdehyde (I), protocatechuic acid (fig. 2) (II), 1,7-bis(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)hepta-4E,6E-dien-3-one (III) and 2,3,7-trihydroxy-5-(3,4-dihydroxy-E-styryl)6,7,8,9-tetrahydro-5H-benzocycloheptene (IV) on the basis of spectroscopic evidence. (Kikuzaki et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 178023d.

A. subulatum contained 1,8-cineole (72.7%) as main constituent determined by GC-MS. (Rahman et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 134, 338101n.


Pharmacology:

Large cardamom (fruit of Amomum subulatum) used in Unani system of medicine in gastrointestinal disorders. A crude methanolic extract and its different fractios, viz. essential oil, petroleum ether (60-80°C), ethyl acetate and methanolic fractions were studied in rats for their ability to inhibit the gastric lesions induced by aspirin, ethanol and pylorus ligature. In addition their effects on wall mucus, output of gastric acid and pepsin concentration were recorded. The crude methanolic extract of A. subulatum and its fraction, viz. essential oil, petroleum ether and ethyl acetate, inhibited gastric lesions induces by ethanol significantly, but not those which were induced by pylorus ligation and aspirin. However, ethyl acetate fraction increase the wall mucos in pylorus ligated rats. The results suggests a direct effect of ethyl acetate fraction on gastric mucosal barrier. While the observation of decrease in gastric motility by essential oil and petroleum ether fraction suggests the gastroprotective action of the test drug. (Jafri et al, 2001).

In order to gain insight into the antioxidant effect of cardamom (Ammomum subulatum; Zingiberaceae) hepatic and cardiac antioxidant enzymes, glutathione (GSH) content and lipid conjugated dienes were studied in rats fed high fat diet with cardamom. The antioxidant enzyme activities were found to be significantly enhanced whereas GSH content was markedly restored in rats fed a fat diet with spices. In addition, these spices partially counteracted increase in lipids conjugated dienes and hydroperoxides, the primary products of lipid peroxidation. Thus, it appears that Cardimomum exert antioxidant protection through their ability to activate the antioxidant enzymes. (Dhuley, 1999).

Ammomum subulatum are used in Unani medicine for skin care. (Latif and Rahman, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9901-0204.

Ammomum subulatum were fractioned into three fractions, the dichloromethane extract, and the Ethyl acetate solution extract and water solution fractions of 70% aquous acetone extract. The ethyl acetate solution fraction showed a high radical scavenging activity against 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH). (Kikuzaki et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 178023d.

Effect of extract of 18 plant species including Ammomum subulatum was evaluated against the sensitivity of the keratinophilic fungi by dry weight method. The maximum inhibition of mycelial growth was shown by M.gypseum (86.62%) followed by T.terrestre (81.86%) and C.tropicum (74.06%) when treated with S. hirusta whereas the minimum inhibition was exhibited by M.gypseum (0.29%), C. tropicum (0.16%) and T. terrestre (1.76%) when tested with extract of P. edulis, A. vasica and B.difusa, respectively. (Qureshi et al, 1997). MAPA 1999, 9904, 2554.

A. subulatum shows antifungal activity of essential oil 1,8-cineole (72.7%) against various pathogenic fungi (Aspergillus flavus, A. niger, Candida albicans, Fusarium oxysporum var. lycopersici, microsporum canis, pseudallesheria boydii, Trichopyton mentagrophytes and t. simii). (Rahman et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 338101n.
