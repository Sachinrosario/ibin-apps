DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual herbs or undershrubs, 1.0-1.5 m. high; branches sparsely stellate tomentose, glabrescent. Leaves ovate rounded, cordate at base, palmately 3, 5 or 7 lobed; lobes ovate-rounded or oblong-elliptic, acute or apiculate at apex; petioles 1.5-3.5 cm long; stipules  linear to lanceolate, 0.5-1 cm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: Pedicellate, bracteate, hermaphrodite, complete, irregular; Calyx: Truncate or obsoletely crenulate, much shorter than the involucre; Corolla: Yellow with purple base, petals obovate-cuneate; Androceium: Staminal tube bearing numerous filaments below the naked antheriferous apex; Gynoecium: Ovary 5-celled, cells many ovuled, style club-shaped, 5-furrowed, stigmas 5; Fruit: Capsule ovoid-globose, mucronate; Seed: 6-7, ovoid with white cotton overlaying a grayish firmly adherent down; Flowering & Fruiting time: April- June. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by   wind, birds or animals.             .

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers axillary, solitary; pedicels 0.6-1.5 cm long. Epicalyx segments ovate-cordate, 1-2 cm long, slightly connate at base.  Calyx cup-shaped, 7-10 mm long, undulate or truncate. Corolla yellowish with purple centre; petals obovate, 2.5-3.5 x 2.5-4.0 cm. Staminal column c 1.0 cm long. Capsules oblong-obtuse, 3-4 cm long, c 2.5 cm across , 4-loculed. Seeds 5-7 in each locule, 5-8 x3-6 mm, ovoid with floss and fuzz; flass greyish-white.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds and vegetatively by cuttings.

Soil type:  Plant requires well drained loamy soil.

Nutrient requirements: Farmyard manure and NPK fertilizers are beneficial for plant.

Water management:  Irrigation is required at periodic intervals.                                     

Harvesting period / Method: The crop is harvested when the boll is fully mature, fully open and floss is puffed up.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Roots, barks and seeds are used as abortifacient.
Chemistry:
Keeping cotyledons, excised from 2-wk-old cotton seedlings, for 30 min at 37˚ decreased their wt. by 10% and increased the inhibitory activity of endogenous abscisic acid 1.5-2-fold. In drought – resistant cultivars, the levels of abscisic acid activity were 2-3-fold higher than in susceptible cultivars. The high levels of abscisic acid in the drought reistant cultivars of hastened closing of the stomas in response to water stress1.
