Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large, evergreen tree with spreading crown and flat top. Occurs in moist tropical and subtropical climates, ascending to 1200 m. Distributed throughout the sub-Himalayan valleys up to 1,200 m, in Assam, West Bengal, Bihar, South India and the Andamans.

Moderate light demander.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Bark gray, fairly smooth, with occasional prominent horizontal wrinkles and furrows and small vertical wrinkles. Leaves with 8-15 pairs of pinnae, leaflets 50-80, lanceolate. Flowers white with pinkish filaments, in terminal and axillary panicles. Pods thin, brown, 15-20 cm, 8-12 seeded.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood is good fuelwood and was earlier used extensively in locomotives. The calorific value of the sapwood and heartwood is 4,358 and 4,845 calories, respectively.

Wood: The wood resembles that of other albizias, but is lighter in colour (sp gr 0.72; wt 736.92 kg/cu m). The sapwood is large, white; heartwood is brown, soft, not very durable.

It is used for box making, especially tea-boxes, packing cases and for matches. It is also used for planking, dugouts, well-curbs, small turnery articles, combs, spoons, yokes and agricultural implements. The wood yields 47.8-49.0 per cent pulp by the sulphate process. It is suitable for writing and printing paper after it is mixed with suitable long-fibred pulp.

Other uses:

Wood is very hard and is used for beams, carts and ploughs.

Leaves - Leaves and twigs are used as fodder.

Tannin - Bark yields 22% tannin. Its infusion is used as a lotion for cuts, scabies and other skin diseases.

Plant extract is reported to have piscicidal and oxytocic properties.

Cultivation - The tree is extensively cultivated for shade in coffee and tea-gardens.
