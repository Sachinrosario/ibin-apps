Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A tree; occurs in the greater part of India ascending up to an altitude of 1,000 m and in the Andamans. Rare in arid regions.


Availability :

West Bengal and Assam supply fairly large quantities. Moderate supplies from west coast forests of Mysore and Kerala. Also from Andamans.


Conservation/ Threat Status : Wild/ naturalised/ cultivated

Lower risk - least concern

 

Back to Top

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A tree. Bark grey, somewhat warty, light red and fibrous inside. Branches few, spreading. Leaves crowded at the end of the branches, palmately 5-7 lobed, glabrescent or thinly stellately hairy above, velvety and with spreading hairs with intermixed with pubescence beneath, lobes long acuminate, base cordate. Petioles 6-15 in. long, pubescent, enlarged and very downy near the top. Stipules lanceolate, caducous.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers pinkish, in much-branched rusty-pubescent terminal panicles. Males and females intermixed, the males much the more numerous. Calyx companulate, downy outside, divided to the middle. Lobes oblong-lanceolate, acute, nerved.

Male flowers : Staminal column recurved. Stamens 10.

Female flowers : Ovary strigose, with stellate hairs; gynophore stout, cylindric; style stout, hairy, deflexed. Staminodes on a ring beneath the ovary. Follicles about 5, oblong, spreading, rusty-villous. Seeds oblong, smooth black.

Tree strong light demander.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: The seeds germinate at the beginning of the rains, soon after falling. They are somewhat perishable and the rate of fertility is not high, but in suitable localities, natural regeneration is adequate. Experimental trials with transplanting the seedlings and stump plating, respectively, gave 66 and 85 per cent success.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:Wood good fuel. Calorific values: sapwood – 4,890 cal., 8,802 Btu.

Wood: Sapwood and heartwood not distinct. Wood pale yellowish or greyish white to light greyish brown, often discoloured. Extremely soft to soft and often spongy and easily dealt with a finger nail, extremely light to light. Specific gravity 0.16 – 0.46 air dry. Somewhat lustrous on the quarter sawn or radial surface. Straight grained even and coarse textured. Easily perishable.

Wood used chiefly for tea boxes and other light packing cases. The other possible, for inferior matchboxes and splints, three-ply work, and as a core material in sandwich construction employed to withstand buckling stresses or deflection by loads normal to the surfaces. Timber is used for tea chests.

Processing Techniques:

Can be seasoned easily without much degrade, cracking and warping being entirely absent. Timber liable to stain. Quick conversion and stacking in open under cover recommended to avoid discolouration.

Other uses:

The inner layers of the bark yield a coarse and strong fiber, which strips off in broad flakes, having a net like appearance. This fiber used for cordage and for making rough bags. Much used for breastbands of elephants for dragging timber and for tying cattle.

Seeds eaten, cooked or roasted. Pericarp yields a dye when burnt.

Bark exudes a dye, reported to be used in veterinary practices.

Bark - Bark fibre is used for elephant harness, for making coarse canvas bags, etc. also used for making paper.
