DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Medium sized to large dioecios evergreen tree, 15-30 m tall; bark brownish, rough, young parts brown pubescient. Leaves simple, alternate, ovate-elliptic, elliptic oblong, oblong to oblanceolate, dark green above, paler beneath, cuneate or long acuminate at apex, minutely appressed puberulous beneath, stipules linear, caducous, puberulous. Flowers greenish, 3-6 each in 2-3 racemose fascicles on ferruginous tomentose peduncle. Sepals unequal, broadly ovate to elliptic-orbicular, glabrous within, densely rusty, puberulous without. Petals ovate to suborbicular, concave, margin ciliate; stamens 5, anthers reniform. Female flowers solitary or binate a top a common peduncle; staminode 5, ovary rudimentary, ovoid, beat at apex, densely yellowish pilose; stigma 5 lobed. Berries globose or obovoid, pericarp redish brown, tomentose. Seeds 15-20, ovoid-oblong, longitudinally striated, embedded in pulp.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Seeds and oil are acrid, astringent, bitter and used as thermogenic, emollient, depurative, vermifuge, anodyne, purgative, emitic, carminative, stomachic, suppurative, alterant, haematinic and tonic. They are useful in leprosy, skin diseases, pruritis, leucoderma, dermatitis, bronchopathy, eczema, phthisis, and vitiated conditions of “Vata” and “Kapha”. It is also use in sprains, bruises, tubercular laryngitis, chronic ulcers, dyspepsia, colic, flatulence, verminosis, diabetes and wounds.


Chemistry:
Structure of a new flavono – lignan – hydnocarpin (fig. 1a)– isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Isohydnocarpin (fig. 2) isolated from seed hulls and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Methoxyhydnocarpin (fig. 1b) along with apigein, luteolin and chrusoeriol isolated from seed hulls. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Two new flavonolignans – hydnowightin (fig. 3) and neohydnocarpin (fig. 4) were isolated from seed coat and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Two new cyclopentenoid cyanohydrin glycosides – (1S,4R) and (1R,4S)1-[6-O(a-L-rhamnopyranosyl)-b-D-glucopyranosyloxy]-4-hydroxy-2-cyclopentene-1-carbonitrile – isolated from seeds and their structures elucidated; epivolkenin and taraktophyllin also present in small amounts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Ursolic, betulinic, acetylbetulinic and acetylursolic acids isolated from stembark; first three acids also detected in leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
