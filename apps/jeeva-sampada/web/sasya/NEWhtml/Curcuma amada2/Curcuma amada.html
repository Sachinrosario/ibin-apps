HABIT & HABITAT
Herbaceous rootstock large, ovoid; tubers sessile thick, found in almost throughout India. Widely cultivated. Herb; root stock ovoid, sessile, tubers thick, cylindric, pale yellow inside; leafy tuft 60-90 cm high, petiole as long as the lamina, leaf blade 30-45 cm by 15 cm or more, pale green, tapering to base and apex, scape from the center of the leafy tuft, spike 7.5-15.0 cm by 3.7 cm, bracts pale green, those of the coma tinted with pink; flowers as long as the bracts, whitish with pale yellow lip.


Availability :  
   Cultivated in Gujarat; wild in parts of West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Rootstock large, ovoid; tubers sessile thick, cylindric. Leaves in tufts, long-petiolete, oblong-lanceolate, 30-45 x 7.5-12.5 cm long, acute or acuminate at apex, narrowed to base; petioles as long as the blade


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers in 7.5-15.0 x 3.7-5.0 cm long spikes; peduncles c 15 cm long or more, hidden by the sheathing bases of the leaves. Flower bracts about 2.0-2.5 cm long, those of the coma tinged with pink. Corolla white or pale yellow, tube c 2.5 cm long; lip semi-elliptic, 3-lobed, yellow.
 CIMAP:     Inflorescence: Autumnal spikes; Flower: Bracteate, greenish-white, complete, hermaphrodite,,; Calyx: Obtusely 3-toothed ; Corolla: White or very pale yellow, lobes oblong, acute, lip semi-elliptic, yellow ; Androecium: only 1 perfect rest is reduced to staminodes,, anthers linear 2-celled ; Gynoecium: Ovary 3-celled ,inferior, ovules many, style slender, stigma entire; Fruit: Loculicidally 3-valved capsule ; Seed: Often arillate. Flowering & Fruiting : September-November. 
Floral and seed biology:
May - September
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.
 
Genetic variability:

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine the root is used in biliousness, itching and skin diseases, brochitis, asmtha, hiccough. In Unani system root is used in inflammation, mouth and ear troubles, ulcers on penis, scabies, stomatitis (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
Parts used: Rhizome-carminative, stomachic, incontusions and sprains.


Chemistry:
d-a-pinene, bp. 155°C/ 176mm., d-camphor, mp 175°C, 1-b-curcumene as hydrochloride, mp. 83°C, d-curcumene, mp. 115°C, phytosterol and an unidentified compound mp. 142°C, from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

The essential oil obtained by hydrodistilation from the rhizome of Curcuma amada was analyzed by GC/MS.  28 Constituents were identified, of which a-curcumene (28.1%), b-curcumene (11.2%), camphor (11.2%), curzerenone (7.1%), and 1,8-cineole (6.0%) were found as the major compounds. (Srivastava et al., 2001) CA. 2001, 135: 293792.
Essential oil contains a-Pinene, a and b-curcumene, camphor, cuminyl alcohol myristic acid and turmerone. Carnene and cis-ocimene, contribute the characteristic mango odour of the rhizome.

Pharmacology:

Significant decrease was observed in liver total lipids and serum triglycerides of adult female rats when fed 10.0% mango ginger or 10.0% curcumin along with normal diet or a sucrose based hypertriglyceridaemic diet. Feeding of 0.3% curcumin-free lipid fraction from mango ginger (corresponding to 10.0% mango ginger) along with two diets also decreased liver triglycerides in both cases, but total lipids and triglycerides of serum were decreased only in rats on hypertriglyceridaemic diet. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Certain diterpenes as well as extracts of the plant Curcuma amada containing at least one such diterpene can be used as immunosuppressants and for the alleviation of pain.  Specifically, they are used for preparing medicaments for the treatment or prevention of hypersensitivity diseases, in particular IgE-mediated allergic reactions and conditions, autoimmune disorders. (Weidner et al., 1999).

Pharmacognostical studies on rhizome and roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
