HABIT & HABITAT
A large tree, found in the evergreen forests of  Western Ghats from Konkan southwards.

It is sensitive to frost in early stages and can tolerate certain amount of shade.  
A. triphysa is a light demanding Asian tree found in wet evergreen climax forests of the western Ghats, from the Konkan, North Kanara and Karnataka southwards to Travancore.

Altitude: 60-1 500 m Mean annual temperature: 27 deg C Mean annual rainfall: 1 920 mm Soil type: Prefers well drained light/sandy soils.

Availability :  
Native: India, Myanmar
In India its natural habitat is tropical Rain forests of the western ghats, West Bengal, Assam and Sikkim.  It is more commonly distributed on the western slope of the Western Ghats.  It also occurs in Australian Rain Forests and in Sri Lanka.

Also Grown in Mangalore, in the evergreen tropical forests of Western Ghats upto 1500 mtrs. Often cultivated in South India.  


Status : 

Tree of Subtropical, dry and littoral rainforests.
Garden Use: It is an ornamental tree as it has a candelabrum type branch structure and the crown of leaflets gives its foliage a feathery appearance.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

Ailanthus triphysa is a single stemmed tree or shrub. Bole cylindrical, attaining a height of 30 m and diameter of 1.2 m. Branchlets covered with many leaf scars. Young stems pubescent. Bark gray, rough, inner bark, 1.3 cm thick, yellow and fibrous.

Leaves pinnate, large 45-60 cm long, crowded at branch ends; leaflets 5-10 pairs, ovate, oblong, sickle-shaped, tapering from the base, 7.5-15 x 2.5-5 cm, thin, shining, glabrous and glaucous beneath, very oblique at the base; petiolules 1 cm long.

Flowers white, polygamous in lax axillary panicles; pedicels short. Calyx lobes minute, pubescent, triangular, acute. Petals about 0.4 cm long, glabrous, oblong-lanceolate.

Fruit a samara, 5-7.5 cm long, reddish-brown, membranous, flat. Seed compressed, circular.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY



Breeding System :
A. triphysa is monoecious and deciduous. 


Phenology :
Flowering in November-January.

Fruits ripens during March-April, however there is variation in ripening time of fruits. Fruits every year, but good seed years is once in two or three years.  


Pollinators:


Seed Biology :

The fruit is light and wind dispersed, hence the best method of collection is by lopping the branches and allowing the fruits to dry under shade.  Around 10,000 fruits weigh one kg.  Seeds remain viable for a period of 3 months.  Fruits can be stored, provided they are well protected from insects.


Seedling Germination :
Propagated from fresh seed. Seed is broadcasted in the raised beds under shade and lightly covered with soil.  Heavy watering causes rotting of the seeds, hence should be avoided.  Germination starts within 10 days and is complete within 20 days.  Germination is very good and often 100% from good quality seeds.  Picking out is done when the seedlings are 7-8cm in afternoons on cloudy days.  Seedlings in the nursery are susceptible to defoliator attacks and can be controlled by timely spraying of insecticides.  

A. triphysa is propagated by direct sowing. It has been raised successfully by planting nursery-raised seedlings.

It can also be propagated by air layering.  

Cultivation:
Planting of bare root seedlings under suitable conditions give very good result.  The species is a strong light demander, hence comes up quite well in the open.  It can come up under varying conditions but does not tolerate water logging.  The seedlings planted in crowbar holes on suitable sites gives good result.  

This is a fast growing and hardy tree.  It develops a sparse branched, open crown. The sap of this plant contains alkaloids that have been used for medicinal preparations.

Pollarding is recommended in managing A. triphysa under a 10 year rotation. Seedlings are vulnerable to weeds and shade.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Bark, leaves, wood, fruit

Uses

  • Yields a fragrant resin called "mutteepal," which is powdered and administered in small doses, in milk, for dysentery and bronchitis. It is also employed as incense. The bark is tonic, febrifuge, and valued in dyspepsia
  • Oleo-resin : It is an excellent remedy for dysentery and bronchitis. Also used as an incense  and forms an ingredient of Agarbatti.  
  • It gives a black dye used for colouring satin.  Leaves are recommended in Cephalagia and Gastralgia.  


Harvesting Practices
Soft gum-resin is obtained when incisions are made on the bark. The exudate gradually hardens into a brittle but plastic resin with strong balsamic odour.

Produce and Products
Oleo-resin (Guggal dhoop)

Other Uses
  1. Bark-has bitter flavour. Considered as a valuable tonic, febrifuge and carminative. Juice of the fresh bark is used as a remedy for dysentery, asthma and bronchitis. Decoction of the bark is reported to be given in typhoid and constipation
  2. Leaves - black dye used for colouring satin. Leaves are recommended in Cephalagia and Gastralgia.
  3. Wood - light packing-cases, match boxes, splints and paper pulp. Also used for catamarans, veneers and plywood.
  4. A fatty oil is extracted from the fruit.
  5. Timber: Wood is used for making boats, matches, fishing floats and weaponry accessories e.g. sword handles and spear sheaths.
  6. Essential oil: Aromatic oils are obtained from the bark.
  7. Medicine: The plant roots, leaves, bark and gum exudates are used as medicine in India.
  8. Shade or shelter: A useful shade provider.
  9. Soil improver: Leaf litter of A. triphysa on decomposition restores soil fertility.
  10. Ornamental: A tree often planted for aesthetic purposes.
  11. Intercropping: Used as live stakes for supporting black pepper (Piper nigrum).
  12. Other services: The dried bark and gum exudates are burnt as incense.
