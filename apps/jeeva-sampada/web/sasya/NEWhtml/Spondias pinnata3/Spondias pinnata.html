HABIT & HABITAT
INDIA: Widely distributed in deciduous forests ascending up to 1650 m in Himalayas. Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Sikkim, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra and Andaman Islands. It is also found in Pakistan, Nepal, Myanmar, Sri lanka, Java, Philippines, Malay Peninsula and Thailand.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Deciduous trees, up to 27 m tall, with a pleasant aromatic smell. Leaves imparipinnate; leaflets 9-13, elliptic-oblng, 6-18 x 5.0-8.5 cm, abruptly long acuminate, entire, base obliquely rounded, membranous, shining with intramarginal veins; petioles 5-15 cm long.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in panicles, white, bisexual, 5 mm across, subsessile. Calyx salver shaped, 5-6 lobed, pebescent. Petals 5 to 6, 2.5 x 1 mm, glabrous. Drupes ovoid-oblong, 1.5-5.0 x 1.0-3.5 cm, 5-loculed; pulp soft, acidic, aromatic; stone semiwoody, fibrous with many cavities outside. It flowers and fruits during April to December.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 Fruit are edible, both raw and in ripe condition. Wood is used in making packing-boxes. Root is useful in regulating menstruation. The bark is used as medicine for dysentery and diarrhoea. It is also used for tanning.


Chemistry:  

b-amyrin and oleanolic acid isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
Lignoceric acid, 24-methylenecycloartanone, stigmast-4-en-3-one, b-sitosterol and its glucoside isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
Glycine, cystine, serine, alanine and leucine present in fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany: Medicine for cholera, diarrhoea (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Dysentery (Bark, Negi et al, 1985; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Earache (Leaf, Negi et al, 1985); Itch (Leaf, Srivastava and Varma, 1981); Night fever, Opthalmia (Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Revive taste (Fruit, Bhattacharjee et al, 1980); Rhumatism (Bark, Puri, 1983); Ringworm (Leaf, Srivastava and Varma, 1981); stomachache (Bark, Tarafder, 1983); Edible (Fruit, Shah, 1984; Maji and Sikdar, 1982; Jain and De, 1966; Negi et al, 1985; Jain, 1964; Bhargava, 1959; Jain et al, 1977; Maheshwari et al, 1981; Bhargava, 1981; Flower, Negi et al, 1985); Vegetable (Leaf, Kumar and Rao, 1987; Jain, 1963)
