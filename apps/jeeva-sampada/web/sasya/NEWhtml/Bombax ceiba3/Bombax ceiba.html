REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: 1-flowered; Flower: iregular, hermaphrodite, numerous near at the end of brancfes, appearing before the new leaves. ; Calyx: thick, usually 3-lobed, lobes rounded, densely silky  within, glabrous, without coming out of the receptacle with the stamens and corolla ; Corolla: bright red, tomentose on outside, sparaingly pubescent within, elliptic-oblong, recurved, with close parallel veins  ;Androecium: Stamens more than 60, arranged in 5 bundles of about 9-12 each and inner bundle of 15 of which 5 inner mosat are longest, filaments flattened, slightly pubescent, connate only at the base of the bundles  ; Gynoecium: Pentacarpellary, conical, glabrous, style a longer than a stamen, stigma 5-linear; Fruit: Capsule, long, ovoid, downy, 5-valved, lined within white silky hairs ; Seed: Numerous, ovoid, packed in white cotton . Flowering & Fruiting :.Flowers are pollinated by  insects.Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Chemistry:
Hydrolysis of gum yielded arabinose, galactose, galaturonic acid and traces of rhamnose; partial hydrolysis gave 6-O-(b-D-galactopyranosyl-urinoic acid)-D-galactopyranose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

2,3,4,6-tetra-,2,6-di and 2,4-di-O-methyl-O-D-galactose and 2,3,5-tri- and 2,5-di-O-methyl-L-arabinose identified as hydrolytic products of methylated gum. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

calyx contained moisture (85.66), crude protein (1.38), carbohydrate (11.95) and ash (1.09%); Ca, P and Mg contents of ash determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Lupeol, b-sitosterol, a naphthoquinone, m.p. 87°C, (fig. 1) and potassium nitrate isolated from root bark and stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A highly branched polysaccharide isolated from flowers; it had continuous back bone of 4(1à4)b-linked D-galactopyranose and 2(1à3)-blinked L-arabinopyranose units with b-linked D-galactose and a-linked L-rhamnose and L-arabinose units as end group. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Isolation and structure elucidation of anthocyanin A (fig. 5a) and Anthocyanin B (fig. 5b) from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A napthol (fig. 2), m.p. 144°C, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Three new biosides isolated from flower characterized as 24b-ethylcholest-5-en-3b-O-a-L-arabinopyranosyl(1à6)-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig. 3), 3,5-dihydroxy-4’-methoxyflavone-7-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à6)-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig. 4a) and 4’,5,7-trihydroxy-flavone-3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)-a-L-rhamnopyranoside (fig. 4b). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

A new lactone (fig. 6) isolated from root bark and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

N-Hexacosanol and palmitic acid isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

A new glycosides – 3’,4’,5,7-tetrahydroxy-6-methoxyflavan-3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-a-Dxylopyranoside (fig. 7) isolated from roots along with triacontanol and b-sitosterol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Four new aromatic sesquiterpenes (II fig. 8a, III, 8b, IV, 8c, V, fig. 9) isolated from root bark and their structures elucidated; previously reported hemigossypol-6-methyl ether has now been identified as isohemigossypol-1-methyl ether. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

1,6-dihydroxy-3-methyl-5-isopropyl-7-methoxy-8-napthalene carboxylic acid(8à1)lactone (fig. 10) isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Lysine, arginie, alamine, glutamic acid, leucine, fructose, glucose, galactose, sucrose, lactose and arabinose isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Palmitic (43.61), linoleic (26.24), behenic (14.39), myristic (13.44) and arachidic (2.32%) acids, b-sitosterol, a-tocopherol, glucose, xylose and rhamnose identified in seeds; protein content of defatted seed was 9.3% and alanine, valine, leucine, arginine, glycine and aspartic acid were present in protein. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

An electrophoretically homogenous polysaccharide isolated from defatted seeds, composed of galactose and arabinose in ratio 7:3. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

A sesquiterpene lactone isolated from (Salmalia malbaricum) Bombax malbaricum. Isohemigossylic acid (fig. 11) lactone-2-Me ether (R-OH, R2-OMe, R3-H) which in inhibits the growth of verticillium diahliae condida. (Puckhaher and Stipanovic, 2001). C.A. 2001, 134, 193580f.

Physico-chem properties and extraction conditions of the red pigment in flowers of B. malabaricum were investigated. (Wang, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 208201e.

Content of polysaccharides in B. malabaricum was determined by spectrometery using H2SO4 – anthraquinone. Wang et al, 2000). C.A. 2001, 134, 219800c.

Shamimin a new flavonol C-glycoside has been isolated as a pale yellow powder from the ethanolic extract of fresh, undried leaves of Bombax ceiba. Its structure has been elucidated as 2-(2,4,5-trihydroxyphenyl)-3,5,7-trihydroxy-6-C- glucopyranosyloxy-4H-1-benzopyran-4-one through extensive spectroscopic methods (IR, mass, 1H- and 13C-NMR), and 2D-NMR experiments (Faizi and Ali).


Pharmacology:
Shamimin, a c-flavanol glucoside from Bombax ceiba leaves showed significant potency as a hypotensive agent at the doses of 15,3 and 1 mg/kg and significant hypoglycemic activity at 500 mg/kg in rats. (Saleem et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 120680s.

Shamimin, a C-flavonol glucoside from Bombax ceiba leaves showed significant potency as a hypotensive agent at the doses of 15 mg/kg, 3 mg/kg, 1 mg/kg and significant hypoglycaemic activity at 500 mg/kg in Sprague-Dawley rats. (Ahmad et al., 1999)
