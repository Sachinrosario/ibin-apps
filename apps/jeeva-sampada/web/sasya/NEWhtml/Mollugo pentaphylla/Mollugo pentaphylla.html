DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual, diffuse, 15-20 cm long, glabrous herb. Stems numerous, with many quadrangular leafy dichotomously arranged branches. Leaves in false whorls of 3-9; lower ones rosulate, spathulate, distinctly petioled; upper linear-lanceolate to obovate, 1.0-3.8 x 0.3-0.8 cm, obtuse or acute at apex, sometimes apiculate, much narrowed at the base, shortly petioled or subsessile. Flowers white, numerous, in lax corymbose terminal cymes; bracts small, persistent; pedicels erecto-patent, thin, 0.1-0.5 cm long, persistent. Sepals ovate-oblong, 1-2 mm long, obtuse, parallel nerved. Stamens usually 3. Capsules subglobose to ellipsoid, c 2 mm long, 3-lobed. Seeds numerous, reniform, compressed, tubercled, brownish-black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
It is useful in stomachic, aperient and antiseptic properties. An infusion of the plant is given to women to promote the menstrual discharge. The bitter leaves are antiperiodic.


Chemistry:
8C-a-L-Arabinopyranosyl-apigenin and two 6C-arabinosyl-8C-pentosyl-apigenins isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Structure of one 6C-arabinosyl-8C- pentosyl-apigenin revised as 6C-b-D-xylopyranosyl-8C-a-L-arabinopyranosyl-apigenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Apigenin-8C-glucoside, eriodictyol, apigenidin-5-O-glucoside and pelargonidin-3,5-di-O-glucoside isolated from plant grown in Bhagalpur. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Oleanolic acid, mollugogenols A, B and C, a-L-rhamnopyranosyl(1®4)-b-D-glucopyranosyl(1®3)-mollugogenol A and b-sitosterol isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

An antifungal compound – mollugogenol A - isolated from aerial parts along with inactive mollugenol B. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).


Pharmacology:


Ethnobotany:
Medicine as antiseptic, stomachache (Whole plant, Sharma et al, 1979); Toe fracture (Whole plant, Srivastava and Varma, 1981).
