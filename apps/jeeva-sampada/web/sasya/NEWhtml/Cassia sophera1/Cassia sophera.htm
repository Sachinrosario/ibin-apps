Chemistry
The leaves contain a new flavone glycoside, 3,5,3',4',5'-pentahydroxy-7-methoxyflavone-8-C-L-rhamnopyranoside (C22H22O12, m p 235' decomp) and sennoside (0.07%). Analysis of the seeds gave the following results; moisture-6.7; protein-15.8; pentosans-14.7; and water sol mucilage-20%. They contain: ascorbic acid-93.1 mg/100 and beta-sitosterol. The rootbark contains three new anthraquinones; 1,7-dihydroxy-3, 6-dimethoxy-2-methyl-7-vinylanthraquinone (C19H16O6, m p 261' decomp), 1,8-dihydroxy-2-methylanthraquinone (C18H16O7, m p 305' decomp)besides chrysophanol, physcion and beta-sitosterol

Other Uses

1.The leaves are used for green manure.

2.The plant exhibited anti cancer ativity. It is reported to be extensively used in Homoeopathy. A decoction of the plant is antidiuretic.

3.The leaves possesses purgative properties. Their juice, made in to a paste with sandalwood or mixed with lime juice, is considered specific for ring worm; externally it is used for washing syphilitic sores. The juice in honey or an infusion or decoction is administered as an expectorant for cough. cold, brnachitis, asthma and hiccups. An infusion of fresh leaves is given with sugar in jaundice and in sub-acute stages of gonorrhea. Internally, it is reported to act as a febrifuge in rheumatic and inflammatory fevers and in skin diseases. A paste of the leaves is used in curing piles; also a paste of the young leaves and flower buds is a valuable remedy for mange in horses and dogs.

1.      The seeds are cathartic, and also used as a febrifuge. The powdered seeds, mixed with honey, are administered in diabetes. An ointment made from the seeds of C. sophera and Raphanus sativus Linn., sulphur and water is used to cure ringworm, sores, scabies and psoriasis. In China, they are used for making the eyes bright.

2.      The bark, like leaves and seeds, is cathartic in action. Its infusion is considered useful in diabetes and its juice in asthma. It is also used in skin diseases and in the treatment of ringworm but sometimes is reported to cause dermatitis.

3.      The roots are considered diuretic. A paste of the roots is sometimes used to adulterate those of C. occidentalis.
