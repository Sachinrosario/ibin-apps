 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Erect, branched, aromatic, sparingly hispidulous herb, 60-200 cm tall. It is found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and Rajasthan up to 2500-4000 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves triangular-ovate, 20-50 cm in diam., 1-3 ternate or ternate-pinnate; leaflets ovate-lanceolate to oval, 2-12 x 1-6 cm, acuminate at apex, coarsely mucronate-dentate along margin, sometimes sparsely hispidulous beneath; petioles stout, c 20 cm long; upper cauline leaves with much reduced blades and broadly dilated sheaths. Peduncles stout, 10-30 cm long. Involucre and involucel of 5-10 linearly subulate, reflexed bracts or bracteoles, 5-20 mm long. Rays 10-40, slender, sub-equal to unequal, 2-6 cm long, glabrous to hispidulous, spreading-ascending. Umbellets 20-40 flowered, mature pedicels slender, unequal, spreading-ascending, 1-2 cm long. Flowers greenish-white; petals spatulate to obovate, glabrous or spreading hispidulous; calyx obsolete; stylopodium low conical, styles recurved; ovaries glabrous. Fruits oblong-ovate, 7-14 x 4-8 mm, emarginate at apex and base, glabrous, dorsal ribs prominent, corky, lateral broadly corky winged, wing broader than body. Vittae solitary in intervals, 2-4 on slightly concave commissure.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Umbels; Flower: Greenish, primary and secondary ray numerous; Calyx: Sepals 5, tiny; Corolla:  Petals 5Androecium: Stamens 5 ;Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 2-celled, ; Fruit: Mericarp, with white lateral wings and conspicuous ribs. Flowering & Fruiting: June – August. Flowers are pollinated by insects and seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Whole plant is used as cardial and stimulant. It is also useful in flatulence and dyspepsia.

Psoralen by degradation and synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Umbelliprenin, isoimperatorin, bergapten,-prangolarin, ostruthol and oxypeucedanin hydrate (fig. 3) from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Archangelicain, prangolarin, angalicain, ostruthol, m.p. 138° and oxypeucedanin hydrate, m.p. 134°, from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)d.

Phellopterin (fig. 2) from fruits identified as 4-methoxy-7(-g,g-dimethylallyloxy)-psoralen by degradation and synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)e.

Isolation and structure of new flavanone – archangelenone (fig. 4) from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Structure of archangelin (fig. 6) revised and confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Revised structure of angelicain (fig. 2). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A new macrocuyclic lactone – 12-methyl-w-tridecanolide (fig. 5) – from root oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Determination of limonene (22.7), a-phellanderene (22.5%) in root oil; a-phallendrene (67.8) in leaf oil and cineole (53.8%) in fruit oils as main compounds by chromatographic analysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Imperatorin, isoimperatorin, bergapten, isoimpinellin and 8-hydroxy-5-methoxypsoralen along with acetates of lupeol and a-amyrin isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

Root extract showed presence of lauric, tridecanoic, myristic, pentadecanoic, palmitic, palmitoleic, stearic, oleic, linoleic and linolenic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)g.

Out of seventeen compounds found in fruits, imperatorin, bergapten, xanthotoxol, xanthotoxin and isoimpenellin isolated and identified. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)h.

Petroselininc, linoleic, stearic, palmitic acids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)i.

Lauric, myristic,palmitoleic, arachidic, eicosanoic acids (1.0%), b-sitosterol and stigmasterol from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)j.

Detection of a-pinene and b-phelladrene in leaves and b-phellandrene in flower and fruit essential oils by TLC and GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)k.

2-nitro-1,5-p-menthadiene (fig. 8), trans- and cis-6-nitro1(7),2-p-menthadiene (II fig. 9a, III fig. 9b). Trans-1(7),5-p-menthadien-2-yl-acetate (IV fig. 9c) and 7-isopropyl-5-methyl-5-bicyclo[2.2.2]octen-2-one (fig. 10) identified in root oil and synthesized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

a- and b-phallendrenes, a-pinene, myrcene, camphene, b-pinene and a-terpinene isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

b-cyclolavandulyloxypsoralen isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

detectionof b-caryophyllne and terpenes in essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)f.

dihydrofuranocoumarin glycosides (VI fig. 11, VII fig. 12) isolated from roots and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)g.

Furanocoumarins xanthotoxin, bergapten and imperatorin were isolated from A. archangelica. (Glowniak et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 124, 4914c.

Recent development made in the chemistry of essential oils of angelica roots (Angelica archangelica) has been reviewed. (Lawrence et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9703, 1759.

The macrolide fraction of Angelica root oil Angelica archangelica syn. Archangelca officanils was analyzed and quantified by GC (coinjection) and GC/MS. Omega alkaloids C13 (50.0%), C14 (0.5%), C15 (42.0%), C16 (trace) and C17 (3.5%) as well as 12-methyl-13-tridecanolide (4.0%) were identified. The enantiomeric composition of the latter copmpound with characteristic odor differences of the enantiomers was found to be percent (12R)-(+)-3 and 27.6% (12S)-(-)-3. (Schultz and Kraft, 1997). MAPA 1998, 980-1118.

Two root samples and 33 seed samples of Angelica archangelica collected from different localities were extracted with n-hexane and analysed by GC-MS. Major compounds in the root oils were (-)-a-pinene (19-42%) and (+)-sabinene (21-28%). One of the oils contained 22% (+)-3-carene but the other one had none at all. The seed oils were dominated by (-)-sabinene, (+)-a-pinene, myrcene, (-)-a-phellandrene, (-)-a-pinene and (-)limonene. Some statistically significant differences between seed samples from different localities were found. (Holm et al, 1997). MAPA 1998, 9805-3014.

The isolated Coumarins from Angelica archangelica (Syn. Archangelica officinalis) were analyzed by HPLC-MS with atmospheric pressure Chemical ionization (APCI). (Roos et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 99858g.

Major compounds in the root oils were (-)-a-pinene (19-42%) and (+)-sabinene (21-28%) one of the oils contained 22% (+)-3-carene but the other one had none at all. The seed oils were dominated by (-)-b-phellandrene (>60%) other major compounds were (+)-sabinene, (+)-a-pinene, myrcene (-)-a-phellandrene (-)-a-pinene and (-)-limonene. (Holm et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 190403r.

Pharmacology:
The isolated coumarins were tested for anti-inflammatory activity in terms of in vivo cyclo-oxygenase-1 (COX-1) and 5-lipoxygenase (5-LO) inhibition. They were found to be not active in the COX-1 assay. Concerning 5-LO inhibition, osthol and oxypeucedanin hydrate isovalerate turned out to be active, whereas the other observed effects were only weak, not concentration dependent and possibly explainable by the calcium antagonistic properties of these coumarins. (Roos et al, 1997). C.A. 1998, 128, 99858g.
