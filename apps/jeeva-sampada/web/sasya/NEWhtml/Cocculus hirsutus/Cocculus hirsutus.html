REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Inflorescence: Shining axillary panicles; Flower: Regular, complete ; Calyx: Sepals 6, 2-seriate,  ; Corolla:  Petals 5, smaller, usually auricled; Male Flower: Bracteate, minute, stamens embraced by petals, anthers subglobose, cells bursting transversely ; Gynoecium: Ovary 3, styles usually cylindric ; Fruit:Drupe, laterally compressed; Seed: Curved, albumen fleshy. Flowering & Fruiting : August- November. Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by birds or animals

Chemistry:
D-Trilobine and DL-coclaurine from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

b-sitosterol, ginnol and monomethyl ether of inositol, m.p. 226°C was isolated; essential oil, b.p. 127°C and two alkaloids were also isolated but not characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Trilobine, isotrilobine, coclaurine and magnoflorine from stems and roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

A new alkaloid – cocsuline-N2-oxide was isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Isolation of trilobine, isotrilobine, (+)syringaresinol and protoquercitol from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

New triterpenoid alcohol – hirsudiol (fig. 1) isolated and its structure determined; nonacosan-10-ol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Isolation of novel alkaloid – cohirsine (fig. 4a)– and determination of its structure and stereostructure by 2D NOESY and NOE difference measurements. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Isolation of a new alkaloid – cohirsinine – and its structure elucidation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Isolation and structure determination of new alkaloid – shaheenine (fig. 4b). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

An isoquinoline alkaloid – cohirsitinine (fig. 3) was isolated and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Another new isoquinoline alkaloid – cohirsitine ( 2) was isolated from aerial parts and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Aerial parts afforded a new alkaloid – haiderine (fig. 6) whose structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Isolation of new alkaloid – jamtinine (fig. 7) and its structure determination. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

New alkaloid – hirsutine (fig. 5) was isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

Pharmacology:

The water-soluble fraction of ammoniacal ethanol extract of stems and roots showed sedative, hypotensive, bradycardiac and spasmolytic actions. It had slight anticonvulsant action but no analgesic and diuretic activities. The hypotensive and bradycardiac action were not found to be mediated through nervous system. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

The aqueous extract of the Cocculus hirsutus aerial parts (100 and 200 mg/kg, p.o.) showed significant diuretic activity and laxative effect in rats. The acute toxicity, orally evaluated in mice, was found to be higher than 3000 mg/kg. (Ganapaty et al, 2002).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for bed-wetting by children (Leaf, Bhalla et al, 1982; Vir Jee et al, 1984); Blood dysentery (Part not mentioned, Saxena et al, 1981); Conjunctivitis, other eye disorder (Leaf, Audichya et al, 1983; Bedi, 1978; Stem, Shah et al, 1983); Cooling (Leaf, Bedi, 1978; Chaudhuri et al, 1975; Saxena et al, 1981); Cuts (Leaf, Joshi, 1982); Eczema (Leaf, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Shah et al, 1981); Fever (Leaf, Shah, 1984; Shah et al, 1981; Root, Shah, 1984); Gonorrhoea (Leaf, Shah, 1984; Shah et al, 1983; Venugopal and Raghunathan, 1982); Gout (Root, Goel et al, 1984); Irritation on skin (Leaf, Shah, 1984; Shah et al, 1981; Root, Shah, 1984); Laxative (Leaf, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973); Leucorrhoea (Leaf, Chaudhuri et al, 1975; Hemadri and Rao, 1983); Nursing mother (Leaf, Goel et al, 1984); Rheumatism (Leaf, Shah, 1984; Shah et al, 1983; Singh and Pandey, 1980; Shah et al, 1981; Root, Shah, 1984; Singh and Pandey, 1980); Skin Disease (Leaf, Shah et al, 1983); Sores (Leaf, Joshi, 1982); Stomach disease (Stem, Shah et al, 1983; Part not mentioned, Saxena et al, 1981); Urinary disease (Leaf, Bhalla et al, 1982; Saxena and Dutta, 1975); Venereal complaints (Leaf, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Root, Goel et al, 1984); Wounds (Leaf, Joshi, 1982); veterinary galactagogue ( Leaf + Shoot, Shah et al, 1981; Part not mentioned, Sebastian, 1984); Edible as vegetable (Leaf, Vartak, 1981; Jain, 1964; Stem, Srivastava and Varma, 1981)
