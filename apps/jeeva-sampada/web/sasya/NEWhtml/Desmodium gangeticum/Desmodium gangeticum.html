REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Terminal and axillary raceme ;Flower: Pedicels filiform, pubescent, bract subulate, triangular, hermaphrodite, complete, regular; Calyx: Hairy, teeth triangular, longer than the campanulate tube ; Corolla: Violet or white cuneate at the base, wings obliquely oblong, more or less adhering to the keel, keel obtuse; Androceium: Vexillary stamen entirely free, the other 9 united, anthers uniform; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, ovules infinite, style incurved, stigma terminal, minute, capitate; Fruit: Pods sub falcate, deeply indented on the lower, slightly indented on the upper edge, sparsely covered with minute hairs; Seed: 1;

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (fig. 1), N,N-dimethyltryptamine (fig. 2), their N-oxides, N-methyl-tetrahydroharman, 6-methoxy-b-carbolinium cation (regenerated from Reinecke salt) from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Alkaloids isolated from aerial parts were found responsible for anticholinesterase, smooth muscle stimulant, CNS stimulant and depressor responses in test animals. Tert-b-phenylethylamines and candicine present in roots, responsible for nicotine like effects on dog intestine in situ and carotid blood pressure. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

A new pterocarpan – gangetin (fig. 3)– isolated and characterized as 7a,12a-dihydro-13-methoxy-3,3-dimethyl-11-(3-methyl-2-butenyl)- 3H,7H-benzofuro[3,2-C]pyrano[3,2-g]benzopyran-10-ol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Twelve alkaloids of our structural types (carboxylated and decarboxylated tryptamine, b-carbolines and b-phenylethylamines) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Two pterocarpanoids – gangetinin (fig. 4) and desmodin (fig. 5)– isolated and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

A new antifungal isoflavanoid phytoalexin desmocarpin (fig. 6) was isolated together with genistein, 2’-hydroxygenistein, dalbergioiden, diphysolone and kievitone from fungus-inoculated leaflets; its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

24-ethylcholesta-5,22-dien-3b-ol, 24-ethylcholest-5-en-3b-ol and 24-methylcholest-5-en-3b-ol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

A novel flavone glycoside, 4’,5,7-trihydroxy-8-prenyl-flavone 4’-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1à6)-b-D-glucopyranoside was isolated from the stem of  Desmodium gangeticum. (Yadav and Tripathi, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 220376v.


Pharmacology:

An inhibitory influence of gangetin over the reproductive organs is belived to be attributed primarily to the antiprolactin nature of gangetin and secondarily to the significant fall in plasma testosterone level caused by gangetin. (Latha and Govindasamy, 1997).

Daily administration of gangetin (0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2mg/kg body weight for 30 days) caused and impairment of fertility and also caused a reduction in the vaginal sperm count and an enhancement of Pre-implantation  losses. Complete recovery of fertility was evident 30days after the with drawal of gangetin treatment. (Latha et al, 1997)


Ethnobotany:
Medicine as antidote to snake venom (Root, Goel et al, 1984); Bronchitis (Whole plant, Karnick et al, 1981); Cough (Root, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973; Sharma et al, 1979); Dysentery (Root, Rao, 1981; Saxena and Vyas, 1983; Maheshwari et al, 1986; Part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Eye infection (Part not mentioned, Goel et al, 1984); fever (Root, Sharma et al, 1979; Rajwar, 1983; Whole plant, Karnick et al, 1981); Tonic (Whole plant, Karnick et al, 1981); Vomiting (Root, Sharma et al, 1979); paper (Stem fibre, Viswanathan et al, 1981)
