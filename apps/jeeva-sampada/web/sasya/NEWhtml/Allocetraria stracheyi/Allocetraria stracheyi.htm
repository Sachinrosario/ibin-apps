Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A. stracheyi, a foliose lichen usually grows on soil in alpine meadows at the altitudes of 4200-5800m. occasionally epiphytic on Rhodendron.
It is also found on soil among mosses at 3800-4500 m. altitudes in North
America (Randlane & Saag, 2001).

Availability :

This taxon has generally been considered a typical Himalayan species, known from China, India, Nepal and North America (Awasthi,1982). It is widely distributed throughout the temperate Himalayas as follows.
Arunachal Pradesh : West Kameng district,Bomdila to Ralang and Shergaon. Uttaranchal : Almora district ,Dhakuri ridge; Khati to Dwali ; Kumaon, Khathi; Uttarkashi district, near Jamnotry.West Bengal : Darjeeling district,Tiger hill.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

The Thallus is dorsiventral, It is yellow on the upper surface and brown on the lower surface with elongate lobes, which are rather narrow (up to 4mm) and distinctly convex on the upper side. The Medulla is white or pale yellow to orange in colour. (Randlane & Saag 2001).

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
As medicine: The species is listed in Pharmacopoeia Universalis of 1846, purely on the basis of their folkloric medicinal uses. In Sweden, this species was used for treating diabetes, lung diseases and cataract. Even today Cetraria islandica can be purchased in European pharmacies for the preparation of herbal medicines(Richardson, 1988;Elix A.J.,1996).
Cetraria islandica thalli are made into pastilles and teas. Decoctions of the later are valued for their astringent flavour. The firm of f. Hunziker at Dietikon near Zürich uses 150 kg per year of this lichen imported from the Balkan states to manufacture such products(Richardson,1975).

As food: In Europe this lichen was used very widely as food in times of hardship. In Norway, during the years of bad harvest from 1807 to 1814, dried Iceland moss was used to supplement flour. It was considered a better substitute than bark flour (phloem tissue from elm, pine or birch), which was also used to a great extent. The lichen was soaked in lye (aqueous extract of fresh wood ash) for 24 hrs, which presumably neutralized the lichen acids. It was then dried and blended with grain before being ground into flour. Unfermented flat breads or porridge were usually made from the flour.
The species probably rates first as human lichen food. In Norway and Sweden, Cetraria islandica powder is mixed with various cereals and mashed potatoes from which uncommonly healthful bread is prepared. The powder is mixed with flour, making the bread less friable or less subject to Weevil attack.

Similar to other Cetraria species the taxon is rich in usnic acid contents, may be utilized for its antibiotic properties.
