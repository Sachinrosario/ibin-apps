Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Moderate sized tree, occurs in rainfed areas in the temperate region. Some varieties can also be grown under sub-tropical regions. Found at the Himalayan foothills and central Indian foothills. found almost naturalised in north western Himalayas, Particularly in the valleys of Kashmir, Chenab and Kulu and in Simla hills at altitudes up to nearly 3,000 m.

Susceptible to frost, cultivated only in warm temperate areas. Thrives well in areas with moderate summer temperatures, at altitudes of 850-1700 m. A porous, light but rich, well-drained loamy soil is said to be very suitable.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

5-8 m tall and 4-6 m spread with strong but shabby branches. Canopy is generally oval to conical. Main trunk is straight but many branches arise above union point, which should be avoided. Bark is scaly, light brown to dark brown in colour, sometimes bark may be of reddish brown colour. Branches curl or twist. Root system is both tap and adventitious. It may go 10 m deep and 5 m in spread.

Leaves are simple, thin, leathery, petioled, green to lush green in colour, ovate to round with sharply pointed tip, veins less prominent except the mid vein, size 6X4 cm. Shining on upper surface.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers white or pinkish white, usually appear solitary or in pairs before the emergence of leaves. Bloom lasts for 3-5 days.

Fruits are yellow to golden yellow and may have reddish blush on the skin, size is 3-6X2-4 cm. Mesocarp is yellow to golden yellow, 0.5 to 1.5 cm thick, juicy, sweet to slightly acidic in nature, edible along with the skin, fragrant and sometimes fibrous to some extent. Endocarp is covering of the seed. Fruits may be round to conical, oblong or globose, hairy or velvety, divided by a central groove or suture.

Seed is covered by a thin brown hard-coat which has a bold kernel, one or two, oblong in shape, 0.5X0.25 cm.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Wild apricot seeds are used for raising the seedling which are later grafted or budded before transplanting in the field. Spacing is 4-5 m.

Grown by branch cuttings. Propagated by "T" or sheild budding on wild apricot or peach or myrobalan plum rootstocks.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Nectria cinnabarina (Tode) Fr. - coral spot of wood

Phyllosticta prunicola (Opiz) Sacc. - brown patch rot of leaf

Polyporus hispidus (Bull.) Fr. - spongy heart rot

Puccinia pruni-spinosae Pers. - brown rust

Pseudomonas sp. - canker, serious disease, cankers appear on trunk, branches and fruits, exuding large drops of gum.

Emperorrhinus defoliator Marshall - weevil

Anomala polita Blanchard - Chafer beetle, damage shoots and leaves.

Caterpillars of moth Lymantria obfuscata Wlk. - sometimes completely defoliate the plant.

Oryzaephilus (Silvanus) surinamensis Linn. - saw-toothed beetle, on dried fruit.

Eurytoma samsonowi Vasiljev - chalcid

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel. Cake left after extraction of oil from kernel is utilized as a fuel.

The pit shells have been used for the preparation of activated carbon. High quality charcoal for use in case-hardening or heat treatment of steels by carburizing is obtained by destructive distillation of the shells.

Wood: Wood is good for furniture. Also yields timber.

Other uses:

Beverage - Apricot nector is a popular beverage. It is prepared by steaming and converting the ripe soft fruit into purae and mixing it with sugar syrup containing some citric acid. Apricot beverages have low acidity and so tend to be somewhat flat. They blend particularly well withmore acid fruit juices.

Apricot leather - Kamaradin or apricot leather, extensively used in drinks and sauces in the Middle East, is prepared by drying the

Fruits - Fruits are eaten fresh or dried. They are processed to make preparations like jam, nectar, squash, etc. Fruits are edible. Eaten fresh or tinned or used to make juice, brandy, liqueurs.

 

Kernel - Kernels may be sweet or bitter, depending upon the type. Sweet kerenls resemble almond in taste and are used as its substitute in pastes and confectionary and can be added to apricot jam.

Kernel oil - Fatty oil extracted from apricot kernels, is commercially important. It is employed as an adulterant or substitute for almond oil. Also used for food purposes and in cosmetics and pharmaceutical preparations. Sells at higher price than most other food oil, but at a lower price than almond oil. Also useful medicinally. Cake left after extraction utilized as.

Seeds - Seeds are used for extraction of oil which is used for cooking and in ships. It is a good lubricant. The cake left after pressing out the oil is used as manure or fuel. Seed - Oil is obtained from seeds, which is used in cosmetics.

Miscellaneous Information

 Deciduous tree suitable for house compounds and field edges.

Prunus armeniaca includes a number of botanical varieties and cultivated types, some of them often considered as distinct species or sub-species.

The various types are classified on three characteristics of the fruit, viz. Sewwt or bitter kernel, clingstone or freestone and stone pervious or not. Fruits are rich in vitamin A. 10 monoterpenes are active in flavour.
Cultivated for edible fruits.
