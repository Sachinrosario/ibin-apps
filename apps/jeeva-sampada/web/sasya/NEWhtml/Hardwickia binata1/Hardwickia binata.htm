Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Deciduous tree found in subhumid Sutlej-Ganga Basin, subhumid to humid eastern and southeastern upland and arid western plain. Found in dry Savannah forests of the Deccan Peninsula, Central India and parts of Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.

It can stand dry, shallow rocky ground. Its best development is attained in sandy loams and reddish gravelly sand overlying sandstones, conglomerates, granites and schists. It is frost- and drought hardy.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Tree with numerous spreading branches. Bark dark grey, rough, peeling off in papery flakes. Leaves alternate, rachis tipped with a minute deciduous bristle; petioles glabrous; stipules small, caducous. Leaflets 1 pair, subsessile, obtuse, 3-6 nerved from the base, tinged with red when young, glabrous. Flowers in axillary and terminal panicles, pedicels filiform. Calyx segments oblong, obtuse, membranous, veined, often dotted. Stamens usually 10, filaments very slender. Stigma peltate. Pods 2 3, star-shaped, veined, glabrous, narrowed at both the ends, thin, flexible, opening at the apex. Seed 1, near the tip of the pod.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Plant propagated by seed. Tree sheds its pods in May and seed germinate at the commencement of the rains. The factors favouring the natural reproduction are: porosity and depth of soil, protection from sun in the early stages, and prevention from fire and grazing. Best time for plantation is July.

Tree coppices indifferently. It reproduces by root suckers.

Artificial propagation is by direct sowing or transplanting. Direct sowing is more successful.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Uses:

Wood used as fuel. Extremely durable wood makes excellent piles for bridge-foundations. Wood also used as timber. It is largely used locally for naves of cart wheels, oil mills, ploughs, clod crushers and for posts and beams, mine props, bridges, wells, pontoons, oars and parquet floors. It is also used for carving, turning and ornamental work. It is suitable for bench screws, lathe chucks, tool handles, sheaves of rope blocks, railway keys, tent pegs and brake blocks. It is used as a substitute for Lignum Vitae (from Guaiacum officinale) for bearings. It is the heaviest of the Indian timbers. Used for construction and ornamental work.


Parts Used: Bark, fruits and leaves.

Miscellaneous Information


Wood: Wood is extremely hard, very heavy (sp. gr. 1.08; wt. 69 lb./ cu.ft.) and durable. It is dark red to dark brown in colour with darker streaks, often with a purplish cast, dull with oily feel, irregularly interlocked-grained and coarse-textured.

Bole gives substantial biomass under proper silvi-pastoral treatments. The branchwood is much less as compared to bolewood.

The trees are planted as windbreak. The tree is often pollarded not only for the fodder and manure supplied by the leaves, but also for the fibre obtained from the young branches. Presence of resin helps preservation of wood.
Good avenue tree.
