Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large tree in humid west Himalayan region. Distributed in Himalayas from Kangra eastwards to the Goalpara division of Assam and southwards throughout the peninsula, ascending to an altitude of about 1,200 m.

Tree attains the largest dimensions on deep, rich, alluvial soil. On poor and shallow soil, particularly on hilly grounds, though often plentiful, it remains stunted. It favours stiff, clayey soil, where it often becomes gregarious in depressions and round the edges of swamps. Grows on variety of geological formations. Strong light demander, suppressed under shade. Sensitive to frost, tolerant to damage by fire. Deep root system, providing root suckers wherever roots are exposed. Common associate of sal and teak.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

 

Large, straight stemmed tree with spreading branches and heavy crown. Bark grey to black with deep long fissures and transverse cracks. Leaves elliptic or ovate with 1-2 glands near the base.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers dull yellow in terminal and axillary panicles. Fruit up to 5 cm long, with 5 broad, horizontally veined wings.

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Old leaves fall during January - February. Tree is ompletely leaflessduring March - April. New leaves appear by June - July. Flowering is during May - July. Fruits mature by October.


Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:


Seed Germination & Propagation: Seed propagation is practised. Best time for plantation is July. Sedlings are fairly hardy against drought.

Natural regeneration is by seeds and coppicing. Artificial regeneration is by direct sowing, transplanting and through root or shoot cuttings. Planting the entire seedlings is not generally satisfactory and if attempted should be done before taproot has reached any great length.

Coppice and pollard well.



MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

A number of insects have been recorded on the tree. The larvae of insects defoliate the tree. Acrocercops vanula and Phylocnists syngupta mine leaves.

Several fungi like Daedala flavida (white spongy rot), Fomes melanoporus (white butt rot), Ganoderma lucidum (spongy rot and butt rot), Termates straminae (white stub rot), Irpex flavus Klotz., Polyporus hirsutus Wulf. are found on the tree but damage is seldom serious.

Phyllactinia terminaliae - leaves

Clavania sp.

Nymphs often attack the ovary and prevent formation of fruit, laeding to production of galls (mistaken for fruits).

Defoliators also attack the tree, but the damage is rarely serious. Among the more important of these are: Metanastria hyrtaca Cramer, Dasychira mendosa Hubner and Prodenia litura Fabr.

Seeds are susceptible to insect attack soon after maturity on trees.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Top ends, branches and wood logs make excellent fuelwood and charcoal. Calorific value : sapwood – 5,047 kcal, 9,084 Btu., heartwood – 5,373 cal, 9,672 Btu. Calorific value is 84-91% of that of pure carbon.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, pinkish-white to pinkish-grey or pale grey. Heartwood walnut-brown to deep brown with darker streaks. Wood hard to very hard, heavy to very heavy, average weight 880 kg/m3 (55lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; coarse-textured with straight to interlocked grain.

A diffuse-porous wood. Growth rings present, usually distinct under the lens, delimited by a fine line of parenchyma. Pores large, clearly visible to the eye, scanty, solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3, open or partially filled with tyloses; vessel lines prominent on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly aliform forming light-coloured eyelets round the pores or occasionally confluent connecting adjacent pores, and also as a fine more or less continuous line delimiting growth rings. Rays fine to very fine, visible only under the lens, as numerous, closely-spaced lines.

It is one of the most universally employed of all the important Indian timbers. Wood widely used for buildings, door frames, timber, window frames, construction of carts, furniture, rice pounders, toys, shafts, agricultural implements, irrigation water wheels, tool handles. Decorative plywood and electric transmission poles are made.

It is also used in construction of oil-mills, rice-pounders, engine brake-blocks, electric casing and rough carpentry. In Kolar Gold Fields, it is largely used as side-props in shafts and galleries. In railway wagons, it is used for floor-boards and doors. Treated timber is very much used for railway sleepers. The timber is suitable for use as telegraphic or electric poles after treatment.

It can be used for making unbleached sulphate pulps for production of wrapping-paper and for bleached pulps for production of writing and printing-papers.

It is a suitable timber for use in fire-proof buildings, as it shows considerable natural resistance to fire.

Wood yields timber. Common timber.

Other uses:

Leaves - Leaves are considered a good fodder. Leaves used as feed of tasar silkworm. Leaves are used as food for silkworms.

Bark - Bark yields tannin, used for tanning and dyeing material. In southern India, bark is burnt to obtain its rich lime content, which is eaten with betel leaf. Also useful medicinally.

Gum - Tree exudes a gel-like fluid which dries up to a light-yellow to amber-coloured gum, used as purgative and as an adhesive.
