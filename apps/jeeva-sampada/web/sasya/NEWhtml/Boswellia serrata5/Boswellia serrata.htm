Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Dry forests from Punjab to W.B. and in peninsular India.
Common at the foot of the Western Himalayas in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, M.P., Bihar, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh and further South in the peninsula. Large forest in the Khandesh and Nagpur-Wardha Dw in Maharashtra.

The tree prefers the dry, hot exposures of rocky hills especially the volcanic traps. On poor and shallow sold or where the ground is ferriginous, it is found pure or nearly so.
In North India, it is found on the Southern slopes of the Sivalik hills, flanking the Himalayas. In Raj, occupies the drier hills on metamorphic rocks, chaflygneiss and schists.
In central and peninsular India, it occupies the hotter slopes and ridges, usually on trap.
The tree is able to thrive and search fair dimensions even where other species remain stunted.
It is a strong light-demander but frost tender.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A medium to large sized deciduous, balsmiferous tree, upto 18m in t 82.4 mm in girth.
Bark greenish grey, yellow or reddish grey, fairly thick, smooth, oxfoliating in thin papery flakes, resinous inside.
Leaves impair pinnate, 30-45 cm long;
Leaflets 2.5-6.3 cm x 1.2-3.0 cm, ovate or ovate-lanceolate, variable;
Flowers small, white, in axillary racemes or panicles;
Drupes 12 mm long, trigonous, splitting along 3 values;
Pyreness compressed, hard, winged.
The tree produces root suckers.

Seedling germination :

Natural regeneration from seed and root-suckers is usually good, even on the poorest soils. Artificial regeneration is done through large cuttings, over 10 cm in diameter; they should be planted in the rainy season when soil is damp.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Timber - The sapwood is grayish white turning grayish brown, resinous and very susceptible to fungal sap-satin; the heartwood is yellowish brown to dark greenish brown, often streaked with darker brown wavy lines.

The wood is dull with a rough feel, faintly scented, light to heavy (wt. 448-672 kg/m2). Treatment with preservatives make the wood more durable.

Pulp from the wood is short-fiberd (as length 0.88mm; da 24u). Oleo-gum-resin - This is the only non-conferous source of turpentine and rosin in India. The resin secreted from the cortex is fragrant, transparent and golden yellow, and solidifies to brownish yellow tears or drops and crusts. It burns readily with an agreeable odour, and is chiefly used as incense.

Economic Use :
In folk medicines - dried, powder flowers used in colds, fevers.

Bark - used in diarrhoea, pile and skin disease, powdered back mixed with clarified butter as an poultrice on bleeding wound.

Fresh back - in tooth ache and to remove pus formation in wound

Gum is astringent, stimulant, expectorant, diuretic, diaphoretc, emmenagogue, ecbolic, antiseptic.

Parts Used:

Timber, gum, bark.


Harvesting Practices:
Oleo-gum-resin-
Healthy, mature trees with a girth of 90 cm or more are selected for tapping. The trees are tapped by sharing off a thin band of bark about 20 cm wide and 30 cm long or height, at a height of 15 cm from the base of the tree. Depth of the cut is about half the thickness of the bark. Tapping should start from Nov. and should be stopped before the monsoon.

The exudates starts oozing out soon after the blazes the made and may be collected initially after a month. Subsequent collection and freshening may be done at fortnightly or weekly intervals upto May.

The resin is scraped off and collected in a circular tray suitably placed around the trunk. It is collected in a semi-solid state and the vegetable impurities are manually removed. It is ten kept in baskets upto 30 days on a cemented and sloping floor, when the fluid portion containing the volatile oil is collected and used in paints and varnishes. The remaining semi-solid to solid part is thoroughly dried and sometimes treated with soapstone powder to make it brittle. It is then broken into small pieces, cleaned and graded for marketing.

Produce and Products:
Plywood, newspaper, wrapping and packing paper, Rosin, gum.
Timber - plywood, vencers, building boards, inferior planking, cheap furniture, cooperage, cheap boxes and packing cases, well-construction, water pipes, hookah-stems, sheath for knives swords, toys, carving and boat-masts, match splints, ammunition box (better than those from teak).

Paper pulp - newsprint, writing, printing paper, Braille papers, wrapping and parking paper; manufacture of cellulose esters and cellulose ethers for plasting industry, for furfural used for nylon.

Oleo-gum-resin - Indian olifanum, Indian frankincense or Salai gugged volatile oil for varnishes making, paint making, additive to hydrocarbon so prevent corrosive action of sulphur on Cu.

Rosin - as substitute for caada balsam snusro oil. Used in maniyar of soaps, high class paints, varnishes, lacquers and printing ink.

Gum - incense sticks, in calico-printing and textile industry for dying and finishing, in distemper. Useful in ulcer, tumours, goiter, cystic bread, diarrhoea and dysentery, piles and skin diseases, ointment for coris, syphilis (mixed with butter). May be anti-hyperlipidemic and anti-antherosclerotic agent.


Chemistry:
The oleo-gum-resin has the following composition. Moisture, 10-11; volaitle oil, 8-9, rosin, 45-50; gum, 30-34; and insol matter, 4-5%.

The volatile oil is usually pale yellow and has an agreeable odour. Oil has campholytic acid which has a strong odour. It resembles turpentine oil and can be substituted for turpentine of commence. It readily dissolves colophony, dammar and other resins.

The resins range from faint yellow to transparent-golden-brown, dark brown, or reddish brown. It is soluble in most-organic solvents, but does not dissolve completely in Na2CO3 solution.
