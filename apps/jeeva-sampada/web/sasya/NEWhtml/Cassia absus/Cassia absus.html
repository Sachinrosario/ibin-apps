DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence: Terminal few flowered racemes ; Flower: Bracteate, pedicillate, complete, viscous hairy ; Calyx: hairy, segment oblong, subequal, obtuse ; Corolla: Petals, obovate, cuneate, reddish-yellow, tender, veined ;Androecium: Stamens 5, equal, all perfect. Gynoecium: ovary densely bristy with long hairs, at the bottom of the calyx, ovules many, style short stigma many ; Fruit: Pods ligulate, nearly straight, oblique, compressed, clothed with bristle hairs.  ; Seed: 4-6, trapezoid-ovoid, black shining .


Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : September.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.
 
Genetic variability:

The plant has reported chromosome number 2n=26.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Chemical Variability: The major lipid classes and their constituent fatty acids in maturing seeds of c. absus has been analyzed. Generally, a continuous increased in the contents of triacylglycerol was observed  whereas the contents of polar lipids on the whole maturity. The contents of polar lipids on the whole decreased with maturity1.

UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:
b-sitosterol-b-glucoside from seed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

A D-galacto-D-mannan composed of galactose (1 mole) and mannose (3 moles) isolated from seed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Palmitic, gentisic, 5-O-D-glucopyranosylgentisic acids, ethyl-a-D-galactopyranoside, apigenin, luteolin, hydnocarpin and isohydnocarpin isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Chrysophanol and aloe-emodin isolated from roots; leaves contained quercetin and rutin; roots and leaves both contained chaksine and isochaksine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Raffinose (0.25%) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Ethnobotany:
