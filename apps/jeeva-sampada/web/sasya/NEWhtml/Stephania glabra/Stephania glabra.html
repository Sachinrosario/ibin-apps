Chemistry:

·        Stepharine, mp. 1790, compound II, mp. 1270, stepharotine (as hydrobromide, mp. 2270) along with (-) tetrahydropalmatine, isolated from tubers of Indian plant whereas the last compound and cycleanine obtained from plants grown in Caucasus on coast of Black Sea. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Revised structure of rotundine and its identity with tetrahydropalmatine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Alkaloid A, mp. 790, alkaloid C, mp. 790, alkaloid C, mp. 1820 and alkaloid D, mp. 1530, from bulbs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Hyndarine and cycleanine from bulbs; cycleanine and a base, mp. 2040, from grass. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Tuduranine isolated from root tuber; its synthesis from stepharine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Dehydrocorydalmine chloride and a new protoberberine alkaloid – stepharanine –from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Isolation of (+) pronuciferine, (+) stepharine, (-) corydalmine and (-) stepholidine and chemical studies. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969). 

·        Tetrahydropalmatine, its tautomer and an alkaloid, mp. 1800, isolated from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Palmatine, dehydrocorydalmine, palmatrubine, stepharanin, tetrahydropalmatine, corydalmine and stepholidine isolated from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Stereostructure of cycleanine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        A new alkaloid –N-demethylcycleanine-isolated from rhizomes along with capaurine, corynoxidine, corydalmine, tetrahydropalmatine, stepholidine, pronuciferine, stepharine and jatrorrhizine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Capaurine, corydalmine, dehydrocyrydalmine, corynoxidine, jatrorrhizine, stepharanine, stepharanine, stpholidine, palmatine and tetrahydropalmatine isolated from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Isolation of cepharamine and (-) tetrahydropalmatine from leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Total alkaloid fraction (1.0 to 10.0 mg/ml) decreased amplitude and frequency of natural contraction of intestine and uterus. It antagonized contractions of smooth muscle of intestine and uterus induced by acetylcholine, serotonin, histamine and barium chloride. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Isoflavonoid C-glycoside from Stephania glabra. (Pal et al., 1995)


Pharmacology:

·        Rotundine had neuro-sedative action similar to but weaker than that of chlorpromazine in rats and mice. Its toxicity was 33-50% that of chlorpromazine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Base, mp. 2040, had anticholinesterase and cycleanine had anti-inflammatory properties. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Stepharine inhibited cholinesterase and pseudo-cholinesterase in vitro and increased sensitivity of isolated frog abdominal muscle and rabbit intestine to acetylcholine apparently by inhibiting enzymes. Its MLD in mice was 245 mg/kg, i.v. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Cycleanine (50mg/kg) decreased inflammation, vascular permeability and serum hyaluronidase activity when given i.p. to rats. However, it had no anti-inflammatory activity when given to adrenalectomised rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Gindarine hydrochloride produced marked reduction in spontaneous motor activity with no concomitant muscle weakness in rats and mice. It caused hyperthermia in rats and prolonged pentobarbital induced hypnosis in mice. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Gindarine showed slight and variable actions on systemic arterial blood pressure, heart rate, cardiac contractility and skeletal muscles. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        It was devoid of surface anaesthetic activity but produced conduction and infiltration anaesthesia. It antagonized muscarinic actions of acetylcholine in various isolated smooth muscle (rabbit intestine, guinea pig ileum, rat ileum and trachea) preparations. It abolished tonic extensor component of hind limbs in maximal electric shock seizures. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Gindarine hydrochloride reduced fibrillation time in acetylcholine-induced fibrillation by about 7.5% at a dose of 10mg/kg, i.v., in dog. It also showed mild CNS stimulant activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Cycleanine dimethobromide blocked transminssion of impulses through isolated rabbit supracervical sympathetic ganglion; its EC50 was 58 mg/ml). Similar results obtained in rat phrenic nerve-diaphragm preparation, thus, muscle-relaxant effect of cycleanine dimethobromide was much weaker than that of d-tubocurarine dimethobromide. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Pronuciferine exhibited spasmolytic activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Tetrahydropalmatine when injected i.p. into conscious immobilized by pretreatment with pargyline. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Intravenous administration of (-) stepholidine lowered blood pressure in anaesthetized dogs and rats, hypotension was antagonized by phentolamine or yohimbine. In reserpinised animal, (-) stepholidine increased blood pressure. At large dose (5.50 mg/kg, i.v.) it inhibited pressor response to noradrenaline or clonidine and reversed adrenaline-induced pressor response. At large doses it blocked postsynaptic a-adrenoceptors. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        The alkaloid cycleanine ((12aR-(12aR,24aR))-2,3,12a,13,14,15,24,24a-octahydro-5,6,17,18-tetramethoxy-1,13-dimethyl-8,11:20,23-dietheno-1H,12H (1,10)dioxacyclooctadecino(2,3,4-ij:11,12,13-ij')diisoquinoline) was extracted from the bulbs of Stephania glabra (Roxb) Miers and its effects on cardiac and smooth muscle preparations were studied and compared to those of nifedipine (1,4-dihydro-2,6-dimethyl-4-(2-nitrophenyl)-3,5-pyridine dicarboxylic acid dimethylesther). Cycleanine inhibited the KCl-induced contraction of rabbit aortic rings with higher potency than nifedipine. IC50s for cycleanine and nifedipine were 0.8 and 7cntdot10-9 M respectively. Cycleanine had minor effects on the norepinephrine-induced contraction of rabbit aortic rings. Cycleanine and nifedipine also depressed the contraction of rat ventricular preparations but with lower potency (IC50 = 3 and 0.03-10-6 M respectively). Action potential duration of rat right ventricular strips was decreased by both compounds. L-type Ca-current (ICaL) of single rat ventricular cardiomyocytes was inhibited by cycleanine in a voltage- and frequency-dependent manner. With a higher potency nifedipine inhibited ICaL in a tonic and almost frequency-independent manner. The results suggest that cycleanine can act as a potent vascular selective Ca-antagonist. (Martinez et al., 1998)

·        Preparation and determination of some physicochemical parameters of rotundine sulfate from L-tetrahydropalmatine of Stephania glabra. (Nguyen et al., 1999) CA. 132:212553
