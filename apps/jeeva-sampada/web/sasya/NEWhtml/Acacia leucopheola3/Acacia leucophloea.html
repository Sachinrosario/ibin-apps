DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Terminal tomentose panicles; Flower: Heads numerous, pedicle stout downy, with toothed rings of bracts above the middle ; Calyx: Calyx 1/20 in. long, teeth short ; Corolla:  Corolla twice as long as calyx ; lobes sub-acute; Androecium: Stamens indefinite, exerted, free, anthers small, not gland crested; Gynoecium:  Ovary sessile, ovules many, style filiform, stigma small and terminal; Fruit: Pods sessile, thin, flat,  subindehiscent, skightly curved clothed with pale brown momentum ; Seed: . 10-12 . Flowering & Fruiting : Januarary – Febuarary. Flowers are pollinated by insects.   Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Chemistry:
  •  From stem bark of A. leucophloea (Joshi and Sharma, 1977).

Benzene extract of the stem bark on chromatography yielded the following compounds:

(a)         n-Hexacosanol: Elution with petroleum-ether(60-80)afforded colourless n- hexacosanol, m.p.79-80 °.

(b)         (b). b–Sitosterol: Further elution of the column using petroleum ether-benzene (4:1) yielded b-Sitosterol m.p.136-137 °.

(c)         b- Amyrin : Elution of the column with petroleum ether-benzene (1:1) afforded b- Amyrin   m.p. 198 °  

  • From heartwood of A. leucophloea  (Joshi and Sharma, 1977).

The petroleum ether (60-80 °) extract of the dried and coarsely powdered heartwood on chromatography yielded.

a)      n-Octacosanol : Elution of the above column with petroleum ether yielded n- octacosanol m.p. 83-84 °.

b)      b-Sitosterol : Further elution of the column with petroleum ether – benzene (1 : 1) gave   b-sitosterol   m.p. 136 °.

  • From the root bark of A. leucophloea two new pimar-8(14)-ene diterpenoids have been isolated. Their structure have been established by chemical and spectroscopic means as 1 b,15R,16-trihydroxypimar-8(14)-ene (Leucophleol) (Fig.1a). and 15R,16-epoxy-1 b,11 a-dihydroxypimar-8(14)-ene (Leucophleoxol) (Fig1b).(Bansal,et al   . 1980).
  • A diterpenoid11 a,16-epoxy-1 b,15R-dihydroxy-isopimar-8(14)-ene (leucoxol) (Fig2) having the molecular formula C20H32O3,m.p. 222.5-224 (EtOAc-n-hexane) isolated from root bark of A. leucophloea and its structure was determined. (Perales et al., 1980).
  • New tannin (Fig. 3) along with isookanin, cyanin, and leucodelphinidin – 3 –O- a-L-rhamnopyranoside isolated from highly astringent bark; of A. leucophloea and its structure was elucidated. (Trivedi and Misra, 1984).
  • A saponin-betulinic acid-3-O- b-Dmaltoside (Fig. 4) was isolated from roots and its structure   was elucidated. (Mishra and Srivastava, 1985).
  • Two anthraquinone glycosides-1,3-dihydroxy-5-methoxy –2- methyl anthraquinone-8-O- a-L-rhamnopyranoside (Fig.5a) and 1-hydroxy-8-methoxy-2-methylanthra-quinone-3-O- a-L-rhamnopyranoside (Fig. 5b) together with 1, 5-dihydroxy-8-methoxy 2-methylanthraquinone –3-O- a-L- rhamnopyranoside and galangin –3-O- a-L-rhamnopyranoside which were isolated from roots. (Saxena and Srivastava, 1986).
  • Mannitol, luleolin, b- sitosterol and its acetate isolated from pods. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
  • Four aglycones were extracted and fractioned from fresh flowers of A. leucophloea and the most polar component was characterized as myricetin . The next less polar one was identified as quercetin, by pc, uv and 1H nmr spectral studies.The third component was identified as 3-hydroxy -7- methoxy isoflavone by pc,uv, colour reactions and 13C nmr spectral studies. The least polar has been characterized as apigenin. A yellow solid (m.p.250-252) was separated and suggested by uv,1H nmr and 13C nmr studies as apiegenin- 8C-glucoside. (Valsakumari and Sulochana, 1991).
  • The pimarane-type structure previously suggested for leucophleol and leucophlexol, the two diterpenoids isolated from A. leucoploea, were amended to isopimarane-type derivatives which were supported by NMR spectroscopic studies and by X–ray diffraction method respectively. (del Carmen Apreda Rojas et al., 2001)
