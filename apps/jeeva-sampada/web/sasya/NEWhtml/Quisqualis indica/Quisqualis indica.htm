DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Large, straggling shrubs; stems often twining to the left; old stems often spinous. Leaves opposite, sub-opposite or in fast growing new shoots, alternate, elliptic or elliptic oblong, 5-18 x 2.5-9 cm, rounded or subcordate at base, acuminate at apex, glabrous or pubescent; petioles 0.5-2 cm long; petiolar spines 1-1.5 cm long, bracts lanceolate or elliptic, 6-10 x 2-3 mm, acute or acuminate. Calyx tube 4-10 cm long green pubescent outside; teeth triangular, 1-2 mm long. petals white, turning pink to deep red, pleasantly fragrant, oblong, 10-20 x 3-6 mm. fruits c 3 x 1 cm, ovoid or ovoid-ellipsoid, chocolate brown wings 5.

Miscellaneous Information

 The genus Quisqualis consists of 17 species of woody vines and climbing shrubs native to the Old World tropics. The name is from the Latin quis, who?, and qualis, what? This name was given by the early botanist Rumphius as an expression of his surprise at the variability of the plant's growth and flower color. Q. indica, a native of Burma, Malaya, New Guinea, and the Philippines is a stout woody plant with a vigorous habit growing to a height of up to 30 feet, shrubby below and vine-like above. The younger parts of the plant are covered with a rusty pubescence. The leaves are 3 to 6 inches long. The fragrant flowers are borne in pendulous clusters, terminally. The calyx tube is green, slender and up to 3 inches long. The spreading petals are ½ inch long. Flowers are white at first, then change to pink and finally to red.

Interesting Facts about this plant: The seeds of this plant taste like coconut and are used for flavoring. If ingested in excessive amounts, they are poisonous.
