Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Large unarmed, deciduous, buttressed tree found in subtropical region of East Indian hills and parts of Western Ghats, suitable for marginal lands and for village commons. Occurs in the lower hill-forests of the eastern Himalayas, extending to Nagaland and Manipur, and in the evergreen hill-forests of South India. The tree is now cultivated on large scale in Coorg and South Kanara districts of Karnataka. It can be grown in the areas unsuitable for teak. Occurs in moist tropical rainforest of India.

The tree is sensitive to frost.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Lofty, unarmed, tree up to 60 m in height and 8.1 m in girth, with a clear bole of 18-45 m. bark fairly smooth, greyish brown. Leaves bipinnate with 30 cm or more long rachis, leaflets 5-8 pairs, oblong, elliptic-lanceolate or oblique. Flowers scarlet, red or orange in simple, axillary, dense racemes. Pods long-stalked, flat, thin 5-10 seeded. Seeds brown, obovate, oblique, compressed.

Floral and seed biology:


Seed Germination & Propagation: The tree reproduces well. Under natural conditions the seeds germinate with the onset of monsoon and the young seedlings can be collected for the nursery. The pods are collected when they turn black, from February till the onset of rains, the best seeds can be obtained only during April-May.

The general seed dormancy is  10 months. The seeds sown immediately after collection, show 30-4 per cent germination in  10 days; others may remain dormant for as long as a year. The germination is also erratic, probably because of the hard seed coat; treatment with concentrated sulphuric acid for 10 min or with warm water for 14 hrs ensures germination.

In artificial regeneration, entire transplanting is more successful as compared to direct sowing and stump planting. For direct sowings, the seeds are placed 1.8 m apart or may be broadcast in burnt fuel-coupes. In recent practices, the seeds are mixed with those of Chukrasia tabularis, A. Juss. or Toona ciliata M.Roem. The nursery beds are watered twice daily during germination period, and later every alternate day. The beds are weeded after germination. The seedlings are pricked out as soon as they are sufficiently large for handling and transplanted in June of the same year.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Wood used as fuel.

Wood: Sapwood is white; heartwood is light red with slightly darker lines. The wood is lustrous, without characteristic odour, fairly durable, moderately hard, strong, heavy (sp. gr.0.68 wt, 465-800 kg.cu m), ornamental, somewhat interlocked- or wavygrained or with fiddle-back mottling and extremely coarse-textured. Average unit weight at 12% moisture content 690 kg/m3.

The wood is primarily used for shingles and planking and for making tea-boxes in West Bengal. It is a general-utility timber, suitable for beams, rafters, trusses, doors, windows, etc. For doors and windows, it may be substituted for teak and sal after proper seasoning and preservative treatment. It is a general construction wood and is substituted for Poonspar wood (Calophyllum elatum Bedd.).

Being ornamental, it is a good wood for furniture and cabinet-making. It is also suitable for packing cases for heavy machinery and other similar stores; tool-helves and handles for shovels, rakes and spades. It makes good veneers and plywood. The timber may be used for second-class kraft pulp.

Wood is hard, brown. Important timber species. Employed for making boards, shingles and for building purposes.
