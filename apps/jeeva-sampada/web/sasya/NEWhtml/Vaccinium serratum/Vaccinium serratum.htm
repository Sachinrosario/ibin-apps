HABIT & HABITAT :
Epiphytic shrub, Found in Sikkim, Bhutan and Khasia hills from 4,000 to 8,000 ft. Woodland, Sunny Edge, Dappled Shade. Hill forests of the eastern Himalayas, often epiphytic on trees, at altitudes between 1200 and 2400 meters.

Availability :  
E. Asia - Himalayas. Sikkim, Bhutan and Kharia alt. 3-7000ft., frequent

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A deciduous shrub. Branchlets glabrous, leaves lanceolate, serrate often whorled. peduncles mostly terminal umbel led not clothed with broad braets at their bares, corolla glabrous without(2). A stout epiphyte; branches often greatly thickened at their bare leaves sub sessile, 2 1/2x 3/4 in., acute, remotely crenate serrate nearly to the bare, glabrous. Racemes 2 in., often 3-4 springing umbellately from the terminal whorl of leaves, glabrous or nearly 80 bracts 1/16 in., laneolate, serrate, those at the base of the peduncles hardly larger, calyx tube smooth; teeth minute, triangular; (anthers not spurred, dehiscing by long slits from below the apes Berr) Corolla 1/4 by 1/8 in., tubular very shortly lobed, yellow green, filaments glabrous; anthers not spurred, dehiscing by long slits from below the apex. berry 1/5 in., dia., globose, glistening, white or yellowish.

Cultivation:  
Requires a moist but freely-draining lime free soil, preferring one that is rich in peat or a light loamy soil with added leaf-mould. Prefers a very acid soil with a pH in the range of 4.5 to 6, plants soon become chloratic when lime is present. Succeeds in full sun or light shade though it fruits better in a sunny position. Requires shelter from strong winds. Dislikes root disturbance, plants are best grown in pots until being planted out in their permanent positions.The plant prefers light (sandy) and medium (loamy) soils and requires well-drained soil. The plant prefers acid soils and can grow in very acid soil. It can grow in semi-shade (light woodland) or no shade. It requires moist soil.
