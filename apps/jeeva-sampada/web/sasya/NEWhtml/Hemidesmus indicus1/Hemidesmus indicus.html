Chemistry:
The petrol extract of H.indicus root on repeated chromatographic seperation yielded, hexatriacontane, lupeol octacosanoate, b-amyrin acetate, lupeol acetate, a-amyrin, lupeol, b-amyrin and sitsterol. (Padhy et al, 1972).

A new pregnane ester diglycoside named desinine has been isolated from the dried twigs of Hemidesmus indicus. The structure has been established as drevogenin b-3-O-b andropyranosyl-1(1à4)-b-D-oleandropyranoside. (Oberai et al, 1985).

Hemidesminine (fig.1), a new coumarino-lignoid isoloated from the dried roots of Hemidesmus indicus. (Mandal et al, 1991).

Two new pregnane glycoside, designated indicine I and  hemidine (II) have been isolated from the stems of H. indicus with following structures, calogenin-3-O-b-D-digitoxopyranoside and calogenin-3-O-b-D-biovinopyranoside. (Kamal et al, 1991).

The hexane soluble portion of the ethanol extract of the stem of Hemidesmus indicus afforded a new triterpene lactone, characterized as 3-keto-lup-12-ene-21à28-olide (fig.2), Lupanone, D12-dehydrolupanyl acetate, D12-dehydrolupeol acetate, hexadelanoic acid, 4-hydroxy-3-methoxy benzaldehyde (fig.3), 3-hydroxy 4-methoxy benzaldehyde were also isolated. (Gupta et al, 1992).

Two new coumarino-lingnoids, hemidesmin-1 (fig.4) and hemidesmin-2 (fig.4) have been isolated from the roots of Hemidesmus indicus. (Das et al, 1992).

From the dried stems of Hemidesmus indicus two novel pregnane glycosides, hemidescine and emidine have been isolated- with the structures 20-O-acetyl calogenin 3-O-b-D-digitoxopyranosyl 1 (1à4)-O-b-D-oleandropyranoside and calogenin-3-O-b-D-digitoxopyranosyl 1 (1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxopyranosyl (1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxopyranoside, respectively. (Ruchi chandra et al, 1993).

Structural studies of oligosaccharide segment of hemisine, (O-b-D cymaro pyranosyl (1à4)-O-3-D-methyl-b-Dgluco pyranosyl (1à4)-O-b-D glucopyranosyl (1à4)–o-b-D-cymaropyranose) and demiscine, (o-b-D-xylopyranosyl (1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxopyranosyl (1à4)-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl (1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxopyranose) were isolated from the dried stems of H.indicus. (Desh et al, 1995).

Three new pregnane diglycosides, medidesmine, hemisine and desmisine were isolated from the plant Hemidesmus indicus and identified as Sarcostin-3-O-a-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-oleandropyranoside, calogenin-3-O-b-D-cymaropyranoside(1à4)-o[3-O-methyl]b-D-glucopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl (1à4)-O-b-D-cymaropyranoside and calogenin-3-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxypyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl(1à4)-O-b-D-digitoxypyranoside, respectively. (Desh et al, 1996).


Pharmacology:
The invitro anti-microbial activity different extracts of H. indicus (Asclepiadiaceae) against staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Aspergillus niger have been carried out. The ethanol (95%) extract of chloroform extract of the same plant showed anti fungal activity against Aspergillus niger. (Hiremath et al, 1997)


Ethnobotany:
Medicne for antidote to snake-bite (root, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Blood purification (root, Nagendra Prasad and Abraham, 1984; Malhotra and Moorthi, 1973); Cold (fruit, Saha et al, 1981); Cooling effect (root, Singh and Maheshwari, 1983; Jain et al, 1973; Ramachandaran and Nair, 1981); Cough (fruit, Shah et al, 1981; stem bark, Tarafder, 1986); Diarrhoea (root, Tribedi et al, 1982); Digestive disorder (root, Shah et al, 1983); Dysentry (root, Maheshwari et al, 1986); Fever (fruit, Shah et al, 1981; Sharma et al, 1979); Gonorrhoea (root, Singh and Maheshwari, 1983); Hypertension (root, Shah etal, 1981); Impotence (root, Singh and pandey, 1980; Goel et al, 1984); Menstrual compliant (root, Shah et al, 1983); Nervous disorder (root, Jain et al, 1973); Skin disorder (root, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985, Goel et al, 1984); Stomachache (root, Tribedi et al, 1982; Jain, 1965); Toothache (part not mentioned, Srivastava and Verma, 1981); Urinary complaint (root, Gupta, 1981); Cooling drink (root,  Roy and Chaturvedi, 1987).
