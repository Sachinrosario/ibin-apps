Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

An erect, small to moderate-sized tree. Grows in mangrove swamps. Tree one of the chief constituents of mangrove formation and grows gregariously in more or less pure patches. Gregarious along the intertidal estuarine regions of mangrove forests, often as a pioneer species on newly formed mudflats. Species occurs in the mangrove forests on both the coasts of India from Bombay to Sunderbans, in the tidal forests of the coasts of West Bengal and the greater part of peninsular India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An erect, small to moderate-sized tree up to 30 m tall, crown large, buttresses not prominent. Bark black, smooth, with horizontal, oval lenticels. Pneumatophores 60-150 cm long, arising from horizontal roots, corky, forked twice or thrice, associated with anchor roots and nutrition roots. Branches drooping, slender. Leaves oblong-lanceolate, obtuse, thick, glabrous, much attenuated at the base into a short petiole. Main nerves 10-12 pairs, not conspicuous.

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers solitary or, more commonly, 3 together, large, articulated on pedicels. Calyx cup-shaped, lobes 4, ovate-oblong, acute. Petals 0. Stamens slightly exserted, style shorter than the stamens. Stigma umbrella. Capsule depressed-globose, tipped with the style, smooth. Flowers are ephimeral, ill-smelling, nocturnal.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

A good fuelwood. Calorific value: sapwood - 4,901 cal., 8,084 Btu.

Wood: Sapwood is grey; heartwood light pinkish brown, fading on exposure, dull to somewhat lustrous, shallowly interlocked-grained in broad bands, fine- and even-textured, moderately hard and strong and somewhat light to moderately heavy. Weight: 625 kg/cu.m., moderately hard, specific gravity0.60, 2.2% ash.

Wood mostly used for packing cases. It has been found suitable for opium chests. Also used for house construction as planks, scantlings, door boards, etc. and for rough furniture and boat making. Wood suitable for textile-mill bobbins and jutemill rollers and rove bobbins with 3-ply ends.

Other uses:

Tannin - Bark and fruit tannins suitable for direct tanning. Leather produced is pale buff in colour, soft, supple and strong and quite free from cracks.

Dye - High proportion of yellow colouring matter (11.3%) in the bark of considerable advantage.

Unripe sour fruits said to be eaten in Burma in curries. Pneumatophores - Arenchyma is used as substitute for cork.
