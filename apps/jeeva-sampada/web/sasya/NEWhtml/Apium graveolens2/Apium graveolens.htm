Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Celery is herbaceous annual or biennial erect herb. It is an annual plant in plains, but in colder climate and on the hills celery behaves as a biennial. Celery seed of trade is the dried fruit of Apium graveolens L. Grown mainly as a seed crop in India and not as a vegetable while elsewhere in the world it is grown mainly for the leaves, which are used for flavouring. In and around the cities of North India, Celery is grown for leaves to cater the needs of the big hotels. Like many umbellifers grown in damp places, wild celery has a wide area. The variety of celery plant which produces celery seed, is not the celery eaten as vegetable in USA. The seeds are from a wild celery species native of Europe (1).

Climate and soil
Celery requires a cool climate and plenty of moisture for growth. Best suited as a winter crop in North Indian plains. Often grown in rotation with potato and rice (1). Celery thrives best on sandy loam soil with a good content of organic matter, good drainage, plenty of moisture and a pH range 7.0-7.5 (5).

Availability :

Celery is a native of Europe, distributed from Sweden to Egypt, Algeria, Southern France, China, Abyssinia, Asia and India. Now found naturalized in the foothills of north- western Himalayas, Saharanpur in Uttar Pradesh, Amritsar, Jammunder and Kapurthala in Punjab, Ambala in Haryana and Himachal Pradesh. The most important species is A. graveolens. Genus contains about 20 species.
Conservation/ Threat Status : Cultivated and wild

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Herb reaching a height of about 60-90 cm with conspicuous branches bearing well developed leaves on long expanded petioles. Stems are branched, angular and conspicuously jointed. Leaves are oblong to obovate, 7-18 cm long, radial, pinnate, deeply divided into three segments, once or twice divided into toothed apex. Leaflets are oblong to obovate or ovate, 3-lobed, 2-4.5 cm long. Roots succulent well-developed, numerous (6).

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers white, very small and produced in compound umbel. Calyx teeth are obsolete, petioles 5, ovate with tip inflexed.
Fruit
Fruit a rigid schizocarp, each mericarp containing a single seed, aromatic and bitter in taste. Schizocarp is very small, only a very occasional fruit exceeding 1.6 mm, 1 mm diameter, nearly circular, suborbicular to ellipsoid, greyish brown consists of 2 mericarps attached to a carpophore divided near the top.

CUTIVATION ASPECT:
Local cultivars:
Two varieties occur: var. dulce (Mill.) Pers. and var. rapaceum (Mill.) Gaudich. A. graveolens var. dulce is the celery used as a spice - the leaves are used in salad and for flavouring soups and dressing seed (celery seeds) is used popularly as a spice. A. graveolens var. rapaceum (Mill.) Gaudich is having dark green leaves and are grown for the swollen root tubers (4-8 cm) which are eaten, after cooking. Variety cultivated in India is 'dulce'. The wild celery plant is not stout nor are its leaf-stalks thickened as in the cultivated types. It has a low disc-like reduced stem from which the floral axis arises. Root system of the wild variety as of domesticated ones is shallow and narrow but fibrous and dense. Radical leaves which form a rosette constitute the celery, the inner ones constitute the heart. Petioles are fleshy and ridged, up to 40 cm long. Elongated floral axis is conspicuously jointed, fluted and fistular and the herb in bloom attains a height up to 2.4 m. Laterally compressed mericarps are extensively used as a condiment and as a source of essential oil used for flavouring (Bailey, 1947, I, 702; Greenhalgh, loc. cit.; Work, 494) (6).


Related species:
A. leptophyllum (Pers.) F. Muell. ex Benth. is an erect or decumbent herb with a stout taproot, striated stem, oblong to obovate leaves having ovate to sub-orbicular leaflets and broadly orbicular mericarps with a cordate base. Native to America and occurs commonly in waste places, roadsides and gardens in Dehra Dun and adjacent hills of Chakrata, Nainital and Mussoorie in Uttar Pradesh. Contains bergapten and seselin. Steam distillation yields a pale yellow essential oil (0.25%), containing thymol methyl ether, carvacrol methyl ether, thymoquinol dimethyl ether and hydrocarbons. Also contains 10 minor constituents including thymol, carvacrol and sesquiterpene hydrocarbons (Dutt, J. Res. Indian Med., 1974, 9(1), 96) (6).

Released varieties:
RRL-85-1 is the released variety. There are also two main groups of varieties namely green varieties and self-blanching types. The latter is somewhat inferior in quality but is easier to blanch. The vegetable breeding station in Katrain in Kuluvalley (Himachal Pradesh) has evolved some high yielding, high quality varieties such as Wright's Grove Giant - a medium late, tall variety producing white stalks of high quality. The second one is an early, medium tall variety with medium pink stem and with good flavour. The Fordhook Emperor is a dwarf late variety producing thick broad stalks of good quality.

Selection of planting material:
Seedlings developed from seeds are used as the planting material.

Planting:
For growing celery the soil should be ploughed to a fine tilth incorporating plenty of compost and farmyard manure. Irrigation channels are made before planting and the seedlings are transplanted in well-watered soil. Sowing time in the hills is March-April in nursery seedling and the transplanting time May-June in hilly areas. Crop will be ready for cutting (for use as leaf) in November. In plains when grown as a seed crop, the seeds are sown in September-October, the seedlings are transplanted in December and the crop is ready by May. A seed rate of 1.5 kg seed for raising seedlings for one ha land in 1000 m2 area.
Seeds are usually sown in raised beds or plots in finally powdered soil, take 10-20 days for germination. The soil should be kept always moist using a fine spray of water but never too much wet. The seedlings are ready for transplanting in the second or third week of December. The spacing normally adopted for transplanting is 30 x 30 cm or 45 x 30 cm between/ within rows. For the vegetable crop seedlings are planted in rows 60-120 cm apart and within row 15-20 cm.
Celery requires plenty of nutrients. Before planting 10-20 tons of farmyard manure 500 kg Calcium ammonium nitrate and 25 kg of super phosphate, 30-40 kg of Potash per hectare are added (4).

Management after planting:
A second dose of 500 kg of calcium ammonium nitrate and 25 kg of super phosphate 30-40 kg of Potash per hectare are added just before flowering. A yield of 8 tons of leaf crop removes @ 16 kg of nitrogen, 42 kg of phosphorus and 178 kg of Potash from one ha; celery is highly responsive to nitrogen fertilization. A fertilizer dose of 200 kg of nitrogen and 33 kg of phosphorous/ha has been reported economical. Celery crop requires frequent irrigation; since it is a moisture-loving crop, usually once in a week or in ten days irrigation is given.

Blanching: operation is carried out for whitening the stalk. Blanching effect is obtained by covering the plant to a near dark condition. In self-blanching types, blanching is easy and can be achieved by covering the stalks with paper, waste cloth or by any other means. In green varieties blanching is done by earthing up stalks. This operation is usually done in cool weather. Earthing up is initially done to a few centimeters height but gradually the height is increased until only top of plants protrude out of the ridges (2).

MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:
Pests:
Leaf minor - Liriomyza trifolii.
Symptoms: Damage caused by larvae which makes roll the leaves.
Control: Spray Dimethoate @ 1.5 kg/ha.

Celery fly - Euleia brercolai.
Control: Spray Dimethoate @ 1.5 kg/ha.

Carrot rust fly - Psila rosae.
Symptoms: Maggots eat small roots and burrow into roots.
Control: Apply Phorate granules @ 1 g/m2 or spray 0.025 % Quinalphos.
Insecticides are not usually recommended for crops grown for leaf use.

Diseases:

No major diseases affecting the crop. In early stages the plant is susceptible to blight caused Cercospora apii Fres. and late blight caused by Septoria apii-graveolensis Dorgin. Hot water treatment of seeds is effective in reducing the incidence of the blights. The other diseases reported are Fusarium yellows, pink rot, and rust caused by Puccinia apii Desm. Virus diseases such as mosaic and aster yellow are also reported (6).

Early blight - Caused by fungus Cercospora apii - soil borne
Symptoms: disease first appears on older leaves causing very small circular brownish yellow spots with concentric rings. The spots rapidly enlarge later but the central area of the spot remains light brown.
Control: Treat seeds with hot water (48°C) for 30 minutes, spray copper oxychloride (0.2%).

Late blight - Caused by fungus Septoria apii.
Symptoms: Small circular water soaked areas later turning black on the tip and margin of leaves and later spread to other parts of plants.
Control: Spray Dithane Z-78 (0.2 %) at 10-15 days interval.

Fusarium yellows - Caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. apii.
Symptoms: Yellowing of leaves and stunting of plants, followed by wilting and death.
Control: Crop rotation, cultivate resistant varieties Pascal and All Dark green.

Stem rot: Caused by Rhizoctonia solanii.
Symptoms: Black spots on the collar region of plant, results in death of the plant.
Control: Seed treatments and drenching with Bavistin (0.1 %) solution.

Celery mosaic - Caused by virus.
Symptoms: Infected leaves show leaf mottling and greyish colour of inner leaves.
Control: Remove and destroy infected plants. Spray with Endosulfan (0.1 %) to check aphids.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Seed has a pleasingly crisp texture and subtle flavour, has distinct odour from that of the green stalk and leaves, is widely used in foods, beverages, drugs and perfumery (2). Celery seeds are employed as a substitute for fresh celery and an ingredient in pickling. Mainly used in foreign countries in food preparations. Ground celery is used in large variety of dishes, in flavouring and seasoning mixture, for flavouring soups salads, fruit juice, sauces, meat dishes etc. Celery powder is used in pickles. Celery salt is used in sprinkling over fish, egg, soup and croquets. Celery pepper is used in sprinkling over a variety of vegetarian to non-vegetarian dishes (6). Blanched celery leaves are eaten raw as salad; they are also cooked as a vegetable. Celery seed oil and oleoresin finds major use in food flavouring for imparting flavour to culinary dishes, celery salts and to various kinds of canned food items, liquors and pharmaceutical industry.

Fruit yields 17% of fatty oil. India meets the world's demand of celery seed oil and oleoresin for which a substantial quantity of the produced seed is utilized. Leaves and stalk of celery plant are used in salad and preparation of soups. Blanched celery leaves are eaten raw as salad and also cooked as a vegetable. Stalks and leaves are dehydrated and are marketed dehydrated celery and is used to flavour soups, sauces, meat dishes and canned meat. European variety of celery (A. graveolens var. rapaceum) produces thick, fleshy turnip like roots, eaten after cooking. Roots contain as essential oil, the main components of which are butylphthalide and neoenidilede.

Parts Used:

Dried ripe fruit (seed) and leaf.


Harvesting Practices:
In the hills celery grows as a biennial in nature and produces seeds in the second year. However, in plains the seeding occurs in the first year itself. Crop for seeds matures in about 5 months after transplanting and harvest is in May. Seed crop is ready for harvest when the flower turn reddish or when about 80% of the umbels turn to light brown. There is shattering problem in celery. Plants are cut at the base with sickles. Celery crop grown for leaves comes to harvest in November-December. Plants are cut just below the surface of soil with a sharp knife (6).

Post Harvest Handling
Cut plants are left in fields for 2-3 days for drying and then threshed with sticks to release seeds. Seeds are cleaned by winnowing. Average yield is 1000-1800 kg/ha. Plants cut for leaves, after harvest, are trimmed and prepared for market by removing the suckers damaged leaves, other debris and then graded. Leaves are stored after harvest in trenches and cellars, and in cold storage at 0°C and 98% RH (6).

Produce and Products:

Whole celery, Ground celery, Oil of celery, Celery salt - a mixture of ground celery seeds and salt, Celery pepper - a mixture of celery and black pepper powder and Celery Oleoresin are some of the produce and products.

Medicinal uses
Celery seeds possess stimulant, carminative properties and used a sedative and tonic. Decoction of seed is a village remedy for rheumatism, some liver and spleen troubles. Seeds produce a tranquilizing effect, hence, prescribed for epilepsy (6). Fatty oil and Oil of celery used in medicinal preparations as an antispasmodic, nerve stimulant, has tranquilizing, antifungal and anti-convulsing properties and also used in compounding ayurvedic formulations. Roots have diuretic property and used as stimulant, nervine sedative, tonic, in asthma and liver diseases (2).


Chemistry:
Celery fruit yield 2-3% of a pale yellow volatile oil with a persistent odour characteristic of the plant, thought to be due to oxygenated compounds present in the oil. Volatile oil - 1.5 to 3.0%, d-limonene - 60.0 to 70.0%, b-selinene - 10.0 to 20.0%, sedanonic anhydride and sedanolide (3). Fruits (seeds) contain apiin, apigenin, caffeic acid and chlorogenic acids. There are four furocoumarins reported, rutaretin (C14H14O5, mp 198°), apiumetin (C14H12O4, mp 198°), bergapten and isopimpinellin. Other substances isolated are seselin, isoimperatorin, osthenol, apigravn, graveobioside A (luteolin-7-apiosylglucoside, mp 256-57°), graveobioside B (chrysoeriol-7-apiosylglucoside, mp 234-35°), myristic acid, 8-hydroxy-5-methoxypsoralen (mp 218°), umbelliferone, etc. Celery seeds contain fatty oil (ca. 17%). Fatty acid (17%) composition of the oil: palmitic acid (11.7%), oleic acid (30.5%), linoleic acid (9.7%), petroselinic acid (41.0%) and resin acids (7.0%). Celery leaves contain: moisture - 88.0%, protein - 6.3%, fat - 0.6%, fibre - 1.4%, carbohydrates - 1.6%, minerals - 2.1 g/100 g, calcium - 230.0, Phosphorous - 140.0, iron - 6.3, riboflavin - 0.11%, niacin - 1.2 and vitamin C - 62.0 mg/100 g, carotene - 3900 µg/100 g, cal. Val. - 37 K Cal/100 g. Fresh leaves yield a green yellow essential oil (c. 0.1%) possessing the aroma of fresh plant. leaves also contain malonic acid. Stalk also yields essential oil in minute quantities. Young roots contain citric, isocitric, succinic, fumaric, malic and tartaric acids, and also tyrosine (6).

Celery oil is dominated by terpenes, mostly limonene (70 to 80%) and the sesquiterpenes beta-selinene (10%) and humulene; but its characteristic fragrance is caused by phthalides (3-butylphthalid and its 5,6-dihydro derivate sedanenolid), although the latter occur only in traces. The furo-coumarine bergaptene is a potent photosensitizer and may cause photo-dermatitis (skin irritation by light) in field workers (7).

Economic potential to the country

Saharanpur in Uttar Pradesh, Amritsar, Jammunder and Kapurthala in Punjab, Panipat, Ambala in Haryana and Himachal Pradesh. Indian celery dominates the world market (5).
Area of production in India: 5000-5300 ha.
Production: 5000-5500 tons.
Yield: 1298 t/ha.

Export potential:
India provides the bulk of celery seed for world trade and is used for preparation of oil of celery and oleoresinoids (6). Several commercial grades of celery, besides its essential oils, are exported to USA, Singapore, France, Japan and the UK (5). USA, Canada, UK, Kuwait, Netherlands and Germany are the major importing countries.
Export: 8,891 t (source: Spices Statistics, Spices Board, 1996-97).

Miscellaneous Information

 · The species name means "strongly smelling" (Latin gravis "grave, heavy" and olens "smelling" from the verb olere).
· The Romans valued celery more for cooking than for religion; on the other hand, much superstition was connected with it: The plant was thought to bring bad fortune under certain circumstances (7).
