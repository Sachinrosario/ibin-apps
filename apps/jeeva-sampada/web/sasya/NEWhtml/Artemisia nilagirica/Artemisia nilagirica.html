HABIT & HABITAT:
Robust herb, hoary pubescent or tomentose, 1.0-1.5 m tall. It occurs almost through out India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem paniculately branched, leafy. Leaves large, ovate-elliptic or incised laciniate, 8.0-17.5 cm long, prominently nerved beneath, nerves and branches densely tomentose. Heads hermaphrodite, crowded or lax in large, lax or dense, sparsely leafy panicles, globose-campanulate. Disc florets small. Corolla densely minutely glandular. Ray florets c 1.2 mm long, 2-3 lobed; disc florets c 2 mm long, 5-lobed. Achenes oblong-ellipsoid, c 0.8 mm long, brown; pappus absent.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Heads ovoid or sub-globose, solitary, spicate, , suberect or horizontal panicled racemes; Flower:  outer Flowers hermaphrodite, slender, 1 seriate; inner flower pistillate, fertile; involucre bracts villous and with scarious margins ; the outer ovate, acute ; the inner oblong, obtuse, much larger than the the outer, sometimes membranous ; Pappus 0; Corolla: Corolla of outer flower slender, tubular, shortly, 2-3 fid at the apex; corolla of pistillate flower regular, tubular, the limb shortly enlarged, campanulate, 5-fid; Androecium:  Stamens 5, inserted on the corolla and alternating with the segments, filaments usually free above, anther bases obtuse, entire; Gynoecium: Ovary 1-celled, ovules solitary erect, style arms of pistillate flower truncate, usually penicillate ; tips often connate, in the sterile flower; Fruit: Oblong-ellipsoid, minute Seed: Erect, radicle short. Flowering & Fruiting:  September-January.Flowers are pollinated by insect. Seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  Leaves and flowering tops are bitter, astringent, acrid, thermogenic, aromatic, anodyne, anti-inflammatory, depurative, diuretic, emmenagogue, aphrodisiac,appetizer, digestive, stomachic, anthelmintic, febrifuge, deobstruent, alexeteric and haematinic. They are useful in vitiated conditions of Vata and Kapha, cough, asthma, bronchitis, cephalagia, nervous and spasmodic affections, inflammations, leprosy, skin diseases, pruritus, strangury, amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea, anorexia, dyspepsia, flatulence, colic, intestinal worms, fever, hysteria, measles and anaemia.


Chemistry:
Trideca-1,3,5-trien-7,9,11-triyne, artemisia ketone and 1-acetoxytetradeca-4,6-dien-8,10,12-triyne isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

A pentacyclic triterpene- fernenol (fig. 1), m.p. 194°C- from rhizomes. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

(-)thujone a-amyrin and its acetate, fernenol, stigmasterol and b-sitosterol isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)c.

Acetylenic compounds- trideca-1,3,5-trien-7,9,11-triyne, tetradeca-4,6-dien-8,10,12-triyne-1-ol, heptadeca-1,7,9-trien-11,13,15-triyne, cis-dehydromatricaria ester and tetradeca-6-en-8,10,12-triyne-3-one- isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Sesquiterpenoid lactones - psilostachyin A (fig. 2) and psilostachyin C (fig. 3) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Twenty one irregular monoterpenes (non-head-to-tail isoprenoid skeleton) containing either artemisyl or santolinyl skeleton isolated from essential oil and identified; eight new compounds characterized as 5,6-epoxy-3,3,6-trimethyl-1-heptane-4-one (fig. 4a), 3-methyl-1-(1,1-dimethyl-2-propenyl)-2-butenyl propionate (fig. 4b), (2E) 2,5-dimethyl-4-vinyl-2,5-hexadienyl propionate (fig. 5a), (2Z) 2,5-dimethy-4-vinyl-2,5-hexadienyl acetate (fig. 5b), 5-methyl-2-methylidene-3-vinyl-4-hexenyl acetate (fig. 6a), 1,1,4-trimethyl-2-vinyl-3-pentenyl acetate (fig. 6b) 1,1,4-trimethyl-3-oxo-2-vinyl-4-pentenyl acetate (fig. 7a) and two diastereo isomers of 3-hydroxy-1,1,4-trimethyl-2-vinyl-4-pentenyl acetate (fig. 7b). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Arabinose, galactose, glucose,maltose,raffinose, ribose, sucrose and crude protein obtained from stem; crude protein (15.0%) on hydrolysis yield alanine, cystine, glycine, leucine, lysine and aspartic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Detection of camphor, b-eudesmol, 1,8-cineole, borneol, artemisia alcohol, camphene, a-gurjunene, p-cymene, terpinen-4-ol and a-pinene in essential oil by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Threonine (14.82), leucine and isoleucine (12.21), glycine (12.82), glutamic acid (9.8) and arginine (8.28%) present in protein (7.2%) isolated from leaves.

a-thujone (56.3%), g-cadinene, camphor, caryophyllene, p-cymene, geraniol, linalool, b-pinene, a-terpineol and b-thujone, identified in oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

Oil from plants grown in north-eastern region of India contained camphor (40.30-56.35%) along with car-3-ene, 1,8-cineole, citral, (+)limonene, linalool, a- and b-pinenes and thujone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Isolation of (-) quebrachitol [(-) 2-O-methylchiroinositol] and structure determined by NMR. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Lipid constituents of the hexane extract of the leaves of Artemisia nilagirica have been investigated using GC-MS analysis. Among the 19 components, 16 having chain length ranging between C9 and C25 have been identified. The principal components are n-pentacos-3-ene (28%) and n-tetracosane (27%). (Khan et al, 1997).


Pharmacology:
Essential oil exhibited 90% mosquito repellency in bioassays with Aedes aegypti. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

These essential oils are camphor and terpinen-4-ol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)f.

Artemisia nilagarica synonym iA. Vulgaris described as the correct and authentic source of natural drug ‘Biranjasaf’ being used in Unani system of medicine as anti-inflammatory and deobstruent. (Rashid and Ahmad, 1996).
