HABIT & HABITAT
Perennial, erect, corymbosely branched, hirtellous herb, 50-150 cm tall, sparsely leafy above, taproot fusiform. It is fiund to occur in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Sikkim, Assam, Meghalaya, Orissa, Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Basal leaves simple or ternate, or leaflets ovate to orbicular-cordate, 3-10 cm in diam., acute to accuminate at apex, crenate-dentate to coarsly serrate or incised at margin, hirsutulous on both surfaces, or only along veins, beneath; petioles slender, 3-18 cm long, hirsutulous, conspicuously sheathing; lower cauline leaves ternate to pinnate, leaflets ovate to lanceolate, cordate to cuneate and often obloque at base, uppermost greatly reduced. Peduncles slender, hirsutulous, 1-15 cm long. Involucre lacking, or occasionally of 1-3 scarious linear bracts. Rays 5-20, slender, spreading-ascending, unequal, hirsutulous to glabrate, 1-3 cm long. Umbellets 8-16 flowered, mature pedicels unequal, 1-5 mm long, hirsutulous. Involucel of 3-6 linear bractioles 1.5 mm long, or rarely lacking. Flowers white or greenish or yellowish; petals ovate to obovate, hirsutulous to glabraous, sometimes unequal; calyx obsolete, stylopodium low conical, style slender, often reflexed; ovary hirtellous. Fruit ovoid to globose, 1.5-2.5 mm in diam., narrowed at apex, rounded at base, compressed laterally, constricted at commissure and subdidymous, mericarps subterate in transection, scaberulous-papillose to glabrate, ribs slender. Vittae 2-4 in intervals, 2-4 on commissure, or obscure. Carpophore entire or bifid.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The herb is used as carminative.


Chemistry:

·         Ammirin (isoangenomalin) (fig. 1) and oxypeucedanin isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·         Identification of (+)(Z)2-methyl-2-butanoate and (+)isobutyrate esters of 4-methoxy-2-[(E)3-methyloxiranyl]phenol as major components in root essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·         A new phenylpropenyl ester (I) along with known phenylpropenyl ester (I, fig. 2) isolated from seeds of Pakistani plant and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·         Angelate-isobutyrate and diangelate ester of 2-[(E)3-methoxyoxiranyl]hydroquinone identified in root essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

·         Determination of 4-substituted-2-[(E)3-methyloxiranyl]phenyl esters (I-VI, fig. 3 & 4) (78.0) and C10, C12- and C15-hydrocarbons (20.0%) in root essential oil of flowering plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1989-1994).


Pharmacology:

·         Phenylpropenyl ester (I) at 1.0 mM completely inhibited germination of carrot andryegrass seeds and at 0.1 mM caused 50.0% inhibition of germination of lettuce seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1989)d.
