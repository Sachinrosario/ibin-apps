DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

An annual herb, glabrous, reaching a height of 4-5 feet. Branches purplish and spotted with white patches. Leaves alternate, long petiolate, obliquely and broadly ovate-acute, toothed or entire, 2.5-6 inch long by 2.5 inch wide; petiole 2-3 inch long. flowers axillary, solitary, shortly pedunculate; peduncle 0.5 inch long, more or less. Calyx cylindrical, 2-3.5 inch long, 5-lobed; lobes about 0.5 inch long, lanceolate-acute. Corolla large, 6-10 inch long, trumpet or funnel-shaped, white, violate or tinged with purple, with 5-6 folds and 5-6 lobes which are more or less cuspidate. Stamens 5, filaments attached to the corolla tube for half their length; anthers 0.3 inch long. Ovary glabrous; style as long as the corolla; stigma capitate, bifid. Fruit a globose capsule, spiny, 1 inch in daim., glabrous, indehiscent, opening irregularly at the top. Seeds flattened, furrowed, tubercled.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
ORNAMENTAL HERB
An erect herb 4-5 ft. high. The double and triple forms of this species, which are common in cultivation, are extremely handsome when in bloom during the rains. Easily grown from seed.
The seeds of this plant are highly poisonous and have been in sue for centuries for criminal purposes. The victim is drugged by means of food or drink to which the ground seeds have been added, as a preliminary to the theft of his property. The action of poison is, however, so powerful that sometimes the victim does not recover. The leaves are use for making cigarettes and fumigating powders for the relief of asthma. Ganja eaters often adulterate the ganja with datura leaves. The juice of this plant is believed to be a cure for hydrophobia. The leaves are boiled and used as a poultice to relieve pain.
The seeds contain both hyoscyamine and scopolamine (Jour. Chem. Ind. ; 1911)
