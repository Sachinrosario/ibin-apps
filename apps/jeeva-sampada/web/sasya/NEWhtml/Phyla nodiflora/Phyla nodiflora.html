DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves opposite, subsessile, obovate-spathulate, 1.0-2.5 x 0.5-1.0 cm, obtuse at apex, cuneate at base, decurrent into petiole, serrate above the middle, entire towards base, glabrascent, hairy on both the surfaces. Inflorescence solitary in the upper leaf axils, cylindric, densely many flowered; peduncles solitary in each axil, slender, densely or sparsely pubescent; bracteoles absent. Calyx cupular; lobes 2, lanceolate, acute. Corolla purplish white, 2-2.5 mm across; tube c 1-5 mm long. Drupes globose, c 1.0 mm across, nearly dry, hard, brown.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
In Ayurveda system of medicine the plant is used as aphrodisiac, astringent, stomachic, vulnerary, anthelmintic, alexiteric and cooling. It is useful in heart, blood, eye diseases and also useful in ulcer, wounds, burning sensatoin, asthma, bronchitis and thirst. In Unani system of medicine plants are used as diuretic, fever, cold and urinary diseases.

 


Chemistry:
Glycosides, nodiflorin A, nodiflorin B-lactose, maltose, fructose, glucose and xylose were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Flavone nodifloretin (fig. 1) was characterisd as 4’,5,6,7-tretrahydroxy-3’-methoxyflavone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Nodifloridins A and B were isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Nodifloretin isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Two flavone glycosides lippiflorin A (fig. 2) and lippiflorin B (fig. 3) were isolated and characterized as3’,4’,5,6,-tetrahydroxy-7-O-L-arabinosyl flavone and 4’-O-L-rhamnoside of lippiflorin A respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

6-hydroxyluteolin-7-O-apioside, luteolin-7-O-glucoside, 6-hydroxyluteolin, nepetin and batatofolin isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

A complex mixture of hydrocarbons and oxygenated compounds were isolated from volatile fraction; among hydrocarbons calamene and b-caryophyllene predominated whereas 1-octen-3-ol, phenethyl alcohol linalool, p-cymen-8-ol and methyl salicylate were constituted 10% to 20% of total oxygenated compounds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Halleridone and Hallerone from Phyla nodiflora as taxonomic markers. (Ravikant et al, 2000).

 


Pharmacology:
Petroleum ether and chloroform extract suppressed exploratory behaviour and spontaneous motor activity of mice; petroleum ether extract antagonized (+)amphetamine stereotypy, suppressed acetic acid writhing and carregeenin-induced oedema without inhibiting granulation tissue formation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.


Ethnobotany:
 Medicine for boils (Leaf, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973); diuretic (Leaf, Sudhakar and Rolla, 1985; Whole plant, Singh, 1945); Fever (Whole plant, Singh, 1945); menstrual complaints (Whole plant, Audichya et al, 1983); Postnatal fever (Whole plant, Singh and Pandey, 1980)
