DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Small to moderate-sized trees, up to 10 m tall; twigs densely brownish, with small scurfy scales. Leaves deltoid or ovate-disc shaped, 5-20 x 5.5-15 cm, deeply cordate at base with a broad sinus, densely covered with minute scales, 7-nerved at base, prominent with domalia in axils of  main  veins beneath, densely brownish with scurfy scales; petioles 5-15 cm long, scaly; stipules lanceolate to subulate, 0.4-1 cm long, early falling, scaly.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flowers solitary, axillary, rarely 2-together, 6-8 cm across; pedicels 3-12 cm long, curve downward, densely brownish with small scurfy scales, jointed near base. Epicalyx segments 3, linear-oblong or lanceolate, 0.5-2 mm long, scaly very early falling. Calyx 8-10 mm with 5 minute teeth, densely scaly outsides, silky at base, 5-7 x 4.0-5.5 cm across, accrescent and flattened in fruit, Corolla light yellow with dark purple centre, bell-shaped, 5-6 cm long; lobes obliquely obovate, rounded at apex, densely scaly outside, glabrous inside. Staminal column included, 1.5-2.5 cm long. Capsule globose, obtuse at apex or slightly depressed; exocarp smooth, separated from tough endocarp  by loose, fibrous-spongy mesocarp, dehiscing into 5-valves, black when  ripe. Seeds 4 per locule, ovoid, c 10 x 6 mm, angular, densely covered with short clavate or bulbous hairs.
Floral and seed biology:
Throughout the year, but chiefly during July to January.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Barks, leaves, flowers and fruits are applied in cutaneous affections. Bark, roots &Fruits are useful in dysentry and haemorrhoids. Leaves are also useful in scabies, constipation and swell joints.


Chemistry:

·          A flavonoid – thespesin mp. 1900 – isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960 -1969).

·          (+) gossypol, mp. 1810, from flowers, fruits and bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·          Populneol isolated and identified as monobenzyl ether of g-resacetophenone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·          Gossypol isolated from flowers; its methylation yielded three isomeric optically active hexamethyl ethers, which were characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·          Kaempferol, quercetin, rutin, kaempferol -3- rutinoside, kaempferol – 3 – glucoside, kaempferol -5-glucoside and quercetin – 3 – glucoside isolated from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·          Calycopterin isolated from heartwood. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·          Two new sesquiterpenoid quinines – thespesone and thespone – isolated from heartwood and their structure elucidated; mansonones C,D,E and F also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·          Isolation of alanine, arginine, methionine and tryptophan from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·          Lupeol, lupenone and b-sitosterol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·           Lupenone, lupeol, b-sitosterol, alkanes (C19-33) and esters (C40-52) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·          New flavonoids were isolated from the ether and ethyl acetate fractions of the ethanol extract of the flowers of Thespesia populnea. These include a new flavone, 5,8-dihydroxy-7-methoxyflavone ,  an isoflavone 7-hydroxyisoflavone , and the hitherto unreported glycosides namely tamarixetin-7-O-beta-D-glucoside , and kaempferol-7-0-beta-D-rutinoside . (Shirwaikar and Srinivasan, 1996)

A new mansonone, 7-hydroxy-2,3,5,6-tetrahydro-3,6,9-trimethylnaphtho(1,8-b,c)pyran-4,8-dione, in addition to mansonones D, E and F were isolated from the heartwood of Thespesia populnea. (Milbrodt et al., 1997) CA. 127:202879.

Pharmacology:

·          Lupenone, lupeol, b-sitosterol, alkanes (C19-33) and esters (C40-52) were evaluated against Staphylococcus aureus, S. albus, Bacillus subtilis, Escherichia coli, Klebsiella, Shigella dysenteriae, Proteus vulgaris and Pseudomonas pyocyanee in vitro. Alkanes, lupenone and lupeol were active against both Gram +ve bacteria. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·          The antihepatotoxic activity of ethanol extract fractions of Thespesia populnea administered orally to groups of rats was evaluated using the CCl4 model of liver injury. All fractions showed significant activity. A rare flavanoid, quercetin-7-O-rhamnoglucoside, was isolated from this plant and its identity confirmed by spectral studies. (Shirwaikar et al., 1995)

·          Anti-steroidogenic activity of various extracts of T. populnea was screened in female albino mice. The weight of the uterus and ovaries were reduced significantly and the cholesterol and ascorbic acid content in ovaries were significantly elevated due to the treatment with extract of T. populnea. The significant inhibition of DELTA5, 3beta hydroxy steroid dehydrogenase and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase, the two key enzymes involved in ovarian steroidogenesis were also observed in mouse ovaries after 15 days of treatment. (Kavimani et al., 1999)

·          Aqueous extract of T. populnea fruit showed significant wound healing activity in the excision wound and incision wound models in rats following topical and oral administration, respectively. (Nagappa and Cheriyan, 2001)

·          Flow cytometric estimation on cytotoxic activity of leaf extracts from seashore plants in subtropical Japan: isolation, quantification and cytotoxic action of (-)-deoxypodophyllotoxin. (Masuda et al., 2002)

·          Antioxidant activity of the aqueous (AET) and methanolic extracts (MET) of the Thespesia populnea bark was investigated in rats by inducing liver injury with carbon tetrachloride:olive oil (1:1). The extracts exhibited significant antioxidant activity showing increased levels of glutathione peroxidase (GPX), glutathione S-transferase (GST), glutathione reductase (GRD), superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) and decreased level of lipid peroxidation (LPO). Thespesia populnea bark extracts, AET and MET, at a dose level of 500 mg/kg showed significant antioxidant activity against carbon tetrachloride-induced liver injury in rats. (Ilavarasan et al., 2003)

·          Male swiss mice were administered with the leaf extract of Thespesia populnea at a daily dose of 400mg/kg body weight for 15 days and the testis were subjected to structural analysis. The structure of the seminiferous tubule in the testis of treated animal was elongated. Sertoli cells were enlarged in its structure and spermatids became round and disintegrated. It is suggested that the extract of T. populnea treatment leads to pathological changes in the seminiferous tubules, sertoli cells and spermatids of the testis.(Krishnamoorthy et al., 2003)

 

Ethnobotany:

Medicine for constipation (Leaf, Bhargava,1983); scabies (Leaf, Nagendra Prasad and Abraham, 1984); skin disease (fruit, Shah, 1984, Shah et al., 1984); swelling joints (Leaf, Shah, 1984).
