UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:
·        The seeds of Trichosanthes dioica contain a large amount of peptides in the range of 2-8 kd (Kabir, et al 2000). BA 108 (1) 1772, CA-134: 206917y.

·        Free sugar and dietry fibre contents of Trichosanthes dioica was determined. Total free sugar content varied between 0.6-4.9 percent (fresh wt. basis). Glucose and fructose were the only two free sugars identified in the vegetables. Soluble Dietary fiber (DF) fraction was lower than the corresponding insoluble DF. Rhamnose, arabinose, xylose,mannose, galactose and glucose are present in varying proportions in all the SDF samples. (Nahar, et. al., 1998). C.A. 1999, 130-181748w.

Pharmacology:

·        Seed extract lowered blood sugar, serum cholesterol and triglycerides and increased the levels of phospholipids and HDL-cholesterol in rabbits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        T. dioica is found to have hypoglycemic activity, helping in lowering of blood sugar levels. (Mukherjee, 1996). MAPA 9701-0152.

·        Treatment of the patient with Abyanga local application of oil containing trichosanthes dioica and other herbs, (1/2 teaspoonful twice a day) and Arogyawardhini vati produced remarkable improvement in skin pathology and related symptoms. (Zanchi, et. al., 1998). MAPA 9903-1620.

 

Ethnobotany:
·        Medicine for boils (Fruit, Tarafder, 1984); Spermatorrhoea (Fruit, Bhalla et al, 1982); Edibel as vegetable (Fruit, Arora, 1981)

Miscellaneous Information

·         Antibacterial activity of the nonsaponifiable parts of the fixed oils of Tichosanthes dioca along with other species of Trichosanthes was studies. Petroleum ether extracts of the seeds were found to possess appreciable antibacterial activity against Bacilis anthracis and Xanthomonas malracearum. (Hariti and Rathee, 1995). MAPA 9603-1513.
