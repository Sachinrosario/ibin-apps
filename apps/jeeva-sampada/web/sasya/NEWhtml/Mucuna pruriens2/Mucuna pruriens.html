DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
It is an annual, haif-woody, twining herb with long cylindrical branches. Leaves are alternate, 3-foliate; petioles are 6.3-11.3 cm. Long; are ovate or rhomboid, membranous and glabrescent above, 15-20 cm. Long and are on short, thick, hairy stalks with stipulate at their base. Flowering racemes are 15-30 cm. Long and are solitary or 2-3 together bearing purplish flowers. Pods is 5-7.5 by 1.2 cm.; 4-6 seeded, turgid. Falcately curved on both end, densely clothed with persistent irritant bristles, which are at first pale brown and afterwards turn steel grey.Seeds nare 4-5, separated by cellular partitions, about 0.5-0.75 cm long , ovoid somewhat compressed smooth , brownish often mottled with black, Hilum is large and oblong.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Seeds- anthelmintic (hairs) aphrodisiac, astringent, diuretic and nervine tonic. It is useful in cholera (in the form of a strong infusion), dropsy general debility, leucorrhoea, seminal weakness and as a tonic.


Chemistry:
NBPGR: The seeds contains alkaloids mucuadine, mucuadinine, mucuadininene and pruienidine along with mucunin, mucunadine and small amount of nicotine. Seeds also contain L-dopa, glutarthione lecthin, gallic acid, a glucoside and aminoacids. Trichomes from pods yield serotonin.

The total indole alkylamines produced marked behavioral changes, antagonized pentabarbitone – induced hypnosis, inhibited reserpine – induced ptosis, hypothermia, chlorpromazine – induced catatonia and enchanced amphetamine toxicity aggregated rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

b-Sitosterol, stigmasterol, a-amyrin acetate, ursolic acid, betulinic acid, acacetin and luteolin were isolated and characterized by chem. and spectroscopic methods. (Aruna et al, 1998)


Pharmacology:
Plant extractive showed marked  hypotensive and hypocholesterolaemic effects in normal rats (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Mucuna pruriens endocarp showed twice the antiparkinsonian activity compared with synthetic L-DOPA in inducing contralateral rotation in the parkinsonian animal model.  Thus, Mucuna pruriens endocarp may contain unidentified antiparkinsonian compounds, in addition to L-DOPA, or it may have adjuvants that enhance the efficacy of L-DOPA.  (Hussain and Manyam, 1997) C.A. 1997, 127:326410

The antisnake properties of an extract of Mucuna pruriens' seeds (MP101UJ) in vivo were recently demonstrated and one is now investigating its biochemical mechanism. Echis carinatus venom (EV) contains a mixture of proteins that affect the coagulative cascade, causing severe bleeding and haemorrhage. Here the effect of an extract of MP101UJ in prothrombin activation by EV in vitro by clotting and chromogenic assay is studied. An increase in procoagulant activity was found.( Guerranti et al,2001)

Alcohol extract of the seeds of M. pruriens has an antilipid peroxidation property, which is mediated through the removal of superoxides and hydroxyl radicals.( Tripathi and Upadhyay, 2002)

The effect of Momordica charantia and Mucuna pruriens in experimental diabetes and their effect on key metabolic enzymes involved in carbohydrate metabolism. (Rathi et al, 2002)
