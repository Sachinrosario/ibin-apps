DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Inflorescence: Solitary or axillary umbellate heads ; Flower: White, fragrant, more or less pubescent, hermaphrodite, pentamerous ; Calyx: Pubescent, teeth short-deltoid; Corolla: Funnel-shaped, tube glabrous, petals connate at the base, lobes triangular, acute, pubescent outside ;Androecium: Stamens indefinite, monoadelphous at the base, filaments much longer than the corolla, connate at the base into a tube; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, style filiform, stigma minute, capitate  ; Fruit:Pods 4-12 linear-oblong, bluntly pointed, thin, pale yellow, reticulately veined above the seeds, smooth shining ; Seed:4-12, ellipsoid, compressed, foveolate on the faces pale brown ;  . Flowering & Fruiting : March – May.

Chemistry:

Sapogenin acaeic acid (m.p. 277-79°C) was identified in bark of Albizzia lebbeck. (Hasan et al, 1961).  

Albigenin (fig.1), a triterpenoid sapogenin was isolated from the beans of Albizzia lebbeck has been shown to be 3b-hydroxy-16-oxo-13(18)en-28-noroleanane. (Barua and Raman, 1962).  

Echinocystic acid (fig.2) and b-sitosterol were identified in extract from leaves of A.lebbeck. ( Varshney and Sharma, 1969), C.A. 160352, 75,1971.  

Benzyl acetate, Benzyl benzoate and crocetin were isolated from the flowers of Albizzia lebbeck. ( Jain and Mishra, 1963).  

(-)-melacacidin (m.p. 190°C) and lebbecacidin was isolated from ether soluble fraction of pet ether extract of heartwood. (Rayudu and Rajadurai, 1965), C.A. 2045g, 64, 1966.  

Saponin from A. lebbeck pods after hydrolysis yielded echinocystic acid, oleanolic acid, albigenin and albigenic acid. (Varshney et al, 1971).  

A glycoside lebbekanin C was isolated, on hydrolysis it yielded Echinocytic acid, glucose and rhamnose. (Varshney et al, 1973).  

Neutral constituents, Friedelin (friedelan-3-one, m.p. 260-262°C) and g-sitosterol were isolated from bark of this plant. (Tripathi and Dasgupta, 1974).  

The seeds of Albizzia lebbeck yielded a triterpenic saponin lebbekanin-A (fig.3), (m.p. 205-6°C), on hydrolysis yields glucose, galactose, arabinose, xylose, fucose and rhamnose in the molar ratio of 5:1:1:1:1:2 and echinocystic acid as the genin. (Varshney and Handa, 1973).  

Lebbekanin D, a triterpenic saponin has been isolated from the flowers of Albizzia lebbeck Benth. On acid hydrolysis it gives echinocystic acid as sapogenin and glucose, galactose, arabinose, xylose and rhamnose as sugar in molar ratio of 2:2:5:3:3. (Varshney et al, 1975).  

Lebbekanin E was isolated from the wood of Albizzia lebbeck, on hydrolysis, it yields echinocystic acid and glucose, arabinose, xylose and rhamnose in molar ratio of 4:2:1:1. (Varshney et al, 1976). Vicenin-II, veynoutrin, rutin, myricitin and robinin were obtained from the leaves of Albizzia leebeck. (Morita et al, 1977). C.A. 148947b, 88, 1978.

From the heartwood of Albizzia lebbeck two compounds, (-)-2,3-cis-3,4-cis-3-O-methylmelacacidin (fig.4a) as its methyl ether (fig.4b) and 3`-O-methylmelanoxetin (fig.4c) have been isolated in addition to melacacidin melanoxetin (IN). (Deshpande and Shastri, 1977).

The flower of Albizzia lebbeck yields four saponin glycosides named as lebbekanins D, F, G and H, on acid hydrolysis gave the same genin echinocystric acid. Lebbekanin, F and G gave glucose, arabinose, xylose, fucose and rhamnose in molar ratios of 2:2:2:1:3 and 3:2:2:2:3 respectively. Lebbekanin, D and H gave galactose, glucose, arabinose, xylose and rhamnose in the molar ratios of 2:2:5:3:3 and 2:4:3:3:3 respectively. (Varshney 1978).

Lebbekanin E (m.p. 125-6°C) was isolated from wood of A. lebbeck, It is a glycoside of acacic acid with glucose, arabinose, xylose and rhamnose in a molar ratio of 4:2:1:1. (Varshney et al, 1979), C.A. 120369c, 91, 1979.

A saponin has been isolated from the roots of A. lebbeck and identified as echinocystic acid, 3-O-L-rhamnopyranosyl (1à5)-O-D-xylofuranosyl (1à5)-O-D-arabinofuranosyl(1à4)-O-D-glucopyranoside. (Shrivastava and saxena, 1986).

Hexane fraction of the seeds of A. leebeck gave yellow brown oil (4.8%) that contained myristic (0.11%), palmitic (16.17%), stearic (2.71%), arachidic (2.01%), behenic (4.57%), oleic (18.23%) linoleic (54.06%) and linolenic (0.16%) acids along with vitamin A and vitamin D. (Ahmed et al, 1990) C.A. 52299c, 112, 1990.

A new acyclic ester heneicos-7(z)enyl 24-hydroxy-tetracos-10 (z)enoate, (m.p.103°C), in addition to lupeol, oleanolic acid, docosanoic acid and b-sitosterol have been isolated and characterized from the hexane extract of Albizzia lebbeck. (Agarwal and Singh,1991).

Three main saponins named albiziasaponins A, B and C were isolated from the barks of Albizia lebbeck and their structures were established as acacic acid lactone-3-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à2)-a-L-arabinopyranosyl-(1à6)-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig.5a), 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1à2)-O-[a-L-arabinopyranosyl-(1à6)]-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig.5b) and 3-O-b-D-xylopyranosyl-(1à2)-a-L-arabinopyranosyl-(1à6)-O-[b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1à2)]-b-D-glucopyranoside. (Pal et al, 1995).

Methanolic extract of the seeds of Alibizzia lebbeck has yielded three new macrocyclic spermine alkaloids, budmunchiamines L1-L3 (fig.6), structures have been determined spectral analysis. (Mishra et al, 1995).

Two new tri-O-glycoside flavanols, Kaempferol and quercetin 3-O-a-rhamnopyranosyl(1à6)-b-glucopyranosyl(1à6)-b-galactopyranosides were identified from the leaves of Albizzia lebbeck. (Mousallamy, 1997).

Methanolic extract of the seeds of Albizzia lebbeck gave new macrocyclic alkaloids budmunochiamines L4,L5 and L6 (fig.6). (Dixit and Misra, 1997).

 


Pharmacology:

Decoction of the bark and flower of Albizzia lebbeck were studied for its anti-asthmatic and anti-anaphylatic activity. The decoctions protected the guinea pig against histamine as well as acetylchoile induced broncho-spasm. Chronic treatment with the bark decoction has also protected the sensitized guinea pigs against antigen challenge (Tripathi and Das, 1977).

Saponin fraction of Albizzia lebbeck has been studied on the mas cells in the mesentery and peritoneal fluid of rats subjected to anaphylaxis (Johri et al, 1984).

Mode of action study of the active principles against aerobes showed that the glycosides caused leakage of cytoplasmic constituents. Electron micrographs of S. aureus cells treated with minimum inhibitory concentration of anthraquinones reveal granulation of the cytoplasmic matrix, vacuolation of the cells and in a few cases, disruption of the cell surface (Ganguli and Bhatt, 1993).

The chloroform fraction of methanolic extract of A. lebbeck leaves protected mice against maximal electroshocks, pentylenetetrazol and picrotoxin induced convulsions (Kasture et al, 1996).

A. lebbeck leaf aqueous extract caused stimulation followed by inhibition of the motility of the rabbit jejunum and guinea pig ileum (Relaxant activity), (Taniva et al, 1996).

Hot aqueous extract of stem bark of A. lebbeck were evaluated for their antiallergic and antianaphylatic activity using invivo and invitro models by inhibiting passive cutaneous anaphylaxis in rat (Baruah et al, 1997).

Ethanolic extract of leaves of Albizzia lebbeck exhibited anticonvulsant activity, which protected animals from maximum electroshock, electrical kindling, lithium-pilocarpine, phentylenetetrazole induced convulsions in mice (Kasture et al, 2000).

The immunomodulatory effect of the bark of Alibizzia lebbeck was evaluates by studying humoral and cell mediated immune response. A. lebbeck treated mice developed higher serum antibody titre (Barua et al, 2000).

The effect of saponin containing n-butanolic fraction extracted from dried leaves of Albizzia lebbeck was studied on cognitive behaviour in albino mice, study suggests involvement of gamma-aminobutric acid in the nootropic and anxiolytic activity of saponins obtained from A. lebbeck (Une et al, 2001).
