HABIT & HABITAT
Small tree or shrub, reaching up to 9 m high; bark yellowish, young part glabraous or slightly pubescent, sometimes spinous.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves broadly elliptic, 5-9 x 3.2-6.3 cm, acuminate at apex, entire or upper half with a few coarse teeth, glabraous, minutely hairy along nerves on both surfaces; nerves 4-5 pairs; petioles 1-1.6 cm long. Flowers small, in terminal pubescnt paniculate corymbose cymes, greenish yellow, with a disagreeable odour; bract minute lancelate. Calyx 2.5 cm long, thick, glabraous, 2-lipped, one lip-2-toothed. Corolla glabrous on outer surface; tube 3 x 3 mm, cylindric, throat hairy, sub-equally 4-lobed; lobes oblong, rounded, 1.2 mm long. Stamens slightly exerted, filaments hairy at base. Ovary and style glabrous, stigma of 2 equal divaricate lobes. Fruits 4 mm long, pear-shaped, eendocarp ridges, bony, 4-celled, 4-seeded.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Roots are used as laxative, stomachic and alexipharmic. It is also used in anaemia, diabetes chyluria, inflammation, swellings, bronchitis, dyspepsia, piles, constipation and fever. Leaves are also useful in piles and tumours.

In Unani system of medicine roots are used as laxative, stomachic and liver complaints. Soup made from leaves is occasionally used as stomachic and carminative. A decoction of leaves are given in flactulence.


Chemistry:

A compound, mp. 155°C, isolated from root bark, was active only against gram-positive organisms. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
b-sitosterol isolated from leaves and stem bark; betulin isolated from stem bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).
Isolation of aphelandrine and luteolin from stem bark and leaves respectively. A new sesquiterpene - 5-hydroxy-4,5-dihydrocaryophyllen-3-one (fig.1 ) - and two new diterpenes - 11b-hydroxy-7-ketosandaracopimar-8(14),15-diene (fig.2 ) and premnenol (fig.3 ) isolated from root bark; premnaspirodiene, sandaracopimar-8(14),15-diene, its 7a-hydroxy derivative and caryophyllen-3-one also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Roots afforded 6-C-b-D-glucopyranosyl-8-C-b-D-xylopyranosyl apigenin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Isolation of two new phenolic diterpenoids - 11,12,16-trihydroxy-abieta-5,8,11,13-tetraen-7-one and 11,14-dihydroxy-12,16-epoxy-abitea-5,8,11,13-tetraen-7-one from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.
The isoxazole alkaloid - premnazole - isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.
An iridoid glucoside conjugate - premcoryoside (fig. 4) isolated from leaves along with verbacoside (acetoside), 2''-caffeoyl-, 2''-trans- and cis-p-coumaroyl-, 3''-p-coumaroyl esters of 6-O-a-L-rhamnopyranosylcatalpol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Pharmacology: Premnazole exhibited anti-inflammatory activity comparable to phenylbutazone in reducing pellet-induced granuloma formation in rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.
The effect of Premna integrifolia on blood glucose in sreptozotocin induced type 1 and type 2 diabetic rats was studied. (Alamgir et al, 2001).
Premna integrifolia was evaluated for hypoglycaemic activity inalloxan diabetic rats. (Kar et al, 2003).
