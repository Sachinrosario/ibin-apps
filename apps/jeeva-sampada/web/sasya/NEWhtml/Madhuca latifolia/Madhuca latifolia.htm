Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Large tree occurs in tropical medium rainfall areas of Indo-Gangetic Plain and the Peninsula (moist tropical), Dry areas of Rajasthan and Peninsular India (dry tropical). Ocurs in northern and central India and northern parts of the Indian Peninsula. Suitable for open lands around village boundaries and in village commons.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A large deciduous tree with a rounded crown. Bark grey, brown or blackish, with shallow wrinkles and cracks. Leaves leathery, clustered at the ends of branches 13-20 cm, elliptic-oblong on 2.5-3.7 cm long stalks which reveal drops of milky juice when cut. Flowers in clusters at or near ends of branches, 1.25 cm across, cream coloured, scented, on 2.5-5 cm long woolly stalks. Calyx leathery, deeply 4-lobed. Corolla fleshy, 1.5 cm, sweet, with 8 or 9 lobes, but sometimes 7 or up to 14, falling off early. Stamens 24-26, anthers on very short filaments. The ovary hjas prominent long style. Fruit a green juicy berry 2.5-5 cm long, containing 1-4 brown polished seeds 2-3.3 X 1.25-1.8 cm.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

 

Breeding system and varietal improvement:

Phenology:

Flowers during February-April. Fruit ripens during June to August.

Leaves fall from February-April, new leaves about April or early May and are coppery red when young.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: Grown by entire transplanting and by direct sowing.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Diseases:

Scopella echinulata (Niessl) Mains – foliage rust from Calcutta, Palamau, Pusa, Tholkobad, Varanasi and Banglore

Pestalotiopsis sp. – leaf spot from Allahabad (U.P.)

Miscellaneous Information

The scented, sweetish, fleshy flowers have an irresistible attraction for bears, deer and fruit and nectar-eating birds, such as mynas, bulbuls, parakeets, flowerpeckers and white-eyes. The birds are reported to get drunk when they peck fermented flowers.

One of the most important forest trees of India.
