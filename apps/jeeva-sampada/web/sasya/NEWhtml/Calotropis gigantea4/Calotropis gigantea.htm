Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A much – branched, hardy, erect, wooly shrub, 1-5 m in height, native to India, found growing up to an altitude of 900 m throughout India including the Andamans. 


Availability :

They occur almost throughout India from Punjab and Rajasthan in nothr to kanniya kumari in the south, extending into West Bengal and Assam in the east. Both species are common in West Bengal ,Assam, and South India. 
Conservation/ Threat Status : Plants are practically wild in all parts of India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stems woody, round, tender ones covered with soft, loosely appressed, whitish, waxy or sometimes powdery pubescence; bark thick, light yellow or ash-grey, soft, corky, deeply fissured; leaves fleshy, cuneate-obovate or obovate-oblong, with a narrow cordate or often ampexicaule base, 10.0-20.0 cm X 2.5-7.5 cm, smooth above, cottony below; flowers lilac or pale rose or purple, rarely light greenish yellow or white, in simple or compound cymose-corymbs; follicles 2 or 1 fleshy, recurved, 7-10 cm long; seeds brown, numerous, broadly ovate, flattened with 2.5-3.2 cm long, white, tuft of silky hair at the pointed end.

Miscellaneous Information

 The rootbark or both species resembles ipecae in its action, and is used as a substitute for it. In small doses, the rootbark is diaphoretic and expectorant. It acts as a mild stimulant, and is given with carminatives in dyspepsia. It is useful in leprosy, and eczema. The powder rootbark gives relief in diarrhoea and dysentery. It is also given in cough and asthma, and as febrifuge. In large doses, the drug causes nausea, vomitng and diarrhoea. Prolonged higher doses cause headache, burning in micturition ahd leucorrhoea. Calotropain has marked anti-blood coagulating activity, which does not last very long but is desirable in drugs used for treating coronary thrombosis. In the Homeopathic system of medicine, it is reported to be useful in elephantiasis lupus and chronic rheumatism. 

In pharmacological trials, the extracts of root exhibited, was observed to show hypertensive effect with irregularity of cardiac function in the frog-heart, cardiotonic activitydeep tonic contraction of reversible nature on the uterus of guineapig, and strong reaction on the intestinal loop, depending upon the concentration and the dose. 

Accidental splashing of latex into the eye causes congestion of the eye with tear and local anaesthesia, followed by deeper effect due to absorption. Washing the eye with water followed by application of atropine and chloramphenical ointment gives relief. The juice of Calligonum polygonoides is reported to be an antidote. The latex of the both the species is reported to be used to induce abortion, it is also used as an infanticide. 

The seedlings and leaves possess anti-fungal properties, the former inhibiting the growth of Fusarium nivale F. oxysporum, Helminthosporium sativum, Colletotrichumfalcatum.

The soot and leaf extracts possess strong anti-bacterial properties, they are particularly active against Escherichia coli, Micrococcus pyogenes, Treponema pallidum. 

The tender, fresh leaves are often used in the indigenous system of medicine to cure fits and convulsions in children. The extract of the leaves which have been smeared with oil and rock-salt and warmed, is poured into the ear for earache. The fresh or warmed leaves or their poultice is bandaged on painful rheumatic joints, swellings, sores and wounds, A powder of the leaves is dusted on wounds and ulcers to inhibit excessive granulation. In cutaneous diseases, the leaves boiled with turmeric and oil are used. A tincture of the leaves is useful in remittent fevers. The alcoholic, aqueous and petroleum ether extract of leaves and flowers of C.gigantean and C.procera have shown anti-implantation activity; the petroleum ether extract showed abortifacient activity. The leaves are also used to destroy lice; they are rubbedon the skin of elephant to treat Kesarayar disease.

The flower tops contain an active, to extract if anti-bacterial activity against Micrococcus pyogenes, Treponema pallidum, Neisseria gonnorrhoea, Salmonella typhi, Shigella dysenteriae. 

It causes widespread testicular necrosis, and damage to liver.
