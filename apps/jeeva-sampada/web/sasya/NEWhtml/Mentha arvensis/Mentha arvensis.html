DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Erect or decumbent herbs, c 33.5 cm high. Stems short, branched, densely blackish tomentose, 4-angled. Leaves ovate-lanceolate, 1-4 x 0.5-2.5 cm, acute at apex, narrowed at base, serrate along margin, lateral nerves 4-5 pairs, more prominent on the lower surface, diffuse hair on upper surface; petioles 0.2-1.3 cm long. Flowers tinge dull violet, mixed a little with white, in axillary, distant whorls. Calyx tubular, pubescent; lobes acuminate at apex. Corolla tinged with violet mixed with a little white, lined hairy outside. Nutlets dry, smooth.

NBPGR:  Habit erect, stem short, branched, with short hairs dense and turned back;  leaves narrowed below, stalked, ovate, oblong, lanceolate, toothed, the upper similar and large; flowers are lilac, in axillary distant whorls, none at the top, calhyx bell shaped with triangular, short teeth. Throat of calyx naked.  Corolla lined with hairs and hairy outside.  Bracteoles acute, not as long as the flowers.  Nutlets dry smooth.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
The plant is usefully prescribed in the diseases of liver, spleen, asthma and pains in the joints. The dried plant is refrigerant, stomachic, diuretic. It possesses antispasmodic properties and often  used in jaundice. Infusion of leaves and stem is used  as antispasmodic and also given in fever, indigestion and cephalalgia. The  juice of leaves is applied to the stings or bite of poisonous animals. The leaves powdered with salt is applied to the white low (Unani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).
NBPGR:  The dried plant is refrigerant, stomachic, diuretic and stimulant.  It posses antipasmodic and emmenagogue properties.  It is used in jaundice, and is frequently given to stop vomiting.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:  Major components of essential oil are menthol and methole.  Others are α and β pinene, α– thujene,1-limonene, β-phellandrene, furfural, methylcyclohex – anone and camphene.

NBRI : Detection of a-pinene, b-pinene, limonene, and octan-3-ol, menthion, neomenthol, 1-menthol, isomenthol, pulegone and piperitone in essential oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Detection of a- and b- pinen, myrcene, limonene, p-cymene, 3-octanol, methane, (-)menthol,menthylacetate, (+)pulegone, piperitone, piperitone oxide and piperitenone oxide in essential oil of North Vietnamese plant by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Essential oil from Vietnamese plants found to contain menthol (15.6-45.0), menthone (12.5-260), pulegone (8.2-26.0), piperitone oxide (3.2-14.5) and piperitenone oxide (2.6-22.8%).(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Determination of linalool (48.5), geraniol (10.3), a- and b-citral (6.2),linalyl acetate (5.3), geranyl acetate (4.6), nerol (3.3), a-pinene (1.62), citronellol (1.48), limonene (1.0), b-pinene (0.65) and piperitone (0.5%) in essential oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Twelve compounds were found in essential oil of  Mentha arvensis with the help of GC-MS.( Qaisar et al, 1996). C.A. 1997,126:342589

The essential oil of Mentha arvensis L. var. piperascens Malinv. growing in Cuba has been analyzed by GC and GC/MS.  Twenty-one compds. were identified, of which menthol (51.68%), menthone (26.08%) and menthyl acetate (10.55%) were the major constituents.( Pino et al, 1996)

 


Pharmacology:
Alcoholic extract of leaves at a dose of 100 mg/kg and 500 mg/kg showed 80 and 100% inhibition of implementation in female rats. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Alcoholic extract of leaves also showed encouraging anti-ovulatory activity in rabbits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

The petroleum ether extract of the leaves of M. arvensis possess reversible antifertility property without adverse toxicity in male mice. (Sharma and Jacob, 2002).

Chloroform extract provides protection against the radiation-induced sickness and mortality and the optimum protective dose of 10 mg/kg is safe from the point of drug-induced toxicity. ( Jagetia and Baliga, 2002).

Reversible contraceptive efficacy of methanolic extract of Mentha arvensis leaves. Aqueous solution of the extract (10 mg per day per mouse) when administered orally to male mice of proven fertility for 20, 40 and 60 days caused inhibition of fertility while maintaining their normal sexual behaviour. With the increase in treatment duration, there occurred a corresponding decrease in the mean weight of testis and accessory organs of reproduction. (Sharma and Jacob, 2002).
