HABIT & HABITAT:
 The plant is abundant and grows wild throughout the plains of India and up to an elelvation of 6000 ft. Found throughout the Eastern and Southern Peninsulas of India from Kumaon to Bengal, Assam, Cachar, MAdras and Ceylon.  Its cultivation is limitted to parts of Bengal and Chennai.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A glabrous robust sedge with culms 2-5 ft. high arising from a creeping woody rhizome. It grows on heavy clayey soils deficient in drainage, and on low lying swamps and saline lands unfit for any other crop.

Cultivation:
Propagation: It is propagated by suckers or slips with bulbs containing short lengths of culms. About 16,000 bundles each containing 10-15 slips, are requird to plant 1 acre. Selected bulbs are palnted 4-5 in. apart. 2-3 weedings are given during the first year and in subsequent years, one weeding after each cutting.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Culms

Uses

  • Culms are used for making mats
  • Eaten occasionally by buffaloes.


Harvesting Practices
The plant is ready for harvest in about 10 months after planting. Culms are cut before the flower heads dry up. The harvested culms obtained in the first year are thick and coarse and the yield is low. Thin culms of good quality are obtained only from later cuttings, and 2 harvests are taken in a year from the second year onwards. After harvesting, the stems are removed to the drying floor, damaged culms rejected, flower heads cut off and the culms stired in to different lengths. They are then dried for 2-3 hrs. and split into 2 or more pieces by a special knife. Split stems are further dried for a day or two and stacked.
Yields up to 5 tonns per acre are possible.
