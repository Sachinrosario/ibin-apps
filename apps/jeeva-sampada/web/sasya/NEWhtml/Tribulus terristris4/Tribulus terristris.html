Chemistry:

·        Seed extract showed diuretic effect in rats which could be attributed to presence of potassium salts in high concentration. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Diosgenin, gitogenin, mp. 2670 and clhorogenin, mp. 2750, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Kaempferol, its 3-glucoside, its 3-glucoside, its 3-rutinoside and tribuloside isolated from fruits and leaves; tribuloside characterized as kaempferol -3b-D-(6”-p-coumaroyl) glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        Diosgenin, ruscogenin and a dihydroxy spirosterolidal sapogenin isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Five diosgenin glycosides isolated; one contained glucose, arabinose and rhamnose, two contained glucose and rahmnose and remaining two contained only glucose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Campesterol, b-sitosterol, stigmasteril, diosgenin and neotigogenin isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        A new saponin –terrestroside F-along with saponins C and G isolated from aerial parts; saponins C and G proved to be mixture of two tigogenin and diosgenin glycosides each containing glucose and rhamnose; astragalin also isolated; partial structure of purified saponin C proposed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Trillin, gracillin and dioscin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Hecogenin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Structure elucidation studies on tribuloside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Process for isolation of diosgenin developed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Two new steroidal glycosides – neohecogenin glucoside and tribulosin isolated from aerial parts and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Four glycosides of kaempferol, six glycosides of quercetin and eight glycosides of isorhamnetin isolated and characterized; sugar units in all cases attached at C-3, four of these compounds also carry additional glucose unit at C-7. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Isolation of kaempferol and quercetin from fruits and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Tigogenin, hecogenin and gitogenin identified in hydrolysed extract of fruits, roots, leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Diosgenin content determined by HPLC in roots (0.13%), stems (0.78%), leaves (0.12%) and fruits of plant growing in Turkey. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Isolation of diosgenin, hecogenin, ruscogenin and spirosta-3, 5-diene from flowers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        A new cinnamic amide – terrestriaminde – isolated along with 7-methylhydroindanone and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        The known furostanol  saponins methyl protodioscin  and  protodioscin  and two new sulfated saponins Na salt of  26-O-b-glucopyranosyl-22a-methoxy-25( R)   furost –5-ene-3b 26 diol- 3-O-a- rhamnopyranosyl–(1®2)-b-4-O-sulfo  glucopyranoside (methyl prototribestin) and Na salt of 26–O-b glucopyranosyl- 22 a- hydroxy (25R) furost –5-ene- 3b,26- diol –3-O-a-rhamnopyranosyl–(1®2)-b-4-0-sulfo- glucopyranoside (prototribestin) have been isolated  from the aerial parts of T. terrestris L. growing  in Bulgaria. The structures of the new compounds were elucidated on the basis of 1D and 2D (DQF–COSY, TOCSY HSQC- TOCSY, HSQC, HMBC, ROESY) NMR data ESI mass spectra and chemical Information. (Kostova et al., 2002)

·        Various chromatographic techniques were used to separate chemical constituents. ESIMS IR12 HNMR13 CNMR and 1HMBC were used to determine the structure of the isolated constituents. Two new compounds were isolated from the fruits of T. terrestris and were identified as neohecogenin 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl (1®2)-b-D-gluopyranosyl (1®4) b-D galactopyranoside(I); neohecogenin-3-0-b-D-glucopyranosyl (1®4) b-D-galactopyranoside (II). (Xu. Ya-juan, et. al., 2001).

Pharmacology:

·        Studies on the histopathological  effects of 45 days sub lethal concentrations (1.2 mg/kg body wt of the animal) of Hg2Cl2 on  mice  revealed that  these  metallic  salts  are capable of  producing  severe damage  and changes  in  cellular levels of the  stomach leading  to death of the animal. The intoxicated mice again treated with T. terrestris extract of different solvent fractions promote the regeneration of its cellular histo-architecture of stomach tissue. (Kavita, et al., 2001)

·        This study concerns the effect of an aqueous extract of T. terrestris on the metabolism of oxalate in male rats fed sodium glycolate. Glycolate feeding  resulted in hyper-oxaluria as well as  increased activites  of  oxalate synthesizing  enzymes of the  liver  i.e glycolate oxidase (G AO),  glycolate dehydrogenoses (GAD) and lacatate  dehydrogenase (LDH) and  decreased kidney LDH  activity. T. terrestris  administration to Na glycolate fed rats produced a significant  increase in  urinary glyoxalate excretion as fed animals. The supplementation of T. terrestris with Na glycolate compared to Na glycolate also caused a reduction in liver GAO and GAD activities whereas liver LDH activity remained unaltered. The iso-enzyme pattern of kidney LDH revealed the normalization of kidney LDH by T. terrestris feeding was mainly due to an increase in the LDH 5 fraction. The LDH iso- enzyme remained unchanged in all the groups. (Sangeeta et al., 1994)

·        Immunological aspects of T. terrestris with respect to human male fertility have been discussed. (Stanisalavov et al., 2000)

·        T. terrestris is one of the constituents of dasamoola used in Srilanka. (Tissera et al., 1997)

·        Clinical laboratory data is available for the use of T. terrestris in relation human reproduction.(Mikolava et al., 2000)

·        Protodioscin is a photochemical agent derived from T. terrestris L.  plant,  which  has  been clinically proven to improve  sexual  desire and  enhance  erection  via  the conversion  of protodiscine to DHEA (DE- Hydro- Epi- androsterone). Preliminary observations suggest that T.terrestris L. grown on different soils does not consistently produce the active component protodioscin. (Adimoelja, 2000).  

Ethnobotany:

Medicine for asthma (fruit, Singh and Pandey, 1980); corns (Leaf, Singh, et, al., 1987); cough (fruit, Malhotra and Moorthy, 1973, Singh and Pandey, 1980, Sharma, et. al., 1979); diuretic (fruit, Gupta, et. al., 1981); impotence (whole plant, Shekhawat and Sunil 1984); leucorrhoea (fruit, Saxena and Vyas, 1983); liver complaint (fruit, Gopal and Shah, 1985); urinary complaint (fruit, Shah, et. al., 1981). Edible (fruit, Bhandari, 1974, Bhargava, 1959, King, 1969, vegetable (whole plant, Singh, 1945; Leaf, Singh and Singh, 1981.
