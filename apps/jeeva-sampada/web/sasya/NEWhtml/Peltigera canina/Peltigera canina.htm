Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Peltigera canina is a leafy lichen, forming circular patches of upto 10-cm across. Common on ground along with mosses, sometimes on rocks and at base of tree trunks in moist temperate regions of Western, Eastern Himalaya and at higher altitudes in south India. The taxon is widely distributed in Europe, North America, Japan and Nepal.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: The pale brown to brown thalli of the species are generally large, flexible, thin and irregularly lobed. The lobe margin may be wavy and recurved at apices. The apothecia are reddish brown, vertical and longitudinally reflexed and appear like that of the canine of a dog.
Economic potential 
During the middle ages lichens figured prominently in the herbals used by medical practitioners. According to Doctorine of signatures, Peltigera canina having canine like structure of fruiting bodies (vertical apothecia) was used for rabies.
A half ounce of powdered Peltigera canina mixed with two drachmas of black pepper. This mixture was taken on four consecutive days in a half pint of warm milk. Linnaeus named this particular lichen Lichen caninus, the Dog lichen.
Peltigera canina is eaten in India as a remedy for liver ailments and its high content of the amino acid methionine may be the basis for its alleged curative power.
Chemistry: Tenuiorin, unidentified fatty acids, 3-O--D-glucopyranosyl-D-mannitol, D-mannitol, ergosterol, aminoacids-alanine, arginine, glycine, leucine, methionine, phenylalanine, theonine, tyrosine and valine as free amino acids and isoleucine, lycine, serine and tryptophan as combined amino acids, additional amino acids are aspartic acid, glutamic acid, histidine, and unidentified substance.
