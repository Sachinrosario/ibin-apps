HABIT & HABITAT
A middle sized deciduous tree, attaining a height of about 45 ft., found in North-Western Himalayas at altitudes of 2,000-6,000 ft. Also found in the Northern parts of the Punjab, ascending the Himalaya, and extending into Kashmir, Afghanistan and Beluchistan. Also distributed in E. Asia - China, Japan& Mixed mountain forests to 1500 metres in China

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
  A middle sized deciduous tree, attaining a height of about 45 ft(30 m in warmer countries).   Old bark deeply cracked. Leaves alternate, elliptic to ovate-oblong, 5-15 cm long, 2.5-6 cm wide. Margin entire, apex acute to acuminate. Lamina dark green and glabrous above, paler with scattered hairs beneath. Small greenish flowers, not very showy, with a large calyx in June-July. Fruits 1-2 cm across, yellowish and bloomy, becoming bluish-black when completely ripe, globose or ovoid.  Hardy. The wood is greyish, close-grained and moderately hard.

Seed Germination :
Cultivation: The plant prefers light (sandy), medium (loamy) and heavy (clay) soils and requires well-drained soil. The plant prefers acid, neutral and basic (alkaline) soils. It can grow in semi-shade (light woodland) or no shade. It requires moist soil.
Requires a good deep loamy soil in sun or light shade. When being grown for its fruit, the tree should be given a warm, sheltered, sunny position[K]. It dislikes very acid or wet and poorly drained soils.

Dormant plants are very cold-hardy, but the young growth in spring, even on mature plants, is frost-tender and so it is best to grow the plants in a position sheltered from the early morning sun.

Dioecious, but the female tree can produce seedless fruits in the absence of a pollinator[1]. It is likely that unfertilized fruits are more astringent than fertilized fruits since this is the case with D. kaki.

Plants have a long tap root and are difficult to transplant. It is best to plant them out in their permanent position as soon as possible and to give protection over winter for the first year or two.

This species is sometimes cultivated for its edible fruit in Italy and E. Asia, there are some named varieties.

Propagation

Seed - best sown in a cold frame as soon as it is ripe. Stored seed requires a period of cold-stratification and should be sown as early in the year as possible. It usually germinates in 1 - 6 months at 15°c. Pot up the young seedlings as soon as they are large enough to handle into fairly deep pots and plant them out into their permanent positions in early summer. Give them some protection from winter cold for their first year or two outdoors.

Cuttings of half-ripe wood, July/August in a frame.

Layering in spring.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruits, Plant.

Uses

  • Fruits are sweetish and eaten fresh or dried. They are sometimes used in the preparation of sherbets. In France they are said to be consumed when half rotten.
  • The fruit is febrifuge. It is also used to promote secretions.
  • The seed is regarded in China as being sedative.

Chemistry
The fruit contains tannic acid, invert sugar (11.25%) and malic acid (0.38%). Exposure of unripe fruits to ethylene for 72 hrs. reduces the tannin content. The fruit contains about 1.9% protein, 0.2% fat, 47.7% carbohydrate, 1% ash.

Other Uses

  1. The wood is greyish, close-grained and moderately hard. It is nowhere abundant for being used as timber. Diospyrous lotus is used as root stock for grafting or ring budding kaki persimmon. Grafted trees are reported to remain productive for centuries.
