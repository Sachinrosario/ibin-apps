DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Bulbs 15-30 cm long, 5-10 cm in diam., narrowed into a neck, clothed with old leaf-sheaths. Leaves 20-30, thin, linear-lanceolate, 90-150 x 13-18 cm, apex shortly acuminate with a sheathing base, margins smooth. Scapes 10-16 cm long. Flowers 15-50 in umbel, white, fragrant at night; bracts 7.5-10.0 cm long; pedicels 0.6-2.5 cm long. Perianth tube cylindric, 7.5-10.0 cm long, slender, greenish-white; lobes nearly as long as tube, linear, recurved or revolute. Stamens 6, reddish. Capsules subglobose, 2.5-5.0 cm across, 1-2 seeded, beaked by fleshy base of perianth, dehiscing irregularly. Seeds large, rugose.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Large umbels ; Flower: White, fragrant at night, tube greenish, lobe revolute ; Perianth: Funnel or salver shaped tube, straight ;Androecium: Stamens on the throat of perianth, erect, spreading, anthers linear ; Gynoecium: Ovules many, style filiform, stigma minute; Fruit: Sub-globose 1-seeded; Seed: Few, large, roundish .

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Tuber is pungent, bitter; laxative, carminative, antipyretic, anthelmintic. It is used in strangury, biliousness, snake-bite, vomiting, urinary discharges, tumours, vaginal diseases and blood diseases (Ayurveda). Tuber is also used in bronchitis, chest and lungs diseases, gonorrhoea, night blindness, spleen diseases, toothache, bad smell of perspiration. Seeds are used in diseases of kidney and furunculosis (Unani) (Kirtikar and Basu, 1933).


Chemistry:
One C16-acyloxy alkaloid – palmilycorine – and an acylglucosyloxy derivative – lycoriside – isolated from fruits and characterized as 1-O-palmitoyllycorine and lycorine-1-O-(6’-O-palmitoyl-b-D-glucopyranoside) respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Two phenanthridone alkaloids – crinasiatine (fig. 2) and crinasiadine (fig. 1)– isolated from flowering bulbs. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Isolation of two new alkaloids – ungeremine (fig. 3a) and criasbetaine (fig. 3b)– from fruits and their structures determinatin and confirmation by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

A new schiff’s base – isocraugsodine (fig. 4)– isolated from fruits and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)d.

Synthesis of ungeremine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Procedure for purification and analysis of lycorine by high-performance liquid chromatography from two crinum species described a method that allows the simultaneous analysis of ascorbic acid and lycorine in tissue extracts of Crinum asiaticum by micellar electrokinetic chromatography. (Davey et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 267819r.

Hot water extracts of crinum asiaticum showed strong inhibition of calprotection – induced cytotoxicity in vitro. By purification studies it was identified that alkaloid lycorine was the active inhibitory molecule. Lycorine inhibits not only induction of MM46 cell death by calprotectin but also inhibits the suppressive effect of calprotectin on target DNA synthesis at half effective concentration of 0.1-0.5 g/ml. (Yui et al, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 75906m.

Alkaloid, criasiaticidine A (fig. 5) was isolated from the bulbs of Cassia asiaticum var, Japonicum, together with pratorimine, lycorine and 4’-hydroxy-7-methoxyflavan. The structure of the new alkaloid was found to be 4,5-etheno-9,10-dihydroxy-6-phenthridone by spectroscopic means. (Min et al, 2001). C.A. 2001, 135, 355312v.


Pharmacology:
Crinasiatine and crinasiadine exhibited bacteriostatic and tumor inhibiting activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)e.

C. asiaticum was serially extracted with petroleum ether, followed by chloroform and lastly, methanol. The chloroform and methanol extracts of the plant given orally (50 mg kg-1) caused significant (p < 0.05; n = 7) reduction in paw oedema but the petroleum ether extract did not induce significant effect (p > 0.05) on paw oedema. The chloroform fraction of methanol extract (CFME) treatment (50 mg kg(-1)) significantly reduced (p < 0.05; n = 7) the acute paw oedema. This may indicate that active anti-inflammatory compounds are present in the CFME. (Samud et al, 1999).

Crinum asiaticum var. japonicum (root) showed significant anti-HIV-1 activity (ED(50) = 12.5 microg/mL) with a favourable SI value of 16. (Min et al, 2001)
