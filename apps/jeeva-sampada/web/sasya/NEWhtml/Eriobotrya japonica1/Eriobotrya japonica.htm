Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT
 The loquat is an evergreen fruit tree grown extensively in the subtropical regions of the world. Many authors around the world believe that loquat originated in China .It has been reported that loquat is indigenous to south-eastern China . In fact various species of Eriobotrya are found in south-western China . The Dadu River Valley in China is now considered the centre of origin of the genus Eriobotrya in China and a large number of communities off japonica. Since some primitive types of E. japonica are found in Japan , some authors consider the origin to be both China and Japan . The loquat has long been grown in Japan and China and several important cultivars have originated in these two countries, particularly in Japan .
Besides China and Japan , loquat is commercially grown in the Mediterranean region Italy , Spain , Tunisia , Algeria , Egypt , Greece , Turkey , Israel , New Zealand , Australia , South Africa , Argentina , Brazil , Chile , Ecuador , California , Florida , Hawaii , India and also in Armenia , Azerbaijan , Georgia .
Botanical Gardens (now Horticultural Experiment and Training Centre), Saharanpur , Uttar Pradesh is said to be the first garden to grow it, from where it has been distributed all over the country. The commercial cultivation in India is mostly confined to Uttar Pradesh ( Saharanpur , Dehra Dun , Muzaffamagar, Meerut , Farrukhabad, Kanpur and Bareilly ), Delhi , Punjab ( Amritsar , Hoshiarpur and Gurdaspur) and Himachal Pradesh (Kangra). It is also grown to a limited extent in Assam , Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu (Nandi hills).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES  
  Small to large trees, leaves densely crowded at twig tips.
An evergreen tree growing to 9m by 5m at a medium rate. It is in leaf all year, The scented flowers are hermaphrodite (have both male and female organs) and are pollinated by Insects.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
 After fertilization, the loquat fruit develops very rapidly. The first indication of fruit enlargement is thickening of floral rim immediately above the carpel level. The whole toral region undergoes cell division and enlargement uniformly throughout. The sepals grow towards the centre and cover in 'hood' fashion the distal portion of the carpels. The sepal bases thicken and persist as permanent structures whereas the petals, stamens and styles dry up. The hood or cap enclosing the distal portion of the carpels can be excised from immature fruits and five carpels are exposed to view.
Breeding System :

Phenology :
Loquat bears flowers at the terminal end of current year's growth. The first flush of flowers can be noticed during August-September, however, due to non-viable pollen grains there is no fruit set. The second flush of flowering takes place during October- November and sets maximum fruits. Another flush of flowers can also be noticed during December-January but hot and desiccating winds in early summer have adverse influence and thereby reduce the fruit size. In two seasons,it was observed that flower bud formation started in early August, and peaked in November. Each bud developed into a panicle inflorescence with an average of 81.95 flowers Flowering started in November and December, and the number of open flowers peaked in December and January in the first and second season respectively. Fruit set started in the last week of December. Tanaka loquats had one reproductive flush. The average percentage final fruit set for both seasons was 5.05%.
Pollinators:
Apis dorsata was the main flower visitor. Other species of insects found occasionally included syrphids, houseflies, Myrmeleontidae, Bombinae and Pierisrapae.. Honey bees also pollinate the flowers.

Seed Biology :

Seed Germination:
Propagation by seed has been a traditional practice. After extraction from fruits theseeds should immediately be sown as exposure to heat and light tends to result in poor seed germination. Due to high cross-pollination, seedling trees should not be used for commercial cultivation. So it is propagated through Vegetative Cutting, Air-layering, Grafting, Budding, Rootstocks, Micro propagation.

Cultivation:
Prefers a fertile well-drained soil in full sun or light shade. Succeeds in any well-drained soil but dislikes too much lime . Prefers to be near the coast, tolerating maritime exposure but dislikes cold winds. Tolerates dry soils.

Succeeds in an open position in the milder areas of Britain but usually requires wall protection in most of this country. Plants are hardy to at least -5°c, and can survive to between -12 and -17°c but with some damage . Plants from different provenances differ widely in their hardiness, it is best to obtain stock as cuttings from plants that are established in this country. Trees usually only flower and produce fruit in Britain after a long hot summer, but a tree at Maidwell Hall in Northants fruits regularly. The main difficulty with producing a crop of fruit from this tree is that the plant flower in the winter and will not ripen fruit of a satisfactory standard if the winter is cold. Indeed, the fruit is rendered inedible by hard frosts in cold areas.

Often cultivated for its edible fruit in warm temperate regions, there are more than 800 cultivars in Japan . 'Advance' is a dwarf tree with very juicy fruits.

The flowers emit a most potent oriental perfume.

MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES

Pests:
Fruit flies, aphids and scale-insects have been reported attacking the loquat. None of these are, however, very severe in India . Bark-eating caterpillar is sometimes a common pest. This can be controlled by injecting kerosene oil or a mixture of equal parts of creosote and chloroform or petrol into the bored holes with the help of a syringe. Cotton or wool soaked in carbon disulphide or chloroform can also be used inside the holes to kill the caterpillars. It was suggested to spray O. 1 per cent parathion emulsion. Grapholitha molesta is a major pest in China . In Japan , Aprona japonica, Grapholitha molesta, Phalera flavescens and Rhynchites heros often damage loquat. Apart from preventive measures, the pests are mainly controlled by using pesticides.

Diseases:
In India , the common disease of loquat is collar rot caused by Diplodia natelensis. The fungus attacks the bark at collar region which turns brown, cracks and sometimes peels off. The entire tree may eventually be killed. For controlling this malady, the infected portion should be scraped off and painted with solution containing fytolan 225 g, lime 112 g, water 2.27 litres. Seasonal occurrence and control of 5 main diseases of loquat in Taiwan were briefly discussed., leaf spot (Pestalofiopsis eriobotryicola], pink disease {Erythriciumsalmomcolor), anthracnose [Collelotrichum gloeosporioides (Glomerella cingulata}}, white root rot (Rosellinia necatrix) and blotch (Clasterosporium erioboiryae). In both China and Japan , white root rot disease is often severe and it was  suggested drenching of soil with solution ofbavistin or benlate and then covering with transparent polythene for effective control.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Fruit, Seed, Leaves and Wood.

Uses

·        The fruits are generally used for dessert purposes as well as for making jam, jelly, preserves, juice, wine and canning. Chutney is also made from semi-ripe fruits.

·        The loquat jelly is an important product because the fruits are good source of acid and pectin.

Harvesting Practices
The fruits should be left on tree until they are fully mature and maximum amount of sugar is accumulated. Loquats reach maturity in about 150 days from opening of flowers. Change of skin colour to natural ripe colour is a useful indicator of harvest stage. Harvesting of immature loquat fruits should be discouraged. All fruits in a cluster usually mature uniformly and therefore, the entire cluster may be cut. However, in some cases where the fruits at the base ripen before those at terminal end of cluster, care is to be taken to harvest only mature fruits by clipping with stalk. As the fruits are easily damaged, they should be harvested and handled very carefully. Effect of harvest date on production and quality of loquat cv. Huabao was investigated in China by Hu. Fruit weight increased with harvest dates by 32.01, 18.14 and 12.21% for fruits sampled on 30 May compared with harvest on 15, 20 and 25 May respectively. The soluble solids content of fruits harvested on 30 May was increased by 6.94, 5.04 and 3.01%, respectively compared with sampling on 15, 20 and 25 May.

Chemistry
The fruit consists of pulp 60-70 per cent and seed 15-20 per cent. The edible parts of fruit contain 87.4 per cent water, 10.2 per cent carbohydrates, 0.7 per cent protein, 0.3 per cent fat, 0.5 per cent mineral, 0.9 per cent fibre, 0.03 per cent calcium, 0.02 per cent phosphorus and 0.7 mg iron/100 g. The carotenoids of loquat fruits are mainly responsible for flesh and skin colour which varied from yellowish white, yellow to deep orange.Total carotenoid values, especially carotene vary widely in fresh fruit peel and pulp and the content of carotene in yellow-orange fruit was 5-10 times higher than in the yellow-white fruit.

Other Uses

1.       Wood - hard, close grained. Used for rulers etc.

2.     Medicinal Uses: The loquat is one of the most popular cough remedies in the Far East , it is the ingredient of many patent medicines.The leaves are analgesic, antibacterial, antiemetic, antitussive, antiviral, astringent and expectorant. A decoction of the leaves or young shoots is used as an intestinal astringent and as a mouthwash in cases of thrush and also in the treatment of bronchitis, coughs, feverish colds etc.

3.     The leaves are harvested as required and can be used fresh or dried. The hairs should be removed from the leaves in order to prevent irritation of the throat.

4.     The flowers are expectorant.

5.     The fruit is slightly astringent, expectorant and sedative. It is used in allaying vomiting and thirst.
We have a more details factsheet on the history and medicinal use of this plant. Email webmaster@pfaf.org for details.

6.     Leaves of loquat are used in curing skin diseases and diabetes. An alcoholic extract has been shown to exhibit anti-inflammatory, antiviral and hypoglycaemic effects. Loquats are highly nectariferous, with a heavy fragrance and high honey potential.The plant is important for delicious fruits.

Economical Potential to the country

The loquat tree starts bearing fruit generally from the third year of planting. The yield increases as the tree grows older and the maximum yield is obtained when the tree is about 15 years old. The average yield of fruit is about 16-20 kg/tree. However, a healthy well-managed tree should give 30 to 40 kg fruits.

Miscellaneous Information

Cultivars

The loquat cultivars have been grouped on the basis of their origin and time of maturity, and these have been described below :

(a) Chinese group : The fruits are large, pyriform and deep orange coloured.
(b) Japanese group : The fruits are small, slender and light coloured. Golden Yellow Tree tall, upright to spreading, vigorous, crown oval and compact; leaves lanceolate, remotely toothed; fruits medium, oval to oblong, golden yellow in colour; pulp medium, pale orange, smooth, soft; taste mild, subacid with few seeds. Improved Golden YellowTree tall, spreading, vigorous, crown oval and open; leaves lanceolate to elliptic-lan- ceolate, remotely toothed; fruit large, oval to pyriform, orange-yellow in colour; pulp thick, orange colour, smooth, crisp; mild taste, subacid, moderately seedy.
Safeda:Tree medium to tall, upright, vigorous, crown rounded; leaves elliptic-lanceolate, serrate; fruits large, oblong-pyriform; pulp thick, creamy white, smooth, melting; taste excellent, subacid, moderately seeded.
Fire Ball:Tree tall, upright, moderately vigorous, crown semiglobose and compact; leaveselliptic-lanceolate, serrate; fruits small, oblong to ovate, saffron-yellow; pulp thick, smooth, crisp; taste mild, subacid and moderately seedy.
Improved Pale Yellow
Tree large, spreading, moderately vigorous, crown oval and open; leaves elliptic-
lanceolate, serrate; fruits medium, oblong-pyriform, pale yellow; pulp medium thick, creamy white, smooth; firm, taste subacid and moderately seedy.
Mammoth
Tree tall, upright, moderately vigorous, crown uneven, compact; leaves elliptic lanceolate, slightly serrated; fruits small, oblong-pyriform; pulp medium thick, pale orange, coarse, granular, pleasant taste, subacid, few seeds.
California Advance
Tree dwarf, upright, moderately vigorous, crown round and open; leaves oblanceolate, remotely toothed; fruits medium, oblong-pyriform, pale yellow; pulp thick, creamy white, smooth, melting; taste excellent, subacid, few seeds.
Tanaka
Tree dwarf to medium, upright, moderately vigorous, crown semiglobose and com-pact; leaves elliptic-lanceolate; fruit small, ovate, orange colour; pulp medium, yellow, coarse, firm; taste pleasant, subacid, moderately seedy. The cultivar was introduced to the United States prior to 1900, and later to other countries.
Nectar De Cristal
This cultivar was obtained by open pollination of the cultivar Togoshi in Japan and subsequent selection in Brazil (Anon., 1982); fruits medium, (30-40 g), yellow in colour with virtually no blotches; seeds medium, about 4 per fruit.

Parmogi
This was also obtained in Japan , by open pollination of Mogi and seedlings selection was carried out in Brazil (Anon., 1982); fruits medium (40 g) and orange in colour with few blotches; seeds medium size, and 4-5 per fruit.
Centenaria The cultivar is derived from Mizuho x Mogi; fruits large (60 g), attractive in appearance and uniform with little occurrence of purple spotting; seeds medium.

Vegetative Cutting
Cuttings of loquat treated with IBA rooted well under mist. the rate of survival was reported to be less than 50%. In Egypt , El-Shazly et al. (1994) recorded 65% rooting in cuttings ofcv. El-Soukari by treatment with 4000 ppm IBA.

Propagation:
Air-layering
Propagation of loquat through air-layering is easy. In this, 3-month-old shoots are ringed and layered. Treatment of ringed shoots with 3 per cent NAA or 2,500 ppm IBA has been recommended for improved rooting. Polat and Kaska (1992b) reported that the highest rooting success in air layers was 40% by IBA treatment. In China , many fanners successfully propagate loquat by air layering.

Grafting
Inarching is the simplest form of grafting and is, therefore, commonly practised. In the earlv years, top grafts, root grafts and cleft grafts were generally employed in Japan . The Chinese used cleft grafting in loquat on a large scale for centuries and this method is still used in Japan for topworking. An improved method called 'young stock cleft graft' was developed in China and used on large scale multiplication of loquat (Huang, 1978). Veneer grafting is commonly practised in Japan (Sato. 1996) and in some parts of China . Whip, tongue and bridge grafting have been practised in some loquat growing countries.

Budding
Shield budding, using buds from three month-old branches in January and February gave encouraging success, while poor success was observed during September- October. Ring budding was also used. In Turkey ,  chip, patch and T (shield) budding at 15-day intervals from 15 January-15 May. March was the most suitable time for budding with 95.48% bud-take. Topworking
A large percentage of loquat trees from seedling with poor fruit quality arc coinmonlv seen. These uneconomic trees can be converted to profitable ones by topworking with promising cultivars. After beheading the seedling trees, several methods, such as shield budding, cleft grafting or bark grafting could be employed. However, bark grafting was found to be more successful as compared with other methods.
Rootstocks
The promising loquat cultivars arc generally propagated on seedlings of commercial cultivars. Several other rootstocks, such as apple, pear, Mespiliis and Cyciuma have also been used in certain occasions. The quince has long been used as dwarfing stock in Europe , Japan , Australia , Israel , Egypt and California Besides controlling tree size. quince rootstocks are also tolerant to most of the pests and diseases and also tolerates heavy and wetter soils However, in plains ofUttar Pradcsh, quince had proved unsuccessful.

Micropropagation
Protocol for micropropagation using shoot-tip has been standardized (Yang et al., 1983; Chen et al., 1995). Successful micropropagation was achieved from axillary shoots derived from shoot-tip culture
The micropropagated plants derived from shoot-tip cultures have performed better than grafted plants and are used by growers in China and Japan for planting.

Physiological disorders:
The main physiological disorders of loquat are stated to be purple spot, cold injury, fire wind injury and fruit cracking. Takeuchi et al. (1997) reported shihan-sho, a disorder in loquat which caused the fruits to rum reddish brown. Mizuho Oobusa, Satomi and Nojimawase cultivars had a high incidence. Application of grey paper bags, black inside with a low light transmittance reduced the incidence of shihan-sho.

Climate:
Loquat is highly exacting in its climatic requirement. A milder subtropical climate with an average rainfall of 60 to 100 cm well-distributed throughout the year is best suited for its normal growth and fruiting. Since flowering takes place from October to late January, at certain places the crop may be destroyed by moderate winter frosts.
The plants are permanently injured when the temperature falls to zero and remains for any length of time. The fruit is most susceptible to frost injury when it just starts coloration. In places where summer sets in early and scorching hot winds begin to blow before the fruits ripen, the fruits remain very small and do not mature properly.
