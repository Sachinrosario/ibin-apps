REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Compound, corymbose racemes ; Flower: yellow, hermaphrodite, fascicled, complete, regular; Calyx: Sepals 6, with 2-3 apressed bracts, imbricate in two series; Corolla: petals 6, usually imbricate, in 2 series, usually with 2 basal glands inside, subentire ;Androecium: Stamens 6, free, anther-cells opening by recurved valves; Gynoecium: Ovary simple, ovules few, basal, style short, stigma sessile, discoid; Fruit: Berry oblong-ovoid, compressed, red; Seed:Seeds 2-5,with crustaceous fleshy testa .

Floral and seed biology:

Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by  wind.  
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 28.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Carotenoids -a- and b-carotenes- lutein, zeaxanthin (fig. 1), chrysanthemaxanthin, flavoxanthin, auroxanthin and caspanthin identified in berries. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Detection of tannin (5.56), carbohydrate (5.82), organic acids (3.69), manganese (7.20), pectic substances (0.48%), vitamin C, 156.60 mg in beries. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Analysis and identification of root, stem and leaf alkaloids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Synthesis of zeaxanthin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Cyanidin-3-glucoside, peonidine-3-glucoside and petunidin-3-glucoside and glycosides of cyanidin, pelargonidin, petunidin, peonidin and delphinidin containing glucose and rutinose identified in fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Isolation of Berlambine, lambertine, beramunine, berberine, berbamine, yzuziphine, columbamine, palmatine, and hydroxyacanthine from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-94).

The bases were isolated from the roots, young shoots and leaves of Berberis vulgaris. Individual bases were obtained by separating the mixture on a column of silica gel and were identified by direct comparison with authentic specimens and from their spectral characteristics, and the percentage amounts of the total and of the individual alkaloids were calculated on the weight of the dry plant. Berberine was main alkaloid in roots, oxyeanthine in young shoots, and thalicimidine in leaves. Other alkaloids identified were berbamunine, jatrorrhizine and isocorydine. (Khamidov et al, 1995). MAPA 1997, 9703, 1742.

The root bark and stem bark of Berberis vulgaris (16.2 and 6.0 weight percent of total alkaloids) yielded berberine, magnoflorine and jatrorrhizine as major quaternary alkaloids along with small amounts of palmitive and alkaloid BV1. (Slavika and Slavikova, 1995). MAPA, 1996, 9601, 0450.

The separation of ten plant extracts using automated multiple development thin-layer chromatography (AMD-TLC) is described. Alcoholic extracts were obtained from Berberis vulgaris. The separation was performed on silica plates (Sil G-50 UV 254 (Macherey-Nagel), 10 x 20 cm). AMD was achieved in 25 steps using methanol, ethyl acetate, toluene, 1,2-dichloroethane, 25% ammonia solution and anhydrous formic acid as modifiers. The chromatograms were evaluated with a Shimadzu CS-9000 dual –wavelength flying-spot scanner. Better separations were obtained using AMD than isocratic elution. (Gocan et al, 1996).

The detection and identification of isoquinoline alkaloids in crude methanolic extracts of Berberis vulgaris L. was carried out by a technique of capillary electrophoresis- mass spectrometery (CE-MS). For the CE separations ammonium formate buffer solutions (70 or 100mM, pH 3.0 or 4.0) containing 10% methanol or 20 – 60% acetonitrile as additives were used. The applied voltage was 25 kV, the thermostating temperature was kept constant at 25°C. Coupling with the mass spectrometer was performed via an atmospherical pressure ionization (API) interface and the electrospray ionization technique (ESI). As sheath liquid 5mM formic acid in acetonitrile at a flow rate of 3 microL/min was used. The spray voltage was 4.5 kV and the temperature of the heated capillary was chosen to be 200°C. Detection in the positive ionization mode resulted in mass spectra showing either the molecular ions [M]+ or the protonated molecular ions [M+H]+. (Strum and Stuppner, 1998).

A study on berburine derivatives of Berberis vulgaris, a series of compound bearing 9-O-acyl(4-6) and 9-O-benzoyl (fig. 2) substitutents was synthesized. (Wien Hong et al, 2000). MAPA 2000-05-3011.


Pharmacology:
The investigations of the chemical composition and immunological properties show that their activity is due to the alkaloid constituents. In the study the anti-inflammatory properties of total ethanol extract (TEE), three alkaloid fractions, a major alkaloid barberine and oxycanthine isolated from Berberis vulgaris root were compared. All these were applied in acute inflammation (carrageenan- and zymosan- induced paw odema), as the TEE showed the highest reducing effect. Their ability to alter invivo and in vitro complement activity was determined. Also, the TEE was most effective in a chronic inflammatory model of adjuvant arthritis. The protoberberine fractionjs Bv2, Bv3 and berberine suppressed a delayed type hypersensitivity (DTH). Reaction. Fraction Bv1 and berberine diminished antibody response against SRBC in vivo. The in vitro treatment of splenocytes with berberine showed that the anti-SRBC antibody synthesis was influenced in a different manner depending on the time course of its application. Oxycanthine was less effective than berberine in the tests used. (Ivanovaska and Philipov, 1996).

The antihistaminic and anticholinergic activity of aqueous extract of Berberis vulgaris fruits were investigated on isolated guinea-pig ileum. The results indicated antihistaminic and anticholinergic activity of extract that seems to be of the competitive type. The extract displayed the cumulative hstamine dose response curve towards higher concentrations. (Shamsa et al, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9904, 2477.

The protoberberine alkaloid berberine is isolated as a main alkaloid from the roots and bark of Berberis vulgaris. Berberuine strongly inhibited in vitro the proliferative response of mouse spleen cells to T-dependent mitogens concanavalin A (Con A) and phytochemagglutinin (PHA). Spleen cells obtained from berberine treated mice (10 mg/kg/3days) expressed enhanced proliferative response to both mitogens. Berberine was applied to mice at different intervals before or after the induction of arthritis (AIA) and Candida albicans (C. albicans) infection. The application of the alkaloid to new born mice (5 days after birth at a dose of 5mg/kg/3 days) did not change the course of AIA and C. Albicans infection. Its application at three 10 days intervals (5 mg/kg), starting from the 5 day after birth increased the joint inflammation in AIA. The host resistant to C. albicans infection was not affected, while the delayed type hypersensitivity (DTH)-reaction against the pathogen was enhanced. The alkaloid inhibited the development of AIA when applied after its onset (10 mg/kg from day +3 to +12 day). Berberine treatment during the ongoing infection did not influence its outcome (from +2 to +10 day). (Ivanovska et al, 1999).

Barberry (Berberis vulgaris) is a medicinal plant also used as food. The antihistaminic and anticholinergic activity of aqueous extract of barberry fruits were investigated on isolated guinea-pig ileum, and dose response curves of histamine and acetylcholine with and without extract were plotted. The pA2 values for antihistaminic activity of extract and dexchlorpheniramine were calculated (extract; pA2 +/- S.E.M. = 4.50 +/- 0.01 [-log C(g/1)]; dexachlorpheniramine; pA2 +/- S.E.M. = 9.36 +/- 0.14[-log C (M)] and compared with each other. The pA2 values for anticholinergic activity of extract and atropine were also calculated (extract, pA 2+/- S.E.M. = 4.37 +/- 0.03[-log C(g/1)]; atropine, pA 2+/- S.E.M. = 8.99 +/- 0.13[-log C(M)]) and compared. The results indicated antihistaminic and anticholinergic activity of extract that seems to be of the competitive type. (Shamsa et al, 1999).

Berberrubine derivatives, 9-lauroylberberrubine chloride was active against Gram-positive as well as the Gram-negative bacteria. Results suggested that the presence of lipophilic substituents of certain size and structures may be responsible for the antimicrobial activity. (Wien Hong et al, 2000). MAPA 2000-05-3011.
