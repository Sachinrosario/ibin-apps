 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, erect herb with a strong odour of turpentine. It is found in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Madhya pradesh and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.


Availability :  
Specimen examined:  Punjab: Reasi, Udhampur dist., 03.04.1987, Ajai Swami, 1083 (BSD); Uttar Pradesh: Mirzapur, 09.02.1961, U.C. Bhattacharyya, 13294 (BSD); Rajasthan: Lohargal, 10.03.1960, N.C. Nair, 955 (BSD).
Conservation/ Threat Status :
Plant grows wild.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem arising from woody base, branched, up to 1 m high, densely long villous, glandular. Leaves obovate, oblong or elliptic-oblong, 1.5-5.5 x 0.8-1.7 cm, acute or obtuse at apex, narrowed at base, lyrately lobed, entire or coarsely dentate along margin, glandular or densely hairy on both surfaces. Heads axillary, 5-7 mm in diam.,  on 5-15 mm long peduncle. Involucral bracts slightly longer than florets, linear, 2.0-6.5 mm long, acute, glandular hairy; inner bracts with scarious margins. Receptacles 2-3 mm in diam., alveolate, glabrous. Corolla of bisexual florest yellow, tubular, up to 5.5 mm long, 5-lobed; lobes triangular; corolla of female florets 3-4 mm long, 2-3-lobed, glabrous. Achenes brown, oblong, angular, terete, 0.5 mm long, sparsely hairy. Pappus white, 4 mm long.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Heads numerous, in short, axillary cymes and terminal spiciform panicles; Flower: Yellow,outer flower hermaphrodite, fertile; inner flower few, fertile, involucre ovoid, bracts imbricate, narrow, acute, herbaceous, the outer gradually smaller; Corolla: Corolla lobes of outer flower nearly glabrous,  filiform ,slender, those of inner flower regular, tubular, slender; Pappus white ;  Androecium:  Stamens 5, inserted on the corolla and alternating with the segments, filaments usually free above, anther bases obtuse, entire ;Gynoecium: Ovary 1-celled, ovules solitary erect, style arms of pistillate flower truncate, usually penicillate ; tips often connate, in the sterile flower; Fruit: Achenes not ribbed, small, oblong, sub 4-gonous.  Seed: Erect. Flowering & Fruiting:  January-April. Flowers are pollinated by insect. Seeds are dispersed by wind and birds.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Leaf juice is used as an anthelmintic, febrifuge, astringent and diuretic. The juice mixed with black pipper given in piles. Root mixed with black pipper is given in cholera.


Chemistry:
Campesterol isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

5-hydroxy-3, 6, 7, 3’, 4’ - pentamethoxyflavone, 5,3’, 4’ – trihydoxy - 3, 6, 7- trimethoxyflavone and an unidentified flavone isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Two new glycosides, the triterpenoid glycoside 19alpha-hydroxyurs-12-ene-24,28-dioate 3-O-beta-D-xylopyranoside ( fig. 1) (C37H37O10, m.p. 147°C) and the phenol glycoside 2-isoprenyl-5-isopropylphenol 4-O-beta-D-xylopyranoside ( fig. 2) (C19H28O6, m.p. 203°) have been isolated from the extract of whole plant of B. lacera and their structures were elucidated by means of spectral and chemical studies. (Agarwal et al, 1995). MAPA, 1995, 9504, 2437.


Pharmacology:
Essential oil showed analgesic, hypothermic and tranquillising activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine in burns, cuts, wounds (Leaf, Goel et al, 1984); bronchitis, fever (Whole plant, Shah et al, 1983); piles (Leaf, Tarafder, 1987; Singh, 1988); toe injury (Leaf, Srivasta and Varma, 1981); urine complaints (Leaf, Joshi et al, 1980); fish poison (Whole plant, Joshi, 1986)
