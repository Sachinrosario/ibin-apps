DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A medium size tree, upto 21m in height and 2.13 m in girth;
Bark light grey, smooth, shallowly fissured;
Leaves variable, obliquely ovate or oblong, 7.6-20.0 cm, entire to variously lobed;
Flowers unisexual, male in cylindric catkins, female in globose heads;
Fruits fleshy, red, shining.

The tree has been introduced into various places in India, from almost sea level upto an elevation of 1000m, having an annual rainfall of 80 cm and above, but has not flourished where the rainfall is less than 125 cm. Light, moist but well-drained, sandy-loam soils are the best suited. It is reasonably frost-hardy and useful as a soil binder.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling germination :
The tree regenerates profusely from seed and root suckers. It spreads rapidly and its power of coppiring is high.

Artificial regeneration is done from seeds, transplants, cuttings, root suckers and slumps. Direct sowing of seeds also gives satisfactory results. Air layering also gives promising results.

For transplants, seeds are sown directly. They take 15 days to germinate and the germination percentage ranges from 5 to 50. Artificial ripening of fruits with ethylene gas improves the germinative capacity and reduces the germination periods. About 18 g seed is sufficient to transplant a hectare at a spacing of 1.8 x1.8m. The plants take 3 years to establish themselves.

Since the spp. is dioeciously, it is necessary to ensure that sufficient no. of male trees also exist for regular fruiting and seedling.

The tree attains the pulp wood size in 7-10 years.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

Powdery mildew

Batocera rufomaculata - a borer
Application of kerosene or fuel - oil and p-dichlorobenzene or spraying a repellent during the oviposition is found useful.

Extensive damage is caused to the ripe fruit by monkeys, buds and rodents

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
Wood - sapwood is grayish white; the heartwood is light brown. The wood is light (wt. 350-450 kg/m2), soft, easy to work with an machines and with tools, perishable, moderately coarse-textured and straight grained). Wood has an average life of C23 months.

The tree bark yields a strong and lustrous fiber (length 6-15 mm; dia 25-35 um) which is of two types; thick fibers with small and pointed ends; ribbon like fibers with broad and rounded ends and exhibiting some twist.

Produce and Products:
Wood - plywood, cheap furniture, toys, shoe heels, cigar boxes, sports goods, packing-cases, building boards, pencils, match splint, ink.

Paper pulp - newsprint, writing, printing paper, viscose rayon.

Inner bark - fiber - for paper making in China and Japan - high grade leathery paper, umbrella covers, paper-lanterns, handkerchiefs, lens-paper, cosmetic tissues. Clothes for sleeping in are made from this paper (in Java). In Polynesia - bark cloth, tapa or kappa from the bark. In Burma - Burmex parabek or paper-slates. Seeds - tonic and diuretic. Leaves - for feeding silkworm, fodder; used in indigestion and as diaphoretic. Bark extract - against eczema.

Processing Techniques:
The wood is suitable for the production of mechanical pulp for newsprint. High yield (84%) of pulp can be obtained by improved cold caustic soda process with wood shavings.

Yield of bleached pulp can be obtained by sulphate process. An admixture with 25%, long - fiberd pulp of bamboo or safai grass (Eucaliopsis binata C.E. Hubbard) is necessary for commercial paper-making. Admixture of the pulp form the bark may be helpful in improving the tear of the paper.

Long fiber from bark is removed from the stem after boiling the latter. It is soaked in water for a few hours, scraped and boiled in wood ash to separate the fibers. The pulp is prepared by beating the fibers with wooden mallets.
