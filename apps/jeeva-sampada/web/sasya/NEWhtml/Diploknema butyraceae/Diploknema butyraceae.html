Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

The Indian butter tree is a medium sized light demanding evergreen tree species with some tolerance to frost. The height of the tree varies from 3 to 10 meters, depending upon the site quality. It has been successfully planted from 400 m to 1,700 m on poor stony soils.

The Chiuri tree is a medium size tree which is native of Nepal. Madhuca butyracea [Diploknema butyracea] is endemic to the Pithoragarh District of Kumaon hills in Uttar Pradesh. It has also been recorded from Andaman Islands where it is frequently found in semi-deciduous and evergreen forests. It is a fast growing tree found chiefly along the sides of ravines in hills and in shady valleys (1, 3, 6, 8, 13, 16, 17).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

The chiuri tree is a large deciduous tree, having a girth of 6-10 feet broad, with dark grey or brownish bark. The leaves are 8-14 inches long, 3.5-6 inches broad, crowded near the ends of branches, obovate, obovate-oblong or elliptic, obtuse or acute, entire, base acute, dark rather glossy green above and pubescent when young but becoming glabrous with age, clothed more or less densely with long adpressed pale hairs beneath, lateral nerves 13-18 pairs prominent beneath. (1, 6, 9, 16)

The flowers are white in colour with a sticky fragrance. 0.8-1.0 inch in diameter, crowded in fascicles chiefly just below the leaves and sometimes a few between the leaves. Pedicels 1.8-2.8 cm. long, curved downwards, rusty-tomentose. Corolla-tube 6 mm. Long, cylindric slightly dilated upwards, somewhat thickned but not fleshy with 8-12 lobes spreading at first but soon recurved. Stamens 30-40. Fruit is a berry, ellipsoid, green, 0.8-1.75 inches long, shining, enclosing 1-3 seeds. (1, 6, 16)

Phenology :

The earliest variety of Chiuri, tomyo flowers in September-October and ripens in April-May. This type is found mostly at higher elevations. In the lower river valleys, there is another variety of Chiuri. Known as langyo, flowers in October-November and ripens in June-July. Between these seasons, the wayo flowers in January-February and ripens in August-September whereas all-season variety- jayo flowers all year round and ripens from May to September. (1, 6, 12)

Seed Biology :

Seed is glossy brown, 0.7-0.8 inch long, with a whitish, almond-shaped kernel (1, 16).

Seedling Germination :

Seed germination is less than three weeks. Soaking seeds in water for 24 h increased germination. Seeds lost their viability within 2 to 3 weeks, so sowing immediately after collection is recommended. (6, 15)

Cultivation:

Diploknema (Aesandra) butyracea is a light-demanding species, with some tolerance to frost. Chiuri trees are both domesticated and grown naturally or wild. The people plant the trees on their own farms as well as in the forested areas. For chiuri domestication the seedlings are collected from nearby forests. Sometimes they also protect naturally growing seedlings. Chiuri tree is a good soil binding species. This is also one of the reasons for planting the trees in the farmlands.

Generally, the people collect about six to fifteen months old seedlings from the forest and plant them on farmlands. Protection of the seedlings is done by fencing individual seedlings up to the sapling stage with local materials. The seedlings are planted at a spacing of about seven meters from one plant to another. Sometimes, young saplings are kept upright by tying them with ropes to sticks. Mostly, chiuri trees are planted in terrace of the marginal lands and non-irrigated fields of the hilly slopes.

Vegetative propagation: The cuttings came from a natural population and were dipped in 500 ppm each of IBA + NAA solution for 24 h prior to planting. This resulted in 79.7% rooting after 75 days after planting in summer (6, 9, 10, 13)

Economic Potential and Uses

The seeds yield a butyraceous substance known as Phulwarwa or fulwa-butter. This ghee is used to cook vegetables and roti. It is used mostly as a substitute or as a an adulterant to ghee. It is also used as substitue for cocoa butter in choclate manufacture. It may be employed in the manufacture of soaps and candles and as a lamp oil. It burns without emitting smoke or unpleasent odour. In solid form, the oil is used for making "forma" (images made during religious ceremonies). Oilcake contains saponins and finds use as manure. The cake is also used as substitute for soap and fish poison. It is also used as worm killer and applied over lawns and golf greens for destroying the earthworms (1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 17).

Harvesting Practices

Harvesting Techniques: Chiuri tree is found on steep and difficult slopes. Climbing the tree is difficult and it is dangerous to harvest the Chiuri fruit. In many instances people fall from the tree and succumb to serious injury and sometime loss of life has also occurred. The producers place doko (traditional basket) on one shoulder and pick up the fruit with their other hand and place it into the doko . Normally, male producers climb the tree and women gather the fruit that has fallen down from the tree. Children also help in the gathering of the fruits. The yeild per tree ranges from minimum of 1 to 14 kg averaging about 5.25 kg per tree. Similarly, the yield of the fruit per hectare is estimated to be 100-800 kg. The number of tree per hectare is estimated to be 37-90 averaging about 40 (4, 9).

Processing Techniques

Traditional method: There are mainly two methods of extracting Chiuri seed oil: kol and chepuwa.

Kol method: The fruits of chiuri are about 2.5-5 cm long, glossy green, each producing 1-2 seeds. First, ripe chiuri fruits are harvested. Then the outer skin and flesh layer is peeled off. After drying in the sun, the seeds are crushed in a small foot-pumped mill. Next, the crushed seeds are steamed and put in a bamboo basket suspended between two logs. The logs are called kole. The logs compress the suspended bamboo basket, squeezing out seed oil from the basket. The collected oil and solidifies to become ghee. The oil content of the seed is about 55-60 per cent. One quintal of chiuri seed crushing require 5 man-days of labours.

Chepuwa method: The kernels are dried and then pounded with a food-driven rice-hulling beam. The pounded kernels are steamed for an hour in an earthen pot. The steaming is done as in the distillation of local liquor, where vapour is passed through the mashed grain. This steaming process is repeated, after which the steamed kernel are transferred to a tightly woven basket of split bamboo, with only two faces and a tapering lower end. The kernels are then pressed by two wooden horizontal beams fixed on a vertical wooden pole and tightened by a leather strip. At lower pointed end of the basket, a vessel, preferably a wooden one is placed to collect the oil. After the first extraction, the whole process of steaming and pressing is repeated in order to ensure complete extraction. The oil is solidified either in a hollow banana shoot on a leaf plate- or in a utensil. (1, 2, 4, 6, 9)


Modern method: Hydraulic Oil Press Machine

The hydraulic oil press is a manual machine designed by ITDG Nepal especially for expelling chiuri seed oil. It is made of mild steel frame, base and ram with 10-ton hydraulic jack to apply pressure. The seed container with pores is made of aluminium alloy. The jack, seed container and oil bowl is detachable and can be carried separately during transportation. The hydraulic press has been field tested in the Chepang community. The advantages observed include high oil yield, significant reduction in labour and less time consumed.

Operation

The pounded chiuri seed flour is put into the container and adjusted according to the position of the stationary ram and jack. As pressure is applied on the lever of the jack, the container is pushed forward until it comes in contact with the Ram. Additional pressure on the jack squeezes out the oil that comes out through the pores and collects in the bowl connected with a small outlet pipe. The jack is released after this process and the cake is separated. The operation is very simple and manageable. (11).

Other Uses

The oil from the seeds have antirheumatic virtues and have been used in skin disorders. After extracting the butter, the remaining cake is toxic and is used as fish poison. The butter is an excellent emollient for chapped hands, etc., during the winter months (6, 16, 17).

Economic potential to the country

Economic Potential To The Country

Aesandra [Diploknema] butyracea is a multipurpose tree species found in the valley regions of Pithoragarh District of the Kumaon subdivision of the Himalayas, in northern India. The tree is economically important as food, fodder, bee forage, a source of edible oil, medicine, fertilizer (seed cake), fish poison, pesticide, timber, detergent (from pressed seed cake) and in soil conservation.

No literature is available about the chiuri oil traded in India, while it is a popular oil among the Chepang community of Nepal. The price of the fruit is determined by the dryness of the fruit. Dried fruit fetches a higher price to a raw fruit. Raw fruit is black in colour and turns white when dried. Preharvest selling of the fruit to the traders is common. The producers sell the fruits at Rs 12-14 per 2.8 Kg. and the ghee is sold at about NRs 65 per kg. In most of the case the producers directly sell the products to the traders hence the traders do not have to pay the transportation from producing area to the collection centre. Traders bear the cost of the packaging which cost them about NRs 10 per bag. Normally the traders store the Chiuri for almost 2 months probably to remove excess moisture. The traders assure the quality of the Chiuri before buying. Large size and fleshy Chiuri fruit are considered good quality whereas off-colour fruit is considered inferior. Traders/middlemen are involved in marketing of dried chiuri with a profit of Rs 3-4 per pathi (Rs1.25 per kg). The average profit was estimated to be in the range of 10-12 per cent. (4, 12, 13).

Miscellaneous Information

Advantages of modern method of processing in comparison to indigenous practice

  • Fast expelling oil and saving time

  • Higher yield, indigenous practice (37 - 42%), Hydraulic oil press machine (74 – 78 %).

  • Good quality and hygienic oil without cake residues

  • Easy to operate reducing labour.

Precautions:

  • Do not apply pressure on the jack more than what is required.

  • After each process and during transportation, the oil/air knob has to be closed to prevent spillage of oil.

  • The machine has to be kept clean at all times and the seed container has to be cleaned/rinsed with hot water to prevent pores being blocked with solidified oil (ghee). It is better to keep the container indoors after use to avoid contamination.

  • Provide shade for the machine to safeguard it against direct sunlight and rainfall.

  • Keep the machine out of reach of children (11).
