HABIT & HABITAT
A robust, erect perennial with a stout stem upto 2m. in height, In Kashmir at an altitude between 2500-4000 m. Cultivated in Kashmir and neighboring regions like Lahul in Himachal Pradesh and Garhwal in Uttranchal at 2700-4000m; also found as a casual in irrigated areas.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves membranous basal ones very large, upper ones small, irregularly toothed, basal clasping the stems by two lopes; flowers bluish purple in axillary and terminal heads; fruits compressed, curved achenes. The roots are stout, upto 60 cm. long having a penetrating characteristic odour. It is brown with longitudinal ridges and rough reticulated surface. It is brittle, the fractured surface having a resinous appearance and dirty white colour.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 Roots – disinfectant, tonic, stomachic, carminative, stimulant, diuretic in cholera, cardiac complaints, bronchial asthma, particularly of vagotonic type; root oil-fumigant, aphrodisiac, atterative in chronic skin diseases in rheumatism.


Chemistry:
Roots yielded 22-dihydrostigmasterol. The roots of the Punjab variety gave costunolide, dehydrocostuslactone, costicacil, palmitic and linoleic acids, β-sitosterol and α-cyclocostunolide. The Kashmir variety gave in addition to costunolide, dehydrocostuslaclone and α-cyclocostunolide, alantolactone, β-cyclocostunolide and iso-olentolactone.  A novel terpenoid C14 ketone characterized as (E)-9—isopropyl – 6 – methyldeca-5, 9-dien –2- one has also been reported from root oil. The acidic fraction of essential oil from roots contained acetic acid, 3-methylbutyric acid, hexanoic acid, heptanoic acid, octanoic acid, 7-octenoic acid, 3-iso-propylpentanoic acid, 4-ethyloctanoic acid and α – amorphenic acid.  Leaves contain taraxasterol and its acetate, α-amyrin stearate, β-amyrin and lupeol palmitates and an alkaloid – saussurin. 

·        b-Sitosterol and elementaly g-monoclinic sulphur (S8) isolated from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Glucotropaeolin isolted from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

·        Isolation of b-sitosterol, its 3-O-b-D-glucoside, octacosanol and 1-triacontanol from stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        An indole alkaloid –n salvadoricine – isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

·        Two new glycosides – salvadoside and salvadoraside – isolated from stems and characterized as sodium 1-O-benzyl-b-D-glucoside-2-sulphate and 5, 5/-dimethoxylariciresinol-4, 4/-bis-O’-b-D-glucoside respectively; syringin, liriodendrin and sitosterol -3-O-glucoside also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

·        Kaempferol 3-alpha-L-rhamnosyl-7-beta   -xylopyranoside 1 alongwith Quercetin and Kaempferol from the 10% EtOH extract   of the leaves of Salvadora persica through centrifugal partition chromatography. (Kamil et al., 2000).

·        Salvadora persica, is a rich source of oil (40.0%) and is found to contain lauric acid (31.4%), myristic acid (38.7%), palmitic acid (10.4%), stearic acid (2.2%), oleic acid(4.8%), linoleic acid (1.2%), malvalic acid (7.8%) and sterculic acid (3.5%).  The fatty acids were detected by UV, FTIR, 1H NMR and characterized by derivatisation followed by GLC-techniques. (Hosamani and Pattanashettar, 2002).

Pharmacology:

·        Plant extract showed antibacterial, weak anti-inflammatory and mild hypoglycaemic activities. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        The antibacterial and antimycotic effect of an ethanolic extract of   Salvadora persica (miswak) was investigated and compared with the effect of   sodium hypochlorite. Various concentrations of sodium hypochlorite (NaClO) and   miswak extract were prepared, inoculated with Streptococcus faecalis and   Candida albicans, and incubated at 37 degree C for 7 days. The turbidity was determined daily using a spectrophotometer. Results showed that 5% and 1% NaClO and the miswak extract (200 mg/ml) had bacteriocidal and antimycotic effects.   Whereas a 50% concentration of miswak extract (100 mg/ml) had a bacteriocidal effect and a fungistatic affect for up to 48 h, at 25% the extract (50 mg/ml) showed no bacteriocidal or antimycotic properties. (Al-Samh and Al-Bagieh, 1996).

·        Sticks from Salvadora persica, the most common   source for meswak, were analyzed for their soluble and total content of   fluoride, calcium, phosphorus and silica. Results showed that the fluoride released from meswak soaked in water was negligible (<0.07 mug/ml).  Approximately 39% of the total fluoride in the sticks was in a form that could   be leached out. The leached calcium and phosphorus averaged 582 mg/ml and 34   g/ml, respectively, representing 19.6% and 26.4% of their total content in the sticks. There was a substantial amount of silica in the ashes of meswak. It   appears that meswak is probably not particularly active against caries through   its fluoride content, but it does act as a brush for removing dental plaque and polishing the teeth. (Hattab, 1997).

·        Decoction of Salvadora persica L. possessed significant protective action against ethanol and stress-induced ulcers. Confirmation of the antiulcer activity of Salvadora persica decoction using optical microscopy. The elements of gastric mucosa tended to be reestablished normally in treated rats. (Sanogo et al., 1999).

·        Effectiveness of antimicrobial activity of Arak chewing stick's aqueous extracts at various concentrations. The microbial inhibition was measured using blood agar and ditch plate method up to 48 hours. The effect may be due to the difference of their chemical composition and variability in their PH. (Almas, et al., 1999).

·        The effects of prolonged administration of a lyophilized stem decoction of Salvadora persica were evaluated in diet-induced rat hypercholesterolemia. S. persica decoction significantly lowered cholesterol and LDL plasma levels in rats, proving to be more active at 30 days of treatment. The systemic administration of Triton results in a rise in plasma cholesterol and triglyceride levels and decoction was inactive at 18 hr after treatment, whereas at 27 hr, it was able to reduce cholesterol and LDL plasma levels.  (Galati et al., 1999).

·        Study was to compare the prophylactic effects of the natural antioxidant from spinach on the hepatic oxidative stress and liver damage induced by lipopolysaccharide. (Ben et al., 2000).

·        The root and stem extracts contained chloride, sulphate, thiocyanate and nitrate in the following concentrations (w/w %) in   stem and root extracts, respectively: 6.84% and 4.64%, 20.1% and 19.85%, 0.38   and 0.28%, and 0.05% and 0.05%. Only the differences in chloride were statistically significant (p< 0.05). (Darout  et al., 2000).

·        Lyophilized decoction of Salvadora persica roots possesses a significant protective effect on ulceration induced by ethanol, indomethacin, cold resistant stress in rats. After treatment some changes were detected in profiles of various cytoplasm organelles of parital cells. Results showed that cells tend to recover a moderate secretory activity and it possesses anti-inflammatory activity. (Monforte et al., 2001).

·        The antiplasmodial activity of the different extracts of Salvadora persica (Salvadoraceae) against P. falciparum NF54 strain were found to be 0.6   mug/ml (stems) and 0.7 mug/ml (leaves). (Ali et al., 2002).

·        Bacterial plaque is solely responsible for the initiation and progression of periodontal diseases. There are different mechanical and chemical methods available for the maintenance of oral health through plaque control. Toothbrushes and miswak (chewing sticks) are widely used for the mechanical removal of plaque. Chlorhexidine gluconate (CHX) is one of the best-proven anti-plaque agents. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of CHX and miswak extract on healthy and periodontally involved human dentin. Sixteen human premolars recently extracted for orthodontic and periodontal reasons were used in the study. Teeth were free from caries, cervical restorations, or erosions. The dentin disc specimens were prepared and half of them were etched with 6% citric acid for 120 sec. Both etched and unetched were further treated with CHX and 50% miswak extract and prepared for Scanning Electron Microscopic (SEM) examination. It was concluded that CHX 0.2% and miswak extract 50% had a similar effect on dentin in the control group. Miswak extract removed more smear layer as compared to CHX. Further research is needed in vivo to compare the effects of CHX and miswak extract on periodontally involved teeth and teeth with dentinal hypersensitivity. (Almas, 2002).

·        This study investigated the toxic effects of an extract of Meswak from Salvadora persica for 30 days on the reproductive system of the mouse. The results showed that exposure to Meswak extract did not have much effect on female mouse fertility, although it caused a significant decrease in the relative weights of the ovary and an increase in uterine weights. Exposure of male mice to Meswak extract resulted in a 72% reduction in pregnancies in untreated females impregnated by test males. The relative weights of the testes and preputial glands were significantly increased and that of the seminal vesicles was significantly decreased in test males. The results indicate that Meswak has adverse effects on male and female reproductive system and fertility. (Darmani et al., 2003).

 

CLINICAL STUDIES:

·        Effect of aqueous extracts of miswak on healthy and periodontally involved human dentine with Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) in vitro. 25% aqueous extract of freshly prepared miswak solution was used for the study. Twelve human premolars teeth (6 healthy and 6 with periodontal disease) recently extracted for orthodontic and periodontal reasons were used. 24 SEM specimens were prepared and treated with miswak extract with different conditions e.g. soaking and burnishing with miswak extract. Soaking the healthy and periodontally diseased root dentine in miswak extract resulted in partial removal of smear layer and occlusion of dentinal tubules was observed in dentine specimens burnished with miswak solution. (Almas et al., 2001).
