Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

Moderate size tree with greyish brown rough bark exfoliating in narrow strips. Capable of growing on very poor sites like riverbeds and skeletal soils where very few species can grow. Occur throughout India in dry types of mixed forests on a variety of geological formations and soils. It is common throughout the sub-Himalayan tract from Punjab to Assam, ascending to an altitude of 1,200 m. It is quiet common in peninsular region, particularly in the drier parts. In Madhya Pradesh, it occurs in open grasslands and in teak forests; in Maharashtra and Gujrat, it is seen in dry open thorn forests. in Bihar, Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu, it occurs in dry mixed deciduous forests. Common associates of the plant are Acacia leucophloea, Albizia odoratissima Benth, Anogeissus latifolia Wall., Buchanania lanzan Spreng., Diospyros melanoxylon Roxb., Lagerstroemia parviflora Roxb., Terminalia chebula Retz., T. tomentosa Wight & Arn.

The species grow gregariously on alluvial beds along river courses as one of the first colonizers of such soils, along with sissoo (Dalbergia sissoo Roxb.). It grows best on porous alluvium and on loam or gravel with varying proportions of sand and clay; it also grows on black cotton soil. It also occurs in drier locations away from water courses mixed with other species characteristic of dry regions, where the rainfall varies from 50 cm to over 200 cm, the absolute maximum shade temperature from 40 to 480, and the absolute minimum from 1.0 to 12.80. Khair is frost-resistant and fire hardy. The tree is a strong light demander.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Moderate sized tree, attaining stem up to 150 cm girth with 6 m bole on good locations, though a small tree with 2-3 m bole and 5-15 cm girth on poor sites. Bark dark colored, rough, young shoots dark brown or purple, glabrous. Leaves bipinnate, main rachis pubescent, with glands between many of the pairs of pinnae and a large conspicuous gland at or near the middle of the petiole; stipular spines short, hooked, pinnae 10-30 pairs. Leaflets 30 -50 pairs, linear, subacute, sessile, often ciliate.

Three varieties, var. catechu with calyx, petals and rachis covered with spreading hairs, var. catechuoides with calyx and petals glabrous, rachis puberulous and var. sundra with calyx, petals and rachis glabrous

Flowers sessile, pale yellow or creamy in lax axillary cylindrical spikes. Corolla 2-3 times longer than calyx. Stamens numerous.

Pods stalked, straight, flat, dark brown, shiny, with triangular beak at the apex and narrowed at the base. 5-6 seeded. Seeds orbicular, dark brown, flat

Phenology:

The tree is leafless for a time during the hot season. Leaf shedding occurs during February and new leaves appear during April-May. Flowering commences in June and continues till July - August. Pods become full sized by September-October and are fully ripe by January.

Pollinators:

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling germination: The seeds germinate readily with moderate rain, the seedlings grow fast on loose soil free from weeds and protected from grazing.

Direct sowing, particularly line sowing, is recommended for raising khair plantations. Loose soil free from weeds, and abundant light help the crop. Thinning out of young plants and irrigation also stimulate growth of the plant.

Propagated from cuttings of half-ripened shoots. It can be also planted by stump planting. Requires loose soil and abundant light help the crop.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel: Stem and branches used as fuel wood. It is excellent for fuel and charcoal. Charcoal of good quality is prepared from the wood.

Wood: Sapwood distinct from heartwood, light yellowish-white. Heartwood reddish-brown to dark brown; somewhat lustrous. Wood hard to very hard, heavy to very heavy, average weight 1010 kg/m3 (63 lb/ft3) at 12 per cent moisture content; somewhat coarse and even-textured with straight to interlocked grain.

A diffuse porous wood. Growth rings distinct to indistinct. Pores medium-sized to large, fairly distinct to the eye, moderately numerous, mostly solitary or in short radial multiples of 2-3, often filled with white chalky deposits; vessel lines distinct on longitudinal surfaces. Soft tissues predominantly vasicentric, in thin light-coloured sheaths round the pores, occasionally forming eyelets. Rays moderately broad, distinct to the eye and rather prominent under the lens, fairly widely-spaced.

The wood is a fairly good source of timber. It is used for posts and agricultural implements. It is also used for making rice-pestles, oil- and sugarcane-crushers, bows, spear handles, ploughs, cold-crushers, harrow-teeth, boats, wells, furniture, combs, carts, bedpost legs, sword handles, rolling pins, hookah stems, flutes, tent pegs, etc. in the Kolar gold-fields it is used for side props of shafts and galleries. It is eminently suitable for tools and tool handles, particularly for mallet and plane bodies as a substitute for beech and boxwood; it is excellent for felloes, spokes and hubs of wheels.

It has a maximum hydration temperature comparable to that of fir and has been found suitable for the manufacture of portland cement-bonded woodwool boards.

Heartwood has foreign matter less than 2%, total ash less than 2%, acid insoluble ash less than 0.2%, alcohol soluble extractive more than 1% and water soluble extractive more than 3%.

Harvesting Practices:


Produce and Products:

Charcoal. Yields catechu or cutch (used in tanning), by digestion of the wood in hot water. In India, two types of catechu are marketed - katha (pale catechu) and cutch (dark catechu). With cutch, the true khaki cloth is dyed and shrunk.

A third product, called kheersal, which is a white powder or crystalline deposit of catechin.

Chemistry:

Processing Techniques:

The wood is used for extraction of cutch and katha which are obtained by boiling heartwood chips.

Other uses:

Tannin (katha) used in Indian medicine and widely used with betel leaves.

Leaves extensively used as fodder for goat and cattle

Tannin used as a dye. Leather produced is harsh and apt to contain yellow stains, so this does hold a high position in tanning industry. Cutch is used in the dyeing of ship sails and mail-bags.

Gum - Khair gum is of very good quality and is regarded as a good substitute for true gum arabic.

Lac can be grown on khair trees. It should be inoculated in July, as it does not possess significant vitality during late winter and the early summer months to bear a lac crop.

Miscellaneous Information

 
The tree can be severely damaged by porcupines which grows on the bark and in extreme cases, may girdle the trees.

Seedlings show mycorrhizal association. This helps the seedlings in successful transplantation and establishment on barren lands or under stressed conditions.


Plants are micro propagated.
