HABIT & HABITAT
Sub-Himalayan tract up to 1000 m and other hilly areas of Central, Eastern and Southern India. Cultivated in greater part of the country for its fruits and planted as an avenue tree. Nepal,  Bangladesh, Myanmar,  Malaysia.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Evergreen trees, 20-45 m tall, glabrous. Leaves lanceolate to oblong-lanceolate, 9-30 x 3-6.5 cm, apex acute-acuminate, margin entire, thinly coriaceous; petioles 1.5-6.0 cm long, glabrous. 
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   

Floral and seed biology:
Flowers in terminal or rarely axillary panicles, pubescent, 5 mm across; pedicels 1.5-3.0 mm long. Calyx 5-lobed; lobes 2 x 1 mm, tomentose. Petals elliptic, 3-5 x 1.2 mm, tips curved downward. Stamens 5. Drupes of variable size, oblong to subreniform, fleshy with sweet juice.  It flowers and fruits between January to June.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
 The unripe fruits are used in making chutneys, jellies, pickles, etc. The wood is used for making furniture, planks, ceiling, floor, boards, tea-chests, boxes etc. The fruit juice is considered as a restorative tonic and astringent and is used in diarrhoea. The powdered seeds are used as an astringent in bleeding of piles and in menorrhagia.
