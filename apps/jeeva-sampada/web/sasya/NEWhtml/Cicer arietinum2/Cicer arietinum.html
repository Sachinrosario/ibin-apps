DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flower: White-pinkish flowers usually borne singly ; Calyx:  Sepals 5, more or less united in tube; Corolla: Paplionaceous with unequal five petals and have bilateral symmetry ;Androecium: Stamens 10 arranged in single whorl in mature flower, diadelphous, anthers dithecous, introrse and dehisce longitudinally; Gynoecium:  Monocarpellary with superior ovary, ovules many, placentation marginal, style simple and stigma capitate; Fruit: Inflated pods with one-two seeds ; Seed: Wrinkled or smooth. Flowering & Fruiting : January-March.Flowers are pollinated by insects.  Seeds are dispersed by  wind.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant thrives best in deep aluvial soil.

Nutrient requirements: Application of farmyard manure and compost is suitable for crop.

Water management: Plant require very less irrigation except rainfall.                                      

Harvesting period / Method: Plant is harvested when it becomes mature after 4-5 months of sowing.

Chemistry:
Isoliquirtigenin, isoliquiritigenin-4¢-glucoside, 3,4¢,7-trihydroxyflavone, diadzein, pratensein, p-coumaric acid, garbenzol (fig. 1)and biochanin-7-glucoside from seedlings. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Biochanin A content in plant 8.0 mg/kg; formononetin also detected. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Vanillic and p-hydroxybenzoic acids from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Ononin, isoquercitrin, kaempferol-3-glucoside, astragalin, populnin, biochanin A-7-glucoside, isorhamnetin, protensein and garbanzol isolated from foliage. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

A butanoic acid lactone (Fig. 2)) isolated from leaves of water-stressed plant and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Theophylline-9-b-D-glucopyranosyl-6’-monophosphate isolated and identified. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Pongamic acid isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Ciceritol (fig. 3) isolated from cotyledons and galactopyranosyl-(1à6)-O-a-D-galactopyranosyl(1à2)-1D-4-O-methylchiroinositol isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

Pyrimidine neucleotides-uracil-5-b-D-fructofuranosyl-1’-monophosphate and uracil-5-b-D-ribofuranosyl-2’,3’-cyclic monophosphate – isolated from seeds and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Kaempferol-3-(malonyl)glucoside and kaempferol-3-(apiosyl-malonyl) glucoside identified in  leaves and stems. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

24-Epiclerosterol, 24a- and 24b-methyl-5a-cholest-8(14)-en-3b-ols, 24a-ethyl-5a-cholest-8(14)-en-3b-ol, 14a,24b-dimethyl-5a-cholest-9(11)-en-3b-ols, 14a-methyl-24a-ethyl-5a-cholest-9(11)-en-3b-ol and 14a-methyl-24b-ethyl-5a-cholesta-9(11),25-dien-3b-ol and b-sitosterol identified in seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
CIMAP:  The effect of storage of pigeon pea, greengram and chikpea (C. arietinum) for 12 mo on the biol. Quality of protein and the contents of lysine [56-87-1], methionine [63-68-3] tryptophan [73-22-3] was studied. The decrease in protein quality and in these 3 amino acids was low in the case of chickpeas (10 – 15%) and pigeonpea (10- 20%) and high with green gram protein (20- 30%). Insect infestation caused a further decrease in protein quality & lysine, methionine and tryptophan content1; Chickpea seedlings were raised in presence of toxic concns. of A13+. Phenol, ascorbic acid & proline were increased while RNA content and ascorbic acid oxidase activity decreased 2; Degradnlosses in tocopherol were highest during flaking & drying process (23- 31.4%), while losses were lowest (11.2 – 15.7 %) during cooking and drying and freeze- thaw drying methods. During storage, tocopherol degraded in all dhals but losses were highest in Bengal gram dhal and lowest in black gram dhal. After 10 mo of storage, the retention of tocopherol ranged from 28.2 to 53.2% and 37° C and 54.8 to 86.2 % at 0° C in various dhals, when stored in PEP packs3.
