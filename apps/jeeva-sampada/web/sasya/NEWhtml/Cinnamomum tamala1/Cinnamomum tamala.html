DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Small, evergreen, 7-8 m tall tree. Bark blackish, rough. Leaves opposite or  alternate, ovate or lanceolate, 12-20 x 5.0-7.5 cm, coriaceous, glabrous, shining above, glaucous beneath. Flowers yellowish, 7.5 mm long, axillary and terminal  panicles. Drupes ovoid, 13 mm long, fleshy, black in colour, supported by enlarged perianth tube.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Axillary stalked panicles; Flower: Pale yellowish, complete, hermaphrodite, trimerous, bracteate; Perianth: With oblong lobes and short tubes ; Androecium: Stamens 9, fertile; Gynoecium:  Ovary superior,  surrounded by cup shaped base, hypogynous, one chambered with single ovule, style slender; Fruit: Black succulent somewhat ovoid berry  sealed on somewhat enlarged perianth tube . Flowering & Fruiting : April-May. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The leaf is used in the treatment of anus and rectum, piles, heart troubles, ozoena, bad taste(Ayurveda). The leaf is tonic of brain, anthelmintic and useful in liver and spleen; in inflammation, sore eyes and used to stop salivation. The leaves also used in rheumatism, colic and diarrhoea. Decoction or powder suppres lochia after child birth(Yunani) (Kirtikar & Basu, 1933).


Chemistry:
Essential oil from leaves contained cinnamic aldehyde and eugenol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Linalool, cinnamaldehyde and two unidentified compounds isolated from leaf essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

3,4’,5,7-tetrahydroxyflavone, 3,3’,4’,5,7-pentahydroxyflavone, kaempferol-3-O-glucopyranoside, kaempferol-3-O-sophoroside, kaempferol-3,7-di-O-rhamnopyranoside and quercetin-3-O-rutinoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Linalool (54.66), a-pinene (9.67), p-cymene (6.43), b-pinene (4.45) and limonene (2.64%) determined in leaf oil of Nepalese plant by GC-MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

Determination of eugenol (37.0), 1,8-cineole (29.2), geraniol (8.7), a-terpineol (7.5) and terpinen-4-ol (7.3%) in wood oil of plant from Kampuchea. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

An essential oil (0.7%), obtained by hydrodistillation of fresh leaves of C. tamala was examined by gas chromatography. Fourteen oil components were identified which constituted 93.44% of the oil. Linalool was the main component (60.73%), whereas eugenol and cinamic aldehyde were detected in trace amounts. Other components of significant occurance (3%), a-pinene (10.54%)b-pinene (10.42%), limonene (3.21%) and camhene (3.06%). (Nath et al, 1994). MAPA, 1995, 9505-3270.

The gall  tissue in Cinnamomum tamala contain more sulphydryls. (Joshi and Tandon, 1994). C.A. 1996, 124, 82386w.


Pharmacology:

Screening of Cinnamomum tamala’s essential oil was done against ringworm fungi. (Yadav and Dubey, 1994). MAPA 1995, 9503-1640.

The gall tissue in C. tamala contained more sulfhydryls. (Joshi and Tondon, 1994). C.A. 1996, 124, 82386w.

The essential oils of Cinammomum tamala was screened for fungicidal activity against Fusarium moniliforme. (Baruah et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701-0217.

Ethanolic extract of Cinnamomum tamala leaves significantly lowered the plasma glucose levels in normoglycaemic and streptozotocin hyperglycaemic and antihypertriglyceridemic effects in streptozotocin-hyperglycaemic rats. (Sharma et al, 1996). MAPA 1996, 9604-2181.

Cinnamomum tamala with other palnt was tried in 20 patient of diabetes. Dose was determined on the basis of conditions of patients as 2caps TID to insulin dependent cases, 2 Caps BD to moderate cases and 1 Cap TID to mild diabetics; for at least three weeks. It is concluded that the drug provides a total beneficial therapy in all types of diabetes and tends to increase insulin secretion from islets of pancreas. (Bhagwan, 1997), MAPA 1997, 9703-1423.

The essential oils of Cinnamomum tamala exhibited absolute inhibition of mycelial growth on dermatophytes Viz., Trichophyton mentagrophytes and microsporum audouini. (Dubey et al, 1998). MAPA 1999, 9905-3177.

Formulation containing Cinamomum tamala with other plant shows anti-diabetic properties. (Dhaliwal, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 227764d.

The essential oil of Cinnamomum tamala was found exhibit antidermatophytic activity against two ringworm fungi viz., Microsporum audouinii and Trichophyton mentagrophytes. The ointment of essential oil prepared in polythene glycol showed pronounced efficacy as herbal antifungal agent in cure of induced dermatocyosis of guinea pigs. The LD50 of C. tamala oil was assessed for its oral toxicity onmice and was recorded to be 5.36 ml/kg weight. The essential oil was also characterized by its different physicochemical properties and GLC. (Yadav et al, 1999). MAPA 1999, 9905-3164.

Tejpat and its fractions containing chlorophyll showed pro-oxygenic activity and the catalytic action increased with concentration of chlorophyll in the fractions. On the other hand fractions freed of chlorophyll were devoid of pro-oxygenic activity. (Semwal et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 321771w.
