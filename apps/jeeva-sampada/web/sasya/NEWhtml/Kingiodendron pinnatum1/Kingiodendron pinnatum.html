HABIT & HABITAT:
Perennial, deciduous, much branched, drooping, unarmed tree, c 6.0-7.5 m tall. It is found in Western Ghats.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Branches cylindrical, glabrous, bark greyish. Leaves alternate, paired, sessile or subsessile, obliquely rhomboid or obovate-obtuse, 1.5-3.5 x 1.5-3.5 cm, rounded or shallowly lobed at apex, oblique at base, entire along margins, glabrous on both surfaces, coriacious; petiolules 4-6 mm long. Flowers in axillary and terminal panicled raceme, yellowish white or yellowish green; pedicels 1-2 mm long. Calyx broadly campanulate, less than 2 mm long, with one pair of adpressed minute bracteoles. Pods samaroid, linear oblong, 1.2-5.5 cm long, fleshy, with a ponted tip, sublignose, rigid. Seed 1, compressed.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  Balsam is used in the treatment of gonorrhoea and oleoresin used as dressing for sores of elephant.


Chemistry:
Sesquiterpene portion from oleoresin gave copaene (4.5), carophyllene (75.0), hmulene (1300, Carophyllene oxide (2.5), humulene oxide – II (0.5), b-caryophyllene alcohol (1.0) and unidentified compounds (3.0%); whereas from diterpenoid portion five new compounds were obtained – hardwickiicacid, mp. 106°C, kolavic cid, mp. 228°C methyl ester, bp. 179°C/ 0.4 mm., kolavenol, bp. 140°C/ 0.25 mm. And an alcohol, mp. 138°C. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Stereostructures of kolavenol determined; a norditerpene (I) possibly an artifact also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Absolute stereostructures of kolavenic, kolavic, kolavonic and kolavenolic acids established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Stereostructures of hardwickiic acid elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Seeds yielded 16.0% oil which contained vernolic acid (cis-12,13-epoxy-cis-9,10-octadecenoic acid) (7.1%).(Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).
