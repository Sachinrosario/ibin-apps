Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:

Small deciduous tree, belived to be a native of China. Occurs in temperate Himalayan region. Best suited for temperate climate but also grown under sub-tropical and plain conditions. In India, peaches are grown in Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, sub-montane tracts of Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and to a limited extent in the Nilgiris.

In the Himalayan region, elevations of 850-1700 m. are most suitable for its cultivation. In the Nilgiris, it is being grown at elevations of 1,300-1,750 m are most suitable for its cultivation. Tree can grow on a great range of soil types, the best soils are, however, well drained, light sandy, gravelly or rocky loams, which are fairly fertile. The soil nitrogen requirement of the peach tree is said to be higher than that of the other temperate fruite, except almond.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

8 m tall and 4-5 m spread but seedling tree may grow taller. Branches are shabby, mostly spreading and then going upright. Main stem is short but stout. Bark is dark brown in colour with splits, which peels off as new bark appears. Root system tap but adventitious roots also appear.

Leaves deep green, glossy on upper surface and rough on the lower surface, slightly leathery, lanceolate with toothed and wavy margins, 15X6 cm.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Flowers generally pink but may have white tinge also, axillary, waxy, appear in clusters.

Fruits are usually pink to peach colour when ripe, drupe, round to oval, pitted in the centre from both the ends with a typical suture. Epicarp is hairy, edible and thin. Pulp (mesocarp) is thick, cream to peach in colour, sweet, juicy, 1 to 1.5 cm thick, fibrous.

In the middle is a seed of any shape from round to conical with pitted or ridged surface. Seed coat is hard, light brown and 1.5X1 cm in size.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Germination & Propagation: In India, peach is generally propagated by ring budding or shield budding on seedling peach stock. Seedlings are raised from wild peach. Seeds are stratified, and then sown. Seedlings of normal size are budded or grafted with a suitable bud or scion.

Cuttings and rootstocks can be used for raising stock material.

Transplanting is done at a spacing of 5X5 m.

Other uses:

Fruits - Fruits are consumed fresh. They can be processed into jam, peach halves, nectar, etc. They are sliced and dried for use as wafers. Chiefly used for stewing.

Peach kernel oil - Yellow coloured oil with a strong flavour is obtained from peach kernel.

Shells - Peach pit shells on powdering form a soft grit useful in cleaning machine parts by blasting. On destructive distillation, they yield a high quality charcoal suitable for use in case hardening or heat treatment of steels by carburizing.

Volatile oil - Leaves yield a volatile oil upon distillation and the distillation water prepared from the leaves contain 0.04-0.14 per cent of hydrocyanic acid.

Medicinal - Infusion of leave or bark is given in coughs, especially in whooping cough. Flowers are said to be purgative and anthelmintic.

Root bark - Source of dye.

Gum - Tree exudes gum.
