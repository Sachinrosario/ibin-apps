Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A tall handsome, often buttressed tree. Chiefly in moist situations in evergreen forests. Does well in dry localities.found in the eastern Himalayas and Assam and in the western ghats from North Kanara southwards. It is also found in Andamans.

The tree is found sporadically, cheifly in the moist situations in evergreen forests, often towering over surrounding vegetation.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A tall handsome, often buttressed tree with a straight, cylindrical bole. Bark greyish brown, fairly smooth. Young parts densely covered with dense golden stellate pubescence. Leaves crowded about the ends of the branches, falling before the flowers appear, ovate, cordate, acute or shortly acuminate, glabrous on both the surfaces, 3-7 nerved; petioles terete, subglabrous; stipules minute, subulate.

 

Back to Top

 

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:

Flowers in subpanicled, few-flowered racemes from the old axils below the leaves; pedicels short; bracts ensiform, caducous. Calyx companulate, divided almost to the base; lobes 5 (rarely 6), valvate, thick and fleshy, linear-lanceolate, coated without with dense ferruginous stellate down, striate within with red and yellow veins.

Male flowers : Staminal column slender, three-fourths the length of the length of the lobes of the calyx, bearing 5 or 6 very small sterile ovaries at its apex, round which are arranged 4-5 phalanges of subsessile stamens; pollen smooth, yellow.

Female / hermaphrodite flowers : Ovaries 5, sessile; ovules numerous; styles very short, pilose, recurved; stigma 2-lobed. Staminodes or stamens round the base of the ovary. Follicles 5, subglobose, hard and woody, clothed with mealy pubescence without, corky within. Seeds about 40, oblong, compressed, furnished with a corky oblong-obovate wing; cotyledons not separable from the mealy albumen; radicle small, close to the hilum. Seeds with wings to 6 cm.

Floral and seed biology:

Seedling Germination: Natural regeneration through seed is quite common, the seedlings being capable of withstanding fairly dense shade.

Artificial reproduction may be done through direct sowing of freshly collected seeds or transplanting 20 cm. high nursery-raised seedlings during rainy season, giving a spacing of 2m X 2m.Stump planting has also been found to be successful. The rate of growth of the tree is fast.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood very good fuel. Calorific value : 5,160 cal., 9,290 Btu.

Wood: Sapwood and heartwood not distinct. Wood creamy-white to oat-meal coloured, often showing dirty grey colouration. Soft to very soft, often easily dented with a finger nail, very light. Specific gravity 0.21 – 0.34 air dry, 385-657 kg/ c m. Lustrous on radial surface when not discoloured. Straight grained, even and coarse textured.

Very perishable in exposed situations and in contact with the ground, if properly seasoned lasts fairly well under cover.

Wood chiefly used for tea boxes and other light packing cases. Also suitable for planking and plywood and for making light furniture, match-boxes and splints.

In Nepal, wood used for making drums.

Processing Techniques:

Easy to seasoning without much degrade. Logs should be converted absolutely green immediately after felling and open stacked under cover so as to prevent sap stain and discolouration. Kiln seasoning gives the best results.


Other uses:

Roasted seeds eaten in eastern parts of Assam and Burma. Reported to be used as cheap substitute for opium.

Dried seeds yield a fixed oil.
