HABIT & HABITAT
A pubescent herb found in open woods and waste places in the Deccan Peninsula, parts of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan. A genus of herbs and shrubs distributed in the Warmer parts of the world, chiefly in America. Nine species are found wild in India and one species has been cultivated in gardens for its fragrant flowers.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 An erect herb, covered with sticky hairs. Leaves cordate-ovate, shallowly 3-5 lobed, dentate or the lower ones entire; flowers axillary, pink or white, fragrant. The herb and the roots possess a musk-like odour.

Chemistry
Steam-distillation of dried roots yield (0.5%) a green coloured essential oil, with an offensive odour. It has the following characteristics: d30', 0.9345)D30 -l5ol2°'' nD30 1.4612 ; acid val., 310; and ester val., 31. The oil contains: isovaleric acid, 69; sovaleraldehyde, 3 ; aromadendrene, 8 ; a tricyclic esquiterpene pavonenc (C15H24,b.p. 124-25° / 10 mm.) 6 ; a-terpinene. 5 ; and azulcne, 4% ; a scsquiterpene alcohol, named pavonenol (C15.H24O, m.p. 52-55°) has been isolated. Removal of isovaleric acid, by treatment with saturated sodium carbonate solution and subsequent extraction with ether, gives an oil which possesses a pleasant aroma

Other Uses
The roots are reported to be used in Indian perfumery, and enter into the composition of a perfume known as Hina'. The roots are aromatic, and possess refrigerant, antipyretic, stomachic and astringent properties. They are used in dysentery and inflammation and hemorrhage of intestines 
The oil is suitable for soap packing.

The leaves of the plant have a disagreeable odour and a bitter taste. They contain vitamin C (80 mg./ 100 g.) and a small amount of a dark greenish brown essential oil. Leaves possess sudorific, emmenagogue yid anthelmindc properties. A decoction of leaves is good for rheumatism. Powdered root mixed with mustard oil is applied to the hair to destroy vermin.
