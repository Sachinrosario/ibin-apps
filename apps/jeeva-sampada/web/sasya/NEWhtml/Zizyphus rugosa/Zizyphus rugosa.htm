 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT: A large, evergreen, scrambling shrub or small tree, of the Sub- Himalalayan tract from Kumaon eastwards. Found in Uttar pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Western Peninsula of India, Myanmar and Srilanka.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES: 
A large shurb sometimes climbing; young branches clothed with fulvous tomentum. Leaves 5-12.5 cm. long, broadly elliptic, shortly acuminate, denticulate, glabrous above, petioles 6-12 mm. long, tomentose prickless from a broad base, solitary, short, recurved, tomentose except the tips. Flowers in long peduncled tomentose cymes arranged along usually leafless spinous branches forming a panicle; buds globose and as well as the peduncles and pedicels densely tomentose. Calyx pubescent outside; lobes ovate acute, the keel on the inner face reduced to a line. Disk 5- lobed. ovary 2 celled. Styles 2, connate below the middle. Drupe 6-8 mm. dia. globosse, or pyriform, white when ripe; stone thin, 1 celled, 1 seeded.

Chemistry:

Chemical analysis of the edible portion of the fruits are as follows in  grams: Moisture 55.3; protein 3.2; fat 1.3; fiber 4.9; other carbohydrates 33.3 and minerals 2.0/ 100g. Calcium 270 and phosphorus 94 mg/ 100g.

The crushed, defatted seeds yield starch 68.4 % on oven-dry basis.

The starch is as easily susceptible to acid hydrolysis as that obtained from sweet potato. It gelatinizes fairly well at comparatively low temperatures.

A new glycoside, zizyphoside(C52H 82 O 21. 3H2O; m. p.3150 has been isolated from the alcoholic extract of the plant.Betulic, oleanolic, alphitolic and 2 alfa- hydroxy-urolic acids have also been isolated.

Other Uses :
  1. The bark, powdered and mixed with ghee is applied to the swelling in the cheek caused by toothache, and is als given for ulsers in the mouth. In Bombay it is used as an astringent in diarrhoea.
  2. The flowers with an equal quantity of the petioles of betel leaf, and half as much lime, are givan in 4-grain pills twice a day for menorrhagia.
  3. The branches are used to make heaped- up fences.
  4. Wood reddish, moderately hard, readily attacked by insects; weight 45 lb per cubic foot, good as fuel. The calorific valu of sapwood-5019 cal.,9035 B.t.u.
