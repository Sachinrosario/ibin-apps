Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
Herbaceous perennial strictly aquatic, "obligate" (requiring a wet habitat) in freshwaters, entirely submersed, spreading at surface. It will grow in lakes, rivers, streams and ponds, oligotrophic to eutrophic in its original southern Africa range, it grows in high mountain streams and ponds. Lagarosiphon major is native in southern Africa there are about 15 other species of Lagarosiphon native in southern Africa, Madagascar and India New Zealand. Scattered throughout NI, more local in north and east SI. Originally from South Africa.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
It is an herbaceous perennial growing from bottom to surface in water to 18 feet (6.6 m) deep forming dense stands of stems in the water rooted submersed macrophyte; leafy submersed stems; stems to 20 ft. long; leaves alternate on the stems, blades recurved downward; tiny white (female) flowers reach the surface on long, thin tubes (pollination occurs on the water surface) . stems submersed; brittle; 3 mm (1/8 in.) in diameter; growing to 20 feet long; branching every 10-to-12 nodes; reaching the surface to spread into thick mats. Leaves submersed; greatly recurved; stiff; alternate spirally along the stem; leaves linear to linear-lanceolate; to 16 mm (1 in.) long by 2 mm (1/16 to 1/8 in.) wide; leaves 3-veined with visible midvein; leaf margins minutely toothed; at stem tips, leaves are very densely crowded. Flowers tiny, transparent to white or pinkish; all parts in 3's; in its native range, female flowers reach the surface on long thread-like tubes (to 10 in. long); on the surface they bump into and are pollinated by free-floating male flowers ; male flowers form in the leaf axils, after which they rise to the surface where they sail about; staminate spathes enclose many flower buds, carpellate spathes enclose only one flower. Fruit capsule is beaked; seeds 1/8 in. long, averaging nine to a fruit.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:


Breeding system and varietal improvement:
The flowers are borne singly in the axils of leaves on a long stem (called a hypanthia) and they float on the surface of the water. The flower has a sail composed of 3 staminodes (petals) and thy blow freely across the surface of the water. Pollination is achieved when a male and female flower encounter each other on the surface.

Cultivation:
It reproduces vegetatively by stem fragments. It prefers cooler waters; temperature tolerances: this plant is winter hardy; optimum temperature, 20-23o C (68-74oF); maximum temperature, 25o C (77o F), it is an efficient photosynthesizer and has a low light compensation point, facts which promote its weediness and has consequence of greater tolerance to pH stress.

Miscellaneous Information

 Lagarosiphon is a native of southern Africa. It causes serious weed problems in New Zealand and parts of Europe. Although confined mostly to aquaria in Australia, L. major is naturalised locally in Victoria. Lagarosiphon grows towards the water surface where it branches and forms thick weed mats at or just below the surface. These prevent light penetration which causes the death and exclusion of other plants.
In addition, Lagarosiphon can block water storages, inland waterways and irrigation systems. It is easily uprooted during storms and then deposited as rotting masses on banks and beaches. These, and the thick mats that form threaten angling and other sporting activities. In New Zealand, damage to hydro-electric installations has occurred when large mats have broken loose during storms and blocked intake structures. Lagarosiphon prefers cool conditions and high light levels. It grows well in water of low nutrient status on silty or sandy beds. It has been observed growing from a depth of 6.5m.

It does not grow in fast flowing water, although it will grow in canals, drainage ditches and slow-flowing rivers.
