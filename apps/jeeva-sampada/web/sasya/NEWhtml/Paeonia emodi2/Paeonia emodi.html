UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Gallotannin (7.0%) and glucogallin (3.6%) from petals. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Starch (9.5%), sucrose (5.4%), malic acid (0.47%), oxalic acid (0.36%), tartaric acid (0.34%) and benzoic acid present in roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Unsaponifiable lipid from root oil contained C14-33n-alkanes, b-amyrin, butyrospermol, cycloartenol,lupeol, 24-methylenecycloartanol, cholesterol, campesterol and sitosterol; saponifiable lipid contined octanoic, decanoic, lauric, myristic, myristoleic, palmitic, palmitoleic, stearic, oleic and linoleic aicds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

A novel beta-glucuronidase inhibiting triterpene (1) has been isolated from the chloroform fraction of Paeonia emodi beta-glucuronidase inhibit established ase chloroform fraction of Pa 11,beta,5alpha,23,24-pentahydroxy-30-norolean-12,20(29)-d ien-28-oic acid on the basis of spectroscopic analysis, including high resolution mass spectroscopy, one and two-dimensional NMR techniques. Oleanolic acid, betulinic acid, ethyl gallate, methyl grevillate and 1,5-dihydroxy-3-methylanthraquinone are reported. (Nawaz et al, 2000).


Pharmacology:
Hot aqueous extract of plant had direct depressant effect on frog myocardium and direct spasmodic effect on guinea pig and rabbit ileum; cold aqueous extract reported to be devoid of any activity. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for blood purification (Root, Uniyal and Chauhan, 1973; Gaur et al, 1983); cuts ulcers, wounds (Root, Uniyal, 1968; Uniyal and Chauhan, 1973); Dysentery (Leaf, Stem, Uniyal, 1968); Colic, convulsion, dropsy, epilepsy, hysteria (Root, Gaur et al, 1983); Mental disorder (Root, Uniyal and Chauhan, 1973); rheumatism (Root, Gaur et al, 1980); Urinary disorder (Root, Gaur et al, 1983); uterine disease (Root, Balodi, 1988); Edible (Stem, Leaf, Uniyal, 1968; Maheshwari et al, 1981; Gupta, 1962;Fruit, Balodi, 1988).
