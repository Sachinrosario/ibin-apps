DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Rhizome  bearing oblong tubers, c 5 cm long. Leaves oblong or ovate-lanceolate, 30-45 x 11-12 cm, acuminate at apex, usually variegated with darker and lighter green above and tinged purple beneath.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Flowers 4-6 appearing before the leaves, sessile, fragrant. Corolla tube 5-7.5 cm long with spreading linear petals nearly as long as the tube. Staminodes oblong acute, 3.8-5.0 cm long, white. Lip lilac or redish, rather shorter, 2-fid, segments suborbicular.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The whole plant, in the form of powder is used in the form of an ointment, has wonderful efficacy in healing fresh wounds. It removes coagulated blood or purulent matter that may be within the body. The root is a useful medicine in anasarcous swellings.


Chemistry:
A compound, mp. 149°C, from tubers which, yielded benzoic acid on hydrolysis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Crotepoxide isolated from tubers. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

Bioassay-guided isolation afforded two phenyl-butanoides from rhizomes of Z. cassumanar which had LC50 value against neonate larvae of 121 and 127 ppm respectively in the chronic feeding bioassay. The presence of oxygenated substituent (-OH or OAc group) in the side chain nullified insecticidal activity. Rhizome of Kaempferia rotunda yielded two active metabolite benzyl benzoate and cyclohexane derivative crotepoxide compared to bioactive phenyl butanoids benzyl benzoate act as an insecticidal activity. (Nugroho et al, 1996). C.A. 1996, 124, 82141n.

Three new cyclohexane diepoxide (I fig. 1, II fig. 2, III fig. 3). Together with crotepoxide and (-)-zeylenal were isolated from the rhizome of Kaempferia rotunda. Their structure were characterized by spectral analysis and chemical correlations. (Pancharoen et al, 1996).
