HABIT & HABITAT
Perennial herb, creeping, twining or prostrate, it is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh. More or less throughout the temperate and subtropical regions of the World.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial herb, creeping, twining or prostrate. Leaves linear or ovate-lanceolate to oblong, 1.5-8.0 x 0.4-4.5 cm, acute at apex, repand, hastate or sagittate at base. Flowers in axillary, 1-5 or 1-3 flowered cymose. Sepals 4-5 mm long. Corolla glabrous, lilac with dark purple throat. Capsules globose, 4-seeded. Seeds tuberculate, brown.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flower: Solitary, pedicillate, complete, hermaphrodite; Calyx: Sepals 5, subequal, broadly elliptic, obtuse or truncate, glabrous; Corolla: Broadly infundibuliform, pink or nearly white, glabrous;Androecium: Stamens 5, attached near the base of corolla, included, filiform often subequal ; Gynoecium: Ovary 2-celled, ovules 4, style filiform, stigma two; Fruit: Globose, glabrous capsule; Seed: Sub-trigonous, dark reddish brown.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The root possesses cathartic properties. It is used as a substitute for jalap. Roots are mild purgative.


Chemistry:
·        b-methylesculetin detected in all parts of plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

·        a-amyrin, campesterol, stigmasterol, b-sitosterol, n-alkanes and n-alkanols isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

·        Umbelliferone and scopoletin isolated; presence of isoferulic acid confirmed. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984).

Pharmacology:

·         Convolvulus arvensis was found to contain the tropane alkaloids tropine, pseudotropine, and tropinone and the pyrrolidine alkaloids cuscohygrine and hygrine. Laboratory mice readily ate C. arvensis and exhibited a variety of abnormal clinical signs depending on the amount eaten. Similar alkaloids have been found in other Convolvulus species and cuscohygrine and calystegines (polyhydroxytropanes) have been previously reported from C. arvensis roots. (Todd et al., 1995)

·         The alkaloid extract from roots of naturally growing Convolvulus arvensis, purified by ion-exchange chromatography, showed significant inhibitory activity toward beta-glucosidase and alpha-galactosidase. (Molyneux et al., 1993)

TOXICOLOGY:  

  • The effects of feeding high and low doses of field bindweed (Convolvulus arvensis) to mice were investigated. Bindweed contains several alkaloids, including pseudotropine, and lesser amounts of tropine, tropinone, and meso-cuscohygrine. Mice fed bindweed exclusively died or were euthanized after 4-7 d and had severe hepatic necrosis and gastritis with ulceration or erosions. Mice fed low doses of bindweed along with standard laboratory mouse diet for 6 or 8 w had no clinical disease or gross lesions on necropsy examination but did have histologic lesions of mild multifocal hepatitis and gastritis. (Schultheiss et al., 1995)

 

Ethnobotany:
Medicine for purgative (Root, Das et al, 1983; Sharma et al, 1979); Detergent (Whole plant, Koelz, 1979)
