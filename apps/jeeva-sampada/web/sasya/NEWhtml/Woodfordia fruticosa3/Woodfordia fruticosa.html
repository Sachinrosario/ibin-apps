 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT
commonly occurring throughout North India, ascending to an altitude of c. 1,500 m. in the Himalayas, but rather scarce in South India. It is sometimes cultivated in gardens for its flowers, which are borne during the summer months.

This herb is a large shrub found throughout India. Has bright red flowers.


Availability :  
Commonly found in the deciduous forests, particularly in Sub-Himalyan tracts, Siwalicks and North eastern region of India.  Found in South East Asia, Indo-china, Nepal, China, Butan, Srilanka.

Status : Commonly found in waste lands & walls of old buildings.
It is listed under Low risk category of the Red list of FAO

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES

A much-branched, beautiful shrub, with fluted stems and long, spreading branches, 1-3 m. high, rarely up to 7 m.,  Bark reddish brown, peeling off in thin, fibrous strips; leaves lanceolate, oblong-lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate; flowers numerous, brilliant red in dense axillary paniculate-cymose clusters; capsules ellipsoid, membranous; seeds brown, minute, smooth obovate.

 Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Flowers, twigs, leaves, bark, wood

Use

The leaves and flowers have a high tannin content and are used for tanning and in ayurvedic medicine for bleeding conditions such as haemorrhoids

  • Bark contains 20-27% tannins, used in the tanning  of   
     crust-leathers.
  • The flowers are employed through out India in dyeing either to produce a colour of themselves, or as an adjunct or mordant, principally with Morinda citrifolia.  
  • The flowers are also used to small extent for tanning in certain parts of India, but appear to have been neglected for this purpose.  the flower tannin may also prove useful in heavy leather tannage by suitable blending with catechol type of tannin.
  • Fruits are also rich in tannin.
  • Leaves contain 12-20 percent tannin and can possibly be exploited for the production of tan-extracts.
  • The bark contains 20-27 percent tannin and has been extensively used in tannin of crust-leathers.  The tannin penetrates and imparts a fine pale-cream colour to the leather.  It approaches in its tanning properties the bark of Cassia auriculata and can be used either directly or as an extract. 
  • The leaves and twigs yield a yellow dye called nauti, which is used occasionally Northern India in calico printing.  Leaves are also one among the most commonly employed in Indian Tanning materials.  
  • Gum of this plant know as dhaura or dhau-ka-gond is collected largely in Harauti and Mewar.  It is said to resemble gum tragacanth and to swell in water.  It is employed in dyeing to coat the parts of fabric which are required to remain uncoloured. 


Harvesting Practices

The plant starts flowering from February and during which period the blossoms are gathered and dried and in districts where the leaves are used as a tan, these are collected and dried in autumn.  

Produce and Products
Dye, Gum, Tannin

Chemistry
Octacosonol and ß-sitosterol have been isolated from its stem. High proportion of ellagic acid and polyphenols were detected in leaves and flowers.

The leaves also contain lawsone(2-hydroxy-1:4-napthoquinone), the dyeing principle of henna.

Back to Top



Processing Techniques
The flowers are either boiled in water, or else steeped for a considerable time in cold water.  To the solution thus prepared, alum or lime and alum, is added as a mordant, and the material to be dyed is immersed in this solution several times until a pink colour of the required depth is obtained.

Other Uses
  1. Leaves are reported to have antibiotic activity
  2. Wood  used as fuel and in making  axe-handles.
  3. Leaves have antibiotic activity invitro.  It is used against biliousness, headache and fever.
  4. Externally, flower powder-ulcers, wounds; decoction as a lotion
  5. The flowers are useful in dysentery, diarrhoea, leucorrhoea and menorrhagia. Powdered flower is sprinkled over foul ulcers and wounds to enhance healing. Flowers of the plant are added to almost every formulation of asava or arishta. Dried flowers are credited with stimulant and astringent properties and used to treat bowel complaints, hemorrhages and  is also  administered in menorrhagia and seminal weakness and  antipyretic.
