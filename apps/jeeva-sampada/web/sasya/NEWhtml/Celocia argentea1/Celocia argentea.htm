Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT:
An erect, glabrous annual, up to 1m in height, commonly found on the river banks and open places as a weed throughout India, occasionally ascending up to an altitude of 1500m in the Himalayas.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Stem stout or slender, simple or branched; leaves narrow, entire; flowers white, glistening in feathered, conical to long oblong spikes; capsules with 4-8 black, shining seeds.

Chemistry
Analysis of the leaves gave: moisture-88.0; protein-2.0; fat-0.7; carbohydrates-5.8; fibre-1.5; and minerals-2.0 g/100g; calcium-323; phosphorous-38 mg/100g; and energy-38 Kcal/100g. The leaves are rich in vitamin B1 and B6. The vitamin content of the green leaves (dry basis): vitamin B1-29; B2-5.4; and B6-18.2 and naicin-7.7 mg/100g. The fresh leaves are rich in Vit. C (553 mg/100g on dry basis) presence of hydrocyanic acid (2 mg/100g) and high content of oxalic acid (11.3%) is also reported.

Other Uses

1.The aqueous root extract and root exudates have inhibitory effect upon the radical growth of germinating bajra seedlings. The chloroform extract of the whole plant was found to inhibit the growth of E. coli Castell.. & Chalm. and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Shroeter) Mig.,; whereas, methanol extarct inhibited the growth of Micrococcus pyogenes Lehman & Neuman var. aureus Hucker syn. Staphylococcus aureus Rosenb. The aqueous extract of leaves and roots was found to inhibit the growth of Rhizobium sp. An ethanolic extract extarct (50%) of the flowers and seeds was reported to inhibit the growth of several bacteria.

2.Folk Medicine: The dried plant is considered antiscorbutic and a cooling agent in China and is employed in Sudan to cure wounds. The paste of the leaves is applied on scorpion sting. The flowers are useful in blood-dysentry, spitting of blood and menorrhagia. The plant contains a steroidal sapogenin.
