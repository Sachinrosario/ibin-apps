Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large deciduous tree occurs widely in Sub- Himalayan tract from Punjab to Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Sikkim, West Bengal and southwards in Madhya Pradesh, Bihar and Orissa, also introduced as an ornamental tree in parts of Maharashtra, attains a height upto 37 m and a girth of about 3.7 m, but the girth and height varies with locality. It is supposed to be a fire resistant and colonize tree.. Tree is capable of standing fire as well as it can withstand frost.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

The bark is rough, dark brown with conspicuous vertical furrow. Axillary bids are produced at the axils of the cotyledones, when the main shoot is damaged or withers, the axillary bids grow as shoot.

Floral and seed biology:

Seed Biology:
Seeds are produced every year but there is tendency for heavy seed production once in three years.
Seed Germination & Propagation:
Seeds are very fertile but the variability is very short, it never germinates two weeks after its collection. During germination radical grows very fast whereas the plemule remains for a long time within the cotyledonary sheath.


MAJOR PEST AND DISEASES:

It is attacked by Lymontria mathura in Assam and north India whereas in Bengal it is attacked by Maurillia iconica both cause defoliation. When stored in sun it is attacked by borer beetles in U. P. and M. P. Excessive exudation of resin is considered as a symptom of borer attack (Holocerambyx spinicornis). The insects attack both sapwood and heartwood. Xylothrips flavipes and Xylodectus ornatus usually attack sapwood. Major damage to both sapwood and heartwood is done by Hoplocerambyx spinicornis and Aeolesthes holosericea. Among the other borers, which cause damage to both sapwood and heartwood, belong to Ceramycidae, Curculiondae, Anthribidae, Eucnemidae, Scolytidae, Platypodidae and others. It also subjected to action of marine boring organisms. Insects of the families Termitidae, Arbelidae and Coccidae damage bark. Wood is also attacked by powder post beetles or ghoon (Bostrychidae) and sapwood & heartwood are attacked by ambrossia beetles.
The common fungi attacking the wood are Fomes lividus, F. lignosus, Polystictus steirheilianus, Tremates cubensis, Stereum hirsutum and Polyporus ostreoformis. Fire, frost and suppression working individually or collectively are the factors predispose Shorea robusta to attack by Fomes carryophylli (popularly known as 'Gauj'), while with H. rubiginosa, fire is the principal contributing factor. Many of the fungi cause decay of heartwood, these fungi falls into two groups, in one lignin is destroyed by them and in the other cellulose is mainly destroyed.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Fuel:
Sal is very good for fuel. The calorific value of the wood is as Sapwood- 5095 calories, 9173 BTU, Heartwood- 5433 calories, 9779 BTU.
Wood:
Heaviest wood as compared to other Shorea spp. average wood weight is about 880 kg per cu. m. at 12% moisture content. Tyloses are well developed in the wood. Sapwood is distinct from heartwood, inner sapwood also differs from outer sapwood having a reddish colour similar to that of true heartwood. Fresh sapwood is pale white with a brownish tinge, if not protected, it turns black due to fungal attack. Heartwood is light brown to brown, it turns reddish brown on exposure. Heartwood is supposed to be more valuable commercially as compared to sapwood. Wood is very durable ranges from 227- 423 months depending upon locality.
Wood is suitable and commonly used for fireboard, bridges, beams, cooperage, fence posts, motor lorry and bus bodies, railway sleepers, tent poles and pegs, boat construction, furniture works and other carpentry works, carbon papers, varnishes and paints, rafters, floors, piles, wagon building, dug-out boats, ladders, carts, spokes, hubs of wheal, tool handles, picker arms, dyeing vats, beer and oil casks, ship building industry and harbour works, and for marine piles.

Other uses:

Stem is the source of aromatic gum called Shal- dammar, Rhal or Dhooma.
Pounded fruits are used in antidiarrheal medicine.
Seeds are rich in fats and starch, yields sal-butter that contains 18- 20% fat and used for cooking, lighting and for adulterating ghee.
Bark is used for tanning as having 9 - 10 % tannins, or making tan extracts, a brown dye is extracted from the bark. Bark yields a black dye and resin- dammar used in typewriter ribbons and carbon paper. The bast fibre is used to make ropes. The bark is also useful as fuel.
Young twigs and leaves have about 22% tannins.
After extraction of tannin, the by-product has been found to yield 45% cellulose.
Tree yields natural resin and it is frequently burned as incense in religious ceremonies, cremation rites and as mosquito repellent. The resin is used for caulking the boats, it is also useful in paints, used as astringent. From a mature plant about 5 kg of resin is produced per year.
Silk worm grows on it (Tussar silkworm) and feeds on caster leaves.

Miscellaneous Information

 It is one of the most important forestry of India.
Charak classified sal as a common 'Arankya' species. Kautilya has mentioned in his 'Arthshashtra' Sal as one of the strong timber-yielding tree of the forest. In ancient literature it is known as Aswakarna
