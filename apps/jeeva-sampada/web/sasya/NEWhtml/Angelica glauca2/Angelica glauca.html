 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
A glabrous, aromatic herb, 1.2 – 3.6 m. in height found to occur in Kashmir to Himachal Pradesh and up to an altitude from 2700 to 3000 m. It also reported from Rajasthan at an altitude of 1,200m, and from Bihar.

Conservation/ Threat Status : 
Plant is rare in wild. Usually found in W. Himalaya.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Leaves: usually large, 1-3 pinnate; leaflets often in threes or reduced to 3, sometimes to 1, ovate or lanceolate undivided or lobed irregularly and sharply toothed; upper surface dark green, lower flaucous. Umbels compound, long stalked.  Bracts several, linear, up to 2.5 cm. Rays many, nearly equal.  Bracteoles many, linear, 6mm.  Flowers white or purple, many in an umbel; calyx teeth none.  Fruit glabrous, flattened oblong ridges expanded in to membranous broad, free wings so that the fruit is surrounded by a double or 2 leaved border.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Compound umbels ; Flower: White, yellowish or purpulish; Calyx: Calyx teeth obsolete; Corolla: Petal obovate, white or urple, emarginate ;Androecium: Stamens 5, epipetalous ; Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 2-celled, disk 2-lobed, style 2, stigma capitellate; Fruit: Schizocarps sub-quadrate, somewhat corky; Seed:  Grooved on inner face, dorsally compressed. Flowering & Fruiting : Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: The plant is propagated through fruits (mericarp).

Soil type:  It grows well in humus rich soil.

Water management:  It requires moist soil conditions.                                      

Harvesting period / Method: Stem are cut in early summer, leaves are collected before flowering, roots are collected in autumn. Seeds are harvested when they are semi-ripe.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Herb: in dyspepsia, constipation, ulcer of palate, infantile atrophy, dysentery, menorrhagia and rinderpest; Root-powder; in stomach trouble of children, vomiting, and as cardiac stimulant; Dal  or curry flavoured with this gives vitality and strength to women after delivery.


Chemistry:
Dried roots – an essential oil (0.4%).  It contains lactones, sesquiterpenes, d-a-cadinene, d-a- pinene, d-a-phallandrene, selinene, umbelliprenin and a terpene alcohol. Fruit – an essential oil.
 A lactone (0.06%), m.p. 101°, from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Isoimperatorin, prangolarin and a new coumarin (I, Fig. 1) isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979).

New lactone – angeolide (fig. 2.) isolated and its structure elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Isolation and charcaterisation of angelicolide (fig. 3) from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Isolation of new furocoumarin – 2’’-O-acetyloxypeucedanin hydrate – from roots along with archangelin and oxypeucedanin and its characterization. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

2,2-Dimethyloxirane, formic acid, 6-methyl-bicyclo[3,2,0]-hepten-2-one, 6-propyl-bicyclo[3,2,0]-hept-6-dien-2-one, 6-propylbicyclo[3,2,0]-hept-6-one, [1(S)-(1a, 3ab,4a,8ab)]-1,4-methanozulene, b-chamigrene, dehydroledol and elemol identified in root essential oil by GC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

The essential oil of Angelica glauca roots was analysed by GC and GC/MS. Sixty-eight constituents representing about 92% of the oil were identified. The main constituents of the oil are b-phellandrene (15.29%) and (z)-ligustilide (31.55%). (Kaul et al, 1996). MAPA 1997, 9701, 0399.

Constituents were isolated from the essential oil of Angelica glauca by means of computerized gas chromatography. The mean components were cnidilide 64.37%, sedanenolide 2.10%, octanal 1.02%,ligustilide 17.33% and butylidene phthalide 2.9%.(Bin and Cuier, 1996)
