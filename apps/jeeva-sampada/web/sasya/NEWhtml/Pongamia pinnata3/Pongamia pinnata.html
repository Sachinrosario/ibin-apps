DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Perennial, much branched, evergreen, tree up to 20 m tall, glabraous; bark soft, grey; stipules oblong, caducous. Laeves imperipinate, 3-9, opposite, obovate, broadly ovate or elliptic, 3.5-12 x 1.8-8.0 cm, acumminate at apex, obtuse-cuneate at base, entire along margin, glabrous on both surfaces, glossy above, coriacious. Flowers in lax, axillary, drooping raceme, c 1 cm acroos, pinkish white or white with purple tinge. Calyx tube campanulate, truncate at apex, c 7 mm long, lobes minute. Corolla pinkish white, clawed, 1-1.2 cm long, vexillum orbicular, 1-2 x 1 cm, wings oblong, 12 x 4 mm; keels 1.0-3.0 mm, obtuse. Stamens 10, monadelphous, vexillary stamen free at base and apex. Pods obliquely oblong, 4-7 x 2-3 cm, woody, indehiscent, compressed, glabrous with slightly curved pointed tip. Seeds reddish brown, elliptic, rugose.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 In Ayurveda system of medicine roots and bark are bitter and useful in eye diseases, skin diseases and vaginal discharge. It cures tumours, piles, wounds, ulcers, urinary discharges and enlargement of spleen and abdomen. Leaves are used in piles, wounds and inflammation. Flowers cure biliousness and diabetes. Fruits and seeds are used in head, brain, eye and skin diseases and oil cures eye diseases, rheumatic pain, leucoderma, wounds and skin diseases. In Unani system of medicine seeds are used in inflammation, earache, chest complaints, fever, hydrocele and in purifying blood. Seed oil is useful in scabes, leprosy, piles, ulcers, chronic fever and liver.
NBPGR:
In scrophulous enlargements; leaf-caminative, antidiarrhoel, bechic in dyspepsia, as preservative for books and stored grains, stem bark in bleeding piles, beri-beri; rind of pod and seed tonic febrifuge, in whooping cough, bronchitis: seed oil (Pongam oil)- used internally as a cholagogue, stomachic, in dyspepsia with sluggish liver, used for external applications in scabies, herpes, sores, leucoderma and other skin diseases, applied locally over chest in pneumonia, cold, chest pain; root and leaf antigonorrhaeic, in foul ulcers; leaf and seeds-antileprotic; leaf and seed oil antirheumatic. Use of chlorinated insecticides and that of the seed cake as a cheep cattle feed is suggested.


Chemistry:
NBPGR:  The tree is rich in flavonoids and related compounds. These include simple flavones, furanoflavonoids, chromenoflavones, chromenochalcones, coumarones, flavone glucosides sterols triterpenes and a modified phenylalanine dipeptide. These seeds and seed oil gave karanjin, pongamol, pongapm and kanjone; Seeds in addition gave lanceolatin β, iso-pengaflavone and pengol; seed oil also contained iso-pongachromene and pongalglabrone; glanrachalcone (a chromenochalcone) was also isolated from the seed oil, which also a number of fatty acids-oleic and linolenic acids being the major constituents. The Leordwood gave chromenochalconeslanchocarpin, pongachalcone I, pongachalcone II, glabrachromene II and glabrachromene and flavones pongaglabrone, kanugm (I), pongachromene and desmethoxykanugin (II).
