 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual or perennial, much brunched, grey or white-silky, shrub, 0.5-4 m tall. It is found to occur in Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Nagaland, Manipur, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves  3, elliptic or lanceolate, 1.2-13.7 x 0.3-5.5 cm, thinley silky hairy above, densely beneath. Flowers in raceme; corolla yellow, with reddish brown tinge. Pods oblong or sickle shaped, 4-10 x 0.6-1 cm, hairy. Seeds 4-6, globose or ellipsoid, 0.6-0.8 cm in diam.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Racemose ; Flower: Yellow, veined with dark brown; Calyx:  Calyx tube campanulte, teeth short; Corolla: Corolla much exserted, its petal of equal length ;Androecium: Stamens diadelphous, anthers uniform; Gynoecium: Ovary sub-sessile, few ovuled, style long, filiform, stigma capitate; Fruit: Pods obliquely grooved between seed ; Seed: Hard, dark choclate red.

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : August-November. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.  
Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 22, 44, 66.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type: Plant requires sandy loam or clayey loam for its cultivation.

Nutrient requirements: Plant responds well to the application of farmyard manure and fertilizers.

Water management:  Plant requires 60-65 cm of annual rainfall.                                     

Harvesting period / Method: Plant are harvested when the pods becomes mature.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:  In Ayurveda system of medicine seeds are used as anthelmintic and leprosy. It is useful in mouth ulcers, tumours, bronchitis, vomiting, heart disease, piles and cough. In Unani system of medicine seeds are used in constipation, griping, biliousness, diarrhoea, weakness and liver diseases. Leaves are good for piles and teeth.


Chemistry:
C26-37 alkanes (16.6%), C36-62 esters (8.4%), C20-34alcohols (26.4%), lupeol (19.2%) and phytosterols (29.4%) and cholesterol (0.3%) which consisted of b-sitosterol (52.1%), stigmasterol (39.3%), campesterol (8.3%) and cholesterol (0.3%) from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Isolation and structure of new flavanone – cajaflavanone (fig. 2). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Structure of major phytoalexin – cajanol (fig. 3) revised to 5,4’-dihydroxy-7,2’-dimethoxy-isoflavanone. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A new isoflavanone – 2’-O-methylcajanone (fig. 5)– isolated and its structure established. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Isolation and structure of new isoflavone glucoside – 5,2’-dihydroxyisoflavone-7-O-b-D-glucoside (fig. 4) – and isolation of sitosterol-b-D-glucoside, genistein, sitosterol, lupeol, a- and b-amyrins. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

A new anthraquinone – cajaquinone (fig. 1) isolated from roots and bark and its structure determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)f.

A new isoflavone – cajaisoflavone (fig. 6) was isolated from root bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Two new stilbene-2-carboxylic acids (I (fig. 7a), II (fig. 7b)) along with pinostrobin chalcone isolated from leaves infected with Botytis cinerea. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Two new isoprenylated isoflavones (III (fig. 8), IV (fig. 9)) along with cajanin and cajanol isolated from plant incubated in presence of its native microflora; their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Vitexin, salicylic acid, hentriacontane, 2-carboxyl-3-hydroxy-4-isoprenyl-5-methoxystilbene, laccerol, longistylin A, Longistylin C, pinostrobin, naringenin-7,4’-dimethyl ether, b-amyrin and b-sitosterol isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

24-Epiclerosterol, 24a- and 24b-methyl-5a-cholest-8(14)-en-3b-ols, 24a-ethyl-5a-cholest-8(14)-en-3b-ol, 14a,24a- and 14a,24b-dimethyl-5a-cholest-9(11)-en-3b-ols, 14a-methyl-24a-ethyl-5a-cholest-9(11)-en-3b-ol, 14a-methyl-24b-ethyl-5a-cholesta-9(11),25-dien-3b-ol and b-sitosterol was identified in seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

acoradiene, b-selinene, a- and b-guaienes, a-himachalene, benzoyl benzoate and eremophilene identified in leaf essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Linoleic (54.8), palmitic (21.4), carpylic, lauric,oleic, and eicosenoic acids were determined in oils. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

A phytotoxic compound – 3-hydroxy-5-methoxystilbene-2-carboxylic acid was isolated from leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

Cajanus cajan lectin was isolated by ammonimum sulfate fractionation and affinity chromatography on an IgM-Sepharose 6B column. The lectin with Mr 18,000 on SDS-PAGE had gel filteration behaviour which was consistent with a mol. Wt. of 39 Kda and a stokes radius of 2.74 nm. (Siddiqui et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 50177s.

Five flavanoids vitexin,isovitexin, apigenin,luteolin, naringenine –4’,7-dimethyl ethanol and b-sitosterol were isolated by ethanol from the leaves of Cajanus cajan. (Lin et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 16420w.

This paper reports the isolation of vitexin and its isomeric compound (isovitexin) from herba cajani by the methods of solvent crystal and column chromatography. Their structures have been determined by IR and UV 13CNMR and 1H NMR spectroscopy. (Lin et al, 1999). C.A. 2000, 133, 117416w.


Pharmacology:
3-Hydroxy-5-methoxystilbene-2-carboxylic acid (0.5 mM/L) inhibted (80%) lettuce radical elongation. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

Administration of a protein fraction isolated from the leaves of C. indicus counteracted thje action of CCl4 on transaminase phosphatase showing hepatoprotectin. Daily treatment with purified protein fraction from C. indicus (0.5 mouse ml i.p.; 50-60 mg/ml) to a period of 7, 14, 21 days respectively showed decreased activities of serum transaminase alkaloid phosphatase and decreased levels of serum bilirubin. (Datta et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 240520z.

The effect of unroasted seeds of C. cajan on serum glucose levels of normal and alloxan diabetic mice were studied. Single doses of unroasted seeds (60% and 80%) on administration to normal as well as alloxanized mice showed significant reduction in the serum glucose levels after 1-2 hr and a significant rise at 3 hr. (Amalraj, 1998).

Ethyl acetate fraction of the ethanol extract has antibacterial activity. (Lin et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 16420w.

 Peptide 1 &2 in acetone and ethanol prepared sample of white seeds of Cajanus cajan showed inhibitory activity against E. Coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae and B. subtilis. However which showed notable inhibitory effect against fungi such as Aspergillus niger, A. fumigatus and Penicillium expecisum. (Dahot and Soomro, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 297498q.

A lectin present in roots of Cajanus cajan seedlings was isolated and purified by affinity chromatography. Sugar specificity assayed by hemagglutination-inhibition activity indicated that lectin belongs to glucose/mannose-specific group and different spectroscopic methods, suggesting that C. cajan root lectin-lipopolysaccharide interaction is specific. (Naeem et al, 2001).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine for gargle (Leaf, Shah et al, 1981); revive taste (Seed, Tarafder, 1978); Skin burn (Leaf, Singh et al, 1987); sores in Mouth, tongue (Leaf & Flower, Borthakur, 1976; Edible vegetable pulse (Seed, Shah, 1984)
