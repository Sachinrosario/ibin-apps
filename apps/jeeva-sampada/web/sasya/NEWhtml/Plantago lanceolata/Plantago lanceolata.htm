DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 A perennial herb found in the Western Himalayas from Kashmir to Shimla at altitudes of 1,500 – 2,400 m.  Leaves lanceolate, entire or toothed, 20-30 cm long and 2-4 cm. broad; spikes ovoid, sub-globose or cylindric; seeds oblong; concavo-convex, black.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Parts used:
 Leaf and root-astringent, vulnerary alterative, bechic, antiasthmatic, anti-inflammatory, in wounds; seeds-diuretic, cathartic, haemostatic.


Chemistry: Agnuside (fig. 1b), mp. 145°C and aucubin (fig. 1a), mp. 182°C, from leaves; former characterized as p-hydroxybenzoate of aucubin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).
Apigenin-7-O-glucoside isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.
1,3-dihydroxy-3,5,5-trimethylcyclohex-4a-ene-acetic acid lactone (fig. 2) and a caffeic acid glycoside (glucose and rhamnose), mp. 141°C, isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.
P-hydroxybenzoic, protocatechuic, gentisic, vanillic, syringic, p-coumaric, caffeic, ferulic and p-hydroxyphenylacetic acids found by PC in leaves; aucuboside and catalpol also present in leaves and seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.
Galactose (44.0), arabinose (32.0), glucose (9.0), rhamnose (7.0), mannose (4.0%) along with xylose and fucose obtained by complete hydrolysis of total mucilage. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.
Crude polysaccharide from leaves composed of galacturonic acid (31.0), galactose (28.0), arabinose (20.0), glucuronic acid (7.0), glucose (6.0), rhamnose (4.0), mannose (2.0%), fucose and xylose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.
Aucubin, catalpol, globularin and desacety,lasperulosidic acid methyl ester isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).
From the leaves of Plantago lanceolata, a new derivative of benzoic acid, pluchoic acid has been isolated and its structure established as 2-methoxy-4-hydroxy benzoic acid by chemical and spectroscopic data. (Chopra et al, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 16764h.
The petroleum ether extract of plantago lanceolata root afforded two aliphatic fraction (A and B). Fraction A was a mixture of n-alkenes while fraction B was a mixture of wax esters. On the other hand petroleum ether extract of leaves/ stem afforded fraction C and D while chloroform soluble part and methanolic extract gave fraction E. Fraction C, D and E were identified as mixture of aliphatic esters, wax, acids and n-alkanols respectively. (Kaith and Singha, 2000). C.A. 2000, 133, 19736f.
Plantago lanceolata was found to have amounts of aucubin and catalpol plus two further iridoids. (Willinger and Dobler, 2001).
New iridoid glycosides were found in Plantago lanceolata. (Suomi et al, 2002).
NBPGR:   Leaves gave aucubin and esculatin, polysaccharides, whole plant yielded rhamosidoglycosides of caffeic acid, α-1, 3-dihydroxy –3, 5, 5-trimethylcyclohexane acetic acid lactone, seed oil gave fatty acids-myristic, palmitic, oleic and, linolenic acid.

Pharmacology: The five phenylethanoids, acetoside (1), cistanoside F (2), lavandulifolioside (3), plantamajoside (4), and isoacetoside (5) were isolated from the herb of Plantago lanceolata. Compounds 1,the major phenylethanoid in the herb of Plantago lanceolata showed inhibitory effects on arachidonic acid-induced mouse ear edema. (Murai et al, 1995).
Neolupenol, a pentacyclic triterpene isolated from Plantago lanceolata flowers, was studied to determine its anti-inflammatory activity against carrageenan-induced rat paw oedema. Neolupenol at a dose of 100 mg/kg p.o. showed 70% edema inhibition. (Kaith, 1996). C.A. 1995, 124, 306860m.
Plantin (Plantago lanceolata L.) is used for anti-inflammatory action in upper respiratory tract infections. (Wegener and Kraft, 1999).
The ethanolic extract of Plantago lanceolata studied using the carbon tetrachloride induced hepatotoxicity models in rats. (Aktay et al, 2000). MAPA 2001-02-0729.
Purifiaction and characterization of the main allergaen of Plantago lanceolata pollen, Pla l 1 was performed by ELISA and immunoblotiing after SDS-PAGE with sera from plantain-allergic patients. (Calabozo et al, 2001).


Ethnobotany: Medicine for blood purification (Leaf, Dar et al, 1984)
