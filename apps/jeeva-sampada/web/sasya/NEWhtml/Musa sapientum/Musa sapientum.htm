DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

Large herbs; pseudostems composed of tightly convolute leaf sheaths, cylindrical; leaves simple, spiral, forming a compact crown, very large, petiolate, mid rib thick, with lateral, parallel nerves, oblong, undulate, glabrous; flowers in terminal, solitary, drooping spikes, mostly unisexual, subtended by coloured bracts, bract ovate, large, coriaceous, caducous, male flowers towards the terminal portion while female flowers towards the basal portion of peduncle, calyx 3-5 lobed, tubular, corolla membraceous, stamens fertile 5, ovary inferior, 3-locular; fruits berries, oblong, 3-5 angled.

Processing Techniques:


Other uses: Normal ripe (just ripened, yellow coloured) and overripe (blackened skins) banana fruits were obtained from the local market. Bananas were peeled and homogenized in a waring blender for about 2 to 3 min to obtain pulp. Potassium metabisulphite (100 ppm), was added at this stage to prevent browning and to check the growth of undesirable micro-organisms. Preliminary studies were carried out to optimise the conditions for maximum extraction of juice using different levels of pectinase enzyme (commercial preparation, PEC (Pecticenzyme concentrate) supplied by Microzyme Pvt. Ltd., Bombay) and different incubation periods at 2820C . Based on these studies, 0.2 percent pectinase and 4 hours incubation time were selected for obtaining the juice from the pulp. The juice was separated by certifugation at 5000g for 20 min. The clear juice was used for the preparation of wine by slight modification of the method described by Kundu et al (1976). The TSS of the juice (0Brix) was adjusted to 230 Brix using sucrose. The acidity was adjusted to 0.7% using citric acid. Diammonium phospate (O.05g/100ml) was added to the juice as a source of nitrogen and phosphorus for the yeast. The juice was then pastcurized by heating in water bath at 82-830C for 30 min.

Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae var ellipsoideus) inoculum was preparaed 2 days in advance, using similarly extracted and pasteurized juice. This inoculum was added at 2% level to the pasteurized and cooled juice. The inoculated juice was incubated at 300C upto 25 days. The fermented juice was clarified using 0.1% bentonite. Results and Discussion
Banana Juice
The recovery of juice was more from over-ripe fruits (67.6%) than from normal ripe fruits (60.2%) (Table 2). The juice from over-ripe fruits contained more TSS, reducing sugars, and less acidity and tannins than the juice from normal ripe fruits. The higher levels of TSS and sugars in overripe fruits were obvious because of rapid conversion of polysaccharides into sugars during ripening. The decrease in acidity in over-ripe fruits may be due to the utilization of organic acids for the respiratory acitivity.

Banana Wine
The wine prepared from over-ripe fruits contained less TSS, reducing sugars, acidity, tannins and SO2 and more alcohol than the wine prepared from normal ripe fruits . The organoleptic evaluation of wines showed that the wine prepared from over-ripe fruits was comparable to that from normal ripe fruits in colour, taste and astringency characteristics. 


The following steps are involved in the preparation of wine in Goan method.


Methods of wine preparation:

Boil the bananas in 2 litres of water and mesh them. When boiling, add 1/2 of the sugar. When lukewarm, add 2gms Potassium Metabisulphite, tea and the lemon juice. Do the pulp fermentation in a bucket. After 4 days, strain through a cloth in a fermentation jar. Add the remaining sugar, stir, and add the yeast. Add the remaining water boiled and slightly warm (lukewarm) and stir. Keep for fermentation for 21 days or until bubbling has stopped.

Rack 3 times and bottle after 3 months. Mature at least for 6 months. Don't forget to add 2gms Potassium Metabisulphite after each racking (Saldanha, 2001).
