HABIT & HABITAT

Tree native to Central America. Moist tropics; >1000 mm rainfall; cool areas; above 500(?) m. it can grow on many different soils, including infertile ones, and even grows on heavily compacted clay-type soils with poor aeration.

Seed Germination & Propagation:

Plantations are easily established by direct sowing or by seedlings. Seeds or seedlings are usually planted at the beginning of the wet season. Seedlings are transplanted from nurseries at about 4-6 months at spacings of 2m X 2m or 1m X 1m.

Seeds are treated with hot water and then soaked in cold water for 24 hours.

Plant coppices well.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Parts Used:

Firewood (specific gravity 0.65; 40 m3/ha annually); calorific value is 4,500-4,750 kcal per kg. and its ash content is 1.8 per cent. It is used for cooking as well as in small industries; for example, those making lime, tiles or bricks. It is a fuel crop.

Other uses:

Forage (high tannin); green manure

Erosion control – The species grows very quickly, its dense foliage provides ground cover and its extensive and deep root system binds soil, thereby making calliandra calothyrsus particularly suitable for erosion control on slopes and for rejuvinating degraded soils.

Soil improvement – By its nitrogen fixation and litter production, calliandra improves soil quality and productivity.

Fodder – Livestock relish the leaves and the plant is a good fodder crop. It has been grown together with elephant grass for fodder in large areas previously unable to support any crop.

Ornamental – the bush is an exciting ornamental, producing beautiful red "powderpuff" flowers. It forms attractive hedges.

Bee forage – Honey produced by bees that forage on calliandra flowers has bittersweet flavour.
