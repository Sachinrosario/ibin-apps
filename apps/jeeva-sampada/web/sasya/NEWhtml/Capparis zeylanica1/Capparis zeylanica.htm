Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT  
A climbing or rambling shrub, 2-10 m in height, armed with 3-6 mm long, recurved thorns, distributed largely east of the line Bombay - Delhi - Dehra Dun and south of the Himalayas, and in the Andaman Islands.
Availability :   
Burma,Ceylon,E.Asia,Indomalasia,Java,Myanmar,Philipines,Srilanka

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
The branches are tomentose when young, glabrous later; leaves coriaceous, elliptic or broadly lanceolate, base rounded, apex mucronate; flowers profuse, showy, pinkish white, later turning pink, axillary, solitary or 2-3 together, berry globular or ellipsoid, 3-4 cm in diam, bright scarlet, 5-7 seeded; seeds globose, embedded in white pulp. The plant makes a good hedge for dry areas.

Economic Potential and Uses

Parts used
Roots, Root bark, Fruits, Seeds and wood as fuel.

Uses

1. The fruits are used as a vegetable and sometimes for curry in Konkan (Maharashtra). They are also pickled.

2. The young shoots and leaves are eaten by goats and elephants.

Chemistry
Analysis of fruits gave: moisture, 67.0; protein, 6.1- fat, 3.8; carbohydrates, 11.5; fibre, 9.6; and minerals, 2.0%; calcium, 64; phosphorus, 81 mg; and energy, 105 Kcal/100g. The seeds and leaves contain glucocapparin, amyrin, n-triacontane and a fixed oil.

Other Uses
 1.The rootbark is bitter, stomachic, sedative, antihidrotic and useful in fever. The paste of the rootbark is reported to contain an alkaloid, a phytosterol, a water-soluble acid, and a mucilaginous substance. The paste of the roots is used by Mundas of Ranchi district (Bihar)for body-ache.

2.The leaves contain p-carotene. They are applied as a poultice to piles, boils and swellings and also as a counter- irritant. The plant is useful in fever. It contains a saponin and p-hydroxybenzoic, syringic,vanillic, ferulic and p-coumaric acids.
