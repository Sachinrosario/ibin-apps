HABIT & HABITAT
Annual, erect or diffuse, much branched, densely hairy herbs, 30-60 cm high found almost throughout India. Common weed on dry as well as moist sandy soils.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Annual, erect or diffuse, much branched, densely hairy herbs, 30-60 cm high. Stem tetrangular hispid or scabrid, grayish; internodes 3-8 cm long, grooved. Leaves petiolate, oblong-lanceolate to linear, 3-8 x 0.7-2 cm, obtuse at apex, narrowed at base, entire to shallowly rounded toothed at margin, appressed hairy on both surfaces; petioles c 0.5 cm long, absent in younger ones.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Flowers white, arranged in axillary and terminal whorls; bracts linear, ciliate, almost equaling the calyx. Calyx tubular, mouth oblique, contracted, 8-12 mm long, base tapering, usually smooth and glabrous below, hairs projected towards mouth; teeth short, deltoid. Corolla white, c 10 mm long, bilabiate. Style gynobasic; stigma capitate. Carpels 4. Nutlets 2.5-2 mm, smooth, brown.August-November

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
 The plant is commonly used as antipyretic. Leaf juice is applied externally in psoriasis, chronic skin-eruptions and painful swellings. Flowers mixed with honey is used for the treatment of cough and cold.


Chemistry:

·        Two sesquiterpenes-ligularol and ligularone- isolated and characterized as 6β-hydroxyfurano-eremophilane and 6-oxofurano-eremophilane respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a .

·        a-sitosterol, β-sitosterol and a compound A, mp. 610, isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a .

·        Triterpenoids/steroids occurred in the entire plant. Alkaloids were also present in all parts except the stem. (Kamat and Singh, 1994). CA 122:51306z.   

·        A new trierpenoid lactone-leucolactone-isolated from roots and characterized as 3β, 16a-dihydroxyoleanan-28→13β-olide. ( Rastogi and Mehrotra 1990-1995)a.

·        Isolation from shoots and structure determination of 1-hydroxytetratriacontan-4-one and 32-methytetratriacontan-8-ol; dotriacontanol also isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1995)b.

·        From the shoots of Leucas aspera a novel phenolic compound 4-(2, 4 hydroxy 4-1’oxo-5’n- prophyl tetra cosnyl) phenol has been isolated along with non atriacotane. These compounds have been characterized by spectral data and chemical studies. (Mishra et al., 1995).

Pharmacology:

·        Plant used as antipyretic, juice of leaves applied externally in psoriasis. Extract showed antibacterial activity against Micrococcus pypgenes and Escherichia coli. (Rastogi and Mehrotra 1970-1979)a .

·        The Gounda tribals in Trupattur are often victims of poisonous snake bite as a professional hazard as they work in fields and forests. The area has many species of snakes, out of which the cobra, Russells viper, Swa scaled viper and Krait are the poisonous ones. The tribals use the readily available local plants such as Lucas aspera to annual poison.   ( Masilamani, 1995).

·        Effects of petroleum ether, chloroform ethanol and water extract of Leucas aspera. Whole plant was investigated for anti-inflammatory and analgesic activities in albino rats and mice, respectively at a dose of 400 mg/ kg body weight, orally. Ethanol and distilled water extract exhibited significant anti-inflammatory activity, whereas significant analgesic effect was shown by petroleum ether and ethanol extract when compared with respective controls and were comparable with those of standard drugs, diclofenac sodium and analgin. (Saundane et al., 2000). MAPA 2000-04-2278.

·        Anti-inflammatory effects of Leucas aspera on carrageenin induced hind paw odema and cotton pellet granuloma in rats has been investigated. L. aspera produced significant anti-inflammatory activity in both acute and subacute models of inflammation. In acute inflammation, L. aspera was more effective than acetyl salycyclic acid. However in subactue inflammation the drug was found to be less effective than phenylbutazone. (Srinivas and Rao, 2000). MAPA 2000-04-2291

·        Leucas aspera is used in various ailments of the eye viz. night blindness, cataract, conjunctivitis, eyesores etc by tribals of Phulbani district Orissa. (Sahoo,  1995). MAPA 9605-3166.

·        The tribal of Ground tribe use Leucs aspera to annul snake poison. (Masilamani, 1997). MAPA 2000-04-2554.

Miscellaneous Information

·        The smoke of leaves of Leucas aspera was found to be more toxic to the filariol nectar mosquito (Culex quinquefasciatus) than the synthetic mosquitio mats which contain 4% d- allethrin.  (Pandian et al., 1994). MAPA 9502-0991.

·         L. aespera completely inhibites conidial germination in Drechslera oryzae. (Ganesan and Krishnaraju,  1995). MAPA 9506-3623.
