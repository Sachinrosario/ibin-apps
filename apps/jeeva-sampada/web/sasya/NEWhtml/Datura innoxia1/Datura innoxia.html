REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
   Inflorescence: Cymes; Flower: Large, erect, funnel shaped flowers; Calyx: Calyx tubular, 5-toothed, in fruit circumsciss above the base ; Corolla: Tubular, funnel shaped, limb entire; Androecium: Stamens 5, adnate to the base, filaments filiform, anther included; Gynoecium: Ovary superior, 2-celled, style filiform, stigma 2-lobed; Fruit: Capsule 4-valved,  ellisoid Seed: Many, compressed . 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : March - April. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by wind.
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number 2n=24.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses:
Chemistry:

·        Hyoscyamine, hyoscine and meteloidine isolated from leaves and roots; 7-hydroxy-3,6-bis-tigloyloxytropane, (-)3a,6b-bistiloyloxytropane, tropine and pseudotropine from roots. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

·        Scopolamine and unknown alkaloid isolated from aerial parts. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

·        Scopolamine level in roots was high during flowering (0.55% ), decreased at beginning fruit -bearing (0.30%) and increased again; scopolamine content in stems and leaves was lower. Root contained about 0.3% atropine which decreased slightly at end of flowering. In stems atropine was high (0.11-0.18%) during initial and final stages of fruit -bearing period. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

·        Two new pentacyclic triterpenes -daturadiol (fig. 1a) and daturaolone (fig. 1b) - from seeds and their structures elucidated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

·        A new alkaloid –6b-propionyloxy-3a-tigloyloxytropane (fig. 2) -isolated from roots and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

·        Method of separation scopolamine from tropine alkaloids. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

·        Scopolamine isolated from fresh plant. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

·        Seed glycerides contained linoleic acid (53.7) and oleic acid (27.1%). Hyoscyamine, scopolamine and meteloidine isolated from leaves and seeds; leaves also contained quercetin-7-glucosido-3-sophoroside, quercetin-7-glucoside-3-glucogalactoside and their esters with caffeic and p-coumaric acids, kaempferol-7-glucosido-3-sophoroside and kaempferol-7-glucosido-3-glucogalactoside and their esters with caffeic acid. Small amounts of daturadiol and daturaolone also isolated from seeds. Roots contained 3a,6b-ditigloyloxytropane, 6b-propanoyloxy-3a-tigloyloxytropane and 3a,6b-tigloyloxytropane-7b-ol. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

·        Two lectins – lectin 11 and lectin 12- composed of arabinose, fucose, xylose, mannose, galactose and glucose in molar ratio of 6:1:2:1:2:2 and 1:2:1:1:3 respectively, isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

·        Two isomeric (R and S) N-methylpyrrolidinylhygrines identified in aerial parts by GLC and GLC-MS along with hygrine and tyramine. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

·        Two alkaloids - a and b-scopodonnines – isolated from seeds and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

Pharmacology:

·        Scopolamine (5,8 or 10 mg/kg), given i.v. in man mainly impairs acquisition of new information but has less effect on retrieval of information. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

Ethnobotany:
Medicine as antihydrophobic (Whole plant, Shah et al, 1983); Asthma (Leaf, Shah et al, 1983; Singh et al, 1987); Pyorrhoea (Seed, Shah and Joshi, 1971); Rheumatism (Fruit, Saxena and Vyas, 1981); In country liquor (Seed, Shekhawat and Anand, 1984); Symbolic offering to Shiva, taboo to Vishnu (Agarwal,  et al 1981; Hajra, 1987)
