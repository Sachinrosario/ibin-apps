DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Terminal or lax flowered racemes ; Flower: pedicellate, bracteate, showy, hermaphrodite, complete ; Calyx: Membranous, faintly pubescent, tbe short, campanulate, teeth subequal, triangular acute; Corolla: Much exserted, bluish purple and white or rarely completely whiteyellow with purple tinge, winged with strong rib down the middle of base ;Androecium: Stamens monadelphous, anthers dimorphic ; Gynoecium: Ovary sessile, usually many ovulate, style long abruptly incurved the base, more or less bearded upwards, stigma minute and oblique; Fruit: Pods densely villous when young, shortly pubescent when ripe, oblong, cylindrical or stalked; Seed: 12-20 . 

Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : October - November. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind
Genetic variability:
reported chromosome number 2n=16.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Quercetin, quercetin-3-O-glucoside, m.p. 228°C, and isoquercitrin from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969).

Crystal structure of retusine (fig. 1) determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Methyl-esters-tridecanoate (2.0), myristate (0.93), lactonate (1.12), palmitate (36.67), stearate (1.30), oleate (13.68), linoleate (40.64) and linolenate (2.69%) – identified in seed oil by GLC. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Monocrotaline (5.0), retusine (0.001) and spectabiline (fig. 2) (0.0003%) isolated from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

Crystal structure of spectabiline. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)d.

Vitexin-4’-O-galactoside from seeds. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)e.

Determination of linoleic (50.2), palmitic (16.9), oleic (13.0), ricinoleic (9.8), stearic (5.3), sterculic (3.5) and malvalic (1.3%) acids in seed oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989).

Pharmacognostic studies of leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).

A novel cardenolide, 5,6-dehydrosarmentogenin 3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-(1à6)-O-b-D-galactopyranoside have been isolated from the seeds of C. retusa. (Yadav and Thakur, 1995). C.A 1995, 123, 193651c.
