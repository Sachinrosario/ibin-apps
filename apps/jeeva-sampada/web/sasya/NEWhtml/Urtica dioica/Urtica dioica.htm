Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT: 
A robust, dioecious herb, found in the Himalayas from Kashmir to Kumaun. Commomly known as Nettles, a perennial plant growing worldwide in wasteland areas. It is distributed in the temperate and tropical zones. Four species occur in India. A robust, dioecious herb up to 2 m. high, with grooved  stems abundantly armed with stinging hair found in the Himalayas from Kashmir to Kumaun at an  altitudes of 2,100-3,200 m. Leaves ovate or lanceolate, usually cordate, serrate; flowers greenish, in axillary cymes.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
Plant height of 2m. with grooved stems abundantly armed with stinging hair.  Leaves ovate or lanceolate, usually cordate, serrate, flowers greenish in axillary cyme. Nettles have a reputation for their savage sting from the hairs and bristles present on the leaves and stem.

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY
Breeding System :
The stamens are bent down inward in the bud, and when ripe spring violently upwards and bend out of the flower, the anther turning inside out, so that the loose powdery pollen in ejected as a little cloud, and may be borne by wind to stigma. The female flower has a I-loc., I-ovuled with a large brush like stigma.

Chemistry: 
Nettle root constituents include lignans, scopoletin, sterols (beta sitosterol and sito-sterol-3-o-glucoside), oleannoic acid and 9-hydroxyl-10-trans-12-cis-octadecanoic acid. Other chemicals are the high molecular weight compounds such as five acids and neutral polysaccharides and isolectins.

The urticating properties of the hairs are attributed to the presence of acetylcholine, histamine, and 5-hydroxy- tryptamine (5-HT); a histamine-liberating enzyme is also present. The same active principles occur in U. parviflora and a European species U. urens. They are believed to be in solution in the volatile fatty acids, occurring in the cell-sap of the hairs.
