 Other Biological Details
HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, strongly, sweet aromatic, glandular pubescent herb; It is found to occur in Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan at altitudes up to 1333-3333 m.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Stem 15-45 cm long, branches numerous, recurved, spreading. Leaves stalked, oval or oblong, 2.5-7.5 cm in long, pinnately 4-6 lobes on each side. Flower in numerous clusters, short axillary panicle forming large tarminal panical. Perianth segments acuminate at the apex, with broad membranous margin, rounded on the back, nearly keelless. Seeds horizontal or vertical, smooth or with shallow pits or spiral curves.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence:Short, axillary, long terminal racemes ; Flower: Minute, bisexual ; Perianth:  Sepals 3-5, glandular,  pubescent; Androecium: Stamens 1-5 opposite to sepals, anther 2-celled; Gynoecium: Ovary ovoid, free, compressed, styles 2-3 ; Fruit:  Membranous utricle included in perianth  ; Seed: Horizontal, subglobose, smooth . Flowering & Fruiting : Flowers are pollinated by insects.Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: It is used in cataract and humoral asthma.


Chemistry:
Twenty six compounds identified in essential oil by GLC and a new acid – chenopodic acid-isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Chrysoseriol, quercetin-3-O-b-D-glucopyranoside and quercetin-3-O-b-D-glucopyranosyl-6-b-D-glucopyranoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

A new alkaloid, m.p. 145-7°C, isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

11-acetoxyselinan-4a-ol (fig. 1), a-chenopodiol (fig. 2), b-chenopodiol (fig. 3), botrydiol (fig. 4), a-chenopodiol monoacetate, a-, b- and g-eudesmol acetates along with six known sesquiterpenes isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

Three new eudesmanes (I fig. 5a, II fig. 5b and III fig. 5c) isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)e.

a-selinene, b-selinene, b-elemene, 6a-acetoxyselina-4(15),11-diene,3,11-selinadien-6a-ol and selina-4(15),11-dien-6a-ol obtained from essential oil. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Tweleve new sesquiterpenes – 6a-acetoxyselin-4(15)-en-11-ol (Fig. 6a), 11-acetoxy-cis-guai-10(14)-en-4a-ol (fig. 6b), 6a-acetoxy-selin-3-en-11-ol-2-one (fig. 6c), 4a-acetoxyselinan-6a,11-diol (fig. 6d), selin-4(15)-en-3b,11-diol (fig. 6e), selin-3,5-dien-11-ol-2-one (fig. 6f), cis-guai-10-en-4a,11-diol (fig. 6g), selin-4(15)-en-3a,6a,11-triol (fig. 6h), selin-4(15)-en-3b,6a,11-triol (fig. 6i), selin-4-en-3a,6a,11-triol (fig. 6j), selinane-3a,4a,6a,11-tetraol (fig. 6k) and selinane-3b,4a,6a,11-tetraol (fig. 6l) - isolated from aerial parts and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Hispidulin, salvigenin, 5-methylsalvigenin, 7-methyleupatulin and sinensetin isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)c.

The essential oil of Chenopodium botrys was chromatographically analyzed. Two terpene alcoholic viz. a and b-eudesmols were separated from the oil and their structures confirmed by NMR. The toxicity of the oil was also measured using brine-shrimp lethality procedure. (El-Sayed and Aly, 1994). C.A. 1995, 123, 251365a.

The headspace of Chenopodium botrys was analyzed. Monoterpenes (Camphor, D-3-carene, fenchone, linalool, menthone, nerol, b-pinene, pulegone, terpineol-4 and thujone) and sesquiterpenes b-elemene, elemol and b-eudesmol were found to be responsible for the aromatic, herbaceous, earthy, dull, heavy and pine-like odor of slimy anserine. (Buchbauer et al, 1995). MAPA 1995, 9506-3669.


Pharmacology:
Essential oil inhibited growth of Staphylococcus aureus and Bacillus subtilis, respective MICs being 100.0 and 50.0 mg/ml. Essential oil contained a- and b-eudesmols. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994).


Ethnobotany:
Medicine anthelm., diuretic, headache, laxative, liver complaints, stomach ache (Whole plant, Gupta et al, 1981); Aromatic for flavour in cheese, soup (Koelz, 1979)
