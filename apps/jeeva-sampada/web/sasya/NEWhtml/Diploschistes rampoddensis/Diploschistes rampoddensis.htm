Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

This rock inhabiting lichen grows on sandstone and siliceous rocks in moist, exposed and covered places in tropical and temperate regions.
Availability :

Outside India, it is known from Nepal and Sri Lanka (Pant & Upreti, 1993). In India it is distributed as follows.
Assam: Without localities. Madhya Pradesh: Hosangabad district, Purana Pachmarhi; on way to Ekantgiri. Manipur: Korong, Hiphe, near river; Tegnoupal. Nagaland: Kohima district, Kohima-Wokha road, near Govt. High School; Peren - Tenning road, at 1 km point; Mon district, Near Nyasa village. Tamil Nadu: Madurai district, Palni hills, Shembaganur, Kodaikanal, along Leving's Path; Bhooparai area. Uttaranchal: Almora district, on way to Kasardevi; Almora - Takula (enroute to Pindari Glacier); Sitoli forest. Dehradun district, Mussoorie Hills, on way to Moosy fall; Chamoli district, Mandakini river valley, on way from Sonprayag to Gauri Kund; Guptakashi, 2 km from temple in forest; Tehri Garhwal, near Silkara, en route to Jamunotri; Pithoragarh district, on way from Merthi to Didihat; Kuchigar Quercus forest; on way from Didihat, Shan dev; Nainipatal forest; Askote, above village Barakote. West Bengal: Darjeeling district, Tiger Hill, Senchal lake area.

Conservation/ Threat Status : Common.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

The species is distinguished by its saxicolous, verrucose, thin thallus,upto 3 mm wide apothecioid ascomata, 8- spored asci,18 -24 x 6 -12 µm, submuriform, ascospores and lecanoric acid.( Awasthi, 1991).

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
The species can be utilized for preparation dyes as it contain red coloured acids similar to many lichens utilized in dying.

Parts Used:
