HABIT & HABITAT
Middle sized tree; found to occur in  Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Madhya Pradesh, Deccan Peninsular regions of India. An element of dry deciduous forests. Rarely met in orchards and roadsides, outside forest areas.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Middle sized tree; young parts covered with peltate, orbicular scales. Leaves crowded at the end of branchlets, oblong or oblong-lanceolate, obtuse, subacute or bluntly acuminate at apex, serrate or crenate at margin, glabrous, covered with minute raised dots, pale beneath, base narrowed to the petiole. Racemes axillary or terminal, males above, females below. Flowers pale yellow, solitary or in fascicles. Male flowers: pedicels filiform, sepals 5, slightly connate, ovate, obtuse; petals 5, concave, oblong, woolly; stamens 12, inflexed in bud, filaments hairy at base; disc of 5 glands. Female flowers: sepals 5, acute, ciliate; petals obovate, woolly; disk annular. Ovary trigonous, densely covered with stellate scales, trilocular; ovule one in each locule. Fruit subglobose, 3-1obed. Seeds ellipsoid, smooth.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
Flower: Pale-yellowish green, solitary or fascicled in the axils of minute bracts on long erect racemes, males in the upper part of raceme and female on lower part  ; Male flower: pedicellate, segments obtuse, petals elliptic lanceolate, stamens 12, inflexed in the bud, filaments hairy ; Female flower: Pedicellate, sepals more acute than males, petals ovate with dense wooly margins, styles subdivided in to long slender branches ; Fruit: Capsule subglobose clothed with smooth orbicular scales ; Seed: Ellipsoid, rounded and quite smooth on the back . Flowering & Fruiting :  October- January. 
Floral and seed biology:
Fl. & Fr.: December - April

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
Uses: Seeds and fruits are purgative. In Konkan and Goa bark prescribed in chronic enlargement of liver and remittent fever. It is also applied in sprains, bruises and rheumatic swelling. In Southern Konkan it is used as remedy for snakebite. Santals use root and bark as purgative and as an alterative in dysentery. Roots enter into the composition of pills used by the Mundas of Chota-Nagpur for chronic rheumatism. Root bark is given in small doses as a purge, poisonous in large quantity. Leaves are used externally for liver complaints and scabies.

Chemistry:
b-sitosterol, a diterpene alcohol, mp. 143°C and a new diterpene – oblongifoliol (fig. 1a) from seeds, roots and bark. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)a.

Isolation of deoxyoblongifoliol (fig. 1b) and elucidation of structure as 3a-hydroxydraut-8(14),15-diene [3a-hydroxy (-)pimara-8(14),15-diene]. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969)b.

Ent-isopimara-7,15-diene, ent-isopimara-7,15-dien-19-ol and ent-isoimaradien-19-al isolated. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Acetylaleuritolic acid (fig. 2) isolated and characterized as 3b-acetoxyolean-14(15)-en-28-oic acid. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Flavanoid isolated from root bark, stem bark and wood, whose aglycone identified as quercetin and procyanidin. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

A new diterpenoid acid (-)11-dehydrohardwickiic acid (fig. 3) along with (-) hardwickiic acid isolated and characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)d.

A new cembranoid diterpene neocroto-cembranal (fig. 4) was isolated from stem bark of Croton oblongifolius. (Roengsumran et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 240450p.

Four new labdane diterpenoids, labda-7,12(E),14-triene, labda-7,12(E),14-triene-17-al, labda-7,12(E),14-triene-17-ol and labda-7,12(E),14-triene-17-oic acid were isolated from stem bark of Croton oblongifolius. (Roengsumran et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 130, 249486s.

Two new cembranoids, crotocembraneic acid and neocrotocembraneic acid were isolated from the stem bark of Croton oblongifolius. (roengsumran et al, 1998). C.A. 1998, 128, 268257u.

Three labdane diterpenoids, 2-acetoxy-3-hydroxy-labda-8(17),12(E)-14-triene, 3-acetoxy-2-hydroxy-labda-8(17),12(E)-14-triene, and 2,3-dihydroxy-labda-8(17),12(E),14-triene were isolated from stem bark of Croton oblongifolius. Their structures were established by spectroscopic data, and each was also tested for cytotoxicity against various human tumor cell lines. (Roengsumran et al., 2001)

A new furoclerodane, croblongifolin, together with one known clerodane, crovatin and one known labdane, nidorellol, were isolated from the stem bark of Croton oblongifolius. (Roengsumran et al., 2002)

Pharmacology:

Neocrotocembranal inhibited platelet aggregation induced by thrombin with an IC50 value of 47.21 mg/ml and exhibited cytotoxicity P-388 cells in vitro with an IC 50 values of 6.48 mg/ml. (Roengsumran et al, 1999). C.A. 1999, 131, 240450p.

Croblongifolin from C.sativus showed a significant cytotoxicity against various human tumor cell lines including HEP-G2, SW620, CHAGO, KATO3 and BT474. (Roengsumran et al., 2002)

The methanolic extracts showed the most significant hepatoprotective activity comparable with standard drug silymarin. Other extracts namely petroleum ether and acetone also exhibited a potent activity. (Ahmed et al., 2002).
