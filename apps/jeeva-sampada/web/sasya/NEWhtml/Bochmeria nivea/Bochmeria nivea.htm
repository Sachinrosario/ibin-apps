HABIT & HABITAT

Native to China, Malaysia and Japan; cultivated in several parts of India.
Var. nivea - cultivated in India, China and Taiwan (Formosa).
Var. tenacissima (Gaudic) Miq. - in tropical region, cultivated in the Malay Peninsula, Mexico, Algeria and other parts of Africa.
The plant grows wild, mainly in the sub-Himalayan region and on Nilgiris hills. It is cultivated in Arunachal Pradesh, Assam and West Bengal and also to some extent in the Kangra and Shimla valleys, and Nilgiri hills, though it has not established itself as a profitable crop. The plant has been in cultivation in China for the fiber since ancient times.

Ramie grows well under a fairly wide variety of conditions, but warm and humid climate with an annual rainfall of C 100 cm spread over the year in the best. The area should be free of strong winds as they result in damaging the fiber due to rubbing of the slates.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

- A shrubby rhizomatous, pubescent perennial with slender, round stems, upto 2.4 m long.
- Leaves alternate, thick, broadly ovate or cordate, acuminate, crenate or serrate, scabrid above and whitish below.
- Flowers small, greenish white in branched panicles;
- Achenes oblong, compressed, hairy, brown.

Seed Biology
The plants are mainly propagated by rhizome cuttings, as they produce new plants quickly. They are planted at a spacing of 45-60 cm between the plant, and 60-90 cm between the lines; and C 300 kg of rhizome (10-15 cm) are required for one hectare.

Stem cuttings, C 25 cm in length with at least 3-4 nodes and prepared from 30-40 days old, healthy stalks, are planted during the rainy season. Planting is done either during April-May or after the end of the rainy season viz., Sept-Oct.

The shoots come up in two weeks, and once the plants are established, they grow for a number of years. The crop is irrigated at an interval of 10-12 days during the dry season. Weeding is done only during the initial stages, the crop growth suppress the weeds at later stages. The plant is a heavy feeder and needs plenty of manure.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
In Europe - used for underwear, portieres, upholstery, thread and paper.

Ramie fiber :
Ramie fiber is highly lustrous and exceptionally resistant to bacteria and fungi, highly absorbant and dries quickly. It is very white and does not change colour on exposure to sunlight. The fibers are exceptionally long with an average length of C150 mm. The ultimate fibers have thick walls and are flattened in cross section.

The fiber cannot be spun beyond a certain count and is, therefore, used for making coarse textiles like table - cloths, towels, aprons and coarse dresses.

Harvesting Practices:
In Assam and N. Bengal - two harvests are made - one in May, other in October.

For fiber:
Ramie plants, grown for fiber, are ready for harvest when their growth - rate slows down with the onset of flowering. Treatment with gibberellin promotes stem growth and prevents early flowering. The lower leaves turn yellow and fall off, and the lower portions of stem turn brown. The stem or canes are ready for harvest 75-90 days after planting. Over-mature stems yield a fiber or higher tensile strength, but are difficult to decortication due to increased lignification.

The stems are usually defoliated by hand or with a sickle prior to cutting to ensure quicker drying. The defoliated stems are cut with a sickle close to the ground. The harvested stems are removed from the field quickly and dried immediately to prevent damage due to moulds.



Produce and Products:
Rabimie fiber.
Pulp - bank notes, cigarette paper, Ramie fiber - stem fiber - table - cloths, towels, aprons, coarse dresses, twines, fishing nets, linen. In western countries - for sewing thread, parking, water proof products like canvas, fire hose, filter cloth, upholstery, fabric, sails, carpet backing, electric insulation, gas mantle, mixed with cotton, wool, rayon and artificial fiber to give anti shrink quality.

Ramie waste - from decortication and degumming operations - pulp for bank notes and cigarette paper.
Leaves stop of Ramie - fodder.
· Treated by a moist cross linking process, and a silicon - based softener, the fabric is reported to lose roughness and itchiness, and gains crease-resistance.

 


Chemistry:
Ramie meal is a good source of lysine - and methionine rich protein (21-21%), carotenoids, riboflavin and calcium, and may be made into flour for human consumption.

The seeds contain an oil which could be classed between a typical drying oil and a semi-drying oil. The oil contains a large quantity of free fatty acid and linoleic acid but is free of linolenic acid.

Processing Techniques:
Degumming - Degumming is carried out by the wearing mills by microbiological or chemical methods, some of which are patented. On a commercial scale, degmming is done by boiling the crude fire in caustic soda solution containing a reducing agent like sodium sulphite. Sometimes an oxidising agent is also needed to improve the colour of the fiber. Prior to degumming, the crude fiber is soaked for 24 hrs in water or wetting agents are added to the solution.

Pale creamy fiber is washed and bleached with chlorine and H2O2 to make it white. It is treated with a softening agent like soap, oil or glycerine and dried in the sun or in the open air. It is then pressed into bales for commercial use.

1) Decortication of fresh stem - by manual labour or by decorticator. Decortication of fresh stems produces better quality fiber. An indigenous decorticator, LASIS, recently developed by J.A.R.I. Barzackbore (W.B.) compares favourably well with 5-50 type Japanese decorticator.
2) Degumming of crude fiber - boiling crude fiber incaustic soda solution containing a reducing agent like sodium sulphite - bleaching of fiber - treatment with softening agent.
