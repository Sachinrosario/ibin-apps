Other Biological Details

HABIT & HABITAT

A large, deciduous tree indigenous to central and southern India. Found throughout Madhya Pradesh, in Broach and Panchmahal districts in Gujarath, some coastal districts in Andhra Pradesh, and Ganjam and Puri districts in Orissa.

Fast growing tree. Requires semi-arid to humid climate with annual rainfall above 600 mm. Grows on low altitudes but tolerates temperature from -4 to 400 C and withstands frost. Sandy loams are best suited. Cannot grow under waterloggerd conditions.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

A tree 60-80 ft. high, straight trunk, 60-80 cm in diameter with wide spreading habit. Bark light grey and smooth in young trees, with large leaf-scars, rough, granular and greyish brown in older trees. Leaves unequally or equally pinnate, usually 8-12 in. but sometimes reaching 2-3 ft. long, the younger tomentose, the older more or less so or glabrous. Leaflets 8-14 pairs, alternate or subopposite, very variable in shape, 4-6 in. long, coarsely and irregularly toothed or sublobate, very unequal at the base.

Flowers in large lax often much-branched panicles; pedicels long, slender. Calyx-lobes ovate-triangular. Petals ovate-lanceolate, glabrous, reflexed. Filaments glabrous, about half as long as the anthers. Samara lanceolate, acute at both the ends, reddish-brown, twisted near the base, many-nerved, the nerves reticulate above the seed, otherwise nearly parallel. Seed solitary on the centre of the samara.

Seed Germination & Propagation: The tree can be raised both from seeds and stumps, but entire transplanting is reported to give better results for its artificial regeneration. Excess of moisture should be avoided in the early stage.

Root cuttings can be used. The plant coppices well.

Papery winged fruits contain one or two seeds which cannot be separated easily. Therefore, sowing of entire pod is recommended. Pods can be soaked for 3 days before sowing to improve germination.

Seedlings are sensitive to frost and drought.

Seed germination starts in 10 days and is complete in about a month.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:

Wood used as fuel.

Wood: The wood is white, soft, light (sp. gr. 0.45; wt. 335-480 kg/cu m) but fairly strong. It is perishable in the open but not so in water.

Because of its lightness, it is used especially for making catamarans and small boats. It is also used for veneers and plywood packing-cases, sword handles, spear sheaths, toys and poor quality matchwood.

Easy bleaching pulp with satisfactory strength properties can be prepared under suitable conditions of digestion. It being short-fibred, admixture with long-fibred pulp, such as bamboo pulp, is necessary for the manufacture of paper on a commercial paper-machine.

Wood is used for matches.

Other uses:

Bark is medicinal.

The tree yields an inferior quality of Bassora or Hog gum.

Sericulture - It serves as one of the hosts for 'Eri" silkworms.

The leaves along with the twigs are found to be suitable fodder for cattle, sheep and goats.

Leaves are reported to be used as an adulterant of Adhatoda zeylanica.

Bark - Bark is medicinally important as a fabrifuge and tonic.
