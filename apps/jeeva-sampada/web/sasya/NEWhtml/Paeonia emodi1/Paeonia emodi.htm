HABIT & HABITAT                                                                                  
Herb, distributed in the Himalayas from Kashmir to Kaman at altitudes of 2,000-3,000 m.; it is also grown for ornament in gardens on the hills. The plant often occurs in gregarious patches and reported to be abundant in Liddar valley near Igam (Kashmir).

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
 Herbaceous or a shrubby stout, erect perennial with a cluster fleshy roots; Leaves divided into distinct leaflets: segments oblong lanceolate ; flowers showy, 7-10 cm. diam., white, red, usually solitary, sometimes in groups of 2 or follicles ovoid with a few seeds.

Uses

  2. The infusion of the dried flowers is remedy for diarrhea.

Other Uses
  1. The fleshy roots are used in uterine diseases, biliousness, dropsy and nervous Mions ; they are also prescribed as a blood purifier for children. Excessive doses cause headache, used vision and vomiting. 
  2. The seeds are emetic & cathartic. An infusion of the dried flowers is given to control diarrhoea.
  3. The roots of P. emodi are reported to contain an essential oil, with salicylaldehyde as the chief component, a fixed oil, benzole acid and sucrose. Tubers and seeds of an allied European species, P. officinalis Linn., contain a toxic alkaloid which produces contraction of the renal capillaries and increases the coagulability of blood; this species has medicinal uses similar to those of P. emodi. It is quite likely that P. emodi may contain a similar principle.
