DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
   Inflorescence: Large solitary ; Flower: White, wooly, bracteate, toothed ; Calyx: Tube clavate, lobes large, spreading; Corolla: Petals 5, contorted in bud ;Androecium: Stamens 20 ; Gynoecium:  Ovary 5-celled, styles 5, ovules many in each cell; Fruit:  Sub-globose, intruded at the base and apex firm, 5-celled; Seed: many, small, plano-convex . Flowering & Fruiting : January-March. Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.

UTILIZATION:

Chemistry:
Ursolic acid isolated from fruit and leaves. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)a.

Roseoside isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)b.

Mixtures of n-paraffins. (C23, C25, C27 and C29), alcohols (C22, C24, C26 and C28), b-sitosterol, saringosterol, uvaol, palmitic, oleic, linoleic and ursolic acids from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1970-1979)c.

Marmelolactones A and B isolated from fruits and their structures and absolute configuration assigned as (2R,4S)-2,7-dimethyl-4-hydroxy-5(E)-7-octadienoic acid lactone(fig. 1a) and (2R,4R)-2,7-dimethyl-4-hydroxy-5(E)-7-octadienoic acid lactone (fig. 1b) respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)a.

Trans- (fig. 2a) and cis-3-methyl-5-[(E)-3-methyl-1,3-butadien-1-yl] tetrahydrofuran (fig. 2b) along with sixty other compounds identified in essential oil of fruit. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1980-1984)b.

Three glucosides of procyanidin polymers isolated from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)a.

Synthesis of (+) and (-)marmelo lactones A and B. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)b.

Synthesis of marmelo oxide A (trans-3-methyl-5[(E)-3’-methyl-1’,3’-butadien-1’-yl] tetrahydrofuran and its cis-isomer, marmelo oxide B. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-1989)c.

4-Hydroxy-7,8-dihydro-b-ionone identified in fruit juice by GC/MS. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)a.

An unusual (4-O-methyl-D-glucurono)-D-xylan isolated from seed mucilage and partially characterized. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)b.

Isolation of quince oxepine(fig. 6) and isomeric quince oxepanes (fig. 7) from fruits; structure of former elucidated as (E)2,3,6,7-tetrahydro-4-methyl-2-(3-methylbuta-1,3-dienyl)oxepanes; their structures confirmed by synthesis. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)c.

Structure elucidation and synthesis of two new ionone-type compounds (I and II) (fig. 3 & 4). (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)d.

b-D-gentiobioside and b-D-glucoside of 3(R)-hydroxy-b-ionone isolated from fruits and their structures determined. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)e.

isolation and structure determination of b-D-gentiobioside of 3-hydroxy-b-ionol from fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)f.

(2E,4E)2,7-dimethoxyoctadien-1,8-diol, (2E,4E,7Z)2,7-dimethyldecatrien-1,10-diol and (4E,6E)2,7-dimethyl-8-hydroxyoctadienoic acid, three acyclic precursors of isomeric marmelo oxides, marmelo lactones and quince oxepine respectively, as well as three acyclic C10 compounds – 3(E)2,7-dimethylocten-1,8-diol, (2E,4E)2,7-dimethyl-8-hydroxyoctadienoic acid and (2E,4E)2,7-dimethyloctadiendioic acid – isolated from enzymatic hydrolysate of glycosidic fraction of fruits. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)g.

2,7-dimethyl-8-hydroxy-(4E,6E)-octadien-1-yl-b-D-glucopyranoside (fig. 5) as precursor of isomeric marmelo oxides, isolated from fruits and characterized as its pentaacetate. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)h.

Fruits afforded trans-abscisic alcohol-b-D-glucoside. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1990-1994)i.
