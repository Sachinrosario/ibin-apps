HABIT & HABITAT
Biennial herb, 1-1.5 m tall, branching from the base, scabrous. It is found in Jammu & Kashmir, W.Himalaya up to 1600-3000 m. Cultivated throughtout India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
 Leaves triangular to oblong in outline, 2-3 pinnatisect, oblong-lanceolate, segment incised-dentate, upper leaves linear lanceolate. Umbel with numerous rays, length contracted into a netlike form; bracts of the involucre 3-fid or pinnate, involucel linear, white margined, entire or 2-3 fid; central flower sterile, purple. Fruits 4 x 3 mm, including the prickles; prickles setaceous, as long as diameter of the seed or longer, with 1-3 recurved barbs. Carpophore undivided.
REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:   
      Inflorescence: Compound umbel; Flower: Bracteate, bracteolate, 3-fid, entire; Calyx: Teeth small, obsolete; Corolla: Petals obovate, emarginated, white ;Androecium: tamens 5, epigynous; Gynoecium: Ovary inferior, 2-celled, disc 2-lobed, style 2, stigma capitellate, ovule 1 in each cell; Fruit: Elliptic terete, dorsally compressed, carpophore ; Seed: Terete, dorsally compressed, inner face plane . 
Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting : October-March.
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:
Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind, birds or animals.  
 
Genetic variability:
The reported chromosome number - 18.

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:

Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil type:  Plant requires well drained loose loamy soil with pH 6-7.

Nutrient requirements: Well rotted cattle dung is added to the field and nitrogenous fertilizers for better yield.

Water management:   Plant requires irrigation after period of 10-15 days.                                    

Harvesting period / Method: Roots are harvested when they are young

CIMAP:  Changes in the sol. sugar content of carrot storage roots (cvs. Fontana, and Panther) at harvest and during storage were studies at an exptl. Site and on vegetable farms in 3 successive years. The later the harvest, the higher the content of sol. sugars, esp. sucrose, tended to be, but on farms in 1996, there was a clear decline in dry matter and sugar content due to frost injury in field expts. Even in uninjured carrot stands, changes in sugar content and compn. did not coincide with a cessation of yield increase or changes in storability. Total sugar and sucrose contents at the beginning of the harvest period were higher in the colder year, 1996, than in the warmer years 1995 and 1997. Despite some differences from earlier studies the compositional change obsd. here during storage followed the general pattern of increasing hexose and decreasing sucrose contents. Frost injury, however, resulted in post harvest changes in sugars which were different from earlier results.
