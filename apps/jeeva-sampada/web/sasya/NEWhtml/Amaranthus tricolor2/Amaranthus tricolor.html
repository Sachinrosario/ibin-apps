HABIT & HABITAT:
Annual, erect or ascending, usually much branched, up to 1.5 m tall tall. It is found through out India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:
Stem and branches angular, glabrous or thinly pubescent towards apex. Leaves variable, broadly ovate-rhomboid to ovate-elliptic or ovate-lanceolate, 1-8 x 1-3 cm, emarginated, obtuse or acute at apex, cuneate or attenuate at base, subentire or crenulate at margin, glabrous or thinly pubescent on nerves below; petioles up to 8 cm long. Flowers in more or less globose, 4-25 mm across clusters; clusters axillary or sometimes only lower axillary, upper often in terminal spikes; male and female flowers intermixed; bracts and bracteoles deltoid-ovate; bracteoles awned at apex, broadest near the base. Tepals 3, oblong-elliptic, 3-5 mm long, awned. Utricle ovoid-urceolate, 2.25-2.75 mm long, circumsessile, wrinkled. Seeds lentibular, 1.0-1.5 mm long, black or brown, shining.


REPRODUCTIVE BIOLOGY/ BREEDING BEHAVIOR:
Inflorescence: Dense axillary clusters and in terminal and axillary dense and interuppted spikes; Flower: Numerous, sessile, bracteolate  ; Male Flower: Perianth of 5  membranous, subequal segments, sepals 5, ovate, bristle pointed ; Androecium: Stamens 3, staminode 0, anthers 2-celled, filaments free ; Female flower:  Perianth-segments oblong, erect in fruit, sepals 5, oblong, obtuse, apiculate; Gynoecium: Ovary ovoid, compressed, 1-celled, ovule solitary, subsessile, erect, styles 3; Fruit:  Capsule ovoid, suddenly narrowed at the apex, circumscissile about in the middle, rugose, membranous, ; Seed: Lenticular, smooth, shining, black. 


Floral and seed biology:
Flowering & Fruiting:  September- November. 
Mode of pollination and seed dispersal:

Flowers are pollinated by insects. Seeds are dispersed by wind.

Genetic variability:
The plant has reported chromosome number 2n= 34

AGRO TECHNOLOGY:
Propagation: Plant is propagated through seeds.

Soil Type: Plant thrives well in sandy loams.

Irrigation: First irrigation is given just after the broadcasting the seeds. The next irrigation is given after 15-20 days and then at the interval of 10-15 days till the crop becomes 2-3 months old.

Nutrient requirements: 20-25 carloads of well rotten farmyard manure and the mixture of NPK fertilizers are also recommended.

Harvesting: Grains are harvested they are fully mature, foliage are cut periodically when leaves are crisp and green.

Economic Potential and Uses
UTILIZATION:
The plant is astringent and highly recommended in menorrhagia, diarrhoea, dysentery and haemorrhages from the bowels. Infusion of tender shoots is given to those having vitamin deficiency.


Chemistry:
Amaranthine (Fig.1) and isoamaranthine from leaves identified as -O-(b-D-glucopyranosyluronic acid)-5-O-b-D-glucopyranosides of betanidine  and isobetanidine respectively. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1960-1969). C.A. 1965, 62, 7853g.

Isolation of C25-35 alkanes, C24-32 aliphatic alcohols, cholesterol, campesterol, 24 methylene cholesterol, stigmasterol, sitosterol, fucosterol, 28-isofucosterol, proline, cysteine, tryptophan, phenylalanine, serine, glutamic acid, arginine, leucine, glucose and fructose. (Rastogi and Mehrotra, 1985-89). C.A. 1985, 103, 102046g.

The edible portions of kilkeerai (Amaranthus tricolor) were analysed for moisture, Calcium and total and soluble oxalates in weanling rats. (Pankaja and Prakash, 1994).

The purification and partial characterization of two Amaranthus lectins. One is from A. hypochondriacus, an edible species and other from A. tricolor an ornamental species. (Singh et al, 1994). C.A. 1995, 122, 99927e.

The foliage of 62 specimens of amaranthus belonging to ten species of grain type and four of vegetable type were analysed for V-C content. In the grain type of 62 (A. tricolor) to 209mg/100g (A. tricolor, A.V.-101) in the vegetable type. (Prakash et al, 1995). C.A. 1995, 123, 54523z.

Leaves were analysed for total and extractable calcium and zinc content. The Ca and Zn content of these leaves varied from 1320 to 2120 and 11.70 to 12.60mg/100g DM. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1995).

Ascorbic acid and b-carotene content in amaranthus leaves was investigated after home processing which results in Ascorbic acid content of fresh leaves was 624.1 to 629.0 mg and b-carotene content was 35.3 to 53.1mg/100g dry weight. The percent loss of Ascorbic acid ranged from 1.1 to 6.3 and 55.3 to 65.9 while lower losses (0.0 to 1.3 and 1.5 to 2.1) of b-carotene were observed in leaves stored in refrigerator and at 30degree C, respectively. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1995).

For all leafy vegetable lutein was the major carotenoid with b-carotene present at the substantial levels. Violaxanthin, neoxanthin, antheraxanthin and nintaxanthin were also important contributors in specific produce. (Wills and Rangga, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125,274138j.

V-C content was detected. It was observed that the green vegetable was richer in V-C then others. Friying for 10 min and subsequent 20 min caused loos of V-C. Which may due to rapid decomposition due to ascorbic acid. (Biswas and Mannan, 1996). C.A. 1997, 126, 6817p.

Green vegetables of the western region of India which were consumed frequently by the rural and tribal population were analysed for moisture content. Total carotene and b-carotene were also analysed by HPLC of A. tricolor with other green vegetables. (Nambiar and Seshadri, 1998). C.A. 1999, 130, 123996q.

An experiment was made to study the nutrient content with age of plant. Nutrient content were recorded at the age of 1mo 10 days, 1mo, 23 days, 2mo 6days, 2mo 19days for spinach. The protein, ash, fibre, Ca, P contents found to increases progressively with the increase in age in the plant from 2-4 month. (Shahidulla et al, 1999). C.A.73115z, 1999, 131.

Macroporous chloromethylated copolymers beads of styrene- divinylbenzene is reacted with glucose and Me amine, and then hydrogenated under the catalyst to get anion resin containing N-Me glucosyl amine. The adsorption and elution property of the resins to amaranth of Amaranthus tricolor were investigated also in the paper. Results showed that the adsorption efficiency of the resins to amaranth is 100% and the adsorption capacity is 14.6 mg/ml. The amaranth adsorbed by resins can be eluted with 0.1%- 1.0% HCl efficiently. (Shushen et al, 1999). C.A. 141580z, 1999, 131.

Pharmacology:
Components containing Celosia argentea, Amaranthus tricolor or other plant extracts are useful for the treatment of atopic dermatitis. The extract also can be incorporated into bath preparations and cosmetics. (Mitsuyama and Yoshino, 1996). C.A. 41843v, 1996, 125.
Ethnobotany:
Bronchitis, consumption, cough, dysentery (part not mentioned, Jain and Tarafder, 1970); Pain in joints (Seed, Vir et al, 1984)

 Any other information:
Edible as vegetable. (leaf, Bhargava, 1959; Stem, Leaf, Ramchandaran and Nair, 1981).

Amaranthin synthesis in Amaranthus tricolor seedlings is stimulated by kinetin in darkness. (Piattelli et al, 1971).

A review with number of references. The main achievements on red pigment in A. tricolor in material selection, technology process Physical and Chemical property, conserving and so on are summarized and its safety quality, and value and economic benefits are evaluated. (Huo and Guo, 1996). C.A. 1996, 125, 140725q.

The product prepared from the leaves of Amaranthus tricolor was a good source of iron and it could be included in the daily diets of Indians for improving their iron status. (Yadav and Sehgal, 1997). C.A. 1999, 130, 96017f.

The effects of cyclic 3’,5’-adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) on amaranthin synthesis in the dark or in presence of kinetin or light were investigated in isolated cotyledons of Amaranthus tricolor. (Nicola et al, 1975).

 The characterization and partial purification of an enzyme from Amaranthus tricolor which decolourizes betacyanin are described. The enzyme occours in greatest amounts in the roots and in 3.5 to 4-day-old seedings. Preperation from an acetone powder of roots results in more active and more stable enzyme than that obtained from crude buffer attraction. (Elliot et al, 1983).
