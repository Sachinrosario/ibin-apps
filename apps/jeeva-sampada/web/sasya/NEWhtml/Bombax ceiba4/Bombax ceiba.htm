HABIT & HABITAT:

The trees, in full bloom, present a striking blaze of colour and are grown in avenues. In peninsular India, the tree is very common in the dry as well as moist, mixed deciduous forests; in West Bengal and Assam, it is found in the mixed evergreen forests as well. It grows in the mixed deciduous forests in the sub-Himalayan region and lower valleys and is typical of the allevial Savannah type forests, tending to be gregarious near the river banks. It also occurs in sal (Shorea robusta Gaertn. F.) forests.

It is very common in the shabar tracts of U.P. and Bihar, especially in the open grazing, grounds in the forests. Some of the common associates of this tree are Acacia catechu Wild, Adhatoda zeylanica Medic., Albliza procera Benth., Butea monosperma (Lam.) Kuntze, Cassia fistula Linn., Dillenia pentagyna Roxb.

The tree prefers deep, sandy loam derived from granite but -shows maximum development in the deep, alluvial soil of the valleys. It grows well on well-drained hill stopes.

Grown in avenues; Common in dry as well as moist mixed deciduous forests, in West Bengal and Assam, also found in mixed evergreen forest, is a typical of alluvial - savannah - type forests, also occur in Sal (Shorea robusta Gaertn. f.) forests; common in bhabar tracts of U.P. and Bihar.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES:

- A lofty, deciduous tree buttressed at the base,
- 40m in height 86m or more in girth. Bole of 24-30 cm
- Branches horizontally spreading, more or less in whorls,
- Young stems and branches covered with stout, hard prickles,
- Bark pale ash to silver grey, smooth in the early years, later becoming rough, with irregular vertical cracks,
- Leaves large, spreading, glabrous, digitate, leaflets 5-7, lanceolate, 10-20 cm long;
- Flowers numerous, large, 10-13 cm, in diam., fleshy, bright cumson, yellow or orange, clustered at the end of braches.
- Bisexual, very rarely unisexual.
- Capsules oblong-ovoid, woody, 10-19 cm long.
- Seeds many, obovoid, smooth, 6-9 mm long, oily with dense silky hair.

Floral and seed biology:
Seed biology - many, obovoid, smooth with a brittle testa 6-9 mm long, oily, with dense silky hairs, dark brown.
Seedling germination :

The tree seeds in abundance and the seeds are widely dispersed by wind. Under favorable conditions, natural regeneration takes place of new alluvial, flat grounds, savannahs and other open spaces, and the seedlings establish themselves quickly. In N. India, it is one of the first tree to appear on alluvial ground.

The tree can be raised by (i) direct sowing (ii) entire transplanting, and (iii) stump-planting.

Propagation can also be facilitated through branch-cuttings though the rate of survival is poor and by budding.

Direct sowing is done with the onset of monsoon, in prepared mounds of loose, mineral soil, at a spacing of 3.7m x 3.7m, employing 3-6 seeds per mound.

The saplings for entire transplanting and stump planting are raised in the nursery. Seed is sown during May-June, without any pretreatment at a spacing of C5 cm. in lines which are 23 cm apart,

The tree is fast-growing requires little weeding after the first 1-2 years, depending upon the rate of growth, after which it over tops the weeds.

The tree should be grown preferably mixed with other spp. possessing foliage enough to suppress weeds and also to provide a soil-cover when silk cotton is completely leafless. The three is also raised with field crops under the agri-silvicultural systems.

Economic Potential and Uses

UTILIZATION:
It is eminently suited for afforestation of new ground and of grasslands in the riverian tracts. It is also useful in controlling soil erosion.

Timber - The sapwood and heartwood are generally not distinguishable. The wood is usually white or pale pink, turning pale yellowish brown exposure, is often streaked with sap stain or decay.

The wood is very light (wt. 400 kg/m3), very soft, perishable, straight grained and even 8 coarse textured.

It is easy to saw and work, finishing to a good surface, but the large open pores of the wood prevent it being used for polished works.

Floss -
The seeds are covered with silky cotton, which forms the kapok of commence. The fiber is slightly more brownish yellow than the true kapok from Java (Ceiba pentandra Gaertn.) and is straight. Indian kapok is light, buoyant, soft, strong, elastic, resilient, water repellent.

Length of fiber is 17.78 - 27.94 mm, dia 0-01524-0.03302 mm.

Timber - match industry; cheap grade, light plywood for tea - chests, packing cases, for canoes, shingles, loys, scaffards, musical instruments, pencils, penholders, frames, light cooperage, coffins, well-curbs, brush handles, as cushion for mine props, for inside partition of opium chests.

Wood wool (fine shawings of wood) - for packing.

Wood pulp - wrapping, writing sprinting paper.

Match factory wastes - production of cheap mechanical pulp for boards.
Floss - silk cotton fiber, stuffing life - belts, life sawing appliances, mattresses, cushions, pillows, upholstery, quilts, as insulating material for refrigerators sound proof covers and walls; for packing fragile goods; for making padded surgical dressings.

Manufacture of plushes, laces.

Flowers buds and fleshy calyces - as vegetable.
Dried flowers - powdered and made into bread with or without coin.
Paste of flowers as an application in cutaneous trouble young roots (Hindi - musla-semul, musti) - eaten raw or roas led.

Bark - as denulcent; emetic, tonic; aq extract mixed with curd is used to check blood - dysentery.

Semul gum or mocharus from bark - edible, used for dysentery, haemoptsis in pulmonary tuberculosis, influenza, menorrhagia.
Young fruits - as expectorant, stimulant, diuretic, beneficial in calculous affections, chronic inflammation and ulceration of the bladder and kidneys.

Employed in treatment of gonorrhoea, chronic cystitis and catarrhal affections. Hot, aq. Extract of seeds possesses moderate oxytocic activity. Seed oil - edible, can substitute cotton seed oil; used for soak making, as an illumination.

Seed cake - cattle feed.

Harvesting Practices:
Floss -
In India, floss is usually collected from the fruits which have fallen from the fruits which have fallen after ripening. For better quality floss fruits are collected directly from the trees before they are fully ripe and dried in the sun for 4-6 days. The floss is scooped out of the dry capsules and allowed to dry for 10-12 days.


Produce and Products:


Chemistry:
Back exudes a gum, known as seul gum or mocharus, from natural wounds. The purified gum contains 8.9% mineral matter and a considerable proportion of catechol tannin; on complete hydrolysis, it yields a mixture of L-arabenose, D-galactose, D-galacturonic acid and possibly ramnose (traces). The gum also contains tannic and gallic acids.

Processing Techniques:
For local use, the seeds form the floss are separated by means of indigenous appliances. But for export is machine cleaned and ginned.

The main factors taken into consideration in judging the quality of Rapok are its dryness, odour, colour, luster and refraction.
