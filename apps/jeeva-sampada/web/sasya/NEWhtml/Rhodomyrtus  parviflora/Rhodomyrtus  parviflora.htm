HABIT & HABITAT :
A small genus of shrubs or trees distributed in the Indo-Malaysian region, Japan and Australia, extending to the Pacific Islands. One species occurs in India.

DIAGNOSTIC FEATURES
A handsome, evergreen, tomemose shrub, 1.5-3 m. in height, found in the mountains of South India at altitudes above 1,500 m. The shrub grows on a variety of soils, thriving upon heavy loam or light sand, but when grown at low elevations or in the plains, at seldom bears fruit. It looks very attractive in flower and is grown in tropical and sub- tropical countries for ornament. Bark red, papery; leaves elliptic-oblong or obovate; flowers axillary, solitary or in 2-7-flowered cymes, rose-pink; berry sub-globose c. 12-5 mm.in diam.,crowned with persistent calyx, yellowish to purple; seeds many and compressed.

Seed Biology :

Seedling Germination
Artificial propagation is done by sowing fresh seeds in nursery beds of light soil, pricking out the seedlings when about 5 cm. high. The rate of growth is slow, annual increase in girth being about 1-6 cm. 
Cultivation:
Downy rose myrtle is cultivated for its ornamental and edible attributes.

Parts used:
Fruits ,Wood.

Uses

  1. Fruits are aromatic, sweet and palatable; those from the wild plants are of poor quality. They are eaten fresh and are also used in pies, jams and jellies, giving a flavour resembling that of apple-jelly.

Chemistry
Analysis of the fruit gave the following values : moisture, 82-5; protein, 0-6; fat, 0-2; carbohydrates, 10-7; fiber, 5-6; and mineral matter, 0-4%; calcium,40 mg.; phosphorus, 15 mg.; iron, 0-9 mg.; vitamin A, 74 I.U.; thiamine, 0-07 mg.; riboflavin, 0-04 mg.; and nicotinic acid, 0-3 mg./ 100 g. A decoction of roots and sometimes of leaves is reported to be used for treating diaorrohea and stomach- ache. In Indonesia, leaves are applied to wounds.

Other Uses
  1. Wood is reddish brown, close-grained, hard and heavy (wt., 897-1,040 kg./cu.m.). Though liable to split during seasoning, it is easy to work and presents a fine silver-grain in radial section. Since the wood is available only in small sizes, it is used for turnery, small articles and walking-sticks, and occasionally for cabinet-work and carving. Bark is poisonous. It contains 19 per cent tannin, but is of no commercial importance 
  2. Fruits are also used for the treatment of diarrohea and dysentery.
