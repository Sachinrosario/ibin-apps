External Morphology

 

Shape: color:The colour of this sponge varies from shades of pale yellow, buff, pale orange and brown. Typically it forms a massive fig shape, attached by a short, thick stalk to the substratum. Stalked, spherical sponge.

 

Max size and age: Dramatic contraction to about ¼ of its fully expanded size when disturbed.

 

Surface characteristics:The surface is smooth with a velvety feel. There is usually one compound oscule at the top.

 

Texture: Velvety .

 

Mucus production and smell: Smells faintly of freshly cut Laminaria stipe.

 Identifying Characteristics:

Stalked, spherical sponge. Velvety surface.The growth form of S.carnosus is variable. Although typically spheroid and anchored to the substratum by a short stalk, rather like a fig, it may also be encrusting or massive (growth vertical and lateral but unequal) and lobed, up to 15 cm in height. It is yellow to brown in colour. In the fig-shaped form, there is usually a single, apical osculum (a large surface pore, through which water leaves the body cavity), in massive lobed forms there may be several oscules but usually all on the uppermost surface. Surface is smooth and even. Contraction of the sponge is substantial when disturbed, with a corresponding change in consistency from soft to firm.The skeleton consists of spicules, tylostyles, of two distinct sizes. The largest constitute the main structural megascleres, whereas the smaller are perpendicularly arranged plumose brushes at the surface. The structure is subradiate, the radial arrangement of the spicules being most apparent near the surface. Internally the skeleton is confused and almost 'halichondriod' (like that of the Family Halichondrida, where the spicules of the endosomal skeleton are in disarray). In the stalk region the spicules are condensed to form an axial skeleton.
