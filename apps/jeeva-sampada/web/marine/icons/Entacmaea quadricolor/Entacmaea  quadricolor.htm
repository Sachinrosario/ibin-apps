Distribution

Maldives, Seychelles, Indian Ocean,St. François, St Josephs.

 Other countries: Western Australia (sharks bay), Torres strait ,Murray island, East Africa, Tanzania, Zanzibar, Solomon Islands, Santa Cruz Islands, Eritrea, Masaua, Egypt, Red Sea, near Suez,Tor, Pacific Ocean, American Samoa, Kiribati, Gilbert Islands, New Caledonia , Japan, Indonesia.

Ecosystem: reef ecosystem, symbiotically hosts Clownfish. 

Depth: usually 1-4 m;  some species: 0 - 183m.

Location:

  • Latitude: 4° 38' S

  • Longitude:55 ° 27' E

Key Identification Features: Bubble Tip Anemone exists at two levels in the water. The Anemone living deep inside the water, is green or brown in color, is solitary and has tentacles that are sticky or streaming. The other type of Bubble Tip Anemone, found at the water surface, where there is ample light, is colonial and has bulbous tentacles tips. Sea anemone have fishes that live within it. These anemone come in four colors . Entacmaea quadricolor  undergo asexual reproduction . A polyp can divide longitudinally, resulting in two, somewhat smaller individuals, probably within the space of a few days. Each then grows to an appropriate size, divides, and so on. Thenaria (Endomyaria). Column smooth or provided with projections in the form of verrucae, marginal spherules, pseudospherules or vesicles which never have macrobasic amastigophors. Sphincter absent or endodermal diffuse to circumscribed. Tentacles simple, arranged in cycles. Never more than one tentacle communicating with each endo- and exocoel. Mesenteries not divisible into macro- and microcnemes. Perfect pairs of mesenteries rarely six, as a rule more than six.

 

Museum Record:  Zoological Museum ,Hamburg. Museum of Zoology, Lund University. Date of observation:No later than 1981. Actinia Vas: No later than 1833

Characteristic feeding method

 

 

Bubble Tip Anemone derives its nutrition chiefly through photosynthesis which is performed by zooxanthellae, an acellular photosynthetic alga living symbiotically within

the anemone.

Sociability

 

Moderate aggression in sea anemone comparison  to the other saltwater invertebrates.Anemone fishes have complex social hierarchy that rules not only who is in charge of

Reproduction

Asexual:They undergo asexual reproduction. A polyp can divide longitudinally, resulting in two, somewhat smaller individuals, probably within the space of a few days. Each then grows to an appropriate size, divides, and so on.  

Sexual: All descendants of the original anemone (the result of sexual reproduction) form a clone, a group of genetically identical individuals. In this species, each polyp is relatively small, but clonemates remain next to one another so their tentacles are confluent, and the associated anemonefish apparently regard them as a single large anemone
