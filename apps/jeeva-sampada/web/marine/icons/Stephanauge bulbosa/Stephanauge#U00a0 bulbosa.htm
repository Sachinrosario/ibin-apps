Distribution

South West of Great Nicobar, Bay of Bengal.

Other countries: British towns (Natural Reserves)

 Ecosystem: often attached to gorgonians, sponges or worm-tubes.

Depth: 296 m

Location:

  • Latitude:6.90

  • Longitude:93.48 

Key Identification Features: Stephanauge with well developed base often elongated in the transversal axis, often attached to gorgonians, sponges or worm-tubes. Column rather low, smooth or with longitudinal rows of mesogloeal papillae in its distal part. Cinclides, if present, few, occurring only over the directive chambers. Sphincter well developed, mesogloeal. Tentacles possibly more numerous than the mesenteries at the base, or of the same number; fairly short, with or without mesogloeal thickenings at the base on their outer sides. Longitudinal muscles of tentacles ectodermal. Radial muscles of oral disc ectodermal to ecto-mesogloeal. 2 distinct siphonoglyphs. Perfect pairs of mesenteries 6-12. Retractors and parietobasilar muscles weak. Gonads on all stronger mesenteries except possibly the directives. Acontia few or perhaps sometimes absent. Cnidom: spirocysts, basitrichs, microbasic p-mastigophors.

  Museum Record: Humboldt University Museum. Date of Observation:No later than 1928

Nematocysts: Nematocysts of the acontia basitrichs only.

Mesenteries: Perfect pairs of mesenteries 6-12. Gonads on all stronger mesenteries except possibly the directives.

Retractor muscles: Retractors and parietobasilar muscles weak.

Parietal muscles:

Sphincter muscles:Sphincter well developed, mesogloeal. 

Oral disk:Radial muscles of oral disc ectodermal to ecto-mesogloeal.

Column: Column rather low, smooth or with longitudinal rows of mesogloeal papillae in its distal part. Cinclides, if present, few, occurring only over the directive chambers.

Tentacles: Tentacles possibly more numerous than the mesenteries at the base, or of the same number; fairly short, with or without mesogloeal thickenings at the base on their outer sides. Longitudinal muscles of tentacles ectodermal.

Pedal Disk: well developed base often elongated in the transversal axis.
