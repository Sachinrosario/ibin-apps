Diagnosis               : Head with 5 small gill slits, the last three behind pectoral fin origin; nostril close to front of snout, with short barbels and nasoral grooves connecting them with the mouth (Ref. 13).Very long caudal fin, almost as long as the rest of the body, with a deep subterminal notch but with the lower lobe hardly developed (Ref. 36). Yellow-brown with dark brown spots (Ref. 24), young black with yellow bars (Ref. 26). Adults with longitudinal skin ridges which are lacking in young (Ref. 24). Juveniles smaller than about 70 cm, markedly different; dark with white bars and spots; pale ventrally (Ref. 36

  Ease of identification  :   easy to distinguish from other species.

Biology     

A tropical inshore shark found on sand, rubble, or coral bottoms of the continental and insular shelves (Ref. 9). Recorded to have entered freshwater (Ref. 27). Rather sluggish at least during the day (Ref. 9). Probably nocturnal, feeds mainly on mollusks, but also small bony fishes (Ref. 16). Oviparous (Ref. 28). Slow-swimming and able to squirm into narrow cracks, crevices and channel in reefs while searching for food (Ref. 9). Readily kept in captivity (Ref. 9). May bite when provoked (Ref. 9). Utilized fresh and dried-salted for human consumption and also for fishmeal; livers processed for vitamins; fins dried for the oriental sharkfin trade; offal utilized for fishmeal (Ref. 9). Possibly reaching 354 cm TL (Ref. 29). Caught in drift net intended for sharks (Ref. 30). Reported from freshwater in the Philippines but needs to be confirmed (Ref. 31)
