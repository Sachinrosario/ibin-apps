 Distribution

                       Circumglobal. Indo-Pacific: Red Sea, Arabian Sea, Maldives (Ref. 9), Somalia, South Africa, Western Australia, China, Taiwan, Japan, New Caledonia, Hawaiian Islands and Tahiti. Eastern Pacific: Gulf of California and the Galapagos. Reliable records lacking partly due to its confusion with Alopias vulpinus.

                        Diagnosis  : A small thresher with moderately large eyes, a broadly convex forehead, a very narrow caudal tip, and straight, broad-tipped pectoral fins (Ref. 11). Upper lobe of caudal fin very long and strap-like, almost equal to length of rest of shark; lower lobe short but strong; terminal lobe very small (Ref. 12). Dark blue on back and sides, underside white; no white patch over pectoral fin bases (Ref. 11).

Vision

               

                            Max.Size        : 347 cm TL (male/unsexed; Ref.  13); 383 cm TL (female).

                            Max.Age        :  29 years.

         Biology : Primarily an oceanic species but sometimes close inshore (Ref. 10, 11); neritic to oceanic, 0-152 m (Ref. 14). Mesopelagic in the tropics; may enter atoll lagoons (Ref. 5). Stuns its prey with its tail, presumably feeding on small fishes and cephalopods (Ref. 2). Ovoviviparous, embryos feeding on yolk sac and other ova produced by the mother (Ref. 15). Sometimes caught by ski-boat anglers (Ref. 11). Utilized for human consumption, liver oil for vitamin extraction, hides for leather, and fins for shark-fin soup (Ref. 12).Maximum and common size of males estimated from discussion in Ref. 247. Adult females may reach at least 330 cm TL (Ref. 16).

Reproduction

                                  Mode                             :  dioecism

                                  Fertilization                   :  internal (oviduct)

                                 Spawning frequency       :        

                                 Reproductive guild         :  bearers, internal live bearers.

  Description of life cycle and mating behavior :  : Exhibit ovoviparity (aplacental viviparity), with embryos feeding on other ova produced by the mother (oophagy) after the yolk sac is absorbed (Ref. 15). Usually with at least two young (Ref. 2). Size at birth about 100 cm (Ref. 2). Distinct pairing with embrace (Ref. 20).
