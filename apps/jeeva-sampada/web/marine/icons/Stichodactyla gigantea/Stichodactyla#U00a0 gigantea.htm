Taxonomic Details

Scientific Name: Stichodactyla  gigantea 

Original bionem: Priapus   giganteus 

 Common Name: Giant carpet anemone

Valid Name: Stichodactyla gigantea (Forskål, 1775)

Distribution

Gujarat ,Okha , Mithapur coast, Gulf of Mannar, off Tuticorin, Arabian sea, Okha-Dwaraka area.

Other countries: Australia, Queensland, Torres strait, Great Barrier Reef, Indonesia, Japan, Micronesia, federated states of, New caledonia, Papua new guinea, Solomon islands.

Ecosystem:

Depth:

Location:

  • Latitude:: 22.42 , 8.80 , 22.42

  • Longitude:69.02 , 78.26 , 69.02 

Key Identification Features: Thenaria (Endomyaria) with well developed pedal disc. Column usually with verrucae. Sphincter diffuse to circumscribed, usually not very strong. Tentacles short, wart-like or longer, never very long. From all or some of the endocoels the tentacles arise in radial series; sometimes there is more than one row per endocoel, sometimes only one; rarely the radial arrangement is indistinct or absent. Only one tentacle communicates with each exocoel. Longitudinal muscles of tentacles and radial muscles of oral disc ectodermal. Pairs of perfect mesenteries numerous, all the stronger ones, with or without the directives, fertile. Retractors weaker or stronger, always diffuse. Cnidom: spirocysts, basitrichs, microbasic p-mastigophors.

Museum Record: Natural History Museum, Leiden. Date of observation: No later than 1993

Mesenteries: Pairs of perfect mesenteries numerous, all the stronger ones, with or without the directives, fertile.

Retractor muscles:Retractors weaker or stronger, always diffuse.

Parietal muscles:

Sphincter muscles: Sphincter diffuse to circumscribed, usually not very strong.

Oral disk: Radial muscles ectodermal.

Column: Column usually with verrucae.

Tentacles: Tentacles short, wart-like or longer, never very long. Tentacles arise in radial series. Only one tentacle communicates with each exocoel. Longitudinal muscles ectodermal. tentacles are numerous and only about a ½ inch (1.25 cm) long.

Pedal Disk: Well developed pedal disc.
