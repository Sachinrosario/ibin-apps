                        Diagnosis  : Fusiform body (Ref. 36). Head little and pointed (Ref. 36). Mouth large, extending mostly after the eye and has well developed teeth (Ref. 36). Teeth on shaft of vomer numerous and strongly developed (Ref. 39). Caudal fin with 18-19 rays (Ref. 40). Caudal peduncle thick and rounded (Ref. 36). Little scales (Ref. 36). Body is grey-blue colored with numerous spots, also below the lateral line (Ref. 36). Blackish colored on upper part of body, usually orange on sides, surrounded by pale halos.                           Max.Size    :  140 cm SL (male/unsexed; Ref. 42); max. published weight: 50.0 kg (Ref. 42); max.                                                   reported age: 38 years

                           Max.Age     : 38 years

                          Biology   :Spends 1 to 5 years in fresh water and 6 months to 5 years in salt water (Ref. 36). Matures in 3-4 years (Ref. 43). It reproduces in rivers (Ref. 44), normally more than one time (Ref. 36). It prefers cold, well-oxygenated upland waters although their tolerance limits are lower than those of rainbow trout and favors large streams in the mountainous areas with adequate cover in the form of submerged rocks, undercut banks, and overhanging vegetation (Ref. 45). Life history and spawning behavior is similar to the salmon Salmo salar (Ref. 36). Each female produces about 10.000 eggs (Ref. 42, Ref. 36). Mainly diurnal    (Ref. 42).Juveniles feed mainly on aquatic and terrestrial insects; adults on mollusks, crustaceans and small fish (Ref. 46, Ref. 36). Marketed fresh and smoked; eaten fried, broiled, boiled, cooked in microwave, and baked (Ref. 47)

Reproduction

                                  Mode                             :  dioecism

                                  Fertilization                   :   external

                                 Spawning frequency       :  One clear seasonal peak per year

                                 Reproductive guild         :  guarders, nesters

  Description of life cycle and mating behavior : Female covers the eggs by  restirring the sand and fine gravel (Ref. 55). After hatching at 12 mm, larval brown trout remain in the gravel for 2-3 weeks until they are about 25 mm long, when they emerge to begin feeding in the water column. Brown trout are territorial and begin establishing territories as juveniles. Juvenile trout from lake populations move from their natal inlets to lakes during the first 2 years of life (Ref. 3).

             Larvae : 

Larval Nursery System (Ref. 56)

 

Nursery System    : troughs or ponds

 

Details:             Abant trout larvae obtained from eggs of wild Abant trout broodstocks and rainbow trout larvae obtained from eggs of the cultured broodstocks at the station. The experiment was carried out in hatchery troughs of 200 x 40 x 30 cm and growing tanks of 100 L and lasted for 350 days. Total water volumes of the hatchery troughs and growing tanks were 30 L/min and 25 L/min, respectively. A total of 8 experimental groups in Abant and rainbow trout (4 experimental and 4 parallel groups in trials I and II) were set up. In both trials, a total of 1784 larvae were used.

 

Main food:        Larvae were fed pelleted diet containing 56% crude protein and 8% crude lipid.

Description of nutrient input: For large fish, an extruded granule diet containing about 47% crude protein and 8% crude lipid was fed twice a day by hand. Juveniles were fed ad libitum by hand, until they weighed 5 g. Fish were not fed at over 18°C, the proteins become inactivated, inhibiting metabolism.

 

Comments: This study compares the food consumption (FC) and feed conversion ratios (FCR) in Abant and rainbow trout in pond culture. At the end of day 350, although the mean wet weight of Abant trout was around 5 g, the maximum increase in weight was found in rainbow trout. The final mean wet weight of rainbow trout was between 116.33 and 173.42 g. The rearing period of abant trout is much longer than the rainbow trout because they do not accept commercial pellet food and require special environmental conditions. In conclusion, the desired level of growth performance of Abant trout was not obtained in pond culture. The growth, food consumption and FCR of rainbow trout were clearly better than those of Abant trout. Name used for this study was Salmo trutta abanticus.
