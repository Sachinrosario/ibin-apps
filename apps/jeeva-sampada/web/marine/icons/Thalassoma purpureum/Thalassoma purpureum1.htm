 Distribution

                   Indo-Pacific: Red Sea and East Africa (Ref. 6) to the Hawaiian, Marquesan, and Easter islands, north to southern Japan, south to Lord Howe, Kermadec, and Rapa islands. Southeast Atlantic: southeast coast of South Africa (Ref. 6). Replaced by Thalassoma virens in the Revillagigedo Islands (Ref. 10).

Descriptive characters:

       Body shape                                                         

                                        Lateral                         : elongated

                                        Cross section               : circular

                                        Dorsal head profile      :  more or less straight

                                        Type of eyes                 :  more or less normal

                                        Type of mouth/snout     :  more or less normal

                                       Position of mouth          : terminal

                        Diagnosis : T. purpureum and T. trilobatum have nearly identical initial phases (Ref. 10). They differ slightly in details of the head markings, and T. purpureum has a slightly longer head, shorter pectoral fins, and attains a larger size (Ref.10, 13). Females best distinguished by the 'V' mark on the snout (Ref. 13). Initial phase with a vertical dark red line below front of eye usually with a branch to front of snout (Ref. 12 ).

Vision                       

                          Max.Size    : 46.0 cm TL (male/unsexed; Ref. 14); max. published weight: 1,210 g (Ref. 15)

                           Max.Age     :

                          Biology   : Found almost exclusively in the surge zone of outer reef flats, reef margins, and rocky coastlines, down to a depth of about 10 m (Ref. 16). Occurs in groups of females that are spread out over large reef sections and dominated by few males. Males grow much larger than females (Ref. 13). Feeds on small invertebrates (crabs, sea urchins, brittlestars, mollusks), small fishes, echinoids, ophiuroids and polychaetes (Ref. 10). Protogynous (Ref. 17)
