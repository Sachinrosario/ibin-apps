Distribution

Widespread in temperate and tropical seas (Ref. 18). Western Atlantic: Canada to the Virgin Islands; Brazil to Argentina . Eastern Atlantic: British Isles to Côte d'Ivoire , including the Mediterranean . Indo-Pacific: South Africa to Sri Lanka ; southern Siberia to Viet Nam (Ref. 18); southern Australia , New Zealand , and Hawaii (Ref. 18). Eastern Pacific: northern California , USA to Chile .

Biology      

Occurs inshore and well offshore (Ref. 19), over continental and insular shelves (Ref. 3). Migrates northward in summer; young often in large aggregations of hundreds of individuals (Ref. 18). Prefers to feed on small sharks, skates and stingrays, but also preys on bony fishes, shrimps, crabs, barnacles and cephalopods (Ref. 3). Viviparous (Ref. 22). Regarded as being dangerous to people, though only few can be tentatively attributed to this species due to its occurrence in temperate waters (Ref. 3). Reported to cause poisoning (Ref. 23). Meat utilized fresh, dried-salted, and possibly smoked for human consumption; liver oil for vitamins, fins for soup, hide for leather, and carcasses for fishmeal (Ref. 3). Used in Chinese medicine (Ref. 24). Become sexually mature when 250 to 300 cm long. The female gives birth to 30 - 40 young (Ref. 20)
