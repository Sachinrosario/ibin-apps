 Distribution

                   Circumtropical in tropical seas. Western Atlantic: Nova Scotia, Canada to Argentina (Ref. 16). Eastern Atlantic: British Isles (rare vagrant) and Bay of Biscay to Angola, including the Mediterranean and Canary Islands. Eastern Pacific: Vancouver Island (British Columbia, Canada) to the Galapagos Islands (Ref. 17). Common throughout the Indian Ocean (Ref. 18).

Descriptive characters:

       Body shape

                                        Dorsal head profile      : more or less straight

                                        Type of eyes                 : more or less normal

                                        Type of mouth/snout     : more or less normal

                                        Position of mouth          : terminal

                        Diagnosis  : Body elongate, slender, and not strongly compressed; posterior end of upper jaw located at anterior rim of eye; dorsal fin with 4 or 5 spines followed by another spine and 25 to 29 soft rays (IV-V+I 25-29); lateral line without scutes; caudal peduncle with well developed fleshy keels and with dorsal and ventral peduncular fossae (Ref. 21). Body dark to pale bluish, with 6-7 broad, dark bars; white tips on caudal lobes and on second dorsal and anal lobes (Ref. 18).

Vision                       

                          Max.Size    : 70.0 cm TL (male/unsexed; Ref. 4)

                           Max.Age     :  

                          Biology   :  Oceanic species with a semi-obligate commensal relationship with sharks, rays, other bony fishes and turtles (Ref. 22). Young are usually associated with jellyfish and drifting seaweed (Ref.  17). Feeds on scraps of host's left over, parasites and excrement; also on small fishes and invertebrates (Ref. 30). Marketed fresh and salted or dried (Ref. 23). Captured at the surface using hand nets (Ref. 24).
