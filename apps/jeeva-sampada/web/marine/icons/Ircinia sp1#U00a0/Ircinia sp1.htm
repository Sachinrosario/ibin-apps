Habitat and Ecology

Ecosystem : It can typically be found on protected sediment and rock bottoms in the inshore and coastal waters. This species is also seen to a lesser degree on coral reefs surrounding the Island. found on reef seagrass beds .

Depth: 0.5 and 5 meters deep

Ecotoxicology: Physical or behavioural defences are rare amongst sponges. Thus, a few sponges seem to derive protection from the presence of sharp spicules (e.g. Tetilla) or of special cortical spicules providing a superficial armour (Geodia). Others live in crevices or holes, or are covered by epibiotic organisms which can ensure them an effective camouflage. As a consequence, we may expect that chemical defence should by far be the commonest mode of defence found amongst sponges. Indeed, many of them are toxic, especially in the tropics, to a wide range of organism or at least unpalatable to most predators.

 Identifying Characteristics:

Species of Ircinia display a wide range of forms, with an unarmoured, conulose surface, and a skeleton of primary and secondary fibres, and filaments. Primary fibres are cored with foreign debris, and form massive fascicles. Secondary fibres are simple and uncored. The consistency of these sponges is soft to firm, though they are extremely tough, and are often difficult to cut or tear.Order Keratosa is characterized by the presence of spongin fibers rather than calcium carbonate or silecious spicules. Family Spongiidae is characterized by having a skeleton composed of interwoven fibers, fairly homogenous in cross section, the incorporation of sand grains and other foreign material is common in this family (Parker 1982). The form of fibres, particularly the primary fibres, can be a useful character in identification e.g., the presence of massive fascicular primary fibres is a feature typical of Ircinia species. A simple primary fibre is essentially an undivided fibre, with secondary fibres branching off to interconnect with adjacent primary fibres
