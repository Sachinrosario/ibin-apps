SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  Head moderate, slightly distinct from neck; 1 pre and 2 postoculars; 2 anterior temporals, placed one above the other; 7-8 supralabials, the 2nd in contact with the prefrontal, the 3rd, 4th and 5th, or only two of them, in contact with the eye; 4 lower labials.  Eye small in the adult.  Body rather cylindrical anteriorly and compressed poseteriorly; scale imbricate throughout, with a central keel or row of tubercles, arranged in from 37 to 47 rows at the midbody; ventrals 290-390, distinct throughout; preanals much enlarged as in most members of tghe genus Hydrophis; many of the ventrals are fragmented, making an accurate count difficult.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The young are olivaceous or yellowish in colour, with a lightcoloured horseshoe - shaped mark on top of the head, and a pattern of black crossbands on the back, which is much variable as follows: in some, the bands are broader and encircle the body completely; in others, the bands are complete and broadest dorsally and ventrally, sometimes leaving a colourless area on the flanks; in some others, the bands taper to a point on the sides.  In addition, a black stripe is present ventrally.  The characteristic pattern of black crossbands as well as the mark on the head disappear with age.

Additional remarks:  The annulated sea snake is a widely distributed species, found from the Persian Gulf to the Idju Sea of Japan southward to Sri Lanka and the islands of Indonesia.  Rare south of the equator.

This species frequent the shallow, muddy mangrove swamps during the monsoon season.  It is reported to crawl on land without much effort and while doing so, an adult can lift its head upto 10cm or less above the surface.  The snake readily bites if restrained.

The venom -  yield of this sea snake is small and less toic than that of the hooknosed sea snake (Enhydrina schistosus).  However, this sea snake is considered a dangerous species as the recorded deaths due to its bite indicate.

The female is said to produce from three to fifteen live young at a time.
