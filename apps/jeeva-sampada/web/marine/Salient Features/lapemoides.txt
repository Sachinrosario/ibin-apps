Morphological:  Head and eye moderate, like that of the Annulated sea snake (H.cyanocinctus); 1 pre - and 2-3 postoculars; 2 04 3 anterior temporals and the same number posteriorly; 8 supralabials, the 2nd usually in contact with the prefrontal, the 3rd and 4th or 3rd to 5th touching the eye, the last 3-4 small; 4 lowerlabials in contact with the genials, which are fairly well developed and in contact with one another or the posterior pair separated; a series of small scales at the oral margin around the 2nd lower labial.  Body robust (in the adult), not markedly elongate; scales feebly imbricate on neck, juxtaposed on posterior half of body with a central tubercle; scale rows ar midbody 43-51; ventrals 314-372, distinct throughout, twice as broad as the adjacent dorsal scales, less so posteriorly; preanals five, moderately enlarged.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The young are yellowish or whitish in colour, with 33-43 dark bands, usually strongly dilated in the vertebral region but becoming less evident and narrow on sides; these blackish bands may not encircle the body and may turn paler or greenish in clour, and may disappear completely on the underside of the body as age advances.  The head is yellow, with a yellow curved mark, which may or may not persist in the adult.  The adults are pale gray, shading to white laterally and ventrally.

Additional remarks:  The Persian Gulft Sea Snake, as the common name suggests, is confined to the waters of the Persian Gulf, being found occasionally on the coasts of India and Sri Lanka.  On the whole, it is extremely rare and known from a few examples.  Murthy during his studies on sea snakes of the east coast (1985-1987), has identified one example contained in the hydrophiid collections of ZSI, Berhampore as belonging to this species.

The sea snake, whcih is sometimes confused with Hydrophis ornatus, can be distinguished by the scale formula of the body, shape of scales and the presence of markings on the head.

The slender juveniles of this sea snake seem to attain their maximum length before they become heavy - bodied adults.
