
SALIENT  FEATURES :
Morphological:     

Rostrum short, sharply pointed, less uptilted, bearing 3 dorsal teeth and a minute pterygostomian spine. No ventral spines present; antennal and hepatic spines well developed. Basial and ischial spines absent on 1st pereopod; dorsomedian spine present only on 6th ab.seg. and a small anal spine present; pereopods1-3 chelate and 4 -5 without exopods. Uropods well developed; exopod with 17-19 plumose setae. Telson bearing 7+7 setae; no median spine present.Diagnostic characteristics:Rostrum very short; thoracic legs without or with vestigial exopods; dorsomedian spine only on 6th abdominal segment.                  

Sex attributes:  Sex separate (heterosexual).Descriptive characters : Rostrum straight, latterly flattened and short, reaching hardly ¼ the length of the eyes; bearing 2/3 dorsal teeth in post larva II. Epigastric spine present in addition to rostral teeth; pleopods uniramous and exopods bearing setae.

Feeding habit: Pelagic zone 
Main food:  Live and dead planktonic organisms; detritus,organic matter with mud and sand
Feeding type: Omnivorous.

Size and age: 
Postlarvae I: length 3.2-3.4mm
Protozoea III: length 2.03 mm
Juveniles: length 10-12 cm.


Eggs: Opaque, a diameter of 0.25-0.27mm; hatch out after 15-17 hours. LARVAL STAGES:6 naupliar stages; 3 protozoeae; 5 mysis and a postlarva are  detailed.Nauplii I-VI: length 0.27- 0.36mm; A naupliar eye present in all. Antennule uniramous, antenna and mandibles  biramous. Caudal furca bear 1+1 to 7+7 setae in stages 1-6. The number of setae on A1 and A2 increases as the stage advances. The number of setae on the exopods and endopods of mandible remains 3 throughout. The tip of the 4th seta of antennal exopod is bifid  from 2nd  stage onwards.rotozoea I-III: length 0.78-2.03mm.Carapace rounded anteriorly with a median notch; frontal organs overhung by frontal horns; ocellus persists. Furcal lobes  with 7+7 setae. Antennule 3segmented,proximal segment subdivided into 5 segments. Mandible asymmetrical  in protozoea II. Rostrum prominent, eyes stalked. In protozoea III, rostrum long and prominent; abdominal segments 1-5 with posterodorsal spines; 5th also bears a pair of posterolateral spines and 6th segment with a pair of ventrolateral spines. Uropods biramous, telson  furcal  lobe with 7 setae.Mysis I-V: length 2.4mm-3.3mm.Mysis I, The rostrum is devoid of dorsal tooth; extends beyond eyes; antennal and pterygostomian spines present; dorsomedian spines present only on abdominal segments 5-6; no trace of pleopopds; telson with a deep median cleft and 7+7 setae.Mysis II: Rostrum with one dorsal tooth; a small hepatic spine present; pleopods appear in very minute,bud-like stage; telson cleft reduced;  other characters remain unchanged.Mysis III: Hepatic spine prominent,other spines remain as well; telson cleft reduced to the level of posterior end; pleopods present in the form of small buds, unsegmented.Mysis IV: rostrum with a dorsal tooth and the epigastric tooth; other spines on carapace remain same; thoracic legs with exopods; pleopods 2-segmented; telson truncate posteriorly.Mysis V: Rostrum reduced in length; thoracic legs 1-3 chelate; pleopods more developed without setae; dorsomedian spines on abdominal segmens 5-6 remain; telson truncate with 7+7 setae.Post larva: length 3.4mm; Rostrum bears 2 dorsal teeth + epigastric spine; very short and sharply pointed; the mandibular palp is bigger than the mandible; thoracic legs 1-3 well chelate; exopods reduced and absent on legs 4 -5; pleopods uniramous and setose; telson convex in shape posteriorly with 7+7 spines. Characteristics:  The supraorbital spine is reduced or absent; antennal, pterygostomian and hepatic spines present on carapace; the reduction in the length of rostrum and exopods from thoracic legs; endopods 5-segmented; abdominal dorsal median spines only on 5th and 6th segments; pleopods uniramous and telson squarish with 7+7 setae.Abundance: It contributes a major share in the prawn fisheries of  Bombay and Saurashtra regions in the Indian Ocean;  also an abundant species and available in the northwest, northeast and southeast coasts of India.
