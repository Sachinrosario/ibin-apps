Salient Features:
Morphological:

Body surface of adult male (4.2mm) (Fig1.A) rough, covered with short and few slightly long setae. Cephalon anterior margin strongly carinated, entire lateral sides of pereon and pleotelson (Fig. 1B) fringed with fine setae. Pleotelson apex (Fig. 1c) with an elongated notch, which is subtriangular when seen ventrally (Fig. 1D). Dorslly (Fig. 1C) each lateral side of the notch extends medially with a row of minute denticles. Anterior side of the notch covered by the small hanging distal projection of pleotelson.

Penes(Fig.1E) long fused at base, tapering slightly before bifurcation, then becoming parellel sided for a short distance, and again tapering to acute apices, broad portion bearing marginal spinules.

Pleopod2 (Fig. 1F) with appendix masculina long, reflected anteriorly, proximal portion broad, lateral surface bearing scales, reflected portion narrow, tapering to acute apex. Pleopod 3 expod without articulation, that of pleopod 4 with complete articulation.

Uropod (Fig. 1A,D) large thick, extending well beyond the apex of pleotelson endopod; flat, distal margin truncate with short setae, lateral margin slightly concave; exopod entire, lateral margin strongly deflected medially, bearing short setae on the outer surface and margins.

Sexual dimorphism distinct. Ovigerous female (3.1mm) generally smaller than adult male. Pleotelson(Fig 2.A) with dome bearing weak longitudinal depression, tubercles and setae, apex with simple elliptical notch, having no dorsal denticles. Uropod rami thin with fine marginal steae, not extending as far as pleotelson tip, exopod shorter than endopod, with subcircular apices.

Pereonites 2-4 bearing mambranous oostegites at base of each pereopod, right 3-4 oostegites overlapping the left ones in the midline.

Non-ovigerous female (2.5-3.2 mm)same as ovigerous female but with brood pouch.

Body surface similar to that of adult male. Pleopod 3 exopod like adult male, without subterminal articulation, pleopod 4 exopod with complete articulation.

The cephalon is comparitively larger with prominent eyes. The first peraeon segment is longer than all others. The coaxial plate an almost completely fused with the peraeon segments and their outer margin is fringed with fine hairs.

The first peduncular segment of first antenna is as long as the rest of the peduncle (Fig. 3A), the flagellum is formed of nine or ten or thirteen segments. The fifth peduncular segment of second antenna is the longest, The flagellum is eighteen to 20 segmented (Fig.3D). The epistome is triangular with a subtruncate apex, the arms are stout and enclose a very small part of the upper lip. (Fig.3E). The insicor process of the mandible is four dentate, the accesory lobe is three- dentate, the molar is toothed and the spine row consists of five or six stout spines, the second segments of the palpof the mandibble carries four setae and the third segment a comb of setae (Fig. 3F,G). The outer THe outer lobe of the first maxilla carries six blunt spines and four sharp barked spines, the inner lobe outer and one spine(Fig.3H).

The inner lobe of the extended maxilla has seven or eight strongly barbed seate, each of the other two lobes has three stout spines and several setae, the segments of the palp are prouched inwards.

The first pereopod is short its 4th segment has a stout outer spine seta, the inner border of segments 3 to 6  is hairy but lacks lunchy hairs. The seventh peraeopod is long and dendes, with bushy hairs on segments 3 to 6 the outer distal part of the fifth segment carries two larger barbed spines.(Fig. 3K).

The exopod is one segmented, that of the fourth is two segmented (fig. 3O,P). The exopod of the fifth pleopod has five spiny thickened areas of which one is placed in the middle of the inner border (Fig. 3Q). The rami of uropods are armed with spines and setae, the distal border of the endopod is subtruncate, the exopod is charecteristically folded length wise.

The body is white with a greyish things due to the presence of dark chromatophores.

Additional Remarks: 

Pillai (1954) described the new species dynoides amblysinus based on a subadult male specimen collected from vizhinjam. Subsequently he in 1965 created the new genus dynoidella and transfered this species to three new genus. Harrisson and holdrich 1984 described a new species , namely clianella brucei, which was actually based on adult specimen of dynoidella amblysinus (Pillai). Therefore, according to javed and youseph (1995). The correct species name should be clianella aamblysinus (Pillai). According to pillai 1965, the telsonof subadult male, is broad based triangle, its proximal part  turned and delocated  into a pair odf bylgrings more closely packed.

Pleotelsonic notch is broadly open and simple in ammature makes almost similar to female but as the given males grow, the the notch deepens dorsally lateral sides of the notch extend medially develop marginal teeth which gradually come closer to each other. In adult male the margin are so close that the notch appears closed posteriorly.

Sexual dimorphism is verydistinct in this speciesOvigerous females are smaller than adult male and their pleostelson with the dome bearing weak longitudinal depression tubercles and setae and with pleotelson apex with a simple elliptical notch, having no dorsal denticles. Uropod rami thin with fine marginal setae, not extending as far as pleotelson tip, exopod shorter than endopod, with subcircular species.
Pleoptelson 2-4 bearing membraneous oostegites overlapping the left ones in the middle.

The holotype is deposited in the indian meuseum kolkatta.
