Morphological
  Diagnostic characteristics: Body elongate and strongly compressed, its greatest depth less than or equal to head length. Lower arm of the preoperculam prominently projecting backwards as much longer process than the upper arm. Jaw teeth moderately compressed, those in the lower jaw longer. Gill-rakers 1-4 on upper arm, 6 to 9 on lower arm of the first arch. Two dorsal fins, first dorsal fin with 15-17 spines, second fin with 19-23 soft rays, followed by 8-9 finlets. Anal fin with 20-22 soft rays, followed by 8-10 finlets. Lateral line without a prominent dip under the second dorsal fin, with auxillary branches off the anterior part of the lateral line. Intestine with two folds and three distinct limbs.

Colour: Back of the body iridescent blue, sides below lateral line silvery; usually 3 irregular rows of dark round spots (equal in size to eye) along the sides of the body. Spinous dorsalfin black up to the eight dorsal spine, and the rest white and tipped with black

Feeding habit:    Surface feeder, carnivore
  Main food: The spotted seer (S.guttatus) is essentially a surface feeder. The fish feeds on a limited number of about five species of which sardines are the most important and whitebaits the next. The shifts from whitebait which constitute the exclusive diet of the young ones to sardines which form the major food of the adults takes place at about 315 mm, about ¼ th the asymptotic length. The important food items encountered are the sardines, Anchoviella, Carangids, Prawns and Loligo                Ref.No. 15,18,32

  Feeding type: Carnivore

Additional remarks: This fish is far less aggressive than S.commerson. and its other cousins, as is evident from its inability to compete ably in times of food shortage. There are two active feeding a day, one between 7 and 10 p.m and the other between 6 and 10 a.m. Food intake does not slacken in the maturing or ripe fish and was generally high from September to March. Young fishes less than 300 mm length, distributed in the near shore areas were better than the adults in grounds beyond the 20 m depth line. Young spotted seer (say 1 to 3 year old) consume more food per unit weight than old fish (say 4 year old), but the former require much less food to produce a unit weight growth than the latter.

Eggs and larvae                Ref.No. 24
Large numbers of late larval and juvenile specimens of Scomberomorous guttatus are caught in shore seines during February to May along the south Kerala coast and the specimens described in this account are mostly from Vizhinjam near Trivandrum. Juvenile specimens from Madras and Waltair were also examined in the course of this study and no significant differences were noticed between them. (Ref: Jones, 1962)
S.guttatus showed 48-49 vertebrae + urostyle. (S.commerson has 43-44 + urostyle).
  Description as given by Jones (1962) is given below (Ref.No. 24).

  14.8 mm stage (All lengths given are standard length)

The head is 2.7 and height 4.4 in standard length. Most of the larval characters are lost except the pre-opercular spines of which the upper most is the largest. Teeth are small than those in the corresponding stage in S.commerson. All fins have their full complement of rays and spines but the membranes connection between the finlets still persists.The general colour is white in formalin and chromatophores are distributed as shown in figure. On the head they are present in patches at the tip of the snout, above and below the orbitals, above the region of the hind brain and on the posterior region of the opercle.

  41.2 mm. Stage

Head 3.3 and height 4.7 in standard length. The pre-opercular spines have become smaller and inconspicuous. The lateral line could be made out up to the vertical below the third dorsal finlet. The chromatophores have appreciably increased in number and appear as continuous band, but not quite uniform, from the head to the caudal base. Lateral band of chromatophores have merged with the dorsal band on each side of the body, those at he base of the anal fin and finlets have disappeared.
