SALIENT FEATURES :

Morphological :Body long elliptical in the female, with the segments laterally overlapping and not appearing deeply incised. Body of the male narrower in the anterior part and slowly widening backwards. In juveniles the body is perfectly parallel sided, with segments deeply incised laterally as illustrated by Stebbing (1900). Apparently Stebbings illustration is that of a juvenile.

Head with a prominent truncated rostrum and two deep concavities on either side. The small first segment of pleon only partially visible in adults, but distinct in juveniles. In a few specimens, more often females, telson is almost circular, with the postero-median part bulged. In males the telson is more elongated.

Uropods almost half as long as telson or even slightly shorter. Endopod longer than exopod, occassonally and viceversa. The small scale on third segment of antennal peduncle totally absent. Upper lip bilobed, with long setae. Inner lobe of first maxilla apically produced into a setose process with four large plumose setae. Outer lobe with twelve strong spines, serrate on iner border. Maxilliped with one or rarely two coupling hooks and the segments of palp only moderately expanded, gradually increasing in length towards the apex. Sixth segment of all peraeopods produced into a conspicuous process, not of the first alone as previously observed; unguis clearly trifid. First pleopod of male with its penduncular parts united and gradually widening distalwards. Rami not united, their inner margin setose. Outer border of second pleopod hairy throughout; stylet narrowing towards the apex and not overreaching the ramus.

Body opaque white or transluscent, turning cream yellow in spirit.

Additional Remarks:

IAIS PUBESCENS is found in assosiation with wood-boring SPHAEROMATIDS and its Ecology has been described by Barnard (1914). In the backwaters of Travancore it is found in assosiation with Sphaeroma Terebrans and Sphaeroma Annandalei. Upto one to nine specimens were found along with the host. (Pillai, 1958). These small ISOPODS are always found Huddled together under the SPHAEROMATID.

Sexual Dimorphism observed by Barnard was not noticeable in the specimens examined by Pillai (1958). Menzies and Barnard (1951) include IAIS PUBESLNS under “species inquirende” and express the opinion that the specimens described under this name my not be IAIS at all.
