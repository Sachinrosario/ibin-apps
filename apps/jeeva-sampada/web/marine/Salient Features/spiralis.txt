Morphological:  Head rather large and slightly distinct from neck; one preocular; 1 or 2 postoculars; 1 (anterior) +2 (posterior) temporals; 7 supralabials, the 3rd and 4th touching the eye; 4 lower labials in contact with the genials, which are well developed and in contact with one another; occasional specimens with a small scale at the oral margin of the 3rd and 4th lower labial.  Body slender and compressed; scales smooth or feebly keeled posteriorly, smooth or each one with a central tubercle or short keel, in 33-39 rows at midbody; ventrals 295-362, twice as broad as the adjacent scales; preanals 2-5, considerbly enlarged.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The ground clour is golden yellow to yellow to yellowish green, encircled by 44-48 more or less complete dark rings which are widest at the midbody, becoming narrower on flanks and ventrally; the scales in the ligh areas are margined with black; the scales in the light areas are margined with back, the belly both in the juvenile and the adult is yellowish; tail banded and with an irregular black patch near the tip.  The head is uniformly yellow in the adult, blackish, with a yellow horseshoe - shaped mark in the young, which in addition may have a blak line down the middle.

Additional remarks:  The yellow sea snake, which can be readily identified by its size (large unusual adults grow to a length of 2.5 metres or nearly 3 metres) and its characteristic colour - yellowish to yellowish green, with complete black rings crossing the body.  Unlike some other sea snakes which inhabit the mangrove creeks and swamps, the yellow sea snake is rarely or not reported from such situations.  This species, like most other kinds of sea snakes, loves to come to the surface, probably with an inclination to bask in the sunlight.  Murthy (1977) has collected specimens of this species well off the coastal areas of Chennai.

The venom of yellow sea snake is said to be as toxic as that of the annulated sea snake.  H.Cyanocinctus.  The global range of this sea snake is from the Persian Gulf to the coasts of India to Celebes and Philippines.  The gravid females, usually encountered in January and Jund, are reported contain 5 to 15 young in one brood.
