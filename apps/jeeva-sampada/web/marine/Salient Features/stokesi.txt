Morphological:  Head large, shields entire and regular, arranged as follows : on pre and 2 postoculars; 2-3 anterior temporals; 8-10 supralabials, the 2nd and sometimes the 3rd, in contact with the prefrontal, the 4th - 6th touching the eye; 10-12 infralabials; no distinct genials.  Nostrils superior, nasal shields in contact with one another.  Body stout and covered with strongly imbricate, pointed and keeled scales, keels often broken up into tubercles; the scales on the forebody sometimes with dentate tips; 37-47 scales on the neck, and 47-59 on the body.  Ventrals 226-286, in two halves except a few of which being entire anteriorly; preanals strongly enlarged.

Diagnostic characteritics:  This sea snake is easily distinguished by its size, the strongly imbricate body scales and divided belly plates.  It is brownish or yellowish - brown to orange-red above, with a dorsal pattern of 24-36 broad, dark brown or blak bands or with dorsal bars and ventral spots; the dorsal bars are sometimes interrupted by spots or narrow bars.  head dark olive to yellowish.  This sea snake, considered the largest if not the longest of the sea snakes, is involved in the largest massing together of sea snakes reported by Naturalists.

Additional remarks:  Sea snakes are air-breathing, highly venomous reptiles closely allied to the land-living cobras and kraits.  They are characterised by a body more or less compressed posteriorly and a strongly compressed paddle-shaped tail.  In daddition to the positioning of the nostrils on the top of the head, sea snakes exhibit special adaptations needed in a marine environment:  all have salt glands to get rid the body of excess salt and their right lung is extensile and is used for absorptionof oxygen and also as a hydrostatic organ which enables the sea snakes to remain submerged for several hours if occasion so demands.  The large belly scales, whose function is minimal under water, are greatly reduced or absent.  Sea snakes are often confused with the eels.  However, the dry scaly covering of the sea snake distinguishes it from an eel which has a smooth, slimy skin besides fins and a gill pouch in the area of the neck.  Sea snakes feed almost exclusively on fish.  Some 50 kinds, included in two subfamilies viz.  the Hydrophriinae containing the entirely naquatic species (36) and the Laticaudinae including a few species (14), which spend a good deal of their time on land, where they feed as well as breed.  The longest sea snake measures 2.75 metres.  Majority of the sea snakes mate at sea and produce live young but a few (i.e. Laticaudinae) lay eggs.  Sea snakes inhabit mainly the tropical Pacific and Indian Oceans.  Sea snakes, common in Indian waters, are known by 20 species.

All sea snakes are highly venomous, the venom of some of the members of the subfamily Hydrophiinae being considerably more powrful than that of the cobra.
