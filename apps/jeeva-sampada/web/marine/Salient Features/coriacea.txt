SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  The only sea turtle that is devoid of an outer covering of horny shields and which instead, has a leathery skin.  Shell torpedo - like. Snout blunt; upper jaw with two strongly marked cusps.  Neck not retractile.  Limbs modified as flippers and clawless, the anterior pair being much larger than the posterior.  Tail short.

Diagnostic characteritics:  Young blackish above, while the adults are predominantly dark brown or black above, spotted with white or pale yellow.  Plastron paler.  Some bones of the dermal mosaic enlarged, forming twelve longitudinal ridges, seven on the carapace and five on the plastron.

Additional remarks:  The Leatherback is rare everywhere probably because it leads a truly pelagic life, diving at the least sign of danger, and coming onto the land only for the sake of breeding.  Using its huge front flippers, the animal seems to roam the seas far and wide.  The leatherback used to frequent the outskirts of Tangasseri reef, off the coast of Quilon, Kerala in the early 1900s in good number but has become a rare visitor now.

The female lays from 90 to 130 eggs in about 3 to 4 times a year.  Incubation takes about two months.

The flesh of the leatherback is not edible but the eggs are considered a delicacy when flesh.  The oil, extracted fom the carapace, is used as a lubricant for the wooden boats.  The skin is also of good value.
