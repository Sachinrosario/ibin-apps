Morphological:  Head flattened, distinct from neck and covered with large symmetrical shields; snout long; nostrils large, directed upwards and positioned high on the snout; anterior and posterior lower labials with pits, which are cavities richly supplied with blood vessels and neves and act as accessory organs of the snake.  Eye moderate, with a vertically elliptic pupil.  Body massive, somewhat compressed and covered with smooth scales.  Tail moderate to short, highly prehensile. Rudimentary hindimbs (claw - like spurs on either side of the vent) present; these vestigial organs are highly developed in the males than in the females.

Diagnostic characteritics:   The Indian Python is grey or yellowish - brown above with a net work of dorsal and lateral series of elongate, large, rhomboidal dark - edged patches; head with a distinct spearhead - shaped dark mark; belly dirty whitish to yellow with black cpots (on the ventral scales); underside of tail marbled with orange and black.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Carnivorous.

Main food:  Mice, birds, and mammals.

Feeding type:  Constriction, that is coiling around the prey and squeezing it to death.

Additional remarks:  Pythons are sluggish snakes, generally most active at night and during day time.  They do not move from their resting places unless provoked or are awfully hungry.  They are good climbers and quite at home in water.  The limit of a huge python's swallowing powers would be a big boar or a leopard.  Instances of pythons attacking humans are rare.  Pythons do not attack deliberately nor become aggressive unless provoked.  A six-metre long python, if it choose to be aggressive, can easily knock a man off his feet by coiling about his legs and possibly could kill him by retaining its vice-like grip on him.

Pythons reproduce by laying eggs, sometimes as many as 107.  Th female (Indian python displays unusual maternal instinct by remaining coiled around the eggs for the entire period of incubation (i.e. 60 to 80 days).

Pythons are, among the few snakes, eaten particularly in some pockets of northeastern India.  The snake's flesh is said to be 'sweet'.  The ornamental skin of pythons is in great demand.

The Indian python is an endangered species and is now a strictly protected snake.
