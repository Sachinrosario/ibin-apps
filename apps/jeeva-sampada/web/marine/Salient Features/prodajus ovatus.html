SALIENT FEATURES :
Morphological :

Female:

Body much swollen due to the presence of masses of large eggs. General shape oblong, with the posterior part narrow and drawn out. Dorso-median part of the body with a broad longitudinal ridge. Cephalon deflexed towards the ventral side. Cephalic mass somewhat, circular with its posterior border prominently cleft. Buccal mass with three distinct lobes, probably formed of the hypopharynx and the maxillipeds, directed backwards. Legs five pairs, crowded together on either side of the buccal mass. Lateral parts of the peraeon considerably enlarged, produced anteriorly into two rounded lobes which reach far beyond the cephalon and meeting along the median line and posteriorly produced into two narrow lamellar processes reaching beyond the apex of the badomen. Two pairs of brood lamellae present, the posterior pair merging into the posterior prolongation of the peraeon. Pleon very narrow and five jointed, distal segment bifid. Pleopods absent.


Male:

Usually found attached to the side of the hosts abdomen, between the posterior prolongations of the peraeon and the pleon. Body curved. Cephalon somewhat semicircular and partially fused with the first thoracic somite. Antennules rudimentary, antennae long and five jointed, distal segment with a very long seta. Hypostome conical and directed forwards. All the peraeon segments visible, seventh longest. Seven pairs of peraeopods present, the length and thickness of the legs successively increasing from first to the seventh. Pleon six segmented, about half as long as the peraeon and narrowing towards the tip, sixth segment with a pair of uropods. Pleopods appearing as small buds.

Body with small dark patches as shown in the illustrations.

Additional Remarks:

Prodajus ovatus closely resembles Dajus afromysidies Pillai, but could be easily distinguished from the latter by the five jointed pleon and the greatly enlarged antero-lateral lobes of the peraeon.

The host, Gastrosaccus muticus is planktonic.
