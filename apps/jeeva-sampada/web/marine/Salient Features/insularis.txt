Morphological: This Skink is rather long, worm-like, with a slender
cylindrical body and a short tail. No limbs. The head is small and dorsoventrally
flattened and the snout is wedge-shaped by means pf which the lizard digs 
tunnels in loose earth with ease. The eyes are small but functional; 
the upper eyelid is vestigial and the lower one is the scaly and movable.
The ear-opening is minute. The head is covered with shields and the body
with smooth scales. The tounge is long, slender and bifid and is short out at
frequent intervals. The tail is cylindrical and rounded at the tip. The blunt
snout and the rounded tip of this skink are the source of its Oriya name.
"Deemundia" which means 'double-headed'.

Diagnostic Characteristics: The rare limbless skink is usually glossy brown above 
with a central black dot on each scale which fuse to form 8 to 14 longitudinal
lones down the back and along the tail. The belly is coloured white.
Top of head clouded with brown.

Feeding Habit : Insectivorous
Main Food : A Variety of small, mostly soil-living arthropods such as the 
termites and the beetle larvae.

Additional Remarks: The limbless skink, which has adapted mostly fossorial
existence, is a highly specializedlizard for living in the surface substrata.
The type specimen was dug up from the loose earth among roots of a banyan
tree on the barkuda Island of the Chilka Lake. This skink is a very agile 
crature as it burrows with great rapidity in loose earth. It seems to be a
night-active reptile which is why it is rarely seen. Apart from the mearge
Knowledge that it is oviparous,little is known of the breeding habits 
of this skink.

The borrowing limbless skink is a rare and unique member of the Family
Scincidae. It seems to prefer a coastal environment, where it is found 
inhabiting the loose sandy soil near the coast or on the Islands off the 
estuaries.
