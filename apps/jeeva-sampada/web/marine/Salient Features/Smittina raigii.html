SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Zoarium often large, encrusting. Young zooecia with uniform small tubercles, the marginal areolae small. Spines 2-4. Peristomes raised laterally into a pair of acute or rounded cusps limiting a sinus of variable width, often narrow. Lyrula moderate or wide, often with a minute transverse tooth at each distal corner. Lateral avicularia sometimes large, raised, acute; the rostrum long and narrow, directed proximally or distally; or smaller, wanting on many zooecia which have no ovicells. Small rounded lateral oral avicularia rather long and narrow, or expanding distally, rarely enlarged, rounded not gigantic. Avicularia common along the ovicells, the acute type variable in size, often large. Ovicells small, globose, with minute pores, becoming immersed, the entooecium generally not overgrown by the ectooecium.
Diagnostic characters: The scarcity of avicularia in the young unilaminar condition is a marked character of S.raigii. The secondary orifices are typically sinuate but are often widely open; both conditions occurring in the same colony.
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters: Many of the specimens have remained in the juvenile unilaminar condition; the zooecia being usually elongate, convex, with irregularly arranged small tubercles and 2 spines or their bases. The peristomes are generally bimucronate, the cusps sometimes triangular and acute. They are often rounded and may be wanting, particularly in old or superposed specimens; but even in these they can sometimes be found in a few zooecia. When well developed their median borders limit a peristomial sinus which may be a long slit, extending nearly to the base of the peristome.
The superposed zooecia are, as usual, irregular in their orientation and thus differ markedly from those of the unilaminar parts. They may be smaller, their peristomes less obviously bimuronate. Triangular avicularia, large or small, with inconsistent orientation, are found on the zooecia or among the ovicells; and are sometimes numerous. These are large, thick, multilaminar encrustations. Their surface is raised into short chimney-like elevations composed of several layers of zooecia and each with a large central opening indicating that this modification is due to the presence of a commensal. The avicularia are exceptionally numerous; some very large, resembling those of typical S. raigii, acute or slightly rounded distally. The ovicells are small and globose, with not much overgrowth of the ectooecium, as in typical specimens of that species, from which these specimens differ, however, in usually having paired avicularia, directed proximally, one being generally larger than the other.
