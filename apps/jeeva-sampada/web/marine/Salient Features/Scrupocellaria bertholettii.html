SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Branching zoarium erect, whitish. Somewhat elongate zooecia, with opesia occupying more than half of the front. The number of spines vary greatly. Usually three external and two internal. Scutum present, may be a simple spine or a forked one. Frontal avicularia present small or very large. In the large ones, the rostrum hooked. Vibracular chamber not triangular, a proximo-laterally placed rhizoidal chamber. Axial vibracula single. Joints traverse the opesia of the outer zooecia. Cryptocyst present, not tuberculated. Ovicells large with perforations placed at the tip of tubercles. Diagnostic characters: The important character which separates the present species from S. panamensis is the presence of giant frontal avicularia. In the absence of both scuta and frontal avicularia S. inermis distinguishes itself from S. bertholetti. S. varians which appears to be rather close to S. bertholetti can be distinguished on the basis of the occurrence of imperforate ovicells.
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters:   A study of this species from the available literature shows that characters like the number of spines and the nature of the scuta show considerable variations. A detailed examination of the present material also reveals that both the number of spines as well as the nature of scuta do exhibit variations. Thus the spine number in the present specimens ranges from 2 to 5. A median spine is found in a number of zooecia, a feature which, however, has not been shown in Osburn's figures. The scutum may vary from a simple spine overarching the median portion of the opesia to a forked structure. It is interesting to note that Osburn in his material found the scutum to vary from a "single spine curving over the opesia to a triple-forked structure with 6 or more sharp points" (Osburn, 1950). It is also noticed in the present instance that a few zooecia did not possess scuta at all. It is not impossible that the scuta might have been lost owing to rough handling. It is noteworthy that all these variations noted in the shape of the scutum are a common feature in this species. Frontal avicularia are noticed in almost all zooecia and occasionally the size of these avicularia are found to assume giant nature with mandibles strongly bent at the tips.
