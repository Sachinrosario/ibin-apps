SALIENT FEATURES:Morphological:  All colonies collected were encrusting on molluscan shells.  Zooecia were broadest in the middle and slightly tapering both ways.  Gymnocyst not discernible.  Cryptocyst extensive proximally, extending about a third of the distance towards the distal end and less developed laterally.  Cryptocyast steep and hence the opesia very much depressed and occupies the anterior half.  Opesia more or less triangular, or egg-shaped.  Vicarious, paired, slightly raised avicularia present with thin pointed portions directing distally and occupying the lateral position of zooecia.  Large vicarious avicularia present with a very enlarged rostrum and an extensive cryptocyst proximally.

Descriptive characters:   	The distal ends of the zooecia are slightly pointed in the specimens collected by Menon, whereas in Silen's figures they are shown as rounded.  The slight crenulation seen at the distal edge of the cryptocyst seen in the specimen of Menon is not shown in Silen's figure.  Majority of the zooids possess two, rarely one anteriorly directed vicarious avicularia which are invariably placed at the junction of adjacent zooecia.  A large vicarious avicularium with a well developed proximal cryptocyst and a nearly rounded rostrum noticed by Menon is a variation from that observed by Silen.  Silen's (1941) modified description based on the adventitious avicularia that "Distally to all or most autozooids one single or a pair of laterally arranged, small, pointed avicualria, which in some (or all?) species are vicarious", seems to be applicable in all cases.  Menon and Mawatari observed the specimens to be encrusting on shells whereas Silen found his specimens growing erect.  Antropora erecta is described as "erect, branching and has the structure of a cylinder surrounding a cavity, which is open at the ends of the branches".   However, the erect nature might probably be owing to the fact that the substratum on which the specimen encrusted, might have been destroyed later, leaving an erect colony.
