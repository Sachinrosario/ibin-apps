Descriptive characters:  Valleys are of indefinite length, sinuous or radiating and are very wide (averaging 35 mm) with; 8-10 septa per cm.  These vary greatly in thickness from approximately 1 mm to very fine lamellae, but are not arranged in distinct orders.  Principal septa are thick and have very coarse dentations.  The number of dentations increases as the width of the septa decreases.  Columellae are arranged in two rows, longitudinally connected by 2-6 evenly spaced, well developed, lamellar linkages.  One or two lamellar linkages may adjoin adjacent centres within valleys, otherwise they are completely separated by lamellar linkages running along the valley centre.  Columellae are seldom well developed and consist largely of the inner margins of septa twisted together, usually in a clockwise direction.  The endotheca is vesicular and well developed.  Living colonies are uniform brown, green or red or are composed of two colours.  They are readily recognized underwater (Veron & Pichon, 1979, p. 293).

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

IMPORTANCE: Reef builder.  Source of CaCO3 Curio

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
