Descriptive characters:  Colonies composed of vertical and irregular leaves, each being no more than 5 cm tall.  Leaves are always thin, not more than 4-5 mm thick, and the basal parts of a high proportion of leaves are dead and sometimes actually detached from the jumble of older fronds of the colony.  Septa are less than 1 mm in diameter and lie flush with the surface of the leaf.  They run in rows parallel to the edge of the leaf.  Rows are separated by 3 or 4mm.

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

Additional remarks:  P. cactus is separated from P. decussata in the presence of thinner fronds that are lobed at the summit.  Calices are smaller in P. cactus, further carinae are not developed.  P. formosa (Dana) could be skeletal variant.  The type of P. formosa has broader (5 cm) fronds than in P. catus.  VAUGHAN’s pl. 56, figs. 1, 1 a shows formosa, which he puts to cactus.

IMPORTANCE: Reef builder.  Source of CaCO3  Curio.

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
