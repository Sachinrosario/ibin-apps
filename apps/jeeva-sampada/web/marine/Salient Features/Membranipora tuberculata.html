Morphological: The zoarium forms a white lace-work on Sargassum floating over the wide oceans and occurs on attached algae along shore in warmer waters. So popularly known as “Gulfweed” bryozoan. Zooecia variable in proportions, basal wall uncalcified. Operculum not quite distal. A pair of strong, rounded tubercles at the proximal end of each zooecium. Cryptocyst little developed in young zooecia, more extensive in the older state, rather distantly tuberculated. Rosette-plates two lateral, multiporous; two terminal or a single row of septula at least twelve in number, across the entire width of the wall, close to its basal edge.

Diagnostic characteristics:   Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite
Descriptive characters: The zooecia are quadrangular, but shorter and wider than in M. membranacea and the walls are more heavily calcified. Characteristically there is a pair of tubercles at the distal corners, which appear to be formed as folds of the distal rim as they are open and covered by membrane on the distal side. They are very variable, frequently fusing across the end of the zooecium and occasionally they are cornuate, extending forward with the points curved toward each other, and more rarely a third tubercle is developed between these in the midline. A short gymnocyst is sometimes present and the cryptocyst with lateral spinules is usually well developed at the proximal end.
Waters (1898) had also stated that the lateral rosette-plates are 4-6 (i.e., 2-3 on the distal half of the wall), and that septula were numerous on the terminal wall. Harmer also reported the same in the Siboga specimens. In the Siboga specimen examined by Harmer as well as in the specimens examined by Thornely (1908), the tubercles did not completely close at its base distally where its membrane remained continuous with the frontal membrane. The tubercles were for the most part distinct, without uniting into a single mass. Ovicells were constantly absent.
