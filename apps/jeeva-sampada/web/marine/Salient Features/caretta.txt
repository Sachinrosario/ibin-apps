SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  Distinguished from other sea turtles by its large, massive head (which gives the turtle its common name), broad, short neck and  the complete ossification of the carapace in the adult.  Jaws very strong and hooked with a prominent ridge on the upper beak.  Head not retractile. Flippers with a pair of claws or one in the adult.  Tail extremely short.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The general colour of the shell is reddish brown above and yellowish below.  In the very young specimens, it is uniform dark brown or blakish above and below.

Sex attributes:  Males have longer tails, longer claws, with rather a broader carapace and shorter plastra than females.

Descriptive characters:  

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Carnivorous

Main food:  Marine plants, molluscs, sponges sea urchins, marine plants and fish.

Feeding type :  

Additional remarks:  This turle seems to be rare in the coastal watters (east and west) directly surronding India and, in fact, is considered rare throughout its range.  
The female comes ashore at night and digs a large hle in the sand above the high water mark, in which leathery eggs, from 25 to 170 in number are laid.  The process may be repeated several times in a nesting season.  The eggs hatch out in approximately two months and the hatchlings head toward the sea after emerging.
Though the flesh of the loggerhead is not edible, its eggs are much relished and sought after.  The shell can be put to good use in the preparation of crude ornaments.  This sea turtle is said to be nasty in disposition as it can deliver bites with its formidable jaws.
