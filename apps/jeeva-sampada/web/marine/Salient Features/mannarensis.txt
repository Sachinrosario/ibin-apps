Descriptive characters:  Corallum massive, globular or hemispherical, surface lobulate.  The colonies are found unattached on the reef.  They start their initial growth on a branch of Acropora and as the Porites grows the substratum gets broken leaving the coral free.  In most cases the substratum is partly retained in the Porites colony which project out.  In some cases the colony expands after breaking away attaining a hemispherical form.  The lobulations on the surface 2 to 3 mm thick and high with valleys in between.  Calices neatly rounded, average 1 mm in diameter, sometimes only 0.75 mm, moderately deep.  Some of the calices at the extreme edge of the colony are angular.  Wall porous and reticular, thin or slightly thickened, in the latter case the mural denticles coarse.  Septal sides smooth in young calices, granular in old ones.  The ventral triplet of the septa is without a trident formation.  Inter septal loculi wider than the septa.  Septal denticles absent in the calices located at the top of the corallum, though visible at the lower parts of the colony.  They always stand below the level of the pali.  Outer synapticular ring near the wall, inner below the palar crown.  Pali in full compliment, i.e. eight those of the lateral pairs of septa very prominent.  Columella a deep seated style, jointed to the septal ends by radii.

Living coral as well as washed and cleaned coralla deep brown.


Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.


Additional remarks:  It was common in Manauli Island before large scale quarrying was done.

IMPORTANCE: Reef builder.  Raw material for CaCO3. 

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
