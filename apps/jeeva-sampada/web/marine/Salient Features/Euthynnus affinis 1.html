SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:
  Body fairly robust, elongate and fusiform. Body squamation confined to an anterior well-developed corselet and lateral line. Teeth in jaws uniserial, minute and conical; present or absent on vomer and palatines; upper surface of tongue with two longitudinal cartilaginous ridges. Two dorsal fins, more or less continuous or barely separated by only a narrow interspace; first dorsal fin with 11 to 14 spines, second dorsal with 12 to 15 soft rays, followed by 6 to 8 finlets. Two flaps (interpelvic process) between pelvic fins. Caudal peduncle with two small keels and another between them on either side. Gill-rakers well developed on first arch. Several characteristic black spots often between pelvic and pectoral fin.

Diagnostic characteristics:                Ref.No.17

  Body robust, elongate and fusiform. Gill-rakers 29 to 34 on first arch (in Euthynnus yaito there are 43 gill-rakers). Two dorsal fins, narrowly separated; first dorsal fin with 14 spines, second dorsal fin with 11-14 soft rays, followed by 8-10 fin lets. Anal fin with 12-15 soft rays, followed by 6-8 fin lets. Pectoral fin short, its tip not extending to interspace between dorsal fins. Caudal peduncle very slender, with a prominent lateral keel between two smaller keels at base of caudal fin.

  Colour: Back of the body bluish black, with complicated stripped pattern. Dorsal to the lateral line and behind the corselet there are a number of blue-black broken wavy lines directed backwards and upwards. Lower sides and belly silvery; several characteristic small dark spots (up to 7or 8) below level of pectoral fin. Pelvic fin blue-black, exception inner edges of lighter hue.

The stomach examination of juvenile and adult specimens of E.affinis of Vizhinjam along the west coast of India revealed mollusks (squids, cuttle fish and pteropods), fish (anchovies, silver bellies, carangids etc.) and crustaceans (stomatopod larvae, penaeid prawns, phyllosoma larvae and larvae of other decapod species). Specimens obtained from Tirunelveli in the east coast of India too revealed essentially the same food items category-wise but with dominance of euphosids and penaeid prawns.

There is no information on the embryonic or larval stages of E.affinis from Indian waters.

However, there are in all 82 specimens measuring 24.5 mm to 321.5 mm recorded from various areas. Of these 54 specimens are below 75 mm, all of which are from Vizhinjam except three which are from Calicut. Of the remaining larger specimens only five are from Vizhinjam and the rest from Calicut.

Biochemical aspects:

Proximate analysis: moisture/fat/protein/carbohydrate/ash     Ref.No.23

Euthynnus affinis is a coastal species of red meat tuna. The red and white meat tunas show distinct differences in their biochemical compositions. Moisture, protein, non-protein, nitrogen, sarcoplasmic proteins, free amino nitrogen, lactic acid, inorganic phosphorus and ribose are more in white meat tunas, while salt soluble protein, glycogen, fat, pigments etc are more in the red meat form as E.affinis. Concentration of haemoglobin and myoglobin are about five times more in red meat than in white meat. The red meat that is about 11% of the whole weight of the fish is accumulated more in the middle portion (12.8%) compared to the head (10.5%) or tail (9.5%) portions
