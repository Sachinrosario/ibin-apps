SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Zoarium erect, branching and escharan. Zooecia with regularly tuberculated convex front, arranged in longitudinal rows alternately. Zooecia separated by deep grooves. Marginal pores present. Key-hole shaped orifice with slightly narrow sinus. Peristome very low and smooth. Operculum with strong marginal sclerite. Avicularia often paired, placed on swollen projections one of which occupies the lateral sub-oral position and the other one lateral distal or parallel to the orifice. Lateral avicularium with slightly rounded rostrum and with triangular mandible, the sub-oral one with slightly pointed rostrum and long setiform mandible projecting beyond the tip of the rostrum. In both the avicularia, the calcareous bar complete. Globose perforated ovicells, with small tubercles, placed above the operculum. No rootlets noticed. Diagnostic characters: Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters:    This form agrees fairly well with the figures and description of Cleidochasma biavicularium (Canu & Bassler) given by Harmer (1957). Few differences are, however, noticed. The present collection contains only fragments from the erect portion of the colony. Therefore, no rootlet could be observed. The position of the lateral avicularium in the present material is a little above the position of the same shown by Harmer. The marginal pores are comparatively larger here. Single row of pores was observed on either side of the swollen portion of the sub-oral avicularium. This is not shown in Harmer's figure. The porous nature of the ovicell found in the form collected by Menon seems to be different from what Harmer has observed in his material for he has neither stated about this in the text nor shown in the figure.
  Another feature is about the dimension of the fragments. As no complete zoarium was obtained by Menon, the dimensions of only fragments could be given here. Even the biggest fragment measured 14mm long and 4mm wide. This shows that the complete zoarium should have been definitely larger than what Harmer (1957) has observed in his collection. He has stated "the largest zoarium (57.W) is 10mm long, its greatest width 1mm". Obliquely transverse row of zooecia was observed in the regions of branching.
