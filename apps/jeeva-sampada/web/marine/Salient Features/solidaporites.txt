Descriptive characters:  The following description is based on several specimens studied both in the laboratory and in the field from several localities from the Indian Ocean.  Corallum massive, surface rising into gibbosities or undulate.  Larger colonies in Lakshadweep were as much as 1 m in diameter.  Calices polygonal, 1.25 to 2 mm in greater length (average 1.5 mm) deep.  In some cases, especially at the top of the colonies the calices look rounded due to the thickening of the intercorallite wall.  Wall generally thin, sometimes thickened, with compressed frosted mural denticles.  Septa start a little below the level of the summit of the wall, the triplet remain free without forming a trident.  Septal edges with one or two denticles, the outer one near the wall.  The inner one larger than the outer and form a ring outer to the ring of pali.  Septal side smooth, interseptal loculi wider than the septa.  Pali generally poorly developed in polygonal calices.  Generally six small pali only slightly developed.  Solumella styliform joined to the fused ends of septa by radii.  Living coral, brown, dull green or pinkish.


Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.


Additional remarks:  This is a major reef builder and is used for industrial purposes.

IMPORTANCE: Reef builder.  Raw material for CaCO3. 

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
