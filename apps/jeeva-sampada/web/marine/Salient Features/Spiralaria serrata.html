SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Encrusting, unilaminar raised to form frills at certain portions of the colonies. Elongated, rectangular, distinct, moderately calcified zooecia arranged in longitudinal rows quincuncially. The definite and clearly serrated mural rims devoid of spines. Two tubercles present, placed at the distal lateral corners of the zooecia. Operculum membraniporine. Avicularia inter-zooecial with a moderately sized rectangular frontal region. The elongated, disto-laterally directed rostrum extends to the lateral sides of the zooecia, mandible elongated, resulting in width distally, with a curved tip. Ovicells entozooecial, sub-globular, non-porous and smooth.Diagnostic characters:   Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite   Descriptive characters:   The important differences noticed in the present form from Spiralaria serrata given by Silen (1941) and Mawatari (1952) are the absence of spines, serrated mural rim, thick lateral walls, and the presence of two distal tubercles.
  The spine-like projections are absent in the present form and Silen (1941) also did not find them in his material. In the nature of the avicularia and the ovicells, the present form closely resembles the figure and description given by Silen.
  The avicularia in the present specimen is exactly like those Mawatari found in his specimens. Ovicells are noticed in many zooecia and these are entozooecial occupying the proximal region of the succeeding zooecia. The presence of tubercles at the disto-lateral angle seems to be a feature not noticed in this species by the previous workers. If the presence or absence of spines is a character of specific importance, as may be inferred from the description given by Okada and Mawatari (1936); Silen (1941) and Mawatari (1952) and on the basis of the characters shown by this material, the present form may prove to be one different from S. serrata (Mac Gillivray).

Feeding type: Suspension feederAdditional remarks: In the areas of union of two portions of the colony some interesting features have been noticed. It may be seen from the figure that the position of the operculum of a zooecium which evidently is formed partly from one part of the colony and partly from the other, has changed. The development of the operculum is inhibited in one of the zooecia at the region of the union. The region of union can be distinguished easily, as this part will be raised from the substratum, and the zooecia of the two regions demarcated by vague thickening. It is noteworthy that the union of two regions of the same colony can affect the orientation of the operculum and eventually the disposition of the polypide, and naturally budding has been inhibited resulting in a cessation of growth in that particular region.
