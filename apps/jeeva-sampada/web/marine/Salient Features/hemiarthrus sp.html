Salient Features:

Morphological:
Female :
Body highly asymmetrical whitish with almost entire dorsal surface of thorax coloured dark brown.
The head is deeply set in the thorax and is somewhat longer than broad. Its posterior margin is regularly rounded. Eyes seem to be totally absent.

The segmentation of the thorax totally obliterated on the longer side of the body while that on the other is faintly indicated. The segments are very closely crowded together. All the seven legs are present on the shorter side each having the usual structure. The last four legs of this side are altogether absent. The oostegites of one side are highly developed and as usual enclose those on the other side.
The abdomen is proportionately large and though the lines of demarcation cannot be clearly made out, seems to consist of the usual number of segments. The four pairs of pleopods show a considerable development and look like those of H.abdominals. The pleopods of the longer side are markedly larger than those of the other. The outer rami of both sides are in the form of broad thin lamellae spreading out on the sides. The inner rami are proportionately much reduced and those of the longer side are moderately broad while those of the other are narrow, especially towards the proximal ends. Besides these four pairs of pleopods the ultimate segment is also provided with appendages. These are small biramous lamellae looking from their position and structure very much like uropods. They stand out from the posterior end of the animal, one on each side of the body and each consists of two small leaf-like plates, the outer of which, like that in the pleopods is larger than the inner.
Male not known.

Diagnostic Characteristics:
Highly assymetrical body typical of the genus Hemiarthrus. A pair of Biramous appendages (Uropods ?) present on last abdominal segment of adult female. Only one thoracic leg present on longer side of the body.

Feeding Habit :
Abdominal Paasite.
Additional Remarks:
Chopra (1927) coukd not find any leg on the longer side of the body, but he observes that the first leg must originally have been present and in all probability must have lost but due to a mechanical injury of any sort. The presence of well developed Biramous Appendages on the last abdominal segment in the female has not been hithertho recorded in any species of Hemiarthrus. Because of this the generic placement of the specimen is questionable. However since the specimen closely resembled Hemiarthus Abdominalis, Chopra 1923 retained his specimen under Hemiarthrus without assigning a specific name.
