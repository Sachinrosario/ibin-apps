SALIENT  FEATURES :
Morphological:                                 

Diagnostic characteristics:      

Female: Body short and robust; separation between pedigerous segment I and cephalon distinct; dorsal eye lenses feebly developed; rostrum long and pointed; pedigerous segment V: posterior margin lobate with pointed tips; urosome two-segmented, genital segment longer than anal segment; segmentation between right caudal rami and anal segment indistinct; caudal rami symmetrical; Antenna I with 17 joints, extending to caudal rami; leg 5: symmetrical; Ri ending in acute apically bifid processes; Re uniramous, armed distally with two elongated setae  and a median serrated flagellum; terminal segment with one outer marginal seta; basipod II with elongated marginal seta.     

Male: Prosome similar to that of female; dorsal eye lenses close togerher; posterior marginal lobes  of pedigerous segment V rounded, with minute acumination at tip; urosome five segmented, genital segment longer than others, caudal rami symmetrical, right Antenna I: geniculate, with a long blunt dorsal spine; denticulated plates present on dorsal margins of segments 17, 18 and fusion segment 19-21; latter, with a row of 10-11 spines, elongated, and arranged one behind the other above a crescentic projection. 5th  leg: asymmetrical, right leg 5 ending in a chela; hand broad, more or less conical, movable finger blunt and claw like; hand with a conical tooth-like elevation on inner margin and a seta; finger with one inner marginal and two outer marginal setae; inner margin of finger irregular with small elevations; penultimate segment with well developed plumose seta; left leg: distal segment with prominent outer marginal spine and three distal subequal spines; inner margin of terminal segment with broad tuft of marginal hairs; subterminal segment with a disto-lateral spine.

Additional  remarks:     
Grice (1962) from equatorial Pacific Ocean recorded the females of this species possessing leg 5 with Ri forked on one leg and single and pointed on the other. Recently, Park (1968) described the females of P.plumata with unequal, acuminate Ri with no distal forks from central north Pacific Ocean. However, the specimens recorded from the various localities in the Indian Seas were with apically bifid Ri. The male leg 5 showed slight differences in the specimens collected from the neritic waters off the west coast of Indian and from the Laccadive Sea. In the oceanic specimens the movable finger of male leg 5 is provided with a spatulate tip, while the specimens from neritic waters does not show any such modification. The distal outer marginal seta on the finger is spinous in the neritic forms while in the oceanic specimens it is more slender and setose. These characters were not observed by earlier workers.
