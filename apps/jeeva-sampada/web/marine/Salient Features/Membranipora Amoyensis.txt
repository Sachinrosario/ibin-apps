SALIENT FEATURES:  Morphological: Zooecia encrusting, large and usually quadrangular and arranged in alternate fashion.  Zooecia show variations in shape in the region where overlapping has taken place in the colony.  Gymnocyst developed, more extensive proximally, bearing small spines directed inwards   from the clear calcareous margin of the gymnocyst.  Smaller spines occupy the spaces between the larger ones.  Opesia oval.  Operculum membraniporine with very thin margin.  Spines which may be simple or trifid arise from two lateral tubercular areas distally
Diagnostic characteristics:   The trifid spines present at the distal corners of the zooecium arose from two well marked tubercular areas.  These tubercles had chitinous borders, which were continuous with the thickened chitinous lines separating each zooecium. 
Sex attributes:  Hermaphrodite
Descriptive characters:   The trifid spines shown in Robertson's (1921) figures were very stout, but in the specimens collected by Menon, though the pattern of branching was similar they were much slender and longer.  Whereas Robertson's figures showed the presence of up to seventeen marginal spines none of the zooecia in the form collected by Menon had more than twelve spines.
