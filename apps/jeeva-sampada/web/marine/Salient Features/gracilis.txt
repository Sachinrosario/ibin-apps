Morphological:  Head very small, and not distinct from the narrow neck, snout projecting beyond the lower jaw, rostral shield large, extending almost to the upper surface of the snout; eye moderate; one each of pre - and post oculars; 1 anterior temporal followed by another scale as large or larger; 5 or 6 supralabials, 2nd touching the prefrontal and the 3rd and 4th in contact with the eye; six or seven (sometimes 4) lower labials whcih are in contact with the genials.  Body stout and laterally compressed; scales anteriorly smooth, elongate and imbricate and those posteriorly juxtaposed, hexagonal, with small central tubercles, in 29-43 rows at midbody; ventrals distincly larger than the adjacent scales on anterior third of body, divided and with heavy tubercles posteriorly; 256-261 (in males), 247 - 276 (female);  2 to 4 preanals.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The common small headed sea snake is easily identified by its small head, slender neck and forebody, and the contrasting markedly compressed rear part of the body.  The colouration is also striking, helping in the identification of the species.  The young are dark, with a series of whitish dorsal bands or oval, paired lateral spots on he slender part of the body, and more or less complete bands posteriorly, numbering from 40-60.  The markings, however, fade with growth and the adults are dark grey above and whitish below, with the obscure bars or bands, particularly on the thickest part of the body.  The head is usually olive or yellow.

Additional remarks:  The common small - headed sea snake, despite being an agile creature swimming swiftly by making use of its elongated forebody, is virtually helpless on land.  The sea snake has been collected in net scoops of shrimps.  It is reported from the mangrove swamps.  This species has caused human fatalities.

The range of this species is from the Persian Gulf around the coasts of India, north along the coasts of China to Hong Kong, and south to Borneo and probably northern Australia.
