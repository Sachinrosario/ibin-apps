SALIENT  FEATURES :
Morphological:                                 

Diagnostic characteristics:      

Female: Separation between cephalon and pedigerous segment I distinct; lateral cephalic hooks absent; posterior corners of pedigerous segment V produced into two conspicuous asymmetrical lobes, each with a ventro-lateral marginal spine: urosome three segmented, asymmetrical; genital segment posteriorly enlarged, with a conspicuous swelling dorsally as well as on its left side; genital segment with a ventral elevation and genital opening ventro-lateral; urosome – II narrow and broad; anal segment posteriorly bifid, right half slightly larger; anal lamina well developed, conical and extending to posterior margin of caudal rami; caudal rami laterally placed and nearly circular in shape; right ramus is larger and anteriorly placed; caudal setae well developed, second seta from inner margin swollen; 5th leg: slightly asymmetrical, Re of left leg being stouter; with three outer marginal spines and with a subequally bifid tip, inner distal spine being smaller, Ri slender with a broad basal part.     

Male: Cephalon as infemale except that dorsal eye lenses are large and placed close together; rostrum short and with acuminate prongs; posterior corners of pedigerous segment V asymmetrical, pointed and lobate, right side being larger; urosome five segmented; caudal rami slightly asymmetrical, left ramus slightly shorter; caudal seta bulbous at its base; Right antenna-I: geniculate, with denticulated plates on dorsal margins of segments 17 to 19-21; a proximal knobular projection present on segment 18; plates on fusion segment 19-21 run along distal knobular projection; 5th leg: Right leg: terminal segment forming a chela; thumb well developed, hand broad but long; movable finger slender elongate and curved inwards; base of thumb and distal end of hand with one seta each and a pair of seta along elevated inner margin of finger; left leg: terminal segment with four elongated, subequal distal spinous processes, second from base being longest; terminal portion as well ass inner margin of segment with marginal hairs; subterminal segment short with a distolateral spine.

Additional  remarks:     
There are many discrepancies in the description of L. detruncata. Fleminger (1965) stated that Brady’s (1883) record of this species from the off Buenos Aires refers in part to L. nerii Kroyer. Brady (1883) described and illustrated a variation in the leg-5 of L. detruncata  which has also  been reported by Farran (1936) from the Great Barrier Reef material; this variation refers to the condition of the leg-5 of copepodite – V. Wolfenden’s (1906) record of L. detruncata from Malay Archipelago and the     
‘L. detruncata Sydney  var.’ described by Dakin and Colefax (1940) are referable to L. detruncata sensu stricto and not to the variant (Cop V) of Brady. Vervoot (1965) indicated that the L. detruncata var. intermedia described by T. Scot t (1894)  from the Gulf of Guinea is a synonym of L. nerii, a view which was earlier expressed by Giesbrecht (1892). As such there is no definite record of this species from the Atlantic Ocean.
