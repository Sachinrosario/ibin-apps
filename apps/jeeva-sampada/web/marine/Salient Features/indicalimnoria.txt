Sex Attributes:
Lives in pairs, female remaining at the blind end of the burrow; embryonic and larvel developments take place inside a brood pouch formed by the brood lamellae.

Feeding Habit: Bores into wood and eats the saw dust.

Main Food: Wood cellulose.

Additional Remarks:
The sculpturing on the male pleotelson is variable. In most males examined, the lateral pair of puncta are either small, or absent and represented by only a long simple seta. Also, in most males and the holotype, the poosterior pair of puncta are directly behind the anterior pair. However, some specimens do have one or both hind puncta slightly more lateral than the anterior pair of puncta, although not to the degree shown by Becker and Kampf (1958). Juveniles have the same pleotelsonal sculpture found in females. Some small males also have the pleotelsonal carinae and puncta found in females but with an extra pair of puncta are larger and the posterior carinae lost.

According to Muller (1988), L. Indica may be a synonym of L. Simulata which in fact is an algel borer. However after a thorough study of literature on both species, cookson(1991) cautious about the synonyms and considers L.Indica as a valid species.

Density of occurence of L.Indica along the Indian coasts is very low and damage caused to timber stryctures is not important, except perhaps in andaman waters, other than untreated and treated timbers, L.Indica has also been reported attacking roots of red mangrove in belize.
