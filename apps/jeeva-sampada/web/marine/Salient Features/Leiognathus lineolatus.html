SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:

Diagnostic characters: Mouth pointing downwards when protracted; conspicuous scales on breast, back greenish to brownish, with relatively sparse grey, irregular vermiculations extending down to a little below lateral line, the lowest marks in the form of blotches in a row. It is distinguished from a very similar  species  L.berbis by the less crowded vermiculations on back and and the absence of a more or less distinct notch at nape.

 

Sex attributes:  Sexes undistinguishable externally.

 

Descriptive characters: Body oblong , compressed and rather elongate , dorsal and ventral profiles equally convex. A slight  concavity over occiput. Snout pointed . Eyes somewhat shorter than snout. Mouth small. Lips narrow and thick. Mouth when protracted forms a tube directed downwards. Commencement of gape of mouth over lower one third of  eye. Tips of maxilla just reaches the front border of the eye. Inferior edge of mandibles very slightly concave. Teeth small , numerous , in a single series in jaws.

 

Inter-orbital space bordered on each side by a smooth supra-orbital ridge, continued as the outer border of the nuchal spine. Median portion of the nuchal spine is raised. A pair of spines on top of the head, over the anterior third of the orbit , the outer one directed backwards and more prominent than the inner, opposite which the ridge bounding the inter-orbital space commences. Pre-opercle with an obtuse angle, its lower margin straight and finely serrated. Scales small , all over the body including breast except head. First part of lateral line with a concavity, later running less convex to the dorsal profile, extending posteriorly to the caudal base.

 

Dorsal and anal spines weak. Anal spine slender. 2nd to 4th dorsal spines sub-equal. 2nd anal spine arched and shorter than 2nd dorsal. 3rd and 4th  dorsal spine and 3rd anal spine , serrated anteriorly for some distance from base. Ventrals with axillary scale and their tips do not quite reach the origin of the annals. Caudal small and deeply forked.

 

Colour:

 Belly silvery, back brownish with relatively sparse  vertical zigzag  lines or grey irregular vermiculations from behind head to caudal base, laterally extending down to a little below the lateral line. Ventral half of the body with fine black dots. Tip of snout dotted black. Inner side of pectoral base dotted black, as also the lower half of the gill opening covered by the opercular flap.

Meristic Characters: D. VIII, 16; A.III, 14. Ll.60.                                  

 

Feeding Habit:

 

Main food: It feeds mainly on small crustaceans, chaetognaths, nematodes, bivalves and gastropods.

 

 Feeding type: Carnivorous and bottom feeding mainly.                                        

                                                                                                                          Ref no: 4
Additional Remarks:  It is marketed fresh and dried salted, together with other ponyfishes; also used for fishmeal.

Description of larvae:

Characteristics:

 

The following description of the larvae of  L.lineolatus  was given by John, 1951, based on the work done in the Zoological Research Laboratory of the Madras university during 1934-1936.

 

                         

 

                                             Larva of  L.lineolatus

 

                    9mm Larva : The body is compressed laterally and almost oval in shape. The dorsal and ventral profiles of the body are equally convex. Maximum height of the body is between the ventral fins and the first dorsal spine. The mouth is terminal and extremely protractile with minute pointed teeth on both the jaws. The eyes are dorso-lateral with a concave inter-orbital space. The supra-orbital edge is finely serrated. There are two anterior supra-orbital recurved spines. The pre-opercular edge is finely serrated. The angular spine of the pre-opercle is extremely small. An occipital blade, transparent and serrated with a posterior free point is present between the dorsal fin and  the orbital regon.This appears to be definitely a larval character since it disappears during the post-larval period. The body is scaleless and smooth. There are only 24 vertebrae.

                    In very young forms the fins are not clearly and fully differentiated with the full set of rays. The single dorsal fin of 10 spines and 16 rays starts from behind the occipital blade. There is a notch between the spinous and soft portions of the dorsal as the posterior spines are smaller than either the anterior spines or the posterior rays. The first spine is the shortest, the second is the longest and the strongest and the rest are slender and shorter. The proximal region of the 2nd, 3rd and 4th spine is serrated. The caudal fin of 17 rays is deeply forked. The anal has 3 spines and 14 rays. The 2nd spine is the longest and strongest and is serrated at the base. The ventral fins each of one spine and 5 rays are thoracic and small, reaching only half way to the anal fin. The pectorals are almost pointed with 15 rays.

                    Chromatophores , both simple and stellate are developed here and there on the transparent body. A group of chromatophores are seen on the occipital region, below which there is a thick black vertical streak. There are a few pigment marks scattered without any definite arrangement on the abdominal region. these extend to the opecular region also. A black arc is developed at the ventral orbital edge. In front of the dorsal fin there are 3 stellate chromatophores. A pair of them is seen at the middle as well as the end of the fin. There is a black dot at the base of each of the anal fin rays. There are 3 stellate chromatophores on the ventral edge of the body between the caudal and the anal fins. A pair of chromatophores is seen at the base of the caudal and anal fins and another pair at the base of the caudal fin ventrally. Along the lateral line there are 3 stellate black spots at the anterior end and another  set of  three at the caudal end.
