SALIENT FEATURES :
Morphological :

Female :

Body slightly longer than broad, almost pear shaped and very nearly symmetrical, except with regard to the coaxial plates. Head slightly broader than long, with a distince longitudinal median groove, postero-median part convex and slightly bilobed and the postero-lateral corners prominent; frontal lamina half sa long as the head and extending beyond its lateral margins. Peraeon moderately convex. Coxal plates well developed and somewhat curved upwards. All the peraeon segments clearly defined. Ovarian bosses present on the first four segments. First two pairs of coxal plates overlapping the lateral parts of the head; frontal lamina and the first peraeon segment; coxal plates one or two symmetrically developed, three to five large but not overlapping, five to seven smaller and poorly developed and directed onwards and not forwards as the proceeding ones. On the deformed side. coxal plates one and two similar to those of the opposite side, three and four narrow and ridge like, five and six poorly developed. Pleon five segmented and symmetrical. Pleural lamellae well developed and separated from the pleon segments by deep incisions. Each lamella apically acuminate and tuberculated on both sides. First pleon segment without tuberculated on both sides. First pleon segment without pleural lamellae but with a distinct postero-median point.

First antenna short and stout, four jointed; second antenna fivejointed, segments one and two very large. Posterior lamina with two lateral processes or lobes, each with a number of highly branched digitiform processes. Posterior lobe of first oostegite prominently curved and apically rounded. Inner crest very high and with closely packed short processes. Maxilliped with a very well developed palp, and the posterior lobe small roughly triangular. Basis of peraeopods with large boss, inferior border of segments three to six spiny and lower surface scaly. Propodus produced into a hemispherical lobe with a few spine like setae. Uropods small, biramous and tubeculated. Pleopods highly tuberculated and hidden under the well developed pleural lamellae. Pleon segments wrinkled on the ventral side, presenting a corrugated appearance. Brood pouch open in the center.

Male :

Body about three times as long a sbroad. Head twice as long as broad. Peraeon segments deeply cut laterally and the segments very slightly but progressively becoming broader backwards. Telson somewhat triangular, slightly broader than long. First antenna three jointed, first segment very long. Second antenna four jointed, first segment short and second long. Both antennae with a number of hairs on the distal segments. External border of peraeopods prominently setose, dactylus stout and propodus proportionately large.

Additional Remarks:

Absence of pleural lamellae on the small fifth abdominal segment is a very distinguishing character which separated Epiperiaeon anomalous from all the known species og the genus.

Pillai 1954 ercated a new species Epipenaeon ovalis without describing the species in detail with illustration. Subsequently he Pillai 1958 gave a detailed description of the ssame female specimen, collected from the host Parapenaeopsis stylifera, under a new name, E.anomalous. Since the complete description is given under the specific name anomalous, the same is taken as the valid name.
