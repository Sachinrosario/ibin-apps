SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: Shell similar to Martesia striata (Linnaeus), white, variable in size and shape, generally pear shaped in outline and producing a callum in adult stage. Anterior margin of valves margin of valves with a teredo-like angled embayment, pedal gape large. Anterior slopes separated from disc by a shallow but distinct umbonal ventral sulcus. Sculpture of denticulated ridges on anterior slope and concentric growth lines on disc and posterior slope. Periostracum thin, light, straw yellow and persistent. Umbos prominent, located medially in young and anteriorly in adults. Umbonal reflection thin, free and broadly reflected anteriorly forming funnel-shaped pit below, closely appressed over umbo and with sickle-shaped flanges (attachment sites for anterior adductor muscle) forming ridges over umbos anteriorly but raised well above the surface of valves posteriorly. Pedal gape in adult closed by callum except for minute pore in periostracum joining the two halves medially.

  “Interior of valves white, glazed; umbonal ventral ridge and ventral condyle well defined in young specimens, ridge often obscure except near condyle in adults. Apophyses long, thin; chodrophore and internal ligament moderately well developed. Muscle scar well impressed, anterior adductor scars covering medial surfaces of umbonal flanges and extending ventrally onto surface of anterior slope about midway between umbo and posterior end of valves; pallial line well marked, pallial sinus extending anteriorly to about midway below the posterior adductor scar; ventral adductor muscle scar elongate, bringing umbonal-ventral ridge near ventral condyle.”
  
  “Mesoplax in one piece, semicircular in young specimens with an anterior embayment and fitting beneath free ends of umbonal flanges. Mesoplax in adults inflated, usually rounded posteriorly, laterally lobed anteriorly, and sculptured with irregular wrinkles. Metaplax and hypoplax long, narrow, pointed anteriorly, forked posteriorly and extending over the lobes of the siphonoplax. Siphonoplax with a large, squarish central lobe borded by triangular dorsal and ventral lobes.”

  Mantle and siphons creamy white. Siphons combined except at tips, surrounded by an outer ring of tentacles and capable of extension beyond the shell. The suction foot in young actively boring specimens large truncate, broadly elliptical and somewhat pointed anteriorly and posteriorly. Foot in adult atrophied after the completion of the callum. The young of nairi based solely on the valves, are virtually indistinguishable from the young of other Martesia and dried or incomplete adult specimens lacking their accessory plates are equally difficult to distinguish (Turner and Santhakumaran, 1989).

Main food: Plankton.

Additional remarks:
      This species is common in the mangrove estuaries of Kerala, Karnataka and Goa where it bores into living and dead mangroves. It has the rare ability to bore into living mangroves such as Rhizophora mucronata, Avicennia officinalis and Exoecaria agallocha.
  This species is abundant in the northern backwaters of Kerala namely Nilewaram, Valapatanam, Badagara, Korapuzha and Kallayi occurring both in living trees and dead stumps. It is present in large numbers in almost all types of timber available in these backwaters. The nature of distribution in these systems suggests that it can tolerate wide ranges of salinity. The reason for its absence in the southern backwaters is not clear. In the Mandovi and Zuari estuaries of Goa, nairi is common and here the salinity fluctuates between 1 and 35%0 and almost fresh water conditions prevail during the monsoon period.
Occurrence: Pilerna, Britona, Calvi, Merces, Agacaim, Cortalim, Cumbarjua in Goa; Talapady mangrove in Karnataka; Neendakara, Ashtamudi estuary; Pichavaram mangroves, Porto-Nova, Tamil Nadu (Turner and Santhakumaran, 1989; Nair, 1994); Estuarie sof Northern Kerala (Nair, 1992, 1994a).
