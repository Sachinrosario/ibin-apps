SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Encrusting on shells. Size of the zooecia vary, usually small in the central part of the colony, size increasing towards the periphery. Arrangement of zooecia in longitudinal rows, with shallow grooves separating them. Gymnocyst vestigial, spines absent, opesia rounded or oval, when oval, rounded proximally. Paired adventitious avicularia present at the distal sides of the zooecia. Avicularium with a slightly acute rostrum moderately raised distally and directed transversally inwards to the zooecia. A triangular space present by the side of the rostrum distally. Ovicells present, hyperstomial, half of which raised from the surface. The distal region of the fertile zooecia raised and thickened. Only one vicarious avicularium (?) noticed near the ancestrula of one colony.
Diagnostic characters:   A single vicarious avicularium (?) is noticed in one colony placed near the ancestrula. The solitary representation of the vicarious avicularium is an interesting feature. The structure of this vicarious avicularium is very much similar to the ancestrula of the colony. The ancestrula is peculiar in the present form being more or less oval with slightly broad distal regions. The similarity between the ancestrula and vicarious avicularium prompts to state that these may be the first two zooids to be formed in the colony.Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite   Descriptive characters:   Though some differences are evident, the present specimens seem to agree with the figure and description of Parellisina curvirostris given by Osburn (1950). The shape of the kenozooecium is in close conformity with what Osburn (1950) has described. These spaces are slightly sunk and the region directed to the proximal part of the avicularia is usually extended in certain cases. At one instance it was noticed that the rim of the triangular space of the kenozooecium bear very small tubercles. The kenozooecium is separated from the avicularium by a vertical wall. The size and shape of the kenozooecia show variations. The rostrum of the avicularium, which is placed transversely inwards, is invariably raised from the zooecial plane distally in the present specimens. The cryptocyst of the avicularia is strongly calcified. The structure of the cryptocyst of the autozooecium is very much similar to that of Ellisina curvirostris Harmer. Cryptocyst well developed.
