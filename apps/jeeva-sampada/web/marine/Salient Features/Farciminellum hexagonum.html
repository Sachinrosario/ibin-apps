SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Zoarium only slightly calcified, reaching a length of at least 100mm, rootlets very delicate. Stalk bifurcating rarely, at an acute angle. Branches with as many as 12-13 longitudinal rows of zooecia, on each surface, towards their distal ends. On the frontal surface all the zooecia are provided with an operculum and orifice, and are very long, parallel-sided, without spines, the orifice terminal, and the cryptocyst undeveloped. On the basal surface, the two marginal rows alone possess fully developed zooecia, the remainder having the form and size of zooecia, but without operculum or orifice, the opesia sometimes greatly reduced by an extensive cryptocyst. Avicularia restricted to the basal surface, occurring at the junction of the distal wall of a marginal zooecium with a kenozooecium; conical, with a nearly semicircular mandible. Ovicells wanting, the fertile zooecium producing an extremely large egg. At the bifurcation; the formation of an axillary zooecium is preceded by the doubling of a row or rows of zooecia in such a way as to provide the median rows of the branches.Diagnostic characters: Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  
Descriptive characters: In the specimens, which Harmer (1926) collected, the stem at its origin has a calcareous axis, which appear to be constituted by the central parts of the walls of zooecia which are not individually recognizable. The axis dilate at regular intervals, a process accompanied by the formation of 6 longitudinal ridges on it; and the ridges have each a transverse chitinous line at the middle, together constituting a joint. When the zooecia become distinct, the stem is quadriserial, the four series each constituting one side of a stalk which is nearly square in section. On the basal surface, all the zooecia including the axillary zooecium, with the exception of the two marginal rows, remain as kenozooecia. Avicularia begin to appear, on the basal surface only, at the distal end of the second pair of recognizable zooecia, but at first alternately on successive zooecia. The avicularia are paired after the second bifurcation.
  In another specimen collected by Harmer, the second bifurcation of one of the branches shows a variation, as the axillary zooecium form no terminal wall in the usual place, but extend on each side, without interruption of its cavity, where a normal orifice is developed on each side. That zooecium has, accordingly, the form of a Y with short arms, each of which ended in an orifice, no median orifice being present. These irregularities, according to Harmer, have to be regarded as variations of a structure which is perhaps becoming a kenozooecium.
