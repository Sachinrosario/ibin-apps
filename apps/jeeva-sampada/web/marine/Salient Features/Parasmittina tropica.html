SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Encrusting. Whitish in hue. Zooecia arranged in longitudinal rows, separated by grooves. Marginal pores represented in every zooecium. Front convex and tuberculated. Oral spines present, usually three in number. Distally rounded aperture with a distinct rounded or elongate-oval sinus. The sinus limited by two lateral cusps, which may be pointed or even bifurcated at the tip. Lateral oral avicularia represented in all zooecia, very long and acute, directed proximally, placed over a swelling of the front. Rostrum everted distally with well-developed distal spinules. Mandible long and pointed. Primary orifice rounded, provided with a broad and truncate lyrule and two backwardly directed cardelles. Ovicells small, convex and perforated. Ectooecium do not cover the frontal surface. Diagnostic characters: The form of the secondary orifice is "typically narrowly sinuate", which can sometimes be "triangular oval or even circular". Another important character of specific importance is the presence of "long and narrow avicularia" directed proximally and found on one or both sides of the orifice. The third important character is the absence of spatulate avicularia. This character helps to distinguish this species from Smittina parsevalii, with which it shows resemblance. Yet another distinctive feature, according to Harmer is the 'complete exposure of the entooecium'.
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters: The presence of long, acute, sub-oral lateral avicularia with the distally everted rostra, represented in every zooecium is a characteristic feature of this material. In this respect, the present form shows striking resemblance to Smittina tropica from India and Torres Strait examined by Harmer (1957). Harmer feels that the specimens identified as Smittina trispinosa (Johnston) and Smittia rostriformis Kirkpatrick, by Thornely (1905 & 1907) from Ceylon and Station 90 of the Indian Marine Survey respectively, are synonymous to Smittina tropica (Waters). Thornely has given neither descriptions nor figures.
    An examination of the probable range of variations indicated by Harmer shows that, most of them are connected with the adventitious avicularia. The avicularia in the present form are placed over swellings, the size of which diminishes as they approach the distal end of the avicularia. The swellings are highly tuberculated. The occurrence of a small avicularium on the lateral proximal region of one of the marginal zooecia is an interesting feature. This avicularium is provided with two blunt calcareous protuberances on the lateral distal region.
  The number of oral spines noticed by Waters (1909) was two and by Harmer (1957) three, but in the present specimen, it ranges from three to four. The pointed and bifurcated nature of the cusps is yet another character of interest in the present form.
  The available literature indicates that this species shows a wide range of variations, and the variations noticed here are probably only of an ecotypical nature.
