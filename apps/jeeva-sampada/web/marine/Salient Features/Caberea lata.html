SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Frontal surface of branches flat, basal surface moderately carinate, owing to the arrangement of the vibracula. Cryptocyst well developed, sloping down to the body cavity, the oral shelf moderately distinct. Distal spines, 1-1, long in the young condition, the external spine the stronger; short or vestigial in the old zooecia. Scutum wanting. Frontal avicularia small, either one or two on the proximal side of the opesia. Marginal avicularia lying on the frontal surface of the vibracula near their distal ends, usually minute, sometimes considerably enlarged, projecting laterally with a swollen proximal surface, the rostrum and mandible short and wide, the mandible with a strong vertical terminal spike. Vibracula strong with an elongate groove, crossed near the articulation of the seta by the usual calcareous bridge, the outer lobe of the proximal end of the rostrum forming a broad tooth projecting at the edge of the branch. Seta strong and long, its proximal part flattened, its distal half bearing a unilateral series of strong serrations, which are wanting or vestigial in young colonies. Ovicells short with a conspicuous, curved, frontal fenestra not covered by the ectooecium.Diagnostic characters: Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite   
Descriptive characters: In the Siboga specimens, young colonies were delicate; mostly biserial whereas older colonies were robust, usually 4-serial. A colony might reach a considerable size without becoming triserial. Enlarged marginal avicularia had been noticed by Busk (1852) and Harmer (1926). Such colonies showed an additional series of serrations, due to the enlarged avicularia alternating with the projecting ends of the vibracula. Harmer (1926) had found in nearly all of his specimens that the number of longitudinal series of zooecia was 4. But Busk figured a 4-7 serial branch with 2-5 rows of central cells. This showed a difference between the marginal and median zooecia in Busks material.
  In the 4-serial colonies which Harmer had examined, there was a difference between the two branches, one being biserial at its origin and the other, which might be regarded the continuation of the main branch, being triserial, owing to an intercalation of an additional row starting off from the axillary zooecium. Branches which had a biserial origin were given off alternately on opposite sides of the parent branch. Each biserial branch quickly became 4-serial, its first biserial offshoot being on the side which was external with reference to the preceding bifurcation. The branch which commenced in the biserial condition seemed to be usually jointed at its base. Meristic characteristics:
