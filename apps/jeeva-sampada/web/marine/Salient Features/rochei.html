Morphological                                                                    Ref.No.20

Body robust, elongate and rounded. Two dorsal fins separated by a long inter space. Body naked except for the corselet, which is well developed in Auxis rochei (in Auxis thazard. the corselet is short) in its posterior part (more than 6 scales wide under second dorsal fin origin). Caudal peduncle slender with a strong central keel on each side between two smaller keels. Inter pelvic process well developed than in Euthynnus affinis.

 

There is an overlapping of characters in A.rochei and A.thazard. However the two species can be easily distinguished. In the latter the corselet is abruptly short where as in the former the corselet is long and broader on the lateral line. In A.thazard the gill rakers less in number than A.rochei. The body in a cross section is almost round in A.rochei, but a little laterally compressed in the other species.

Characteristics:

        Under the Genus Auxis , two species are known – A.thazard and A,rochei. Of these the short corseted former is found in all the warm seas while the long corseleted A.thynnoides (=A.rochei) is considered to be confined to the Indo-Pacific.

 

The larval stages of Auxis sp. and juveniles stages of A.thynnoides (=A.rochei) collected from the seas around India have been described. However, the inferences were tentative.                                   Ref.No. 9

 

The hauls taken from Dana Station No. 3946 (lat. 3º 26’S; long. 42º 58'E)on 03.01.1930 were most rich in larval Auxis spp. Both A.thazard and A.rochei were found in the collections. The size of larvae in the collection presumed to belong Auxis thynnoides (=A.rochei) appear to range from about 4mm to nearly 9 mm.

 

4.46 mm larva: This stage is more or less comparable to the 5.1 mm larva described by Wade (1951, Ref.No.25) and the 5.2 mm larva described by Matsumoto (1959, Ref.No. 14). The difference between 4.5 mm Auxis type 1 larva  described by Matsumoto (1959, Ref.No. 14) and 4.4mm larva of Jones (1960, Ref.No. 9) which are now presumed to be A.thazard is striking.

                                Scanned picture Jones Page 419  4.66 mm larva       

 

6.54mm larva: This is comparable to the 6.8 mm larva described by Wade (1951, Ref.No.25 ) and to some extent to the 7.2 mm Auxis type II larva described by Matsumoto (1959, Ref.No.14). Pigmentation on the body is  much less than in  A.thazard type larva of corresponding size described by Matsumoto (1958, Ref.No.13) and Jones (1960, Ref.No.9).

 

               Scanned picture of larva Jones 1960, Page 419 6.54 mm larva

 

 

8.68 mm larva: No larva of comparable size has been described earlier which could be placed under A.thynnoides (=A.rochei). Except for the shorter length of head and comparatively less pigmentation it is difficult to distinguish the specimen from A.tazard type larva.

Abundance:

Recent studies on Auxis larvae by different authors have brought to light the presence of two types distributed in all the three oceans.  On the basis of existing knowledge what they actually represent could only be a conjecture and the need for further investigations especially towards the correct determination of the species under the genus and their distribution is essential.
