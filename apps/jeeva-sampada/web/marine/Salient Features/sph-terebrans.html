SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: Body elongate oblong. Cephalon longer than succeeding peraeon segments, eyes small. Posterior half of the dorsal side of the body with pustules of varying size and tuft of stiff setae. Border of peraeon and pleon segments hairy. Second peraeon segment shortest and fourth the longest. Peraeonites 2-7 with a transverse ridge situated anteriorly in the anterior segments and slightly shifting backwards in the succeeding segments. Peraeon 6-7 bear four tubercles, each two sub median and two laterals, with a bunch of long thin hairs. Composite pleon segment with 4 large tubercles, two sub median and two laterals as in the posterior peraeonites. Lateral parts with three oblique sutures. Telson triangular; proximal dorsal side convex. The proximal part of the telson carries four tubercles; two-sub median and two laterals. The dorsal surface of pleon and telson carries a number of pustules, the pustules being surrounded by hairs, apex rounded, slightly curved upwards with minute apical hairs.

  First antenna with 3-segmented peduncle, proximal segment large, distal long and slender. Flagellum with setae at the distal margin of each segment and 8 or 9 segmented. Second antenna bears a five jointed peduncle, flagellum 11 segmented, lateral margin of peduncular segments fringed with hairy setae, distal superior margins of flagellar segments armed with setae.

  Mandible with a bidentate incisor process. Molar process well developed. Palp 3-segmented, first segment long and hairy, middle segment with three distal pectinate spines and the third segment bear a comb of setae. First maxilla composed of two lobes; outer lobe with nine stout dentate and three simple spines apically. Second maxilla with 3 triangular lobes, inner lobe larger and armed with long pectinate setae, outer lobe conspicuously hairy. Maxilliped with a jointed sympod, leaf shaped endite, inner lateral margin and apex armed with slender, spiny setae, 5-segmented palp, and inner border of segments with a thick matting of hairs. All peraeopods ambulatory, characteristic of genus. First three pairs subsimilar, slender and bent forwards and bears long setae on the upper border. Paraeopod 1, basis long, ischium one and a half times as long as basis merus half as long as ischium, carpus short and triangular. Upper border of ischium, merus and propodus with long setae. Peraeopods 4 and 5 short, stout; peraeopod 5 short, hairy segments in comparison to that of anterior peraeopods. Posterior peraeopods more slender successively increase in length, propodus lacks spines. Seventh peraeopod with basis longer than ischium, carpus with stout barbed spines on the distal margin, dactylus biunguiculate.

  Pleopods 1-3 similar, peduncle with three coupling hooks, triangular endopod, exopod sub truncate apically, both rami having marginal setae. Exopod of pleopod 4 two-jointed. Outer margin small, slender spiny setae interspersed between short and hairy setae, endopod fleshy and pleated. Exopod of fifth pleopod 2-segmented, basal segment with one and distal segment with 3 thickened spiny areas, endopod fleshy and pleated.

  Uropod with exopod and endopod attached to the telson, exopod outer border cut into 4 or 5 teeth, apex bears setae, endopod apex acute, hirsute.

Distribution:
Mediterranean, Africa, Congo, Mozambique, Zanzibar, SriLanka, Queensland, Florida, Papua New Guinea, Brazil and India.

Remarks:
  S. terebrans has a very wide distribution. It has been reported from widely separated localities such as Brazil and India. The specimens from Brazil were described as S. terebrans and those from India as S. vastator. Collections from Florida were described as S. destructor by Richardson (1905). The latter two species were synonymised to S. terebrans by Stebbing (1904). Richardson argued against synonymising S. detructor and S. terebrans. The pattern of tuberculation on the posterior body surface, the nature of slightly upturned telson with minute tuberculations all point to the conclusion that S. destructor is S. terebrans. The numbers of seate on the first maxilla differ slightly in the two species. Pillai (1961) has pointed out that this appendage is evidently subject to some variation.
  
Main Food: Plankton, detritus and fungus in decaying wood.

Ecological notes:
  S. terebrans is typically a brackish water species, never having been found in the open sea. It is abundant in the backwaters and estuaries. It causes severe damage to submerged timber. The attack has been observed to be severe in the intertidal-zone. This species has been reported to attack even live mangrove trees.

  This pill bug usually makes burrows just enough to accommodate a single individual. Hence it is presumed that pairing occurs outside the burrow. S. terebrans breeds throughout the year and each female produces more than one brood a year. Fertilization is internal and the fertilized eggs are passed into the brood pouch in which the embryonic and larval development is completed. Each brood carries 20-48 eggs (Krishnan and Nair 1973b). This accounts for the intensity of attack by the species. The phenomenon of mixed brood is exhibited by this species. S. terebrans ids highly gregarious.

  Presence of cellulase has been reported in the alimentary tract of the species (Iyer 1982).
Size and age: 8-12m in length.
