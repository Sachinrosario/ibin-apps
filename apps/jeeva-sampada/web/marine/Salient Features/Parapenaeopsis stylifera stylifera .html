SALIENT FEATURES:         Ref No:103,161

 

Morphological:  Rostrum sigmoidal, toothless in distal half or more, armed with 7 to 9 dorsal

teeth proximally, strongly upcurved and much over-reaching tip of antennular peduncle, somewhat shorter in males; epigastric tooth present; postrostral crest almost reaching posterior margin of carapace; longitudinal suture long, reaching 2/3 of carapace length; telson armed with 4 pairs of lateral fixed spines; antennular flagella as long as carapace; epipod and basial spine present on first and second pereopods; basis of third pereopod unarmed. Distolateral projections of petasma slender, horn-like and straight, directed anterolaterally and with ventro-external openings; distomedian projections small and curved ventrally. Anterior plate of thelycum square and concave, with a slender stem-like posterior process; posterior plate deeply notched anteromedially.

Eggs and larvae:                Ref.No: 59,123,159

 

  Characteristics:    At species level, eggs are opaque and spherical measuring 0.35 to 0.38 mm in diameter, with a wide perivitelline space. The embryo hatches out in 15 to 16 hours and passes through 6 nauplius, 3 protozoea and 7 mysis substages before transforming into postlarva. The last mysis stage is 3.7 to 4.1 mm total length and is characterised by having a short rostrum reaching middle of eye, 2 rostral teeth, no hepatic spine, long two-segmented pleopods without setae, and a telson with its distal margin slightly convex and bearing 8 pairs of setae. In the First Postlarva, an epigastic tooth also develops on the rostrum, hepatic spine is present, and posterior margin of telson is tapering and ending in a prominent median spine which is longer than the 4 pairs of telsonic setae present on either side of it. The pleopods become setose.  

 

  Abundance:        Eggs, larvae and postlarvae of the species have been noticed in varying densities in the inshore waters at Cochin (Rao, 1972) and larvae and postlarvae in the inshore waters of central west-coast region (Goswami et al ., 1977). At Cochin, while eggs and nauplii occurred in October and February,  protozoea and mysis stages were found almost throughout the year except during June to August. They were most abundant during October-December and February-April.

SPAWNING INFORMATION:             Main Ref::157

   Locality:      Within about 20m depth in the sea throughout the range of distribution.

   Season:        Peak spawning Nov-January & Apl on SW coast, Nov-Dec on SE coast.
