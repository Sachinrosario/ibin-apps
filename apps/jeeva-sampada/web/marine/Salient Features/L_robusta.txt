SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:The carapace in the early stages with a long rostrum, extending beyond the antennal scale; 2 small protuberances  on the mid- dorsal line,one  behind  the eye and other towards the posterior end .The anteroinferior margin of carapace with a  spine followed by 3 smaller  teeth and the posteroinferior margin with 2 teeth. The number of teeth increases as the stage advances, to 4 or more; and 5 or more in the anteroinferior and posteroinferior margins of carapace,respectively .The tubercles remain prominent in late stages.Antennular peduncle unjointed with a long, stout plumose seta and one of the flagella bears 3 long aesthetes and a short seta terminally. Antenna has a short peduncle with a stout ventral spine on the basis; endopod is a slender rod, carrying a long plumose seta apically. First pereopod is present in the form of a small rudiment.The 5th abdominal segment bears 2 pairs of posterolateral spines, of which,one pair  is very small. Telson bears 7+7 spines , tip rounded with a small median incisionThe rostrum becomes shorter in older stages. The pleopods appear  in late stages as small buds. The 1st 2 pairs of legs chelate, telson becomes almost parallel-sided..In post larval stages, the carpace still possesses the tubercles; the pleura of abdominal segments 1-5 show serrations, a pair of dorsolateral and a pair of posteroventral spines are present on the 5th& 6th abdominal segments. Second leg has an enormously long,large chela with more than 10 spines in the inner margin of the fingers.  The telson is elongated with 6+6 terminal setae and a pair of spines at the lateral margin.The first pleopod appears only in the 2nd stage postlarva; also there is a reduction in the number  of telsonic spines and its marginal setae. .The fingers of the chela of 2nd leg have more than 35 spines.

Diagnostic Characters:     The delayed development of  pereopods 3-5  and the suppression of  pleopod  I in earlier stages  are features of probable phylogenitic significance.The large chela of 2nd leg with spines in it is an important  character.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit: pelagic feeding.

Main food:  live and dead  planktonic organisms.

Feeding type: omnivorous

Additonal remarks:        Leptochela robusta is largely fed by fishes. Specimens in different stages of digestion were obtained from the stomachs of flying fishes and tunas. Also this species agrees with detailed description of De Man (1920) and Kemp (1925). 

Size and age: 1.8mm (stage I) and 4.1mm (post larva I)

Eggs and larvae: 
Eggs not known.	5 larval and 2 postlarval stages were available.

Mysis stages I-V: length.1.8mm-3.9mm.

Stage I: rostrum long,straight extending beyond the antennular peduncle; mandible with tridentate incisor and molar part is broad with few teeth; the 1st pereopod is  present in the form of bud; the 5th abdominal somite with 2 pairs of posterolateral spines and telson with 7+7 spines.

Stage II: eyes completely free from carapace; few teeth present at the anteroinferior and posteroinferior margins of carapace; telson has 8+8 spines.

Stage III: carapace with pterygostomian spinne; postero ventral margin with 5 teeth; pereood 1 with biramous appendages, abdomen 6-segmented ; no pleopods and telson has 8+8 spines.

Stage IV: median dorsal tubercles present  on carapace; pereopods 1-2  present; pleopods present on 2-5 ab.somites; telson with a pair of lateral spines and 6 pairs of terminal spines.Stage V: The median tubercles on carapace present; antero-postero ventral margins emarginated,with spines; a pair of dorsolateral spines present on the 5th ab.somite; 1st 2 pairs of pereopods chelate; others rudimentary; uropod with exopod with terminal spine;telson parallel-sided; spine formula is 7+7.

Postlarva: the chela of pereopod 1 with 6 spines in the inner side of the finger and leg 2 has 10 spines; pleopod 1 appears inmthis stage; biramous; uropods and telson not changed.
