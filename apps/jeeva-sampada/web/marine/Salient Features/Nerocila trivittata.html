SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:
Female: The female of N. trivittata can be easily distinguished by the almost oval or slightly oblong body, greatly produced coxal plates and the characteristically serrated endopod of the uropod.

Head very small, peraeon segments sub equal. Coxal plates produced into long acute processes, seventh reaching the base of the uropods. Postero-lateral corners of hinder peraeon segments angular and slightly produced. Pleon segments laterally drawn out. Telson almost semicircular, with a prominent dorso-median longitudinal ridge.

First antenna with a very stout first segment and a slender, somewhat triangular second. Posterior peraeopods with a series of spines on the lower margin of segments four to six. Endopod of uropod asymmetrically forked at the tip, inner limb of the fork small, outer border of outer limb irregularly serrated. Mouth parts normal.

Transition stage: Very much similar to the female except in the abrupt expansion of the sixth and seventh peraeon segments. Peraeon segments and the coxal plates less developed than in the female. Sixth segment of anterior peraeopods with six spines, successively increasing in size distal wards. Sixth and seventh segments of seventh peraeopod with sixteen spines in all. Second antenna twelve segmented; only nine segmented in the female, fourth segment with a large plumose seta. Uropods with feebly but regularly serrated endopod.
  
  The male is very much similar to the transition stage except that the peraeon segments are all sub similar.

Additional remarks:
  According to Pillai (1958), the description and illustration of Nerocila sundaica given by Chidambaram and Menon are inadequate for the identification of the species, but the serrated endopod of the uropod, which they have shown, indicates that their species (N. sundaica) might be Nerocila trivittata.

  Several authors have synonymised N. serra with N. trivittata. Hence N. trivittata has been recorded from Orissa coast (Ganjam and Off Devi river) and from Visakapatanom (AndhraPradesh) as N. serra (Barnard 1936) and from south Kerala coasts (Pillai, 1954). They were found either clinging to the abdomen of Arius sagor or attached to the throat of the sea snake Hydrophis obscuras.

Size and age: 21.0mm length.
