SALIENT  FEATURES :Morphological:     

Diagnostic characteristics:        

Female: Prosome about 4 times as long as urosome. Prosome moderately produced; suture between prosome and first pedigerous segment weak. Posterior corners of fifth pedigerous segment rounded, without spines. Relative lengths of urosome segments and caudal rami about 8:4:3:4. Dorsal posterior margins of genital and second urosome segments armed with 4 to 6 spinules. Caudal rami slightly less than twice as long as wide; next-to-innermost of 4 major caudal setae the longest.          

Legs 1 to 4 as in other Acartiidae, with 3-segmented exopods and 2-segmented endopods. Legs 5 quite distinctive. First segments fused medially; second segment about twice as long as wide, with short plumose seta inserted at about 0.6 the distance along the lateral margin. Third segment more than twice as long as setae of second segment, with bilobed process on posterior surface at proximal 1/5, giving the impression of an additional suture and bent inward nearly at right angles at about its midlength.      

Male: Prosome shaped as in female, about 3 times as long as urosome. Relative lengths of urosome segments and caudal rami about 4.8: 7: 3: 5: 5. The lateral margins of first urosome segment bilobed, with a pair of setae emerging on either side between the lobes. Dorsal posterior margins of 2nd, 3rd and 4th urosome segments armed with two to five spines. Posterior margin of anal segment without spines, but produced into a pair of minute knobs. Caudal rami slightly longer than wide.     

Right leg 5 with strong spine on second segment; inner lobe of third segment bilobed, fourth segment narrower and longer. Left leg 5 with rather broad first segment bearing lateral setae at about midlength; second segment narrow; third segment with a leaf-like process and 2 median spines at about mid-length, one longer and the other short and stout, apex with spines and short curved process.   

Additional  remarks:     A. bilobata is referable to Steuer’s subgenus Acanthacartia where it differs most obviously from the other species of the subgenus in the structure of the female fifth leg. It shows some similarities to A. chilkensis Sewell, A. levequei Grice, A. plumosa T. Scott, A. southwelli Sewell, and A. spinata Esterly.
