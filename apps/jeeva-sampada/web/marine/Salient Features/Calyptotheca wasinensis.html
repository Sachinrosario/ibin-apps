SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Loosely encrusting. Zooecia usually short, convex, irregularly reticulo-punctate. Proximal lip of orifice often slightly raised or prolonged into a sort umbo or mucro; usually confined to some of the fertile zooecia.; or extended proximally as a slight median ridge.
Orifices and opercula (ordinary) mostly wider than long, but sometimes with the two diameters equal; a difference depending largely on the extent of the wide rounded sinus, which may be shallow or deep;(fertile) rather larger, wider than long, the sinus shallow. Avicularia very small, a lateral oral pair nearly always present (and no others) in the youngest colonies; in later stages also on lateral sutural lines, where they may be numerous. Ovicells, present in nearly all the specimens, globose, often with triradiate sutural lines.
Diagnostic characters: Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters:    When fully developed the zoaria of C. wasinensis are often wavy bilaminar plates. A Hemescharan form is also common, on Hydroids and other objects. They are at first encrusting , and later tubular, continuing to encircle the Hydroid, but at a distance from it. The zooecia of the primary layer are in regular longitudinal rows, with uniform orientation, their size commonly increasing from the center to the periphery. The superposed zooecia have no constant orientation.

A strong suboral umbo was stated by Waters to be the distinctive feature of var. wasinensis. It is generally present in small proportions of the fertile orifices, where it is often longitudinally ridged. It is rare on ordinary zooecia, where it is generally thinner.

The lateral oral avicularia are nearly constant in their occurance. In some wide zooecia the orifce is near one lateral border of the zooeciaum, so that it consists of two unequal portions. The latter one may have two avicularia, side by side. If the zooecium is narrow distally the avicularia are generally close to the orifice. If it is wide they are at the distal corners, at some distance from the orifice.

In older zooecia avicularia like the lateral oral ones are numerous on the sutural lines.
The character of the ovicells depends on the width of the distal zooecia. If these are narrow the ovicell may be overgrown by its tow lateral neighbors, as well as by the distal zooecium; and it is accordingly crossed by a triradiate suture. In wider zooecia, it may fail to reach either lateral wall of the distal zooecium, from which its overgrowth is entirely derived; sutural lines being thus wanting. In other wide zooecia it is in contact with only one lateral wall, and it is overgrown exclusively by a single zooecim, or sometimes by two zooecia.
