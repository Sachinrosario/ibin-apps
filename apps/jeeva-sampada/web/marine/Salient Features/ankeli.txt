Descriptive characters:  This is the original description of the species: “Corallum prostrate.  Main branches rounded at the base, 1.5 to 2 cm thick, compressed towards the distal part, with an under and upper side.  Branches repeatedly dividing.  The type specimen represents only part of a fair sized colony and is 11.5 x 7.5 cm in length and width.  Branches short, 6 to 7 cm long; branchlets upto 3 cm long with obtuse, rounded or flattened apices, when flattened width 1 to 2 cm.  Top of branchlets with verrucae as on the sides.  Undersides of the branches with obsolete verrucae or they are absent, especially at the basal parts.  Upperside of the branches with crowded round topped verrucae, 2.5 to 3.5 mm in diameter and height but average 3 mm, touching to 3 mm apart.  A few are larger with secondary ones and are destined to become branchlets.  Calices on the verrucae crowded, polygonal with little coenenchyme in between.  The calices between the verrucae on the distal part of the branches are rounded, 0.6 to 0.7 mm in diameter.  Towards the basal parts of the corallum where the verrucae are obsolete the calices are numerous, shallow, 0.5 to 0.6 mm in diameter to 1 mm apart.  Except in very young calices the tow cycles of septa are well developed, mostly subequal but one or both the directives may be pronounced to reach the columella.  Columella a prominent style pointed at the top, almost reaching the level of the wall.  The coenenchymal ornamentation composed of prominent spines.  At the junction of the septa and wall there is a circle of 12 spines so that the septa appears a little exsert.  They are not secondarily frosted and not compressed.  This arrangement of the spines, just above the wall, is regular and characteristic.  Between rows of calices there is generally another row of radically arranged spines.  A second row is visible only when the distance between rows of calices exceeds a millimeter.  The corallum in section is almost solid.  A prostrate corallum with short distally compressed branches with rounded bases, mostly uniform rounded verrucae, small calices with well developed septa and columnella and the peculiar coenenchymal spinulation are characteristic of this species” (Scheer and Pillai, 1974)

Feeding habit: Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food: Plankton.  Extracellular products of symbiotic algae
