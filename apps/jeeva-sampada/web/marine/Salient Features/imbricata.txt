SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  This sea turtle derives its popular name from its bird-like hooked jaws, with sharp but smooth or feebly denticulated ridges.  It also from other sea turtles in having the carapace covered with strongly imbricate shields (except in the very young, which become smooth and juxtaposed in the aged adults.  The head is narrow and slender.  The fore and hind limbs have two claws each.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The young are brown above and blackish below.  The carapace of adult is pale yellow, marbled and streaked with rich brown; the plastron is yellow.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Omnivorous and carnivorous

Main food:  Sponges, fish and molluscs

Feeding type :  Shells of molluscs are crunched and eaten

Additional remarks:  Using its hooked beak, the hawksbill is adept at extracting invertebrates hidden among corals and crevices.  It nests throughout the year but singly.  The nesting activity reaches its peak during the monsoon months.  Some 115 to 140 eggs are laid at a time mostly during the day.  Incubation takes about two months.

Although not eaten, the hawksbill is the source of the famous "tortoise shell", which is obtained from its dermal plates.  This shell, known as 'carey' in trade, is probably the most famous product derived from turtles.  Each turtle may give 6 to 8 Lb of the shell which is used in the making of jewellery and objects d'art.

The meat of the hawksbill is said to become poisonous in certain seasons certain seasons because of the turtle's habit of feeding on certain toxic invetebrates and algae several cases of illness and even death in humans after having consumed the turtle's flesh were reported from the States of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and from Sri Lanka.
