SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:

Diagnostic characters: Body depth contained 1.6 to 2.6 times in standard length. Head strongly concave above eye. Mouth pointing upwards when protracted. Lateral line reaching backward nearly to below end of dorsal fin.

 

Sex attibutes: Male and female undistinguishable externally.

 

Descriptive characters:Body oval , deep, elongated and compressed. Dorsal profile strongly concave in the occipital region. Dorsal profile less convex than ventral profile. Snout pointed ; snout length lesser than orbit diameter. Mouth small and oblique; when protracted forms a tube directed upwards. Gape of mouth opposite about middle of eye.Tips of maxilla far in front of front border of eye and well below the level of lower margin of the eye. Lips broad and thin; Lower lip broader and  smaller than the upper lip which is like a loop over the lower. When mouth is closed , the mandible  is almost vertical . Lower margin of mandible slightly concave. Pre-opercle  with an obtuse angle, its lower margins finely serrate.Teeth minute , numerous , in a villiform band.. Interorbital space concave , the two ridges bounding itcontinuing as the outer edges of the nuchal spine. Scales minute all over the body including breast. Lateral line shows a slight concavity at first, later running less convex to the  dorsal profile, extending posteriorly almost to the base of the caudal. Dorsal and anal spines , weak , compressed , the first minute , the 2nd , 3rd and 4th subequal in length. Second anal spine shorter than the 2nd dorsal. Ventrals with axillary scales , their tips not reaching the origin of anals. Caudal fin deeply forked.

 

Colour: Silvery, back with 10 broken , black, vertical bands, formed of patches, from behind head to end of soft dorsal, laterally extending to a little below lateral line. Abdomen with black  pigment spots. Spinous dorsal fin has the membranes between 2-6 spines black at the upper one third portion. A black curved band from the lower margin of eye to to posterior angle of lower jaw. Inner side of pectoral base dotted black. Caudal fin yellowish and posterior margin of the lobes are dusky.      Ref no: (5,6,14,26).

 

Meristic Characters: D. VIII,16;  A.III,14;P.16; V.I,15;C.24.  Ref no: 15

Feeding Habit:

Main food: Copepods and diatoms, besides Lucifer, filamentous algae, prawn, Mysis and Acetes, Megalopa, small crabs, Veligers , fish eggs , polychate larvae, adult polychates, cirripede Cypris , amphipods and  Amphioxus  larvae were also observed among the stomach contents.

(Locality: Porto Novo)  Ref no: 8

Eggs and Larvae:

 

Characteristics:

 

The following description of the larvae of  S.insidiator  was given by John, 1951, based on the work done in the Zoological Research Laboratory of the Madras university during 1934-1936.

                               

 

                                                  Larva of S.insidiator

 

Body is laterally compressed  and almost oval in shape. The  ventral profile of the body is more convex than that of the dorsal. The mouth in the protruded condition is turned dorsally. The  supra-orbital edge is smooth, with only one supraorbital recurved spine. The  angular spine of the pre-opercle is  weak,  short  and unstriated. The dorsal fin has 8 spines and 17 rays and the pectoral has 18 rays. There is a black mark at the base of each of the last nine anal rays. Along the base of the dorsal fin there are 3 stellate chromatophores of which the first is at the beginning, the second at the middle and the third at the end of the fin. Besides there are 2-4 dark spots at the middle and one or two at the end of the body along the lateral line.

 

The next stage is of 14mm length. In these the serrated occipital blade has disappeared. The opercular serrations are more distinct  and definite. The  dorsal fin has receded a little backwards probably due to growth. Additional chromatophores have developed.There is an increase in the numberof chromatophores of  the occipital group. The irregularly arranged chromatophores of the abdomen and operculum are more prominent. A row of about 10 black spots are formed along the lateral line at the middle of the body. Another row of three spots is present above it. Each of the anal fin rays except the last has a chromatophore at the base. A group of threee of the stellate type is clearly visible below the  spinous dorsal.

 

In the next stage of 18mm length there is no structural difference except for size. The pigment dots  on the  opercular region are arranged in semicircular rows. At the middle of the body black lines of pigment dots are definitely formed. In addition there are  3 or 4 groups of 3-4 stellate chromatophores along the base of the dorsal fin.

 

In the next stage, of  23mm length, the colouration is more prominent. 4 definite  lines of stellate chromatophores broken at four places are formed above the lateral line in such a way that they look like 5 cross bands. Below the  lateral line and close to the abdomen there is a clear black triangular area. More pigment dots are added to the abdominal and opercular areas. At the base of anal fin there is a short oblique line of 3 black dots. Between the anal and caudal fins are 4 stellate chromatophores.              Ref no: 13
