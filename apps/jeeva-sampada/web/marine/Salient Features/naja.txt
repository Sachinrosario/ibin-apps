SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: Head depressed, not very distinct from neck; neck wide
dialatable to form a hood(in life); snout rounded, short; nostril     
large; angular shield called 'cuneate' present between the 4th and 5th
infralabials. One, rarely two preoculars and three postoculars; two
anterior and three or four posterior labials; seven upper labials,
third largest touching both nasal shield and eye; eight (sometimes
7 or9) lower labials. Eye moderate, black with round pupil. Body
dorsoventrally flattened infront and subcylindrical behind. Scales
smooth, strongly oblique, arranged in 21-25 rows at midbody; ventrals
176-200, caudals 48-75,paired.

Diagnostic Characteristics: Generally buff, brown or black, with light 
or dark bands, crossbars, variegations or reticulations which disappear
with age; a black spot on either side of the underside of hood and two
or more black crossbands on the belly, which is usually white or 
yellowish. Hood with the famous spectacle - like marking.

Feeding Habit: Carnivorous

Main Food: Rats, mice, toads and frongs,birds, lizards, eggs and snakes

Additional Remarks: Cobras are remarkably adaptable snakes, being found
everywhere. Their favourite dwelling places are the holes in
embankments, hollows of trees, old termite monds and rat holes. They 
are fond of water.

Cobras, despite being highly dangerous because of their lethal venom,
are not aggressive snakes and tend to escape when encountered in the 
wild. They strike only when accidently stepped on or under extreme 
provocation. When cornered, a cobra spreads the hood hisses, sways
the body from side to side, and strikes repeatedly. Young cobras are
much aggressive than adults.

Females lay usually from 10 to 30 eggs in rat holes or termite mounds
and the young when hatched, are replicas of the parents.

The common cobra, along with the three major killer snakes(i.e, the
krait, the Russell's viper, and the saw-scaled viper) of India is held
responsible for loss of human life, estimated to be 10000-15000 
deaths per annum

Size and Age:
Maximum Length (cm): (male) 121-152 cm
Average Length (cm): (female) 129-165 cm
