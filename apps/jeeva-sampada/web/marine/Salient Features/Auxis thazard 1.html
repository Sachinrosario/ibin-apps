SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:                 Ref.No.16

  Body robust, slightly compressed laterally; head large, tapering to a pointed snout; first dorsal fin with 10-12 spines, separated from the second by a large interspace which is at least equal in length to the base of the first dorsal fin; 8 dorsal fin lets; pectoral fin short, reaching past vertical line from anterior margin of scale less area above the corselet; interpelvic process single; 7 anal fin lets; small pointed teeth present on both jaws; lateral line without a distinct arch, slightly curved and with small undulations; body naked except for corselet; corselet well developed, with a posterior prolongation of scales (not more than five scales under the second dorsal fin origin); central keel on each side of caudal fin base between two small lateral keels; gill rakers close set, long and slender numbering 39-42.

Diagnostic characteristics:

  Corselet of scales short, tapering abruptly along the lateral line behind the first dorsal; it is not more than 4-5 rows of scales wide in a line below second dorsal. In a related species such as Auxis rochei the corselet of scales long, continuing as wide band along lateral line behind first dorsal, it is more than six rows of scales. In A.thazard the gill rakers are 39-42 compared to 40-47 in A.rochei.
  
  Colour:

  Back bluish; head dark, light brown below; about 15 or more narrow, oblique to nearly horizontal black bars in the scale less area above lateral line.

  Tuticorin
At Tuticorin, the major food item of the adult A.thazard appeared to be Loligo spp (61%) (by volume). Othet items noticed were fishes like Anchoviella commersonii, fish larvae, copepods, decapod larvae, Amphipods, Megalopa larvae, Pteropods etc.             Ref. No. 12, 13
        

    Young ones (at Tuticorin) of A.thazard are exclusively plankton feeders unlike the adults. They have been found to feed on crustaceans, copepods, and decapod larvae. Copepods, and decapod larvae were the dominant food items.

Eggs and larvae:             Ref. No. 23

  There are in all 9 larvae in the collection ranging in total length from 3.36mm to 7.94mm which could be assigned to the genus Auxis but their specific identity is not known. All these specimens have been collected from the Lakshadweep Sea (during 1959). Auxis larvae could be distinguished from the larvae of other thunnids by the presence of conspicuous chromatophores along the mid-ventral and mid-dorsal lines of the caudal peduncle. The hauls taken from the Dana Station No: 3946 (lat. 3°26 S., long. 42°58 E) on 3-1-1930 were most rich in larval Auxis and are described here.

4.97mm larva: Chromatophores at the caudal peduncle, characteristic of the Auxis are present. There is a group of chromatophores on the head over the region of the mid-brain and a single one at the tip of the lower jaw. The chromatophores at the symphysis of the pectoral girdle is conspicuous and more of them are seen in the abdominal region. Teeth are present in both the jaws. In addition to the pre-opercular spines a small spinous growth is discernible immediately above the angle of the operculum at the post temporal region. The pelvic fins could be seen as small projections on the ventral side immediately behind the symphysis of the pectoral girdle. The median fins which appear frayed due to shrinkage are devoid of rays and the myomeres at the caudal end are not distinct.
