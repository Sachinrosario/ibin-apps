SALIENT FEATURES:Morphological:  Encrusting, zooecia deep and quincuncial or sometimes quadrangular.  Frontal membrane covers the entire surface.  Gymnocyst absent.  Operculum large and broad occupying nearly three-fourth of the distal margin.  The rim of the operculum heavily chitinised.  Three types of spines are discernible on the zooecium such as those over the operculum, those occupying the rim of the frontal membrane and two large spines on the proximo-lateral corners of the operculum.  Spines on the rim of the frontal membrane long and delicate and arise from a chitinous line.  Those on the operculum arranged in two rows, a proximal row of 3 spines and a distal row of 4, which are comparatively longer and stronger.  A circle of spines present around the operculum, arising from a chitinous base.  The third type of spines long and slender movably placed in sockets on the lateral corners of the operculum.  A well developed cryptocyst present in old zooecium.
Diagnostic characters:  A conspicuous feature of this form is the spinous adornment in the form of a ring around the operculum.  The chitinous rim present in the distal margin of the orifice comes into view when the operculum is flung open.
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite
Descriptive characters:   The zooecia occur in distinct longitudinal lines or with the zooecia of the adjacent rows at the same level.  However, in the specimens collected by Menon, the zooecia of the adjacent rows are not arranged in the same level, but they alternate.  The inner margin is crenulated.  The long, slender, mobile spines present in the lateral corners of the operculum are more bristle-like as shown by Robertson (1921) and Menon and not spinous as drawn in Harmer's figure.  The specimens collected by Menon are noteworthy in the possession of very delicate haphazardly arranged spines on the frontal membrane.
