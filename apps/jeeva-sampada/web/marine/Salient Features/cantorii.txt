SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  head rather small, broad; snout short, rounded; proboscis very short and like a mere projection.  Carapace not high, rather depressed, elongated in the young, oval in adults.  Juveniles with several tubercles and a low vertebral keel that fades with growth.  Gular region marked with small flaps of skin.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The carapace is olive or brown, dotted all over with yellow and the plastron is cream or whitish yellow.  The head is olive and the jaws streaked with darker shades.  The limbs and neck are olive above and cream below.

Additional remarks:  Although inhabiting freshwater sources mainly, the giant softshell adapts easily to conditions of salinity both of the marine and estuarine environments.  In Orissa, the the turtle is reported to undertake long journeys upto large distances in the sea.  During the breeding season, the turtle migrates from the rivers (in Orissa) to nest on the sea coast, sharing the nesting grounds of the Olive ridley sea turtle.  The female lays about 30 eggs at a time.  Though the species is described as sluggish and most docile, it, however, displays a defensive behaviour similar to that of the Chitra turtle.  It seems to be capable of striking with great speed if roughly handled.
The giant softshell is killed in large numbers wherever it occurs for its tasty flesh and delicious eggs.  The species is declared ' Endangered' and protected under Schedule I of the Indian Wildlife Protection Act (1972, amended 1991).
