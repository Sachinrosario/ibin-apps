SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  The carapace of the adult is somewhat arched and heart-shaped.  The head is large when compared to the overall size of the turtle.  The upper jaw is hooked but is without a ridge.  A distinctive feature is the presence of 3 or 4 pores on the marginal region of plastron.  The tail is short in males, longer in females.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The shell of the adult is dark grey above and bluish white or cream below. hatchlings are slight grey above and dull yellow below.

Sex attributes:  Males have longer tails, concave plastra, and a longer (recurved) claw on each forelimb.

Descriptive characters:  

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Carnivorous

Main food:  Crustaceans, molluscs, jellyfish, tunicates, echinoderms, fish and their eggs.

Feeding type  : open - ocean feeder

Additional remarks:  The Olive ridley nests in large numbers on the coasts of India, between November and February on the east coast and during the summer and monsoon months on the west coast.  The incubation period varies from 50 to 70 days.  The Gahirmatha beach, located within the Bhitar Kanika Wildlife Sanctuary, Orissa, is the world's largest nesting gound for the Olive ridley, where the turtles nest en masse annually with considerable variation in the number of nesting females numbering from tens of thousands to several lakhs.  Since the discovery of the mass nesting of the turtle on Gahirmatha beach in 1970s, two more nesting grounds (rookeries) of the species were discovered in the same State (Orissa).  The nesting grounds of the Olive ridley are keenly guarded now.
