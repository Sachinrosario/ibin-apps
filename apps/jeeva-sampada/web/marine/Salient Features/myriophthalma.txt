Descriptive characters:
The following are the general characters of this species:  Corallum explanate, encrusting or sometimes with gibbosities on the surface.  Corallites 1.5 to 2.5 mm in diameter, level or projecting upto 5 mm, 2 to 4 mm apart.  Septa in three cycles, all narrow at the top of the wall, vertically descending, the primaries almost reaching to the centre of the fossa.  Surface coenenchyme with prominent spinules which extends over the thecal wall.  Wall costate.

There are two specimens in the collections of the Madras Government Museum with the locality marked as Pamban (Near Mandapam).  One is labeled A. myriophthalma while the other is labeled A. pulvinaria.  However, there is little difference between the two.  Both are explanate, thick cushion shaped.  The corallites are 1.5 to 2 mm in diameter, only slightly projecting smaller ones level.  Adjacent corallites 2 to 4 mm apart.  The third cycle of septa is rudimentary.  First cycle narrow at first, later broaden to reach the centre of the axial fossa.  These specimens have almost the same characters of the one described by Pillai and Scheer (1976) from Addu Atoll.

Yet another specimen obtained from Kiltan Island in the northern Lakshadweep was found washed ashore.  The surface of the corallum is slightly worn out and there is no way to assess the elevation of the corallites.  The primary cycle of septa is very conspicuous.  

Astraeopora arenaria appears to be not sufficiently demarcated from A. myriophthalma.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
