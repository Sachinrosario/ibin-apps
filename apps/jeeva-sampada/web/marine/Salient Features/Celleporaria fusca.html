SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Zoarium erect, bifurcating, more or less flattened or cylindrical; or multilaminar, sometimes forming thick; brown, mamillated crusts. Oral spines rarely found. Secondary orifices orbicular. Rostra variable; small, bearing a minute ascending suboral avicularium; sometimes projecting beyond the avicularium as a spike; or large, conical, with a correspondingly enlarged avicularium. Suboral avicularia associated with a basal sinus, often visible only in young zooecia. Vicarious avicularia varying in frequency and size; the palate typically elongate, narrowest at the middle and slightly expanded distally; sometimes shorter and wider, with more parallel sides. Ovicells inconspicuous, small, the frontal wall variable in its extent. Opercula nearly semicircular, the proximal margin sometimes slightly curved; with two lateral sclerites. Suboral mandibles with a columella, which is wanting in the vicarious avicularia. Diagnostic characters: The specimens from India and the Indian Ocean are either without rostra or have small conical avicularia not produced into a long spike.
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  
Descriptive characters: As described by Busk (1854), C. fusca is an erect species with flattened branches. It has a large prominent vicarious avicularium, with descending palate; and rostra varying from spiked to complete absence.
Oral spines are rarely visible but have been found, at the sides of the orifice, in Siboga specimens. In extreme cases, the rostra hardly project; and in others the central zooecia have a very low rostrum or none; those of the peripheral zooecia being spikes. These are often narrow and almost cylindrical with a pointed apex. The short conical rostra are not quite median and bear an avicularium with an ascending rostrum on the inner side, its projection often giving rise to a basal sinus. The sinus is of variable occurrence, but it is commonly distinct in young zooecia but not in most of the older ones.
