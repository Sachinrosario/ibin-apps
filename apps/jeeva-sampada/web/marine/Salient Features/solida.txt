Descriptive characters:  Corallum subarborescent, branches much divided.  Top-most branchlets 2-3 cm long, 1-1.5 cm broad, 10-12 mm thick.  A pieces generally devoid of verrucae at the sides of the branches close together or up to 4 mm apart.  Majority of the verrucae are spreading some at right angles to the branch surface.  Calices on verrucae rounded, 1-1.5 mm in diameter, moderately deep.  At the basal parts of the branches the calices are smaller.  At the underside of the branches where the verrucae are poorly developed the calices are very conspicuous and are about 1 mm deep.  Generally 3 to 6 calices are present on an average sized verruca.  Septa and columella poorly developed, mostly absent.

The coenenchymal ornamentation comprises of prominent spines.  Just near the wall there is row of spines, the base slightly invading the calicular fossa, simulating septal ridges.  Between the two rows of spines of the adjacent calices there is usually another set so that the intercalicular area is supplied with three rows of spines.

Remarks:  Pocillopora molokensis Vaughan, 1907 from Hawaii has more or less the same type of growth form as P. solida.  However, P. solida has larger calices and feebler septa than P. molokensis.  The present record of this species is the first from the Indian waters and is based on a unpublished record by Pillai from Wandoor, National Marine Park.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
