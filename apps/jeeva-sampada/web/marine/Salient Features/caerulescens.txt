SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Head moderately small, not distinct from neck which is 
slender but not much elongated; 1 pre and 1 (sometimes2), postoculars; 
2(sometimes3), anterior temporals; 7-8 supralabiales, the 2nd in contact
with the preforntal, the 3rd and 4th touching the eye; 4 lower labials 
in contact with the genials (chin-shields), the posterior pair of which
are usually smaller than the anterior, not well differenciated from the 
surrounding scales. Eye moderate. Body strongly compressed laterally, 
covered with feebly imbricate or juxtaposed scales in 38-54 rows at 
midbody. Ventrals 253-334, distinct throughout, not twice as wide as the
adjascent scales; preanals four.

Diagnostic characteristics: This sea snake is normally bluish-grey above
and yellowish white on the underside, with from 40 to 60 broad dark bands
which are about twice the width of the lighter spaces in between, at last
on the fore body, taper towards the belly and may be incomplete on the thicker
part of the body. With the age pattern may become obscured, the back, in
some old individuals, tuning almost uniform grey. Head black in the young,
sometimes with a light curved mark, dark grey in the adults, often with 
a light streak behind the eye.

Feeding Habit: Piscivorous.
Main food: Fish

Additional Remarks: The subfamily Hydrophiinae of the family Hydrophiidae
covers the maximum number of species (i.e, Eighteen)of sea snakes found 
in the coastal waters of India. All the members are purely aquatic and 
produce live young.

The eighteen species included in the subfamily Hydraphiinae are listed as 
follows:
1. Astrotia stokesii(Grey) 
2. Enhydrina schistousus(Daudin) 
3. Hydrophis caerulescens(shaw) 
4. H.cantoris Gunther
5. H.cyanocinctus(Daudin) 
6. H.fasciatus(schnieder)
7. H.gracilis(shaw)
8. H.lapemoides(grey)
9. H.mamillaris(Daudin)
10. H. nigrocinctus(Daudin)
11. H. obscura (Daudin)
12. H. Ornatus(Grey)
13. H. spiralis(shaw)
14. H. stricticollis(Gunther)
15. Kerillia jerodonii(Grey)

(Abbreviation : H - Hydrophis )
