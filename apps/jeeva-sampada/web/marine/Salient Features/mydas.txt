SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  The carapace is ovoid or heart shaped, smooth, and covered with shields which do not overlap.  The snout is short and the jaws are not hooked, the horny sheath of upper jaw with feebly, of lower jaw with strongly denticulated ridge.  The limbs have generally one claw each on first digit; the second digit of young sometimes has a claw.  The head is not retractile.  The tail is quite short.

Diagnostic characteritics:  Although it is called the green turtle because of its greenish fat, this species is actually light to dark brown in colour, the shields with a pattern of irregular, olive - coloured streaks.  The plastronis yellow.  The young are olive or dark brown above, the limbs margined with yellow, and yellow below, with a large patch on each forelimb and hindlimb. Half-grown specimens bear radiate patterns on the carapace and hence are referred to as "Sunray turtles".

Sex attributes:  Adult males are relatively smaller, with longer tails and somewhat tapered carapaces than adult females.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Adult is a herbivore, feeding upon sea grass and sea weeds, but the young are carnivorous, feeding upon crustaceans, molluscs, and fish.

Main food:  Marine plants

Feeding type

Additional remarks:  In India, the green turtle is recorded from the waters off the coasts of Gujarat, Maharashtra, Gulf of Mannar and off the islands of Andamans and Lakshadweep.  Though it migrates to longer distances, it is primarily a coastal animal, usally inhabiting shallow aters less than 25m in depth and preferes areas sheltered by reefs where it feeds on algae.

The green turtle nests throughout the year but the peak season is sait to be from May to September, as far as India is concerned.  The female turtle lays between 600 to 800 eggs in a year in batches of about 150 eggs each at fortnightly intervals.

The green turtle is considered one of the most valuable of all living marine reptiles.  Every part of the turtle is valuable, and what is more, is marketable; its flesh is turned into tasty speaks; its calipee (The gelatinous material between the shell and bones) is used for turtlye soup; its eggs are a great delicacy; its shell is cut and polished for jewellery.
