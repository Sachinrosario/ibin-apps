SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Fronds narrow, about 2 mm wide. Peristome raised distally and laterally, not tubular except in the marginal zooecia, specially in their young state; the orifice subcircular. Central part of zooecia outlined by a thick, prominent U-shaped ridge, its sides commencing at the lateral margins of the secondary orifice, with a rounded tubercle on either side, proximally to it. Interval between the ridges and the septal lines (which are not prominent) depressed, occupied by numerous, uniserial, small pores. Suboral avicularium of moderate size, wide proximally, directed obliquely to one side of the orifice, the rostrum narrow distally. Ascopore circular, sometimes on a raised tubular projection, at or near the proximal end of the central area. Marginal vicarious avicularia of moderate size, triangular and directed distally. Gonoecia not recognized.

Sex attributes: HermaphroditeDescriptive characters:   As shown by a decalcified specimen, the opercula are deeply situated at the base of a long, immersed peristome; and have not the widely sinuate form of those of Adeonella. The suboral mandibles have an acute inflexed apex.
The ascopore may be funnel-shaped, its narrower end being the external one. This is presumably associated with the necessity for a rapid evacuation of the water in the compensation-sac during the retraction of the polypide, effected by the contraction of strong muscles. The possession of a median pore would seem to be specially useful to a species with a long tubular peristome, the escape of water through which must be partially blocked by the tentacles during retraction.
  Indian specimens recorded by Thornely have specially large and conspicuous ascopores. Except in the greater width of the fronds they agree well with the Siboga specimens of Adeonellopsis arculifera.
