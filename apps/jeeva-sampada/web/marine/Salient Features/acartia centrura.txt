SALIENT  FEATURES :Morphological:   
Diagnostic characteristics:        

Female: Prosome broadly triangular anteriorly, rostral hornwell developed. Relative lengths of prosome and urosome 78:22%. Posterior corners of fifth pedigerous segment rounded with 1 pair of spines on each side, lateral spine longer than dorsal spine. Relative lengths of urosome segments and caudal rami 40:20:16:24%; genital segment longer than wide; posterodorsal surface of genital and second urosome segments armed with 1 pair of spines, former pair smaller; anal segment shortest, without spines, disto-lateral half fringed with hairs. Caudal rami twice as long as wide with hair on lateral margins.         

In leg 5, first segments fused medially, second segment about twice as long as wide with plumose seta nearly twice as long as third segment and terminal spine; third segment expanded basally and narrowed distally terminating in a pointed and non-dentate spine.      

Male: Shape and spination of prosome as in female but lateral spines smaller. Proportions of prosome and urosome 76:24%. Relative lengths of urosome segments and caudal rami 12.4:33.4:25:4:12.8:12.4%. Lateral margins of first urosome segment bilobed with a pair of setae emerging one on either side of lower lobe. Posterior margins of 2nd, 3rd and 4th urosome segments armed with 2 to 6 spines. Small spines seen in between larger ones on segments 3 and 4. Posterior margin of anal segment with 1 pair of spinules. Caudal rami short.          

Right leg of P5 with broad 1st segment, narrow second segment with a strong spine on inner posterior part; 3rd segment with inner lobe of roughly quadrangular shape carrying 1 spinule on distal inner margin; 4th segment long with 1 small spine on inner mid region and terminating in similar spine. Left 5th leg with rather broad first segment; narrow second segment; 3rd segment fringed with hairs on inner proximal region, having leaf-like process and a small basal spine at about mid-length and apex with spine and short curved process. 

Feeding type : Additional  remarks:     Females of 1.22 to 1.45 mm, quite similar to Acartia centrura female, were found in the plankton samples examined from Cochin Backwaters, males were totally absent. This species penetrated and spread throughout  between January and June along with high salinity water from the Shelf (Tranter & Abraham, 1971). It is possible that the males are not as tolerant to the lower salinity conditions of the estuary as their female, which may lead to an ecological segregation of sex. Or it may be that the females are what Hutchinson (1951) has named “fugitive species”. Tranter & Abraham (1971) discussed the possibility that the Acartiidae of Cochin Backwater are a typical example of a “non-equilibrium population”.
