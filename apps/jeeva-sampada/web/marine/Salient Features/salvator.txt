SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  Head elongate, neck long and body flattened. Limbs very strong, armed with sharp, curved claws.  Head very flexible and can be moved in all directions.  Tongue extremely long, slender, deeply forked (like that of a snake) and can be withdrawn into a sheath at its base.  Eyes prominent Ear-opening distinct.  Tail compressed and not shed easily.

Diagnostic characteritics:  Head covered with small scales, body with minute juxtaposed scales and tubercles above and smooth, squarish shields below.  Dark brown or black above, with eyllow-ocelli and yellowish below.  The largest and heaviest of the Indian lizards.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Carnivorous

Main food:  Fish, crabs, birds, eggs, rats, and carrion.

Feeding type

Additional remarks:  Using its laterally compressed tail, the water monitor swims with ease, going far out in the sea in search of food.  Besides the fish and crabs, the water monitor feeds upon molluscs, baby turtles, juvenile crocodiles, and snakes.  The lizard seems to be particularly fond of eggs of other lizards and birds devouring 8 to 10 at on sitting.  The female lays from 15 to 30 eggs and deposits them in holes on river banks or in trees near water.  A monitor, if prevented from escaping, rises high on its legs, hisses loudly, sways its body and lashes its tail in readiness to defend itself.

The monitor's eggs are a great delicacy and the animal itself provides a wholesome meal.  The populations of monitors are depleted because of habitat destruction and overexploitation for their valuable skin.  The two subspecies are the Andaman water monitor, Varanus salvator andamanensis, and the Nicobar water monitor, V.S. nicobarensis.
