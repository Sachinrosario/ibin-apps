SALIENT FEATURES:Morphological:  	Zoaria saucer-shaped.  Zooecia on the convex side of the zoarium.  The rim of the colony crenulated owing to the presence of vibracula.  The concave side of the colony tuberculated and bears thin ridges which radiates from a central point and branch at the region of the basal part of the vibracula, a median ridge always extending to the distal tip.  Ancestrula single and the first two daughter zooids are placed at an obtuse angle to the ancestrula and hence the center is generally triaxial, the first axis passing through the distal proximal extension of the ancestrula and the other two diverging from the primary axis.  The centre of the colony appears as eight rayed star.  The size of the primary and the first two secondary zooecia are generally smaller than those of the rest of the colony.  Central zooecia closed by a lamina at the level of the cryptocyst.  Gymnocyst present, but very narrow.  Cryptocyst uniformly tuberculated and descending.  Oval opesia deep.  Vibracula invariably present in all zooecia, with small and distinct auricular projections on one side. No seta observed.  Ovicells absent.  
Diagnostic characters: Even though a wide range of substrata has been attributed, the present material was collected from substrata where shell fragments and gravelly sand predominate.  
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite
Descriptive characters:  Colonies show the presence of small sand particles in the central region of the concave side of the zoarium, which indicates that the ancestrula attaches to a sandy substratum.  	Usually the gymnocyst is very narrow and slightly crenulated.  The uniformly tuberculated cryptocyst is very extensive in the seven central zooecia.  Usually the central zooecia are covered with irregular lamina, but it is noticed that two colonies have central zooecia without this lamina.  An interesting fact noticed regarding the auricular projection is that they always alternate in the succeeding rows of zooecia starting from the centre of the colony.
