SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:

The body is twisted about the commencement of the abdominal region so that when the abdomen is viewed dorsally the part anterior to it is seen from a lateral aspect.
The segmentation of the body is very clear , especially on the ventral side. As in all isopods, there are seven free thoracic segments in front of which is a large and prominent shield of crescentic shape. This carapace covers the cephalothorax of six segments. The anterior edge of this plate projects freely forwards for a short length. This free, flexible margin is probably of use in getting a closer and firmer contact on the surface of the hosts body that an abrupt sharp edge would secure. The dorsal side of the carapace is marked by a pair of conspicuous eyes consisting of 4 elements. The dorsal surface of the body is distinguished by two rows of 10 phosphorescent spots, one row on each side of the median line behind the carapace. Each spot is circular with irregular edges and is of a dirty yellow colour by daylight. The telson is a broad plate with a median emarginate(fig 9).

Appendages:-
The first antenna extends ventrally beyond the carapace and is not hidden by it. The protopodite appears to be 3 jointed, cylindrical. The endopodite is short, slender and pointed- dactylose while the exopodite is two jointed , twice as long as the endopodite (fig, 3). The second antenna is far longer being nearly twice as long as the first. The three joints of the protopodite are cylindrical and the last two are marked by their distal edges being prolonged into a spine each (fig.4).

The mouth parts are attached medially just behind a rostral or labral plate which however does not form a complete sheath. The mandible, the 1st maxilla and the 2nd maxilla are attached together with their distal tips directed inwards and forwards. The mandible appears to be simple, blunt-tipped and probably with a sharp posterior edge. The tips of the two mandibles are directed medially. The 1st maxilla is three-jointed, the basal joints are stout while the third is long, slender and pointed. The 2nd maxilla is two-jointed and tapers into a pointed process (fig 5). As in all Isopods, there are seven pairs of six articled thoracic legs. The legs are short and prehensile. The proximal part curves outward, while the distal part ending in a long slender claw like dactylus is curved inwards (fig 5).. this facilitates to hold on the hosts body. There are five pairs of biramous foliacious pleopods which are all alike and exhibit no trace of articulation. The exopodite of each bears a short blunt spine at its outer distal edge and four setae. The endopodite bears three setae fig 7,. The uropod on the either side is extended straight behind and is parallel and biramous. The protopodite is single-jointed bearing the endopodite on its inner side a little before its distal edge which is drawn out into a small spine on the outer side and bears the exopodite. The exopodite is long, cylindrical and bears three spines, the middle one being the longest. The endopodite is shorter and more slender. It is single jointed and bears a spine fig 8. the two uropods with their tapering spiny ramii are pressed close together, to form the pointed, posterior end of the body and seve as a hold on the host.

Additional remarks:
Microniscus acartii was described by Gnaramurthi and Krishna swamy 1948, based on specimen collected from the Cephalothorax of the copepod, Acartia erythracea. The authors could not determine whether their specimens were male or female or larvae and hence they were provisionally described as different species of the genus Microniscus with the specific name indicating the genus of the host copepod.
