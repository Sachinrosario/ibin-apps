Descriptive characters:  
Description of specimens from Kiltan Island:  Corallum cespitocorymbose, several branches arise from an encrusting base.  Main branches 6 to 7 cm thick, peripheral ones horizontal, with occasional coalaacens, main branches divides into 4 to 5 branches rising to subequal height, 3 to 4 cm long, average thickness 5 mm.  Axial corallites 3 mm in diameter 1 to 2 mm exsert, wall moderately thick, reticulate, opening rounded.  Primary septa meet down in the fossa.  Often 2 to 3 secondaries are seen. Prominent radial corallites tubular, but the inner wall stands a little below the level of the outer, ascending or appressed, never spreading more than 450.  Larger ones 3 to 4 mm long, average 1.5 mm in thickness, others slightly smaller, larger ones one to two buds.  At the basal parts of the branches many are subimmersed.  Truly immersed corallites are wanting.  In the radials the directive septa are visible, others spiny.  Second cycle wanting.  Wall of the radials striated and strongly vermiculate (In the type kept in USNM the wall is porous and the echinulationsare close set and numerous).  Surface coenenchyme echinulate.  Corallum rather dense in section.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
