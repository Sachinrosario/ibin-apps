SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Main root swelling into a bulb-like enlargement. Branches capable of being strongly inflected by the contraction of flexor zooecii muscles; traversed by alternating oblique joints corresponding in number with the zooecia, each zooecium traversed by two joints. Basal walls of zooecia composed of numerous platelets. Spines and scutum wanting. Marginal avicularia constantly present, rather large,; the rare frontal avicularia much larger, both kinds with a more or less aquiline beak. Ovicells globose, extremely large, wider than long, obliquely situated, occurring in restricted numbers; the opercula of the fertile zooecia much broader than those of the others.
Diagnostic characters: Sex attributes:   HermaphroditeDescriptive characters: Although really biserial, the branches appeared at first sight in serial, owing to the fact that the expanded parts of the zooecia overlap slightly. Each zooecium consisted of three parts:- the third or distal section, expanded and containing the polypide; a narrower second section more proximally; and a short and wide first section which united the zooecium to its predecessor in the same longitudinal row. In the frontal view, the third or principal section was nearly straight on its external border, and strongly curved on the median border. The operculum was nearly terminal, and had a strong sub-marginal sclerite, prolonged basally, on each side, into a projection for the insertion of the occlusor muscle. The polypide entirely confined to the third section; its tentacles, which were 22, lay on the external side of the alimentary canal
  The fertile zooecia were broader than the others, and they occurred in a definite position, no branch developing more than a single ovicell, a fact which Harmer judged to be due to their large size.
