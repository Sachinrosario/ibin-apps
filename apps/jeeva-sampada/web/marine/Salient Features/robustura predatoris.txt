Salient features:

Morphological:

General Characters:  The pleon consisting of three segments is wider than the anterior thoracie segments.  The antennule is of five segments.  Both the peduncle as well as the flagellum of the antenna are five-segmented.  The mandibular palp is reduced to a single spine.  The first pereiopod is a well-armed gnathopod.  The first pleopod is absent in both sexes while the second is absent in the female.  The third pleopod is united at base and is uniramous.  The fourth pleopod consists of a single sessile ramus.  The uropod is short and stout, with exopod reduced and endopod as long as the ptotopod.  There is no external marsupium and the genital openings of female are large.

Species characters:  Body long, cylindrical, long, colourless and tramparent.  Maximum width in the sixth free segment.  Head longer than broad, frontal margin tranverse entire, extending forward slightly over the base of the antennae.  Two faint graves obliquely delimit the subocular areas, but there is no trace of eyes.  Carapace in free of marketings and spines dorsally, except for a pair of semicircular thickenings of chitin near the posterior edge.  In the peracon, the sternal plates are so far larger than the tergal that the articulationof the legs in within a fourth of the depth of each segment and the articulation of all the legs except the first chelate leg are visible on the dorsal side of the body.  The terga of the second, third are fourth free theraciyments have their antric edges produced into a pair of lobes on either side of the mid-dorsal line.  The last thoracic segment in the nobe bears a pair of penial styles turned in oposite direction.  Each is sharp-pointed, armed with two opines.  In the fifth theracic segment of the female, the oviducts from the ovary dilate in a funnel-like manner and open vertrally through two large openings provided with chitinous rims and large flap.  The funnel-like dilatation are present in all adult females.  There is no external brood pouch.  The pleon three-segments in stouter, being broader and deeper and has a mere cylindrical form.  There is no trace of telson.  The uropods are attached terminally to the posterior convex end in each way that they extend behind, diverging at on angle of 30 degree to each other.

Appendages:  The antennule is shorter than the peduncle of the antennae and is of two large proximal joints and three smaller distal joints.  The terminal joint bears a long narrow scale-like aesthete, a long stout spines and three shorter spines.  The antennae are less than a fourth of the body in length.  The five-jointed peduncle forms four fifths of the length while the short flagellum is made up of five very short segments tapering to the tip.  There are four long spines and an aesthete on the terminal joint.  There are a few shorter spines on other joints.  A long articulated aesthete is seen at the base of the flagellum.  The proximal joint of the peduncle bears a short thick recurved lobe.  The mandible has a long, bent body ending in an incisor lobe bearing four stout teeth.  It bears a palpal vestige in the form of a small cushion like joint bearing a long spine.  In front of the mandibles, the labrum, a semi-circular plate, forms the anterior border of the circular oral cavity, while behind the mandibles the narrow horseshoe-shaped labium forms the posterior margin.   The maxillule has a small rounded proximal endite bearing two curved spines and a much larger distal endite armed with zix stout spines.  The maxilla is three-lobed.  The proximal lobe bears a claw wth a spine on either side of it.  It is more posterior and ventral and is usually hidden by the bract-like epipodite of the maxillipede.  The other two lobes are more medial and anterior, each bearing a stout spines.  The maxillipede has a short cushion-like coxa which bears a short broad epipodite sheathing laterally the base of the palp as far as its second joint and also covering the proximal endite of the maxilla.  The basis is shorter and bears a spine as well as a long narrow endite united to its fellow, medially.  Distally the basis bears the four-jointed palp.  The terminal joint tapers and bears at its tip four long stout curved spines, while each of the other three bears a spine on the inner edge.

The first pereiopod is chelate and is similar in both sexes.  The basis has a toothed lobe and a seta as in the other thoracic legs.  The propodus is large and forms nearly half the appendage.  Besides a long spine near the base and four on its distal end, it bears two more spines near the outer edge.  The edge of the propodus, and the large claw-like dactylus are heavily armed.  The finger closes down on an articulated spiny tuberele which bears a banached spines.   Such a powerfully armed gnathopod may be of use in capturing prey.  The carnivorous natur eof this isopod is evidenced by the contents of the gut consisting of dismembered appendages of copepods visible through the transparent body-wall.  The remaining six pereiopods are uniform, similar in both sexes and resemble those of the previous form except in details of setation.  The basis is the largest joint and bears a toothed lobe and a spine.  The propodus bears four spines on the inner edge and as many on the outer.  The dactylus is short and bears a hair-like spine and two stout claws of which one is larger.  In the very young form the last pereiopod is absent and is grown by stages.  At the stage preceding the adult, the dactylus is conical and has no claws, which evidently appear only in the adult.

The pleopods differ in the sexes.  In the male the most anterior pair is best developed and arise close together medially from the hind edge of the first abdominal segment.  The coxopodites appear fused into a broad plate while the bases remain separate.  Each basis bears a vestigil exopod and a large endopod.  The endopods being far longer than their bases, they extend beyond the next segment.  Each bears a small finger like process subterminally while ending in an acute tip.  Behind the tip on the outer aspect is found a deep circular cavity for the reception of sperms.  Comparison with the copulatory endopods of other isopods will sow that this front pair of pleopods is homologous with the second pair of abdominal appendages.  Therefore, it is legitimate to infer that the first segment of the pleon and its appendages are suppressed in this species.  The nest pair of pleopods (the third ) also have a broad fused protopod but is uniramous.  This ramus, probably the endopod, is a single jointed plate like structure which in the young forms has a four-toothed edge but in entire in adults.  While the protopod of this third pleopod is attached to the hind edge of the second abdominal segment,  the next pleopod, the fourth pair, is attached to the anterior region of the third segment.  This pair consists of two single-jointed flat plates, attached one on either side in a sessile manner, their being no protopod.  A little behind the articulation of this pair, the third abdominal segment exhibits a faint groove traversing its entire breadth.  Towards the postero-lateral corners of the abdomen a small fleshy papilla on either side can be seen bearing two long stout spines.  It is possible that these are the vestiges of yet another pair of appendages, the fifth pleopods.  The uropods, the sixth pair of pleopods, appear short, stout and stumpy.  Each has a protopod bearing four spines, of which three are arranged one behind the other on the inner edge with which they are adnate.  The exopod is a short reduced lobe bearing two spines.  Te endopod is far larger, nearly twice the length of the protopod.  It tapers and bears six spines.  Except for the absence of the second copulatory pair of pleopods found in the male, the female resembles the male in all other respects.

Additional remarks:  Twenty-five specimens of this isopod wer collected by Gnanamurthee (1954) from Chennai beach in the region of the said bar laid across the mouth of the river Cooum.  Nevertheless the author considers the species really belonging to the shallow sea bottom.  These isopods are found in the company of copepods, nematodes and other inccropuma of marine origin.
