SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  Head pear-shaped, rather broad at the occiput, slightly distinct from neck; nostrils valvular, directed upwards; eyes small, beady, pupoil vertical.  Lower jaw prominent like that of a bulldog's and hence the common name; rear-fanged, with a mild venom.  Body stout, cylindrical, with strongly keeled scales, in 23-25 rows; ventrals 137-159, well developed anals paired.  Tail short, compressed at base, tapering to a point.

Descriptive characters:  Bluish grey in water and light grey, when dry.  The dorsal pattern consists of a series of ill-defined bars which are conspicuous on the anterior part of the body.  Sometimes the dorsal bars are broken up into spots.  Underside greenish or yellowish, with gregular dark crossbars and lateral blotches.  There is a dark streak running through the eye.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Carnivorous

Main food:  Fish, occasionally eats small crabs

Additional remarks:  The dog-faced watersnake is found in the brackish waters of tidal rivers, creeks and estuaries.  It is a heavily built snake which is adept at swimming as well as climbing.  it moves on land somewhat like a 'sidewinder'.  In shallow water it flicks its tail to lure the fish towards its head.  It often climbs onto the low vegetation of the mud flats and easily slips into the water, if disturbed.  It is a lethargic and inoffensive reptile, biting only under serious provocation.  Despite the enlarged rear fangs and the functional venom glands, the dog-faced watersnake is not regarded as dangerous to humans.  It is a live-bearing species, the female producing 8 to 26 young at a time.
