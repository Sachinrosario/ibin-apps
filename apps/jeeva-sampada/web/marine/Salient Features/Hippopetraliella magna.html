SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:   Zoarium very large, commonly tubular and Hemescharan; covered by a thick brown epitheca on both surfaces. Zooecia also large, in regular longitudinal lines, with distinct marginal valleys; rather flat, reticulato-punctate. Orifices large, slightly constricted laterally, usually widest proximally. Condyles often vestigial and not visible in frontal view. Lyrula and proximal teeth wanting. Avicularia lateral oral, on one or both sides, generally small and sub-circular. Large horizontal frontal avicularia rare. Ovicells not or hardly projecting beyond the general frontal level. Basal surface with scattered minute pores, sometimes with small pore-chambers inconstant in position. Opercula widest basally, the basal sclerite incomplete, represented only by its lateral portions.Diagnostic characters: The ovicells are distinctive of this species. They are so deeply immersed on the distal zooecium that the epitheca covering them is flat, not or hardly raised above the general frontal surface. When the membrane is removed, they have a characteristic appearance, their numerous minute pores contrasting with the large pores of the frontal shield.   
Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters:   DOrbigny has described this form as the largest species known to him. The zoarium is known to reach 100mm in diameter; the tubes being 3-6 mm across. The zooecia are especially large and robust, more or less subquadrate; the lateral parts passing into a thick distal peristome, the orifices generally somewhat sunk. The epitheca conceals the pores, which are large and, after its removal, conspicuous; some being situated distally to the peristome, which may bear small tubercles and the surface is often calcified enough to close these pores. The frontal pores are in a bold reticulation, the thick bars of which are produced into tubercles at their union with one another. The rosette-plates are multiporous, scattered over the lateral and distal walls. The orifices are large, slightly constricted laterally, near their proximal end, by thin marginal lobes.
  The avicularia are generally unilateral, small and nearly circular. Large short and wide avicularia occur rarely on the frontal surface, the end of the rostrum being rounded.
  Hincks did not find any ovicells in L. gigas, they occur in the specimen from Madras determined by Thornely (1916).
