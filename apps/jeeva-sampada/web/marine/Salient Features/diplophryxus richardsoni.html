SALIENT FEATURES :
Morphological :

Female : The body is roughly triangular in shape, 2.0 mm long and 1.5 mm broad. The anterior part of the body is expanded on the right side so that the cephalon is shifted towards the left side, producing a pronounced asymmetry. The cephalon is slightly longer than broad and almost completely immersed in the peraeon, its anterior border is faintly trilobed. The peraeon is large and fully segmented on the left side, but the segmentation disappears towards the right side, which is prominently hunched. The first two peraeon segments are arched forwards and embrace the cephalon. The pleon is clearly five-segmented and the segmentation is incomplete both on the dorsal and ventral sides.

The first antenna are minute and slightly shifted to the dorsal side of the cephalon. The right side of the body has four normally developed pearaopods, the first two are placed in front of the cephalon and the other two appear between the lateral plates of th pleon. All the seven peraeopods are fully developed on the left side. The first peraeopod is placed in front of the cephalon, peracopods two to seven gradually increase in length.

The first four pleon segments carry two pairs of well developed pleopods. The dorsal pair is large, laminate and biramous , each orginates by a ahort flat basal stalk which appears as a continuation of the pleon segment. The ventral pleopods are small, the rami are linear and placed slightly obliquely on the ventral side of the pleon. The fifth pleon segment has no pleopods but there is a pair of uropds. The body is deep violet in colour.

Diagnostic characteristics :

The presence of all the seven peraeopods on the shorter side and of only four Peraeopods on the longer side of the body shows the identity of the species.

The parasite is found attached to the ventral side of the abdomen of the host.
