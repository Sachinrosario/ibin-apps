SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:

Diagnostic characters: Mouth pointing upwards when protracted, lateral line ending below middle of soft portion of dorsal fin; It is distinguished from Secutor insidiator  by its greater body depth (contained 1.4 to 1.74 times in standard length) as well as by the presence of scales on cheek.

 

Sex attributes : Sexual dimorphism is expressed through the visibility of the light organ through transparent patches of skin. In S.ruconius these clear areas are on the ventral margin of the opercular cavity, with the spots in males appearing to be larger.

Internally also the light organ exhibits dimorphism with the males having enlarged ventral lobes.                                                                                   Ref  no: 15

 

Descriptive characters: Body oval , strongly compressed  and deep. Ventral profile much more convex than the dorsal profile. Rostro-occipital line of head concave. Snout length less than that of orbit diameter. Mouth small, oblique, when protracted forms a tube directed upwards. Mouth small, lips broad and thin,; lower lip smaller than the upper which is like a ring over the lower. Gape of mouth opposite middle of eye. Tips of maxilla far in front of front border of eye. Lower margin of lower jaw slightly concave, at right angles to mouth slit. Teeth numerous, very  small, in a villiform band. Inter-orbital space convex bounded by two ridges, which continue as outer edges of the nuchal spine. Median portion is elevated. One small spine on head, immediately above eye and opposite its front border from where the ridge bounding the inter-orbital space commences. Pre-opercle with an obtuse angle, its lower margin finely serrate.

 Scales all over the body including breast. Lateral line convex from the beginning and runs less convex to the dorsal profile. Dorsal and anal spines compressed and weak.  2nd dorsal spine longest. 2nd anal spine shorter than the 2nd dorsal. Ventrals with axillary scales. Their tips do not reach the origin of the anals. Caudal deeply incised, lobes pointed.

 

Colour: Body silvery, with  about 10 black or grey vertical bands on the back, extending to a little below thwe lateral line, anteriorly commencing below tip of nuchal spine and posteriorly extending up to the end of the soft dorsal and are often sub-divided into spots. Membrane between 2nd and 5th dorsal spines black in the upper one third portion. A prominent curved black band, running from lower margin of eye to beyond posterior angle of lower jaw. Abdomen silvery, dotted with black pigmented dots. Pectoral axil black.   

                                                                         Ref no: 1,5,6.

Meristic Characters: D.VIII, 16; A. III, 14; P.16; V.I,15; C.24     Ref  no:12

 

Main food: It feeds mainly on crustaceans. Juveniles of about 4 cm length feed mainly on copepods and cirripede larvae, a few other small crustaceans and larvae of lamellibranchs.                                                                       Ref no:2

 Feeding type: Adults are mainly bottom feeders.

Eggs and Larvae:

Characteristics:

The following description of the larvae of  S.ruconius  was given by John, 1951, based on the work done in the Zoological Research Laboratory of the Madras university during 1934-1936.

 

                                             

                                                      10 mm larva of Secutor ruconius      Ref no: 10

 

             10mm larva: There is only one anterior supra-orbital recurved spine. The pre-opercular edge is  toothed. The angular spine of the pre-opercle is prominently long, strong and is toothed. The dorsal fin has 8 spines  and 16 rays. The 2nd spine is serrated throughout its length. The caudal of 17 rays is slightly wedged. The second anal spine is serrated throughout its length. The pectoral has 20 rays. Arrangement of Chromatophores: In front of the dorsal fin there is only a single black spot. A second one is at the beginning of the soft dorsal. The last 10 rays of the anal fin have each a black spot at the base. The abdomen is  strewn with chromatophores both stellate and simple.
