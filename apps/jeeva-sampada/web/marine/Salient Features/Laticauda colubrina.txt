Morphological:  

Head shields entire; nostrils lateral; nostrils separated by the internasal(s); one preocular and 2 postoculars; 7-8 supralabials, the 3rd and 4 th touching the eye; 1 posterior and 2 anterior temporals; 5 lower labials in contact with the genials, both pairs of which are well developed and touchingone another, the anterior pair amaller than the posterior; oral margin with elongated scales.

Body subcylindrical, only slightly compressed, of equal diameter throughoutl covered with imbricate and smooth scales throughout, in from 21-23 (rarely 25) rows; ventrals 213-245, large, wide, being about four times as broad as long; caudals 37-47 (males), 29-35 (females); anals 2.

Diagnostic characteritics:  

the body colour of this sea krait is light or dark bluish - grey above, with numerous, conspicuous dark - brown or black rings around the body; the bands are of uniform width or narrowing across the belly. the head is entirely black, with a curved yellow mark on the top, this colour extending backwards o each side of the head crossing over the eye to the upper lip; lower jaw with a streak on either side, with an elongated yellow patch in between. the belly is yellow.




Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Piscivorous

Main food:  Fish

  

Additional remarks:  

The banded sea krait is very rare in Indian and Indo-Chinese waters. It is distributed from the Bay odf Bengal and seas south of Japan to the coast of Australia nad islands of Oceania.

Sea snakes of the sunfamily Laticaudinae are amphibous and tend to axcquire terestrial habits. These snakes are usually found on small, rocky islands, generally in the cracks of cliffs under rocks or among the exposed roots of trees that generally grow along the beaches, where they moult, mate and nest. they are reported to be active during the late afternoon or at night. Unlike other sea snakes which produce live young, the laticaudids lay eggs on land and hence are oviparous.

Observations made on the behaviour of the common sea krait indicvate that it is gentle in dispostion and reluctant to bite unless provoked. More research is needed on the biology of the species of the subfamily Laticaudinae.
