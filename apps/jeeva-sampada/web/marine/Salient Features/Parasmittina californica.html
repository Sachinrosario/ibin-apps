SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Zoarium encrusting. Whitish in hue. Flat zooecia arranged in longitudinal rows alternately. Front with numerous small tubercles. Moderately sized marginal areolae. Thin peristome nearly circular and tubular, with six spines or spine bases. Sunk primary orifice. A descending sinus. Lyrula absent. Unilateral avicularia narrow and long, raised from the frontal surface, proximo-lateral to the peristome, directing proximally. No spatulate avicularia. Operculum semi-circular with a tongue prolonging into the sinus. The avicularian mandible narrow and long slightly rounded at the tip.

Sex attributes: HermaphroditeDescriptive characters:     Referable to Smittina elongata (Okada & Mawatari, 1936). Differences are, however noticed. Okada & Mawatari have not noticed the presence of spines in their specimens, but in the present form, six spines or spine bases were present. The lateral position of the long avicularium with a narrow, long, mandible, the absence of lyrula, granulated front and the moderately sized areolar pores seem to justify the present placing of the forms under Smittina elongata. The strong development of the cardelles has created a distinct sinus which is nearly circular. This character is neither shown in the figure nor given in the text by Okada & Mawatari.
  A study of the genus Smittina from the available literature has shown that Smittina elongata shows similarities to Smittina signata, another species of Smittina without lyrule, given by Harmer, 1957. (This species is now grouped under the genus Parasmittina, Osburn). Ryland (1966) feels that it will be reasonable to have yet another generic split separating species of Smittina and Parasmittina on the basis of the presence or absence of lyrule. Harmer writes about the Japanese specimens thus: "every zooecium has an elongate avicularium on one side only, often with a slight lateral curvature, the median border being convex and the outer border slightly concave". This feature of the avicularia very closely resembles the figure given by Okada and Mawatari. It is not clear whether S. elongata is only a variant of S. signata. The lateral curvature of the avicularia was not discernible in the present form. The absence of spatulate avicularia, or avicularia with rounded rostra, is a character by which Smittina elongata differs from Smittina signata. Ovicells have not been noticed in the present material. The ovicells of S. elongata are hyperstomial and porous, those of S. signata are slightly convex, immersed the frontal surface with minute pores.
