Descriptive characters:  The following is the original description of P. nobilis Verrill (1864).  “Corallum firm and dense, forming large round-topped or hemispherical clumps, often a foot or 18 inches in diameter.  Branches nearly equal in length, separated by regular intervals of 0.4 to 0.5 inch, elongated, often nearly rounded, 0.6 to 0.7 of an inch in diameter, regularly forking and not elongated at the obtusely rounded ends, in other cases even in same specimen, dilated at the ends to a breadth of 2 to 3 inches and more or less plicated.  Summits of branches generally verrucose, the verrucae similar to those of the sides, but usually smaller and more crowded.  Lateral verrucae very numerous, rather crowded, the intervals being usually lees than their diameter; small regular spreading obliquely or even standing at right angles to the surface; tapering and somewhat rounded at the ends, but angular and containing but few quite large cells.  Between the verrucae the cells are large, numerous, usually less than half their own diameter apart.  Septa but little developed, very narrow usually indistinct or wholly obsolete.  Columella very small and wanting.  Surface of the coenenchyme regularly covered with rather small spiniform granules.  Colour of the unbleached coral deep yellowish brown”.

Remarks:  P. verrucosa and P. meandrina are very close to each other.  Some authors have treated them as same.  However, the major difference between the two are in the nature of verrucae.  In P. nobilis the verrucae are uniform, mostly rounded at the top and spreading at right angle to the branch surface, while in P. verrucosa the verrucae are large, irregular in size and mostly ascending.
