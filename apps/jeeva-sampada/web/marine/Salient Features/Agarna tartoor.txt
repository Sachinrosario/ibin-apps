SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:
Female: Body highly asymmetrical, hunched on one side. Cephalon small, hidden in dorsal view, its anterior border rounded, posterior part slightly broader. First peraeon segment two and a half times as broad as long, anterior border sinuous, antero-lateral parts highly expanded, with slightly sinuous border, posterior border evenly rounded. Peraeon segments two to seven sub equal, expanded on one side, producing the characteristic hunch. First three pairs of coxal plates visible from above, first and seventh segments without coxal plates, fourth segment with two pairs of plates; coxal plates of the second segment large, almost oval, fourth to seventh narrow and elongate, last two slightly broader behind. Coxal plates of the shorter side of third segment much larger than that of the corresponding side. Pleon slightly immersed in the peraeon, almost as broad as the seventh peraeon segment; only four segments clearly visible, all sub equal in length and breadth. Telson perfectly triangular and slightly longer than broad, sides converging to an acuminate apex; dorso-median carina distally indistinct.

First antennae slightly separated at their base, eight jointed, second antenna nine to ten jointed. Mandible well developed, incisor part tooth like and molar provided with a ciliated membranous lamina, palp stout, segments indistinctly separated, third segment very well. Distal outer part of basal segment of maxillipid expanded, apex with four hooks and a number of minute spines. Distal part of second maxilla produced and covered with scales. Dactyli of legs moderately falcate. Uropods small, peduncle broader than long, rami lamellar, narrowing towards tip and exopod longer than endopod.

Body uniformly white, turning cream yellow in alcohol. 

Male: Body symmetrical, regularly broadening to the seventh peraeon segment. Head roughly triangular, anterior border of cephalon deflexed downwards. First peraeon segment longest but narrowest, its anterior border slightly sinuous at the middle; antero-lateral corners somewhat produced; segments two to seven sub equal in length. Coxal plates well developed, second and third large, fourth to seventh narrow and long. Pleon immersed and considerably narrower than peraeon, segments sub equal in length and breadth. Telson almost triangular, apically blunt, as long as broad. Uropods like those of the female, male appendix on second pleopod shorter than endopod, its proximal border serrated.

	First antenna eight jointed, third and eight segments with a bunch of setae on the dorsal side; segments four to eight with a bunch of short sensory setae on the lower side. Second antenna longer, nine jointed, fourth segment with one and fifth with two setae, last segment with a bunch of setae. Rami of uropods with a few small apical setae.

	Body white, with large dark chromatophores on cephalon and proximal part of Telson.

Second larva: Body long and perfectly parallel sided. Head as long as broad and anteriorly sub truncate; eyes large and elongate oblong. First peraeon segment very long, more than twice as long as second, segments two to six sub equal, seventh small. Pleon as broad as peraeon. Telson equal in length and breadth and apically rounded. Uropods overreaching Telson, endopod broadly oval and exopod elongate ovate, inner and distal borders of both rami setose, exopod with two large apical spines.

	First antenna slender, eight jointed, contiguous at base, each segment with a bunch of dorsal setae, last five segments with long sensory setae. Second antenna ten jointed, last five segments with a bunch of dorsal setae, second and fourth segments with a long plumose seta on the lower border. First peraeopods with an outer spine on the third and fourth segments and two spines on the sixth.

	Body white, with large highly branched chromatophores.

Diagnostic characteristics: The first peraeon segment is considerably expanded so as to hide the cephalon in dorsal view. Pleon is narrower than the peraeon and the Telson is longer than broad, with an acuminate apex. In the male, the pleon is considerably narrower than the peraeon. 

Additional remarks: Original name assigned to the species by Pillai (1954) is Agarna tartoor. However in his doctoral thesis Pillai (1958) changed it to Agarna tardoore. Nevertheless, the priority goes to the name Agarna tartoor.

Size and age: Length of female: 25.0 mm; male: 12.0 mm.
