SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:                                 

Diagnostic characteristics:         

FEMALE : Cephalon enlarged more than twice as long as broad; cephalon anteriorly conically rounded; rostrum bifid, bulged at base and ending in acute points, posterior corners of pedigerous segment V pointed, symmetrical and project posteriorly; lateral margin of pedigerous segment V with a short seta each on either side; Urosome two segmented, genital segment as long as urosome II; genital  opening situated on a cushion-like base, which is bulged; caudal rami three times longer than broad; antenna 1 with 17 perceptible segments; 5th leg asymmetrical; Re of two segments, those of left leg distinctly longer; Re 1 with one median and one disto-lateral spine; Re 2 ending in an acute spine and carry two outer marginal spines; all spines are with fine lateral serrations.        

MALE  :  Prosome resembles that of female except that right posterior margin of pedigerous segment V asymmetrically pointed, being longer than that of left; Urosome of five segments; urosome II produced at its distal posterior margin into a spinous process with acuminate tip; caudal rami more than three times longer than broad; right antenna 1 geniculate, segments 13-16 swollen; segment 17 with a distal dorsal spine directed distally; segment 18 with a denticulated plate  on its dorsal margin carrying about 31 villiform teeth; fusion segment 19-21 with a small teeth on its proximal half; 5th leg: right leg: terminal segment modified as chela; palm – like joint of segment (chela) with three inner marginal processes, which are blunt and tooth – like; distal claw of chela provided with three processes along its inner margin; subterminal segment more than three times longer than broad; left leg: Re 1 with two outer marginal spines; one at its mid – outer margin and another disto – laterally; Re 2 with two outer marginal spines and terminates in a strong acute spine, which is provided with fine setules on its outer margin.

Additional remarks:          This species is fairly widely distributed in the Indian Ocean and the Western Pacific. Wilson’s (1942) record of this species from Carnegie  Cruise No.7, Stn. 32( Caribbean Sea, 15° 18’ N, 68 ° 11’ W) has been shown to be erroneous and the material described as a new species, Calanopia biloba by Bowman(1957).        
Bowman’s (1957) remark that Gurney (1927) did not find C. elliptica in the Suez Canal is not correct as it was recorder by Gurney from Port Taufiq and Kabret, the latter fron the Canal. Apparently what Bowman meant was that C. minor Scott was not obtained during the Cambridge Suez Expedition reported on by Gurney though Thompson and A.Scott (1903) had earlier collected it from Suez Canal and in the Mediterranean near Port Said.      
From its distribution it is clear that C.elliptica is chiefly a neritic species. Giesbrecht (1896) and Cleve (1901) have given descriptions of this species from the Red Sea and Malay Archipelago respectively. There is hardly any account on the life – history stages of this species from this region, and a detailed description of the species itself will be desirable.
