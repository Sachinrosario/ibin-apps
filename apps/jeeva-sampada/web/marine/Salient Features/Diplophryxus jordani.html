Ref No: Chopra, 1923
Morphological:
FEMALE:
The colouration of the body seems to be very characteristic. The general color is white, but the thorax on both its surfaces has large pigmented areas. Dorsally the thorax is coloured dark brown along the shorter side of the body, and a patch of a similar colour, but larger in size, runs along the other side of the body, which is really formed by the large marsupium extending dorsally beyond the limits of the body proper. Ventrally all the oostegites of the shorter side have two broad bands of the thorax.
The head is deeply sunk in the thorax and is, on the dorsal surface partly covered over by the first oostegite. The first pair of antennae are proportionately much longer and are composed of two, somewhat flattened, joints of which the second, is about as long as the first.
The first five thoracic segments are crowded together but the last two, and especially the seventh, are considerably larger than the rest. The incubatory lamellae the shorter sides are four small plates, partially overlapping one another, placed near the anterior end of the shorter side of the body. Only one leg is present on the longer side, and of the seven on the other all but the first are reduced; some of the anterior ones of this side are partly covered by the small oostegites.
The pleopoda are best seen from the surface, where those of the longer side seem to lie against the dorsal surface of the swollen marsupium. Each pleopod arises as a large stalk and after running outward for a considerable distance divides into two leaflike branches .the undivided stalk is almost as the divided portion. The pleopods of the longer side are larger than those of the body are more or less similar in size and shape. The five abdominal segments are clearly distinguishable on the dorsal surface and the terminal one is posteriorly rounded and not provided with appendages.
MALE:
Body with a few pigment spots irregularly scattered over the thorax and abdomen.
The head is small and its posterior boundary is almost straight. A pair of small distinct eyes present near the anterior margin of the head. They are formed of two large joints of which the basal is somewhat broad and the terminal one is provided with a number of stiff setae. Antennae small and do not project beyond the head.
Abdomen larger and is a little less than two-fifths of the total length and is about half as broad as long .All the segments are completely fused and appendages are totally absent.

Additional remarks:
The large biramous and long-stalked pleopods- two pairs on each of the somite [or two biramous pleopods on each side of the somite] are a very characteristics feature of Diplophryxus jordani.
Size And Age: Length of female: 7.0 mm Breadth of female: 5.0 mm (Chopra, 1930) length of male: 1.2 mm; Breadth of male: 0.3 mm (Chopra 1923)
