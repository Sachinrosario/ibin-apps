Descriptive characters:
    Body elongate, moderately slender and slightly compressed. Posterior end of upper jaw straight above, slightly concave and not noticeably slanted anteroventrally. Both jaws with an irregular row of minute teeth anteriorly and a single series posterorly. Eye moderate, with adipose eyelid well developed, completely covering eye except for a vertical slit centred on pupil. Oral valve (membrane) at symphysis of upper jaw dusky or transparent. Scales on top of head do not extend forward to beyond centre of pupil; gill rakers (including rudiments) 10-14 upper, 30-39 lower and 41-53 total on first gill arch.
  Shoulder girdle (Cleithrum) margin with 2 small papillae, the lower papilla the larger.
  Two widely separated dorsal fins, the first with 8 spines, the second with 1 spine and 28 to 33 soft rays (including finlet); anal fin with two detached spines followed by 1 spine and 25-29 soft rays (including finlet); terminal dorsal and anal soft rays each consisting of a widely detached finlet; pectoral fin 76.5 to 97 % of head length, tip of appressed fin falling short of to slightly beyond a vertical line from second dorsal fin origin.  
  Lateral line anteriorly with a low regular arch, with junction of curved and straight parts below second dorsal fin between 11th to 13th soft rays; scales in curved part of lateral line 42-62; scutes in curved part 0-4; straight part with 0-4 scales, followed by 30-40 scutes; total scales and scutes in lateral line (excluding caudal scales ) 77- 102. Vertebrae 10+14.

Colour: In life, bluish green above, silvery below; small black blotch on margin of opercle near upper edge. Caudal fin hyaline to dusky brown, second dorsal fin hyaline basally, light dusky distally; other fins mostly hyaline, except pelvic fins of adult males slightly dark.

Feeding habit:
Main food: Fishes, crustaceans, Molluscs and Polychaetes. Among the fishes, Stolephorus spp, Sardinella spp, fish larvae and Leiognathus spp constitute an important component of the diet, whereas among the crustaceans Penaeus spp, Acetes spp, alima larva and copepods were the major components. Molluscs were represented by the pteropod Cresis spp and the cephalopods Loligo and Sepia spp while polychaetes, chaetognaths and other groups of animals formed an insignificant portion of the food. Feeding type: It is a pelagic carnivore on small fishes and zooplankters. Due to the possession of well developed sieving mechanisms with 31-35 gill rakers in the upper and lower arches respectively, zooplankters such as copepods, euphausids, crustacean larvae, fish eggs and larvae were effectively filtered. Absence of canine teeth and other well developed oral armature also evidence the fact that D.russelli feeds essentially by sieving. Vision is supposed to play a major role in the search for food.

Additional remarks:
Food Preference: In spite of the wide spectrum of food components, certain items were found to occur repeatedly on different occasions and at times wholly in the stomach indicating a selectivity or preference for the particular food items. Such food items in the order of preference were Stolephorus spp, Leiognathus spp, Acetes spp, Penaeus spp, Alima larvae, Cresis spp and these were of the most common occurrence.

Food in relation to size: In smaller size groups, planktonic crustaceans form the total diet and is replaced by fishes in larger size groups. This change in food pattern is dependent on the area of filtration formrd by the gill apparatus , as, in larger fishes the comparatively larger gaps in between the gill rakers cause the detention of larger food organisms. Morover smaller fishes due to slower movement feed mainly on plankton while with increase in size foraging is mainly on shoaling fishes like Stolephorus spp, Leiognathus spp and Sardinella spp.

Food Composition in different months:
The study on the food of D. russelli at Vizhinjam during 1971-1975 indicates the dominance of fishes in the months of January- May, July-August and October-November, whereas crustaceans dominated during June and September and molluscs in December. Availability is the major factor influencing food composition; yet only when the preferred food items are scarce, does the fish look for other items of less preference as evidenced by the presence of fishes alone in the majority of months even though other food items may also be available in the environment.

Diurnal variation in food composition:
Fishes were the major component in the diet, but comparatively more at night. This is possible because of the possession of large eyes, which enable the fish to hunt and prey visually after dark, particularly on fish shoals.

Feeding intensity in relation to Sex and Maturity:
Active feeding was observed in indeterminate, immature, maturing and spent individuals, whereas comparatively less feeding activity was observed in mature fishes.

Yield and mean biomass:
East coast:
The Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY) of 2799 tonnes obtainable in east coast is over 5.7% of more than the present yield and to secure this the effort has to be raised by 80 %. But this reduces the mean biomass level by 30.8% and over the present level and by 72.12 % over the virgin biomass level causing considerable decline in mean biomass. The short life span, the short time within which the species attains maturity and the high natural mortality of this species, all indicated that high fishing pressure can be applied to obtain MSY. But the economic feasibility of increasing the effort by 80% and its effect on the fisheries of other major species in the multispecies gear is to be considered.

Northwest coast:
The stock along the northwest coast shows that the present level of fishing effort is 17% more than the effort needed to obtain MSY. The mean biomass MSY is marginally higher than the present level of production. Further increase in the effort will adversely affect the stock biologically resulting in low production and decline in catch rate. Therefore the effort should either be decreased or maintained at the present level.

Southwest coast:
  An analysis of the stock of Southwest coast indicated that the fishing effort has to be increased by 61% from present level to realise the MSY of 22208 tonnes which is 2 % higher than the present production. On the other hand decrease in mean biomass is 20.5 %. However, considering the short life span and short period of attaining maturity, the stock of D.russelli along the southwest coast will be able to withstand further increase in fishing effort.
