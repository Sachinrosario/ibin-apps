Description: TREE OR SHRUB, up to 8 m. high; STEM often with wide buttresses and short stilt-roots at base; ROOTS with thin elbow-shaped pneumatophores protrude just above the mud; LEAVES simple, opposite, up to 9 x 5 cm; FRUIT will grow 30 cm hypocotyl which is dropped December to March. (Palgrave <halophyte.ref?a=Palgrave&d=1988> 1988)               

SALIENT FEATURES:         

Commercial use       

Probably has the best timber for lumber of the South African mangroves and is also a good fuel; the bark is high in tannin has a red dye used in the batik industry.

It is a protected plant in South Africa. (Palgrave <halophyte.ref?a=Palgrave&d=1988> 1988).

It is used as an analgesic to deaden pain and as a haemostatic to stop bleeding (Aronson 1989:71). [also see Mepham and Mepham 1985]  Decoction from the bark stops haemorrhage and heals bad wounds and is used as a substitute of quinine.
