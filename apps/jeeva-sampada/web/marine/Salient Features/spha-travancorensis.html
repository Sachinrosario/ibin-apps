SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: Body perfectly oblong, stouter than that of S. annandalei, cephalon shorter, anterior border prominently trilobed, antero-lateral borders raised and ridge-like. The peraeon segments more or less equal to one another, and each possess a transverse ridge, the posterior ones brocken up into 4 pairs of transversely elongated tubercles; those on the seventh segment very high and wedge-shaped. These are much larger than those of S. annandalei.
  
  Pleon segments with 4 tubercles i.e. a sub median pair of tubercles flanked on each side by a tubercle. The middle part of the telson with 5 tubercles as in S. annandalei. The first sub median pair is slightly small than the second. The entire dorsal side of the telson is studded with minute pustules. Distal border of telson is perfectly rounded, margin curved upwards with blunt spines. The exposed part of the uropod has five teeth besides the apical tooth. First antenna and second antenna as in S. annandalei, the peduncle of both more hairy.

  The fourth and fifth peduncular segments of second antenna with sensory setae.

  Epistome with stouter arms. Mouth parts as in S. annandalei. Outer lobe of first maxilla less hairy and with 12 barbed spines. First peraeopod has differently shaped, comparatively small, merus with 9 stout outer setae, carpus with 16 outer and 5 inner spines. Male stylet on second pleopod more spiny.

Main food: Plankton, detritus, wood infesting fungi.

Distribution:  India (Kerala, Karnataka and Goa, Tamil Nadu).

Ecological notes:
  Abundant in the estuarine regions, but can occasionally be found in typically marine environment as well. This species is a serious pest in Cochin and Neendakara. In the present collection this species was recorded from a few backwaters along the Southwest coast. Compared to S. terebrans and S. annandalei its incidence was small.

Size and age: 8-12m in length.
