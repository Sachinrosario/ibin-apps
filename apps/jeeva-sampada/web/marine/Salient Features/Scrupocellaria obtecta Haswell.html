SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Occurs as brownish white colonies on sponges, on algal fronds, on polychaete tubes and on rocks.
Zoarium brownish white in colour, usually fifteen to twenty mm. in height. Branching is characteristically dichotomous, but in some regions especially the basal branches, it tends to show variations. Usually the internodes contain four to six zooecia. Zooecia not much elongated, arranged closely, the distal half being occupied by oval opesia. Cryptocyst, slightly developed, smooth and extends uniformly throughout. Number of spines varies, usually three external and two internal ones. Spines jointed. Of the three external spines, the second one shows varied branching, overarching the distal region of the opesia covering the opesia and the proximal region of the ovicells. Scutum present, moderately sized with branching caecal canal, and does not cover the opesia completely. Lateral avicularia always present with straight mandibles, with the divaricator, depressor and adductor muscles attached to the sides. No frontal avicularia noticed. Dorsal vibraculum always present, the elongated vibracular chamber less than half the length of the zooecia. Setae long, usually 500µ (in length). Single vibraculum at the axil of a bifurcation. Radicle chamber occupies the proximal outer border of the vibracular chamber, radicles long and simple. Ovicells present, perforated, pores being connected by wavy lines.Diagnostic characters: The absence of barbing thorns in the radicles is a unique feature in this material in which it differs from those of Harmer (1926) and Osburn (1950).Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite.  
Descriptive characters: The nature of branching shows some peculiarity in this form. Even though the branching appears to be dichotomous, at certain regions the branches of one side of the median axis continuously bend giving the colony a close resemblance to a Tricellaria sp. Osburn (1950) has stated "the joint crosses the proximal part of the opesia of the outer zooecium involving it to near the origin of the scutum; on the inner zooecium the joint crosses immediately proximal to the opesia and sometimes involves its lower border". This is not true in the case of present specimens. The joint crosses only the proximal part of the zooecia below the opesia, which in fact occupies the distal half of the zooecium. The nature of spines is to a certain extent in conformity with what Harmer has observed in his Siboga material. The second spine of the outer group is branched many times and this seems to be different from what Harmer has observed. In the presence of smooth cryptocyst the present material is intermediate between Harmer's and Osburn's materials, where the former noticed the absence of cryptocyst and the latter the presence of "well developed and finely granulated" cryptocyst. Osburn (1950) remarks that the absence of cryptocyst as noticed by Harmer "agrees very well with only the younger zooecia in our specimens". Frontal avicularia were not noticed in the present form. Osburn has failed to observe this "in many zooecia", even though Harmer seems to have noticed them in all zooecia in his material. Meristic characteristics:
