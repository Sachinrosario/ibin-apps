Morphological:                                 

Diagnostic characteristics:         

FEMALE : Prosome widest at middle segment; rostrum bulged proximally and tapers to tip. Posterior margin of pedigerous segment V produced posteriorly into acute spines, tip of which reaching to the middle of genital segmnt; Urosome of two-segments; genital segment swollen proximally and ventrally genital boss is slightly swollen and pigmented; urosome II slightly longer than genital segment and three times longer than broad; caudal rami symmetrical, twice as long as broad; 5th leg: symmetrical; Re one segmented, apex of which terminates in three spines, inner being distinctly longer and plumose at its distal margins; another disto-lateral spine present on Re.         

MALE : Prosome resembles that of female; urosome of five segments; caudal rami symmetrical, twicw as long as broad. Right antenna 1 geniculate and bears a few villiform spines towards distal half of segment 18; fusion segment 19-21 with very small spinules along its dorsal margin; another ill developed spinous seta present along its mid-dorsalmargin; 5th leg :  Right leg: basipod 2 swollen towards its proximal half; palm of terminal segment with a well developed thumb which is provided with one outer marginal seta, one terminal seta and two inner marginal setae; left leg: with basipod 2 highly swollen and gibbose, swollen inner margin being crowned with 10-12 small spinule; Re 1 three times longer than broad and carries a disto-lateral spinous seta; terminal segment ends in two subequal spines of which outer one is much thicker and longer than inner spine; the segment with a dorsal spinous seta towards its mid-length.    

Additional remarks:        
A.Scott (1909) redescribed this species based on material collected fron Ceylon Pearl Banks. Sewell (1912) recorded this species from the coast of southern Burma under Calanopia minor which he later (1932) corrected. C.aurivillii is closely allied to C.minor and C. Americana, latter species being restricted to the Atlantic Ocean.
