SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  

Head short, wide, somewhat depressed and distinct from neck; snout broadly rounded; rostral wider than high, with three short, downward projections fitting in grooves in chin; prefrontals much broader than long, not in contact with the labials; one (rarely long) preoculars; 1-2 postoculars; 7-9 supralabials, the 3rd to the 5th, or only two of them, touching the eye; two anterior and 3-4 posterior temporals; 7 or 8 lower labials, in contact with the chin shields (genials), which are wide and subequal. Eye moderate.

Body moderately thick, laterally compressed posterirly and covered with juxtaposed scales, smooth or with a short central keel; scale rows 38-47 at midbody; ventrals 181-291 (or 226-274); the anterior rows being half the width of the body and gradually becoming narrower posterirly until those just anterior to vent being not even twice as broad as the adjacent scales; preanals fairly enlarged.


Diagnostic characteritics:  

This species can be recognised by its short , wide head, elongate body and large anterior belly-plates. It is normally coloured grey above and white below.Some induviduals have dark rhomboidal spots or 25-38 bars, while some are completely banded.(See 'additional remarks' for details).





Meristic characteristics:

Additional remarks:  

The viperine sea snake rantges from the Persian gulf around the cosats of India to Southern China and the northern coasts of Borneo and Java (Indonesia). It is rather uncommon snake in Indian waters. Murthy (1977) recorded this species from the Chennai coast.

Smith(1943) recognises three colour forms of this snake, which have no relation to geographical races. The three colour forms, two of which are common and  a third rare variety, are recorded from Indian seas. two common forms are :(i) Grey above and lighter below, the two colours sharply separated where they meet and (ii) Grey with a series of dark rhomboidal spots , widest at the top and narrower on the flanks, or with 25-38 dorsal bars. In the third and rare form , the body is completely banded. This rare variety is represented by one example each in the collections of ZSI, recorded from Puri and Karwar.
