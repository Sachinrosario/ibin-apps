SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: Scales on top of the head subimbricate, smooth and not tuberculatre;
supraoculars usually broader and entire; 10-12 upper labials. Dorsal scales in
23-25 rows. Ventrals 171-182 in males, 172-183 in females, caudals 66-74 in males, 
53-59 in females,divided.

Diagonostic Characteristics: The overall coluration of the shore pit viper
is uniformally dark purple-brown above, with or without a whitish line passing
along the belly. The colouration is however, much variable as some individuals 
are olive or grey-brown above, spotted or irregularly marked with brown. This viper tends to live 
in a sea shore environment, generally hiding under stones or basking in the open.

Feeding Habit: Carnivorous
Main Food: Mice and other rodents

Additional Remarks: Pit vipers are distinguished by the presence of a pit- a noticable depression between the eye
and the nose on each side of the face- which functions as a heat-sensory
(thermoregulatory) organ enabling the snake to detect the presecence of warm 
blooded prey such as small mammals(mostly mice)and birds even in total darkness.
Pit-vipers of the genus Trimeresurus differ form other pit vipers in possessing 
numerous small, irregularly arranged scales on the head instead of the large, plate-like scales. The tail of 
tree-living forms is prehensile. Adults vary forom 60cm to 90 cm in length.

Pit vipers are sluggish creatures, making a laboured attempt to escape when approched.
Most pit vipers produce live young. Although a few fatalities have been recorded among 
'humans due to the pit vipers bites, the symptoms in the majority of the cases are not 
severe and quite recoverable.
