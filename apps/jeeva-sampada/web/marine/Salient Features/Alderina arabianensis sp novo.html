SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Encrusting. Colonies grow like flat, disc-like structures in the absence of any hindrance. Zooecia elongated, quadrangular, the distal portion of the preceding zooecium slightly overarch the proximal portion of the succeeding one. Aperture occupying three-fourth of the front, being narrowed distally. Gymnocyst present, slightly extensive proximally. Cryptocyst with spinules, the size of the spinules decrease at the distal portion of the cryptocyst. On the disto-lateral corners of the zooecium bordered at the point of origin by two triangular areas are two spines, which may be simple or branched. Ovicells hyperstomial and ribbed. Ancestrula possesses a pair of branched spines.
Diagnostic characters:   During colony formation, it is noticed that triangular calcareous borders develop first and the spines develop subsequently. In young zooecia, no spines are discernible even though the triangular areas are present. In older colonies towards the centre, spines are well developed, and colonies 25 days old collected during February contained zooecia with the distal spines branched many times.
In zooecia preceding a bifurcation, zooids are noticed with double polypides with an incomplete partition.
Sex attributes:   Hermaphrodite. Ovicells are light brown in colour and project well above the frontal plane of the zooecium as rounded helmets, and are slightly sunk into the proximal region of the succeeding zooecium. They occupy nearly one-fifth of the zooecium distally. On the ectoecium frontally there are two ribs extending from the proximal region and tapering distally. These two are connected by a horizontal rib proximally. At the points where the lateral ribs join the horizontal one, there are two rounded projections. The opening for the escape of the embryo could be distinguished by the presence of a thickened rectangular frame with a transparent inner portion.
Descriptive characters: The salient features of the present form are the simple or branched spines placed at the disto-lateral corners of the zooecium and the hyperstomial and complicately ribbed ovicells. As far as can be ascertained, the genus Alderina Norman, contains not less than four recent species, of which the present form resembles Alderina smitti Osburn (1950). An attempt to treat the present form close to A. smitti is upset by the presence of branched spines placed at the disto-lateral corners of the zooecium and by the conspicuously ribbed ovicells in the present form. Mawatari (1965) states that “the peculiar ribbed ovicells and distal spines may indicate this form not to be identical with Alderina smitti". Based on the differences noticed the present form is designated as a new species Alderina arabianensis and can be defined as follows:
  Zooecia elongated, quadrangular, the distal portion of the preceding zooecium slightly overarches the proximal region of the succeeding one. Aperture occupies three-fourth of the front. Gymnocyst slightly extensive proximally. Cryptocyst with spinules. Simple or bizzare branched spines placed at the disto-lateral corners of the zooecium, bordered at the point of origin by triangular areas. Hyperstomial and ribbed ovicells. Ancestrula with a pair of branched spines.
