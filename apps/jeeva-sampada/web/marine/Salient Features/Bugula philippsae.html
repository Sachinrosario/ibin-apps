Morphological: Zoarium borne on a stalk consisting of a short series of kenozooecia, passing into universal, turbinate zooecia with a short opesia surrounded by numerous spines; later becoming biserial. When the adult characters are acquired, branches become narrow, not spinally arranged, biserial, of uniform width, not interrupted at the junctions of the zooecia, which have straight external margins. Outer part of the zooecium strongly in rolled, the frontal membrane thus facing obliquely towards the axis of the branch. Opesia elongate, its narrow proximal end not far removed from the preceding zooecium. Two inner distal spines, diverging from a single point; one outer distal spine and two others on its proximal side, projecting inwards over the frontal membrane. Avicularia at the base of the colony near the proximal ends of the zooecia, becoming more distal on the younger zooecia; of medium size, the upper profile passing in a nearly straight line from the most prominent part of the head to the rostrum, which is strongly hooked. Rootlets fine, seldom branching.Diagnostic characters: B. philippsae is closely related to B. distrupae Busk. The differences are in the greater development of the stalk, which has a succession from uniserial kenozooecia and zooecia of primitive type, in the numerous slender rootlets, the oblique ovicells and the shorter avicularian rostrum, and in the constancy of the number of the spines.Sex attributes: Ovicells globose, directed obliquely outwards, where they extend beyond the edge of the branch.  Descriptive characters:   The characters of B. philippsae are very constant; and the inrolled margin of the zooecia, the number, form and position of their spines, the position of the ovicells and the mode of origin of the colony are all distinctive features. The colony commences with a rounded swelling separated by a constriction from the rest of zooecium I, which is probably the ancestrula. This zooecium is turbinate, with a short opesia, surrounded by spines; and it gives rise to two daughter-zooecia. One of these, resembles the ancestrula, and produces a similar, but rather longer, distal successor, the branch being incomplete. The other derivative is a kenozooecium, with a vestigial opesia and without spines; and this is followed successively by the kenozooecia. The proximal end of each kenozooecium is strongly forked, like those of the ordinary zooecia.
  The ovicells are directed obliquely outwards, so that their margin extends beyond that of the edge of the branch. The avicularia in the proximal part of the colony originate from the gymnocyst, on its frontal surface and on the outer side of the middle line, usually before the commencement of the opesia. In the younger part of the colony, they are more distal, until, near the ends of the branches, they occur just on the proximal side of the bases of the outer spines, on the basal or lateral part of the inrolled margin of the zooecium. A similar migration of the avicularia, in passing from the proximal to the distal end of the colony, is noticed under Caulibugula dendrographa, to which B. philipsae shows a distinct approach, in its stalk composed of kenozooecia.
