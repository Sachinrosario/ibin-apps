Salient Features:
Morphological:

Cephalon shortr, first peraeon segment longher but narrower than all the others, with a shallow arcuate groove separating the anterior "punctate faveolate" part form a minutely shagreened posterior part.  Segments 2-7 granular and pubescent along the posterior border. Each pleon segment with a lateral oblique ridge, more conspicuous on the hinder segments. Fifth plwon segment with antero-median horse shoe shaped ridge facing a similar postero-median ridge, lateral crests indistinct. Telson nearly circular, dorsally concave, margin strongly raised and conspicuously setose, with a row of submarginal dorsal spine setae. Dorso-median part with a pair of submedian and a pair of lateral tubercles , each followed by a strong ridge with 2 pairs of smaller ridges in between; the tubercles joined by arched carinae. Lateral crests prominent but without tubercles. Dorsal surface of pleon and telson faveolate and with groups of microscopic setules.

Flagellum of second antenna 5 segmented. Structure of mandible not known. Second segment of maxilliped widening distalsards from a narrow base; epipod narrow lanceolate, extending to three-fourths the length of the second segment, broadest at base, apex pointed.

First peraeopod with 6-7 large tubercles on third segment, 1 on fourth, propodus with the 2 usual spines, secondary unguis bifid. Pereopods 1-5 with the margin of segments settate or crenulate, crenulations on third segment sharp. Sixth peraeopod with 3-4 spines on the outer apex of merus, carpus with 3 strong spines.

Peduncle of uropod with a blunt proximal outer knob; endopod half as long as peduncle, exopod unguiform, half as long as peduncle, exopod unguiform, half the length of endopod

Diagnostic Characteristics:
Fifth pleon segment with a pair of horseshoe-shaped carinae facing each other.

Feeding habit: Probably feeds on wood particles.

Main Food: Probably wood Cellulose.

Additional Remarks:
Limnoria Septima is so far known only from the type locality, Andamans. It is a deep water from having collected from 340 to 460 m depth. Barnard(19363) considered this species as closely related to L. (Limnoria) Japonica Richardson, which is another deepwater form. However L. Septima can easily distinguished by the nature of the horse shoe-shaped carinae on the 5th pleon segment which face each other while those of L. Japonica are V-shaped and oppose each other. No information on the habitat of L.Septima is available and is presumed that at this great depth, it is wood.

Size and Age: Maximum length: 6.4mm.
