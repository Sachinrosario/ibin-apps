SALIENT FEATURES:

GRASS, perennial; FLOWERS in globose clusters, dioecious although monoecious populations occur. (N.P. Yensen per. obs.;

Aronson <halophyte.ref?a=Aronson&d=1989> 1989:68; Chaudhary <halophyte.ref?a=Chaudhary&d=1989> 1989:226-8) Aeluropus lagopoides (L.) Trin. var. lagopoides has widely spaced leaves that are neither strongly distichous nor rigid. This latter quality may provide an opportunity to develop a forage variety. (Chaudhary <halophyte.ref?a=Chaudhary&d=1989> 1989:228; N.P. Yensen per. obs.) Aeluropus lagopoides (L.) Trin. var. mesopotamica (Nab.) Bor in Rech. (1964) has closely spaced leaves that are strongly rigid, nerved and distichous (Chaudhary <halophyte.ref?a=Chaudhary&d=1989> 1989:229)          

 

Commercial use       

It has C4 photosynthesis and has some limited use as a forage due to the 'stiff' character of the leaf blades. Potential exists to develop softer palatable cultivars. (N.P. Yensen per. obs.; Aronson <halophyte.ref?a=Aronson&d=1989> 1989:68) Chaudhary (1989:228) mentions a putative hybrid between Al littoralis and A. lagopoides where two populations were growing mixed on Dareene Island, Saudi Arabia. The type locality is India, Burmann. [also see Mepham and Mepham 1985]
