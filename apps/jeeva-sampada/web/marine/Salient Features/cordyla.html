SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:

Diagnostic characteristics:

The  caudal peduncle is strongly compressed with a marked median keel; posterior 7-9 rays of the 2nd dorsal fin  and  the posterior 8-10 rays of the anal fin consists of detached finlets. Straight part of the  lateral line with 51-59 very large scutes . The combination of lateral line with very large scutes and posterior soft dorsal and anal fin rays consisting of a series of detached finlets serve to identify M.cordyla among the carangids.

Sex attributes: Sexes undistinguishable externally.       

 

Descriptive characters:

        Body elongate, sub-cylindrical, dorsal and ventral contour convex, a little compressed posteriorly, height about equal to or somewhat more than the length of head, and the caudal peduncle strongly compressed with a median keel.

Snout and lower jaw prominent and pointed, upper jaw extending  posteriorly upto centre of eye. Upper jaw with small villiform teeth, outer teeth moderately enlarged. The villiform teeth are arranged in a band in the upper jaw and in a single series in the lower jaw. Eye more or less than 4 in head, equal to interorbital space and also to snout   or somewhat shorter. Gill rakers (including rudiments) 8-11 upper, 18-22 lower and 26-32 total on the first gill arch.

Shoulder girdle (cleithrum) margin smooth, without papillae.

        Two dorsal fins separated by a narrow space, the first moderately high with 8 spines; the 3rd and 4th spine, the longest and is lower than the soft rayed second dorsal with one spine and 18-20 soft rays , the posterior 7-9 rays consist of detached finlets. The longest spine of the first dorsal fin has a height about equal to the length of the soft dorsal fin lobe. The anal fin with 2 detached spines, followed by one spine and 16-17 soft rays, posterior 8-10 rays consisting of detached finlets.

        Lateral line strongly arched anteriorly, with the junction of curved and straight parts below 4th or 5th spine of the dorsal fin; chord of the curved part of lateral line contained 3.6-4.7 times in straight part (to caudal base). ; Scales in curved part of the lateral line 21-28; straight part with 51-59 very large scutes; the largest scute 2.5-3 in height of body, the posterior scutes on the strongly depressed tail with a strong spine.

        Breast naked ventrally and laterally in a triangular area for about one third distance to pectoral fin base.

Vertebrae 10+14

 

 

Colour:

        Head and body bluish grey to green dorsally, sides and belly silvery; a large black spot on the posterior part of the operculum. Dorsal and anal fins pale yellow, distally dusky. Caudal fin dark, especially the leading and trailing edges of the fin.

Feeding intensity: Feeding occurs during both day and night, with a higher intensity at night. Feeding intensity is more in juveniles than in immature fishes and least in mature individuals. Feeding is found to be better during the post-monsoon months. Ref no: 25

 

Feeding type: The diet composition shows that M.cordyla is a pelagic carnivore and mainly a piscivore , feeding mainly on fishes, crustaceans, molluscs and polychaetes.

 

Additional remarks: Presence of minute teeth on jaws in bands and well developed gill rakers in the uppermost arch indicate that M.cordyla is adapted to feed mainly on smaller organisms. Its habit in taking actively moving fishes like Stolephorus, Leiognathus, Gazza and Carangoides  as food articles indicates that sight plays an important role, while the habit of taking artificial baits which are coloured, inanimate things devoid of smell shows that the sense of smell is of little help in the feeding activity. (Ref no: 25).It has been observed that the occurrence of Stolephorus spp in the diet coincides with the abundance of its landings. Earlier observations also points to the fact that the availability of Stolephorus in the vicinity of the shoals of M.cordyla  is the factor governing its preference by the latter. (Ref no: 23)

Eggs and larvae:

Characteristics:

 

In general larvae were identified on the basis of meristic and morphometric characters of the adults. Larval characters of selected stages are described in detail.

        2.2mm Stage: Larva with a blunt head and rather short and broad body. Upper jaw margins serrated. With distinct teeth. Branchiostegals absent. Two rows of pre-opercular spines visible, first row with 5 minute spines and second with 3 moderately elongated spines, the middle one being  the longest. Fin folds present on the dorsal and ventral sides of the body.Gill filaments begin to form. Supra-occipital crest present. Myotome number 10+14 closely arranged towards caudal end. A wide chromatophore present in between the eleventh and twelfth myotome on dorsal margin of the body, little anterior to it a small chromatophores is present. Along the ventral side, post anal pigments present up to caudal tip. Region of air bladder with few pigment spots. So also the ventral margin of the stomach region with pigments in aline. Pectoral fin buds with a flat space.

 

        3.7 mm Stage: Slightly serrated supra-occipital crest present. Branchiostegals 7 in number. Fin folds present on both the sides. Caudal tip with a flexion or bent on the ventral side, urostyle slightly turned upwards. Only upper jaw margin serrated. Two small spines at the tip of cleithrum, the upper one shorter than the lower. Slight notch on head in front of the nostril. Two chromatophores on the posterior lobe of occipital and the region just above the pre-opercle. Pigmentation in two blotches on dorsal side and spread towards lateral sides. Roof of the body cavity and ventral margin of the stomach with  thick pigments. Single dash line of pigment on lateral mid line appears at this stage, below which the post-anal pigment lies. Line of pigment on the lower jaw bones quite distinct.

        5.1mm Stage: Fin rays develop rapidly at this stage. Spines and ray basals are differentiated, but rays not separated. Six spine bases and thirteen rays on dorsal side and twelve rays on anal fin are clear at this stage. Anal spines not clear. Rays are yet to be formed hence fin folds on sides. Urostyle turned upwards. Pre-opercular spines seven in lower margin, fourth one in the corner being the longest. Upper margins with seven minute spines. No teeth on lower jaw margin. Scattered chromatophores on almost all parts, except on caudal region. Occipital region and its posterior part with many chromatophores.

        7mm Stage: Pre-opercular margin with double serrations. Nine spines in lower margin, 6th one at the corner the longest. Alternating the lower ones above are eight minute spines on the upper row. Two small teeth appear on the lower jaw. Gills 4, filaments more in the middle ones. Lower and upper gill arches with rudimentary filaments. Dorsal spines VIII, I, 16-17 rays. Anal spines II, I, 15-16 rays, Pectoral 14-15 rays, Caudal with 9+8 rays. Interspinous membranes slightly pigmented. Body pigmented all over. Dorsal pigment up to eighteenth myotome. Post anal pigment up to caudal tip. Snout, pre-opercle and stomacjh region with  few scattered pigments superficially.

        8.0 mm stage: D.I, VIII, I, 18, A.II, I, 15-16.V.5, P.15-16. Pre-opercular spines nine in number, upper row with minute serrations. Superficial pigments more on trunk region. Stellate chromatophores on occipital and punctate forms on interspinous membrane. Five pigment dots along the margin of lower jaw. In between the eye orbit  and pectoral are the  punctate and reticulostellate pigments. Caudal peduncle and the base remain without any pigments until the fin formation is complete. Dorsal pigment is more up to the last 10-12th myotome region, whereas the lateral mid-line pigment extends upto the last 6th myotome.

        10.3 mm Stage: Teeth distinct on both jaws.More spines on pre-opercle (7+5). Lateral line arch not clear. Few of the fin rays on the posterior side of  soft dorsal and anal with thick bases. Body thickly pigmented all over except the caudal portion (last seven myotome region). Three chromatophores on caudal base. Spiny dorsal fin with black pigmented membrane.

 

        14.0 mm Stage: D.I, VIII, I, 18, A.II, I, 15. Lateral line arch straightened  below the middle  portion of the Ist dorsal fin. Fin rays branched at its tip posteriorly. Pre-opercular spines present. Procumbent spines on dorsal side directed anteriorly. Straightened part of lateral line with 40-45 minute scutes. Caudal base with pigments. Only last six vertebral region free of pigment. Other portions with brownish black pigments.

        19.0 mm Stage: Posterior 9 rays on dorsal fin and 8 rays on anal fin with thick bases in the form of finlets. Pre-opercular spines much reduced than in the previous stages, 2 anal spines separated from the anal fin. A small spine at the corner of the pre-opercle present. Straightened portion of the lateral line with 48-50 scutes. Finlets are free. Only the caudal end and snout region with less pigments.        Ref no: 16

 

        Along the southwest coast of India , the larvae of M.cordyla outnumbered all other species in abundance, contributing nearly to 20% of the carangid fish larvae. The concentration of the larvae were more towards the northern region than the southern region. The larval abundance was maximum (48.6 %) in the shelf edge off Calicut area, followed by Karwar, Cochin and Quilon areas. Seasonal distribution showed that there is an increase in the occurrence of larvaefrom March onwards and reaches a peak in June, when nearly 50 % of the  larvae are obtained and from July to September a decrease in value is noticed (Ref no: 17)

Spawning grounds and spawning migrations in M.cordyla along the Soutwest coast:

        The year round availability of spent gonads and the less frequent occurrence of immature and maturing gonads along Cochin area is in contrast to the  trend  noticed towards the southern part of Kerala, since at Vizhinjam a more frequent occurrence of immature and maturing gonads and scanty occurrence of spent gonads is noticed (Srenivasan, 1978). A scrutiny of the  probable pattern of movement of shoals of M.cordyla along the south-west coast shows that maximum accumulations of Horse Mackerel along the central and  Northern shelf areas of Kerala occurs during May-June (Rao et al, 1978) coinciding with the peak spawning season of the species. The larval abundance of M.cordyla was maximum in Calicut region with moderate abundance in Cochin and Quilon  and minimum abundance in Cape Comorin (Premalatha, 1988). Further, the dearth of adult Horse Mackerel with mature stages and the absence of planktonic eggs, larvae and post larvae suggest that M.cordyla spawns far away from the 5 km fishing zone of Vizhinjam.

All these factors lead to the conclusion that the probable breeding ground of Horse Mackerel is along the southwest coast is towards the Northern belt near Calicut with a moderate intensity at Cochin from where the spent fish migrate towards the south with gonads in spent recovering condition (Ref no: 23).
