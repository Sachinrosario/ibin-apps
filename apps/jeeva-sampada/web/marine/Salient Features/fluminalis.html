SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: “Shell typical for genus, variable in size and shape, adults with callum, mesoplax and hypoplax long, narrow and forked posteriorly in adult specimens. Periostracal lamellae on the posterior slope finely fringed, young specimens 3mm. Long often with 3 lamellae clustered at the posterior margin, adults usually within 6 or more lamellae extending up the posterior slope, with those near the umbo typically worn away. Lamellae rounded posteriorly, with deep, central longitudinal furrow on outer face and corresponding ridge on inner surface; distal margin of the lamellae marked with fine grooves, tips free, pointed forming delicate fringe. Umbonal reflection closely apressed over umbos and with large flattened ridge which forms attachment area of anterior adductor muscle”
  “Interior of valves white, glazed, muscle scars in older specimens usually bluish. Umbonal ventral ridge usually pronounced in young but expressed only near ventral condyles in adults. Anterior adductor muscle scar covering the umbonal reflection and extending on to anterior slope, posterior adductor muscle scar elliptical, located high on posterior slope; pedal retractor muscles attached to long thin apophysis, ventral adductor scar elongate, often irregular in shape and bridging umbonal ventral ridge near ventral condyle; pallial sinus broad and deep. Mesoplax divided in young specimens semi-circular ventral portion and posterior portion calcified, dorsal covering of anterior adductor muscle entirely periostracal, mesoplax in adults calcified, variable in shape, usually lobed anteriorly, inflated and irregularly wrinkled. Metaplax and hypoplax found only in old adults, long, narrow and forked posteriorly.” (Turner and Santhakumaran, 1984).
  Lingopholas fluminalis differs from L. rivicola in several respects such as the centrally grooved and delicately fringed lamellae on the posterior slope, the lobed mesoplax, and the posteriorly forked metaplax and hypoplax.
  
Main food: Plankton.

Additional remarks:
  Occurrence:  Dharmaraj and Turner collected this species from the Akathumuri backwater in Kerala (Turner and Santhakumaran, 1989) from mangroves, palm piling and laterite embankments. This species has since been collected in large numbers from the dead stumps occurring in the Vembanad backwaters where the salinity ranged between 4 to 12%0. Santhakumaran and Turner collected it from the mangrove sat Britona and Pilerna in Goa; King Georges Dock, Calcutta (Blanford 1876).

  Distribution:  India, Burma and Sarawak (Turner and santhakumaran, 1989).
