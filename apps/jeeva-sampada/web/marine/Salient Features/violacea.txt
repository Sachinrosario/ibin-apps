Salient Features:

Head hardily distinct from neck, with large shields; nostril in the nasal;
frontal much broader than the supraocular;parieatals elongate; loreal present,
well seperated from the internasal; 5 supralabials, 3rd and 4th below the eye, 
last two largest. Body cylindrical, elongated and coivered with smooth scales, in 19
rows; ventrals 260-291, well developed, not keeled; caudals 53-67, paired; anals dived. Tail short,
slightly compressed.

Dark brown above, with narrow yellow bands and white below.

Feeding Habit: Piscivorous
Main Food:Fish
Additional Remarks:
The banded mangrove snake is a thouroughly aquatic species and is 
equally at home both in fresh and saltwater. In accordance with the 
snake's aquatic mode of existence, the nostril is a crescentic slit 
on the upper surface of the snout and is provided with a more or less 
distinct downward-projecting tounge of tissue, the entire structure 
forming an effective valve.

The snake feeds entirely on fish, which are swallowed under water. It 
produces live young.

Little is known of the natural history of this rare snake which, in India, 
is found only in the mangrove and other vegetation in the vicinity of water 
of the Andaman Islands
