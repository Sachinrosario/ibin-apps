Morphological:  Head not distinct from neck, with large shields (See Fig. 1. A-C); nostril in the nasal; frntal much broader than the supraocular; 1 pre - and 2 suboculars; temporals 1+2 (or 2+2); 7 supralabials (rarely 8, 8th if present, very small), 4th touching the eye; 2 paris of genials.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The snake has a cylindrical body, covered with smooth scales arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  Ventrals well developed, 145-153.  Tail short, subcaudals 29-36, paired; anal divided.

Additional remarks:  This is a small and stout marsh snake with a short tail.  It is dark grey above and whitish below.  This is the smallest of the mud snakes found in India.  It is a sluggish creature on land and is not prone to bite easily.  It is often found in the tidal reivers and estuaries and is sometimes swept ashore.  Like other estuarine snakes, the Glossy marsh snake feeds principally on fish, which are swallowed whole under water.  It is a back-ganged snake, mild in disposition.  The female brings forth live young.
