SALIENT FEATURES:           Ref. No: 103, 111

 

Morphological:     Rostrum long and slender, surpassing tip of  antennular peduncle; armed with 7 to 9 teeth on dorsal and 4 to 6 teeth on ventral margins; its blade becoming  moderately high in  large specimens. Carapace with postrostral carina ending distinctly before posterior margins;  adrostral carina and sulcus extending to about level of epigastric tooth, sulcus shallow; gastro frontal and hepatic carina absent; gastro-orbital carina extending over posterior two–third of distance between hepatic spine and orbital margin. Abdomen carinated  dorsally from posterior half to two-third of fourth somite, keel on sixth somite ending acutely. Telson as long as sixth abdominal somite, grooved, without lateral spines. Dactyl of 3rd maxilliped in adult male as long as propodus, tuft of hairs of propodus reaching or surpassing tip of dactyl. Petasma with distomedian projections overhanging distal margin of costae; ventral costae unarmed; outer surface of lateral lobes with a few rows of minute tubercles.

 

Diagnostic characteristics:     Rostrum long and slender, bearing 7- 9 teeth on dorsal and 4-6 teeth on ventral margins; adrostral carina and sulcus on carapace extending to about level of epigastric tooth, gastrofrontal and hepatic carinae absent, gastro-orbital  carina extending over posterior 2/3 of distance between hepatic spine and orbital margin; dactyl of 3rd maxilliped in adult male as long as propodus.

 

Colour:   Ground body colour yellowish white (semi-translucent), speckled with dark green.

 

 

Feeding habit:

 

Main food:  Vegetable matter (Coscinodiscus, Pleurosigma, Rhizosolenia, planktonic algae and

sea weeds), crustaceans (Copepods, ostracods, amphipods, tiny decapods and their larval stages), molluscs and polychaetes.

 

Feeding type: Omnivorous and detritus feeder

Eggs and larvae:                           Ref. No. 96, 120, 175

 

 Characteristics:  Eggs measure 0.304 to 0.470 mm in diameter. They are transparent and

spherical  with a narrow perivitelline  space. The egg, after hatching, passes through  three larval stages, namely, nauplius (6 sub – stages ), protozoea (3 sub – stages) and mysis (3 sub – stages) as revealed by plankton studies (Menon, 1937 ; Subrahmanyam, 1965) as well as by laboratory rearing (Muthu et al., 1978 ). The third mysis stage is 3.4 to 4.2mm in total length, carapace with rostrum longer than eye, no well developed rostral tooth, telson long and rectangular carrying 6 distal and 2 lateral setae on each side. Mysis passes through a series of postlarval stages before transforming into juvenile.

 

 

Abundance:  Eggs have been found to occur in large numbers in subsurface plankton (3 m

     below surface) in Madras waters in the month of February.

Protozoea , mysis and postlarval stages have been recorded from the inshore surface/sub-surface waters off Cochin and Madras. Plankton collections from Cochin indicated occurrence of large numbers of the larval forms in advanced stages in the inshore areas in early hours of the morning. Active recruitment of postlarvae (0.8 to 1.4 cm TL) into Cochin backwaters occurs in all months except June to September.
