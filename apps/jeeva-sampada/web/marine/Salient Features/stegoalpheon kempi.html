SALIENT FEATURES :
Morphological :

Female:

Body roughly circular but slightly asymmetrical. Head somewhat triangular, broader than long, lateral corners prominent and the dorsal side distinctly bilobed. Anterior border somewhat convex and sinous in the middle, posterior border prominently bilobed. Frontal lamina absent, posterior lamina broadly rounded, with its posterior border thickened, the thickening extending forwards as median thickening. Lateral lobes two pairs, inner pair slightly longer. A pair of eyes clearly visible at the antero-lateral corners of the head. First thoracic segment short , arched forwards, second almost straight; segments three to five subequal in breadth, sixth xomparitively small and the seventh smaller than the sixth. First five pairs of legs widely spacd and the last two pairs situated close together. First five pairs of ovaian bosses clearly visible in the antero-lateral parts of the segments. Coxal plates narroe and borne on the outer side of the ovarian bosses. Pleon slightly broader than long and about half of its dorsal surface covered by the dorsal rami of the pleopods. Sixth pleon segment small, with a postero-median point. Ventrally the pleon segments are separated from one another by deep grooves and the surface of the segments with a number of parallel longitudinal grooves giving it a corrugated appearance.

Antennule four jointed , first segment very large. Antenna five jointed, first segment placed at right angles to the rest of the appendage, distal segments with short, stiff hairs. Maxilliped broadly rounded anteriorly; posterior lobe roughly triangular. Palp not clearly formed but demarcated by a shallow depression on the upper side and a deep incision on the lower side. Anterior lobe of first oostegitr broad and truncated, posterior lobe produced into a blunt prolongation with hairy inner and distal margins; the other oostegites small and almost vertical, thus completely exposing the brood pouch. Basis of all the peraeopods with a small crest. Outer border of merus and carpus of first peraeopod setose, propodus of all the peraeopodss distally bulged and the dactylus folding against it. All the pleopods triramous and projecting outwards thus exposing the lower surface of the pleon; anterior border of dorsal ramus and exopod much thickened, more so on those of one side. Uropods uniramous and elongated.

Body brownish yellow, with the raised portions like the cephalon, ovarian bosses etc. paler, grooves on the surface of the body deep brown.

Male.

Body short, about twice as long as broad. Cephalaon circular, with a slightly angular postero-median projection; eyes distinct. Thoracic segments clearly defined, triangular and laterallydrawn out. Pleon roughly semicircular, almost twice as broad as long.

Antennules short, four jointed, distal segments with setae. Antenna very long, six jointed, projecting considerably beyond the sides of the Cephalon. Upper distal border of the segments with stiff spine like setae. First peraeopod very stout, almost double the size of the second. Carpus and propodus setose, propodous very stout and grooved along its inferior border, sides of the grooves serrated. Dactylus as long the propodus and with three setae on its lower border.

Body straw yellow, with about ten branched chro-matophores on the dorsal surface.

Additional Remarks:

Considerable intraspecific variations are noticed in specimens of Stegoalpheon Kempi, described by Chopra (1923), Shino (1950) and Pillai (1958). In fact, Pillai (1954) created a new species Stegoalpheon Choprai, based on his materials from Alpheus Palludicola collected from Kayamkulam lake, Kerala. But a careful study of the species (Pillai, 1958), has shown that most of the differences are not fundamental, but more or less intraspecific variations. Neverthless, male specimens show marked variations in earlier descriptions. However, S.choprai Pillai is only a synonym of S.Kempi, until more materials are studied and compared.
