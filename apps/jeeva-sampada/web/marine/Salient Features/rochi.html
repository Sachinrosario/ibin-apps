SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological: Pallets elongate, solid and calcified with long, slender and cylindrical stalk, the blade composed of a series of closely to moderately spaced conical units, the calcareous portion of each unit broadly U-shaped and covered by a brownish or amber coloured periostracum which extends as a wide border with the free margin serrated on both the inner and outer face, the awns are short and smooth.

  Shell typical as in other Bankia species.

Feeding habit: Bores into wood and feeds on the sawdust.

Main food: Cellulose, supplemented by plankton.

Additional remarks:  
  In the Sunder bans, W. Bengal it attacks trees such as Avicennia officinalis, A. alba, A. marina of Verbenacea; Excoecaria agallocha of Euphorbiaceae; Ceriops decandra(=C. roxburghiana), C. tagal (C. candolleana) of the Rhizophoaceae; Ageiceras corniculatus of the Myrsinaceae and Xylocarpus molluccensis (=Carapa mollucensis) of Meliaceae. All the above-mentioned trees except the last one are found attacked in the living condition. The burrow is lined by calcareous tubing, which is fairly thick, particularly at the posterior end (Rajagopal, 1964).

Distribution: Indo-Pacific, tropic to subtropical. East coast of Africa (Turner 1966): Madagaskar (Roch, 1935); Red sea, Persian gulf, Indian ocean Islands, Papua-New Guinea (Rayner, 1971); Pacific ocean Islands, Christmas Island, South of Java, (Moll, 1931); Japan (Turner, 1966).
