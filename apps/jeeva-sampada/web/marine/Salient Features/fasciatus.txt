SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:  Head very small; 1 pre - and 1 (rarely 2), postoculars; a single large anterior temporal accompanied by another scale as large or larger; 6 or 7, rarely 5, supralabials, the 2nd in contact with the prefrontal; the 3rd and 4th touching the eye; 4 infralabials.  Body long and very slender anteriorly and larger and much compressed posteriorly; scales subimbricate posteriorly and rather hexagonal in shape, each scale with a short keel or central tubercle, in 39-58 rows at midbody; ventrals 323-514, not twice as broad as the adjacent scales, and each one with two keels.

Diagnostic characteritics:  The head, neck and anterior half of the body are black or dark olive, with pale yellowish oval spots on the sides which may be connected as dorsal bars; the posterior part of the body is greyish above and whitish below, with dark rhomboidal spots that extend down the sides of the body and form complete rings in the young.  The overall colour is greyish or dirty yellow above and white below.

Additional remarks:  Two races of the banded sea snake viz., Hydrophis fasciatus fasciatus and Hydrophis fasciatus atriceps are recognised.  The nominate race occurs generally in the coastal waters of India, Myanmar and the Straits of Malacca, while the other race, atriceps is reported to be common in Indo - Chinese waters.

Some observations made on the race atriceps are worth mentionaing.  it is said that this race is rarely seen and has not been found floating on the surface during the day.  Byt, it is found to be swimming slowly and horizontally at the surface, especially in the illuminated area of the gangway.  They are rather easily captured and like most other sea snakes, are helpless out of the water.  The snake's principal food seems to be eels, mainly of the genus Muraenichthys.
