SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Zoarium reticulate, forming large, open, quadrangular, meshes, one of the normal lateral pairs of connecting tubes wanting; adherent by rootlets given off by the basal surface, normally one by each zooecium, near the proximal end of its expanded part, occasionally two, or even three, all median. Spines small, restricted to the oral region, usually three distally, occasionally two or four, in addition to one on each side of the orifice. Opesia occupying the entire front of the expanded part, widest proximally, its sides nearly straight; the proximal part of the zooecium long and tubular. The lateral connecting tube arises in the proximal half of the expanded part. Avicularia paired, near the proximal end of the opesia, sometimes on one side only, and not found in most of the specimens. Ovicells vestigial.
Diagnostic characters: The species is easily recognizable by its regular quadrangular meshes. These ordinarily occur between longitudinal rows of zooecia, but sometimes where a new row is intercalated, as in the avicularium-bearign individual, a zooecium appears to have two proximal connecting tubes.
Sex attributes: HermaphroditeDescriptive characters:   The siboga specimens closely resemble those described by Thornely (1916), who observed no avicularia, and by Robertson (1921), who found only a single avicularium. The avicularium has a peculiar form, the hooked end of the rostrum being broad at its base, and the mandible, which is very short, having the usual terminal spike.
  Two spines occur on each side of the orifice, and the basal surface of the zooecium is crossed distally, by a curved line, convex towards the proximal end, uniting the bases of distal spines. This arrangement was described by Robertson (1921), who suggested that the structure thus outlined might be a vestigial ovicell, a view which was confirmed by the evidence of Siboga specimens.
  The expanded parts of the zooecia stand up at a considerable angle from the basal network formed by the connecting tubes, and are thus outside the plane of this part of the colony.
