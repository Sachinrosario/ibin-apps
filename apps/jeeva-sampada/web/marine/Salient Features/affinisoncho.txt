SALIENT  FEATURES :
Morphological:    

Diagnostic characteristics:     

Male: Length, 5.68 mm: cephalothorax, 4.63 mm; abdomen, 1.23 mm. The abdomen is  3.8 times in length of the cephalothorax. The cephalothorax oblong ovate. The prosome separates from the 1st thoracic segment. The 4th and 5th pedigerous segments are separate. The lateral distal corner of the last pedigerous segment rounded. The rostrum has a strong base to which two short spines are attached.     
The 2nd to 4th abdominal segments are fringed with fine teeth on the distal border. The furcal rami about as long as wide.    
The 1st antenna 24-jointed, extends to the distal end of the 4th abdominal segment. The joints 19and 20 are fused on the right side. The joints 20, 21 and 24 are devoid of aesthetasks.    
The 2nd antenna has the exopodite ¾ as long as the endopodite. The mandible has no interesting features. The 1st maxilla has very poor setae on the 1st inner lobe. The 2nd maxilla has a strong spine on the 5th lobe. The distal joints poorly developed. In the maxilliped the 2nd basal joint has a row of serrations at the anterior proximal margin. The spinulation of the 1st basal joint much reduced; there is a minute sensory filament at the proximal margin.    
The 1st leg has 3-jointed exopodite and 1-jointed endopodite.  The outer edge spine of the joints slender and long. The posterior surface of the joints of exopodite and endopodite are furnished with groups of spinules.      
The left 5th leg 5-jointed and long.  The distal joint has 2 apical spines. The inner margin haired. The 3rd and 4th joints have each a distal spine. The 2nd joint has 3 curved spines near the distal border. The right leg 4-jointed and short. The distal joint has an apical and a small outer-edge spine

Additional  remarks:      
Tanaka’s specimen agrees with O. affinis With in having 3 curved spines on the 2nd joint of the left 5th leg as described and figured by Vervoort. The female specimen described and figured by A. Scott under the name O. hirtipes is, according to both Sewell and Vervoort, identical with O. affinis With.
