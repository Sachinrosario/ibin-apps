Descriptive characters:  The Present identification of the species is based on Crossland (1941) wherein he has re-described Forskal’s type kept in Copenhagen Zoological Museum.  The following description is based on several specimens collected from Gulf of Mannar near Mandapam.

Corallum grows layer over layer looking massive.  Surface rises into nodular or digitiform branches always enclosing a central worm tube.  The branches can very well be due to the presence of these boring polychaetes.  Calices 0.5 to 0.8 mm in diameter, close together or a diameter apart.  A well developed thecal wall is not often seen.  The development of septa shows wide range of variations in different coralla or even in the different regions of the same colony in number and size.  Generally, the first cycle is prominent, the directives often fuse each other.  Second cycle 3 to 6 in different calices.

The surface coenenchyme may be smooth over a considerable area, especially at the peripheral parts of the corallum or it may display foveolate condition, forming ramparts around the calices.  The papillae are of different size and shapes, some very small, similar to tubercles, others larger with flattened tops.  Often two to five papillae run together to form low ridges.  Large papilla 1 to 1.5 mm in height and thickness.  The smaller, tubercle like ones 0.5 to 1 mm in height and thickness.  Under the lens the coenenchyme reveals a very porous reticulum.  Section shows a very porous single thickening layer resting on an epitheca.  Living coral dull brown.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
