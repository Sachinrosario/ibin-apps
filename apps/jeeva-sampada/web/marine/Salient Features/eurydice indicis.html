SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:

Description of male holotype mouth parts not differing significantly from those of other members of the genus.

Antennule elongate, extending beyond the hind border of the cephalon (fig. 2(a)); fourth peduncular article longest, twice the length of article 2, but less than the combined length of articles 2 and 3. Flagellum short with the combined length of flagellar articles equal to length of peduncular article3.

Antenna with fourth peduncular article longer than the combined length of articles 2 and 3 (Fig.2(b)), article 3 bearing an outer row of eight spines and an inner row of six spines on its outer margin. Antennal flagellum with 15 articles reaching beyond the second abdominal segment.

Thoracic epimera 6-7 covered with granulate patterning, as is the whole of the body surface. Umerous short, setae present epimera hind margins terminating in moderately well-developed points (fig.2(c)).

Peraeopod 1 with article 3 bearing three blunt spines on the posterior border, article 4 a single spine, and propodus with four spines including a terminal spine which is less than half the length of the opposing dactyl (fig.2(d)). Peraeopod 7 narrow (fig.2(e)), with articles 2 and 5 subequal in length and article 4 slightly longer than article 3. Hind margins of articles 2-5 setose , whilst anterior margins are extremely spinose, each article bearing at least two longitudinal rows of blunt spines.

Telson (Fig.2(f)) broader than long with the hind margin approximately one-fifth the width of telson. Dorsal surface bearing numerous small setae and a deeply indented transverse groove. Hind margin straight, bearing two pairs of small movable spines and c.eight plumose setae. Uropods with endopod triangular and just extending beyond the hind margin of the telson. Appendix masculine (Fig.2(g)) stout with the free and recurved, extending beyond the hind margin of pleopod 2 by one-sixth of its length.

Colour in Formalin.
White with black chromatophores arranged in rows on dorsal and ventral surfaces of the body, not on peraeopods.

Female:

As the male, with the exception of male sexual characters and the antennal flagellum which comprises 16 articles and reaches to the seventh peraeon segment.

Additional Remarks:

The recurved structure of the appendix masculine separated Eurydice indicus from all other known species inhabiting the Indian Ocena and Meditteranean regions. This species may be readily distinguished from the closely elated Eurydice peraticis by the relatively broader telson hind margin of the latter species. Holotype and Paratypes are in the British Museum of Natural Histort with registration numbers 1975 :11:1 and 1973 :12:3 respecctively.
