Salient features:
Morphological:

BODY GREYISH BROWN, WITH BRIGHT YELLOW SPOTS FORMING A PATTERN KEEPING WITH THE SURROUNDINGS.
CEPHALON AND ITS APPENDAGES:

  Cephalon ( Fig.1) is wider than long. It is produced into a long median blunt point, extending nearly 1mm. Beyond the margin of the head. The frontal lamina of the head arches over the base of the second antennae and is coalesced anteriorly with the head projection, forming a sort of a knob like expansion. Eyes are red, moderate in size, composite and rounded.

  First antennae ( Fig.2) has its basal article thick and strong. It is longer than the second. Its flagel;lum is thick, its first article being robust and long. The succeeding articles are narrower and shorter and each one bears one to three setae.

  Second antenna (Pl. 1, Fig. 3) has its basal article small and almost incospicous. Its second article is article is short but robust and thick. Third article is longer and triangular with its broad portion on the outer side. Fourth article is narrower than the preceding one and nearly twice as long as the third. Fifth is slenderthan the fourth. Its flagellum is composed of 23 to 25 articles. It is slender towards the apex. The basal articles of the flagellum bear tufts of setae:

  Mandibles are tridentate and thickly chitinsed at their cutting edges (Fig. 4,5)

  First maxilla (Fig.6) has 8 to 9 spines on the strong outer lobe and three plumose setae on the inner apical border of the slender inner lobe. Both the lobes are united by a small thin median article.

  Second maxilla (Fig.7) has expanded, oval and rounded inner lobe with three to four small setae and two slender conical outer lobes with one to three long spines at the apex.

  Maxillipeds (Fig.8) are composed of seven articles. The shape of each article is peculiar, the fifth one being particularly conspicuous by its rounded shape. The distal articles of the maxillipeds, bear number of setae.

  Thorax (Fig.1) is slightly less than 2/3 rds the length of the entire body. The first thoracic segment is larger than the sb-equi succeeding three segments. The fifth, sixth and seventh are broad and long, the seventh being the broadest. Epimeral expansions which are fused along the entire margine of the thoracic segments are well developed expecting in the first segment. They are slightly projected backwards in the posterior direction in some posterior segments.

  The first three pairs of thoracic legs are prehensile and they bear short, robust and blunt setae along their inner margin. The last four pairs are strong with thick tufts of setae.

Abdomen and its Appendages.

  Abdomen (Fig.1) is continuous with the thorax and it is more than 1/3rd the length of the later. The five abdominal segments are more or less equal. The posterior pleotelsonic segment is nearly as long as the anterior five segments together.

  Telson (Fig.9) is triangular with the posterior narrow region rounded. It is serrated and bears long slender setae. The outer lobe of the uropod extends well beyond the posterior extremity. The inner margin of the uropod lobes is serrate and with long setae. The peduncle of the uropod has the inner distal angle extended to more than half the length of the telson. Pleopods of the abdominal segments are simple.

Additional remarks:
  ACCORDING TO JOSHI AND BAL (1959), CIROLANA BOMBAYENSIS STANDS OUT REMARKABLY FROM THE OTHER SPECIES OF THE GENUS BY THE POSSESSION OF A LARGE CEPHALIC PROJECTION.
