Morphological:

  Female. The body is somewhat oval in shape, with the anterior end M-shaped and the posterior tapering and carrying a pair of stout, bifurcate appendages. The dorsal profile of the body is highly arched and the ventral more or less flat. The body is clearly segmented and the dorsal plates are clearly visible along the mid-dorsal part, densely mottled with pigment spots. The lateral parts are much expanded, clear and transparent without showing any marks f segmentation. The ova are visible through this area. Between the bases of the largse hind pair of appendages extends the small unsegmented metasome. Beneath the metasome is a round mass on to which the male holds with its himd legs.
  The first antennae were not seen. The second antennae consist of minute, slender, apparently three-segmented appendages situated far apart from each other in a groove extending on either side from the mouth (fig. 2 and 3). A pair of mandibles and maxillipeds are present, but they could not be studied in detail without dissection. The first four pairs of mesosome appendages are arranged in two lateral rows and are similar in structure, but the anterior ones are a little shorter and stouter than the posterior ones. The appendages are ahelate, the dactylus being minute and claw-like (fig. 4). The fifth pair of appendages (fig.5 and 6) is totally different from the first four in size, shape and position. They originate from the hind end of the body as a pair of massive protrudiug appendages. Each leg is three-segmented; the proximal segment is stout and rod- shaped and in the natural position is directed obliquely inwards, distally contacting the opposite member. The first segment has an obliquely cut distal end to which the second segment is articulated. The second segment is strong claw- like process directed outwards and has a proximal short prolongation articulated to third segment. The latter is somewhat slender and U-shaped. Each of the mesosome appendages carries a brood lamella; those of either side extend almost to the mid-ventral line enclosing a spacious brood-cavity. The last pair is the largest and extends posteriorly as conical plates bebeath the metasome and in between the last pair of legs (fig 5).

Male. Its body is narrow and elongate and consists of a prosome, a segmented mesome and an unsegmented metasome. The prosome carries a pair of tubercles representing the first antennae, a pair of apparently three-segmented second-antennae, a pair of styliform mandibles protruding through the mouth, a pair of maxillipeds and the first pair of paraeopods. The mesosome consists of five segments and a sith one partially fused with the metasome. Altogether, there are seven pairs of slender, subchelate paraeopodes; the anterior pairs are slightly shorter and stouter than the posterior ones. Uropods are absent.

Size and age : Length: 2.9mm.
