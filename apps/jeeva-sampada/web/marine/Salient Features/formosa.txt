Sex attributes:

Descriptive characters:  Arborescent, main branches 1 to 1.5 cm thick, laxly dividing, ultimate branches 4 to 7 cm long, 8 to 10 mm thick, tips slightly tapering to an axial corallite 2 to 2.5 mm thick and so much exsert.  Two cycles of septa, secondaries smaller.  Radial corallites tubular, slender, ascending, crowded, inner wall less developed than the outer; 3 to 4 mm long, 1 to 1.75 mm thick at the top, larger ones with one or two buds.  Towards the lower parts of the branches smaller tubular radials are found mixed with larger ones, and finally they become all immersed.  In some specimens the radials may spread, but not perpendicular to the branch surface.  In the radials generally, only six septa are visible.  Surface coenenchyme recticulate and echinulate, rather porous in section.  Living coral dull brown with pink or white axial corallite.

Remarks:  Gravely (1927) reported A. cervicornis from Krusadai Island; but Pillai (1986) regarded Gravely’s A. cervicornis as only A. formosa for, since Dana (1846) these were kept separate (Brook, 1893).  Of recent, some Australian workers have synonymised this species with the West-Indian A. cervicornis.

Meristic characteristics:

Feeding habit:  Filter feeder.  Catching food by tentacles.

Main food:  Plankton.   Extracellular products of symbiotic algae.

Egg and larvae: Planula.  Planktonic
