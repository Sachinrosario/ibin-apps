SALIENT FEATURES :
Morphological :
Ref No: Biju Kumar & Bruce, 1997

Ovigerous Female:
Body 1.9 times as long as wide, widest at Peraeon segment, Suboval, Asymmetrical, Slightly twisted to one side or another; Dorsum weakly vaulted.

Colour diffuse, some specimens yellowish, some light pink, but always lighter than the colour of the host. Chromatophores more or less uniformly distributed. Cephalon 1.2-1.3 times as long as wide, anteriorly somewhat triangular. Slightly constricted anterior to eyes, posterior margin smoothly rounded not trisinuate or weakly so; anterior margin rounded. Eyes 0.4 times width of cephalon,ovate,facets distinct,dark brown.

Pereon broad,convex twisted to one side. Pereonite 4 longest,7 shortest, former 1.5 times longer than the latter. Posterolateral margins of pereonite 1 not produced; those pf pereonites 2-7 progressively produced. Posterolateral margins of pereonite 7 indented. Coxae of pereopods 6 and 7, rarely that of 5, visible in dorsal view.

Pleonites distinct, extending slightly beyond the lateral margins of pereonite 7 and of pleotelson. Pleonite 1 shorter and narrower than 2 partially covered by peronite 7. pleonites 2-5 progressively narrower towards posterior lateral borders of pleonites 2-5 free and slightly expanded; pleonite 2 longest in dorsal view. Pleotelson 1.5 times as wide as long, posterior margin hemispherical, slightly narrower than pleon.

Antennule with 8 or 9 articles, not reaching posterior of cephalon; proximal article slightly expanded; article 3 bearing one spine on anterodistal angle; terminal article with a tuft of setae. Antenna extending to or beyond posterior margin to cephalon composed of 11 or 12 articles; proximal 4th and 5th article with one spine on the posterodistal angle, terminal article with a tuft of fine setae. Antennule and antenna both with tuft of very fine setae distally on all articles.

Mandible with molar lobe and dorsolateral lobe. Palp article 1 longest, article 3 half as long as 2, palp article 3 with 10 spines on distolateral margin. distalmost longest 2 with 2 spines initially . Maxillule with 1 large and 3 small terminal spines. Maxilla widest basally; postero-lateral invagination producing a hemispherical proximal part; lateral lobe with 4 recurved co-planar spines. Inner dorsolateral side of amxilla with setae. MAxilliped with oostegital lobe bearing plumose setae; article 2 with 2 terminal recurved spines.

Pereopod bases without carina, dactyli short,stout,0.8 times as long as propdus. Pereopod 1 shortest; length of pereopods increasing slightly from pereopod 2 to 7; 1-3 similar in morphology. Brood pouch made up of pairs of alternatively overlapping oostegites arising from sternites 2,3,4 and 5.

Pleopod rami lamellar, unequal or subequal (showing variation among individuals) with diffuse chromatophores, chromatophore density and size decreasing slightly from pleonite 1 to 5. Bases with poorly developed accessory lamellae. Pleopod 2 without appendix masculina.

Uropods short, barely reaching beyond anterior 1/4 th of pleotelson, biramous. rami subequal, buntly rounded, non-setose, densely pigmented.

Description of Male :
Body bilaterally symmetrical. slighter smaller than that of female. Mouthpartssimilar to those of female. Pleopod 2 with appendix masculina small shorter than Endopod. Uropod slightly larger thanin female, about half as long as pleotelson. Paired penes present. Colour yellowish.

DIAGNOSTIC CHARACTERISTICS:
Head with anterior margin rounded, not produced; pleonite1 obvisously shorterand narrower than pleonite 2 ; pleotelson rounded; peraeopod dactyli all short, subequal in length to propodi; posterior peraeopods without carina on basis and uropodal rami subequal in length, not reaching posterior margin of pleotelson, apices bluntally rounded.

Additional Remarks:
Elthusa Samariscii is the only species of this genus reported from India. Material Collected by  Biju Kumar and Bruce (1997) are deposited in the Zoologisk Museum, University of Copenhagem, Denmark under REG. No. ZMUC  CRU - 1975.
These parasites are located in the anterior dorsal part of the branchial chamber of the host attached to the branchial arch, with the head projecting into the buccal cavity. The dorsum  of large specimens presses hard against the operculum, making a small oval depression in the inner side. Erosion of the lamellar epithelium and wounds in the branchial arches as a result of the attachment of peraeopods can be clearly seen on the host, when the p[arasite is removed from the branchial chamber.
