SALIENT  FEATURES :
Morphological:  

Diagnostic characteristics:      
Immature female: Length, 6.86 mm: cephalothorax, 5.30 mm; abdomen, 1,56 mm. The prosome separates from the 1st pedigerous segment. The frontal margin of the prosome is rounded, without any trace of a median crest. The last 2 pedigerous segment produced into a pointed tooth.     
The abdomen 4-jointed; the segments and furca in the proportions 23:30:21:15:11 = 100. The furcal rami as long as wide, and each furnished with a small spine and a seta near the base of the 3rd inner seta on the dorsal surface. The 2nd to 4th segments are striated finely on the marginal cuticle.     
The 1st antenna 24-jointed, reaches back to the posterior margin of the last thoracic segment; the joints 8-9 are fused, but 24 and 25 are separate. The 1st joint produced proximally on the anterior proximal corner.      
The mouth appendages agree fairly with Sewell’s description and figures. The 2nd maxilla has 7 sensory appendages on the endopodite, of which the distal one is much longer than the others. In the maxillipede the 2nd basal is furnished proximally with a row of fine spinules, the middle part with spines which increases in size distally, and a large isolated spine apart from the others. There is a longitudinal row of denticles, 6 in number, along the anterior margin of the posterior surface. The 1st joint of the endopodite of the maxillipede bears 4 unequal setae, the 2nd joint has a short seta and a long spine coarsely beset with teeth, 11 in number, along the proximal 2.3 of its length; the distal portion of the spine finely denticulated. The 3rd joint of the endopodite has the similar strong spine which has 14 isolated teeth and spinules on the distal portion.    
The 5th pair of legs consist of 3 joints of about equal lengths on either side; the distal joint shows slight indication of demarcation at the proximal 1.4 of the joint. 

Additional  remarks:      

This is closely related to C. chelifer and also C. indicus Sewell. C. chelifer  is characterized by the possession of the cephalic spine. The spine differs from  C. robustus Vervoort in the structure of the 5th pair of legs.  The specimen is not clearly identical with C. simplex Wolfenden in its larger size and by the possession of an isolated tooth on the second  basal joint of the maxillipede. Brodsky (1950) recorded the occurrence of C. indicus  from the deep waters of  Far-eastern and polar seas. According to Sewell’s figure the last pedigerous segment produced triangularly but the apex is bluntly pointed. Brodsky’s specimen has the last pedigerous segment with short sharp point at the apex; the 5th pair of legs figured by Brodsky agree well with the 5th leg of this specimen in the copepodite stage V; the distal joint is furnished with an apical and subapical spines; the surface of the joint is poorly furnished with spinules.
