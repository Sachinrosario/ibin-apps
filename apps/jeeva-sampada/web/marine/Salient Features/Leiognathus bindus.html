SALIENT FEATURES:

Morphological:

Diagnostic characters: Abdomen trenchant, more convex than opposite dorsal profile, Spinous dorsal with an orange spot, inferiorly edged with black.. Protracted mouth parts  form an horizontal tube, Scales small, all over the body including breast. Lateral line straight at first , later running less convex to the dorsal profile and ending below the middle of the soft dorsal.      

 

Sex attributes: Sexes are differentiated by the visibility of the light organs. Leiognathids have a well developed  internal luminescent system with a light organ. The outer surface of the light organ in males is densely pigmented with melanophores and is visible to the exterior as a black spot close to the pectoral fin base  where the muscle is transparent.

In the female there is no transparent region under the pectoral fin and hence the light organ is not visible                                               Ref no: 16

 

Descriptive characters: Body deep oval and strongly compressed, particularly  in the lower part, Ventral profile of body more markedly convex than the dorsal profile. Abdomen before anal is more strongly convex , the convexity becoming more pronounced as the specimens become larger. Occipital region shows a slight concavity and gradually rises to the dorsal profile. Protracted mouth parts point forward. Lips thin and narrow. Commencement of gape of mouth below level of lower margin of eye. Tips of maxilla just reaches the front border of the eye. Mandible slightly concave. Teeth , small , numerous , in a single series in both the jaws. Inter-orbital space concave , bounded by the supra orbital ridges which continue  posteriorly as the outer edges of the nuchal spine.. The median portion of the nuchal  spine is elevated as a ridge. Two small spines on the top of the head, opposite front border of the eye, equally prominent, the inner opposite the ridge forming the outer borders of the inter-orbital space.. Upper rim of orbit behind the spines finely serrulate. Pre opercle with an obtuse angle, its lower margin finely serrate. Scales small, all over body including breast; head scaleless. Dorsal and anal spines weak and laterally compressed. 2nd anal spine shorter and weaker than the 2nd dorsal spine. 3rd and 4th dorsal spine and 3rd anal spine serrated anteriorly. For about half length from the base. Ventrals short , their tips ending far in front of origin of anal. Ventral fin with a long axillary scale. Caudal fin deeply forked with spreading pointed lobes.

Colour: Body silvery; abdomen more silvery than back. Dark irregular , somewhat vermiculate or semicircular markings in a zigzag pattern, commencing immediately behind head and extending to the end of the soft dorsal, laterally extending down to less  than half height. Spinous part of dorsal fin , black at half height, above which the membrane between the 2nd and fith spines bears a bright orange blotch which turns yellow on preservation in formalin. Tip of snout and ventral half of body with grey dots, which tend to be arranged in rows, midlaterally. Pectoral axil dark. Faint yellow colour on basal part of spinous anal fin membrane. Caudal, especially its posterior margins are dusky.      Ref No: (9,10,34).

Meristic Characters:D.VIII,16; A.III,14; P.18-20; C.24.   Ref no: (9,10,18)

 

Feeding Habit:

Main food: Copepods (Acartia, Centropages, Pseudodiaptomus and Eucalanus)

The other items of importance include Polychaetes and Cladocerans (Evadne and Penilia). The other items found in the stomach contents were penaeid prawns, larval bivalves and  larval crustaceans. Phytoplankton (mainly diatoms) formed only a minor portion constituting less than 10% of the total volume.

Copepods were seen in maximum abundance in November, Polychaetes during May and October and Cladocerans during July and September.

Feeding type: Mainly zooplankton feeder.  The feeding proclivities reveal that they are not highly voracious. There is also no evidence for a reduction or temporary suspension of feeding activity during the period of spawning.                Ref no:2, 35
