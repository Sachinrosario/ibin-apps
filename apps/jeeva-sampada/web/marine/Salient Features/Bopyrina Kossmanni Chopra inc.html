Salient Features
Morphological :
Female: The parasite appears pinkish through the carapace of the host the color being due to the mass of eggs in the large incubatory cavity. Body of the female is markedly asymmetrical in form one side of the body being mush shorter than the other. The lateral margins of all the segments are to a greater or less extent pigmented .The colour patches diminish in size towards the posterior thoracic segments and on the last somite especially on the deformed side, only a minute speck is visible .A small colour patch covers the antero- lateral corners of the head also. On the ventral surface of all the oostegites are coloured and the pigment is denser than the that in the foregoing species. There a few irregular spots on the abdomen also. The pigment is dark brown and not black as it is in most species.
The head seen from the dorsal surface, appears as a rounded mass with an anterior lamina projecting forwards. The latter is much narrower and its antero-latteral angles are not sharp the posterior limit of the head is not entirely obliterated by its fusion with the thorax, but the line of demarcation between the two is faintly visible. Near the anterior margin is placed a pair of small eyes. The thoracic segments are distinct from one another both dorsally and laterally. The first segment is the shortest of all , and in its mid dorsal portion appears slightly more than half as long as the second. On both the side the segments are notched and the margins of the first four are separated in to two each by a slight cleft, ovarian bosses absent and the epimera are very poorly developed. The length of the abdomen is much less than the breadth and the dorsal fusion of its segments is less complete. The lines of demarcation between some of the anterior segments, though somewhat indistinct can be made on the dorsal surface for a considerable distance. Both the lateral margins are notched with relation to the segments, but the notches on the shorter side of the body are much deeper than those on the other. The margins of the fifth segment extent beyond the posterior extremity of the sixth, but not to as great an extent the backwardly projecting margins of the fifth segment are rounded instead of being as is the case in the proceeding species. The posterior margin of the sixth segment is entire regularly rounded. The anterior margin of the head is formed by the forwardly projecting anterior lamina forms the posterior boundary the antennules and antennae are greatly reduced and the former appear to be shorter than the latter. The antennules are formed of three segments with the basal segment swollen and the terminal greatly reduced. There appear to be no hairs on the terminal segment in both the pairs of antennae. All the other appendages are normal. The maxillipeds are proportionately broader and the palp which carries seven hairs is more rounded anteriorly. The large incubator cavity is only partially roofed over by the oostegites. The anterior margin however is not straight but is convexly rounded. The internal crust is not entire for it is provided near its proximal end with two or three small lobs the posterior lobe is somewhat posteriorly. The remaining oostegites are small, the fifth pair extending well backwards, so as to cover a part of the ventral surface of the abdomen. The boss on the basal segment of the thoracic legs is not strongly developed, and the external margins of the merus and carpus are covered with hairs.
The pleopods somewhat well developed each are a triangular plate with pointed apex, covering portion of the succeeding appendage. The fifth pair of pleopods and uropods are absent

MALE:   Body whitish in colour with the borders of the thorax slightly brownish. There is s streak of the same colour on each of the last three segments and a few irregulars spots scattered on the abdomen.
The head is semicircular in shape, with the anterior margin almost straight. A pair of prominent eyes is placed near the antero-lateral corners of the head. On the ventral surface the usual appendages are present.
The thoracic segments are distinct and their lateral margins are deeply notched. The legs are well developed and have strong claws. The propodus is proportionately large and carries a prominent projection, bearing hairs, against which the dactylus closes.
The abdomen is almost as broad as long, and the breadth diminishes gradually posteriorly, so that the last segment is about half as broad as the first. The segments are more or less completely fused dorsally and even on the lateral margins only the first two are indicated by slight undulations. The terminal segment does not carry any anal spines. Pleopods and uropods are altogether absent.

Diagnostic characteristics: abdomen broader than long,; abdominal segments fused dorsally but quite distinct on both sides; fifth abdominal segment extending beyond sixth, its posterior extremities rounded; abdomen of male almost as long as broad and tapering slightly at interior; all abdominal segments in male completely fused dorsally first two slightly indicated laterally. Terminal segment rounded; anal spines absent.
