SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological: Encrusting, colonies white in colour and may grow to large dimensions. Zooecia delicate, rectangular with adjacent rows arranged in alternate fashion and separated by distinct grooves. The distal end of the preceding zooecium slightly over arch the proximal end of the succeeding one. Gymnocyst moderately developed proximally. Opesia elongate with a very vestigial proximal cryptocyst. Three spines present at the distal end, one above the median line of the operculum the other two lateral. Six to eight pairs of basally jointed rarely branching slender spines over arch the frontal membrane. Operculum large and quite distinct from the frontal membrane, chitinised at the border. A pair of long forked (once, twice or thrice) spines ornament the operculum.
Diagnostic characteristics: In live specimens when the opercula are open the spines which they bear are seen parallel to the frontal membrane a fact which testifies to the flexibility of the spines. From the middle of the front surface of the operculum arises a pair of very elongate, bifurcate chitinous spines which extend far over the base of the distal zooecium. It is evident that this anomalous condition together with the spinous adornment of the marginal region are the characters which gave specific distinction for this species.Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite
Descriptive characters: Osburn noticed in his specimens opercular spines with a single bifurcation, and so is the case with M. bengalensis figured by Thornely (1907). But in the material collected by Menon, zooecia occurred with opercular spines which had undergone branching of the second or even the third order. Simple spines were also noticed. The ancestrula of this species is single and probably this is the only dependable character to distinguish Electra from Membranipora.
