SALIENT FEATURES:
Morphological:   Often encrusting. Zooecia large, distinct aboral areas, sometimes with tubercles. Sinus of the opesia deep. The cryptocyst is much depressed at the level of the opesiules and the median process ascends steeply towards the opesia, its direction being continued by the lower lip, which is thus nearly vertical. The lower lip nearly fills the sinus, leaving a very narrow slit between its free edge and the base of the operculum, which is raised far above the level of the depressed part of the cryptocyst. The avicularia are prominent distally, their mandibles acute with decurved points. The larger opesiules alone reaches the base, frequently expanding considerably before it completes its closed, loop-like insertion. Two kinds of calipers are present, large and small respectively, the larger ones being much thicker. Diagnostic characters: A pointed mandible, with its hooked termination even though not always sharply marked is specially characteristic of this species and its varieties. The halves of the main sclerite approach one another distally, where they may be parallel for a short distance. Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters:   The form collected by Harmer had an encrusting habit. Even though ovicells are described by Hincks (1880) and Maplestone (1905) in the forms which they collected, they are wanting in Siboga specimens.
  All the salient features are similar to those illustrated by Levinsen (1909) except that the lower lip was smaller or completely wanting. Other features are the deep sinus of the opesia and the marked asymmetry of the opesiules, of which the smaller one is shallow and close to the lateral wall. The aboral tubercles are variable. The basal view shows the usual mode of insertion of the larger opesiules and further demonstrates the fact that this belongs to the side of the zooecium which contains the alimentary canal. The size of the insertion varies much, but it is constantly closed. In the specimen from India, many of the opesiules had simple, delicate, calcareous spines projecting into them from various parts of their walls, near the frontal surface.
  The aboral cavities are large and conspicuous, particularly in young zooecia and avicularia, those of the avicularia being much larger. The mandible is very acutely pointed, its free tip being bent down in a hook-like manner.
  In the Paumben specimen (Siboga) some slight evidence has been obtained with regard to the mode of origin of the larger spicules, but development seem to take place so rapidly that the smaller spicules are complete by the time they are distinguishable. The growing margin of the colony contained considerable mass of more or less solid tissue, in which are embedded numerous spicules. The large calipers calcified from the middle outwards, the recurved tips being the parts last formed.
