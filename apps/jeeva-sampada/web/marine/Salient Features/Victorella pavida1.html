Morphological: Colonies pale brown but show a range from almost translucency to deep earthy brown. Colonies spread out from a central point. Each zooecium typically composed of a proximal adnate tubular portion, a median broad adnate part and an erect cylindrical distal region. Proximal, adnate tubular portion of the zooecium vary in colour, in length and in thickness. Orifice squarish but in resting zooids as well as in zooecia without polypides orifice rounded. Tentacles eight in number. Pleated collar well developed provided with thin chitinous rods. Diagnostic characters: Sex attributes: Hermaphrodite  Descriptive characters:   Annandale (1907a) who described this species for the first time from Indian waters noticed differences between the Indian material and the British material. He stated that possibly there are slight differences between the Bengal and the British races but it is difficult to be sure that such differences are constant without examining a large number of specimens from different localities. Annandale (1907a) noticed hibernacula in his specimens, but this structure has not been noticed in the present form even though numerous resting buds were observed in the month of August. One point which Annandale noticed in the forms collected during the earlier period of winter, that the "zooecia were however closely packed together" was observed only during the monsoon and early post-monsoon periods. In the colonies collected both in November and in January Annandale noticed zooids without polypides. He remarked "frequently even empty zooecia retain their external form except that the aperture was tightly closed and the adjacent region circular in cross section, and in many cases the collar persisted as wrinkled and pleated funnel-shaped membrane extruded from the distal extremity of the zooecium". An interesting process has been observed in nearly fifty percent of the zooids from a panel exposed for a period of 33 days. In the zooids the polypides were seen to be in various stages of ejection resulting in empty zooecia. The reason for this curious 'evisceration' is not clear. Probably this will explain the presence of empty zooids which Annandale noticed and also seen in the present specimens. The process of 'evisceration' is preceded by the disintegration of the anterior region of the polypides.
  Annandale's creation of V. bengalensis was based on four characters he noticed in the zooids collected on 24 December from a pond at Port Canning. They are:-
1)  In the small size of the swelling from which the zooecia arise.
2)  In the fact that a considerable number of zooecia are frequently grouped together with very short intervening stola.
3)  In the more powerful development of the gizzard.
4)  In the fact that the distal part of the adult zoeocia is approximately circular in cross section.
One difference noticed in Victorella pavida colonies occurring in Cochin waters was their more luxuriant growth during the monsoon period when the salinity of the medium falls considerably. Buds at the distal region of the zooecia are never a usual occurrence even though colonies collected during the month of December possessed individuals with distal buds. In the light of these observations it seems that Annandale's creation of a new species of V. bengalensis was unwarranted and the forms occurring in Indian waters seem in all probability representatives of the cosmopolitan species Victorella pavida.
