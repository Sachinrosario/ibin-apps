Salient features:
Morphological:

  The whole body is hirsute on the dorsal surface. There is no torsion of the body which is marked by a number of densely scattered red pigment spots giving the parasite a very light reddish colour. The edge of the cephalic plate is toothed and has no free flexible margine.

Appendages:- The 1st antenna is short, 3-jointed and uniramous. The basipodite has 3 spinous processes on its outer side. The 2nd joint is half as broad as the 1st and bears 3 setae on its inner surface and a tuft of hairs on the outer side. The 3rd joint has 4 terminal bristles (Fig.3). The 2nd (Fig. 4) antenna is nearly 5 times longer than the 1st. It is 8-jointed and flagellar. Each joint bears one or two spines on its distal edge while the terminal joint bears three long bristles.

  The mouth parts are similar to those of M. acartii, expecting for the difference in the length of the joints of the 2nd maxilla. In the present form all the mouth parts are directed anteriorly (Fig.6).

  The thoracic legs appear better fitted for prehension. The products is large and swollen, with the sharp-pointed dactylus folding on it giving the leg a chelate termination (Fig.5).

  There are five parts of biramous pleopods. The endopodite bears two long setae and the exopodite bears four long setae (Fig.7).

  The uropods are biramous. The protopodite is single-jointed, stout, cylindrical. The exopodite has a sharp ridge bearing three spines and ends in a long curved spine.

  The sides of the joint are produced into two stout, short spines, one on either side. The endopodite is smaller but single jointed. Its distal edge is drawn into two short stout spines, while it terminates in a long stout spine (Fig. 8).


Additional remarks:
  Microniscus EUCALANII was described by Gnanamuthu and Krishnaswamy (1948), based on one specimen, collected from the Cephalothorax of the copepod, Eucalanus crassus. There was no means of determining whether it was a female, male or larva and hence it was provisionally described as different species of the genus MICRONISCUS, with the specific name indicating the genus of the host copepod
