## Bird Identification – Version 3 – Species (3.0.0)

Version 3 identifies the **species**, not only the group: 1,326 birds recorded in India.

Proprietary right holder: Dr. K.N. Ganeshaiah · Updated in 2026 by Dr. Sachin A. Rosario

### What is new
- **Species-level identification** for 1,326 Indian birds, with each bird's chance shown in per cent.
- **13 simple questions, in any order, each with "Don't know":**
  - kind of bird and size;
  - colour of the head, of the back and wings, and of the breast and belly;
  - markings (20 kinds, for example crest, eyebrow, wing bars, streaks);
  - bill shape and bill colour;
  - leg colour and leg length;
  - tail length;
  - habitat and behaviour.
- **Best next question.** The app suggests the question that splits the likely birds most.
- **Male and female plumages** are both counted for the 284 species where they differ.
- **Place and month are a backup.** They move up birds recorded in that state or month (GBIF records) and move the others down, but never remove them.
- **Updated names:** eBird/Clements Checklist 2025. Older names are shown in brackets and can be searched.
- **Species pages**, each with:
  - credited photos;
  - how to recognise the bird, including female and young plumage;
  - size from AVONET measurements;
  - a Wikipedia description and a link to eBird;
  - records by month and by state;
  - similar birds, with a Compare button.
- **All species list** with filters by kind, family and state; **Compare** any two birds; **Search**.
- **48 new drawings** for the questions.
- **Feedback by e-mail**, and works on phones and offline.

### How good is it?
Each species was tested with a simulated observer who makes realistic mistakes:

| Test | Right bird 1st | In top 3 | In top 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| All questions | 74% | 93% | 99% |
| All questions + place and month | 84% | 97% | 99.8% |
| Only the 8 suggested questions + place and month | 76% | 91% | 98.5% |
| Only the 5 suggested questions + place and month | 56% | 78% | 92% |

The simulation uses the app's own descriptions, so real use will score somewhat lower. The hardest birds are leaf warblers, pipits, larks and female sunbirds.
